# What's provable about the dual without committing to $b_\lambda$

User's challenge: prove what we can about the SDP/LP dual side
*without* an explicit demand vector $b$, then characterise what
$b_\lambda$ has to look like for the magic-predicate to fall out.

The exercise is useful because:
(i) general LP/SDP facts hold for *any* $b$;
(ii) a magic-predicate constraint pins $b_\lambda$ to a small family;
(iii) the family's shape tells us what physical / algebraic role $b$
plays.

## Part I — Provable without $b_\lambda$

Setup: substrate $q > 1$ (generic, not a root of unity), strand
count $n$, Wedderburn-block index $\lambda \vdash n$. Cost vector
$c$ is the **Hecke energy**: $c_\lambda = w_\lambda(q) =
\dim_q(V_\lambda)^2 / [n]_q!$.

### I.1 — Positivity of cost

**Lemma 1.** For all $q > 1$ and all $\lambda \vdash n$, $w_\lambda(q) > 0$.

*Proof:* $[k]_q = q^{k-1} + q^{k-3} + \cdots + q^{-(k-1)}$ is a sum
of positive terms for $q > 0$. Hence $[k]_q > 0$. The hook-length
factorisation $\dim_q(V_\lambda) = [n]_q! / \prod [h(x)]_q$ gives
$\dim_q(V_\lambda) > 0$. Squaring and dividing by $[n]_q!$
preserves positivity. $\square$

**Corollary.** The cost vector $c$ lies strictly in the interior of
the positive orthant $\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{p(n)}$ at every $q > 1$.

### I.2 — Dual feasibility (independent of $b$)

**Lemma 2.** $y = 0$ is dual-feasible regardless of $b$, since
$A^\top \cdot 0 = 0 \preceq c$ (because $c > 0$).

**Corollary.** $\langle b, 0 \rangle = 0$ is a trivial lower bound on
the primal optimum. The dual is bounded below by 0 for any feasible
$b$.

### I.3 — Hook-length symmetry under conjugation

**Lemma 3.** For every $\lambda \vdash n$, $w_\lambda(q) =
w_{\lambda'}(q)$ where $\lambda'$ is the transposed partition.

*Proof:* Hook lengths are preserved under transposition (already
proved as `hook_lengths_invariant_under_transpose`). Hence
$\prod_x [h(x)]_q$ is unchanged. Squaring and dividing by $[n]_q!$
gives the same value. $\square$

**Consequence:** the cost vector $c$ has a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-symmetry
under $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$. Self-conjugate $\lambda$
are fixed points; non-self-conjugate $\lambda$ come in pairs with
identical costs.

### I.4 — Complementary slackness without $b$

**Lemma 4.** At any LP/SDP optimum $(T^*, u^*)$, complementary
slackness holds:

$$
  u^*_\lambda \cdot (c_\lambda - (A^\top u^*)_\lambda) \;=\; 0
  \qquad \forall \lambda.
$$

This is independent of $b$.

**Corollary.** $\operatorname{supp}(u^*) \subseteq
\{\lambda : (A^\top u^*)_\lambda = c_\lambda\}$ (the "tight" set).

### I.5 — Active basis at distinct-cost points

**Lemma 5.** If the cost vector $c$ has all-distinct entries (i.e.,
$c_\lambda \neq c_\mu$ for $\lambda \neq \mu$ when $\lambda$ and
$\mu$ are not conjugate), then the dual polytope's vertices have
$|\operatorname{supp}(y)| \leq m$ where $m$ = number of primal
equality constraints.

For our SDP, $m$ depends on the specific constraint structure but
is bounded by the dimension of the demand space — typically much
smaller than $p(n)$.

### I.6 — Parametric stability

**Lemma 6.** For fixed $b$, the function $q \mapsto u^*(n, q, b)$ is
piecewise-rational on $(0, \infty)$, with regime boundaries at
critical $q^*$ where the active basis changes.

*Proof sketch:* the LP/SDP optimum is given by KKT equations, which
are polynomial in the data $(c, A, b)$. Since $c$ is rational in $q$,
so is the optimum on each regime. Regime boundaries are at $q^*$
where two solutions tie. $\square$

### I.7 — All of the above is independent of $b$

The lemmas above use only the cost $c$ (= Hecke energy), the
constraint matrix $A$ (= Wedderburn structure), and the sign
structure ($c > 0$). They constrain the dual polytope without
specifying which point on it is the optimum.

The optimum's *location* (and hence $\operatorname{supp}(u^*)$) is
where $b$ enters.

## Part II — What $b_\lambda$ must look like for magic at $n$

For the magic predicate $|\operatorname{supp}(u^*(n, q_0, b))| = 1$
to hold at strand count $n$, the demand vector $b$ must satisfy
specific structural conditions.

### II.1 — Necessary condition: $b$ in cone of one constraint normal

**Theorem (necessary condition for magic).** Let $b$ be such that
the dual optimum has $|\operatorname{supp}(u^*)| = 1$, with the
sole positive component at block $\lambda^*$. Then $b$ lies in the
cone generated by the constraint normal $A_{\lambda^*}$:

$$
  b \;=\; A_{\lambda^*} \cdot t
  \quad\text{for some}\quad t \geq 0.
$$

*Proof sketch:* by KKT, at the optimum we have $u^*_{\lambda^*} >
0$ and $u^*_\mu = 0$ for $\mu \neq \lambda^*$. Primal feasibility
requires $\sum_\lambda T^*_\lambda \cdot A_\lambda = b$. Since all
$\mu \neq \lambda^*$ have inactive primal constraint, we can take
$T^*_\mu = 0$. Hence $b = T^*_{\lambda^*} \cdot A_{\lambda^*}$,
proving $b$ is a non-negative scalar multiple of the
$\lambda^*$-normal. $\square$

**Operational reading:** the demand $b$ must be aligned with the
extremal ray of the dual cone corresponding to $\lambda^*$. In
Hecke-algebraic language, $b$ is a Wedderburn-block indicator
weighted by the constraint-direction at $\lambda^*$.

### II.2 — Sufficient condition: $b$ on the right ray, $\lambda^*$ argmax

**Theorem (sufficient condition).** Suppose at strand count $n$,
substrate $q$:

(a) The cost vector $c$ has a unique argmax at some $\lambda^*$
    (modulo conjugate-pair ties, in which case $\{\lambda^*,
    (\lambda^*)'\}$).

(b) The demand vector $b$ is a positive multiple of the constraint
    normal $A_{\lambda^*}$.

Then the LP optimum has $\operatorname{supp}(u^*) = \{\lambda^*\}$.

*Proof sketch:* the LP minimises $\langle c, T \rangle$ subject to
$AT = b$, $T \geq 0$. With $b \propto A_{\lambda^*}$, the unique
optimum is $T^* \propto e_{\lambda^*}$ (the standard basis vector).
Dual: $u^*_{\lambda^*} > 0$, others zero, by complementary
slackness. $\square$

### II.3 — The demand-shape conjecture

**Conjecture (proposed shape of $b_\lambda$ at strand count $n$,
substrate $q_0$).** The demand vector $b_n^{q_0}$ is the
**Markov-trace pairing** of a fixed "vacuum braid" $w_n^{\mathrm{vac}}
\in H_n(q_0)$ — concretely:

$$
  b^{q_0}_{n, \lambda}
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{tr}_M\bigl(\rho_\lambda(w_n^{\mathrm{vac}})\bigr)
$$

where $\rho_\lambda \colon H_n(q_0) \to \mathrm{End}(V_\lambda)$ is
the Hoefsmit representation. The "vacuum braid" is the canonical
Markov closure of the trivial braid (or a specific identity-like
element).

Under this conjecture, the demand at each $n$ is:

- Determined by $q_0$ (since $\mathrm{tr}_M$ is $q$-dependent).
- Concentrated on the dominant Wedderburn blocks of
  $w_n^{\mathrm{vac}}$.
- At magic $n$, conjecturally aligned with the *single*
  self-conjugate $\lambda^*$ where the SDP optimum is rank-1.

This is a *q-dependent* choice of demand. The simplification: at
generic $q$, the demand has a clean structural form derivable from
the Hecke / Markov-trace machinery.

### II.4 — Cleaner alternative: $b$ uniform on self-conjugate blocks

**Alternative conjecture:** $b_{n, \lambda} = 1$ if $\lambda =
\lambda'$ (self-conjugate), $0$ otherwise.

This is *q-independent* and respects the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-symmetry of
the cost (Lemma 3). The LP/SDP then minimises Hecke energy subject
to "the answer must be in the self-conjugate sector."

At magic $n$, conjecturally the unique self-conjugate $\lambda^*$
that minimises cost is the active block. At non-magic $n$, multiple
self-conjugates compete and the optimum is degenerate (rank > 1).

This alternative has the advantage of being *q-independent*, which
matches the user's earlier intuition that magic should hold at
generic $q > 1$.

## Part III — Decision tree for fixing $b$

To make the conjecture provable, we need to commit to one of:

| Option | $b_{n, \lambda}$ | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(A) Markov trace** | $\mathrm{tr}_M(\rho_\lambda(w_n^{\mathrm{vac}}))$ | natural Hecke-algebraic | $q$-dependent, hard symbolic |
| **(B) Self-conj indicator** | $\mathbf{1}[\lambda = \lambda']$ | $q$-independent, simple | doesn't pick out magic alone |
| **(C) Markov + self-conj** | $\mathrm{tr}_M$ restricted to self-conj | best of both | needs proof of consistency |
| **(D) BB-stratum boundary** | indicator of complete-intersection BB-strata | matches `conj:homological-shell-condensation` | abstract, not directly computable |

Each option produces a different magic-predicate prediction. The
empirical match at $q_0$ — that the predicted set equals
$\{1, 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126\}$ — selects the *correct* option.

Without committing yet, I observe:

- Option (A) corresponds to the existing `conj:mass-endomorphism`
  framework where the Markov trace is the central object.
- Option (B) is the simplest q-independent test.
- Option (C) is conjecturally "right" since both ingredients appear
  in the framework.
- Option (D) is the most abstract but matches the spectral-sequence
  picture.

## Conclusion

**Provable without $b$:** general LP/SDP facts (positivity,
feasibility, complementary slackness, conjugate symmetry, parametric
piecewise-rationality).

**Requires $b$ to prove:** any specific magic-predicate claim.

**The shape of $b$:** four candidates above, all consistent with
the framework. The empirical match at $q_0$ selects.

## Part IV — Empirical refutation of candidate (B)

The simplest candidate — option (B), self-conjugate-block indicator
demand — has been **empirically tested** via
[`algebraic_primality_probe.py`](algebraic_primality_probe.py).
Result: it does **not** produce the magic ladder at $q_0$.

Probe summary at $q_0 \approx 1.10974$ (candidate-B demand: LP
optimum = argmax-Wedderburn-weight self-conjugate $\lambda$):

| $n$ | empirical magic | candidate-B predicts | agrees? |
|---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | yes | yes | yes |
| 2 | yes | no (q(2)=0, no self-conj) | no |
| 3 | no | yes (one SC: [2,1]) | no |
| 4 | no | yes ([2,2]) | no |
| 5–7 | no | yes (each has single SC) | no |
| 8 | yes | yes ([4,2,1,1]) | yes |
| 9–16 | no | yes (multiple SC, but unique argmax) | no |

Agreements: **2 of 13** $n$-values. Most non-magic $n$ have a
unique-argmax self-conj block, so candidate-B incorrectly flags
them as magic. The $n = 2$ degenerate case (zero self-conj) flags
as non-magic, contradicting empirical magic-2.

So **candidate (B) is rejected**. This adds to our growing list of
negative results:

1. Partition statistics (count, Durfee, hook spread) — fail.
2. LR self-fusion self-conjugacy — fail.
3. q-dim argmax — fail (regime change at n=14, no magic alignment).
4. **Candidate-B demand** — fail (this section).

The demand vector must be richer than the self-conjugate
indicator. Likely candidates remaining:

- (A) Markov trace pairing — needs specification of "vacuum braid"
  at each $n$.
- (C) Markov restricted to self-conjugate — combination.
- (D) BB-stratum boundary — abstract, hardest to compute.

The numerical cvxpy probe is **blocked** until one of these is
specified concretely. Either route requires extracting the demand
from the existing `prop:confinement-sdp` formulation.
