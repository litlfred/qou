The mass endomorphism forms the structural spine of the framework's physical predictions. Every observable mass—from the electron to uranium—factors through a single categorical object: the Hecke mass element $\Phi_{\mathrm{mass}}(\beta) \in H_n(R, q)$. Its Markov trace yields the categorical mass $\mathfrak{m}(\varrho) = |\mathrm{tr}_M(\rho(\beta))| \cdot \mathrm{VolDensity}(K, q_0)$.

We first state the mass endomorphism conjecture. We then develop its foundational structure: the categorical mass identity ([mass identity](#def:mass-identity)) with its existence and uniqueness proofs, the topological decomposition theorem ([topological mass](#thm:topological-mass)), and the archimedean corollary. The decomposition theorem shows how the mass integrand splits into eigenspace, holonomy, transient, and quantum-dilogarithm contributions. The archimedean corollary realises the categorical mass as a non-abelian multiplicative integral.

The three calibration inputs ($m_e$, $m_\mu/m_e$, $m_\pi$) act as categorical constraints. They fix the substrate parameter $q_0$ and the chiral scale. We do not use them as archimedean fitting targets; we derive everything else.

A note on scope: this section's closed-form blocks supply the substrate-level mass identity used throughout the chapter cycle. Subsequent sub-sections specialise the identity to particular mass channels (Markov-trace endomorphism, $\chi$-character closed forms, Hecke-Baer-star chirality). The substrate-level statement established here factorises through each specialisation without modification. Readers focused on a specific channel may treat the identity as a black box. Readers focused on structural content should work through the existence and uniqueness proofs.
