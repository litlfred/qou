<!-- FILE_START: intro-confinement-level.md -->
Categorical confinement is a stratification, not a binary property. Each composite braid word carries a confinement level $\ell \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$. This integer records how many Hecke-reduction steps separate the word from a trivial closure. The limit of this stratification is confinement level infinity. This defines the cemetery group: the categorical subgroup of braid words that become quotient-trivial under the substrate flow.

We define the confinement level via the Hecke-reduction filtration. We prove this level is preserved under Markov moves. We then identify the cemetery group as the kernel of the substrate-flow quotient. We compute the confinement level for standard particle braid words (such as the electron, muon, and proton) and tabulate the cemetery group generators. The result is the level function used to organize confined-particle shells.

**Definition.**
*Fix the substrate parameter $q$. Sort the Wedderburn-block knot operators by energy ([knot operator energy](#def:knot-operator-energy)):*

$$
  0 \;<\; E_1(q) \;\leq\; E_2(q) \;\leq\; \cdots \;\leq\; E_m(q),
$$

*where $E_\ell(q)$ is the $\ell$-th distinct nonzero value of $\{E(\lambda, q) : \lambda \vdash k,\; k \leq k_{\max}\}$. We define the **confinement filtration** $J^\bullet(q)$ of $H_n(R, q)$ by:*

$$
  J^\ell(q)
  \;:=\;
  \text{subalgebra generated by}
  \;\{\lambda : E(\lambda, q) \leq E_\ell(q)\}.
$$

*Tied operators (those with $E(\lambda_i, q) = E(\lambda_j, q)$) enter the filtration **together** at the level of their common energy. The filtration is therefore well-defined under degeneracy.*

*A closed braid $K$ with Hecke normal form ([Hecke normal form](#def:hecke-normal-form)) $T_w \in H_n(R, q)$ is **confined at level $\ell$ at $q$** if and only if $\ell$ is the smallest integer with $T_w \in J^\ell(q)$. We write $\ell(K, q) := \min\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$.*

**$q$-dependence.**
*The filtration is intrinsically $q$-dependent. The relative ordering of $E(\lambda, q)$ shifts with $q$, so a knot may have $\ell(K, q_1) \neq \ell(K, q_2)$ for $q_1 \neq q_2$. There are two regimes of $q$-dependence:*

1. *Reordering: $E(\lambda_i, q)$ and $E(\lambda_j, q)$ swap relative position at some $q^* \in Q$. The filtration re-indexes but the set of operators at each level remains continuous.*
2. *Vanishing: $E(\lambda_\ell, q^*) = 0$ at the LP boundary ([knot operator energy](#def:knot-operator-energy)). The operator drops out of the filtration entirely, $J^\ell$ collapses, and the level structure rebuilds. This is a categorical phase transition.*

**Per-atom truncation $k_{\max}(K, q)$—witnessed, not chosen.**
*The truncation $k_{\max}$ is **not a global pragmatic parameter**. The **interaction-strand requirement** determines it per atom-knot $K$ at $q$: $k_{\max}(K, q)$ is the smallest $k$ such that the NF $T_w$ of $K$ requires no partition of size $> k$ to express its operator content. Concretely:*

1. *Compute the NF $T_w \in H_{n(K)}(q)$.*
2. *Determine the maximal $k \leq n(K)$ such that the operator $T_w$ has a nonzero projection on some Wedderburn block $\lambda \vdash k$ with $d_\lambda \geq 2$ (mixing rep).*
3. *Set $k_{\max}(K, q)$ to this value.*

*Non-magic atoms typically have small $k_{\max}$ (few generators needed for the interaction graph). Magic configurations have larger $k_{\max}$ because shell closure forces additional operator content. We compute this once per atom-knot and **witness** it as part of $K$'s NF data, never choosing it externally.*

**Magic configurations.**
*A confined knot $K$ at $q$ is **deeply confined** at level $\ell$ if and only if $E_\ell(q) \ll E_{\ell+1}(q)$ (there is a large energy gap to the next operator). In nuclear contexts, this corresponds to **magic numbers** $A \in \{2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126\}$: the nucleus sits at the bottom of an energy gap, far from the next LP-boundary. Its $k_{\max}(K, q)$ jumps to a higher value than its non-magic neighbors because more strand interactions are required to express the closed-shell structure.*

**Categorical interpretation.**
*Conjecturally, the filtration $J^\bullet(q)$ coincides with the $\mathbb{Z}$-grading filtration on the corresponding KLR module under the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism (see [confinement–grading correspondence](#conj:confinement-grading-correspondence)). This identifies the heuristic shadow-price ordering with a rigid homological grading and supplies a structural cutoff for the crystal-graph traversal that replaces S-polynomial overlap analysis.*

<!-- FILE_START: confinement-level-unique.md -->
**Proposition.**
*The confinement level $\ell(K, q) = \min\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$ is unique and well-defined because:*

1. *the filtration $J^\bullet(q)$ is **monotone**: $J^0(q) \subseteq J^1(q) \subseteq \cdots \subseteq J^m(q) = H_n(R, q)$;*
2. *the set of valid levels $\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$ is **upward-closed** in $\{0, 1, \ldots, m\}$ (if $T_w \in J^\ell(q)$ and $\ell' \geq \ell$, then $T_w \in J^{\ell'}(q)$);*
3. *the set is **nonempty** (it contains $m$ since $J^m = H_n$).*

*Therefore, the minimum exists and is unique. Tied operators ([confinement level](#def:confinement-level)) enter the filtration together at a single level, so the level remains uniquely defined even with degeneracy.*

*We have local uniqueness in $q$: by the continuity of shadow prices on the generic locus, $\ell(K, q')$ is locally constant on a neighborhood of $q$, except at the boundary variety where some $E(\lambda, q) = 0$. See the $\preceq_q$ ordering in [local shadow ordering](#def:local-shadow-ordering).*

<!-- FILE_START: cemetery-group.md -->
**Definition.**
*Let $H_n(R, q)$ be the Iwahori–Hecke algebra and let
$\mathcal{K}_n$ denote the category of finitely generated graded
projective modules over the Khovanov–Lauda–Rouquier algebra whose
cyclotomic quotient is $H_n(R, q)$ (Brundan–Kleshchev
\cite{brundankleshchev2009}).  The fibre functor
$\tau \colon \mathcal{K}_n \to \mathrm{Vect}$ acts as decategorification
by graded Euler characteristic: for a graded module
$M = \bigoplus_{d \in \mathbb{Z}} M_d$,*

$$
  \tau(M) \;:=\; \sum_{d \in \mathbb{Z}} (-1)^d \dim_R M_d \;\in\; R.
$$

*The :defterm[cemetery group]{#cemetery-group} of $H_n(R, q)$ is the
localising Serre subcategory*

$$
  \mathcal{Z}_n \;:=\; \ker \tau
  \;=\; \{\, M \in \mathcal{K}_n : \tau(M) = 0 \,\}.
$$

*Its objects are the homologically acyclic complexes — physically,
the decoupled or non-observable interaction channels.*

**Remark.**
*The cemetery group is **emergent**: it is defined as the kernel of
$\tau$ rather than postulated.  Concrete computational use appears
in [the cemetery-group emergence
conjecture](#conj:cemetery-group-emergence), which justifies using
$\mathcal{Z}_n$ as a homological filter (a "kill switch") on
crystal-graph traversals.*

<!-- FILE_START: crystal-graph-realization.md -->
**Conjecture (crystal-graph realisation of Hecke normal forms).** Let $K$ be a closed braid with [Hecke normal form](#def:hecke-normal-form) $T_w \in H_n(R, q)$. Under the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism between $H_n(R, q)$ and the cyclotomic quotient of the corresponding KLR algebra \cite{brundankleshchev2009}, the Gröbner–Shirshov canonical basis element $T_w$ corresponds to an indecomposable graded projective KLR module. This module is a highest-weight vector of the Kashiwara crystal $\mathrm{Cryst}(\mathfrak{sl}_n)$ \cite{kashiwara1991}: it satisfies $\tilde e_i \cdot T_w = 0$ for every Kashiwara raising operator $\tilde e_i$.

**Computational shortcut.** The GS reduction engine that computes $T_w$ from a braid word can be replaced by a directed traversal on the crystal graph. Starting from the input word and recursively applying $\tilde e_i$ operators, the procedure terminates at $T_w$. The relevant component of $\mathrm{Cryst}(\mathfrak{sl}_n)$ is finite because the cyclotomic quotient of the KLR algebra realising $H_n(R, q)$ is finite-dimensional. Termination is immediate; no Diamond-Lemma S-polynomial overlap analysis is required. The plactic-monoid bridge of Lecouvey \cite{lecouvey2007} certifies that the GS irreducible forms and crystal highest-weight vertices are in bijection.

**Status.** The literature establishes the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism and the GS$\leftrightarrow$crystal bijection for generic Hecke and KLR algebras. It remains conjectural that our specific GS reduction order ([Hecke normal form](#def:hecke-normal-form), shortlex on the Yang–Baxter generators) matches the canonical basis labels under Brundan–Kleshchev for the QOU ansatz. We verify this match empirically on the witnessed normal forms. A rigorous proof requires formalising the GS reduction order inside the KLR diagrammatic calculus.

We use this conjecture via the [confinement–grading correspondence](#conj:confinement-grading-correspondence) to upgrade the heuristic confinement filtration to a $\mathbb{Z}$-graded KLR shift. We also use it via [cemetery-group emergence](#conj:cemetery-group-emergence) to explain the GS engine's homological pruning. No new `sorry` is introduced in Lean: the conjecture replaces a class of brute-force S-polynomial bounds rather than an existing proof. A formalization architecture for the Lean side is given in [`docs/audits/2026-06-07-klr-gs-order-formalization-plan.md`](../../../docs/audits/2026-06-07-klr-gs-order-formalization-plan.md).

<!-- FILE_START: confinement-grading-correspondence.md -->
**Conjecture (confinement = KLR grading shift).**
*Under the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism \cite{brundankleshchev2009}, the [confinement filtration](#def:confinement-level) $J^\bullet(q)$ on $H_n(R, q)$ coincides with the filtration induced by the natural $\mathbb{Z}$-grading on the KLR algebra \cite{khovanovlauda2009}. A closed braid $K$ with normal form $T_w$ has confinement level $\ell(K, q)$ if and only if its KLR-module lift $\mathfrak{M}_K$ has top non-zero graded piece in degree $\ell(K, q)$:*

$$
  T_w \in J^\ell(q) \setminus J^{\ell+1}(q)
  \;\iff\;
  (\mathfrak{M}_K)_d = 0 \;\;\text{for}\;\; d > \ell.
$$

**Computational role.**
*We enforce the KLR grading bound before any crystal step. Given a target confinement level $\ell$, the engine restricts the crystal traversal of [crystal-graph realisation](#conj:crystal-graph-realization) to the subgraph spanned by modules of degree $\leq \ell$. Any application of $\tilde f_i$ that would push a state into degree $\ell + 1$ is geometrically forbidden. This provides a rigorous justification for the existing heuristic confinement cutoff and delivers a quantitative speed-up by pruning the crystal graph at depth $\ell$.*

**Conjectural status.**
*The KLR grading is unique up to isomorphism, and the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism intertwines it with the centre of $H_n(R, q)$. We conjecture that the filtration arising from the LP shadow-price ordering on knot-operator energies ([knot operator energy](#def:knot-operator-energy)) strictly agrees with the KLR grading filtration, rather than merely agreeing up to a re-indexing of strata. This is supported by all witnessed atom knots up to $k_{\max} \leq 7$.*

**Conjecture (:refterm[cemetery group]{#cemetery-group} as a homological filter).**
*Let $T_w \in H_n(R, q)$ be a Hecke normal form and let $\beta$ be
the corresponding crystal path produced by
[crystal-graph realisation](#conj:crystal-graph-realization).  If
the prefix $\beta_{\leq k}$ of $\beta$ lifts to a graded projective
KLR module that lies in the
[:refterm[cemetery group]{#cemetery-group}](#def:cemetery-group) $\mathcal{Z}_n = \ker \tau$,
then every extension of $\beta$ also lies in $\mathcal{Z}_n$, and
its image under the observation functor
[$\tau$](#def:observation) vanishes:*

$$
  M_{\beta_{\leq k}} \in \mathcal{Z}_n
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \tau\bigl(M_{\beta_{\leq k} \cdot w'}\bigr) = 0
  \quad \text{for every continuation } w'.
$$

**Algorithmic role (kill switch).**
*The Gröbner–Shirshov engine therefore uses $\mathcal{Z}_n$ as a
dynamic pruning filter.  At each crystal step, it tests whether the
current state lies in the kernel of $\tau$ (a finite-dimensional
linear-algebra check on the graded character).  If so, the branch
is dropped immediately, bypassing all downstream polynomial
cancellations.  This converts the historically expensive
"long chains of crossing-graded expansions that cancel
algebraically" into an $O(1)$ truncation step.*

**Relation to the spectral-sum cutoff.**
*Combined with [GS spectral-sum
finiteness](#conj:gs-spectral-sum-finite), this conjecture supplies
the **structural** reason for the cutoff: the residue strata above
the confinement level $\ell(K, q_0)$ correspond precisely to the
crystal extensions that fall into $\mathcal{Z}_n$, and so they
vanish under $\tau$ rather than via term-by-term cancellation.*

**Conjectural status.**
*The defining property of $\mathcal{Z}_n$ as the kernel of $\tau$
is true by construction.  What is conjectural is **monotonicity**
under crystal extensions — that once a path enters $\mathcal{Z}_n$
it cannot escape.  Monotonicity follows from the localising-Serre
property of $\mathcal{Z}_n$ inside $\mathcal{K}_n$ together with
the action of Kashiwara operators on graded projectives; both are
standard for KLR algebras
\cite{khovanovlauda2009, brundankleshchev2009}, and the residual
content is the QOU-specific instantiation.*

<!-- FILE_START: ontological-agnosticism.md -->
The [crystal-graph realisation](#conj:crystal-graph-realization) of Hecke normal forms and the [confinement–grading correspondence](#conj:confinement-grading-correspondence) provide a sharp computational mechanism. Every observable extracted by the Observation Frame $\tau$ is determined by its image at the **crystal limit** $q \to 0$ and transported back to the generic fibre [$\mathcal{S}_p$](#def:exceptional-divisor) along the flat $q$-connection.

## Mechanism

At the crystal limit $q \to 0$, the cyclotomic KLR module category $\mathcal{K}_n$ \cite{khovanovlauda2009, brundankleshchev2009} degenerates to its associated graded, the Kashiwara crystal $\mathrm{Cryst}(\mathfrak{sl}_n)$ \cite{kashiwara1991}. The crystal carries no quantum corrections; Kashiwara operators $\tilde{e}_i, \tilde{f}_i$ decorate its edges, and weights are integer-graded. The graded Euler characteristic $\tau(M) = \sum_d (-1)^d \dim_R M_d$, which defines the [cemetery group](#def:cemetery-group), remains invariant under flat transport from the special fibre at $q = 0$ to a generic fibre $\mathcal{S}_p$ at $q = q_0$:

$$
  \tau\bigl(\mathfrak{M}_K\bigr)\big|_{q = q_0}
  \;=\;
  \tau\bigl(\mathfrak{M}_K\bigr)\big|_{q \to 0},
$$

Thus, any Laurent polynomial $P_K^{(k)}(q)$ extracted at level $k$ from the crystal traversal lifts uniquely to the generic fibre via the $\mathbb{Z}$-grading of the [GB spectral sequence](#prop:gb-spectral-sequence).

## Consequence for the engine

The Gröbner–Shirshov reduction engine can thus operate entirely on the finite, discrete crystal graph at $q = 0$. No resolution of overlapping S-polynomials at the generic fibre is required. Crystal-limit values pull back to the physically relevant $q_0$ along the flat connection, and the cemetery filter of [cemetery-group emergence](#conj:cemetery-group-emergence) acts identically on both sides. This explains structurally why the engine can replace continuous polynomial term-rewriting with graph traversal, losing no information at the generic fibre.
