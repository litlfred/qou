<!-- FILE_START: dq-squared-elaboration-intro.md -->
We specialise the $d_q^2$ obstruction and the $q$-Lie bracket Jacobiator to a concrete manifold $M$. At $q = 1$, all deformed operators recover their classical counterparts.
<!-- FILE_END: dq-squared-elaboration-intro.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: dq-squared.md -->
The archimedean specialisation yields $d_q^2\alpha =  q^{-1}(1 - q^{-1})d\varTheta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha$. Here $d\varTheta$ operates as the symplectic form on $H$. $d_q^2 = 0$ if and only if $q = 1$ or $\alpha$ is Reeb-invariant.
<!-- FILE_END: dq-squared.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: q-jacobiator.md -->
The archimedean specialisation violates the Jacobi identity for horizontal vector fields when $q \neq 1$. The Jacobiator reads

$$
  \mathrm{Jac}_q(U,V,W) = -q^{-1}(1-q^{-1})\!\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}} d\varTheta(V,W)\mathcal{L}_\xi U.
$$

Since the Levi form $d\varTheta|_{H \times H}$ is non-degenerate, $(\mathfrak{X}(M), [\cdot,\cdot]_q)$ forms a Leibniz algebra.
<!-- FILE_END: q-jacobiator.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: classical-recovery.md -->
At $q = 1$, the deformed operators recover their classical forms: $d_1 = d$, $[\cdot,\cdot]_1 = [\cdot,\cdot]$, $A_1 = A$, and $\xi_1 = \xi$.
<!-- FILE_END: classical-recovery.md -->
