This chapter develops the computational engine that derives particle interactions, nuclear structure, and atomic construction from the categorical framework of Parts I and II. We deploy two principal tools: Gröbner--Shirshov reduction in the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_A(q)$ (which yields unique normal forms for nuclear braid words) and linear programming duality over the Frobenius Gram matrix (which selects confinement operators by minimizing categorical action).

First, the reduction engine dictates that every nucleus $\mathcal{N}(Z,N)$ forms a braid word in $B_A$ with a unique, path-independent Gröbner--Shirshov normal form in $H_A(q)$. Next, the linear programming framework recasts confinement operator selection as a linear program. Strong duality yields the Borromean link $\mathcal{B}_3$ as the unique optimum. From these two mechanisms, we derive nucleon structure, nuclear binding, magic numbers, beta decay via transfer matrices, and the Wedderburn mass formula for atomic masses.

These derivations remain symbolic in the substrate parameter $q$. Witness evaluations at $q_0$ provide numerical verification against experimental data. This chapter isolates the *how* of particle interactions from the *what* of particle definitions ([Chapter 9](#chap:brings-surface)) and the *observed* predictions ([Chapter 11](#chap:observations)). We develop molecular extensions of this machinery in [Chapter 12](#chap:molecular-construction).

<!-- FILE_START: nuclear-groebner-reduction.md -->
Every nucleus $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ corresponds to a braid word in the braid group $B_A$ ([braid group](#def:braid-group)), where $A = Z + N$. This braid word is not unique; different orderings of Hopf crossings give different link representatives. The **Gröbner--Shirshov reduction** in the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_A(q)$ produces a unique **normal form** representing the lowest-energy state.

**Convention.** We let $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ denote the **reduced** (Gröbner--Shirshov normal form) nuclear braid word unless explicitly stated otherwise.

**Proposition.**
Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$, where $q$ is the braiding eigenvalue ([eigenvalue extraction](#prop:braiding-eigenvalue-extraction)). This $q$ is a categorical invariant of $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$, not a free parameter. Let $R[B_A]$ be the group algebra of the braid group on $A$ strands, and let $I$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the Skein relation:

$$
  \sigma_i - \sigma_i^{-1}
  - (q - q^{-1})
   \in  I
  \qquad (i = 1, \ldots, A-1).
$$

The Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_A(q) = R[B_A] / I$ admits an admissible system ([admissible system](#unital-groebner-bases:def:admissible-system)) $(B, <)$ where:

- $B$ denotes positive braid monomials $\sigma_{i_1} \sigma_{i_2} \cdots \sigma_{i_k}$ for $i_j \in \{1, \ldots, A-1\}$.
- $<$ denotes the length-lexicographic ordering.

The Gröbner--Shirshov basis $G$ for $I$ with respect to $(B, <)$ consists of:

1. **Hecke relation**: $\sigma_i^2 - (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i - 1$ (eliminates all squares).
2. **Yang--Baxter relation**: $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i - \sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$ (braid relation, reduces to the length-lexicographically smaller form).
3. **Far commutativity**: $\sigma_i\sigma_j - \sigma_j\sigma_i$ for $|i - j| \geq 2$ (sorts distant generators).

The division algorithm ([division algorithm](#unital-groebner-bases:def:division-algorithm)) computes the remainder $r(\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{raw}}(Z, N), G)$, yielding the **unique normal form**:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(Z, N)
   := 
  r\bigl(\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{raw}}(Z, N), G\bigr)
   \in  H_A(q).
$$

By the main theorem of Gröbner--Shirshov basis theory ([Gröbner--Shirshov basis](#unital-groebner-bases:def:groebner-basis)), the remainder is path-independent, guaranteeing uniqueness.

**The nuclear braid word.**
The unreduced braid word for $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ in $B_A$ is:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(Z, N)
  = 
  \prod_{1 \leq i < j \leq A}
  \sigma_{i}^{\epsilon(i, j)}
$$

where the crossing sign $\epsilon(i, j)$ depends on the nucleon types at positions $i$ and $j$:

$$
  \epsilon(i, j) = 
  \begin{cases}
    +1 & \text{if } i, j \text{ both protons} \\[3pt]
    -1 & \text{if } i, j \text{ both neutrons} \\[3pt]
    0  & \text{if mixed (resolved as }
         \tfrac{1}{2}(\sigma_i + \sigma_i^{-1})\text{)}
  \end{cases}
$$

The Hecke relation eliminates inverses in $H_A(q)$: $\sigma_i^{-1} = \sigma_i - (q - q^{-1})$. The mixed crossing $\tfrac{1}{2}(\sigma_i + \sigma_i^{-1})$ evaluates to $\sigma_i - \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})$. This yields $2^{|{\mathrm{mixed}}|}$ terms before reduction, where $|{\mathrm{mixed}}| = Z \cdot N$. Gröbner--Shirshov reduction collapses this exponential expansion into the normal form.

**Computational results.**
For small nuclei (exact computation via `nuclear-braid-reduction.py`):

| Nucleus | $A$ | Unreduced | Reduced | Max length | $\Delta\mathrm{Weight}$ |
|---------|-----|-----------|---------|------------|------------------------|
| Deuteron | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | $-0.174$ |
| ${}^{3}\mathrm{H}$ | 3 | 6 | 4 | 2 | $-0.344$ |
| ${}^{3}\mathrm{He}$ | 3 | 4 | 4 | 2 | $-0.307$ |
| ${}^{4}\mathrm{He}$ | 4 | 18 | 8 | 3 | $-0.605$ |
| ${}^{6}\mathrm{Li}$ | 6 | 384 | 32 | 5 | $-2.763$ |

The $\Delta\mathrm{Weight}$ column shows the change in total Hecke weight under beta decay $\mathcal{N}(Z, N) \to \mathcal{N}(Z+1, N-1)$. This change is always **negative**: the daughter has lower Hecke weight, confirming that beta decay releases energy.

**Normal form of ${}^{3}\mathrm{H}$.**
The reduced braid word for tritium is:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(1, 2)
  = 
  -0.145
  +  (-0.015)\sigma_0
  +  1.539\sigma_1
  +  0.162\sigma_0\sigma_1.
$$

The dominant term is $+1.539\sigma_1$. This represents the strong-force Hopf crossing between the two neutrons, amplified by repeated Hecke reduction of higher-order braid words.

**Connection to half-life.**
The beta-decay half-life depends on the difference between parent and daughter normal forms:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(Z, N)
  - \mathcal{N}(Z+1, N-1)
  = 
  \sum_{w \in B} \Delta c_w \cdot w
$$

where $\Delta c_w$ denotes the coefficient differences. The Markov trace of this difference determines the transition amplitude:

$$
  |\mathcal{M}_q|^2
   \propto 
  \left|
    \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Markov}}\!\left(
      \mathcal{N}(Z, N)
      - \mathcal{N}(Z+1, N-1)
    \right)
  \right|^2.
$$

Fermi's golden rule relates this amplitude to the half-life. For ${}^{3}\mathrm{H} \to {}^{3}\mathrm{He}$, the large $\Delta\mathrm{Weight} = -0.344$ indicates maximal overlap (mirror transition), yielding a short half-life. For ${}^{14}\mathrm{C} \to {}^{14}\mathrm{N}$, the normal forms differ by many more terms due to the $N = 8$ shell structure, producing a smaller Markov trace and longer half-life.

**Scaling.**
The number of reduced terms grows polynomially in $A$. The Gröbner--Shirshov basis eliminates all squares and sorts commuting generators. The maximum word length in the normal form is $A - 1$ (the longest reduced word in $H_A$ is $\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \cdots \sigma_{A-1}$). For ${}^{14}\mathrm{C}$ ($A = 14$), the normal form contains at most $\binom{13}{k}$ terms of length $k$, totalling at most $2^{13} = 8192$ terms. This ensures tractability for numerical evaluation via the Markov trace.

<!-- FILE_START: nuclear-reduction-algorithm.md -->
We compute the Gröbner--Shirshov normal form of a nuclear braid word via the division algorithm of \cite{leitnerpawloski2004}, specialized to the Iwahori--Hecke algebra.

**Input:** Mass number $A = Z + N$, proton number $Z$, neutron number $N$. The Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_A(q) = R[B_A] / I$ over $R = \mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$. The admissible system ([admissible system](#unital-groebner-bases:def:admissible-system)) $(B, <)$, where $B$ contains positive braid monomials and $<$ denotes the length-lexicographic order. The Gröbner--Shirshov basis $G = \{\sigma_i^2 - (q-q^{-1})\sigma_i - 1,\; \sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i - \sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1},\; \sigma_i\sigma_j - \sigma_j\sigma_i\}$ for $|i-j| \geq 2$.

**Output:** The unique normal form $\mathcal{N}(Z, N) \in H_A(q)$ and its Markov trace $\mathrm{tr}_M(\mathcal{N}(Z, N))$.

1. Set $\mathcal{N}_{\leq 1} := \mathfrak{N}_1$ (the normal form of the first nucleon in $H_1(q)$: proton $\mathfrak{p}$ or neutron $\mathfrak{n}$).
2. **For** $k = 1, \ldots, A-1$:
   - Determine the crossing type $C(\mathfrak{N}_k, \mathfrak{N}_{k+1}; \sigma_k)$:

     $$
       C = \begin{cases}
         \sigma_k & \text{if both protons ($pp$)} \\[3pt]
         \sigma_k^{-1} = \sigma_k - (q - q^{-1})
           & \text{if both neutrons ($nn$)} \\[3pt]
         \sigma_k - \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})
           & \text{if mixed ($pn$ or $np$)}
       \end{cases}
     $$

   - Form the product $f_k := \mathcal{N}_{\leq k} \cdot C \cdot \mathfrak{N}_{k+1}$ in $H_{k+1}(q)$.
   - Apply the division algorithm ([division algorithm](#unital-groebner-bases:def:division-algorithm)): set $i := 0$, $f_0 := f_k$.
   - **While** $f_i \neq 0$:
     - Set $i := i + 1$.
     - **If** no $g \in G$ satisfies $\mathrm{LT}(g) \mid f_{i-1}$: set $r_i := \mathrm{LT}(f_{i-1})$ and $f_i := f_{i-1} - r_i$.
     - **Else**: choose $g \in G$ satisfying $\mathrm{LT}(g) \mid f_{i-1}$. Choose $\lambda \in R^*$ and $u, v \in B$ such that $\lambda\,\mathrm{LT}(u \cdot g \cdot v) = \mathrm{LT}(f_{i-1})$. Set $f_i := f_{i-1} - \lambda\,u \cdot g \cdot v$ and $r_i := 0$.
   - Set $\mathcal{N}_{\leq k+1} := \sum_i r_i$.
3. Return $\mathcal{N}(Z, N) := \mathcal{N}_{\leq A}$ and $\mathrm{tr}_M(\mathcal{N}(Z, N))$.

**Correctness.** The output remains independent of the choice of $g \in G$ at each step because $G$ is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis ([main theorem](#unital-groebner-bases:thm:main-theorem)).

**Complexity.** This inductive strategy (reducing after each multiplication) requires $O(A^2)$ multiplications and $O(A^3)$ Hecke reduction steps ([inductive reduction](#prop:inductive-gb-reduction)). Multiplying all crossings before reduction would generate $O(2^{Z \cdot N})$ terms.

<!-- FILE_START: groebner-shirshov-energy.md -->
The Gröbner--Shirshov basis theory for the Iwahori--Hecke algebra
$H_A(q)$ gives a stronger result than uniqueness of normal
forms: the **energy** of the nuclear braid word is a topological
invariant, computed by the division algorithm with no path
dependence.

**Theorem.**
Let $G = \{\sigma_i^2 - (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i - 1, \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i - \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}, \sigma_i \sigma_j - \sigma_j \sigma_i\}$ be the Gröbner--Shirshov basis
for $H_A(q)$
([nuclear Gröbner--Shirshov reduction](#prop:nuclear-groebner-reduction)).
Define the **energy functional** on $H_A(q)$ by

$$
  E(\xi) =  \sum_{w \in B} |c_w(q)| \cdot \ell(w)
$$

where $\xi = \sum_w c_w(q) \cdot w$ is the normal form
$\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ and $\ell(w)$ is the word length.
Then:

1. **Only Hecke reductions change energy.**
   The Yang--Baxter relation $\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}$ and the far
   commutativity $\sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i$
   preserve all coefficients and word lengths.  The Hecke
   relation $\sigma_i^2 = (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i + 1$ is the
   unique energy-changing generator in $G$.

2. **Energy is path-independent.**
   The total energy change from the unreduced braid word
   $\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{raw}}(Z, N)$ to the normal form
   $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ is independent of the order
   in which the Gröbner--Shirshov basis elements are applied.  This
   follows from the confluence (Bergman diamond lemma) of the
   Gröbner--Shirshov basis: all reduction paths lead to the
   same normal form, and the energy is a function of the
   normal form alone.

3. **Each Hecke step has a definite energy.**
   When $\sigma_i^2$ is replaced by $(q - q^{-1})\sigma_i + 1$,
   the energy change at that step is

   $$
     \Delta E_{\mathrm{step}}
     = 
     |c| \cdot \bigl(|q - q^{-1}| + 1 - 1\bigr)
     = 
     |c| \cdot |q - q^{-1}|
   $$

   where $c$ is the coefficient of the $\sigma_i^2$ term.  Every
   Hecke step **increases** the total weight by $|c| \cdot (q - q^{-1})$
   (since $q > 1$ ensures $q - q^{-1} > 0$).

**Archimedean specialisation (§7c).** The energy $|c|\,(q - q^{-1})$ and its positivity for $q > 1$ are stated over $\mathbb{R}$ using the real ordering, so this block sits on the archimedean side of the substrate wall.

**Remark (physical interpretation).**
The energy functional $E(\mathcal{N}(Z, N))$ gives the Skein binding energy of the nucleus, expressed in the Gröbner--Shirshov normal form. Each Hecke step $\sigma_i^2 \to (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i + 1$ resolves a double crossing into a weighted single crossing plus a resolved (identity) term:

$$
  \underset{\text{double crossing}}{\sigma_i^2}
   \longrightarrow 
  \underset{\text{weighted single}}{(q - q^{-1})\sigma_i}
  + 
  \underset{\text{resolved}}{1}
$$

The factor $(q - q^{-1})$ acts as the crossing asymmetry. This is the same quantity that determines the crossing asymmetry $(q - q^{-1})$ from the Hecke relation. The Hecke reduction algebraically expresses the physical process of resolving a double crossing into its electromagnetic and strong components.

<!-- FILE_START: nucleon-ordering-factorization.md -->
The Wedderburn--Artin decomposition of $H_3(q)$ into irreducible representations, combined with the jet-descended confinement of hadrons, factorizes the nuclear Markov trace. This factorization renders the ground-state ordering search trivial in two of the three channels.

## Statement

**Proposition.**
*Let $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ be a nuclear braid in $H_A(q)$ with nucleon ordering $\pi = (t_1, \ldots, t_A)$ where $t_k \in \{\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{n}\}$. The Markov trace decomposes as:*

$$
  \mathrm{tr}_M(\mathcal{N})
  =
  W_{\mathrm{sym}} \cdot S(\pi)
  + W_{\mathrm{std}} \cdot (z \cdot a_\pi + b_\pi)
  + W_{\mathrm{alt}} \cdot A(\pi)
$$

*where:*

**(i) Ordering independence of scalar channels.**
*The symmetric product $S(\pi) = \prod_k s_k$ and alternating product $A(\pi) = \prod_k a_k$ are products of commutative scalars. Because scalar multiplication commutes, $S$ and $A$ remain independent of the nucleon ordering $\pi$:*

$$
  S(\pi) = S(\pi') = \prod_{k} (q + d_k),
  \qquad
  A(\pi) = A(\pi') = \prod_{k} (-q^{-1} + d_k)
$$

*for any two orderings $\pi, \pi'$ with the same nucleon composition $(Z, N)$.*

**(ii) Standard-channel factorization.**
*The standard-channel state $(a_\pi, b_\pi) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ emerges from a $2 \times 2$ matrix product:*

$$
  \begin{pmatrix} a_\pi \\ b_\pi \end{pmatrix}
  =
  \left(\prod_{k=1}^{K} \sigma_k^{\mathrm{std}}\right)
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \sigma_k^{\mathrm{std}}(c, d)
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    c(q - q^{-1}) + d & c \\
    c & d
  \end{pmatrix}
$$

*where $K$ totals the number of crossings (nucleon-internal $+$ inter-nucleon $+$ electron).*

**(iii) Jet-descended nucleon blocks.**
*The confinement theorem ([confinement-topological](#prop:confinement-topological)) establishes the proton and neutron as topologically stable normal forms in $H_3(q)$. The ordering-jet compatibility theorem ([ordering-jet-compatibility](#prop:ordering-jet-compatibility)) ensures the admissible ordering propagates through jet levels. Thus, the intra-nucleon crossings ($\mathcal{B}_3$ $+$ quark pairs $+$ gluon dressing) yield fixed $2 \times 2$ transfer matrices:*

$$
  T_\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{std}}
  =
  \prod_{i=1}^{n_p}
  \sigma_i^{\mathrm{std}}(c_i, d_i)
  \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2},
  \qquad
  T_\mathfrak{n}^{\mathrm{std}}
  =
  \prod_{j=1}^{n_n}
  \sigma_j^{\mathrm{std}}(c_j, d_j)
  \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}
$$

*We precompute these once and reuse them for every nucleus.*

**(iv) Inter-nucleon crossings (transient).**
*In the jet-stratified language ([jet-stratified-reduction](#unital-groebner-bases:prop:jet-stratified-reduction)), the nucleon blocks are **descended** (resolved at the quark level). The inter-nucleon crossings are **transient** (new at the nuclear level). Each carries a $2 \times 2$ matrix dictated by the nucleon pair type:*

$$
  T_{\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{std}}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix} q - q^{-1} & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
  \quad
  T_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}}^{\mathrm{std}}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & -(q - q^{-1}) \end{pmatrix},
  \quad
  T_{\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{n}}^{\mathrm{std}}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1}) & 1 \\
    1 & -\tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})
  \end{pmatrix}
$$

## Computational consequence

The ground-state ordering search for $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ tests $\binom{A}{Z}$ nucleon permutations. For each permutation:

| Component | Cost (original) | Cost (factorized) |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------|
| Symmetric channel | $O(K)$ scalar mults | **Precomputed** (ordering-independent) |
| Alternating channel | $O(K)$ scalar mults | **Precomputed** (ordering-independent) |
| Standard channel | $O(K)$ $\times$ 6 ops per crossing | $O(A^2)$ $\times$ 4 ops per $2 \times 2$ mult |
| Nucleon-internal | $O(n_p A)$ crossings ($n_p \approx 11$) | **Precomputed** ($T_\mathfrak{p}, T_\mathfrak{n}$ cached) |

**Effective speedup factors:**

1. **Channel elimination**: We compute symmetric and alternating products once per $(Z, N)$, not per ordering. This saves $2/3$ of the per-crossing work.
2. **Nucleon precomputation**: Each nucleon contributes $n_p$ crossings to the chain. Precomputing $T_\mathfrak{p}$ and $T_\mathfrak{n}$ collapses these to a single $2 \times 2$ matrix per nucleon per generator.
3. **$2 \times 2$ vs $6 \times 6$**: The standard-channel matrix multiplication requires $O(4)$ rather than $O(36)$ operations per crossing.

Combined, the ordering search cost drops from $\binom{A}{Z} \times O(A^2) \times O(36)$ to $\binom{A}{Z} \times O(A^2) \times O(4)$. This provides a $\sim 9 \times$ reduction per ordering and eliminates redundant channel computations.

## Compact nucleon notation

The factorization suggests a natural **nucleon-level alphabet**:

$$
  \mathfrak{p}
  \;\longleftrightarrow\;
  (T_\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{std}},\; s_\mathfrak{p},\; a_\mathfrak{p})
  \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2} \times \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}
$$

$$
  \mathfrak{n}
  \;\longleftrightarrow\;
  (T_\mathfrak{n}^{\mathrm{std}},\; s_\mathfrak{n},\; a_\mathfrak{n})
  \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2} \times \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}
$$

A nucleus $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ with ordering $\pi$ is written:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(\pi)
  =
  \mathfrak{t}_1 \cdot \mathfrak{t}_2 \cdots \mathfrak{t}_A
$$

Here, each $\mathfrak{t}_k \in \{\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{n}\}$ and the multiplication rule encodes the inter-nucleon crossings. Evaluating the $2 \times 2$ product and combining it with the precomputed scalar channels recovers the full $H_3(q)$ normal form.

This constitutes the **hadronic Hecke alphabet**. Six normal-form coefficients per nucleon collapse to a $2 \times 2$ matrix (4 numbers) in the standard channel, plus two precomputed scalars. For ${}^{56}\mathrm{Fe}$, the $6 \times 56 = 336$ normal-form coefficients reduce to $56 \times (4 + 2) = 336$ hadronic parameters. While this yields no size reduction, the multiplication executes $9\times$ faster and $2/3$ of the per-ordering work vanishes.

   $$

   Since $E_{\mathrm{box}}$ is structurally disjoint from the unknot mass scale $m_\nu = \mu_0(q - 1)$, the decay channel is strongly suppressed.

The canonical trace decomposes as a sum of generator volumes, one for each braid generator $\sigma_i$ in $B_A$. A transfer matrix product computes each generator's volume. These terms organize as a jet expansion in the nucleon count. Each jet order corresponds to a color degree.

**Proposition.**
Let $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ be the nuclear braid word in $H_A(q)$ ([nuclear Gröbner--Shirshov reduction](#prop:nuclear-groebner-reduction)). At each generator $\sigma_i$ ($i = 0, \ldots, A-2$), nucleon $i$ crosses with nucleons $j = i+1, \ldots, A-1$. These $k_i = A - i - 1$ crossings act on $\sigma_i$ and reduce via the Hecke relation to a linear combination:

$$
  \prod_{j=i+1}^{A-1} C_{ij}(\sigma_i)
  = 
  a_i  \sigma_i + b_i
$$

where each crossing factor $C_{ij}(\sigma_i) = c_j \sigma_i + d_j$ has coefficients determined by the pair type:

$$
  (c_j, d_j) = 
  \begin{cases}
    (1, 0) & \text{if } i, j \text{ both protons (}\sigma\text{)} \\[3pt]
    (1, -(q - q^{-1})) & \text{if } i, j \text{ both neutrons (}\sigma^{-1}\text{)} \\[3pt]
    (1, -\tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1}))
      & \text{if mixed (}\tfrac{1}{2}(\sigma + \sigma^{-1})\text{)}
  \end{cases}
$$

The coefficients $(a_i, b_i)$ satisfy a recurrence. We start from $(a_0, b_0) = (0, 1)$ (the identity), multiply $(a_k \sigma + b_k)(c_j \sigma + d_j)$, and apply $T^2 = (q - q^{-1})T + 1$:

$$
  a_{k+1} = a_k c_j (q - q^{-1}) + a_k d_j + b_k c_j,
  \qquad
  b_{k+1} = a_k c_j + b_k d_j.
$$

This defines a $2 \times 2$ transfer matrix $T_j$ at each crossing.

## Jet expansion in the nucleon count

The transfer matrix product at generator $\sigma_i$:

$$
  M_i =  \prod_{j=i+1}^{A-1} T_j
$$

is a product of $k_i = A - i - 1$ matrices. This product admits a jet expansion. This is a Taylor series in the number of crossings, where each order corresponds to a color degree:

$$
  M_i =  \mathbb{1}
  +  \underbrace{\sum_{j} T_j^{(1)}}_{\text{1-jet: pair interactions}}
  +  \underbrace{\sum_{j < k} T_j^{(1)} T_k^{(1)}}_{\text{2-jet: triple correlations}}
  +  \cdots
  +  \underbrace{\prod_{j} T_j^{(1)}}_{\text{$k_i$-jet: full product}}
$$

where $T_j = \mathbb{1} + T_j^{(1)}$, with $T_j^{(1)}$ measuring the deviation from the identity.

The color degree at generator $\sigma_i$ equals $k_i = A - i - 1$.

| Jet order $r$ | Color degree | Physical content | Multiplicity |
|:---:|:---:|---|:---:|
| $0$ | $0$ | Free nucleon (identity) | $1$ |
| $1$ | $1$ | Pair interactions ($i$-$j$ crossings) | $k_i$ |
| $2$ | $2$ | Triple correlations ($i$-$j$-$k$) | $\binom{k_i}{2}$ |
| $r$ | $r$ | $(r+1)$-body correlations | $\binom{k_i}{r}$ |
| $k_i$ | $k_i$ | Full product (exact) | $1$ |

The total number of terms across all jets and all generators is:

$$
  \sum_{i=0}^{A-2} \sum_{r=0}^{k_i} \binom{k_i}{r}
  = 
  \sum_{i=0}^{A-2} 2^{k_i}
$$

This sum scales exponentially. However, the transfer matrix product computes the algebraic trace in exactly $k_i$ steps (one per crossing). The total step count across all generators is:

$$
  \sum_{i=0}^{A-2} k_i
  = 
  \sum_{i=0}^{A-2} (A - i - 1)
  = 
  \frac{A(A-1)}{2}
  = 
  \binom{A}{2}
$$

The transfer matrix product requires exactly $\binom{A}{2}$ algebraic steps. This matches the number of nucleon pairs.

## Beta decay as the 1-jet variation

Single beta decay ($\beta^{\pm}$) changes one nucleon type. The trace variation $\delta E$ represents the first jet (first variation) of the volume functional in the nucleon-type variable $\tau_p \in \{\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{n}\}$:

$$
  \delta E =  \sum_{i=0}^{p} (V_i^{\mathrm{dau}} - V_i^{\mathrm{par}})
$$

We sum only over generators affected by the converting nucleon at position $p$. Generators $i > p$ remain unaffected and cancel exactly. This yields the 1-jet of $E$ in the fibre direction: a single crossing change at one nucleon.

## Double beta decay as the 2-jet variation

Double beta decay ($2\beta$) changes two nucleons simultaneously. If nucleons at positions $p_1$ and $p_2$ (with $p_1 < p_2$) both convert $\mathfrak{n} \to \mathfrak{p}$, the trace variation includes a cross-term:

$$
  \delta^2 E
  = 
  \underbrace{\delta E_{p_1} + \delta E_{p_2}}_{\text{sum of single variations}}
  + 
  \underbrace{\Delta_{p_1 p_2}}_{\text{2-jet cross-term}}
$$

The cross-term $\Delta_{p_1 p_2}$ arises because at generators $\sigma_i$ with $i \leq p_1$, both converting nucleons' crossings change. The transfer matrix at these generators encounters two modified crossing factors. The product $T_{p_1}^{(1)} T_{p_2}^{(1)}$ contributes a new term that cannot be reduced to the sum of individual variations.

For generator $\sigma_i$ with $i \leq p_1$, the transfer matrix product changes at positions $j = p_1$ and $j = p_2$. We write $T_j = T_j^{(\mathrm{par})} + \delta T_j$:

$$
  M_i^{(2\beta)}
  = 
  M_i^{(\mathrm{par})}
  + 
  \underbrace{\delta T_{p_1} \cdot (\text{rest})
    + (\text{rest}) \cdot \delta T_{p_2}}_{\text{1-jet terms (linear)}}
  + 
  \underbrace{\delta T_{p_1} \cdot (\text{between}) \cdot \delta T_{p_2}}_{\text{2-jet cross-term}}
$$

The 2-jet cross-term encodes the correlation between the two converting nucleons. All nucleons between positions $p_1$ and $p_2$ mediate this correlation. This term provides the nuclear matrix element $M^{(2\nu)}$ of double beta decay.

For neutrinoless double beta decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$), the 2-jet cross-term adopts a different structure. The two $L_0^{-}$ smoothings remain coupled. The two emitted electrons share a single neutrino propagator. In the braid framework, this corresponds to a single unknot $T_{1,0}$ threading both crossing changes.

## The jet bundle structure

The generator volumes $\{V_i\}_{i=0}^{A-2}$ form a section of a jet bundle over the nuclear configuration space. The base is the space of nucleon-type assignments $\tau : \{0, \ldots, A-1\} \to \{\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{n}\}$. The fibre at each assignment is the vector of generator volumes $(V_0, \ldots, V_{A-2})$.

| Object | Jet bundle interpretation |
|--------|-------------------------|
| Nucleon-type assignment $\tau$ | Point in the base |
| Generator volumes $(V_0, \ldots, V_{A-2})$ | Section of the fibre |
| Single beta decay $\delta\tau_{p}$ | 1-jet (first derivative) |
| Double beta decay $\delta\tau_{p_1} \delta\tau_{p_2}$ | 2-jet (second derivative) |
| $r$-fold decay | $r$-jet ($r$-th derivative) |
| Full transfer matrix product | $\infty$-jet (exact) |

The color degree of the $r$-jet at generator $\sigma_i$ equals $\min(r, k_i)$. The number of crossings at that generator bounds this degree.

## Generator volume profile

The Markov-traced volume at generator $\sigma_i$ is:

$$
  \hat{V}_i =  z \cdot a_i + b_i
$$

where $z = 1/(q^{1/2} + q^{-1/2})$ provides the Markov parameter.
The canonical trace decomposes as:

$$
  \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\bigl(\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{N}) \cdot \overline{\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{N})}\bigr) =
  \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{A-2} \ln\bigl(\hat{V}_i \cdot \overline{\hat{V}_i}\bigr)
$$

Exactly $k_i$ transfer matrix steps determine each term $\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(\hat{V}_i \cdot \overline{\hat{V}_i})$.

## The volume of the ideal

The Gröbner--Shirshov ideal $I$ at generator $\sigma_i$ absorbs the difference between the unreduced weight and the reduced weight. The ideal volume at generator $i$ is:

$$
  V_i^I =  k_i - V_i
$$

where $k_i = A - i - 1$ counts the number of crossings. This represents the trace contribution removed by the Hecke relation (the resolved crossings). The total ideal volume $V^I = \sum_i V_i^I = \binom{A}{2} - E$ measures the portion of the naive crossing count absorbed by double-crossing resolutions.

## Connection to the Reeb decomposition

The color degree at each generator corresponds to the vertical degree $\deg\alpha_V$ of the Reeb flow:

- Generator $\sigma_0$: color degree $A - 1$ (sees all other nucleons). This forms the lowest Reeb stratum.
- Generator $\sigma_{A-2}$: color degree $1$ (sees one nucleon). This forms the highest Reeb stratum.

The jet expansion at each generator gives the Taylor series of the transfer matrix product along the Reeb flow direction. Each additional crossing adds one color degree. This corresponds to one more nucleon entering the interaction region.

The generator volume decomposition provides the discrete analogue of the jet bundle of the canonical action $\mathfrak{S}_q$ over the nuclear configuration space. The 1-jet captures single beta decay changes. The 2-jet captures double beta decay changes. The full $\infty$-jet recovers the exact algebraic trace. The same $\binom{A}{2}$ transfer matrix steps compute all of these.
