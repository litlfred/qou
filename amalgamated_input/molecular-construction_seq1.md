<!-- FILE_START: molecular-assembly-projector-intro.md -->
A molecule is a composite braid: $k$ sub-units (atoms, or smaller
fragments) assembled into a single word in $H_N(q)$, where $N$ is the
total strand count.  Before specialising to any one molecule, we record
the *general* algebraic structure that the assembly forces — structure
that does not depend on knowing the chemistry in advance.

The $k$ sub-units determine a **strand-partition** $\lambda =
(n_1,\dots,n_k)$ with $\sum_i n_i = N$, and the assembly factors through
the **parabolic (Young) subalgebra** $H_\lambda(q) = H_{n_1}(q) \otimes
\cdots \otimes H_{n_k}(q) \hookrightarrow H_N(q)$.  Every such
factorisation carries canonical **assembly projectors** onto the
Wedderburn blocks of $H_N(q)$; projection onto the *symmetric* block is
delocalisation.

Three things must be kept strictly distinct — conflating them is the
"post-justification" trap:

1. **Existence** of the projector is pure algebra — true for every
   $\lambda$, carrying no energy claim.
2. **Optimality** (which block the molecule actually occupies) is the
   *physics*, selected by the binding LP/SDP.
3. **Energetics**: the binding energy is the trace-weight *gap* between
   the selected block and the next configuration.

Benzene is recovered as the special case $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ (three
acetylene sub-units on six carbons), but the definitions below are
stated for arbitrary $\lambda$, and the same machinery applies to
$\mathrm{CH_4}$, $\mathrm{CO_2}$, and the other molecules of this
chapter.

<!-- FILE_START: molecular-strand-partition.md -->
**Definition.**
*Let a molecule be assembled from $k$ sub-units carrying strand counts
$n_1,\dots,n_k$.  The **molecular strand-partition** is the composition*

$$
  \lambda = (n_1,\dots,n_k), \qquad
  |\lambda| = \sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i = N,
$$

*which records how the $N$ strands of the composite braid in $H_N(q)$ are
distributed among the sub-units.  We write $\ell(\lambda) = k$ for the
number of sub-units.*

The strand-partition is the only datum the assembly requires: it is
fixed by *what is being joined to what*, not by any chemical
foreknowledge.  For three acetylene units (each contributing its two
carbons) the carbon strand-partition is $\lambda = (2,2,2)$, with
$N = 6$ and $k = 3$.  It is an ordered composition rather than an
unordered partition because the sub-units are individuated (sub-unit $1$,
sub-unit $2$, $\ldots$); reordering them is a separate symmetry, discussed with
the [assembly projector](#def:molecular-assembly-projector).
<!-- FILE_END: molecular-strand-partition.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: parabolic-hecke-subalgebra.md -->
**Definition.**
*Given a [strand-partition](#def:molecular-strand-partition)
$\lambda = (n_1,\dots,n_k)$ with $\sum_i n_i = N$, the **parabolic
(Young) Hecke subalgebra** is the tensor product*

$$
  H_\lambda(q) \;=\; H_{n_1}(q) \otimes \cdots \otimes H_{n_k}(q)
  \;\hookrightarrow\; H_N(q),
$$

*generated by the Hecke generators $\sigma_j$ that act **within** a single
sub-unit block.  The cross-block generators — those braiding strands of
distinct sub-units — are omitted; they are precisely the crossings
created when the sub-units **bond**.*

Equivalently $H_\lambda(q)$ is the $q$-deformation of the Young subgroup
$S_\lambda = S_{n_1} \times \cdots \times S_{n_k} \subset S_N$ sitting
inside $H_N(q)$ ([the Iwahori--Hecke
algebra](#rem:hecke-algebra-from-braiding)).  Its intra-unit generator
count is

$$
  \sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_i - 1) \;=\; N - k,
$$

consequently the **bonding** degrees of freedom number $(N-1) - (N-k) = k-1$ — one
per join between sub-units.  For benzene's $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ this is
$N - k = 3$ intra-unit generators and $k - 1 = 2$ bonding generators
linking the three units into a ring (the third join closes the ring).
<!-- FILE_END: parabolic-hecke-subalgebra.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: molecular-assembly-projector.md -->
**Definition.**
*Let $\lambda$ be a [strand-partition](#def:molecular-strand-partition)
of $N$ and $\mu \vdash N$ a Wedderburn block of $H_N(q)$.  The **assembly
projector** $e_{\lambda,\mu} \in H_N(q)$ is the canonical idempotent
($e_{\lambda,\mu}^2 = e_{\lambda,\mu}$) projecting the composite's module
— built up over the [parabolic subalgebra](#def:parabolic-hecke-subalgebra)
$H_\lambda(q)$ — onto the irreducible block $\mu$.*

It is the $q$-Young symmetriser / Wenzl projector for the pair
$(\lambda,\mu)$, the $q$-deformation of the classical Young symmetriser
([$H_3(q)$ idempotents](#rem:h3-hecke-idempotents) give the explicit
$N=3$ instances).  The distinguished case $\mu = (N)$ — projection onto
the one-row **symmetric** block — is **delocalisation**: the composite's
shared content is spread symmetrically across all $N$ strands rather than
localised in pairwise sub-blocks.

For benzene, $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ projects the six $\pi$-electrons onto the
symmetric block — the categorical content of the aromatic ring, realised
geometrically as its $T(2,6)$ threading link.  Reordering the three
sub-units (the $S_3$ permuting the acetylene units inside the $S_6$ of
the carbon ring) leaves $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ invariant; that invariance is
the algebraic expression of the ring's cyclic symmetry.
<!-- FILE_END: molecular-assembly-projector.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: assembly-projector-exists.md -->
**Proposition.**
*For every [strand-partition](#def:molecular-strand-partition) $\lambda$
of $N$ there exists a symmetric-block
[assembly projector](#def:molecular-assembly-projector) $e_{\lambda,(N)}
\in H_N(q)$ — the $q$-Young symmetriser onto the one-row block
$\mu = (N)$ — and it is idempotent, $e_{\lambda,(N)}^2 = e_{\lambda,(N)}$,
for generic $q$.*

This is the $q$-deformation of the classical Young symmetriser; the
idempotent is Wenzl's projector and exists for all $N$ at generic $q$
\cite{wenzl1988,kassel1995}.
<!-- FILE_END: assembly-projector-exists.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: assembly-projector-scope.md -->
**What this proposition does *not* assert.** Existence is pure algebra:
it holds for *every* $\lambda$ — bonded or not, stable or not — and says
**nothing** about energy or stability.  A molecule is stable only when
the binding LP/SDP *selects* this symmetric block as its optimum, and the
binding energy is then the trace-weight **gap** to the next admissible
configuration.  Existence is therefore *necessary* (one needs the
operator in order to project onto the block) but not *sufficient*; the
variational claim (the LP selects $\mu=(N)$) and the energetic claim (the
gap sets the binding energy) are recorded separately, so that the free
algebraic fact is never conflated with the earned physical one.  In
particular, the high stability of an aromatic ring follows from the
*irreducibility* of $e_{\lambda,(N)}$ over all $N$ strands — the ring
cannot be opened one bond at a time — not from the projector's bare
existence.
<!-- FILE_END: assembly-projector-scope.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: benzene-symmetric-projector.md -->
*Benzene is the special case $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ of the
[assembly projector](#def:molecular-assembly-projector): three acetylene
sub-units, each contributing two carbons, give six carbon strands, and
$e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ projects the six $\pi$-electrons onto the symmetric
Wedderburn block $\mu = (6)$.*

Projection onto $\mu = (6)$ is the categorical content of
**aromaticity**: the shared $\pi$-content is spread symmetrically over all
six carbons rather than localised in three alternating double bonds (the
two Kekulé structures are the symmetry-broken, non-projected
configurations).  Geometrically, $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ realises the
[$T(2,6)$ threading link](#def:threading-link) that winds through all six
ring atoms at once.

The partition $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ is *not* an input tuned to benzene: it
is forced by "three two-carbon units."  The same construction applied to
$\mathrm{CH_4}$ or $\mathrm{CO}$ yields their (non-aromatic) assembly
projectors with no symmetric ring — the symmetric block is selected by
the binding LP only when the geometry supports delocalisation.  That
*selection*, not the existence of $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$, is what makes
benzene special.
<!-- FILE_END: benzene-symmetric-projector.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: aromaticity-as-symmetric-projection.md -->
The [existence proposition](#prop:assembly-projector-exists) is
deliberately silent about energy.  Stability is a *separate*, three-step
chain, and keeping the steps distinct is what makes the aromatic-stability
claim falsifiable rather than circular.

1. **Existence** (algebra).  For any $\lambda$ the symmetric-block
   projector $e_{\lambda,(N)}$ exists — true of every molecule, stable or
   not.
2. **Optimality** (the physics).  Among admissible configurations the
   binding LP/SDP *selects* the one minimising the trace objective under
   Frobenius positivity.  Stability is this selection: the projected state
   is an energy minimum because it is the LP optimum, not because the
   projector exists.
3. **Energetics**.  The binding energy is the trace-weight *gap* between
   the selected block and the next configuration.  For the six-carbon ring
   the threaded $T(2,6)$ configuration carries Alexander weight
   $\approx 3$ while the open chain carries weight $5$; the gap $5 - 3 =
   2$, scaled by the molecular energy unit, is the ring-closure/resonance
   energy ([ring-closure barrier](#prop:ring-closure-barrier)).

**Energy released by breaking the ring.**  The
ring's projector $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ is *irreducible* over all six strands:
the delocalised $\pi$-system is a single object, not six separable bonds.
One cannot open the ring a single bond at a time — breaking it collapses
the entire symmetric block back to the localised chain, paying the whole
weight-gap at once (cooperatively).  An open chain breaks one localised
bond at a time, at small local cost; the ring resists as a unit, at large
concentrated cost.  This irreducibility of the global projector is the
categorical content of aromatic stability — benzene declining to behave as
three independent double bonds.

The account is testable, not retrofitted: step 2 requires the binding LP
to actually select $\mu = (N)$ for the ring geometry (computable with no
chemical foreknowledge), and step 3 requires the weight-gap times the
molecular scale to match the measured resonance energy — the
[$T(2,N)$ threading link](#def:threading-link) supplying the $N/2$ weight.
