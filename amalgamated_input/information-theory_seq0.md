We derive the gravitational constant $G_q$ from the $q$-Yang--Mills--Levi action ([$q$-YM-Levi](#def:q-yang-mills-levi)). The coupling is determined by the **in-progress braid content** of a Planck cell. These are sections of the jet tower $A_0 \hookrightarrow A_1 \hookrightarrow \cdots$ that remain partially evaluated and not yet resolved to observable knots ([gravitational constant](#def:gravitational-constant)).

The central formula is:

$$
  G_q = \frac{c_q^3 \hbar_q}{S_{\mathrm{cell}}}.
$$

Here $S_{\mathrm{cell}}$ measures the total information content per fibre, counting all in-progress braids. To maintain self-consistency with Planck units, the cell entropy must evaluate to $S_{\mathrm{cell}} = (q-1)^2/q^3 \approx 0.009$ $q$-bits. This represents $1.0\%$ of the single-operation entropy $\eta_q \approx 0.892$. The small ratio reflects the sparsity of partially-evaluated braids in the near-classical regime.

Gravity is weak because most braiding at a typical fibre is already resolved. Fully resolved braids contribute to $T^{(q)}$ as matter. Only the unresolved, in-progress tails establish the gravitational coupling.
