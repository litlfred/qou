The categorical jet object $J^1(A)$ ([categorical jet](#def:categorical-jet))
encodes first-order deformations of the Frobenius--Hopf object. In this
section we develop the *lifting* programme: promoting a quantum
observable universe on $A$ to one on $J^1(A)$, and iterating to build
the brane tower.

<!-- FILE_START: categorical-jet-prolongation.md -->
Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum universe with connection $\nabla$ ([categorical connection](#def:categorical-connection)). Then $J^1(A)$ ([categorical jet](#def:categorical-jet)) inherits a Frobenius--Hopf structure from $A$. The quadruple $(\mathbf{C}, J^1(A), \tau', q)$, with $\tau'$ the induced fibre functor, is again a quantum universe.

```tex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jet-prolongation-diagram}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3em]
  (\mathbf{C}, A, \tau) \ar[r, "\nabla"]
  & (\mathbf{C}, J^1(A), \tau') \\
  A \ar[u, phantom, "\ni"] \ar[r, hook, "j^1"']
  & J^1(A) \ar[u, phantom, "\ni"]
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad\text{(jet prolongation)}
\end{equation}
```

## Proof (sketch)

The first jet $J^1(A)$ of a Frobenius--Hopf object $A$ inherits a Frobenius--Hopf structure by transport along the connection $\nabla$, via the synthetic-differential-geometry construction of the jet bundle of an algebra object (\cite{kock2004}, §1).

1. **Frobenius--Hopf transport.** The jet inclusion $j^1 \colon A \hookrightarrow J^1(A)$ is an algebra map, and $\nabla$ splits $J^1(A) \cong A \oplus (\Omega^1 \otimes A)$. The multiplication, comultiplication, unit, counit and antipode of $A$ extend to $J^1(A)$ through the Leibniz rule for $\nabla$, making $J^1(A)$ a Frobenius--Hopf object.

2. **Induced fibre functor.** With $\pi \colon J^1(A) \to A$ the jet projection, $\tau' = \tau \circ \pi$ remains monoidal, faithful and exact. Thus, $(\mathbf{C}, J^1(A), \tau', q)$ satisfies the four quantum-universe axioms with the identical braiding eigenvalue $q$. $\square$

<!-- FILE_START: categorical-q-reeb-flow.md -->
Let $\xi$ be the Reeb derivation of $A$ ([categorical Reeb derivation](#def:categorical-reeb)) and $[\cdot,\cdot]_q$ the $q$-Lie bracket ([categorical $q$-Lie bracket](#def:categorical-q-lie-bracket)). The *$q$-deformed Reeb derivation* $\xi_q$ is the derivation on $A$ defined by

$$
  \xi_q(a) =  q^{-1}\xi(a)
  + (1 - q^{-1})[\xi, X_a]_q,
$$

where $X_a$ is the horizontal derivation associated to $a$, satisfying $\pi_H(da) = \iota_{X_a} \varTheta_A$. The **$q$-Reeb flow** is the one-parameter family of automorphisms $\phi_t^q \in \mathrm{Aut}(A)$ generated by $\xi_q$.

```tex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-reeb-flow-diagram}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3em]
  A \ar[r, "\phi_t^q"] \ar[d, "\xi_q"']
  & A \ar[d, "\xi_q"] \\
  A \ar[r, "\phi_t^q"']
  & A
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad (\phi_0^q = \mathrm{id})
\end{equation}
```

<!-- FILE_START: reeb-primes-as-orbits.md -->
The $q$-Reeb flow $\phi_t^q$ transforms the classical picture of
primes. In classical number theory, the natural logarithm $\ln x$
serves as the clock for counting primes: the prime number theorem says
roughly one prime per $\ln x$ integers near $x$, and the primes are
discrete, isolated events on the number line.

The $q$-deformation replaces this static picture with a dynamical one.
The derivation $\xi_q$ generates a continuous flow on the substrate
algebra $A$, and the closed orbits of this flow --- the periodic
trajectories of $\phi_t^q$ --- correspond to the prime ideals of the
underlying geometry. Each prime is no longer a point; it is a *closed
orbit* winding through the brane tower
([recursive brane tower](#def:recursive-brane-tower)).

This shift has three consequences that will unfold over the next
chapters:

1. **Counting becomes spectral.** Instead of summing $1/\ln p$ over
   primes $p \le x$ (the classical logarithmic integral), one traces
   the eigenvalues of $\xi_q$. The discrete Reeb spectrum replaces
   the continuous logarithm.

2. **The holonomy encodes the weight.** The contribution of each
   prime orbit to the count is not a scalar but a holonomy matrix ---
   the parallel transport of the adjoint representation around the
   closed orbit. This promotes the abelian counting of classical
   number theory to a nonabelian, matrix-valued integral
   (Chapter 8).

3. **Stability becomes geometric.** Whether the prime distribution
   has bounded fluctuations (the content of the Riemann hypothesis)
   reduces to whether the holonomy matrices are unitary --- a
   geometric constraint on the Reeb flow, not an analytic property of
   a complex function.

The machinery of lifting and :refterm[descent]{#descent} developed in this chapter
provides the algebraic infrastructure for making these ideas precise.
