The algebraic substrate is the Frobenius--Hopf algebra $A$ viewed as
a graded ring equipped with the Reeb derivation --- the differential
operator forced by the categorical volume constraint.  This chapter
develops the five-step algebraic programme: substrate structure and
Reeb derivation, the unit particle as a categorical invariant,
spectral trace and Tannaka--Krein duality, geometric stability via
the Hasse--Weil bound, and the classical limit that recovers the
macroscopic continuum.

The main results are a spectral trace formula that relates the
$q$-zeta function of $A$ to the Chebyshev function of the Reeb
flow, a stability criterion derived from the Weil bound on the
fibre geometry, and a precise characterisation of the classical limit
$q \to 1$ as a functor that fails to be an equivalence --- the
obstruction to classicality being a non-trivial categorical
invariant.

<!-- FILE_START: frobenius-hopf-substrate.md -->
Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a rigid :refterm[monoidal category]{#monoidal-category}. A **Frobenius--Hopf
substrate** is a sextuple $(A, \mu, \eta, \Delta, \varepsilon, S)$ in
$\mathbf{C}$ equipped with a Frobenius form
$\lambda \colon A \to \mathbf{1}$ satisfying the following axioms.

**Algebra structure.** The morphisms $\mu \colon A \otimes A \to A$
(multiplication) and $\eta \colon \mathbf{1} \to A$ (unit) make $A$
into an associative unital algebra object in $\mathbf{C}$.

**Coalgebra structure.** The morphisms
$\Delta \colon A \to A \otimes A$ (comultiplication) and
$\varepsilon \colon A \to \mathbf{1}$ (counit) make $A$ into a
coassociative counital coalgebra object, with antipode
$S \colon A \to A$ satisfying the Hopf compatibility:

$$
\varepsilon \circ \mu = u \circ (\varepsilon \otimes \varepsilon),
$$

where $u \colon \mathbf{1} \otimes \mathbf{1} \xrightarrow{\cong} \mathbf{1}$ is the canonical unit isomorphism, so that $\varepsilon$ is an algebra map into the monoidal unit.

**Frobenius relation.** The Frobenius form $\lambda$ satisfies:

$$
\mu \circ \Delta
= (\Delta \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \alpha \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes \mu)
$$

where $\alpha$ is the associator in $\mathbf{C}$.

**Non-degeneracy.** The Frobenius pairing
$\langle a, b \rangle_F = \lambda(\mu(a \otimes b))$ is non-degenerate:
the induced map $A \to A^\vee$ is an isomorphism, equivalently the
snake identities hold with respect to a coevaluation
$\tilde{\eta} \colon \mathbf{1} \to A \otimes A$.

**Self-duality.** The algebra $A$ is *self-dual as a substrate*: the
non-degenerate Frobenius pairing identifies $A$ with its dual $A^\vee$
canonically, so that the observer trace $\Phi = \lambda \circ \mu$ and
the observed state space are intrinsically paired.

The **substrate parameter** $q \in \mathbb{C}^*$ is the deformation
parameter of the Frobenius--Hopf algebra $A$. It governs the
non-commutativity of the algebraic substrate: when $q = 1$ the algebra
is commutative and the quantum structure degenerates to its classical
shadow.

The **quantum Planck constant** associated to $q$ is

$$
\hbar_q =  1 - q^{-1} =  \frac{q - 1}{q}.
$$

The pair $(q, \hbar_q)$ parametrizes the deviation of $A$ from
commutativity. In the classical limit $q \to 1$ one has $\hbar_q \to 0$,
recovering the commutative coordinate ring and the smooth manifold
geometry of the base space.

Two structural properties follow directly from the definition.
First, the invertibility condition $q \ne 0$ ensures that $A$ is a
genuine Hopf algebra (the antipode is well-defined). Second,
$\hbar_q \ne 0$ whenever $q \ne 1$, so the non-commutative regime
$q \ne 1$ always carries a non-trivial quantum scale.

In the categorical formulation of [Chapter 1](#def:substrate-parameter),
the substrate parameter is an element of $\mathrm{End}(\mathbf{1}) \cong k^\times$.
Here we work with the concrete representative $q \in \mathbb{C}^*$
obtained by evaluating this endomorphism. In geometric realisations
(Chapter 6), $q$ further specialises to a real parameter $q > 1$
governing the Descartes universe.

Let $A$ be a Frobenius--Hopf substrate in a rigid :refterm[monoidal category]{#monoidal-category}
$\mathbf{C}$. The **categorical Reeb derivation** is the morphism
$\xi_q \colon A \to A$ uniquely determined by the following two
conditions.

**Normalization.** The derivation is compatible with the contact form
$\varTheta$:

$$
\xi_q \circ \varTheta =  \varTheta,
$$

so that $\xi_q$ preserves the contact structure on the state bundle
$\mathcal{S}$.

**Derivation property.** The morphism $\xi_q$ satisfies the Leibniz
rule with respect to the multiplication $\mu$ of $A$:

$$
\xi_q \circ \mu
=  \mu \circ (\xi_q \otimes \mathrm{id} + \mathrm{id} \otimes \xi_q).
$$

The *period* of the Reeb derivation is the smallest positive integer
$\Omega_q$ (when it exists) such that the iterated composition satisfies

$$
\xi_q^{\Omega_q} =  \mathrm{id}_A.
$$

The derivation $\xi_q$ encodes the *observer drift*: the intrinsic time
evolution of the substrate as seen from within. The periodicity condition
ensures that the Reeb flow closes, yielding a discrete spectrum of
frequencies on $A$. Each eigenvalue of $\xi_q$ corresponds to a
fundamental mode of the substrate, and the full Reeb spectrum is
determined by the spectral geometry of Bring's curve $\mathcal{B}$.

To appreciate the Reeb derivation $\xi_q$, it helps to contrast
it with the classical picture it replaces.

In classical analytic number theory, the "time" variable is the real
number $x$, and the fundamental counting function is the logarithmic
integral $\mathrm{Li}(x) = \int_2^x \frac{dt}{\ln t}$. Here $\ln t$
plays the role of a clock: it converts multiplicative structure (primes
as factors) into additive structure (primes as points on a number line).
The primes are discrete, isolated events scattered along the continuum
of real numbers.

The $q$-deformed Reeb flow changes this picture fundamentally. The
derivation $\xi_q \colon A \to A$ acts on the Frobenius--Hopf substrate
$A$ as a *continuous dynamical system*: it generates a flow on the
state bundle $\mathcal{S}$ that replaces the classical logarithmic
clock with a periodic orbit structure. Under this flow:

1. **Primes become periodic orbits.** Each prime ideal
   $\mathfrak{m}$ of the coordinate ring
   $\mathcal{O}_q(\mathcal{B})$
   ([Bring's coordinate ring](#def:brings-coordinate-ring))
   corresponds not to a point on a number line but to a *closed orbit*
   of the Reeb flow --- a vortex filament winding along one of the
   color tubes of the fibre geometry.

2. **Counting becomes flow integration.** The classical sum
   $\sum_{p \le x} 1$ is replaced by the spectral trace over Reeb
   eigenvalues. The period $\Omega_q$ of the Reeb derivation
   discretizes the spectrum, so the "time" variable is no longer the
   continuous real line but the iterated composition
   $\xi_q, \xi_q^2, \ldots, \xi_q^{\Omega_q} = \mathrm{id}$.

3. **The logarithm becomes a $q$-logarithm.** The classical $\ln t$
   is replaced by the $q$-deformed logarithm $\ln_q(t)$, which
   encodes the non-commutative correction to the clock rate. As
   $q \to 1$ the $q$-logarithm degenerates to the ordinary logarithm,
   recovering the classical counting.

This transition --- from a static number line with discrete prime
events to a dynamic flow with periodic prime orbits --- is the engine
of the quantum observable universe. Everything that follows in this
chapter exploits the orbit structure of the Reeb flow: defect counting,
the spectral trace, and the stability analysis all reduce to studying
the closed orbits and their eigenvalues under $\xi_q$.

<!-- FILE_START: rigid-dualizing-complex.md -->
Let $A$ be the Frobenius--Hopf substrate algebra and let $D(A)$ denote
its derived category of bimodules. A **rigid dualizing complex** for $A$
is a complex $\omega_A^\bullet$ of $A$-bimodules satisfying the
following conditions.

**Finite injective dimension.** The complex $\omega_A^\bullet$ has
finite injective dimension on both sides: as a left $A$-module and as a
A right $A$-module.

**Rigidifying isomorphism.** There exists a canonical isomorphism

$$
\omega_A^\bullet
\simeq
R\mathrm{Hom}_{A^e}(A, A^e)
$$

in $D(A^e)$, where $A^e = A \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the enveloping
algebra. This isomorphism is the *rigidifying structure*: it pins down
$\omega_A^\bullet$ up to unique isomorphism, eliminating the ambiguity
that afflicts ordinary dualizing complexes.

**Van den Bergh duality.** For any finitely generated $A$-bimodule $M$,
the rigid dualizing complex induces a duality

$$
\mathrm{Ext}^i_A(M, \omega_A^\bullet)
 \cong 
H^{d-i}(M)^\vee,
$$

where $d$ is the homological dimension of $A$.

The rigidity condition \cite{yekutieli2010} ensures that the
dualizing complex is *intrinsic* to $A$ --- it does not depend on a
choice of base ring or presentation. This makes $\omega_A^\bullet$ the
correct categorical substitute for the canonical sheaf in the
non-commutative setting.

Continuing the
[Bring coordinate ring](#def:brings-coordinate-ring), we construct
the [rigid dualizing complex](#def:rigid-dualizing-complex)
$\omega_A^\bullet$ explicitly — replacing "there exists a canonical
isomorphism" with a concrete computation.

**Step 1: identify the algebra.** Set
$A = \mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SU}(2))$, the quantum coordinate ring.
As a $q$-deformation of a $3$-dimensional Lie group, $A$ is an
Artin–Schelter regular algebra of global dimension $d = 3$.

**Step 2: compute the dualizing complex.** For Artin–Schelter
regular algebras of dimension $d$, the rigid dualizing complex is
a shift of $A$ itself:

$$
  \omega_A^\bullet \cong A_\sigma[-d]
$$

where $A_\sigma$ denotes $A$ with bimodule structure twisted by
the Nakayama automorphism $\sigma$, and $[-d]$ is the cohomological
shift by $d = 3$.

**Step 3: compute the Nakayama automorphism.** For
$\mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SU}(2))$, the Nakayama automorphism is
determined by the modular element of the Haar state
\cite{woronowicz1988}. On the
generators:

$$
  \sigma(\alpha) = q^{-2}\alpha, \quad
  \sigma(\beta) = q^{2}\beta, \quad
  \sigma(\bar\alpha) = q^{2}\bar\alpha, \quad
  \sigma(\bar\beta) = q^{-2}\bar\beta.
$$

This is the inner automorphism by the group-like element
$K^2 \in \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$, where $K$ is the
Cartan generator satisfying $K\alpha K^{-1} = q^{-1}\alpha$.

**Step 4: verify the rigidifying isomorphism.** The rigid dualizing
complex must satisfy
$\omega_A^\bullet \simeq R\mathrm{Hom}_{A^e}(A, A^e)$
in $D(A^e)$. For Artin–Schelter regular $A$:

$$
  \mathrm{Ext}^i_{A^e}(A, A^e)
  = \begin{cases}
    A_\sigma & \text{if } i = d = 3, \\
    0 & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
$$

This is Van den Bergh's theorem applied to $\mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SU}(2))$.
The Ext computation uses the Koszul resolution of $A$ as an
$A^e$-module, which for the $3$-dimensional case has the form:

$$
  0 \to A^e \to (A^e)^3 \to (A^e)^3 \to A^e \to A \to 0.
$$

**Step 5: Van den Bergh duality.** For any finitely generated
$A$-bimodule $M$:

$$
  \mathrm{Ext}^i_A(M, \omega_A^\bullet)
  \cong H^{3-i}(M)^\vee
  = \mathrm{Tor}_{3-i}^A(M, A_\sigma)^\vee.
$$

This is Poincaré duality in the noncommutative setting.

**Bring's surface specialisation.** On Bring's QOU, the dualizing
complex $\omega_A^\bullet = A_\sigma[-3]$ encodes the Serre duality
of the genus-$4$ curve. The Nakayama automorphism $\sigma$ at
$q = q_{\mathrm{Bring}}$ acts on the $8$ holomorphic/anti-holomorphic
differentials of $\Sigma_4$ by scaling with $q^{\pm 2}$, reflecting
the $q$-deformed Hodge structure. The rigidity condition ensures this
complex is intrinsic to Bring's curve — independent of any choice of
embedding or coordinates.

The Frobenius form $\lambda \colon A \to \mathbf{1}$ of the substrate
algebra $A$ induces a quasi-isomorphism between $A$, viewed as a
complex concentrated in degree zero, and its rigid dualizing complex
$\omega_A^\bullet$:

$$
A \xrightarrow{ \sim } \omega_A^\bullet
\quad\text{in } D(A).
$$

More precisely, the non-degenerate Frobenius pairing
$\langle a, b \rangle_F = \lambda(\mu(a \otimes b))$ defines an
$A$-bimodule map $\varphi \colon A \to \omega_A^\bullet$ whose cone is
acyclic, establishing a quasi-isomorphism in the derived category of
$A$-bimodules.

**Corollaries.** The quasi-isomorphism $A \simeq \omega_A^\bullet$
implies:

1. **Serre-type duality**: $\mathrm{Ext}^i_A(M, A) \cong H^{d-i}(M)^\vee$
   for finitely generated $A$-modules $M$, where $d$ is the homological
   dimension.

2. **Canonical trace**: the composition $\varepsilon \circ \mu$ factors
   through the Frobenius form, providing a categorical trace on
   $\mathrm{End}(A)$.

3. **Uniqueness**: since $\omega_A^\bullet$ is rigid, the
   quasi-isomorphism is unique up to unique isomorphism --- there is no
   ambiguity in the self-duality.

Self-duality means that the observer trace
$\Phi = \lambda \circ \mu$ and the observed state space are canonically
paired. The algebra $A$ does not require an external arena: it is
simultaneously the algebra of observables and the space on which those
observables act. In the language of rigid dualizing complex theory \cite{yekutieli2010}, the
Frobenius form $\lambda$ is the unique trace that makes $A$ its own
dualizing object.
