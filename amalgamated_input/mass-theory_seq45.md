Tests whether the **Option E**
Kashiwara crystal weight $c_\lambda(q)$ used by
the canonical formalism
preserves the structural $T = {}^3\mathrm{He}$ isospin invariance
established in
([Hopf-antipode invariance](#prop:spin-involution-lp-dual-invariance))

## Setup

The Option E weight is the principal $q$-specialisation of the
Schur polynomial,

$$
  c_\lambda(q) \;:=\; s_\lambda(1, q, q^2, \ldots, q^{n-1})
                \;=\; q^{n(\lambda)} \prod_{(i,j) \in \lambda}
                      \frac{1 - q^{n + (j - i)}}{1 - q^{h(i, j)}}
$$

(Macdonald hook-content closed form), evaluated at the substrate
$q_0 = 1.109978595554181\ldots$ (50 dps).  This is the structural Kashiwara weight on
the symmetric crystal $B(\lambda, n)$, equal at $q \to 1$ to the
Weyl dimension $\dim V_\lambda = |B(\lambda, n)|$.

The canonical evaluation forms the $c_\lambda$-weighted observable

$$
  W_c(K) \;:=\; \sum_{\substack{\lambda \,\vdash\, 3A \\ \mathrm{height}(\lambda) \,\leq\, 6}}
                    c_\lambda(q_0) \cdot \chi^\lambda(\mathcal{B}_K)(q_0)
$$

over the **canonical $\bowtie_K$-coupled atomic word**
$\mathcal{B}_K = \texttt{atom\_braid\_word\_3A}(Z, N)$ for
$K \in \{D, T, {}^3\mathrm{He}\}$.

## Non-triviality of the test

The canonical T and ${}^3\mathrm{He}$ words differ in *interface
type*, not just generator signs:

- $T$ (ordering $[p, n, n]$) has interfaces $(pn{=}\bowtie_K, \,
  nn{=}\sigma^{-1})$;
- ${}^3\mathrm{He}$ (ordering $[p, p, n]$) has interfaces
  $(pp{=}\sigma, \, pn{=}\bowtie_K)$.

Per [crystal-isospin-degeneracy §Open work](#prop:crystal-isospin-degeneracy),
the sign-flip invariance argument does **not** automatically apply
to canonical words.  The canonical evaluation therefore tests the
[Hopf-antipode mechanism](#prop:spin-involution-lp-dual-invariance)
at the $c_\lambda$-weight level.

## Structural reason for the invariance breaking

The Hopf antipode $S$ acts on Wedderburn blocks as the
*transpose-partition involution*
$V_\lambda \leftrightarrow V_{\lambda^T}$.  An $S$-invariant
per-block weight must satisfy $w_\lambda = w_{\lambda^T}$.  Option
E's Kashiwara crystal weight is the principal $q$-specialisation
of the Schur polynomial:

$$
  c_\lambda(q) \;=\; q^{n(\lambda)} \prod_{(i,j) \in \lambda}
                       \frac{1 - q^{n + (j-i)}}{1 - q^{h(i,j)}}.
$$

The factor $q^{n(\lambda)}$ alone is *not* transpose-symmetric:
$n(\lambda^T) = \binom{|\lambda|}{2} - n(\lambda)$ — they differ
unless $\lambda$ is self-conjugate.  The hook-content product also
breaks under transpose because $c(i, j)$ flips sign while $h(i,j)$
is invariant.  Concretely at $A = 3$, $n = 9$:

| $\lambda$ | $c_\lambda(q_0)$ | $\lambda^T$ | $c_{\lambda^T}(q_0)$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| $(6,3)$       | $1.33 \times 10^{7}$  | $(2,2,2,1,1,1)$ | $2.92 \times 10^{5}$ |
| $(5,3,1)$     | $2.12 \times 10^{7}$  | $(3,2,2,1,1)$   | $2.87 \times 10^{6}$ |
| $(5,2,2)$     | $1.24 \times 10^{7}$  | $(3,3,2,1)$     | $6.20 \times 10^{6}$ |
| $(3,3,3)$     | $2.38 \times 10^{6}$  | $(3,3,3)$       | $2.38 \times 10^{6}$ |

Only the self-conjugate $(3,3,3)$ has $c_\lambda = c_{\lambda^T}$;
every other partition contributes asymmetrically.  Therefore
$W_c(K) = \sum_\lambda c_\lambda \chi^\lambda(\mathcal{B}_K)$ inherits a
transpose-asymmetric weighting and cannot be $S$-invariant — even
when $\mathcal{B}_K$ and $\beta_{K^*}$ (mirror partner) realise the same
Hopf-orbit on the Markov-trace pairing.

## Refinement of the mirror-isobar EM-localisation theorem

This refines but does **not** falsify
[mirror-isobar EM-localisation](#rem:isospin-mass-split-em-localisation),
because:

1. The EM-localisation theorem only requires invariance of the
   *Markov-trace observable*, i.e., the σ- and $S$-invariant
   sector of $\tau_K$.
2. Option E's $W_c$ is *not* a Markov-trace observable — $c_\lambda$
   is a Schur-polynomial principal specialisation, not a
   Markov-axiom-compatible per-block coefficient $y_\lambda(q)$.
   The Markov trace lives in the $S$-invariant subspace by the
   Hopf-antipode proposition; Option E does not.
3. Therefore Option E's $\Delta W_c \neq 0$ does *not* contradict
   $\Delta\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{Markov}}(T, {}^3\mathrm{He}) = 0$.

What this does establish: **any per-block weight used for binding-
energy reconstruction must be symmetrised** under $S$ (e.g., taken
as $\tfrac{1}{2}(c_\lambda + c_{\lambda^T})$, or replaced by the
genuine Markov coefficient $y_\lambda$) before it can be paired
with mirror-isobar invariance.  The Option E variant implemented in
the canonical formalism
is therefore a **non-symmetrised** scheme; if Option E predictions
end up agreeing with mirror-isobar mass differences, the agreement
must be tracked to the *non-Markov-trace* part of the formula
(e.g., the calibration of `α_E` in
the canonical formalism),
not to the structural $c_\lambda \cdot \chi^\lambda$ kernel.

## Recommendation (open follow-up)

Define the symmetrised Option E weight

$$
  c_\lambda^{\mathrm{sym}}(q) \;:=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,
        \bigl(c_\lambda(q) + c_{\lambda^T}(q)\bigr)
$$

and re-run the isospin canonical evaluation.  If $W_{c^{\mathrm{sym}}}(T) =
W_{c^{\mathrm{sym}}}({}^3\mathrm{He})$ at $\sim 10^{-10}$,
Option E^sym is the structurally-correct per-block weight that
respects both the EM-localisation theorem and the Hopf-antipode
invariance.  Tracked here as future work.

1. **Closed-form extrapolation fails at $A = 1$** for the neutron

The most parsimonious resolution is **(1)** — the closed-form

  \frac{G}{3} \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{Vol}(B_3)}{24} \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{Vol}(B_3)}{|S_4|},
$$

*the Borromean volume divided by the order of the symmetric group
$S_4$ (4 nucleons).  Suggestive but not yet a derivation: the
factor $24 = |S_4|$ would correspond to averaging over 4-nucleon
permutations.  The remaining $0.16\%$ gap between $G/3$ and
$\alpha^*$ is unexplained.*
