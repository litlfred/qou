With $G \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)$ in hand and the simple
objects $V_j$ labelled by spin, we now isolate the rank-one
subgroup $T \subset G$ that diagonalises the action of every
$V_j$ and read off the resulting decomposition of differential
forms on $A$.

Definition: Maximal torus and weight subcategory takes the
subcategory $\langle V \rangle$ generated by direct sums of
weight spaces of the fundamental object and defines
$T = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau|_{\langle V \rangle})$
as the largest subgroup acting diagonally on each weight space.
Example: Maximal torus on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ realises $T$ as the
diagonal $\mathrm{U}(1) \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ acting on the
weight basis $\{e_+, e_-\}$ by $e^{\pm i\varTheta}$.
Definition: Weight decomposition lifts the $T$-action to the
non-commutative de Rham forms $\Omega^k(A)$, splitting every
form $\alpha$ uniquely into a horizontal (weight-zero) and
vertical (non-zero-weight) component. Theorem: Existence of the weight decomposition — with its proof block — verifies
the splitting and the projectors $\pi_H, \pi_V$.
Remark: Weight decomposition on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ exhibits
the splitting concretely on left-invariant forms.

The section closes with Remark: Physical interpretation of the weight decomposition, which reads the $\alpha = \alpha_H +
\alpha_V$ splitting as the geometric origin of spin--statistics
— purely horizontal forms are bosonic, mixed forms fermionic.
The next section, **Vectors, hadrons, and the PawLei basis**,
takes the rank-two fundamental representation and the diagonal
weight basis as input.

<!-- FILE_START: maximal-torus.md -->
Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a
([quantum observable universe](#def:quantum-observable-universe)) with fundamental
object $V$ (the unique simple object of categorical dimension $2$;
see [spin labels](#prop:spin-labels) for the full labelling). The *weight
subcategory* $\langle V \rangle \subset \mathbf{C}$ is the full
subcategory whose objects are direct sums of weight spaces of $V$
(i.e. simultaneous eigenspaces of the Cartan subalgebra action).
The **maximal torus** of the gauge group is

$$
  T = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau|_{\langle V \rangle}),
$$

the sub-group scheme of
$G = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$
([$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ emergence](#thm:su2-emergence)) consisting of those natural
automorphisms that act diagonally on each weight space of $V$.

```tex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maximal-torus-diagram}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3em]
  T \ar[r, hook] \ar[d, "\mathrm{diag}"']
  & G = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau) \ar[d] \\
  \mathrm{Aut}(\tau(V))
  & \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau) \ar[l]
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad\text{(maximal torus)}
\end{equation}
```

Equivalently, $T$ is the largest subgroup of $G$ for which every
object in $\langle V \rangle$ decomposes into one-dimensional
$T$-eigenspaces.

<!-- FILE_START: maximal-torus-su2.md -->
Continuing [spin labels on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$](#ex:spin-labels-su2), the fundamental object is
$V = \mathbb{C}^2$ with the standard $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-action. The
weight decomposition is $V = \mathbb{C} e_+ \oplus \mathbb{C} e_-$
(the $\pm\tfrac{1}{2}$-eigenspaces of
$e_3 = \frac{1}{2}\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{smallmatrix}$).
The weight subcategory $\langle V \rangle$ consists of representations
that decompose into weight spaces: $V$ itself, $\mathbf{1}$, and their
direct sums. The maximal torus is

$$
  T = \mathrm{U}(1)
  = \left\{
    \begin{pmatrix} e^{i\varTheta} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{-i\varTheta} \end{pmatrix}
    : \varTheta \in \mathbb{R}
  \right\},
$$

the diagonal matrices in $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. Each element of $T$ acts on
$e_{\pm}$ by $e^{\pm i\varTheta}$: one-dimensional eigenspaces, as required.

Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a
([quantum observable universe](#def:quantum-observable-universe)) with maximal torus
$T$ ([maximal torus](#def:maximal-torus)) and higher differential forms
$\Omega^k(A)$ ([higher forms](#def:higher-forms)). The $T$-action on
$\Omega^k(A)$ induces the **weight decomposition**, a
$\mathbb{Z}$-grading by weights. Denoting by
$\pi_H, \pi_V$ the projections onto the weight-$0$ and non-zero-weight
summands, every $\alpha \in \Omega^k(A)$ decomposes uniquely:

```tex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-decomposition-cd}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2em, column sep=3em]
  & \Omega^k(A) \ar[dl, "\pi_H"'] \ar[dr, "\pi_V"] & \\
  \Omega^k_H(A) \ar[rr, hook, "\oplus"']
  & & \Omega^k_V(A)
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad \alpha = \alpha_H + \alpha_V.
\end{equation}
```

Here $\alpha_H = \pi_H(\alpha)$ is the **horizontal component**
(weight-$0$ under $T$) and $\alpha_V = \pi_V(\alpha)$ is the
**vertical component** (non-zero weight). A form is **purely
horizontal** if and only if $\alpha_V = 0$.

<!-- FILE_START: weight-decomposition-exists.md -->
Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a
([quantum observable universe](#def:quantum-observable-universe)) with
[maximal torus](#def:maximal-torus) $T$ acting on
[higher forms](#def:higher-forms) $\Omega^k(A)$. Then there exists a
[weight decomposition](#def:weight-decomposition)
$(\pi_H, \pi_V)$ of $\Omega^k(A)$ satisfying:

1. **Complementarity:** $\pi_H + \pi_V = \mathrm{id}$.
2. **Idempotency:** $\pi_H^2 = \pi_H$ and $\pi_V^2 = \pi_V$.

```tex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-decomposition-exists-diagram}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3em]
  \Omega^k(A) \ar[r, "\pi_H"] \ar[d, "\pi_V"']
  & \Omega^k_H(A) \\
  \Omega^k_V(A)
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad (\pi_H + \pi_V = \mathrm{id}, \pi_H \pi_V = 0)
\end{equation}
```

<!-- FILE_START: weight-decomposition-su2.md -->
Continuing [maximal torus of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$](#ex:maximal-torus-su2), we
construct the [weight decomposition](#def:weight-decomposition)
explicitly, rather than invoking existence.

**Step 1: the $T$-action on $\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$.** The
maximal torus $T = \mathrm{U}(1)$ acts on the coordinate ring
$A = \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ by left translation:
$(t \cdot f)(g) = f(t^{-1}g)$ for
$t = \mathrm{diag}(e^{i\varTheta}, e^{-i\varTheta})$. On the matrix
generators $\alpha, \beta, \bar\alpha, \bar\beta$
($\alpha\bar\alpha + \beta\bar\beta = 1$), the action is diagonal:

$$
  t \cdot \alpha = e^{i\varTheta}\,\alpha, \quad
  t \cdot \beta  = e^{-i\varTheta}\,\beta, \quad
  t \cdot \bar\alpha = e^{-i\varTheta}\,\bar\alpha, \quad
  t \cdot \bar\beta  = e^{i\varTheta}\,\bar\beta.
$$

**Step 2: weight-space projectors.** For each integer weight
$m \in \mathbb{Z}$, the isotypic projector is constructed by
torus averaging:

$$
  e_m(f) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
    e^{-im\varTheta}\,(t_\varTheta \cdot f)\,d\varTheta.
$$

This is a concrete integral, not an abstract existence: evaluate it
on any polynomial in $\alpha, \beta, \bar\alpha, \bar\beta$ by
collecting powers of $e^{i\varTheta}$.

**Step 3: horizontal and vertical projectors.** The horizontal
projector is $\pi_H = e_0$ (the weight-zero component); the vertical
projector is $\pi_V = \mathrm{id} - e_0 = \sum_{m \neq 0} e_m$:

- $\pi_H(\alpha^j \bar\alpha^k \beta^\ell \bar\beta^n)
  = \alpha^j \bar\alpha^k \beta^\ell \bar\beta^n$ when $j - k - \ell + n = 0$,
  and $0$ otherwise.
- $\pi_V$ picks out the complementary monomials.

For the spin-$j$ representation $V_j = \mathrm{Span}\{v_m : m = -j, \ldots, j\}$,
the weight spaces are one-dimensional: $e_m(V_j) = \mathbb{C}\,v_m$.
The decomposition on $\Omega^k(A)$ extends by the Leibniz rule
$e_m(\alpha\,d\beta) = e^{im\varTheta}\,\alpha\,d\beta$ restricted to
forms of total weight $m$.

**Verification.** Complementarity $\pi_H + \pi_V = \mathrm{id}$
holds because $\sum_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} e_m = \mathrm{id}$ (Fourier
completeness on $\mathrm{U}(1)$). Idempotency
$\pi_H^2 = \pi_H$ follows from $e_0^2 = e_0$ (orthogonality of
characters: $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int e^{i(m-n)\varTheta}d\varTheta = \delta_{mn}$).

**Bring's surface specialisation.** On the Bring coordinate ring
$\mathcal{O}_q(\mathcal{B})$ with its $S_5$ symmetry, the maximal
torus is $T = \mathrm{U}(1) \subset \mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ acting on the
quantum generators. The projectors $e_m$ are the same torus integrals
with $q$-deformed matrix coefficients
$t^j_{m,m'}(q) \in \mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ replacing the
classical ones. The weight-zero subspace $e_0(\mathcal{O}_q(\mathcal{B}))$
consists of $S_5$-invariant combinations of the five coordinates
$x_1, \ldots, x_5$ — precisely the symmetric functions that define
Bring's curve.

The decomposition $\alpha = \alpha_H + \alpha_V$ is the geometric origin of
spin--statistics: fermions (non-trivial $\alpha_V$) carry half-integer spin;
bosons (purely horizontal) carry integer spin. See
[*Bring's Surface*](#chap:brings-surface) for experimental confirmation.
