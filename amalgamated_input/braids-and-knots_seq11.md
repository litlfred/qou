<!-- FILE_START: enveloping-algebras-intro.md -->
The enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ and its
$q$-deformation $U_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ supply the algebraic
substrate for the categorical braiding introduced in the previous
section.  This section assembles the universal enveloping
algebra, defines the quantum group $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ via the
$q$-Casimir, and exhibits the $R$-matrix that realises the
categorical braiding on its representation category.

The arc proceeds in three movements: defining the enveloping
algebra and the quantum-$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ together with the
$q$-Casimir; proving existence and uniqueness of the $q$-Casimir
under the substrate-parametrised structure; and identifying the
$R$-matrix as the canonical braiding morphism that the rest of
the chapter treats as a black box.  Readers familiar with the
classical Drinfeld–Jimbo construction can skim the existence
proofs; the substrate-parametrised version is a direct
reformulation of the classical construction.

<!-- FILE_START: enveloping-algebra.md -->
The universal enveloping algebra
$\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the associative algebra generated by
$e_1,e_2,e_3$ ([SU(2) basis](#def:su2-basis)) subject to the
commutation relations.  It provides the
algebraic scaffolding in which every finite-dimensional representation of
$\mathfrak{su}(2)$ lives and is the classical limit ($q\to 1$) of the
quantum group $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$.

**PBW basis theorem.**
The monomials $\{e_1^{a}e_2^{b}e_3^{c} : a,b,c \geq 0\}$ form a
$\mathbb{C}$-basis of
$\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$.
In the $q$-deformed setting, the analogous basis is the set of ordered
monomials $\{E^{(a)}K^{s}F^{(b)}\}$ in the Lusztig divided-power
generators, with ordering enforced by the
Groebner--Shirshov resolution of the $q$-Serre
relations.

In the QOU framework this basis serves two purposes:

1. it provides the explicit coordinates for the fibre algebra
   $\mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)$ at each brane-tower level
   ([direct limit algebra](#def:direct-limit-algebra) in
   [Chapter 2](#chap:torsion));
2. it ensures flatness of the deformation $q \mapsto q_n$, so that
   the transition maps in the direct limit
   $\mathcal{A}_\infty = \varinjlim\Gamma(J^1 \mathcal{S}_n, \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2))$ are well-defined algebra homomorphisms.

<!-- FILE_START: quantum-su2.md -->
Let $q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}$ with $0<\lvert q\rvert\le 1$.  The
**quantum group** $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the
associative $\mathbb{C}$-algebra generated by $E,F,K,K^{-1}$ subject to

```tex
\begin{align}
  KK^{-1}&=K^{-1}K=1,
    \label{eq:quantum-KK}\\[3pt]
  KEK^{-1}&=q^2E,\quad KFK^{-1}=q^{-2}F,
    \label{eq:quantum-KE}\\[3pt]
  [E,F]&=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}.
    \label{eq:quantum-EF}
\end{align}
```

In the classical limit $q\to 1$, the generator $K$ reduces to
$\exp(he_3)$ and the relations recover the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$
commutation relations.
<!-- FILE_END: quantum-su2.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: q-casimir.md -->
The **$q$-Casimir element** is the distinguished central element of the
quantum group $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ (cf.
[def:quantum-su2](#def:quantum-su2)) whose eigenvalue on the spin-$j$
irreducible encodes the categorical $q$-angular momentum.

**Definition.**
Inside $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ with generators $E, F, K,
K^{-1}$ and parameter $q$ satisfying $q^2 \neq 1$ (so that
$q - q^{-1}$ is invertible), the **(normalised) $q$-Casimir element**
is

$$
  C_q  =  F E  +  \frac{q\,K + q^{-1} K^{-1} - (q + q^{-1})}
                       {(q - q^{-1})^2}.
$$

The subtraction of $(q + q^{-1})$ in the numerator is the standard
normalisation (Kassel VI.4): it ensures the eigenvalue vanishes on
the trivial representation and has a finite classical limit $q \to 1$.
Using the commutation relation
$[E, F] = (K - K^{-1})/(q - q^{-1})$, the element is equivalently

$$
  C_q  =  E F  +  \frac{q^{-1}\,K + q\,K^{-1} - (q + q^{-1})}
                        {(q - q^{-1})^2}.
$$

The element is **central**: it commutes with each generator, i.e.
$C_q E = E C_q$, $C_q F = F C_q$, and $C_q K = K C_q$.

**Action on the spin-$j$ representation.**
On the $(2j+1)$-dimensional highest-weight module $V_j$ of
$\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$, the central element acts as the
scalar

$$
  C_q \big|_{V_j}  =  [j]_q\,[j+1]_q\,\mathrm{id}_{V_j},
$$

with $[n]_q = (q^n - q^{-n})/(q - q^{-1})$ the symmetric $q$-number.
This scalar is the **$q$-Casimir eigenvalue**; the downstream block
[def:q-angular-momentum](#def:q-angular-momentum) specialises it to
$J_q^2(K) = [j]_q\,[j+1]_q$, the $q$-angular momentum of a knot.
The archimedean $q \to 1$ classical limit (over $\mathbb{R}$), where the
eigenvalue recovers $j(j+1)$, is the sibling block
[prop:q-casimir-classical-limit](#prop:q-casimir-classical-limit).

**Role in the categorical framework.**
Centrality of $C_q$ realises it as an endomorphism of the identity
functor on $\mathbf{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}_q(2))$ — equivalently, a natural
transformation $C_q \colon \mathrm{id} \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ whose
component at each simple object $V_j$ is
$[j]_q[j+1]_q \cdot \mathrm{id}_{V_j}$.  In the rigid monoidal setting
this is the categorical counterpart of the angular-momentum-squared
operator.

**Terminology.** The $q$-Casimir *element* — the quantum analogue of
Casimir's invariant for $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ — is unrelated to the
Casimir *effect*, the attractive vacuum force between closely spaced
conducting boundaries; the two share only their eponym. The QOU
treatment of the Casimir effect is the $\varepsilon$-vacuum
conjecture
[`conj:casimir-epsilon-vacuum`](#conj:casimir-epsilon-vacuum).

<!-- FILE_START: q-casimir-classical-limit.md -->
The generic $q$-Casimir eigenvalue of [def:q-casimir](#def:q-casimir),
$[j]_q\,[j+1]_q$ with $[n]_q = (q^n - q^{-n})/(q - q^{-1})$, is defined
over an abstract coefficient field. Its **classical limit** is the
archimedean specialisation to $\mathbb{R}$ at $q \to 1$.

**Proposition (classical limit of the $q$-Casimir eigenvalue).**
*Specialising the coefficient field to $\mathbb{R}$, as $q \to 1$ the
symmetric $q$-number converges to its undeformed value,*

$$
  \lim_{q \to 1} [n]_q  =  n,
$$

*and consequently the $q$-Casimir eigenvalue converges to the classical
$\mathfrak{su}(2)$ Casimir eigenvalue,*

$$
  \lim_{q \to 1} [j]_q\,[j+1]_q  =  j\,(j+1).
$$

The limits are taken over the punctured neighbourhood $q \to 1$,
$q \neq 1$, in the order topology of $\mathbb{R}$. The value $j(j+1)$
is the eigenvalue of the usual Casimir
$C = FE + \tfrac{1}{2}H(\tfrac{1}{2}H + 1)$ on the spin-$j$
representation, recovering the classical angular-momentum-squared
spectrum.

**Archimedean specialisation (§7c).** This block sits on the
archimedean side of the substrate wall: the generic algebraic
construction of the $q$-Casimir stays coefficient-field-free in
[def:q-casimir](#def:q-casimir), while the real-analytic $q \to 1$
limit — a `Tendsto` statement over $\mathbb{R}$ — is separated here and
consumes that generic eigenvalue at $K := \mathbb{R}$.
<!-- FILE_END: q-casimir-classical-limit.md -->

For any ground ring $R$ in which $(q - q^{-1})$ is invertible, the
[$q$-Casimir element](#def:q-casimir)

$$
  C_q \;=\; F E + \frac{q  K + q^{-1} K^{-1} - (q + q^{-1})}{(q - q^{-1})^2}
$$

exists as a central element of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ and
acts as $[j]_q [j+1]_q  \mathrm{id}$ on every simple module $V_j$.

Uniqueness up to scalar is
([uniqueness of the $q$-Casimir](#prop:q-casimir-unique)).


**Mathematical Novelty:** This is a standard quantum-group Casimir existence result (Kassel/Lusztig). The formalization axiomatizes this standard mathematical fact pending a complete formalization of $U_q(\mathfrak{su}_2)$, providing a rigorous structural interface without claiming a novel mathematical argument.

The existence of the Casimir element is isolated as an explicit `QCasimir.exists_axiom`, bypassing the construction of the full quantum group internally; the formal derivation obtains `Nonempty QCasimirData` from this axiom.

<!-- FILE_START: q-casimir-unique.md -->
The [$q$-Casimir element](#def:q-casimir) $C_q$ is the unique
central element of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ of the form
$FE + \phi(K, K^{-1})$ (Laurent polynomial in $K$) whose eigenvalue
on the spin-$j$ irrep is $[j]_q [j+1]_q$.  Any two such central
elements differ by a scalar multiple of the identity.


**Mathematical Novelty:** This is a standard quantum-group result regarding the Joseph-Letzter centre (Kassel VI.4.9). It is axiomatized here to support the framework's broader structural proofs; no novel mathematical argument is claimed for the uniqueness itself.

<!-- FILE_START: r-matrix-braiding-structure.md -->
The universal $R$-matrix
$\mathcal{R} \in \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2)) \hat{\otimes} \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$
([quantum SU(2)](#def:quantum-su2)) satisfies the Yang--Baxter equation

$$
  \mathcal{R}_{12}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{23}
  = 
  \mathcal{R}_{23}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{12},
$$

and the braid operator $\check{R} = P \circ \mathcal{R}$ (where $P$ is
the tensor swap) furnishes a representation of the braid group on any
tensor product of finite-dimensional
$\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$-modules, replacing the classical
twist/flip involutions ([braid twist-flip](#def:braid-twist-flip)).


**Mathematical Novelty:** The underlying R-matrix, Yang-Baxter, and braid-representation content is standard quantum-group theory. However, the specific identification of the eigenvalues $q, -q^{-1}$ (Hecke channels) provides the essential framework tie-in, connecting standard representation theory to the physical observable structures in this universe.
<!-- FILE_END: r-matrix-braiding-structure.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: r-matrix-braiding.md -->
The $R$-matrix of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$
([quantum SU(2)](#def:quantum-su2)) is the universal element

$$
  \mathcal{R} \in 
  \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))\hat{\otimes}
  \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))
$$

satisfying the Yang--Baxter equation
$\mathcal{R}_{12}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{23} =\mathcal{R}_{23}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{12}$.
Its action on a tensor product of representations gives the braid
operator $\check{R}=P\circ\mathcal{R}$ (where $P$ is the swap),
furnishing a $q$-deformed braid group representation that replaces
the classical twist/flip involutions
([braid twist-flip](#def:braid-twist-flip)).

**Theorem (D20 Pontryagin self-duality on $S_n^{\mathrm{ab}}$).**
*For every $n \geq 2$, the abelianisation
$S_n^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ of the Coxeter group $S_n$
is canonically Pontryagin self-dual: the character group
$\widehat{S_n^{\mathrm{ab}}} = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{C}^\times)$
is canonically isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2$ itself, via the
identification of the unique non-trivial character with the sign
homomorphism.*
