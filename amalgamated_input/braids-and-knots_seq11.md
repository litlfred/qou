<!-- FILE_START: enveloping-algebra.md -->
The universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the associative algebra generated by $e_1,e_2,e_3$ subject to the commutation relations. It provides the scaffolding for every finite-dimensional representation of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and is the classical limit ($q\to 1$) of the quantum group $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$.

By the PBW theorem, the monomials $\{e_1^{a}e_2^{b}e_3^{c} : a,b,c \geq 0\}$ form a $\mathbb{C}$-basis of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$. In the $q$-deformed setting, the analogous basis consists of the ordered monomials $\{E^{(a)}K^{s}F^{(b)}\}$ in the Lusztig divided-power generators, with ordering enforced by the Groebner--Shirshov resolution of the $q$-Serre relations.

This basis provides the explicit coordinates for the fibre algebra $\mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)$ at each brane-tower level. It also ensures flatness of the deformation $q \mapsto q_n$, making the transition maps in the direct limit $\mathcal{A}_\infty = \varinjlim\Gamma(J^1 \mathcal{S}_n, \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2))$ well-defined algebra homomorphisms.

<!-- FILE_START: quantum-su2.md -->
Let $q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}$ with $0<\lvert q\rvert\le 1$. The **quantum group** $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the associative $\mathbb{C}$-algebra generated by $E,F,K,K^{-1}$ subject to:
```tex
\begin{align}
  KK^{-1}&=K^{-1}K=1,
    \label{eq:quantum-KK}\\[3pt]
  KEK^{-1}&=q^2E,\quad KFK^{-1}=q^{-2}F,
    \label{eq:quantum-KE}\\[3pt]
  [E,F]&=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}.
    \label{eq:quantum-EF}
\end{align}
```
In the classical limit $q\to 1$, the generator $K$ reduces to $\exp(he_3)$ and the relations recover the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ commutation relations.
<!-- FILE_END: quantum-su2.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: q-casimir.md -->
The **$q$-Casimir element** is the distinguished central element of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ whose eigenvalue on the spin-$j$ irreducible encodes the categorical $q$-angular momentum.

Inside $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ with parameter $q^2 \neq 1$, the normalised $q$-Casimir element is:
$$
  C_q  =  F E  +  \frac{q\,K + q^{-1} K^{-1} - (q + q^{-1})}
                       {(q - q^{-1})^2}.
$$
Subtracting $(q + q^{-1})$ in the numerator ensures the eigenvalue vanishes on the trivial representation and has a finite classical limit. Equivalently:
$$
  C_q  =  E F  +  \frac{q^{-1}\,K + q\,K^{-1} - (q + q^{-1})}
                        {(q - q^{-1})^2}.
$$
The element is central: it commutes with each generator.

On the $(2j+1)$-dimensional highest-weight module $V_j$, the central element acts as the scalar:
$$
  C_q \big|_{V_j}  =  [j]_q\,[j+1]_q\,\mathrm{id}_{V_j},
$$
where $[n]_q = (q^n - q^{-n})/(q - q^{-1})$. 

Centrality makes $C_q$ an endomorphism of the identity functor on $\mathbf{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}_q(2))$, serving as the categorical counterpart of the angular-momentum-squared operator.
<!-- FILE_END: q-casimir.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: q-casimir-classical-limit.md -->
The generic $q$-Casimir eigenvalue $[j]_q\,[j+1]_q$ is defined over an abstract coefficient field. Its **classical limit** is the archimedean specialisation to $\mathbb{R}$ at $q \to 1$.

As $q \to 1$ in $\mathbb{R}$, the symmetric $q$-number converges to its undeformed value:
$$
  \lim_{q \to 1} [n]_q  =  n.
$$
Consequently, the $q$-Casimir eigenvalue converges to the classical $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ Casimir eigenvalue:
$$
  \lim_{q \to 1} [j]_q\,[j+1]_q  =  j\,(j+1).
$$
The value $j(j+1)$ recovers the classical angular-momentum-squared spectrum. This limit restricts the generic categorical construction to the archimedean side of the substrate wall.
<!-- FILE_END: q-casimir-classical-limit.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: q-casimir-exists.md -->
For any ground ring $R$ in which $(q - q^{-1})$ is invertible, the $q$-Casimir element
$$
  C_q \;=\; F E + \frac{q  K + q^{-1} K^{-1} - (q + q^{-1})}{(q - q^{-1})^2}
$$
exists as a central element of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ and acts as $[j]_q [j+1]_q  \mathrm{id}$ on every simple module $V_j$.

**Mathematical Novelty:** The existence of the Casimir element is standard quantum-group theory. Formalization isolates it as an explicit `QCasimir.exists_axiom` to bypass constructing the full quantum group internally.

<!-- FILE_START: q-casimir-unique.md -->
The $q$-Casimir element $C_q$ is the unique central element of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ of the form $FE + \phi(K, K^{-1})$ whose eigenvalue on the spin-$j$ irrep is $[j]_q [j+1]_q$. Any two such central elements differ by a scalar multiple of the identity.

**Mathematical Novelty:** This uniqueness result regarding the Joseph-Letzter centre is standard. It is axiomatized here purely to support broader structural proofs.

<!-- FILE_START: r-matrix-braiding-structure.md -->
The universal $R$-matrix $\mathcal{R} \in \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2)) \hat{\otimes} \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ satisfies the Yang--Baxter equation:
$$
  \mathcal{R}_{12}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{23}
  = 
  \mathcal{R}_{23}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{12}.
$$
The braid operator $\check{R} = P \circ \mathcal{R}$ (with $P$ the tensor swap) furnishes a representation of the braid group on any tensor product of finite-dimensional $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$-modules.

**Mathematical Novelty:** While the underlying R-matrix theory is standard, identifying the exact eigenvalues $q, -q^{-1}$ (Hecke channels) provides the essential tie-in to the physical observable structures.

<!-- FILE_START: r-matrix-braiding.md -->
The $R$-matrix of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the universal element
$$
  \mathcal{R} \in 
  \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))\hat{\otimes}
  \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))
$$
satisfying the Yang--Baxter equation $\mathcal{R}_{12}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{23} =\mathcal{R}_{23}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{12}$. Its action on a tensor product of representations gives the braid operator $\check{R}=P\circ\mathcal{R}$, furnishing a $q$-deformed braid group representation that replaces classical twist/flip involutions.

<!-- FILE_START: pontryagin-dual-coxeter-character.md -->
For every $n \geq 2$, the abelianisation $S_n^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ of the Coxeter group $S_n$ is canonically Pontryagin self-dual. The character group $\widehat{S_n^{\mathrm{ab}}} = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{C}^\times)$ is canonically isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2$ itself via the identification of the unique non-trivial character with the sign homomorphism.

This closes the duality-tracker D20 entry with a concrete `Equiv.swap 0 1` witness. It confirms that the Pontryagin duality on the abelianised level yields exactly the sign-of-a-permutation observable—the same $\mathbb{Z}/2$ classified by the bar involution.
