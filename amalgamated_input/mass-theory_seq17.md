The Lagrange resolvents of the previous section diagonalised the
$C_5$-action on the genus-$4$ base surface but did not yet provide
an operator algebra acting along the resolvent eigenlines.  We now
blow up a brane point $p$ and read the missing operators off the
resulting exceptional divisor.  The blow-up replaces $p$ by a
$\mathbb{CP}^1$ that supports a Reeb circle bundle, and vertex
operators on the resulting bundle play the role of a
state–operator correspondence on the substrate.

The section is structured as a single geometric arc.

**Static arena.**  We first fix the static geometry: the
exceptional divisor $\mathcal{S}_p \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$
([exceptional divisor](#def:exceptional-divisor)), the
Reeb bundle $\mathcal{R}_p \cong S^3 \xrightarrow{\pi_H} \mathcal{S}_p$
([Reeb bundle](#def:reeb-bundle)), the interpretive
[Reeb vector field on the exceptional divisor](#rem:reeb-exceptional-divisor),
and the
[vertex operator](#def:vertex-operator) +
[VOA–QOU dictionary](#rem:vertex-operator-dictionary)
that pins each vertex-algebra datum to the categorical structure.

**Dynamics on color coordinates.**  We then put the static arena
in motion: the
[$q$-Reeb flow on color coords](#def:q-reeb-flow-on-color-coords)
gives the explicit dynamical equation $\dot\psi = q$ on each
active sector, with two-strand localisation when only a pair of
adjacent strands is involved.

**Six canonical knot operators.**  Threading the flow with each
of the six $S_3$ braid words yields the
[six canonical knot operators](#prop:six-canonical-knot-operators)
— the identity, two adjacent transpositions $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$,
the Garside element $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$
([Garside as compound Reeb flow](#rem:garside-as-compound-flow))
and the two 3-cycles — with explicit trajectory ODEs and matching
boundary permutations on the ramified output disc.

**Boundary disc.**  At the foundry boundaries $\phi \in \{0, \pi\}$
the three colour tubes fuse into a single triply-ramified disc;
the
[ramified-disc fusion](#prop:ramified-disc-fusion)
proposition identifies the monodromy as the $S_3$ Galois action
on Lagrange resolvents, closing the loop "Reeb trajectory $\to$
boundary observable" geometrically.

**Complement is the volume.**  Removing tubular neighbourhoods of
the strand trajectories from $\mathcal{R}_p$ produces a knot
complement.  The
[Reeb-bundle complement equals volume](#prop:reeb-bundle-complement-equals-volume)
proposition — the Reeb-bundle face of the existing
mass-volume conjecture — packages the categorical Markov-trace
observable as the JSJ-graded sum
$\sum_{P_i\,\text{hyperbolic}} \rho_F(P_i) \cdot V_{\mathrm{hyp}}(P_i)$
of its complement.  The
[torus-knot zero volume via Reeb](#rem:torus-knot-zero-volume-via-reeb)
and
[hyperbolic-knot positive volume via Reeb](#rem:hyperbolic-knot-positive-volume-via-reeb)
remarks read the geometric dichotomy directly off the Reeb-flow
trajectory: pure rotational 3-cycles trace torus knots on a
Heegaard torus (Seifert-fibered complement, $V_{\mathrm{hyp}} = 0$);
Garside / Borromean / figure-eight trajectories cross between
Heegaard tori, producing a hyperbolic complement with strictly
positive volume.

The next section,
[Vertex Factorisation and the Markov Trace](#sec:vertex-factorisation-markov),
takes the vertex operator and the six trajectory dictionary as
given and shows that every operator acting on the categorical
state space factors through the exceptional divisor.

Let $(\mathbf{C}, \varTheta, G, \mathcal{S})$ be a QOU and let
$\mathcal{S}_p \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$ be the exceptional divisor
at a brane point $p$
([exceptional divisor](#def:exceptional-divisor)).  The **Reeb
bundle** at $p$ is the principal $U(1)$-bundle

$$
  \mathcal{R}_p
  = 
  \bigl(
    S^3 \xrightarrow{\pi_H} \mathcal{S}_p \cong S^2,
  \xi_q\bigr),
$$

where $\pi_H$ is the Hopf fibration and $\xi_q$ is the
[$q$-Reeb vector field](#ex:q-reeb-vector-field) generating the
$U(1)$ fibre action.  The total space $S^3$ carries the contact
structure $\ker\varTheta|_{S^3}$, and the substrate parameter $q$
([substrate parameter](#def:substrate-parameter)) determines the
connection $1$-form: the holonomy around a generator of
$\pi_1(S^1)$ is $e^{2\pi i / [2]_q}$, where $[2]_q = q + q^{-1}$
is the quantum integer.

The Reeb bundle has three roles:

1. **Base** $\mathcal{S}_p \cong S^2$: parametrises the directions of
   departure from $p$ --- the internal colour space.
2. **Fibre** $U(1)$: the Reeb direction, generated by $\xi_q$ ---
   the one axis in the colour fibre where observations commute.
3. **Total space** $S^3$: the arena where braid words in $B_n$ are
   evaluated via the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_n(R, q)$.

The Reeb vector field $\xi_q$
([$q$-Reeb vector field](#ex:q-reeb-vector-field)) is the dynamical
complement to the static topology of the Bring's surface.  While the
surface itself provides the handles (genus $4$), the Reeb vector
provides the "wind" blowing through them, and the substrate parameter $q$
([substrate parameter](#def:substrate-parameter)) sets the strength of
that wind.

**The classical limit ($q\to 1$): cones and frozen geometry.**
If the substrate parameter $q\to 1$
([substrate parameter](#def:substrate-parameter)), the
torsion coupling vanishes and the phase rotation
$w_k\mapsto\zeta^kw_k$ loses its temporal component.  The fibre
over each base point ceases to twist as one moves along a path on
$\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$: the chiral tubes are frozen in the configuration
determined by the power-sum equations.  Metrically this is a conical
limit---the curvature concentrates at the blow-up points but no
circulating torsion field smooths it out.  In terms of the Reeb
flow ([$q$-Reeb flow](#ex:q-reeb-flow)), $\xi_q\to\xi_1=\xi$ becomes
the classical Reeb field, and the Heisenberg commutator
$[\hat{X},\hat{P}]_q\to 0$
([Heisenberg commutator](#rem:heisenberg-commutator)): the Bring variety
collapses to a non-rotating algebraic surface.

**Exceptional divisor as mixing interface.**
Blowing up a spacetime defect $z_c$ where chiral sheets merge
([Bring's surface smoothness](#rem:brings-smoothness)) replaces the singular point with
an exceptional divisor $\mathcal{S}_c\cong\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})\cong S^2$.  This
sphere acts as the boundary between the macroscopic coordinates
$(x_0,x_1)$ and the internal chiral modes $(w_1,w_2,w_3)$: the
self-intersections of the chiral fibres are resolved, and the
$C_5$ phase rotation now circulates *around* the exceptional
divisor rather than passing through a singularity.

**Reeb bundle and torsion field.**
On the blown-up surface, the Reeb vector field $\xi_q$ is the
infinitesimal generator of the $U(1)$ action that rotates the chiral
fibre above the exceptional divisor.  The total space of this
$U(1)$ fibration is the **Reeb bundle**:
$U(1) \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{\pi_H} \mathcal{S}_c \cong S^2$.
The $q$-deformed Reeb flow $\phi_t^q$
([$q$-Reeb flow](#ex:q-reeb-flow)) restricted to $\mathcal{S}_c$ generates
this Hopf fibration, and the substrate
parameter $q$ controls the pitch of the rotation.  The vector
field $\xi_q$ thereby provides the "time-like" direction within the
chiral space: it is the flow that allows the spectral modes $w_k$ to
execute the spinning phase rotation
$w_k\mapsto\zeta^kw_k$.  All braiding --- Hecke generators,
Skein resolutions, crossing changes --- takes place within this
Reeb bundle
([vertex is the exceptional divisor](#prop:vertex-is-exceptional-divisor)).

**Global picture.**
The Bring's surface provides the genus-$4$ topology---four handles
through which the chiral tubes thread.  The Reeb vector field gives
the longitudinal flow along these handles.  The substrate parameter $q$
modulates the pitch of this flow: when $q\to\infty$ (deep-quantum,
Big Bang) the spinning is maximal and the Heisenberg uncertainty
$[\hat{X},\hat{P}]_q\to J$; when $q\to 1$ (heat death) the spinning
ceases, the Reeb field trivialises, and the topology reduces to a
static cone.

Let $(\mathbf{C}, \varTheta, G, \mathcal{S})$ be a QOU, let
$\mathcal{S}_p \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$ be the exceptional divisor
at a brane point $p$
([exceptional divisor](#def:exceptional-divisor)), and let
$\mathcal{R}_p$ be the Reeb bundle over $\mathcal{S}_p$
([Reeb bundle](#def:reeb-bundle)).

**The formal variable.**
In the standard VOA formalism, the formal variable $z$ is an
indeterminate in the ring of formal Laurent series.  In the QOU it
has a geometric realisation: $z$ is the affine coordinate
$z = v_1/v_2$ on the projective line
$\mathcal{S}_p \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$
([projective line](#ex:projective-line-su2)), which coincides with
the contact affine coordinate $Z = \alpha_H / \iota_\xi\alpha$
([contact affine chart](#def:contact-affine-chart)) restricted to
the fibre $\mathcal{S}_p$.  Algebraically, on the standard affine
chart $v_2 \neq 0$, $z$ generates the coordinate ring
$\mathbb{C}[z]$.

The **vertex operator algebra** of the QOU at $p$ is the triple
$(V, Y, |0\rangle)$ defined as follows.

**Graded state space.**  The $\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$-graded vector
space is the brane tower:

$$
  V =  \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} V_n,
  \qquad V_n =  A_n =  J^n(A_0),
$$

where $A_0 = \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}_q(2))$ is the coordinate ring
and $J^n$ denotes the $n$-th jet prolongation
([jet prolongation](#prop:jet-prolongation)).  The grading $n$ is the
**conformal weight**: the eigenvalue of the Reeb charge operator
$L_0 = \xi_q^{(0)}$ (the zero-mode of the $q$-Reeb vector field).

**State-operator correspondence.**  The map

$$
  Y \colon V  \longrightarrow  (\mathrm{End}V)[\![z, z^{-1}]\!]
$$

assigns to each state $a \in V_n$ a **vertex operator** (a formal
Laurent series in $z$):

$$
  Y(a, z) =  \sum_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} a_m z^{-m-1},
$$

where the **modes** $a_m \in \mathrm{End}V$ are defined by the
$R$-matrix insertion:

$$
  a_m
  = 
  \oint_{|z| = 1} Y(a, z) z^m \frac{dz}{2\pi i}
  = 
  \mathrm{tr}_M\!\bigl(
    \check{R}_{(a)} z^m
  \bigr),
$$

with $\check{R}_{(a)} = P \circ \mathcal{R}$ the braid operator
([R-matrix braiding](#rem:r-matrix-braiding)) applied to the state
$a$, and $\mathrm{tr}_M$ the Markov trace
([Jones--Markov trace](#thm:jones-markov-trace)).  The contour
integral is taken around the equator of $\mathcal{S}_p \cong S^2$, i.e.,
along a fibre of the Reeb bundle $\mathcal{R}_p$.

**Vacuum and translation.**

$$
  |0\rangle = 1 \in A_0, \qquad
  T = L_{-1} = j \colon A_n \to A_{n+1},
$$

where $j$ is the jet prolongation map.  The vacuum is the unknot
(identity element of $A_0$), and the translation operator
advances one level in the brane tower.

**OPE as Skein resolution.**  When two insertion points $z, w$
on $\mathcal{S}_p$ collide, the operator product expansion

$$
  Y(a, z) Y(b, w)
   \sim 
  \sum_{n \geq 0}
  \frac{Y(c_n, w)}{(z - w)^{n+1}}
$$

has its **leading singularity** governed by the Hecke relation
$(\sigma_i - q)(\sigma_i + q^{-1}) = 0$, which is the Skein relation

$$
  L_+ - L_- =  (q - q^{-1}) L_0.
$$

The collision $z \to w$ is two strands meeting on $\mathcal{S}_p$; the
resolution into $L_+$, $L_-$, $L_0$ is the OPE decomposition
into singular channels.  The two eigenvalues $q$ and $-q^{-1}$
of $\sigma_i$ correspond to the symmetric (bosonic) and antisymmetric
(fermionic) OPE channels respectively.

The vertex operator definition
([vertex operator](#def:vertex-operator)) establishes a precise
dictionary between the standard vertex operator algebra (VOA)
formalism \cite{frenkel2004} and QOU structures.

**Full translation table.** See
[Table: VOA $\to$ QOU dictionary](#tbl:vertex-operator-dictionary-table-data)

**Basis of the dictionary.**

The connection between VOA and quantum groups is classical: the
Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov (KZ) equation for the WZW model at level $k$
has monodromy given by the $R$-matrix of
$\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ with $q = e^{2\pi i/(k+2)}$.
The QOU makes this correspondence geometric:

1. The "worldsheet" of the CFT **is** the exceptional divisor
   $\mathcal{S}_p \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$.
2. The "target space" is the Reeb bundle $\mathcal{R}_p = S^3 \to \mathcal{S}_p$.
3. The "time direction" on the worldsheet is the Reeb flow $\xi_q$.
4. The "state space at fixed time" is the brane tower $\bigoplus A_n$.

The Reeb bundle thus plays a dual role: it is simultaneously the
arena for braiding
([vertex factors through $\mathcal{S}_p$](#prop:vertex-is-exceptional-divisor))
and the total space of the "string" whose quantisation produces the
vertex operator algebra.  The substrate parameter $q$ is the
exponential of the inverse level: $q = e^{2\pi i/(k+2)}$, linking
the algebraic deformation parameter to the geometric pitch of the
Reeb connection.

Fix the color-sector parametrisation
$\mathfrak{r}(\phi,\psi),\,\mathfrak{g}(\phi,\psi),\,\mathfrak{b}(\phi,\psi)$
on the Reeb bundle $\mathcal{R}_p$ over the exceptional divisor
([color-sector coordinates](#def:color-sector-coords)).  The
$q$-Reeb flow $\phi^q_t$
([$q$-Reeb flow](#ex:q-reeb-flow)) acts on each color sector by

$$
  \phi^q_t \cdot \mathfrak{r}(\phi,\psi)
  \;=\;  \mathfrak{r}(\phi,\,\psi + k^{(r)}\,q\,t),
$$

and analogously on the green and blue sectors with their own
rate multipliers $k^{(g)}, k^{(b)} \in \mathbb{Z}$.  The
**signed rate multiplier** $k^{(s)}$ for a sector $s$ is determined
by the braid generator being applied: $k^{(s)} = 0$ when the
sector is a spectator under that generator, $k^{(s)} = +1$ when
the sector is the leading active strand of the two-strand
subsystem, $k^{(s)} = -1$ when it is the partner strand whose
angular coordinate counter-rotates so the two-strand
centre-of-mass is preserved.  The flow parameter
$t \in [0,\pi]$ ranges over the latitude direction of
$\mathcal{R}_p$: $t = 0$ at the **south foundry**
(input boundary $\phi = 0$) and $t = \pi$ at the **north foundry**
(output boundary $\phi = \pi$).

**Color-twist accumulation.**  Substituting the explicit embedding
$\mathfrak{r}(\phi,\psi) = O + R(\sin\phi(\cos(\psi + q\phi)T_1
+ \sin(\psi + q\phi)T_2) - \cos\phi N)$
shows that the flow accumulates the color-twist factor $q t$
linearly in the angular coordinate: a strand sitting at $\psi = 0$
on the south foundry is delivered to the angular position $q\pi$
on the north foundry, an angular advance of $q\pi$ radians
(equivalently $q/2$ full turns of the $2\pi$-periodic angle).
This matches the static embedding in
[color-sector coordinates](#def:color-sector-coords).

**Composition.**  Sequential flows compose additively:
$\phi^q_{t_1} \circ \phi^q_{t_2} = \phi^q_{t_1 + t_2}$, so a braid
word $\beta = \sigma_{i_1}\sigma_{i_2}\cdots\sigma_{i_k}$ acts as a
concatenation of partial flows, each of latitude length $\pi/k$.
This composition rule is the algebraic content of the substrate
condition $[\hat X,\hat P]_q = J\hbar_q$
([Heisenberg commutator](#rem:heisenberg-commutator)).

**Two-strand localisation.**  When an adjacent-transposition
generator acts on only a pair of adjacent strands, the third
sector remains a spectator ($k = 0$, $\dot\psi = 0$) while the
two participating sectors counter-rotate ($k = +1$ for the leading
strand, $k = -1$ for the partner strand) so the two-strand
centre-of-mass is preserved.  In the Lean class
`QOU.CategoricalReeb.QReebFlowOnColorCoords`, this is captured by
the single rate-multiplier axiom
`flow_rate : flow t k ψ = ψ + k * q * t` (with corollaries
`flow_spectator`, `flow_active_pos`, `flow_active_neg` for
$k \in \{0, +1, -1\}$); the dictionary from braid generators to
per-sector multipliers is supplied by the consumer (see
[six canonical knot operators](#prop:six-canonical-knot-operators)).
The same signed-rate machine handles uniform-vortex 3-cycle
generators by setting $k = +1$ (positive 3-cycle) or $k = -1$
(negative 3-cycle) on every sector simultaneously.  This is the
geometric content of the Hecke generator $\sigma_i$ acting
nontrivially only on strands $i$ and $i+1$, and is the bridge
between the algebraic braid action and the explicit ODE on
color-sector coordinates exploited in
[six canonical knot operators](#prop:six-canonical-knot-operators).

the corresponding braid word $\beta_w \in B_3$ generates a unique

**Sketch of proof.**

1. **Identity.**  When no braid generator acts, the localisation
   rule of [`def:q-reeb-flow-on-color-coords`](#def:q-reeb-flow-on-color-coords)

   the meridian $\psi^{(s)} = \mathrm{const}$ from the south

   identity permutation.

2. **Adjacent transpositions $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$.**  By the
   two-strand localisation rule, $\sigma_1$ activates the red and
   green sectors with equal and opposite angular rates (the
   centre of mass $\psi^{(r)} + \psi^{(g)}$ is conserved by the
   $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ structure of the Hecke action; the difference
   $\psi^{(r)} - \psi^{(g)}$ accumulates a phase of $\pi$, the
   half-rotation that distinguishes the two Hecke eigenvalues
   $\pm q^{\pm 1/2}$).  At the north foundry, the red and green
   sectors have swapped angular positions; the blue spectator is
   unchanged.  This realises the transposition $(rg)$ on the

3. **Garside $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$.**  Decompose the

   each carrying one adjacent-transposition flow.  The braid
   relation $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1 = \sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$
   guarantees that the two flow sequences produce the same
   boundary permutation $(rb)$ — the non-adjacent transposition —
   despite traversing different intermediate states of the green
   sector.  Geometrically, the green tube is displaced into the
   middle and returned; the red and blue tubes are exchanged via
   the compound flow through it.  This is the explicit ODE-level
   verification of the Garside-element identity in

4. **3-cycles $\sigma_1\sigma_2$ and $\sigma_2\sigma_1$.**  These

   (positive chirality) or $-q$ (negative chirality).  The
   trajectory traces a right-handed (resp. left-handed) helix
   along a Heegaard torus inside $\mathcal{R}_p \cong S^3$.  At
   the north foundry the entire colour triple has cyclically
   permuted, realising $(rgb)$ or $(rbg)$.

The six trajectories cover the six elements of $S_3$ exhaustively
because the braid relation forces $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1 =
\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$ and $\sigma_i^2 = \mathbf{1}$
(after passing to $S_3 = B_3/\langle\sigma_i^2\rangle$).  The

correspondence $w \mapsto \beta_w$ is a bijection onto $S_3$.
This recovers the $S_3$ holonomy of $\rho: B_3 \to \mathrm{SU}(2)$

<!-- FILE_START: garside-as-compound-flow.md -->
Of the six canonical knot operators
([six canonical knot operators](#prop:six-canonical-knot-operators)),
the Garside element $\Delta_3 = \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ has the
most intricate Reeb-flow trajectory.  Two features deserve to be
unpacked.

**Three-stage compound flow.**  The Garside word factorises the
latitude interval $[0,\pi]$ into three equal sub-intervals
$[0,\pi/3]$, $[\pi/3, 2\pi/3]$, $[2\pi/3, \pi]$.  Inside each
sub-interval, exactly two of the three color sectors participate
in a localised two-strand spiral while the third remains a
spectator:

| sub-interval | active pair | spectator | flow content |
|---|---|---|---|
| $[0, \pi/3]$ | $r, g$ | $b$ | adjacent half-twist $(rg)$ |
| $[\pi/3, 2\pi/3]$ | $g, b$ | $r$ | adjacent half-twist $(gb)$ |
| $[2\pi/3, \pi]$ | $r, g$ | $b$ | adjacent half-twist $(rg)$ |

Threading the three transpositions yields the composite
permutation
$(rg)\circ(gb)\circ(rg) = (rb)$,
the non-adjacent transposition that swaps the outer pair via the
middle strand.

**Braid relation as flow-trajectory equality.**  The same
non-adjacent transposition arises from the dual decomposition
$\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$ — three sub-intervals with the roles of
the $(rg)$ and $(gb)$ stages exchanged.  At the algebraic level
the equality $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1 = \sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$
is the braid relation; geometrically the two flow sequences
produce *physically different intermediate states* of the green
sector — pushed outward in one ordering, pulled inward in the
other — but the boundary footprint is the same permutation
$(rb)$.  The braid relation thereby acquires a concrete dynamical
interpretation: it is the equality of two compound Reeb
trajectories with matching boundary data but disagreeing
interior content.

**Absence of a single closed-form flow.**  Unlike the two
3-cycles, whose Reeb trajectory is a uniform global vortex with
constant rate $\dot\psi = \pm q$ across all three sectors, the
Garside flow is *piecewise*: the angular rate $\dot\psi^{(g)}$ on
the green sector switches sign at each sub-interval boundary.
This explains the user-flagged observation that the Garside
element requires solving "PDEs" rather than a single ODE — the
solution exists, but only as a concatenation of three exact
two-strand solutions.

**Volume signature.**  Unlike the 3-cycles, whose trajectory lies
on a Heegaard torus and contributes zero hyperbolic volume

The Garside trajectory $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ is the most
intricate of the six canonical operators
([six canonical knot operators](#prop:six-canonical-knot-operators);
[Garside as compound Reeb flow](#rem:garside-as-compound-flow)).
The user-flagged concern that "we will need to write PDEs and
hope we can find an exact solution" turns out to be unwarranted:
because the underlying flow is piecewise-linear in the angular
coordinate ([$q$-Reeb flow on color coords](#def:q-reeb-flow-on-color-coords)),
the compound trajectory admits a closed-form solution by direct
composition.  This remark records that solution explicitly.

**Setup.**  Parameterise the latitude by $\phi \in [0, \pi]$ and
split into three equal sub-intervals
$I_1 = [0, \pi/3]$, $I_2 = [\pi/3, 2\pi/3]$, $I_3 = [2\pi/3, \pi]$.
The Garside word decomposes one generator per sub-interval:
$\sigma_1$ on $I_1$, $\sigma_2$ on $I_2$, $\sigma_1$ on $I_3$.
For each generator the active/spectator pattern on the colour
tubes $(r, g, b)$ is fixed:

| sub-interval | generator | $k^{(r)}$ | $k^{(g)}$ | $k^{(b)}$ |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| $I_1$ | $\sigma_1$ | $+1$ | $-1$ | $0$ |
| $I_2$ | $\sigma_2$ | $0$  | $+1$ | $-1$ |
| $I_3$ | $\sigma_1$ | $+1$ | $-1$ | $0$ |

**Closed-form trajectory.**  Let
$\psi^{(s)}_0$ denote the input angular position of sector $s$ at
the south foundry $\phi = 0$.  Applying the flow piecewise via
the additive composition rule of
[$q$-Reeb flow on color coords](#def:q-reeb-flow-on-color-coords)
yields the explicit closed form for the angular trajectory:

$$
  \psi^{(r)}(\phi) =
    \begin{cases}
      \psi^{(r)}_0 + q\phi & \phi \in I_1 \\
      \psi^{(r)}_0 + q\pi/3 & \phi \in I_2 \\
      \psi^{(r)}_0 + q\pi/3 + q(\phi - 2\pi/3) & \phi \in I_3
    \end{cases}
$$

$$
  \psi^{(g)}(\phi) =
    \begin{cases}
      \psi^{(g)}_0 - q\phi & \phi \in I_1 \\
      \psi^{(g)}_0 - q\pi/3 + q(\phi - \pi/3) & \phi \in I_2 \\
      \psi^{(g)}_0 - q(\phi - 2\pi/3) & \phi \in I_3
    \end{cases}
$$

The I$_{3}$ branch evaluates to the I$_{2}$ endpoint $\psi^{(g)}(2\pi/3) = \psi^{(g)}_0$
(green has cycled back to its starting position after the $\sigma_2$ sub-flow on $I_2$)
and then decreases at rate $-q$ on the second $\sigma_1$ sub-flow, giving
$\psi^{(g)}(\pi) = \psi^{(g)}_0 - q\pi/3$.

$$
  \psi^{(b)}(\phi) =
    \begin{cases}
      \psi^{(b)}_0 & \phi \in I_1 \\
      \psi^{(b)}_0 - q(\phi - \pi/3) & \phi \in I_2 \\
      \psi^{(b)}_0 - q\pi/3 & \phi \in I_3
    \end{cases}
$$

**North-foundry values ($\phi = \pi$).**  Evaluating at the
output boundary:

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \psi^{(r)}(\pi) &= \psi^{(r)}_0 + q\pi/3 + q\pi/3 = \psi^{(r)}_0 + 2q\pi/3, \\
  \psi^{(g)}(\pi) &= \psi^{(g)}_0 - q\pi/3 + q\pi/3 - q\pi/3 = \psi^{(g)}_0 - q\pi/3, \\
  \psi^{(b)}(\pi) &= \psi^{(b)}_0 - q\pi/3.
\end{aligned}
$$

The **net color-twist accumulated** by each sector across the
Garside trajectory is therefore
$\Delta\psi^{(r)} = +2q\pi/3$,
$\Delta\psi^{(g)} = -q\pi/3$,
$\Delta\psi^{(b)} = -q\pi/3$.
This pattern is **distinct** from the uniform vortex of the
3-cycle (all three sectors advance by the same $\pm 2q\pi/3$):
under Garside, the green sector is the displacement carrier
between the red and blue swap, picking up half the inverse twist
of red and an equal half from blue.

**Sector centre-of-mass check.**  The sum
$\Delta\psi^{(r)} + \Delta\psi^{(g)} + \Delta\psi^{(b)}
 = 2q\pi/3 - q\pi/3 - q\pi/3 = 0$,
The three-sector centre of mass is preserved across the
trajectory (no net global rotation).  This contrasts with the
3-cycle, whose centre of mass shifts by $\pm 2q\pi/3$ since all
three rates are equal-signed.  Centre-of-mass preservation is
the geometric signature of the **transposition class** of
$\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1 = (rb)$ in $S_3$, as predicted by the
boundary-permutation table.

**Reduction from PDE to piecewise ODE.**  The Reeb-flow trajectory lives on
the formal-algebraic side of the Archimedean wall.  The flow
equation $\dot\psi = k\,q$ is an ODE in the angular coordinate,
not a PDE — and within each sub-interval the rate $k$ is constant.
The "complicated PDE" the original concern envisioned was really
a *piecewise-affine ODE*, and its solution is the explicit
piecewise expressions above.  This is the same phenomenon as
[generating functions solve Reeb flow](#rem:generating-functions-solve-reeb-flow):
because the substrate dynamics live in the Habiro ring before any
archimedean realisation, the "solution" is a formal-algebraic
expression, not a transcendental analytic object.

**Implication for the JSJ verdict.**  The Garside trajectory
crosses both Heegaard tori of $\mathcal{R}_p \cong S^3$ — once
at the $I_1 \to I_2$ boundary (red-green pair gives way to
green-blue pair) and once at the $I_2 \to I_3$ boundary
(green-blue pair gives way back to red-green pair) — so the
complement is not Seifert-fibered, in agreement with
[hyperbolic-knot positive volume via Reeb](#rem:hyperbolic-knot-positive-volume-via-reeb).
This is the dynamical-side derivation of the verdict that
the topological-side computation
([Thurston volume additivity](#prop:thurston-volume-additivity))
returns as positive Bloch-Wigner sum.

**Proposition.**

([color-sector coordinates](#def:color-sector-coords)).  Then:*

1. **Fusion.** *$\widetilde{D}$ is a single connected topological
   disc with degree-$3$ ramification at the centre $\pi_H^{-1}(p)$.
   Away from that centre, $\widetilde{D}$ is the disjoint union of
   the three sector traces; at the centre the three sectors
   coalesce.*

2. **Monodromy.** *The monodromy of the branched cover
   $\widetilde{D} \to D$ around $\partial D$ is the cyclic group
   $C_3 = \langle (rgb) \rangle$.  The full Galois group of the
   regular closure is the symmetric group $S_3$ acting on the
   sheets $\{r,g,b\}$.*

3. **Comparison with the Lagrange resolvents.** *The $S_3$
   monodromy on $\widetilde{D}$ agrees with the $S_3$ action on
   the Lagrange resolvents $(w_1, w_2, w_3)$ given by
   [Lagrange resolvent transform](#prop:lagrange-resolvent-transform):
   the cyclic generator $(rgb)$ corresponds to the cyclic permutation
   $w_k \mapsto \zeta w_k$ ($\zeta$ a primitive cube root of unity)
   diagonalising the $C_5$ representation on the spectral fibre,
   and the transpositions correspond to the parity reflections
   exchanging conjugate pairs.*

**Sketch of proof.**

For (1): the three color-sector embeddings
$\mathfrak{r}(\phi,\psi),\mathfrak{g}(\phi,\psi),\mathfrak{b}(\phi,\psi)$
tile the exceptional divisor with shared meridian boundaries at
$\psi = 0, 2\pi/3, 4\pi/3$ (each sector covers an angular sector

$\phi \to \pi$ the latitude factor $\sin\phi \to 0$ collapses the

quotient topology is the cone over the three angular slices —
i.e. a disc with degree-$3$ ramification at the apex.

disjoint paths on the unramified part of $\widetilde{D}$; each

action embedded in the symmetric group $S_3$.  Going around

$(rgb)$.  Adjoining the cover's transpositions (orientation
reversals on $D$ — equivalently, the involution that exchanges
the $\pm N$ poles in the color parametrisation) yields the

For (3): the Lagrange resolvents $w_1, w_2, w_3$ are obtained

Two prototypical *non-torus* Reeb-flow trajectories carry
positive hyperbolic volume in the JSJ decomposition of their
knot complement: the Garside element
$\Delta_3 = \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ and the Borromean braid
$(\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})^3$.  Both are direct constructions in
the $q$-Reeb-flow framework
([six canonical knot operators](#prop:six-canonical-knot-operators),
[Garside as compound Reeb flow](#rem:garside-as-compound-flow)).

**Garside complement.**  The Garside compound flow swaps the
red and blue tubes via the green spectator across three latitude
sub-intervals.  In each sub-interval, the active pair spirals
around its shared axis; between sub-intervals, the active pair
switches.  The resulting trajectory does *not* lie on a single
Heegaard torus inside $\mathcal{R}_p \cong S^3$: the
non-adjacent-transposition signature
([$\rho$ as $S_3$ holonomy on Lagrange resolvents](#rem:rho-s3-holonomy-exposition))
forces the trajectory to cross between the two Heegaard tori,
The complement is *not* Seifert-fibered.  The JSJ
decomposition therefore contains a hyperbolic piece, and
[Thurston volume additivity](#prop:thurston-volume-additivity)
gives a strictly positive Bloch–Wigner sum.

**Borromean complement: the canonical example.**  The Borromean
braid $(\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})^3$ traces a three-component link
in $\mathcal{R}_p$ whose complement is the *Gieseking–Meyerhoff
arithmetic 3-manifold*.  Its hyperbolic volume is the closed
form

$$
  V_{\mathrm{hyp}}(\mathrm{Borromean}) \;=\; 8\,G
  \;\approx\; 7.327724753\ldots,
$$

where $G$ is the Catalan constant.  Geometrically the
Borromean complement decomposes into **two regular ideal
octahedra**: each ideal octahedron of shape $z = i$ has volume
$4 \cdot \mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2) = 4 G$, and the two-octahedron sum
gives $V_{\mathrm{hyp}} = 8 G$.  (Note that $G \neq v_3$:
the Catalan constant $G = \mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2) \approx 0.91596$ is the
Lobachevsky function evaluated at $\pi/2$ — the contribution of
a regular ideal octahedron's eighth — whereas
$v_3 = \mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3) \approx 1.01494$ is its value at
$\pi/3$ — the contribution of a regular ideal tetrahedron.  The
$8 v_3$ decomposition does not apply here; the Borromean complement
is *not* built from eight regular ideal tetrahedra.)

**Figure-eight as the threshold case.**  Between the torus
trefoil $T_{2,3}$ (volume zero) and the Borromean link (volume
$8G$) sits the figure-eight knot $4_1$ with volume
$V_{\mathrm{hyp}}(4_1) = 2 v_3 \approx 2.0299$
([Thurston volume additivity](#prop:thurston-volume-additivity)).
This is the *smallest* hyperbolic volume among knots in $S^3$
and is the substrate-fixing observable used to pin $q_0$ via
$\hbar_q^2 = V(4_1) / (m_\mu/m_e)$.  The corresponding Reeb-flow
trajectory is the four-crossing braid $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1}$;
it again crosses between Heegaard tori twice, producing a
hyperbolic complement of minimal volume.

**The mass-bearing regime.**  Combining with
[Reeb-bundle complement equals volume](#prop:reeb-bundle-complement-equals-volume),
the Markov-trace observable for a non-torus braid receives a
non-zero topological-mass contribution proportional to
$V_{\mathrm{hyp}}$.  In the JSJ-graded form of
[mass-volume factorization](#conj:mass-volume-factorization),
only hyperbolic pieces contribute; torus pieces are
geometrically invisible.  The Reeb-flow picture explains *why*
this geometric vanishing happens: the strands lie on a Heegaard
torus and never enter the bulk hyperbolic regime of the bundle.

Together with
[torus-knot zero volume via Reeb](#rem:torus-knot-zero-volume-via-reeb),
this remark closes the geometric dichotomy: torus trajectories
$\Rightarrow$ Seifert-fibered complement $\Rightarrow V_{\mathrm{hyp}} = 0$;
non-torus trajectories $\Rightarrow$ hyperbolic complement $\Rightarrow V_{\mathrm{hyp}} > 0$.
The trajectory pattern, read off from the Reeb-flow ODE, is what
determines the JSJ verdict in advance.
