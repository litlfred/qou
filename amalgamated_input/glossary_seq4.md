<!-- FILE_START: glossary-quantum-flatness.md -->
Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe with gauge group $G_q = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$ and connection $\nabla$ on $A$. Let $\mathrm{INN}(G_q)$ denote the inner-automorphism $2$-group of $G_q$, with $2$-form datum $B \in \Omega^2(A) \otimes \mathrm{Ad}(A)$.

The connection $\nabla$ is **quantum flat** if the curvature $F_\nabla$ equals the $2$-form datum:

$$
  F_\nabla = B.
$$

This is not an independent axiom. It is a derived consequence of the instanton condition combined with the simplicity constraint $B \in \Gamma(\Lambda^+ H^* \otimes \mathrm{Ad}(P))$:

```tex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantum-flatness-diagram}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3em]
  F_\nabla \ar[r, equal, "\text{instanton}"]
    \ar[dr, equal, "F_\nabla = B"']
  & F_\nabla^+ \ar[d, equal, "\text{simplicity}"] \\
  & B
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad\text{(quantum flatness)}
\end{equation}
```

The instanton equation $\star_q F_\nabla = F_\nabla$ forces self-dual curvature. The simplicity constraint restricts $B = F_\nabla^+$. Therefore $F_\nabla = B$. Quantum flatness encodes the condition that the $2$-group gauge theory of $\mathrm{INN}(G_q)$ admits no independent $2$-connection. The ordinary connection fully determines the $2$-form datum.
<!-- FILE_END: glossary-quantum-flatness.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: glossary-reeb-gauge-group.md -->
Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe over $R$ with maximal torus $T$ and Reeb derivation $\xi$. The **:defterm[Reeb gauge group]{#reeb-gauge-group}** is the $R$-group scheme

$$
  U_1(R)  :=  T
  =  \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau|_{\langle V \rangle})
$$

which is the maximal torus of the gauge group $G$. Over $\mathbb{C}$, this recovers $U(1)$. Over a general commutative ring $R$, it is the multiplicative group of norm-one elements in the rank-one weight lattice.

The Reeb gauge group plays six simultaneous roles, all arising from $T \subset G$:

1. **Gauge group of the Reeb fibre.** The projective line $\mathbb{P}(V) = G/T$ is a principal $U_1(R)$-bundle. The fibre over each point is a copy of $T$ acting by scalar multiplication on the weight spaces. The Reeb derivation $\xi$ is the infinitesimal generator of this $U_1(R)$-action.
2. **Weight grading.** The $U_1(R)$-action on $\Omega^k(A)$ induces the weight decomposition $\alpha = \alpha_H + \alpha_V$: $\alpha_H \in \ker(\iota_\xi)$ (weight $0$) and $\alpha_V$ (non-zero weight). The characters of $U_1(R)$ label the weights.
3. **Contact structure.** The contact affine chart $U_\xi \subset \mathbb{P}(V)$ is the complement of the $U_1(R)$-fixed locus. The contact form $\varTheta_A$ satisfies $\varTheta_A(\xi) = 1$ and $\iota_\xi d\varTheta_A = 0$, defining a connection on the $U_1(R)$-bundle.
4. **Commutative observation direction.** In the adjoint bundle $\mathrm{Ad}(J^1\mathcal{S})$, the $U_1(R)$-direction is the Cartan subalgebra. It is the one direction in the colour fibre where intents commute: $[\mathfrak{O}_1, \mathfrak{O}_2] = 0$ when both act along $\xi$. Fermionic uncertainty lives in the remaining two directions.
5. **Longitudinal backbone.** In the harmonic decomposition of $H^1(\mathcal{S}_n, \mathrm{ad}\mathcal{S}_n)$, the $U_1(R)$-modes are the longitudinal backbone: steady-state flows aligned with the Reeb direction.
6. **Arrow of time.** The Reeb flow $\phi_t^q$ is the one-parameter subgroup generated by $\xi$. Under the braiding-induced ordering $\prec_c$, forward time is the direction of this flow.

The maximal torus $T$ determines the gauge bundle, the grading, the contact geometry, the commutative sector, the harmonic backbone, and the clock.

<!-- FILE_START: glossary-categorical-transport.md -->
Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe with instanton $\nabla$ and $q$-deformed exterior derivative $d_q$. The **:defterm[categorical transport equation]{#categorical-transport}** for a section $\psi \in \tau(J^1(A))$ is

```tex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:categorical-transport}
  \hbar_q\pi_V(\nabla\psi) =  \hat{H}_q(\psi),
\end{equation}
```

where $\hat{H}_q \colon \tau(J^1(A)) \to \tau(J^1(A))$ is the $q$-Hamiltonian endomorphism $\hat{H}_q = -\tfrac{1}{2}\hbar_q\Delta_q + V_q$, with $V_q$ the potential arising from the curvature $F_\nabla$. The following diagram encodes the transport:

```tex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport-cd}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3em]
  \tau(J^1(A)) \ar[r, "\nabla"] \ar[d, "j^1"']
  & \tau(J^1(A)) \otimes \Omega^1 \ar[d, "\pi_V"] \\
  \tau(A) \ar[r, "\hat{H}_q"']
  & \tau(J^1(A))
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
```

<!-- FILE_START: glossary-categorical-q-torsion-leaf.md -->
A **:defterm[$q$-torsion leaf]{#categorical-q-torsion-leaf}** is a maximal sub-object $L \hookrightarrow A$ such that:

1. **Horizontality:** $L \subset \ker(\pi_V)$ (all forms on $L$ are horizontal).
2. **Flow invariance:** $\phi_t^q(L) = L$ for all $t$ (the $q$-Reeb flow preserves $L$).
<!-- FILE_END: glossary-categorical-q-torsion-leaf.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: glossary-descent.md -->
Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe and let $\{A_n\}_{n \geq 0}$ be the recursive brane tower. The **:refterm[descent]{#descent}** is the reverse traversal of the tower. We pass from level $n$ to level $n{-}1$ by applying the restriction morphism $r_n \colon A_n = J^1(A_{n-1}) \to A_{n-1}$. In the archimedean realisation, this restricts the connection and bundle to the base manifold $M_{n-1}$. The descent terminates in finitely many steps, bounded by $\mathrm{Krulldim} Z(\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{C}}(A))$.
