This paper develops the theory of the quantum observable universe (QOU), a rigid monoidal category that encodes the algebraic structure of quantum observables together with the geometric data needed to recover spacetime physics.

Chapter 1 opens with notational preliminaries from category theory—rigid monoidal categories, dagger structures, and representation-theoretic examples—and gives a succinct axiomatic definition of a quantum observable universe. We derive several consequences that follow from the rigidity of the symmetries: a Frobenius relation, an involutive dagger structure, a canonical volume form, and the emergence of \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) via a Tannaka–Krein reconstruction. We define the categorical action functional and derive the variational equations that govern quantum observable universes. The chapter closes with an elaboration of the categorical and analytic foundations, including jet prolongation, transport, and torsion.

Chapter 2 develops Bring’s surface as a complete worked example of a quantum observable universe, providing explicit coordinate systems for spacetime and colour-time. We construct Lagrange resolvents, colour-tube coordinates, chiral fibrations, quaternionic spectral resolvents, and the Seiberg–Witten resolution.

Chapter 3 establishes lifting and descent theorems for quantum torsion, identifying the key obstructions—both cohomological and categorical—to extending local data to global structures.

Chapter 4 explores knots and braids in the QOU framework, and provides both a fluid-dynamical and an information-theoretic perspective on the resulting invariants.

Chapter 5 examines quantum observable universes over different ground fields: the Archimedean (complex), non-Archimedean (\(\mathfrak{p}\)-adic), and finite-field cases. In the Archimedean setting we compare the QOU construction in detail with traditional mathematical physics, showing how standard objects—gauge connections, instantons, the Hodge star—arise as specialisations of the categorical framework.

Chapter 6 surveys specific experimental and observational evidence for the QOU framework, including predictions arising from the skein-theoretic mass formula and comparison with measured hadronic spectra.

Chapter 7 collects interpretive remarks and speculative extensions of the theory.

Introduction

This chapter introduces the central construction of the paper: the quantum observable universe, a rigid monoidal category encoding the algebraic structure of quantum observables. We begin with the necessary categorical preliminaries, then define the core object and give motivating examples from representation theory and quantum field theory.

Preliminary: rigid monoidal categories

📄

Definition 1.1 ∀ stated (Rigid monoidal category). \(\forall\) A monoidal category \((\mathcal{C}, \otimes, \mathbf{1})\) is a category equipped with a bifunctor \(\otimes \colon \mathcal{C} \times \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}\) (the tensor product), a unit object \(\mathbf{1}\), and natural isomorphisms expressing associativity and unitality:

\[\begin{gathered} \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em] (X \otimes Y) \otimes Z \ar[r, "\alpha_{X,Y,Z}", "\sim"'] & X \otimes (Y \otimes Z) \end{tikzcd} \\[-0.5em] \text{\small associativity} \end{gathered} \qquad \begin{gathered} \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.5em] \mathbf{1} \otimes X \ar[r, "\ell_X", "\sim"'] & X & X \otimes \mathbf{1} \ar[l, "\rho_X"', "\sim"] \end{tikzcd} \\[-0.5em] \text{\small unitality} \end{gathered}\]

The category is rigid if every object \(V\) has a dual \(V^{\vee}\) together with morphisms \(\mathrm{ev}_V \colon V \otimes V^{\vee} \to \mathbf{1}\) and \(\mathrm{coev}_V \colon \mathbf{1} \to V^{\vee} \otimes V\) such that the following snake identity diagrams commute:

\[\label{eq:snake-identities} \begin{gathered} \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2em, column sep=2.5em] V \ar[r, "\mathrm{id} \otimes \mathrm{coev}"] \ar[dr, "\mathrm{id}_V"'] & V \otimes V^{\vee} \otimes V \ar[d, "\mathrm{ev} \otimes \mathrm{id}"] \\ & V \end{tikzcd} \\[-0.5em] \text{\small left snake identity} \end{gathered} \qquad\qquad \begin{gathered} \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2em, column sep=2.5em] V^{\vee} \ar[r, "\mathrm{coev} \otimes \mathrm{id}"] \ar[dr, "\mathrm{id}_{V^{\vee}}"'] & V^{\vee} \otimes V \otimes V^{\vee} \ar[d, "\mathrm{id} \otimes \mathrm{ev}"] \\ & V^{\vee} \end{tikzcd} \\[-0.5em] \text{\small right snake identity} \end{gathered}\]

We say \(\mathcal{C}\) is \(R\)-linear (for a commutative ring \(R\)) if every hom-set is an \(R\)-module and composition is \(R\)-bilinear.

📄

Definition 1.2 ∀ stated (Snake identity). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, \otimes, \mathbf{1})\) be a monoidal category and let \(V\) be an object with dual \(V^{\vee}\), evaluation \(\mathrm{ev} \colon V \otimes V^{\vee} \to \mathbf{1}\), and coevaluation \(\mathrm{coev} \colon \mathbf{1} \to V^{\vee} \otimes V\). The snake identities (also called zig-zag identities) are the two equations:

Left snake identity.

\[(\mathrm{ev} \otimes \mathrm{id}_V) \circ (\mathrm{id}_V \otimes \mathrm{coev}) = \mathrm{id}_V.\]

More precisely, unfolding the associator and unitor isomorphisms, the composite

\[V \xrightarrow{\rho_V^{-1}} V \otimes \mathbf{1} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_V \otimes \mathrm{coev}} V \otimes (V^{\vee} \otimes V) \xrightarrow{\alpha^{-1}_{V, V^{\vee}, V}} (V \otimes V^{\vee}) \otimes V \xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev} \otimes \mathrm{id}_V} \mathbf{1} \otimes V \xrightarrow{\lambda_V} V\]

equals \(\mathrm{id}_V\).

Right snake identity. The composite

\[V^{\vee} \xrightarrow{\lambda_{V^{\vee}}^{-1}} \mathbf{1} \otimes V^{\vee} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{coev} \otimes \mathrm{id}_{V^{\vee}}} (V^{\vee} \otimes V) \otimes V^{\vee} \xrightarrow{\alpha_{V^{\vee}, V, V^{\vee}}} V^{\vee} \otimes (V \otimes V^{\vee}) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{V^{\vee}} \otimes \mathrm{ev}} V^{\vee} \otimes \mathbf{1} \xrightarrow{\rho_{V^{\vee}}} V^{\vee}\]

equals \(\mathrm{id}_{V^{\vee}}\).

These identities express the fact that \((\mathrm{ev}, \mathrm{coev})\) exhibit \(V^{\vee}\) as a right dual of \(V\) (equivalently, that the pair forms an exact pairing in the sense of CategoryTheory.ExactPairing). The terminology "snake" comes from the graphical calculus, where the composites trace out an S-shaped curve that straightens to a vertical line.

Example 1.3 ($\}mathbb{C}[z]$ and $\}mathcal{O}(\}mathrm{SU}(2))$).

The polynomial ring \(\mathbb{C}[z]\) is the algebra of functions on the complex line \(\mathbb{C}\). The category \(\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}\) of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces is the simplest \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear rigid monoidal category: the tensor product is \(V \otimes W\), the unit is \(\mathbf{1} = \mathbb{C}\), and the dual is \(V^{\vee} = \mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathbb{C})\) with \(\mathrm{ev}(v \otimes f) = f(v)\).

Similarly, the coordinate ring \(\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))\) is the algebra of polynomial functions on the group \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\), generated by the matrix entries \(\alpha, \beta, \bar\alpha, \bar\beta\) with \(\alpha\bar\alpha + \beta\bar\beta = 1\). The category \(\mathcal{C} = \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))\) of finite-dimensional representations is \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear rigid monoidal. Its simple objects are the spin-\(j\) representations \(V_j\) (\(j = 0, \tfrac12, 1, \ldots\), \(\dim V_j = 2j+1\)). The fundamental object is \(V = V_{1/2} \cong \mathbb{C}^2\).

Definition 1.4 ∀ stated (Quantum Universe). \(\forall\)

Let \(R\) be a commutative ring. A quantum universe over \(R\) is a triple \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) where:

  1. \((\mathcal{C}, \otimes, \mathbf{1})\) is an \(R\)-linear rigid monoidal category (rigid monoidal category);

  2. \(A\) is a Frobenius–Hopf object in \(\mathcal{C}\): an object equipped with morphisms \[\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1.5em, column sep=2.5em] A \otimes A \ar[r, "\mu"] & A \ar[r, "\lambda"] & \mathbf{1} & {\scriptstyle\text{(multiplication, Frobenius form)}} \\ \mathbf{1} \ar[r, "\eta"] & A \ar[r, "\varepsilon"] & \mathbf{1} & {\scriptstyle\text{(unit, counit)}} \\ A \ar[r, "\Delta"] & A \otimes A & A \ar[l, "S"'] & {\scriptstyle\text{(comultiplication, antipode)}} \end{tikzcd}\] — multiplication \(\mu\), unit \(\eta\), comultiplication \(\Delta\), counit \(\varepsilon\), antipode \(S\), and Frobenius form \(\lambda\) — satisfying: (H) \((A, \mu, \eta, \Delta, \varepsilon, S)\) is a Hopf algebra object in \(\mathcal{C}\); (F) the Frobenius pairing \(\lambda \circ \mu \colon A \otimes A \to \mathbf{1}\) is non-degenerate, inducing an isomorphism \(A \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} A^{\vee}\);

  3. \(\tau \colon \mathcal{C} \to R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}\) is a fibre functor: an exact, faithful, \(R\)-linear monoidal functor;

  4. \(A\) admits a differential calculus: the module of K"ahler differentials \(\Omega^1(A)\) exists in \(\mathcal{C}\).

📄

Definition 1.5 ∀ stated (Frobenius pairing). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum universe with multiplication \(\mu \colon A \otimes A \to A\) and Frobenius form \(\lambda \colon A \to \mathbf{1}\). The Frobenius pairing is the composite

\[\label{eq:frobenius-pairing} \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.5em] A \otimes A \ar[r, "\mu"] & A \ar[r, "\lambda"] & \mathbf{1} \end{tikzcd}\]

We say the Frobenius pairing is non-degenerate when this composite induces an isomorphism \(A \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} A^{\vee}\) to the dual object. Equivalently, the snake identities hold: there exists a coevaluation \(\tilde{\eta} \colon \mathbf{1} \to A \otimes A\) such that the two composites

\[A \xrightarrow{\rho^{-1}} A \otimes \mathbf{1} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id} \otimes \tilde{\eta}} A \otimes A \otimes A \xrightarrow{\alpha^{-1}} (A \otimes A) \otimes A \xrightarrow{(\lambda \circ \mu) \otimes \mathrm{id}} \mathbf{1} \otimes A \xrightarrow{\lambda_A} A\]

and its mirror are both equal to \(\mathrm{id}_A\).

Example 1.6 ✗ no Lean ($\}mathrm{SU}(2)$ as a quantum universe).

Continuing rigid monoidal Vect, we verify each axiom of quantum universe for \(G = \mathrm{SU}(2)\).

(i) Category. \(\mathcal{C} = \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))\), the category of finite-dimensional complex representations. Objects are vector spaces \(V\) with a continuous \(G\)-action \(\rho_V \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)\); morphisms are \(G\)-equivariant linear maps. This is an \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear rigid monoidal category (rigid monoidal Vect). The \(G\)-action on tensor products is diagonal: \(g \cdot (v \otimes w) = (g \cdot v) \otimes (g \cdot w)\); on duals it is the contragredient: \((g \cdot f)(v) = f(g^{-1} \cdot v)\).

(ii) Frobenius–Hopf object. \(A = \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))\), the coordinate ring of polynomial functions on the group. Its Hopf structure encodes the group operations:

| Operation | Formula | Meaning | |———–|———|———| | Multiplication \(\mu(f \otimes g)\) | \(f \cdot g\) | pointwise product of functions | | Unit \(\eta(1)\) | \(1_A\) | constant function \(1\) | | Comultiplication \(\Delta(f)(x, y)\) | \(f(xy)\) | pullback of group multiplication | | Counit \(\varepsilon(f)\) | \(f(e)\) | evaluation at the identity | | Antipode \(S(f)(x)\) | \(f(x^{-1})\) | pullback of inversion | | Frobenius form \(\lambda(f)\) | \(\int_G f\, d\mu\) | Haar integration on \(G\) |

The Frobenius pairing \(\langle f, g \rangle = \lambda(\mu(f \otimes g)) = \int_G f g\, d\mu\) is positive definite (faithfulness of the Haar integral).

(iii) Fibre functor. \(\tau \colon \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2)) \to \mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}\) is the forgetful functor: \(\tau(V, \rho) = V\) (forget the \(G\)-action, keep the vector space). It is exact (short exact sequences of representations split), faithful (a non-zero equivariant map is non-zero as a linear map), and monoidal (\(\tau(V \otimes W) = \tau(V) \otimes \tau(W)\)). By Tannaka–Krein duality, \(G\) can be recovered from \(\tau\): \(G \cong \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)\), the group of monoidal natural automorphisms of the fibre functor.

(iv) Differential calculus. The left-invariant calculus on \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) provides \(\Omega^1(A) = \mathrm{span}\{\omega_1, \omega_2, \omega_3\}\), the left-invariant \(1\)-forms dual to the Lie algebra basis \(\{e_1, e_2, e_3\}\) of \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\) (with \([e_a, e_b] = \epsilon_{abc}\, e_c\); SU(2) basis). The universal derivation \(d \colon A \to \Omega^1(A)\) acts by \(df = \sum_i (\partial_i f)\, \omega_i\), recovering the Lie derivative along each generator.

Definition 1.7 ∀ stated (Higher differential forms). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum universe with differential calculus \(\Omega^1(A)\) (quantum universe). The higher differential forms are the graded \(A\)-bimodule

\[\Omega^k(A) \;=\; \bigwedge\nolimits_A^k \Omega^1(A), \qquad k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\]

(exterior powers of the \(A\)-bimodule \(\Omega^1(A)\) in \(\mathcal{C}\)), with \(\Omega^0(A) = A\). For an object \(V\) in \(\mathcal{C}\), \(\Lambda^k V\) denotes the \(k\)-th exterior power of \(V\) in the sense of rigid monoidal categories. The differential \(d \colon \Omega^k(A) \to \Omega^{k+1}(A)\) extends the universal derivation by the graded Leibniz rule, and satisfies \(d^2 = 0\).

Definition 1.9 ∀ stated (Quantum Observable Universe). \(\forall\)

A quantum universe \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) over \(R\) (quantum universe) is observable if the forgetful functor \(\tau\) (Example) is faithful and the following conditions hold:

  1. (non-degenerate integration) the Frobenius form \(\int_A := \lambda \colon A \to \mathbf{1}\) induces a non-degenerate pairing \((a, b) \mapsto \int_A ab\) (Frobenius pairing), giving an isomorphism \(A \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} A^{\vee}\);

  2. (observability) \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1},\, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0\), where \(\mathrm{Ad}(A)\) is the adjoint object (\(A\) acted on by conjugation); that is, every morphism \(\mathbf{1} \to \mathrm{Ad}(A)\) is zero: \[\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.5em] \mathbf{1} \ar[r, "0"] & \mathrm{Ad}(A) \end{tikzcd} \qquad\text{(observability)}\]

  3. (measurability / anomaly-free) the primary Chern classes of the adjoint object vanish in the cohomology induced by the forgetful projection \(\tau\): \[c_1(\mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad c_2(\mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0.\] The first condition precludes hidden symmetries; the second ensures that the volume form acquires no topological phases.

  4. (categorical volume constraint) let \(V\) denote the fundamental object of \(\mathcal{C}\) (Remark); there exists a non-degenerate epimorphism \(\epsilon\) preserved by the rigid dualities, making the following diagram commute for every \(g \in \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)\): \[\label{eq:categorical-volume-constraint} \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3em] \Lambda^2 V \ar[r, "\Lambda^2 g_V"] \ar[d, "\epsilon"', two heads] & \Lambda^2 V \ar[d, "\epsilon", two heads] \\ \mathbf{1} \ar[r, "g_{\mathbf{1}} = \mathrm{id}"'] & \mathbf{1} \end{tikzcd} \qquad\text{(volume constraint)}\]

Definition 1.11 ∀ stated (Maximal torus and weight subcategory). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a (quantum observable universe) with fundamental object \(V\) (spin labels). The weight subcategory \(\langle V \rangle \subset \mathcal{C}\) is the full subcategory whose objects are direct sums of weight spaces of \(V\) (i.e. simultaneous eigenspaces of the Cartan subalgebra action). The maximal torus of the gauge group is

\[T = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau|_{\langle V \rangle}),\]

the sub-group scheme of \(G = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)\) (SU(2) emergence) consisting of those natural automorphisms that act diagonally on each weight space of \(V\). Equivalently, \(T\) is the largest subgroup of \(G\) for which every object in \(\langle V \rangle\) decomposes into one-dimensional \(T\)-eigenspaces.

Definition 1.13 ∀ stated (Weight decomposition). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a (quantum observable universe) with maximal torus \(T\) (maximal torus) and higher differential forms \(\Omega^k(A)\) (higher forms). The \(T\)-action on \(\Omega^k(A)\) induces a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-grading by weights. Denoting by \(\pi_H, \pi_V\) the projections onto the weight-\(0\) and non-zero-weight summands, every \(\alpha \in \Omega^k(A)\) decomposes uniquely:

\[\label{eq:weight-decomposition-cd} \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2em, column sep=3em] & \Omega^k(A) \ar[dl, "\pi_H"'] \ar[dr, "\pi_V"] & \\ \Omega^k_H(A) \ar[rr, hook, "\oplus"'] & & \Omega^k_V(A) \end{tikzcd} \qquad \alpha = \alpha_H + \alpha_V.\]

Here \(\alpha_H = \pi_H(\alpha)\) is the horizontal component (weight-\(0\) under \(T\)) and \(\alpha_V = \pi_V(\alpha)\) is the vertical component (non-zero weight). A form is purely horizontal if and only if \(\alpha_V = 0\).

Proposition 1.14 ∀ stated (Frobenius relation). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a (quantum universe). Denote by \(\Delta_\mu\) the comultiplication on \(A\) dual to the multiplication \(\mu\) under the Frobenius pairing. Then the following diagram commutes (the Frobenius relation):

\[\label{eq:frobenius-relation} \begin{gathered} \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3.5em] A \otimes A \ar[r, "\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_\mu"] \ar[d, "\mu"'] & A \otimes A \otimes A \ar[d, "\mu \otimes \mathrm{id}"] \\ A \ar[r, "\Delta_\mu"'] & A \otimes A \end{tikzcd} \\[-0.5em] \text{\small Frobenius relation: } (\mu \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_\mu) = \Delta_\mu \circ \mu \end{gathered}\]

That is, sliding a multiplication past a comultiplication (or vice versa) yields the same result as composing them: \((\mu \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_\mu) = \Delta_\mu \circ \mu\).

Proof of Frobenius relation.

Non-degeneracy of the pairing \(\langle a, b \rangle = \lambda(\mu(a \otimes b))\) identifies \(A \cong A^{\vee}\). Under this identification, \(\Delta_\mu\) is the transpose of \(\mu\). The Frobenius relation then follows from the associativity of \(\mu\) by dualising one leg of the multiplication. ◻

Definition 1.16 ∀ stated (Involutive structure ($\}ast$-structure)). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, \otimes, \mathbf{1})\) be an \(R\)-linear rigid monoidal category. An involutive structure (also called a \(\ast\)-structure or dagger structure) on \(\mathcal{C}\) is a contravariant endofunctor \(\dagger \colon \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{C}\) satisfying:

  1. Identity on objects: \(X^{\dagger} = X\) for every object \(X\).

  2. Involutivity: \(f^{\dagger\dagger} = f\) for every morphism \(f\), depicted: \[\label{eq:involutivity-cd} \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em] X \ar[r, "f"] \ar[rr, bend left=30, "f^{\dagger\dagger} = f"] & Y \ar[r, "f^{\dagger}"{description}] & X \end{tikzcd}\]

  3. Monoidal compatibility: \((\mathrm{id}_X \otimes f)^{\dagger} = \mathrm{id}_X \otimes f^{\dagger}\), i.e. the following square commutes: \[\label{eq:monoidal-dagger-cd} \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3em] X \otimes Y \ar[r, "\mathrm{id} \otimes f"] \ar[d, "(\mathrm{id} \otimes f)^{\dagger}"'] & X \otimes Z \ar[d, "\mathrm{id} \otimes f^{\dagger}"] \\ X \otimes Z \ar[r, equal] & X \otimes Z \end{tikzcd}\]

  4. Contravariance: \((g \circ f)^{\dagger} = f^{\dagger} \circ g^{\dagger}\): \[\label{eq:dagger-contravariance} \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em] X \ar[r, "f"] & Y \ar[r, "g"] & Z \end{tikzcd} \quad\xmapsto{\;\dagger\;}\quad \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em] Z \ar[r, "g^{\dagger}"] & Y \ar[r, "f^{\dagger}"] & X \end{tikzcd} \qquad\text{(contravariance)}\]

For the Hopf algebra \(A\) in a quantum universe, the antipode \(S\) provides a natural involutive structure on the representation category via \(f^{\dagger} = \lambda \circ \mu(S(f) \otimes -)\). In the \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) example, this is the Hilbert-space adjoint (dagger on SU(2)).

Theorem 1.20 ∀ stated (Emergence of the gauge group). \(\forall\)

The gauge group \(G\) is defined via Tannaka–Krein duality as the group scheme of tensor-preserving natural automorphisms of the fibre functor \(\tau\). An element \(g \in G\) assigns an invertible linear map \(g_X \colon \tau(X) \to \tau(X)\) to every object \(X \in \mathcal{C}\).

Because \(g\) is a natural transformation, it commutes with all morphisms in \(\mathcal{C}\), including the volume constraint \(\epsilon \colon \Lambda^2 V \to \mathbf{1}\) [eq:categorical-volume-constraint]. Applying \(\tau\) yields the commutative diagram of \(R\)-modules

\[\label{eq:su2-emergence-diagram} \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=3.5em] \Lambda^2 \tau(V) \ar[r, "\Lambda^2 g_V"] \ar[d, "\tau(\epsilon)"'] & \Lambda^2 \tau(V) \ar[d, "\tau(\epsilon)"] \\ \tau(\mathbf{1}) \ar[r, "g_{\mathbf{1}} = \mathrm{id}"'] & \tau(\mathbf{1}) \end{tikzcd}\]

The fundamental object \(V\) has categorical dimension \(2\), so \(\tau(V)\) is a rank-\(2\) free \(R\)-module and \(\Lambda^2 \tau(V)\) has rank \(1\). The induced operator \(\Lambda^2 g_V\) acts on this rank-\(1\) module as scalar multiplication by \(\det(g_V)\). Following diagram [eq:su2-emergence-diagram] from the top-left to the bottom-right:

$$\tau(\epsilon) \circ \Lambda^2 g_V

g_{\mathbf{1}} \circ \tau(\epsilon)

\tau(\epsilon).$$

Since \(\tau(\epsilon)\) is non-degenerate (hence non-zero), this forces

\[\label{eq:det-one} \det(g_V) \;=\; 1,\]

restricting the natural automorphisms from \(\mathrm{GL}(2)\) to the special subgroup \(\mathrm{SL}(2)\).

The QOU non-degeneracy axiom ensures the Frobenius form \(\lambda \colon A \to \mathbf{1}\) is non-degenerate, inducing the Frobenius self-duality \(A \cong A^{\vee}\) (fibre adjoint involution). Define a dagger on morphisms by \(f^{\dagger} = \lambda \circ \mu(S(f) \otimes -)\), where \(S\) is the antipode. Non-degeneracy of \(\lambda\) and faithfulness of \(\tau\) imply \(f^{\dagger} \circ f = 0 \Rightarrow f = 0\), so this is a positive-definite dagger structure (positive definite dagger). Every tensor automorphism \(g \in G\) preserves the dagger (it commutes with \(S\) and \(\lambda\)), forcing \(g_V^{-1} = g_V^{\dagger}\) and locking the group into its unitary real form \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\). ◻

Proposition 1.42 ∀ stated (Variational equations of the categorical action). \(\forall\)

Let \(\mathcal{S}_q\) be the (categorical action). The three variational equations are:

  1. **Gravity (vary \(\lambda\)): \(\delta\mathcal{S}_q / \delta\lambda = 0\) yields the categorical Einstein equation: \[R_\lambda - \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}(R_\lambda)\,\mathrm{id}_A \;=\; T_q[\nabla, \psi],\] where \(T_q\) is the stress-energy endomorphism constructed from the gauge and matter terms.

Proof of Variational equations of the categorical action.

Each equation is obtained by computing \(\delta\mathcal{S}_q = 0\) with respect to the indicated variable, using the non-degeneracy of \(\lambda\).

Item (1): Vary \(\lambda\). The gravity term \(\mathrm{tr}(q \cdot R_\lambda)\) depends on \(\lambda\) through the Frobenius–Ricci endomorphism \(R_\lambda\). The trace subtraction ensures \(\mathrm{tr}(T_q) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}(R_\lambda)\) (the contracted Bianchi identity).

Item (2): Vary \(\nabla\). The gauge term \(\lambda \circ \mathrm{tr}_A(F_\nabla \wedge_q \star_q F_\nabla)\) depends on \(\nabla\) through \(F_\nabla = \nabla^2\). A standard computation using \(\delta F_\nabla = \nabla(\delta\nabla)\) and integration by parts (via non-degeneracy of \(\lambda\)) yields \(\nabla \star_q F_\nabla + q \cdot [F_\nabla,\, \star_q F_\nabla] = J_\psi\). The commutator term \(q \cdot [F_\nabla, \star_q F_\nabla]\) arises from the non-commutativity of the \(q\)-deformed wedge product; it vanishes when \(q = 1\) (the abelian/classical limit).

Item (3): Vary \(\psi\). The matter term \(\lambda(\psi^* \cdot D_\nabla\,\psi)\) is linear in \(\psi\). Varying \(\psi \mapsto \psi + \delta\psi\) and using \((\delta\psi)^* \cdot D_\nabla\,\psi = 0\) for all \(\delta\psi\) gives \(D_\nabla\,\psi = 0\) by non-degeneracy of \(\lambda\) and the dagger structure (positive definite dagger). ◻

Proof of From Tannakian emergence to 2-group quantum flatness.

Step 2: The determinant-one condition descends to the 2-form datum. A \(2\)-connection \((A, B)\) for the principal \(2\)-bundle of \(\mathrm{INN}(G_q)\) takes values in \(\mathfrak{g}_q = \mathfrak{su}_q(2)\). Proposition 1.58 ∀ stated (d_q^2 does not vanish). \(\forall\)

Let \(d_q\) be the \(q\)-deformed exterior derivative (\(q\)-exterior derivative). For constant \(q > 1\), the operator \(d_q^2\) is given by

\[d_q^2\,\alpha \;=\; q^{-1}(1 - q^{-1})\,d\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\,\alpha.\]

In particular, \(d_q^2 = 0\) if and only if \(q = 1\) (classical case) or \(\alpha\) lies in the kernel of \(\mathcal{L}_\xi\) (Reeb-invariant forms). On horizontal \(\mathcal{L}_\xi\)-invariant forms, \(d_q\) does define a cochain complex.

Proof of d_q^2 does not vanish.

Write \(a = q^{-1}\) and \(b = 1 - q^{-1}\), so \(a + b = 1\). Then

\[\begin{aligned} d_q^2\,\alpha &= d_q\bigl(a\,d\alpha + b\,\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha\bigr) \\ &= a^2\,d^2\alpha + ab\,d(\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha) + ab\,\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\,d\alpha + b^2\,\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi(\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha). \end{aligned}\]

The first term vanishes since \(d^2 = 0\). The second expands as \(ab\,d\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha - ab\,\theta \wedge d\mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha\). The third term cancels the second’s negative part because \([d,\, \mathcal{L}_\xi] = 0\) implies \(\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\,d\alpha = \theta \wedge d\,\mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha\). For the fourth term, the Reeb field satisfies \(\mathcal{L}_\xi\theta = 0\) (since \(\iota_\xi d\theta = 0\) and \(\theta(\xi)=1\)), so \(\mathcal{L}_\xi(\theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha) = \theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi^2\alpha\); the factor \(\theta \wedge \theta = 0\) in \(b^2\,\theta \wedge \theta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi^2\alpha\) kills this term. \[\mathrm{Jac}_q(U,V,W) \;=\; -q^{-1}(1-q^{-1})\!\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}} d\theta(V,W)\,\mathcal{L}_\xi U,\]

Jet Prolongation

Proposition 3.1 ∀ stated (Jet Prolongation). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum observable universe over a ring \(R\) (quantum observable universe). Define the jet object

\[J^1(A) \;:=\; A \otimes \Omega^1(A),\]

Proof of Jet Prolongation.

The proof proceeds in the rigid monoidal category underlying the quantum observable universe. Let \(A\) denote the Frobenius–Hopf object representing the gauge-bundle data of \((M,G,P,E)\).

Step 1: Categorical jet object. The first jet bundle \(J^1 E\) is represented categorically by the object \(A \otimes \Omega^1(A)\), where \(\Omega^1(A) = \ker(m\colon A\otimes A \to A)\) is the kernel of the multiplication (the bimodule of Kähler differentials, i.e. the universal first-order differential calculus over \(A\)). Because \(A\) is Frobenius, the non-degenerate pairing \(\varepsilon \circ m \colon A \otimes A \to \mathbf{1}\) exhibits \(\Omega^1(A)\) as a direct summand of \(A \otimes A\), hence \(J^1(A) := A \otimes \Omega^1(A)\) is again an object of the ambient category.

Step 2: Inherited Frobenius–Hopf structure. The Frobenius relation \((\mathrm{Id} \otimes m) \circ (\Delta \otimes \mathrm{Id}) = \Delta \circ m = (m \otimes \mathrm{Id}) \circ (\mathrm{Id} \otimes \Delta)\) ensures that the comultiplication \(\Delta\colon A \to A \otimes A\) restricts to a well-defined comultiplication \(\Delta'\colon J^1(A) \to J^1(A) \otimes J^1(A)\), and the counit \(\varepsilon\colon A \to \mathbf{1}\) induces a counit \(\varepsilon'\) on \(J^1(A)\). The Hopf antipode \(S\) extends to \(J^1(A)\) by functoriality of the Kähler differential construction. Thus \(J^1(A)\) is a Frobenius–Hopf object.

This step relies on the strong multiplicativity condition: the co-multiplication must be compatible with the deformed product at every filtration degree. Over fields of positive characteristic, the rigorous proof that this compatibility survives the deformation is the main theorem of [fred2005], which constructs \(\mathrm{Dist}(G)\) as a Gröbner–Shirshov deformation of the commutative \(\mathrm{Dist}(H)\) and verifies the Frobenius relation at each stage (see also skew poisson integration in Chapter).

Step 3: Non-degeneracy and vanishing. The non-degeneracy of the Frobenius form on \(A\) (the pairing \(\varepsilon \circ m\) is a perfect pairing) implies non-degeneracy of the induced form on \(J^1(A)\), since \(\Omega^1(A)\) is a direct summand. For the vanishing condition, \(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0\) implies \(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(J^1(A))) = 0\): the adjoint representation of \(J^1(A)\) contains \(\mathrm{Ad}(A)\) as a subobject, and any invariant in \(\mathrm{Ad}(J^1(A))\) would project to a non-zero invariant in \(\mathrm{Ad}(A)\), contradicting the hypothesis.

Step 4: Geometric realisation. Applying the fibre functor \(\tau\colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathrm{Vect}_k\) (where \(\mathcal{C}\) is the rigid monoidal category of the quantum observable universe) recovers the geometric data: \(\tau(J^1(A))\) is the space of sections of the first jet bundle \(J^1 E\), the frame bundle \(P'\) and orthonormal frame bundle \(E'\) are the geometric counterparts of the Frobenius–Hopf structure on \(J^1(A)\), and the connection \(A'\) is the image under \(\tau\) of the comultiplication \(\Delta'\). Its uniqueness follows from the irreducibility of \(A'\), which is guaranteed by the inherited vanishing condition of Step 3. ◻

Definition 3.2 ∀ stated (Recursive Brane Tower). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum observable universe over a ring \(R\) (quantum observable universe). Set \(A_0 = A\). Inductively, define \(A_{n+1} = J^1(A_n)\) as the (jet prolongation) of the Frobenius–Hopf object \(A_n\).

The brane relation is the following recursive structure:

  1. Each jet object \(A_n\) serves as the base object for the next level; \(A_n\) is the brane of \(A_{n+1}\).

  2. At each level, the comultiplication \(\Delta_n\) on \(A_n\) encodes the \(n\)-th level connection, inherited from the Frobenius–Hopf structure by jet prolongation.

The recursive brane tower is the sequence \(A_0 \hookrightarrow A_1 \hookrightarrow A_2 \hookrightarrow \cdots\) in \(\mathcal{C}\), where each embedding is the canonical inclusion \(A_n \hookrightarrow J^1(A_n) = A_{n+1}\) via the unit section.

Cosimplicial Crossed Groupoid Structure

The recursive brane tower carries a higher-categorical structure that organises the transitions between levels into a cosimplicial crossed groupoid. The key observation is that Yekutieli’s framework for central extensions of gerbes [yekutieli2010] furnishes exactly the triples \((x, g, a)\) needed to define the face and degeneracy maps.

Definition 3.3 ∀ stated (Yekutieli triple mapping). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum observable universe and let \(\{A_n\}_{n \geq 0}\) be its (recursive brane tower); let \(q\) denote the substrate parameter. Let \(\{U_i\}\) be a covering of the underlying site \(\mathcal{S}\) trivialising every level of the tower. The Yekutieli triple at level \(n\) over an intersection \(U_i \cap U_j\) is the datum \((x_{ij}^{(n)},\, g_{ij}^{(n)},\, a_{ij}^{(n)})\) where:

  1. Objects \(x_i^{(n)} \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathfrak{G}_n|_{U_i})\) are local trivialisations of the Frobenius–Hopf object \(A_n\).

  2. \(1\)-morphisms \(g_{ij}^{(n)} \colon x_i^{(n)} \to x_j^{(n)}\) are the transition automorphisms in \(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n|_{U_i \cap U_j})\), encoding the patching data of the comultiplication \(\Delta_n\).

  3. \(2\)-morphisms \(a_{ij}^{(n)} \colon g_{ij}^{(n)} \Rightarrow g_{ij}^{(n+1)}\) are the deformation parameters living in the band \(\mathcal{B}\), implementing the lift of transition automorphisms from level \(n\) to level \(n{+}1\).

The assignment \(n \mapsto (x^{(n)}, g^{(n)}, a^{(n)})\) is functorial: the (jet prolongation) sends the triple at level \(n\) to the triple at level \(n{+}1\), and the descent homomorphism \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}\) (descent) reverses this assignment. In the archimedean realisation, \(g_{ij}^{(n)} \in \Gamma(U_i \cap U_j,\, \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2))\) are gauge transition functions and \(a_{ij}^{(n)} \in \mathcal{O}_M^{\times}\).

Proposition 3.4 ∀ stated (Cosimplicial crossed groupoid). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum observable universe and let \(\{A_n\}_{n \geq 0}\) be its (recursive brane tower); let \(q\) denote the substrate parameter. Equipped with the Yekutieli triples \((x^{(n)}, g^{(n)}, a^{(n)})\) of Yekutieli triple. Then the assignment

\[[n] \;\longmapsto\; \mathfrak{G}_n\]

from the simplex category \(\Delta\) to the \(2\)-category of gerbes on \(M\) extends to a cosimplicial crossed groupoid: a cosimplicial object in the category of crossed groupoids, where

  1. the coface maps \(\delta^i \colon \mathfrak{G}_{n} \to \mathfrak{G}_{n+1}\) are given by the jet prolongation acting on objects (inserting the \(i\)-th degeneracy in the Cech nerve),

  2. the codegeneracy maps \(\sigma^j \colon \mathfrak{G}_{n+1} \to \mathfrak{G}_{n}\) are given by the descent homomorphism \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}\) (collapsing the \(j\)-th face),

  3. the crossed structure is furnished by the conjugation action of the \(1\)-morphisms \(g_{ij}^{(n)}\) on the \(2\)-morphisms \(a_{ij}^{(n)}\) via the adjoint representation of \(\mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)\).

Proof of Cosimplicial crossed groupoid.

We verify the cosimplicial identities and the crossed-module axioms.

Step 1: Cosimplicial identities. The coface maps are composites of the jet prolongation with the Cech face maps \(\partial^i\) of the cover \(\{U_i\}\). The codegeneracy maps are composites of the descent homomorphism with the Cech degeneracy maps \(s^j\). The identities \(\delta^j \circ \delta^i = \delta^i \circ \delta^{j-1}\) for \(i < j\) and \(\sigma^j \circ \sigma^i = \sigma^i \circ \sigma^{j+1}\) for \(i \leq j\) follow from the functoriality of jet prolongation and descent: the ascent \(\ell_n\) is a natural transformation from the identity functor on \(\mathfrak{G}_n\) to the restriction of \(\mathfrak{G}_{n+1}\), and the descent \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}\) is its left adjoint. The mixed identity \(\sigma^j \circ \delta^i\) reduces to three cases (\(i < j\), \(i = j\) or \(i = j + 1\), \(i > j + 1\)), each verified using the retraction property \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \circ \ell_n = \mathrm{id}\) (descent).

Step 2: Crossed-module structure. At each level \(n\), the pair \((\mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2),\, \mathcal{O}_M^{\times})\) carries the structure of a crossed module in the sense of [yekutieli2010]: the boundary map \(\partial \colon \mathcal{O}_M^{\times} \to \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)\) sends a unit \(a \in \mathcal{O}_M^{\times}\) to the central element \(a \cdot \mathbf{1} \in \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)\) (the central extension), and the action of \(g \in \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)\) on \(a \in \mathcal{O}_M^{\times}\) is conjugation: \(g \cdot a = g\, a\, g^{-1}\). This action is well-defined because \(\mathcal{O}_M^{\times}\) is central in the gerbe band; the Peiffer identity \(\partial(a) \cdot b = a\, b\, a^{-1}\) is automatic for a central extension.

Step 3: Compatibility. The coface and codegeneracy maps respect the crossed-module structure because the jet prolongation preserves the Hopf-algebra multiplication (jet prolongation, Step 2) and the descent homomorphism is a \(*\)-homomorphism (descent, Step 4). Hence the conjugation action of \(g_{ij}^{(n)}\) on \(a_{ij}^{(n)}\) is compatible with the simplicial maps, making the total assignment a cosimplicial crossed groupoid. ◻

Proposition 3.5 ∀ stated (Descent condition — twisted $2$-cocycle). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum observable universe and let \(\{A_n\}_{n \geq 0}\) be its recursive brane tower; let \(q\) denote the substrate parameter. With Yekutieli triples \((x^{(n)}, g^{(n)}, a^{(n)})\) (Yekutieli triple) and let \(A_\infty = \sum_{n,i} c_{n,i}\, \alpha_{n,i}\) (distributional connection) be the distributional connection. Then the global consistency of \(A_\infty\) is equivalent to the satisfaction of the twisted \(2\)-cocycle condition: for every triple intersection \(U_i \cap U_j \cap U_k\),

\[\label{eq:twisted-2-cocycle} d\!\left(g_{ijk}^{(n)}\right) \;=\; a_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot g_{ijk}^{(n)} \cdot \left(a_{jk}^{(n)}\right)^{-1},\]

where \(g_{ijk}^{(n)} = g_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot g_{jk}^{(n)} \cdot (g_{ik}^{(n)})^{-1}\) is the Cech \(1\)-cocycle deviation at level \(n\), \(d(g_{ijk}^{(n)})\) denotes the Cech coboundary (the failure of the \(1\)-cocycle condition), and \(a_{ij}^{(n)}, a_{jk}^{(n)} \in \mathcal{O}_M^{\times}\) are the \(2\)-morphisms (quantum deformation feedback from \(\mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)\)).

Equivalently, the obstruction class \(\kappa_n\) (quantum characteristic classes) is the cohomology class of the twisted \(2\)-cocycle \([a_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot (\cdot) \cdot (a_{jk}^{(n)})^{-1}]\) in \(H^2(M,\, \mathcal{O}_M^{\times})\).

Proof of Descent condition — twisted $2$-cocycle.

Let \(\{U_i\}\) be a good cover trivialising the tower. On \(U_i \cap U_j\) the gauge transition function \(g_{ij}^{(n)}\) patches the connection \(A_n\):

\[A_n|_{U_j} \;=\; g_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot A_n|_{U_i} \cdot (g_{ij}^{(n)})^{-1} + (g_{ij}^{(n)})^{-1}\, dg_{ij}^{(n)}.\]

On a triple overlap \(U_i \cap U_j \cap U_k\), the ordinary cocycle condition \(g_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot g_{jk}^{(n)} = g_{ik}^{(n)}\) holds if and only if the connection \(A_n\) is globally well-defined at level \(n\) with no twist. The passage from level \(n\) to level \(n{+}1\) via the central extension \(\mathfrak{G}_{n+1} \to \mathfrak{G}_n\) introduces the \(2\)-morphism twist: the transition functions at level \(n{+}1\) are related to those at level \(n\) by

\[g_{ij}^{(n+1)} \;=\; a_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot g_{ij}^{(n)},\]

where \(a_{ij}^{(n)} \in \mathcal{O}_M^{\times}\) is the local lift datum (Yekutieli triple). Computing the cocycle deviation at level \(n{+}1\):

\[\begin{aligned} g_{ijk}^{(n+1)} &= g_{ij}^{(n+1)} \cdot g_{jk}^{(n+1)} \cdot (g_{ik}^{(n+1)})^{-1} \\ &= (a_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot g_{ij}^{(n)}) \cdot (a_{jk}^{(n)} \cdot g_{jk}^{(n)}) \cdot (a_{ik}^{(n)} \cdot g_{ik}^{(n)})^{-1}. \end{aligned}\]

Since \(a_{ij}^{(n)}\) is central (it lies in the band \(\mathcal{O}_M^{\times}\) of the gerbe), the gauge factors commute past the \(a\)-factors, giving

\[g_{ijk}^{(n+1)} \;=\; a_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot a_{jk}^{(n)} \cdot (a_{ik}^{(n)})^{-1} \cdot g_{ijk}^{(n)}.\]

The distributional connection \(A_\infty\) is globally consistent if and only if \(g_{ijk}^{(n+1)} = 0\) for all \(n\) in the Cech cohomology, which requires

\[d(g_{ijk}^{(n)}) \;=\; a_{ij}^{(n)} \cdot g_{ijk}^{(n)} \cdot (a_{jk}^{(n)})^{-1},\]

where the left-hand side is the Cech coboundary \(\delta^2(g^{(n)})_{ijk}\) and the right-hand side is the twisted conjugation by the \(2\)-morphisms. The class of this twisted \(2\)-cocycle in \(H^2(M,\, \mathcal{O}_M^{\times})\) is precisely the obstruction class \(\kappa_n\) of quantum characteristic classes: it vanishes if and only if the cocycle deviation at level \(n\) can be absorbed by the \(2\)-morphism data, i.e. the lift from \(\mathfrak{G}_n\) to \(\mathfrak{G}_{n+1}\) is unobstructed.

By [Theorem 1.7, yekutieli2010], this is equivalent to the vanishing of the Yekutieli obstruction class, closing the circle with the lifting criterion (Yekutieli lifting). ◻

Proposition 3.13 ∀ stated (Existence and Uniqueness of the Vacuum State). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a (quantum observable universe); let \(q\) denote the substrate parameter. Let \(\mathcal{A}_\infty\) be its (direct limit algebra). Let \(G_q = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)\) be the (quantum gauge group), acting on \(\mathcal{A}_\infty\) by conjugation. Then there exists a unique \(R\)-linear functional \(\varphi \colon \mathcal{A}_\infty \to R\) that is \(G_q\)-invariant:

\[\varphi(g \cdot a) = \varphi(a) \quad\text{for all } g \in G_q,\; a \in \mathcal{A}_\infty.\]

In the archimedean realisation, \(G_q = \mathrm{SU}_q(2)\) and \(\varphi\) is the unique \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\)-invariant state on the \(C^*\)-algebraic direct limit.

Proof of Existence and Uniqueness of the Vacuum State.

The algebra \(\mathcal{A}_\infty\) is the direct limit \(\varinjlim \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n)\) (direct limit algebra), and the quantum gauge group \(G_q = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)\) (quantum gauge group) acts on it by conjugation.

Existence. The fibre functor \(\tau \colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathrm{Vect}_R\) is \(R\)-linear and exact. It sends the unit \(\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{C}\) to \(R\) and the Frobenius pairing on \(A\) to a non-degenerate \(R\)-bilinear form on \(\tau(A)\). At each finite level, \(\tau\) maps \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n)\) to a finite-dimensional \(R\)-algebra \(\mathrm{End}_R(\tau(A_n))\). The Frobenius trace \(\mathrm{tr}_n \colon \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n) \to R\) defined by

\[\mathrm{tr}_n(\alpha) \;=\; (\varepsilon \circ \alpha \circ \eta)(\mathbf{1}),\]

where \(\eta \colon \mathbf{1} \to A_n\) and \(\varepsilon \colon A_n \to \mathbf{1}\) are the Frobenius unit and counit, is \(G_q\)-invariant: for any \(g \in G_q\), \(\mathrm{tr}_n(g \cdot \alpha) = \mathrm{tr}_n(\alpha)\) by the naturality of \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\) (they are morphisms in \(\mathcal{C}\), hence invariant under monoidal automorphisms of the fibre functor).

The descent homomorphisms \(\mathfrak{d}_n\) (descent) are equivariant and satisfy \(\mathrm{tr}_n \circ \mathfrak{d}_{n+1} = \mathrm{tr}_{n+1}\) (compatibility of the Frobenius trace with descent). By the universal property of the direct limit, the system \(\{\mathrm{tr}_n\}\) determines a \(G_q\)-invariant \(R\)-linear functional \(\varphi \colon \mathcal{A}_\infty \to R\).

Uniqueness. The vanishing condition \(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0\) of the quantum observable universe axioms implies, via Schur’s lemma applied in \(\mathcal{C}\), that \(\mathrm{End}_{G_q}(\mathbf{1}) \cong R\). More precisely: a \(G_q\)-invariant functional \(\psi \colon \mathcal{A}_\infty \to R\) must factor through the \(G_q\)-fixed subalgebra \(\mathcal{A}_\infty^{G_q}\). The vanishing condition forces this fixed subalgebra to be one-dimensional (generated by the identity), since any non-scalar \(G_q\)-invariant endomorphism would yield a non-zero element of \(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A))\). Descent

The descent machinery connects the infinite brane tower to finite-dimensional geometry. Given a quantum observable universe \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) with brane tower \(\{A_n\}_{n \geq 0}\) (recursive brane tower), the descent lemma (descent lemma) shows that each level \(A_n\) inherits the full QOU structure from \(A\). The descent involution (descent involution) then splits each \(A_n\) into eigenspaces whose direct limit recovers the physical algebra. This section develops the descent involution, its eigenspace decomposition, and the topological mass formula that results.

Definition 3.16 ∀ stated (Descent). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum observable universe and let \(\{A_n\}_{n \geq 0}\) be the (recursive brane tower). The descent is the reverse traversal of the tower: starting from level \(n\) one passes to level \(n{-}1\) by applying the restriction morphism \(r_n \colon A_n = J^1(A_{n-1}) \to A_{n-1}\) (the categorical retraction of the jet embedding). In the archimedean realisation, this restricts the connection and bundle to the base manifold \(M_{n-1}\). By QU-to-QOU lifting(1), the descent terminates in finitely many steps, bounded by \(\mathrm{Krull\;dim}\, Z(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A))\).

Lemma 3.17 ∀ stated (Descent preserves the quantum observable universe). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a quantum observable universe and let \(\{A_n\}_{n \geq 0}\) be the (recursive brane tower), with \(n \geq 1\); let \(q\) denote the substrate parameter. The restriction morphism \(r_n \colon A_n \to A_{n-1}\) yields a Frobenius–Hopf object \(A_{n-1}\) such that \((\mathcal{C}, A_{n-1}, \tau)\) is again a quantum observable universe. Moreover, the induced descent homomorphism

\[\mathfrak{d}_n \;\colon\; \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n-1})\]

is a surjective algebra homomorphism that intertwines the \(G_q\)-actions at levels \(n\) and \(n{-}1\). In the archimedean realisation, this recovers the surjective \(*\)-homomorphism \(\Gamma(J^1 E_n,\, \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)) \to \Gamma(J^1 E_{n-1},\, \mathrm{SU}_{q_{n-1}}(2))\).

Proof of Descent preserves the quantum observable universe.

The proof mirrors the ascent (jet prolongation) in reverse, working inside the rigid monoidal category \(\mathcal{C}\).

Step 1: Frobenius restriction. The jet object \(J^1(A) = A \otimes \Omega^1(A)\) at level \(n\) contains \(A\) as a direct summand via the unit section \(\eta \otimes \mathrm{id}\colon \Omega^1(A) \hookrightarrow A \otimes \Omega^1(A)\). Composing with the counit \(\varepsilon\colon A \to \mathbf{1}\) on the first tensor factor gives the restriction map \(r_n\colon J^1(A) \to A\). Because \(A\) is Frobenius, \(r_n\) is a coalgebra morphism: the comultiplication \(\Delta\) commutes with restriction by the Frobenius relation, and the counit restricts by construction. Hence the restricted object inherits a Frobenius–Hopf structure.

Step 2: Connection restriction. The connection \(A_n\) on \(E_n\) is determined by the comultiplication \(\Delta'\) on \(J^1(A)\) (jet prolongation, Step 2). Restricting \(\Delta'\) along \(r_n\) recovers the original comultiplication \(\Delta\) on \(A\), which by the construction of the brane tower corresponds to the connection \(A_{n-1}\) on \(E_{n-1}\). In particular, \(A_{n-1}\) satisfies the Hermitian Yang–Mills equations on \(E_{n-1}\) because it did so at level \(n{-}1\) before the ascent.

Step 3: Non-degeneracy and vanishing. Non-degeneracy of the Frobenius form on the restricted object follows from the direct-summand inclusion \(A \hookrightarrow J^1(A)\): the restriction of a non-degenerate pairing to a direct summand remains non-degenerate. The vanishing condition \(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0\) is unchanged since we recover the original object \(A\).

Step 4: The descent homomorphism. Applying the fibre functor \(\tau\) to \(r_n\) yields \(\mathfrak{d}_n\colon \Gamma(J^1 E_n,\, \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)) \to \Gamma(J^1 E_{n-1},\, \mathrm{SU}_{q_{n-1}}(2))\). This is a \(*\)-homomorphism because \(r_n\) preserves multiplication and the antipode. Surjectivity follows from the fact that the ascent map \(\ell_n\) of jet prolongation is injective (it embeds \(A\) into \(J^1(A)\) as a direct summand), so its categorical adjoint \(r_n\) is surjective.

Step 5: Equivariance. The \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) action on sections at level \(n\) is induced by the adjoint representation of the Hopf algebra. Since \(r_n\) is a Hopf algebra morphism, it intertwines the adjoint actions at levels \(n\) and \(n{-}1\). ◻

Definition 3.18 ∀ stated (Descent involution). \(\forall\)

Let \(\ell_n \colon A_n \hookrightarrow A_{n+1}\) denote the ascent embedding (jet prolongation) and \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \colon \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1}) \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n)\) the descent homomorphism (descent). The descent involution at level \(n\) is the endomorphism of the level-\((n{+}1)\) endomorphism algebra:

\[\hat{\tau}_n \;=\; \ell_n \circ \mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \;\colon\; \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1}) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1}).\]

Since \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \circ \ell_n = \mathrm{id}\) on \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n)\) (the descent of the ascent recovers the original endomorphism, because \(\ell_n\) embeds \(A_n\) as a direct summand of \(A_{n+1}\) and \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}\) is the retraction), we have

\[\hat{\tau}_n^2 \;=\; \ell_n \circ \mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \circ \ell_n \circ \mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \;=\; \ell_n \circ (\mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \circ \ell_n) \circ \mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \;=\; \ell_n \circ \mathrm{id} \circ \mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \;=\; \hat{\tau}_n,\]

so \(\hat{\tau}_n\) is idempotent. Its image is the descended subalgebra \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}} \subseteq \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1})\), consisting of endomorphisms at level \(n{+}1\) that factor through the level-\(n\) algebra via the ascent embedding.

Proposition 3.19 ∀ stated (Eigenspaces of the descent involution). \(\forall\)

The descent involution \(\hat{\tau}_n\) (descent involution) decomposes the level-\((n{+}1)\) endomorphism algebra into a direct sum

\[\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1}) \;=\; \mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}} \;\oplus\; \mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}},\]

where \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}} = \mathrm{im}\,\hat{\tau}_n\) is the descended subalgebra (endomorphisms that factor through level \(n\)) and \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}} = \ker\,\hat{\tau}_n\) is the transient complement (genuinely new endomorphisms at level \(n{+}1\)). The (algebraic filter) refines this decomposition:

  1. The bosonic (longitudinal) modes lifted from level \(n\) lie entirely in \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}}\): they commute with the curvature and survive every round-trip through the tower.

  2. The fermionic (ladder) modes at level \(n{+}1\) split between the two summands; those in \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}}\) are the modes created by the jet prolongation that have no pre-image at level \(n\).

Proof of Eigenspaces of the descent involution.

Since \(\hat{\tau}_n\) is an idempotent endomorphism of the \(R\)-algebra \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1})\), the standard idempotent splitting gives a direct sum decomposition

\[\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1}) \;=\; \mathrm{im}\,\hat{\tau}_n \;\oplus\; \ker\,\hat{\tau}_n.\]

Indeed, every \(\alpha \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1})\) decomposes uniquely as \(\alpha = \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) + (\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha))\), where \(\hat{\tau}_n(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)) = \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)\) (so \(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \in \mathrm{im}\,\hat{\tau}_n\)) and \(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)) = \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) = 0\) (so \(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \in \ker\,\hat{\tau}_n\)). The two summands intersect trivially: if \(\alpha \in \mathrm{im}\,\hat{\tau}_n \cap \ker\,\hat{\tau}_n\), then \(\alpha = \hat{\tau}_n(\beta)\) for some \(\beta\) and \(0 = \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) = \hat{\tau}_n^2(\beta) = \hat{\tau}_n(\beta) = \alpha\).

When \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1})\) carries a \(*\)-involution compatible with \(\hat{\tau}_n\) (i.e. \(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha^*) = \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)^*\)), the decomposition respects the \(*\)-structure: both summands are \(*\)-subalgebras.

For item (1), let \(\alpha \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1})\) lie in the image of the ascent \(\ell_n\): \(\alpha = \ell_n(\beta)\) for some \(\beta \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n)\). Bosonic modes are characterised categorically by the (algebraic filter) as those commuting with the categorical curvature. Then \(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) = \ell_n(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}(\ell_n(\beta))) = \ell_n(\beta) = \alpha\), using \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1} \circ \ell_n = \mathrm{id}\) (descent). Hence \(\alpha \in \mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}}\).

For item (2), an endomorphism \(\alpha \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1})\) that does not factor through level \(n\) satisfies \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}(\alpha) = 0\) in \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n)\), so \(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) = \ell_n(0) = 0\) and \(\alpha \in \mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}}\). Those level-\((n{+}1)\) endomorphisms that do factor through level \(n\) lie in \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}}\) by the argument of item (1). The fermionic/ladder modes therefore split between the two summands according to whether they have a pre-image under \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}\). ◻

Definition 3.25 ∀ stated (Fibre-compatible hadron embedding). \(\forall\)

Let \(H \in \mathrm{Sym}^n(K) \otimes \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}\) be a hadron (Definition) and let \(E_p\) denote the fibre of the knot bundle over a point \(p \in \mathbb{P}(V)\) (Definition). A fibre-compatible hadron embedding is a morphism

  1. Integrability. Concretely, \(\iota_p\) must intertwine the abstract Skein action (operations on hadrons) with the geometric operations on knots in \(E_p\).

  3. Hopf compatibility. Proposition 3.26 ∀ stated (Skein generation of hadrons from mass-zero fundamentals). \(\forall\)

\[q \cdot L_+ \;-\; q^{-1} \cdot L_- \;=\; (q - q^{-1}) \cdot L_0\]

Theorem 3.30 ∀ stated (Topological Decomposition of Mass). \(\forall\)

Let \((\mathcal{C}, A, \tau)\) be a (quantum observable universe); let \(q\) denote the substrate parameter. The mass \(m(\gamma)\) (mass identity) admits a decomposition into descended and transient contributions via the descent involution \(\hat{\tau}_n\) (descent involution):

\[\label{eq:topological-mass-decomp} m(\gamma) \;=\; \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{desc}}(\gamma) \,\bigr\rVert \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{trans}}(\gamma,\, p_i) \,\bigr\rVert,\]

where:

  1. \(\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{desc}}(\gamma)\) is the categorical trace of \((\lambda \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta \circ \gamma\) restricted to the descended subalgebra \(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{desc}} = \mathrm{im}\,\hat{\tau}_n\) (descent eigenspaces). Since \(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{desc}} \cong \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n)\) via the ascent embedding \(\ell_n\), this equals the mass of the base-level cycle \(\gamma_0\): it captures the baseline drag of the Frobenius–Hopf structure along the trivial core.

  2. Each \(\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{trans}}(\gamma, p_i)\) is the categorical trace restricted to the component of the transient complement \(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{trans}} = \ker\,\hat{\tau}_n\) localised at the \(i\)-th blow-up centre \(p_i\) (brane boundary). The localisation is well-defined because the kernels of \(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}\) at distinct blow-up centres are disjoint submodules of \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1})\).

The decomposition follows from the idempotent splitting \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1}) = \mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{desc}} \oplus \mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{trans}}\) (descent eigenspaces) and the linearity of the categorical trace. When the splitting is orthogonal with respect to the Frobenius norm, \(\lVert \alpha \rVert^2 = \lVert \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \rVert^2 + \lVert \alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \rVert^2\), the mass decomposes additively as stated.

Categorical proof of the topological decomposition of mass.

Step 1: Eigenspace decomposition of the mass integrand. By the (mass identity), the mass of \(\gamma\) is

\[m(\gamma) \;=\; \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{tr}\bigl( (\lambda \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta \circ \gamma \bigr) \,\bigr\rVert.\]

The integrand \(\alpha = (\lambda \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta \circ \gamma\) is an element of \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_{n+1})\). The descent involution \(\hat{\tau}_n\) (descent involution) decomposes this algebra into the direct sum \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}} \oplus \mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}}\) (descent eigenspaces):

\[\alpha \;=\; \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \;+\; (\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)).\]

When the Frobenius pairing is compatible with the \(*\)-involution (\(\langle \alpha^*, \beta \rangle = \overline{\langle \alpha, \beta \rangle}\)), the idempotent decomposition is orthogonal with respect to the Frobenius norm. Therefore:

\[\lVert \alpha \rVert^2 \;=\; \lVert \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \rVert^2 \;+\; \lVert \alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \rVert^2,\]

and by the linearity of the categorical trace:

\[m(\gamma) \;=\; \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{tr}(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)) \,\bigr\rVert \;+\; \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{tr}(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)) \,\bigr\rVert.\]

Step 2: Descended term is the base-level mass. The projection \(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)\) lies in \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}} = \mathrm{im}\,\hat{\tau}_n\), which is isomorphic to \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(A_n)\) via the ascent embedding \(\ell_n\) (descent involution). The compatibility of the categorical trace with descent (\(\mathrm{tr}_n \circ \mathfrak{d}_{n+1} = \mathrm{tr}_{n+1}\) on \(\mathrm{im}\,\hat{\tau}_n\)) gives:

\[\mathrm{tr}(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)) \;=\; \mathrm{tr}(\ell_n(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}(\alpha))) \;=\; \mathrm{tr}_n(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}(\alpha)) \;=\; \mathrm{tr}_n(\alpha_0),\]

where \(\alpha_0 = (\lambda \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta \circ \gamma_0\) is the mass integrand of the base-level cycle. Hence \(\lVert \mathrm{tr}(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)) \rVert = m(\gamma_0)\).

Step 3: Transient terms localise to blow-up centres. The transient component \(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \in \mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}} = \ker\,\hat{\tau}_n\). By the structure of the brane boundary (brane boundary), the support of a transient endomorphism is contained in the union of the kernels \(\ker(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}|_{p_i})\) at the blow-up centres \(p_1, \ldots, p_N\). These kernels are mutually disjoint submodules (each arises from a distinct extension step), so the transient trace decomposes as a sum:

\[\mathrm{tr}(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{trans}}(\gamma, p_i),\]

and the norm of the sum equals the sum of the norms (by disjointness of the supports).

Combining Steps 1–3 gives the claimed decomposition. ◻

The proof proceeds in four steps: decomposition of the mass integral using the descent involution, identification of the descended term with holonomy, localisation of the transient terms to exceptional divisors, and evaluation via the quantum dilogarithm.

Step 1: Eigenspace decomposition of the mass integrand. By the (mass identity), the mass of the vortex \(\gamma\) is

\[m(\gamma) \;=\; \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_\Sigma \bigl(\nabla_A q,\; L \wedge F^+\bigr) \,\bigr\rVert,\]

where \(\Sigma\) is a surface with \(\partial\Sigma = \gamma\). The integrand \((\nabla_A q,\; L \wedge F^+)\) is a section of the connection–curvature bundle over \(\Sigma\), and as such it is an element of the section algebra \(\Gamma(J^1 E_{n+1},\, \mathrm{SU}_{q_{n+1}}(2))\) at whatever level \(n{+}1\) accommodates the vortex.

The descent involution \(\hat{\tau}_n\) (descent involution) decomposes this section algebra into the direct sum \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}} \oplus \mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}}\) (descent eigenspaces). Since \(\hat{\tau}_n\) is an idempotent \(*\)-endomorphism, the Killing-form norm \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) on \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\) respects the orthogonal decomposition:

\[\lVert \alpha \rVert^2 \;=\; \lVert \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \rVert^2 \;+\; \lVert \alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \rVert^2\]

for every section \(\alpha\). Applying this to the mass integrand and using the additivity of the nonabelian multiplicative integral over orthogonal direct sums [Proposition 5.3, yekutieli2015]:

\[\label{eq:mi-decomp} \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_\Sigma \alpha \;=\; \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_\Sigma \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \;\cdot\; \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_\Sigma \bigl(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)\bigr),\]

where the product is in the group \(\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)\). Taking norms and using the submultiplicativity of \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) (which is an equality for the orthogonal decomposition of an idempotent):

\[m(\gamma) \;=\; \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_\Sigma \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \,\bigr\rVert \;+\; \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_\Sigma \bigl(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)\bigr) \,\bigr\rVert.\]

Step 2: Descended term is the unknot-core holonomy. The projection \(\hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)\) lies in \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{desc}} = \mathrm{im}\,\hat{\tau}_n\), which is isomorphic to the level-\(n\) section algebra via the ascent embedding \(\ell_n\) (descent involution). The surface \(\Sigma\) with \(\partial\Sigma = \gamma\) decomposes under the topological blow-up as \(\Sigma = \Sigma_0 \cup \bigcup_{i=1}^N D_i\), where \(\Sigma_0\) is a disc spanning the unknot core \(\gamma_0\) and each \(D_i\) is the disc (exceptional divisor) inserted at the \(i\)-th blow-up. On \(\Sigma_0\) the transient component vanishes (\(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) = 0\) on the unknot complement, since \(\gamma_0\) has trivial topology and all sections there factor through level \(n\)). Hence:

\[\mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_\Sigma \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha) \;=\; \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_{\Sigma_0} \alpha \;=\; \mathrm{Hol}_{\nabla_A}\!\bigl(\gamma_0;\, \mathfrak{G}\bigr),\]

where the second equality is the nonabelian Stokes theorem [yekutieli2015] applied to the disc \(\Sigma_0\).

Step 3: Transient terms localise to exceptional divisors. The transient component \(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)\) is annihilated by \(\hat{\tau}_n\): it lies in \(\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}} = \ker\,\hat{\tau}_n\) (descent eigenspaces). By the structure of the defining boundary (brane boundary), the support of a transient section is contained in a neighbourhood of the boundary \(\partial E_n\), which has codimension \(\geq 2\) in \(E_n\) (item (1)) and whose fibre at each blow-up centre \(p_i\) is the exceptional divisor \(E_i \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})\) (item (2)).

On the surface \(\Sigma\), the transient integrand is therefore supported on the discs \(D_i\) centred at the blow-up points. Since the \(D_i\) are disjoint (each blow-up introduces a new connected component of \(\partial E_n\)), the multiplicative integral splits as a product:

\[\mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_\Sigma \bigl(\alpha - \hat{\tau}_n(\alpha)\bigr) \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^{N}\; \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_{D_i} \bigl(\nabla_A q,\; L \wedge F^+\bigr).\]

Since the \(D_i\) are geometrically disjoint and the group elements commute up to the central band \(\mathcal{O}_M^{\times}\) of the gerbe (the transient modes are localised at distinct points, and the central extension ensures that the group-valued integrals commute modulo the band), the norm of the product equals the sum of the norms:

\[\bigl\lVert\, \prod_{i=1}^{N}\; \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_{D_i} \bigl(\nabla_A q,\; L \wedge F^+\bigr) \,\bigr\rVert \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_{E_i} \bigl(\nabla_A q,\; L \wedge F^+\bigr) \,\bigr\rVert,\]

where we replace \(D_i\) by \(E_i\) since the integrand is supported on the exceptional fibre \(E_i \subset D_i\).

Step 4: Quantum dilogarithm evaluation. Combining Steps 1–3:

\[m(\gamma) \;=\; \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{Hol}_{\nabla_A}\!\bigl(\gamma_0;\, \mathfrak{G}\bigr) \,\bigr\rVert \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \bigl\lVert\, \mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_{E_i} \bigl(\nabla_A q,\; L \wedge F^+\bigr) \,\bigr\rVert.\]

This is the claimed decomposition eq:topological-mass-decomp. The identification of each summand with the quantum dilogarithm \(\Phi_q(z_i)\) follows from the Corollary below: integration of the \(q\)-deformed expansion flow over the resolved defect \(E_i\) in the spectral coordinates of the Briggs–Klein chart yields \(\Phi_q(z_i) = \int_0^{z_i} \ln_q(1+t)/t\, dt\). ◻

Introduction

In the QOU framework, traditional path integrals are replaced by finite sums over the discrete fibre structure. Since the base space \(B\) and each fibre are finite, the continuum measure \(\mathcal{D}\phi\) collapses to a combinatorial sum, eliminating the need for renormalisation of divergent integrals.

The braid group acts naturally on the fibre degrees of freedom. What we observe as particle interactions corresponds to the action of braid operators exchanging strands in the fibre bundle. The exchange operations carry a \(q\)-deformation parameter inherited from the quantum group structure of the fibres. Concretely, the \(R\)-matrix of \(\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))\) furnishes a \(q\)-deformed braid operator whose eigenvalues govern the phase acquired under particle exchange.
