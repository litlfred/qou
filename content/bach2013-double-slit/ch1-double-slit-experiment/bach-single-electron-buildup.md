With the mask centered on the double-slit ($P_{12}$ configuration), the
electron source intensity was reduced to a detection rate of
approximately 1 Hz.  At 600 eV kinetic energy, the average distance
between consecutive electrons was $2.3 \times 10^6$ m, ensuring that
only one electron was present in the 1 m system at any time and
eliminating electron--electron interactions.

The electrostatic quadrupole lens was set to magnify the central five
diffraction orders.  Figure 3 of \cite{bach2013} shows the buildup
sequence:

| Stage | Electrons | Observation |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| (a) | 2 | Random hits, no pattern |
| (b) | 7 | Random hits |
| (c) | 209 | Faint clustering visible |
| (d) | 1004 | Pattern discernible |
| (e) | 6235 | Clear five-order diffraction pattern |

The final buildup took approximately 2 hours.  The progression from
apparently random individual detections to a coherent interference
pattern demonstrates Feynman's point: each electron "interferes with
itself" --- the wave function passes through both slits simultaneously,
even though each electron is detected as a single localized event.
