Richard Feynman described electron diffraction as a phenomenon "which
has in it the heart of quantum mechanics.  In reality, it contains the
only mystery" \cite{feynman1965}.  He went on to describe a thought
experiment for which he stated "that you should not try to set up"
because "the apparatus would have to be made on an impossibly small
scale to show the effects we are interested in."

Bach et al. \cite{bach2013} demonstrate the full realization of this
thought experiment.  They report both control over individual slits to
observe probability distributions $P_1$, $P_2$, and $P_{12}$ from
single- and double-slits, and the build-up of a diffraction pattern at
single-electron detection rates.

The general perception that the electron double-slit experiment has
already been performed is true only in the sense that Jönsson (1961)
demonstrated diffraction from multiple micro-slits without single
particle detection, and Tonomura (1989) recorded single-electron
buildup using biprisms rather than true double-slits.  This paper is
the first to combine both elements: true nano-fabricated double-slits
with single-electron event recording.
