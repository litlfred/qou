## 1. The geometric setup

Let $\mathcal{B} \subset \mathbb{P}^4$ be Bring's curve, a genus-4 Riemann surface with maximal $S_5$ symmetry. We consider its affine cone $C(\mathcal{B}) \subset \mathbb{C}^5$, a complex surface (a real 4-manifold), and denote this base manifold $M$.

On a $4m$-dimensional manifold, a **tetraplectic structure** \cite{foth2002} is a closed 4-form $\Omega \in \Omega^4(M)$ such that $\Omega^m$ is a non-degenerate volume form. For our 4-real-dimensional cone ($m = 1$) any 4-form is trivially closed ($d\Omega = 0$), so the tetraplectic requirement reduces to a nowhere-vanishing volume form.

The manifold $M$ inherits the discrete symmetries of the Bring curve, notably an $S_5 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ action where $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is an involution $\tau$. Averaging an arbitrary initial volume form $\omega_0$ over the group action,

$$
\Omega = \frac{1}{|S_5|} \sum_{g \in S_5} \left( \omega_0 ± \tau^*(g^* \omega_0) \right),
$$

constructs an $S_5$-invariant tetraplectic volume form $\Omega$.

## 2. The analytic basis and the Askey scheme

With the invariant tetraplectic measure $\Omega$ fixed, define the state space $\mathcal{H} = L^2(M, \Omega)$. Because $\Omega$ is $S_5$-invariant, $\mathcal{H}$ decomposes into orthogonal irreducible representations of $S_5$, indexed by integer partitions of 5 (Young tableaux).

We conjecture that when these global combinatorial states are projected into local coordinate patches near the singularities of $C(\mathcal{B})$, they approximate standard orthogonal polynomials of the **Askey scheme**:

1. **Radial regimes:** Bessel-type wavefunctions.
2. **Quadratic/harmonic local approximations:** Hermite polynomials.

This alignment of the tetraplectic-orthogonal polynomials with the Askey hierarchy is a conjecture, not a theorem.

## 3. Conjectural link to Seiberg–Witten localization

In Seiberg–Witten theory, masses of BPS states and binding matrix elements are integrals over the 4-manifold, and path integrals over instanton moduli localize (Nekrasov) at fixed points classified by tuples of Young tableaux.

The suggestion is that a physical matrix element

$$
E = \int_M \psi_\lambda^* H \psi_\mu \, \Omega
$$

could then be evaluated combinatorially — via Schur's lemma and the Wigner–Eckart theorem on the $S_5$ partitions, replacing the integral by Clebsch–Gordan data of the symmetric group. We record this as a conjectural bridge between the continuous tetraplectic integral and the discrete categorical data of knot invariants; it is not established here.

## 4. The 3-Sasakian boundary and the Reeb quaternionic twist

The 4-dimensional cone $C(\mathcal{B})$ has a 3-dimensional radial boundary (the link of the singularity). When the bulk is tetraplectic, this boundary carries a **3-Sasakian (degenerate quaternionic-contact) structure**: instead of a single Reeb vector field, an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ triad of contact forms and dual Reeb fields.

Following Foth–Leitner \cite{fothleitner2003} for quaternionic flag manifolds, the Reeb triad is generated by the quaternionic operators $M_x$ ($x \in \{i, j, k\}$), which we model as right-multiplication by the imaginary units on $\mathbb{H}$:

```tex
M_x = \begin{pmatrix} x & 0 \\ 0 & x \end{pmatrix}
```

The dynamics are governed by the **quaternionic twist**: flowing along $M_i$ preserves its complex structure but rotates the complementary structures into one another. This torsion is the bracket

$$
[M_x, M_y] = 2 M_{x \cdot y} \quad (x \neq y),
$$

i.e. $[M_i, M_j] = 2 M_k$ and cyclic — the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ commutation relations. This bracket is formalized sorry-free in
[`BringCone.lean`](lean/QOU/BringCone.lean) (`reeb_twist_ij`/`jk`/`ki`). We *interpret* the Reeb flow along the Bring-cone fibers as carrying an internal $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ structure on the localized states; the identification of this structure with a physical gauge symmetry (and its electroweak alignment via
[`QuaternionicElectroweak.lean`](lean/QOU/QuaternionicElectroweak.lean)) is interpretive, not derived.
