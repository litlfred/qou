In the archimedean realisation
([$\mathbb{C}$-quantum observable universe](#ex:c-quantum-observable-universe)),
the topological decomposition of mass
([topological mass](#thm:topological-mass)) specialises to a closed-form
relationship between the mass of a particle, the hyperbolic volume of its
associated knot, and the quantum Planck constant.

**Conjecture.**
Let $K$ be a hyperbolic knot whose complement carries the vortex topology of
a stable particle in the $\mathbb{C}$-quantum observable universe, generated
by $N$ blow-ups along the brane tower.  Then the mass satisfies

$$m(K) \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(K)}{\hbar_q^{\,2}},$$

where $\operatorname{Vol}(K)$ is the hyperbolic volume of $S^3 \setminus K$
and $\hbar_q = 1 - 1/q$ is the quantum Planck constant
([quantum Planck constant](#def:quantum-planck-constant)) at the substrate
parameter $q$ ([substrate parameter](#def:substrate-parameter)).

This arises from the general decomposition
([topological mass](#thm:topological-mass)) as follows:

1. The baseline holonomy term
   $\lVert \mathrm{Hol}_{\nabla_A}(\gamma_0;\, \mathfrak{G}) \rVert$
   evaluates to $\operatorname{Vol}(K)$ via the Chern--Simons/volume
   correspondence for the hyperbolic knot complement.
2. The exceptional divisor contributions $\Phi_q(z_i)$ sum, after
   evaluation in the Briggs--Klein spectral coordinates, to
   $\operatorname{Vol}(K) \cdot (1/\hbar_q^{\,2} - 1)$.
3. Adding baseline and exceptional terms:
   $m = \operatorname{Vol}(K) + \operatorname{Vol}(K)(1/\hbar_q^{\,2} - 1) = \operatorname{Vol}(K)/\hbar_q^{\,2}$.

Combined with an ansatz identifying particles with specific knots
(e.g.\ the figure-eight knot $4_1$ with the muon), this formula determines
the substrate parameter $q$ from experimental mass ratios
(see [§11](#sec:experimental-evidence)).
