This paper develops the **mechanics** of the quantum observable
universe (QOU).  We do not say what the universe *is* — we say what
its mechanics *do*.  A QOU is a rigid monoidal category: it encodes
the algebraic structure of quantum observables together with the
geometric data needed to recover spacetime physics.  Category theory
is the natural language because it describes morphisms and
structure — the *how* — without committing to ontology.

The central result is that these mechanics are **Turing complete**.
The Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$ of the substrate parameter $q$ admits a
non-vanishing Toffoli amplitude $P_{7,6}(q) \neq 0$ for all
$q \neq 1$: a $q$-deformed AND gate realised by braiding.  Since
the Toffoli gate is universal for reversible computation, any
computable function can be executed as a sequence of braid crossings
at discrete Planck time steps.  This is not a metaphor — it is an
algebraic consequence of the crossing coefficients.

**[Chapter 1](#chap:qu)** establishes the categorical preliminaries:
rigid monoidal categories, fibre functors, and Tannaka–Krein
reconstruction.

**[Chapter 2](#chap:qou)** gives the axiomatic definition of a
quantum observable universe.  We derive the Frobenius relation, the
dagger structure, the categorical action functional $S_q$, and the
three variational equations (Einstein, Yang–Mills, transport) that
govern QOU mechanics.

**[Chapter 3](#chap:torsion)** establishes lifting and descent
theorems for quantum torsion: the obstructions to extending local
data to global structures, and the descent involution $\hat{\tau}$
that decomposes the endomorphism algebra into descended and
transient modes.

**[Chapter 4](#chap:path-integrals-braiding)** develops braids and
knots in the QOU framework.  The Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$ provides
crossing coefficients, the Markov trace computes Skein polynomials,
and the Jones polynomial emerges as a link invariant.  Crossing
number equals jet order equals information content.

**[Chapter 5](#chap:models-qou)** examines quantum observable
universes over different ground fields: the Archimedean (complex),
non-Archimedean ($\pp$-adic), and finite-field cases.

**[Chapter 6](#chap:descartes-universe)** develops the Descartes
condition and the topological mass ansatz.  Bring's surface provides
explicit coordinate systems.  The nuclear Skein polynomial
$P_{\mathcal{N}}(q)$ is computed via transfer matrices, and beta
decay appears as a universal $2 \times 2$ matrix perturbation
$\Delta_\beta(q)$.

**[Chapter 7](#chap:observations)** presents experimental and
observational evidence: the skein-theoretic mass formula, hadronic
spectra, and comparison with measured values.

**[Chapter 8](#chap:info-theory)** develops the information-theoretic
perspective.  Discrete Planck time gives the universe discrete
mathematics for free.  The Toffoli braid proves Turing completeness;
the Skein decomposition of a computation is a discrete path integral.
A conjecture proposes quantum computational universality via descent
to roots of unity, where the root-of-unity level $k$ grows
quadratically in crossing number.

**[Chapter 9](#chap:organic-chemistry)** applies the Hopf chain
formalism to organic chemistry.

The **Glossary** maps paper terminology to canonical Mathlib
declarations.  The **Appendix** collects the knot periodic table.
