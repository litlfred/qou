The confinement of all Reeb eigenvalues to the critical line
$\mathrm{Re}(\rho) = 1/2$ has a direct physical consequence: the
observer drift is **unitarily stable**. No eigenmode grows or decays
exponentially.

Concretely, the oscillatory terms in the explicit formula satisfy:

$$
\left|\frac{X^{\rho_i}}{[\rho_i]_q}\right| = \frac{X^{1/2}}{|[\rho_i]_q|}
$$

for all $X > 0$. This means:

1. **No collapse**: Defects do not cluster (which would require
   $\mathrm{Re}(\rho) > 1/2$ for some mode).
2. **No rarefaction**: Defects do not disperse (which would require
   $\mathrm{Re}(\rho) < 1/2$).
3. **Square-root fluctuations**: The deviation from the average defect
   density is $O(X^{1/2})$, the minimal fluctuation consistent with
   the uncertainty principle.

The substrate is therefore in a **critical phase**: balanced between
order and disorder, with the Reeb flow preserving the $q$-volume form
exactly. This is the algebraic content of the statement that the quantum
observable universe is a stable, self-consistent structure at the
$q$-scale.
