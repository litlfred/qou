The electron mass is the framework's dimensional unit and is an input, not a prediction. The muon mass is the numerical proxy for the neutron $\beta$-decay-width calibration $\mathfrak{D}_\beta$: the ratio $m_\mu/m_e \approx \mathrm{Vol}(4_1)/\hbar_q^2$ pins the substrate modulus $q_0$, so under the canonical (muon) pin the muon mass is exact *by construction* — a calibration tautology, not an accuracy result. Under the experimental (proton) pin, $m_\mu$ becomes a genuine prediction and misses its hundredfold-experimental-precision target by $963.6\times$, the $\mu$–$p$ substrate tension recorded in the precision-goals ledger. Either way, $m_\mu$ does not count as a derived Standard-Model parameter.

The tau mass is open. The framework treats the tau's *decay structure* through the $T(3,5)$ weak vertex — [the tau leptonic-universality proposition](#prop:tau-leptonic-universality) derives lepton universality of the leptonic branching ratios from the Hecke amplitude's independence of the daughter lepton — but universality of ratios is not a mass: no hyperbolic knot has been confirmed as the tau carrier, and no volume computation reproduces $m_\tau/m_e$. The missing object is the tau's knot identification, after which the volume–density chain would apply as it does for the muon.
