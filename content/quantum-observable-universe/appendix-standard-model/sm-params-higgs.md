The Higgs mass has a conjectural identification: the Higgs is assigned the torus link $T(2,8)$ within [the electroweak triplet conjecture](#conj:electroweak-higgs-torus-link-triplet), alongside $W = T(2,4)$ and $Z = T(2,6)$. Because torus links have zero hyperbolic volume, the mass chain for these carriers is not the volume–density chain but a Reshetikhin–Turaev evaluation at a root of unity, and that chain has two open inputs: the level selection and the invariant-to-mass formula. The best numerical probes land within a factor of two of $m_H$; a factor of two is not a prediction, and no formula currently gives unity.

The electroweak scale $v$ has no derivation. The vacuum-vortex morphism proposed in [the $G$-action-on-substrate conjecture](#conj:g-action-on-substrate) is the natural home for it, but that conjecture is itself open at the closure level, and no computation extracts $v$ (equivalently the Fermi constant) from the substrate. Since $v$ normalises every Standard-Model mass via the Yukawa mechanism, its absence means the framework's mass predictions proceed by an entirely different route (calibrated volume–density and Hecke-energy chains) — a structural divergence from the Standard Model parameterisation that any completed derivation would need to reconcile.
