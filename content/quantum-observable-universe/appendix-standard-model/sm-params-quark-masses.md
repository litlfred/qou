All six quark masses are open, and the framework's structural position makes the question different in kind from the Standard Model's. In the Standard Model the quark masses are six independent Yukawa couplings; in the framework, light quarks are not elementary inputs but *components of composite braids* — the up and down quarks appear as constituent structure of the Borromean baryon braid, with the proton–neutron split carried by an isospin assignment layer rather than by two independent masses. A constituent-level density (the structural identity relating the constituent density to the three-strand Markov trace) exists at the baryon level, but no construction extracts individual current-quark masses $m_u, \ldots, m_t$ from it, and the heavy flavours are untouched here: no knot or braid identification exists for $s$, $c$, $b$, or $t$ as mass carriers.

In sum: the framework currently predicts *baryon-level* masses and proposes to treat quark masses as derived constituent data, but the derivation of any individual quark mass — light or heavy — is open, and it is not yet established that the six current-quark masses are even the natural parameters of the framework's flavour sector.

This suggests the correct target is operational rather than definitional. Experimentally, no quark mass is measured directly: light-quark masses are scheme-dependent parameters extracted by inverting hadron spectroscopy through lattice computation and chiral perturbation theory, heavy-quark masses come from quarkonium spectra, and only the top — which decays before hadronizing — is reconstructed from decay products. The framework therefore need not *define* $m_q$ at all; it need only derive the observables that the extraction procedures consume, and may then run the equivalent extraction to produce scheme-comparable numbers. Two consequences fix the programme. First, the cleanest extractable quantity is the isospin-breaking difference $m_d - m_u$: its experimental source is the neutron–proton splitting $\Delta_{pn}$ after removing the electromagnetic self-energy, and the framework derives both $\Delta_{pn}$ *and* its own electromagnetic–strong decomposition (the antisymmetric Coulomb crossing term of [the Coulomb charge](#def:coulomb-charge)), so an extraction of "$m_d - m_u$ in the framework scheme" is well-posed with no new inputs. Second, the combination $m_u + m_d$ is *not* honestly extractable this way: its experimental source is $m_\pi^2$, and the pion mass is CAL-3 — a calibration input — so any extraction through it would be circular. The parameters this section tabulates are thus reinterpreted: not six numbers the framework owes, but six scheme quantities of the observer's inversion procedure, of which the framework can currently ground one difference and must remain silent on the calibration-tainted combinations.
