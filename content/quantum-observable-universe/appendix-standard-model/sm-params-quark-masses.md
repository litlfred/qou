All six quark masses are open, and the framework's structural position makes the question different in kind from the Standard Model's. In the Standard Model the quark masses are six independent Yukawa couplings; in the framework, light quarks are not elementary inputs but *components of composite braids* — the up and down quarks appear as constituent structure of the Borromean baryon braid, with the proton–neutron split carried by an isospin assignment layer rather than by two independent masses. A constituent-level density (the structural identity relating the constituent density to the three-strand Markov trace) exists at the baryon level, but no construction extracts individual current-quark masses $m_u, \ldots, m_t$ from it, and nothing in the corpus addresses the heavy flavours: no knot or braid identification exists for $s$, $c$, $b$, or $t$ as mass carriers.

The honest statement is therefore: the framework currently predicts *baryon-level* masses and proposes to treat quark masses as derived constituent data, but the derivation of any individual quark mass — light or heavy — is open, and it is not yet established that the six current-quark masses are even the natural parameters of the framework's flavour sector.
