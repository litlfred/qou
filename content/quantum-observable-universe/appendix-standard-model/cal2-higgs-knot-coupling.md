The canonical particle–knot table assigns the figure-eight knot $4_1$ to the Higgs boson — an assignment with structural content of its own, since $4_1$ is amphichiral, matching a scalar that is its own antiparticle. Read against that table, the CAL-2 calibration proxy

$$
  \frac{\mathfrak{m}_\mu}{\mathfrak{m}_e} \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{Vol}(4_1)}{\hbar_q^2}
$$

acquires a sharp interpretation: the muon's mass is the electron scale dressed by the *Higgs knot's* hyperbolic volume. What the Standard Model phrases as "the muon acquires mass through its Yukawa coupling to the Higgs field" the framework phrases as "the muon's mass proxy evaluates the volume of the Higgs knot" — the same structural role, played by $\mathrm{Vol}(4_1) \approx 2.0299$ rather than a fitted Yukawa constant.

Two boundaries delimit this reading. First, it is interpretive: the identity is the CAL-2 calibration, established independently of the table, and nothing new is derived by the relabelling. Second, the muon's *identity* knot remains the cinquefoil $5_1$; the figure-eight enters the mass formula as the coupling object, not as the muon itself — the same division of roles the nucleon chain exhibits, where the mass-bearing volume ($\mathcal{B}_3$) is not the identity knot of any single constituent.
