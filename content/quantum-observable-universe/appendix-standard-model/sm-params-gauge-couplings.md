The electromagnetic coupling is the most developed of the three. A conditional fixed-point equation for $\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}$ exists — [the EM-coupling fixed-point proposition](#prop:em-coupling-fixed-point) is a theorem conditional on a named normalization class — and the fibre-level Habiro structure of the coupling is treated in [the bar-involution proposition](#prop:alpha-em-habiro-bar-involution). What remains open is recorded in [the derivation-status remark](#rem:alpha-em-derivation-open-problems): the per-loop measure is not derived, so $\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}$ is *partial*, not closed. Reporting it as derived would launder the conditional hypothesis into an established premise.

The weak mixing angle has a precise categorical candidate — [the Weinberg-angle conjecture](#conj:weinberg-angle-categorical) — but it is a conjecture, and its inputs overlap the conjectural electroweak isotypic structure of [the truncation conjecture](#conj:sm-gauge-truncation); it inherits, in particular, the open hypercharge problem, since $\theta_W$ mixes exactly the two $\mathrm{U}(1)$'s the framework has not yet separated.

The strong coupling is open. The torus-knot anchors assigned to the strong sector are themselves conjectural identifications, and no construction extracts a coupling constant from them; there is no candidate formula for $\alpha_s$ at any scale, and no analogue of the fixed-point equation exists for the colour sector — unsurprisingly, since the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ action itself is open.
