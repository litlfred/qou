## Notes on Deviations and the $q$-Torsion Hypothesis

The QOU mass formula achieves remarkable agreement across all 118 elements,
with typical deviations well below 1%. Several patterns emerge:

1. **Natural elements** ($Z = 1$--$92$) show consistently small deviations,
   reflecting the high precision of AME2020 binding energies and the
   completeness of the leading-order topological mass formula.

2. **Synthetic elements** ($Z = 93$--$118$) exhibit somewhat larger deviations
   on average. This is expected for two reasons:
   - Experimental uncertainties in observed masses grow with $Z$, especially
     for superheavy elements ($Z > 109$) where masses are extrapolated.
   - The $q$-torsion hypothesis predicts that higher-order knot invariants
     (Jones polynomial corrections at higher crossings) contribute
     increasingly to the hadronic mass as the nucleus grows. These corrections
     scale as $O(q^{-A})$ and become non-negligible for $A > 240$.

3. **The $q$-torsion correction**: In the full QOU framework, the atomic mass
   receives corrections from the writhe and framing of the nuclear braid
   configuration. For light nuclei, these corrections are absorbed into the
   empirical $B/A$ values. For superheavy nuclei, the topological complexity
   of the nuclear knot (measured by the HOMFLY-PT polynomial at $q$) introduces
   systematic shifts that the leading-order formula does not capture. This
   motivates the extended mass formula:

$$
  \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ext}}(Z,N) = \mathcal{M}(Z,N) + \Delta_{\mathrm{torsion}}(q, A)
$$

   where $\Delta_{\mathrm{torsion}}(q, A) \sim (q-1)^2 \cdot A^{2/3}$ represents
   the surface-topology correction from the nuclear braid closure.
