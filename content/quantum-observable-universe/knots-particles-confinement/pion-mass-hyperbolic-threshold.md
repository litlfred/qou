The scale-independence proof attributes the missing pion scale to the meson's binding topology: the Hopf link complement is a solid torus, so $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$ and there is no geometric invariant to set $m_\pi$ from $(m_\mathfrak{e}, q_0)$. That is correct, but it reads as a fact about one link. It is a fact about where the pion sits in a ladder, and stating it that way explains the calibration structure rather than merely reporting it.

**The ladder.** The [fundamental knot operator basis](#fundamental-knots-operator-basis) assigns the same braid word to three particles at successive powers:

| $n$ | $(\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})^n$ | closure | type | $\mathrm{Vol}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1}$ | unknot | trivial | $0$ |
| $2$ | figure-eight $4_1$ | hyperbolic | hyperbolic | $\approx 2.0299$ |
| $3$ | Borromean rings $L6a4$ | hyperbolic | hyperbolic | $\approx 7.328 = 8G$ |

The pion is the $n = 1$ rung: [the pion braid is a half figure-eight](#prop:pion-half-figure-eight), $\mathfrak{M}_+$ sharing its eigenvalue phase on $V_{(2,1)}$ with $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1}$, and $4_1 = (\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})^2$ is exactly the square of the pion-type rotation.

**Hyperbolic volume switches on at $n = 2$.** So the vanishing at the pion is not a property of the Hopf link in particular; every rung below $n = 2$ is a torus knot or the unknot, and the operator basis states the rule for those directly — torus knots carry zero hyperbolic volume, hyperbolic knots carry positive volume which translates to mass via $\mathrm{Vol}(K)/\hbar_q^2$. The pion has no volume-derived mass because it lies below the threshold of its own family.

**This is what forces the calibration set.** The three anchors are CAL-1 $= m_\mathfrak{e}$, CAL-2 $= \mathfrak{D}_\beta$ (fixing $q_0$), CAL-3 $= m_\pi$. The two *mass* anchors are exactly the two $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$ particles: the electron is the trefoil $\sigma_1^3$, a torus knot; the pion is the $n=1$ rung. Every derived mass is then either hyperbolic — the muon through $m_\mu/m_\mathfrak{e} = \mathrm{Vol}(4_1)/\hbar_q^2$, the nucleon through $8G$ — or a Hecke character ratio off a $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$ anchor, as $m_K/m_\pi$ is. There are two mass calibrations rather than one or three because the volume formula reaches exactly the hyperbolic sector, and two particles of the catalogue sit outside it.

**A correction the table invites.** The operator basis describes the $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$ entries as "the massless, force-carrying sector". That reading is already contradicted inside the same table: the electron sits there with $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$ and is the mass unit of the whole framework. The defensible statement is weaker and is the one used above — $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$ means the mass is *not derivable from volume*, not that it is zero. The neutrino and the force carriers are massless for other reasons; the electron and the pion are not massless at all, they are uncalibrated by this route, which is why they are measured instead.
