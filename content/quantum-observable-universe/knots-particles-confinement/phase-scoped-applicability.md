**Remark.**
*The phase stratification proposition is the scope caveat attached to every statement in the confinement framework. Any formal statement about a confined particle $P$, its electron shell, a dressing morphism, or a trace-class invariant $\mathcal{F}(P)$ includes the implicit quantifier:*

$$
  \forall\, q \in \Omega_\alpha(P),
$$

*where $\Omega_\alpha(P)$ is a fixed connected component of the confinement locus $\Omega(P)$ — a single confinement phase.*

**Phase-scoped theorems (these hold only within a single $\Omega_\alpha$):**

- *Level sufficiency — preservation of Markov trace under unfolding.*
- *Shell-closure stability — monotonicity of $\sigma_F$ along the dressing chain and local maxima at noble-gas $Z$.*
- *Functoriality of $\mathrm{tr}_M: \mathbf{Conf} \to \mathbb{C}(q)$.*
- *Multi-operator binding structure — constancy of the active support.*

**Boundary phenomena — not covered by within-phase theorems:**

- ***Deconfinement events.*** *At $q \in \partial\Omega(P)$ with Slater-strict feasibility lost, $P$ ceases to be realisable as a closed-braid bound state.*
- ***Regime switches.*** *At $q \in \partial\Omega(P)$ with a $\mu$-jump but preserved feasibility, the active support gains or loses a Wedderburn block.*
- ***Critical scaling.*** *Near a phase boundary, $\sigma_F$ typically has a non-analytic limit.*
- ***Chemical and nuclear rearrangements.*** *Transitions between disconnected components of $\Omega(P)$ for a composite $P$ are realised physically as structural rearrangements. These transitions require an explicit cross-component analysis using the stratification of $\Omega(P)$.*

**Practical reading.**
*When encountering a confinement-framework statement, treat $q$ as ranging over a fixed open phase unless the text addresses boundary behaviour. Numerical witnesses evaluate at a single $q = q_0 \approx 1.110$. As long as that point lies in $\Omega_\alpha(P)$, all within-phase theorems apply. Auditing behaviour across $q$ ranges requires explicit stratification analysis.*
