A particle in this chapter is a strand, and an interaction is a braid. The picture raises a question before any dictionary can be built: when three particles meet, does the collision need an operator of its own, or is it assembled from ordinary two-particle crossings?

The answer is the second, though nothing deeper forces it. A braided monoidal category is by definition one whose exchange datum is binary, and the hexagon identities that govern how that datum meets a composite object are axioms of the structure, not consequences of it. Pairwise interaction is therefore built into the choice of scaffold made in Chapter 1; it is not derived from the choice.

What the proposition below adds is narrower, and still worth stating. Given that scaffold, no *further* many-body assumption is needed: the hexagon already fixes how a composite crosses a third object, the two resolutions of a triple crossing agree, so the assembly is unambiguous. The same proposition also counts the cost of that assembly.

We record this first, because it fixes the scope of everything that follows. Every operator in this chapter acts on two strands.
