The *substrate parameter* $q \in \mathbb{C}^*$ is the deformation
parameter of the Frobenius--Hopf algebra $A$. It governs the
non-commutativity of the algebraic substrate: when $q = 1$ the algebra
is commutative and the quantum structure degenerates to its classical
shadow.

The *quantum Planck constant* associated to $q$ is

$$
\hbar_q \;=\; 1 - q^{-1} \;=\; \frac{q - 1}{q}.
$$

The pair $(q, \hbar_q)$ parametrizes the deviation of $A$ from
commutativity. In the classical limit $q \to 1$ one has $\hbar_q \to 0$,
recovering the commutative coordinate ring and the smooth manifold
geometry of the base space.

Two structural properties follow directly from the definition.
First, the invertibility condition $q \ne 0$ ensures that $A$ is a
genuine Hopf algebra (the antipode is well-defined). Second,
$\hbar_q \ne 0$ whenever $q \ne 1$, so the non-commutative regime
$q \ne 1$ always carries a non-trivial quantum scale. The substrate
parameter therefore provides a single real degree of freedom that
interpolates continuously between the quantum substrate and the
classical continuum.
