**Proposition.**
*The confinement level $\ell(K, q) = \min\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$ is unique and well-defined because:*

1. *the filtration $J^\bullet(q)$ is **monotone**: $J^0(q) \subseteq J^1(q) \subseteq \cdots \subseteq J^m(q) = H_n(R, q)$;*
2. *the set of valid levels $\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$ is **upward-closed** in $\{0, 1, \ldots, m\}$ (if $T_w \in J^\ell(q)$ and $\ell' \geq \ell$, then $T_w \in J^{\ell'}(q)$);*
3. *the set is **nonempty** (it contains $m$ since $J^m = H_n$).*

*Therefore, the minimum exists and is unique. Tied operators ([confinement level](#def:confinement-level)) enter the filtration together at a single level, so the level remains uniquely defined even with degeneracy.*

*We have local uniqueness in $q$: by the continuity of shadow prices on the generic locus, $\ell(K, q')$ is locally constant on a neighborhood of $q$, except at the boundary variety where some $E(\lambda, q) = 0$. See the $\preceq_q$ ordering in [local shadow ordering](#def:local-shadow-ordering).*
