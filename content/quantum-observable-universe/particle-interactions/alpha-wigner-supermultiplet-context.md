**Remark (the $A=4$ closure in the nuclear-physics literature).**

The $A=4$ magic shell identified in
[`conj:wigner-su4-from-spin-isospin`](#conj:wigner-su4-from-spin-isospin) is the
first **Wigner $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ supermultiplet** \cite{wigner1937}. The four
single-nucleon spin-isospin states — $p\uparrow,\ p\downarrow,\ n\uparrow,\
n\downarrow$ — span the four-dimensional fundamental of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, and their
totally antisymmetric product is the one-dimensional singlet
$\Lambda^{4}(\mathbb{C}^{4})$: the closed shell of ${}^{4}\mathrm{He}$. The
vanishing $\dim \Lambda^{A}(\mathbb{C}^{4}) = \binom{4}{A}$ for $A > 4$ is the
categorical statement that the shell admits no fifth nucleon, fixing the $A = 4$
locality of the closure.

In the standard nuclear-mass systematics this closed-shell binding is treated
phenomenologically. The Wigner mass term $E_{W} = W(A)\,|N-Z|/A$ \cite{satula1997}
vanishes identically at $N = Z$, so it does not account for the anomalous extra
binding of the even-even self-conjugate nuclei — the $\alpha$ particle chief among
them; that binding is supplied by a separate cusp correction, with empirical
closed-shell scale $\Delta_{W} \approx 6.3\ \mathrm{MeV}$ \cite{satula1997}.

The framework locates this binding in the representation content of the nucleon
braids rather than fitting it. The $T = 0$ isoscalar channel — the self-conjugate
partition $(2,2) \vdash 4$ — is the subject of
[`prop:wigner-np-pairing`](#prop:wigner-np-pairing), whose $q$-hook scale
reproduces $\Delta_{W}$ without a free parameter; the fully antisymmetric
$\Lambda^{4}$ channel is the closed-shell supermultiplet of the conjecture above.
What nuclear phenomenology encodes in the Wigner term, the framework reads off the
$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ structure of the $A = 4$ shell.
