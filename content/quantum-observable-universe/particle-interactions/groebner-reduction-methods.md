The Hecke algebra normal form is computed by iterative
Gröbner–Shirshov reduction with three rewriting rules:

1. **Hecke relation** (Rule 0):
   $\sigma_i^2 \to (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i + \mathbf{1}$.
   Reduces word length by 1; produces two output words.

2. **Far commutativity** (Rule 1):
   $\sigma_i \sigma_j \to \sigma_j \sigma_i$ for $|i - j| \geq 2$.
   Permutes generators without changing length.

3. **Yang–Baxter relation** (Rule 2):
   $\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \to \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}$.
   Applied in the direction that decreases shortlex order.

The reduction is **confluent**: the final normal form is independent
of the order in which rules are applied.  The intermediate word count,
however, depends critically on the reduction strategy.

## The $H_A$-power factorisation

For the $\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{p}\cdots\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}$
ordering (all protons first, then all neutrons), every normal form
coefficient factors as $\pm 2^a \times H_A^k$ where $a$ counts the
number of $\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{n}$ crossings in the reduction path
and $k$ counts the number of Hecke-relation applications.  This is
verified for ${}^1\mathrm{H}$ through ${}^9\mathrm{Be}$.

The factorisation enables a compressed representation: each word
stores only $(\mathrm{sign}, a, k)$ instead of a full Laurent
polynomial.  For atoms with mixed orderings
($\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{n}\cdots$), the
factorisation breaks and coefficients are general Laurent polynomials.

## Computational engines

Four Rust engines implement the reduction with increasing
optimisation:

| Engine | Representation | Peak memory | Speed |
|:-------|:---------------|:------------|:------|
| v9 (hecke-bigint) | BigInt polynomial | $O(A!)$ bytes | exact, slow |
| v11 (hecke-modular) | $\mathbb{F}_P$ dense array | $O(A! \times 200)$ bytes | 5$\times$ faster |
| v13 (hecke-stream) | streaming + leading-term cancel | $O(N)$ during reduce | lower peak |
| v14 (hecke-hapower) | sparse $H_A^k$ pairs | $O(A! \times 20)$ bytes | fits ${}^{11}\mathrm{B}$ |

The modular engine uses the Mersenne prime $P = 2^{127} - 1$ with
fast Mersenne reduction: $x \bmod P = (x \gg 127) + (x \mathbin{\&} P)$.
Modular multiplication uses four $\texttt{u64} \times \texttt{u64}
\to \texttt{u128}$ products instead of the $O(128)$-step Russian
peasant algorithm.

## LP-dual reduction ordering

Since confluent reductions permit arbitrary rule ordering, the LP
dual determines the most efficient strategy:

- **Cancellation-first**: apply Rule 0 when the shortened word
  already exists in the stable set.  This produces immediate
  cancellation, reducing the word count before other rules fire.

- **Leading-term cancellation**: consolidate pending words
  *before* reduction.  Words with opposite-sign coefficients on the
  same basis element cancel without any reduction work (Buchberger
  S-polynomial criterion applied to Hecke algebras).

- **Crossing order**: within the braid construction, the order of
  crossing multiplication affects intermediate size.  Adjacent
  generators do not commute; only far-commuting pairs
  ($|i - j| \geq 2$) may be reordered.

## Reproducibility

All computations are reproducible from the Rust source in
`tools/hecke-engine/`.  Certificates (JSON files containing
$F_{\mathrm{Pauli}}$, $\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{alt}}$, and
$\mathrm{net}$ polynomials evaluated at $q_0$) are generated by
the engine and committed to the repository.  Witness files in
`computations/` record every mass prediction with
full intermediate values.
