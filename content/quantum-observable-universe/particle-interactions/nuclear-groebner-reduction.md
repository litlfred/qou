Every nucleus $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ is a braid word in the braid group
$B_A$ ([braid group](#def:braid-group)), where $A = Z + N$.  This
braid word is not unique — different orderings of the Hopf crossings
give different representatives of the same link.  The **Gröbner--Shirshov
reduction** in the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_A(q)$ produces a unique
**normal form** — the lowest-energy representative.

**Convention.** Throughout this paper, $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ always
denotes the **reduced** (Gröbner--Shirshov normal form) nuclear braid word
unless explicitly stated otherwise.

**Proposition.**
Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$ — where $q$ is the braiding
eigenvalue ([eigenvalue extraction](#prop:braiding-eigenvalue-extraction)),
a categorical invariant of $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$, not a free
parameter — let $R[B_A]$ be the group algebra of the braid group
on $A$ strands, and let $I$ be the two-sided ideal generated by
the Skein relation

$$
  \sigma_i - \sigma_i^{-1}
  - (q - q^{-1})
   \in  I
  \qquad (i = 1, \ldots, A-1).
$$

The Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_A(q) = R[B_A] / I$ has an admissible system
([admissible system](#unital-groebner-bases:def:admissible-system))
$(B, <)$ where:

- $B$ = positive braid monomials $\sigma_{i_1} \sigma_{i_2} \cdots \sigma_{i_k}$ with $i_j \in \{1, \ldots, A-1\}$,
- $<$ = length-lexicographic ordering (shorter words first, then
  lexicographic on generator indices).

The Gröbner--Shirshov basis $G$ for $I$ with respect to $(B, <)$ consists of:

1. **Hecke relation**: $\sigma_i^2 - (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i - 1$
   (eliminates all squares).

2. **Yang--Baxter relation**: $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i - \sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$ (braid relation,
   reduces to the length-lex-smaller form).

3. **Far commutativity**: $\sigma_i\sigma_j - \sigma_j\sigma_i$
   for $|i - j| \geq 2$ (sorts distant generators).

The remainder $r(\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{raw}}(Z, N), G)$ under the division
algorithm
([division algorithm](#unital-groebner-bases:def:division-algorithm))
is the **unique normal form**:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(Z, N)
   := 
  r\bigl(\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{raw}}(Z, N), G\bigr)
   \in  H_A(q).
$$

Uniqueness follows from the main theorem of the Gröbner--Shirshov basis theory
([Gröbner--Shirshov basis](#unital-groebner-bases:def:groebner-basis)): the
remainder is independent of the reduction path when $G$ is a
Gröbner--Shirshov basis.

**The nuclear braid word.**
The unreduced braid word for $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ in $B_A$ is:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(Z, N)
  = 
  \prod_{1 \leq i < j \leq A}
  \sigma_{i}^{\epsilon(i, j)}
$$

where the crossing sign $\epsilon(i, j)$ depends on the nucleon
types at positions $i$ and $j$:

$$
  \epsilon(i, j) = 
  \begin{cases}
    +1 & \text{if } i, j \text{ both protons} \\[3pt]
    -1 & \text{if } i, j \text{ both neutrons} \\[3pt]
    0  & \text{if mixed (resolved as }
         \tfrac{1}{2}(\sigma_i + \sigma_i^{-1})\text{)}
  \end{cases}
$$

In $H_A(q)$, the inverse $\sigma_i^{-1}$ is eliminated via the
Hecke relation:
$\sigma_i^{-1} = \sigma_i - (q - q^{-1})$.
The mixed crossing $\tfrac{1}{2}(\sigma_i + \sigma_i^{-1})$
becomes $\sigma_i - \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})$.

This gives $2^{|{\mathrm{mixed}}|}$ terms in $H_A(q)$ before
reduction (one per mixed crossing), where
$|{\mathrm{mixed}}| = p \cdot n$.  The Gröbner--Shirshov reduction
collapses this exponential expansion into the normal form.

**Computational results.**
For small nuclei (exact computation via `nuclear-braid-reduction.py`):

| Nucleus | $A$ | Unreduced | Reduced | Max length | $\Delta\mathrm{Weight}$ |
|---------|-----|-----------|---------|------------|------------------------|
| Deuteron | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | $-0.174$ |
| ${}^{3}\mathrm{H}$ | 3 | 6 | 4 | 2 | $-0.344$ |
| ${}^{3}\mathrm{He}$ | 3 | 4 | 4 | 2 | $-0.307$ |
| ${}^{4}\mathrm{He}$ | 4 | 18 | 8 | 3 | $-0.605$ |
| ${}^{6}\mathrm{Li}$ | 6 | 384 | 32 | 5 | $-2.763$ |

The $\Delta\mathrm{Weight}$ column shows the change in total Hecke
weight from parent to daughter under beta decay
$\mathcal{N}(Z, N) \to \mathcal{N}(p\!+\!1, n\!-\!1)$.  It is
always **negative**: the daughter has lower Hecke weight, consistent
with beta decay releasing energy.

**Normal form of ${}^{3}\mathrm{H}$.**
The reduced braid word for tritium is:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(1, 2)
  = 
  -0.145
  +  (-0.015)\sigma_0
  +  1.539\sigma_1
  +  0.162\sigma_0\sigma_1.
$$

The dominant term is $+1.539\sigma_1$ — the strong-force Hopf
crossing between the two neutrons, amplified by repeated Hecke
reduction of higher-order braid words.

**Connection to the half-life.**
The beta-decay half-life depends on the **difference** between the
parent and daughter normal forms:

$$
  \mathcal{N}(Z, N)
  - \mathcal{N}(p\!+\!1, n\!-\!1)
  = 
  \sum_{w \in B} \Delta c_w \cdot w
$$

where $\Delta c_w$ are the coefficient differences.  The Markov
trace of this difference gives the transition amplitude:

$$
  |\mathcal{M}_q|^2
   \propto 
  \left|
    \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Markov}}\!\left(
      \mathcal{N}(Z, N)
      - \mathcal{N}(p\!+\!1, n\!-\!1)
    \right)
  \right|^2.
$$

This is the quantity that determines the half-life via the Fermi
golden rule.  For ${}^{3}\mathrm{H} \to {}^{3}\mathrm{He}$,
the $\Delta\mathrm{Weight} = -0.344$ is large (mirror transition,
maximal overlap), giving the shortest half-life.  For
${}^{14}\mathrm{C} \to {}^{14}\mathrm{N}$, the normal forms
differ by many more terms (the $n = 8$ shell structure), giving a
smaller Markov trace and longer half-life.

**Scaling.**
The number of reduced terms grows polynomially in $A$ (not
exponentially), because the Gröbner--Shirshov basis eliminates all squares
and sorts all commuting generators.  The maximum word length in
the normal form is bounded by $A - 1$ (the longest reduced word
in $H_A$ is $\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \cdots \sigma_{A-1}$).  For
${}^{14}\mathrm{C}$ ($A = 14$): the normal form has at most
$\binom{13}{k}$ terms of length $k$, totalling at most $2^{13} = 8192$ terms — tractable for numerical evaluation via the
Markov trace.

## Proof

Uniqueness of the normal form is the confluence half of the
Gröbner--Shirshov / Diamond-Lemma machinery applied to $H_A(q)$.

1. **The relation set $G$ is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis.** The three
   families — the Hecke quadratic $\sigma_i^2 - (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i - 1$,
   the braid relation $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i - \sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$,
   and far commutativity $\sigma_i\sigma_j - \sigma_j\sigma_i$
   ($|i - j| \ge 2$) — are closed under composition with respect to the
   length-lexicographic order: every overlap ambiguity
   ($\sigma_i^3$, the $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$ overlap,
   and the far-commutation triples) reduces to zero. This is the
   classical admissibility check for the
   [Gröbner--Shirshov basis](#unital-groebner-bases:def:groebner-basis)
   of the Iwahori--Hecke algebra.

2. **Confluence $\Rightarrow$ path-independent remainder.** By the
   Diamond Lemma \cite{bergman1978}, a confluent rewriting system yields a
   remainder under the
   [division algorithm](#unital-groebner-bases:def:division-algorithm)
   that is independent of the reduction path. Hence
   $r(\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{raw}}(Z, N), G)$ is a well-defined element of
   $H_A(q)$ — the unique normal form $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$.

3. **Termination.** Length-lex is a well-order on positive braid
   monomials and each rule strictly decreases it, so reduction
   terminates; with confluence this gives existence and uniqueness
   together.
