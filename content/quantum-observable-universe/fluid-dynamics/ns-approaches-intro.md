The NS obstruction places the classical Navier–Stokes regularity question inside a one-parameter family of well-posed $q$-deformed equations. We present the conjectures, partial results, and proof strategies that organise the degeneration at $q = 1$ into a coherent research programme.

**Breakdown at the classical limit.**
We model the classical limit as a phase transition with a critical point at $q = 1$. The blowup mechanism manifests as a Yekutieli lifting obstruction in the brane tower. The critical exponent of the enstrophy divergence depends solely on the Euler characteristic $\chi(A)$. The transfinite size of the blowup mode exceeds the mass-gap infinitesimal. This structural prediction supplies a potential counter-example to classical Navier–Stokes regularity via the $q \to 1$ degeneration.

**Convergence and blowup criteria.**
The convergence machinery relies on a $q$-deformed Aubin–Lions lemma, which establishes the compactness of the solution family as $q \to 1$. We adapt the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion to the $q$-deformed setting. A vorticity lower bound for anti-parallel filament collision provides the essential last-gap estimate. 

**Geometric mechanism at collision.**
The Levi degeneracy conjecture posits that contact non-degeneracy at vortex collision determines Navier–Stokes regularity. The Townsend evolution under Biot–Savart strain predicts a first-order flattening $\kappa \to 0$. However, hyperbolic-overlap strain saturation tracking the strain dynamics yields $\kappa^2 = \pi\nu/(2\Gamma+\pi\nu)$, preserving Levi non-degeneracy.

**Self-similar blowup and future directions.**
We formulate an explicit self-similar blowup profile using the Leray equations with $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ constraints. This consolidates the quantum Navier–Stokes programme, bridging categorical regularity and the Clay problem. This research programme serves as the foundation for subsequent chapters invoking the brane fluid as a categorical substrate.
