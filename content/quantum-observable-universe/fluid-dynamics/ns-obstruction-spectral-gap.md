Under the categorical origin of the crossing energy ([crossing energy](#def:crossing-energy)), the $q \to 1$ limit corresponds exactly to the **Hecke spectral-gap closure**. The eigenvalues $(q, -q^{-1})$ of the Hecke generator $\sigma$ on $V \otimes V$ collide at $(1, -1)$. The separation between the symmetric and antisymmetric transport channels vanishes. In this reading:

- The obstruction class $\mathrm{NS}(A)$ measures the failure of the $q$-deformed transport to remain diagonalisable as $q \to 1$.
- Blowup of smooth solutions corresponds to the inability of the Markov trace to separate $S^{2} V$ from $\Lambda^{2} V$ in the classical limit. This represents an algebraic failure mode, distinct from the classical continuous-energy blowup picture.
- We have $\mathrm{NS}(A) = 0$ precisely when the categorical volume constraint remains rigid through the $q \to 1$ limit. This occurs when a codimension-$\geq 2$ locus in the eigenvalue moduli dominates the classical limit.

This supplies an algebraic failure criterion for smoothness without reference to continuous energy estimates. We test the obstruction by determining whether the Hecke spectral gap closes to first order or higher order in $(q - 1)$.
