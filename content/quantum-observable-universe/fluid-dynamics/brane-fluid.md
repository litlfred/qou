The brane of a [quantum observable universe](#def:quantum-observable-universe) carries a natural fluid dynamics. The [substrate parameter](#def:substrate-parameter) $q$ sets the effective brane thickness. At $q = 1$, the brane is infinitely thin (classical vacuum). At $q > 1$, it acquires a finite transverse extent controlled by the $q$-deformed contact geometry. The resulting dynamics inherits three features absent from ordinary Navier--Stokes theory:

1. **Algebraic viscosity.** The $q$-Laplacian $\Delta_q$ ([q-Laplacian interpretation](#rem:q-laplacian-interpretation)) replaces the classical Laplacian. Its eigenvalues are $q$-integers $[k]_q$. The viscous dissipation rate is algebraically quantised rather than continuously variable.

2. **Gauge-covariant advection.** The velocity field $v = \nabla_A S$ is defined via the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ connection $A$ rather than a flat gradient. Advection along $v$ preserves the horizontal self-duality $F_A|_H = {*_H}F_A|_H$, coupling fluid motion to the gauge structure.

3. **Topological vortex quantisation.** The circulation around any closed vortex filament $\varrho$ is constrained by the $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ holonomy to discrete values $[j]_q[j+1]_q$. This produces the [discrete vortex spectrum](#prop:discrete-vortex-spectrum).
