We physically interpret the channel algebra of [the two-vortex proposition](#prop:two-vortex-channel-algebra) as the scattering theory of interacting vortex loops.

**The $R$-matrix is the two-vortex $S$-matrix.** Two [geodesic vortices](#rem:geodesic-vortices) interact through the braid operator of the [$R$-matrix braiding](#prop:r-matrix-braiding-structure). These vortices are soliton-like critical points of $\mathcal{E}_q$ that reduce to Hasimoto solitons as $q \to 1$. The Yang--Baxter equation enforces the factorization of multi-vortex scattering. The outcome of a three-loop interaction is independent of the order of pairwise exchanges. The loops pass through one another with only a phase reassignment in each channel and no internal-state production. A factorized $S$-matrix provides no mechanism to disrupt each loop's internal dynamics. The interaction remains weak.

**The Hopf link is the minimal scattering event.** Two loops with linking number one close the braid $\sigma_1^2$. By the spectral square of the proposition, the event decomposes over the [Hopf channel](#def:van-der-waals-hopf-channel) pair as $\sigma_1^2 = q^2 e_+ + q^{-2} e_-$. The vanishing hyperbolic volume of the Hopf link matches the soliton picture: the two loops disturb each other's path while sharing no bonded volume.

**Channel decay is the eigenvalue ratio.** Per crossing, the antisymmetric channel carries weight $-q^{-1}$ against the symmetric channel's $q$. This yields a relative factor $-q^{-2}$ (the eigenvalue-ratio identity of the proposition). At the substrate anchor $q_0 > 1$, this factor acts as a contraction: $q_0^{-2} \approx 0.81$. Each crossing damps the antisymmetric disturbance by roughly a fifth. Together with the gapped second variation of $\mathcal{E}_q$ along each filament ([geodesic vortices](#rem:geodesic-vortices)), this explains how crossing disturbances decay in the substrate rapidly enough for soliton solutions to persist. We observe an algebraic contraction between loops at each crossing and a spectral gap within each loop.
