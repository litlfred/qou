Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum universe with $A$ realised on the torus $\Sigma_1$ (genus $g = 1$). Then

$$
  \chi(A) = 2 - 2g = 0.
$$

**The NS obstruction vanishes.**
The Euler class of the Reeb bundle is $\kappa_0 = e(\xi)$ with $\mathrm{tr}(\kappa_0) = \chi(A) = 0$. For every $n \geq 1$, $2\kappa \mid 0$ for all $\kappa$, so $\kappa_0 \bmod 2\kappa = 0$ at every Kummer level. The [Kummer specialisation](#prop:ns-kummer-specialisation) gives no obstruction:

$$
  \mathrm{NS}(A) = \kappa_0\big|_\Sigma = 0.
$$

The torus quantum universe is **NS-regular**.

**Physical meaning.**
The Levi form is allowed to degenerate at collision loci. Vortex cores flatten into sheets. Vorticity spreads rather than concentrates. The classical Navier--Stokes equation (the $q = 1$ specialisation) admits smooth global solutions for all smooth initial data.

**Failure of the torus to support observed physics.**
The torus has $g = 1$, allowing only one torus knot embedding: $T_{2,3}$ (the trefoil, Seifert genus 1). The knot spectrum contains a single generator. No muon, tau, or higher-generation quarks appear. The fundamental group $\pi_1(\Sigma_1) = \mathbb{Z}^2$ is abelian. All $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ representations factor through $\mathrm{U}(1)$. The gauge group reduces. The full particle spectrum does not emerge.

**Failure of observability.**
On $\Sigma_1$, flat $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ connections exist with holonomy in $\mathrm{U}(1) \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$. These are reducible: $H^0(\Sigma_1, \mathrm{Ad}(P)) \neq 0$ (the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{u}(1) \subset \mathfrak{su}(2)$ provides a nonzero equivariant section). This yields $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) \neq 0$. The observability axiom fails.

The torus constitutes a quantum pre-universe (QPU), not a QOU.

The torus provides the simplest example where $\chi = 0$ forces NS regularity. It is physically trivial. The vanishing Euler characteristic explains why the torus cannot support a rich universe and why its fluid dynamics remains tame.

**Other $\chi = 0$ examples.**
Any genus-1 surface yields $\chi = 0$: the flat torus $\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$, the Heisenberg nilmanifold $\mathrm{Heis}(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathrm{Heis}(\mathbb{R})$, and any Seifert fibration over an elliptic curve. All are NS-regular.
