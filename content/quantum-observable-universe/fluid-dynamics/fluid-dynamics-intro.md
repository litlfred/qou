The brane of a quantum observable universe carries a natural fluid dynamics whose viscosity, vortex spectrum, and turbulence structure are determined by the substrate parameter $q$. This chapter develops the $q$-deformed Navier--Stokes system on the brane and extracts two principal consequences: global regularity of the $q$-deformed equation for $q > 1$, and a categorical obstruction that controls whether the classical Navier--Stokes equation ($q = 1$) admits finite-time blowup.

The first three sections establish the hydrodynamic framework. We derive the $q$-Navier--Stokes equation from the Reeb derivation on the Frobenius--Hopf algebra. We characterise the discrete vortex spectrum and helicity conservation forced by the braided monoidal structure. We then develop the turbulence theory, including $q$-deformed Kolmogorov scaling and acoustic horizons.

The final two sections contain the chapter's main results. We prove global well-posedness of the $q$-NS system for $q > 1$ via spectral Sobolev estimates on the quantum Peter--Weyl decomposition. We identify the NS obstruction — a categorical invariant that measures the failure of regularity at $q = 1$. The obstruction is computable from the Hecke algebra and provides a new criterion for the classical Navier--Stokes regularity problem.
