We define the **classical Riemann zeta function** $\zeta(s)$ as the adelic global limit of the local $q$-geometries. As $q \to 1$, the local zeta functions $Z(\mathcal{B}, q^{-s})$ at each prime fiber assemble into the Euler product:

$$
\zeta(s) = \prod_{p \text{ prime}} \frac{1}{1 - p^{-s}}
         = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^s},
  \qquad \mathrm{Re}(s) > 1
$$

Each maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of norm $N(\mathfrak{m}) = p^f$ contributes a local factor to the product. The adelic completion computes the product over all primes simultaneously to yield the global zeta function.

The non-trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ form the classical shadows of the Reeb eigenvalues. Under the spectral coordinate mapping, $\alpha_i = q^{\rho_i} \to e^{-\rho_i \log p}$ at each prime $p$. The global zeros arise from the coherent alignment of the local spectra.
