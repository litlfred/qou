**Cheat sheet for surreal notation.**
We develop the full surreal-number formalism in the surreal-numbers appendix ([`app:surreals`](#app:surreals)). This formalism includes the Conway construction, birthday function, sign sequences, standard-part functor, and Hahn series. We provide a reading aid below for the substrate-level uses of $\omega$ and $\varepsilon$ in this chapter and in the Millennium-bound discussions ([`sec:millennium-bounds-via-surreals`](#sec:millennium-bounds-via-surreals)).

See [Table](#tbl:surreal-notation-cheat-sheet-table-data).

When $\varepsilon$ appears in substrate-level definitions ([`def:reeb-epsilon`](#def:reeb-epsilon), [`def:q-from-epsilon`](#def:q-from-epsilon), and the joint Galois-braid action), it denotes the positive infinitesimal $1/\omega$. The appendix machinery is not required here.

When a surreal-bound statement appears ([BSD](#rem:bsd-surreal-bound), [Collatz](#rem:collatz-surreal-bound), or the unified [graded-Mikusiński bound](#rem:surreal-bounds-graded-mikusinski)), the cheat-sheet entries for $\mathrm{st}$ and $a \sim b$ suffice. The bound holds up to a $1 + \varepsilon$ factor. Applying $\mathrm{st}$ recovers the classical statement.

To understand the full Conway construction, sign sequences as birthday-graded braid words, the Bloch-Suslin / Borel discharge of the keystone, and standard-part functor limits, refer to the appendix. This cheat sheet supports all substrate-level applications in the current chapter.
