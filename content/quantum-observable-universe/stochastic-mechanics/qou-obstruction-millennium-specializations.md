**Remark (millennium problems as specializations).**
Each millennium problem represents a property $\mathcal{P}$ holding unconditionally at the $q$-scale. The obstruction class is $[\mathcal{P}] \in \mathrm{Obs}(\mathbf{C}, q \to 1) \cong k$. In the QOU, a single obstruction scalar controls all three problems.

See [Table 4](#tbl:qou-obstruction-reduction-table4-data).

The three obstructions are images of the class $\chi(A) \in HH^2 \cong k$ under distinct evaluation maps:

- **RH**: Evaluate $\chi(A)$ on the spectral localization map $HH^2 \to H^2(\mathrm{Spec}(\xi_q))$.
- **NS**: Evaluate $\chi(A)$ on the collision restriction map $HH^2 \to H^2(\Sigma, \mathcal{B}|_\Sigma)$.
- **BSD**: Evaluate $\chi(A)$ on the elliptic restriction map $HH^2 \to H^2(\mathcal{E}_q, \mathcal{B}|_{\mathcal{E}_q})$.

Because $HH^2$ is one-dimensional, these evaluations are proportional. A single number, the Euler characteristic $\chi(A) = -6$, determines whether the millennium problems persist through the classical limit.

**Corollary.** Resolving one millennium problem constrains the other two. If the classical limit functor $\mathcal{L}$ preserves $\mathcal{P}$ for one problem, it exhibits the same behavior for the others. The mechanism is identical; only the evaluation map changes.
