The [categorical proton](#def:categorical-proton) $\mathfrak{p}$ is
uniquely determined up to braid isotopy by the triple
$(\mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{d})$ and the Borromean
braiding pattern $\mathcal{B}_3$.  Its Hecke normal form in $H_3(q)$
is unique, and the $S_3$ permutation symmetry on the colour tubes
ensures the proton does not depend on which positions are assigned
to $(\mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{d})$.


**Strategy.** Similar to the neutron, we rely on the uniqueness of Hecke normal forms. The $S_3$ group action permuting the colored valence tubes corresponds to inner automorphisms, grouping the three configurations into a single equivalence class. Finally, the stabilizer is unique since it solely minimizes $|\mathrm{tr}_M|$.

While the proof of uniqueness up to isotopy employs standard Hecke normal form and $S_3$-symmetry arguments from conventional knot representation theory, establishing this specific topological uniqueness constraint for the categorical proton is a mathematically novel formalization essential to the deterministic spectral predictions of the Quantum Observable Universe.
