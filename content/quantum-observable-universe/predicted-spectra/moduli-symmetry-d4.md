**Proposition (Dihedral $D_4$-action on the moduli line).**
The two natural involutions on the substrate moduli line $\mathbb{C}^\times$,

$$
  \sigma\colon q \mapsto iq
  \qquad\text{(complex rotation by $\pi/2$)},
  \qquad
  \tau\colon q \mapsto q^{-1}
  \qquad\text{(Hecke bar involution)},
$$

generate a faithful action of the **dihedral group of order 8**:

$$
  D_4
   =  \langle\,\sigma,\,\tau \;\mid\; \sigma^4 = \tau^2 = (\sigma\tau)^2 = 1\,\rangle.
$$

The relation $(\sigma\tau)^2 = 1$ is the dihedral commutation
$\tau\sigma\tau = \sigma^{-1}$. Verification:

$$
  \tau\sigma\tau(q)
   =  \tau(\sigma(q^{-1}))
   =  \tau(iq^{-1})
   =  (iq^{-1})^{-1}
   =  -iq
   =  \sigma^{-1}(q).
$$

**The golden octet.**

The $D_4$-orbit of $\varphi$ in $\mathbb{C}^\times$ is the
**golden octet**

$$
  G_8
   :=  D_4 \cdot \varphi
   =  \bigl\{\,\varphi,\;i\varphi,\;-\varphi,\;-i\varphi,\;
              1/\varphi,\;i/\varphi,\;-1/\varphi,\;-i/\varphi\,\bigr\}.
$$

The eight elements partition by stabilizer into two cosets:

| Coset | Elements | Generator | Real / imaginary |
|-------|----------|-----------|------------------|
| $\sigma\langle 1\rangle$-orbit through $\varphi$ | $\{\varphi, i\varphi, -\varphi, -i\varphi\}$ | $\sigma^k \cdot \varphi$ | real (golden quartet $Q_+$) plus imaginary axis |
| $\sigma\langle 1\rangle$-orbit through $1/\varphi$ | $\{1/\varphi, i/\varphi, -1/\varphi, -i/\varphi\}$ | $\sigma^k \cdot 1/\varphi$ | bar-image of $Q_+$, by $\tau$ |

The intersection $G_8 \cap \mathbb{R}$ is the previous golden quartet
$Q = \{\,\varphi,\,-\varphi,\,1/\varphi,\,-1/\varphi\,\}$
([golden-quartet-galois](#prop:golden-quartet-galois));
the intersection $G_8 \cap i\mathbb{R}$ is its $\pi/2$-rotation
$iQ = \{\,i\varphi,\,-i\varphi,\,i/\varphi,\,-i/\varphi\,\}$.

Equivalently, the octet consists of the eight roots of

$$
  P_{\mathbb{C}}(z)  :=  P(z) \cdot P(z/i)
   =  (z^4 - 3z^2 + 1)(z^4 + 3z^2 + 1)
   =  z^8 - 7z^4 + 1
$$

over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, where $P(z) = z^4 - 3z^2 + 1$ is the quartic
of the golden quartet.

$$

**Real subgroup and the golden quartet.**

The subgroup of $D_4$ that preserves the real axis $\mathbb{R}^\times \subset
\mathbb{C}^\times$ is

$$
  D_4^{\mathbb{R}}
   =  \langle\,\sigma^2,\,\tau\,\rangle
   =  \langle\,-1,\;q \mapsto q^{-1}\,\rangle
   \cong  \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2,
$$

generated by sign reversal $q \mapsto -q$ and bar inversion. Its
orbit through $\varphi$ is exactly the **real golden quartet**
$Q$ — the same orbit identified in
[golden-quartet-galois](#prop:golden-quartet-galois). The full
$D_4$ extends this real $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ orbit
by the imaginary $\sigma$-rotations.

**The Möbius matrices have natural lifts to $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])$.**

The baryon integration-limit Möbius matrix
(Möbius golden fixed points)

$$
  M  =  \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 4 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z})
$$

acts on $\mathbb{CP}^1(\mathbb{C})$, fixing the real golden pair
$\{1/\varphi, -\varphi\}$. Under conjugation by
$S = \mathrm{diag}(i, 1) \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])$ — the
matrix realising $\sigma\colon q \mapsto iq$ — the conjugate

$$
  M^\sigma  :=  S^{-1} M S
   =  \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -4i \\ 4i & 3 \end{pmatrix}
   \in  \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])
$$

has fixed points $\{i/\varphi, -i\varphi\}$ — the
**$i$-rotated golden pair**. Its bar conjugate has fixed points
$\{i\varphi, -i/\varphi\}$. The four Möbius matrices

$$
  \{\,M,\;M',\;M^\sigma,\;M'^\sigma\,\}
$$

— two over $\mathbb{Z}$, two over $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ — together fix
the full golden octet $G_8$, with each matrix fixing exactly one
$\sigma\langle 1\rangle$-coset of $G_8$.

This shows that **$\mathbb{Z}[i]$, not $\mathbb{Z}$, is the natural
ring of definition** for the Möbius family: the four Möbius
transformations form a $D_4$-equivariant family on $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])$,
of which the original $M, M' \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ are the two
real-axis elements.

**Connection to the unit circle.**

$D_4$ also has an orbit on the unit circle $|q| = 1$: starting from
$\zeta_8 = e^{i\pi/4}$, the $D_4$-orbit is

$$
  D_4 \cdot \zeta_8
   =  \{\,\zeta_8,\,\zeta_8^3,\,\zeta_8^5,\,\zeta_8^7\,\}
   =  \{\,\text{primitive 8th roots of unity}\,\}.
$$

These are **not** in the golden octet $G_8$ (since $|\zeta_8| = 1$
but $|\varphi| \neq 1$), but they sit inside the same $D_4$-symmetric
universe of fixed and orbit points. Together $G_8 \cup
\{\zeta_8\text{-orbit}\}$ form the full $D_4$-fixed-locus structure
on $\mathbb{C}^\times$, indexed by orbit type:

| Orbit type | Representative | Stabilizer | Orbit size |
|------------|---------------|------------|------------|
| Real golden | $\varphi$ | trivial | 8 (= $G_8$) |
| Unit-circle $\zeta_8$ | $\zeta_8$ | $\langle\sigma\tau\rangle$ | 4 |
| Real $\pm 1$ | $1$ | $\langle\tau\rangle$ | 4 (= 4th roots) |
| Origin / infinity | $0$, $\infty$ | $D_4$ | 1 each |

The natural coordinates for the entire structure are therefore
**$q$ and $i$**: $q$ generates multiplicative motion on a fixed ray
through the origin, while $i$ is the order-4 generator of the
$\sigma$-rotation that connects rays.

**Remark on Mathematical Novelty:** Identifying Z[i]—rather than Z—as the strict ring of definition for the Mobius family under the D_4 action represents a conceptually novel structural constraint, tightly locking the moduli line symmetries to a complex algebraic origin.
