The categorical bound-state assignment from the previous section
distinguishes mesons from nucleons by braid word but does not yet
say which configurations *survive*.  Stability comes from the
torus-knot subclass: a nucleus is stable when its colour braid
admits a presentation as a torus knot $T(p, q)$, since the Hopf
fibration over the torus stabilises the knot under the substrate-
parameter flow.

We first define the stable-nucleus subspace and prove that the
torus-Hopf factorisation is the obstruction to decay.  The
remaining blocks identify which torus knots arise (the
prime-power family $T(p^n, q^n)$), confirm uniqueness of the
genus-four torus knot that mediates the strong-force carrier, and
close with the Bring-surface argument that pins this force-carrier
knot uniquely.  The section's output is the assignment
$\{\text{stable isotope}\} \leftrightarrow \{T(p^n, q^n)\}$ that
the atomic-mass appendix consumes wholesale.
