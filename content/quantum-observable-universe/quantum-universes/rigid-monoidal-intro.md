Before stating the quantum-universe axioms we fix the categorical setting in which they live. **Snake identity** (def) records the zig-zag equations that exhibit $V^{\vee}$ as a right dual of $V$, and **Why monoidal categories?** (rem) tracks two independent routes — Coecke--Kissinger from the grammar of composable processes, and Haag--DHR from superselection-sector analysis — that converge on a rigid braided monoidal framework for any compositional theory of physical systems. **Rigid monoidal category** (def) assembles these into the structure used throughout the chapter: an $R$-linear category with tensor product, unit, duals, and braiding, with the symmetric case isolated by $c_{Y,X} \circ c_{X,Y} = \mathrm{id}$. **$\mathbb{C}[z]$ and $\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$** (ex) closes the section with the two running examples — the polynomial ring on the affine line and the coordinate ring of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ — that recur through every subsequent definition. The next section, *The Quantum Universe*, adds the Frobenius--Hopf object, fibre functor, and differential calculus to this rigid monoidal scaffold.
