The perpetual obstruction ([obstruction calculation](#prop:q-gl-obstruction-calc)) and the classical limit ([classical recovery](#conj:q-gl-classical-limit)) coexist compatibly. This coexistence shows that the $q$-Geometric Langlands correspondence is a singular deformation of the classical theory.

**The classical limit sits at a degenerate point.**
The point $q = 1$ is the universal torsion point. It belongs to $\mu_n$ for every $n \geq 1$. At $q = 1$, the $q$-exterior derivative reduces to $d$, the obstruction $d_q^2$ vanishes, and the $q$-curvature $\mathcal{F}_q = 0$. Classical flatness $\nabla^2 = 0$ holds, as does the Beilinson--Drinfeld equivalence $\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{BD}}$. However, $q = 1$ lies in $\mu_\infty$, outside the non-torsion locus $\mathbb{G}_m^{\mathrm{nt}}$ where $\mathbb{L}_q$ is well-defined. The classical correspondence is the degenerate fibre of the $\mathbb{G}_m$-family, not a generic point.

**The perpetual obstruction constrains the non-torsion locus.**
Let $q \in \mathbb{G}_m^{\mathrm{nt}}$ and $\chi(A) \neq 0$. The obstruction class $\kappa_0$ is non-trivial at density-$1$ Kummer levels. Thus, $q$-flatness is strictly stronger than classical flatness. Fewer connections are $q$-flat. The moduli $\mathrm{LocSys}_{\check{G}_q}(X)$ is a proper subspace of the classical prediction.

**Compatibility.**
We resolve this apparent tension through three observations.

1. The classical limit $q \to 1$ approaches a boundary point of the parameter space $\mathbb{G}_m^{\mathrm{nt}}$, not an interior point. The $\mathbb{G}_m$-family $\{\mathbb{L}_q\}$ is defined on the open complement of $\mu_\infty$. The point $q = 1$ lies in its closure.

2. As $q \to 1$, the $(q-1)$-factor in the $q$-flatness equation $\nabla_q^2 = (q-1)\mathcal{F}_q$ forces $\nabla_q^2 \to 0$, even though $\mathcal{F}_q$ remains non-zero. The vanishing prefactor screens the curvature. The obstruction does not disappear; it becomes invisible.

3. The Kummer constraint (property (4) of the [$q$-GL conjecture](#conj:q-geometric-langlands)) encodes this singular behaviour. The equivalence degenerates precisely at roots of unity. The classical limit is the universal degeneration.

The generic fibre (non-torsion $q$) carries richer structure than the special fibre ($q = 1$). We recover the classical theory not by continuity, but by specialisation to a singular point.
