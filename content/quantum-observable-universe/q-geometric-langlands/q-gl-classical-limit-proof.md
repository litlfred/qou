Each component of the $q$-Geometric Langlands correspondence represents a continuous deformation in $q$. The $q \to 1$ limit of each yields its classical counterpart.

**$q$-Local systems.** At $q = 1$, the $q$-monodromy condition ([q-local system](#def:q-local-system)) becomes $\mathcal{L}_1(\varrho) = \mathrm{id}$ for contractible $\varrho$. This is the classical condition that monodromy depends only on the homotopy class. Hence $\mathrm{LocSys}_{G_1}(X) = \mathrm{LocSys}_G(X)$.

**$q$-$\mathcal{D}$-modules.** The $q$-flatness equation $\nabla_q^2 = (q-1)\mathcal{F}_q$ becomes $\nabla^2 = 0$ at $q=1$. The category $\mathcal{D}_q\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ specialises to the classical category of $\mathcal{D}$-modules.

**$q$-Hecke operators.** The eigenvalues $q$ and $-q^{-1}$ degenerate to $1$ and $-1$. The $q$-Hecke correspondence degenerates to the classical Hecke correspondence, and the factorisation defect $[\mathbf{T}_x, \mathbf{T}_y] = O(q-1)$ vanishes.

**$q$-Opers.** The transversality condition is $q$-independent and persists at $q = 1$, where $q$-opers become classical opers.

Because each ingredient converges and the equivalence $\mathbb{L}_q$ is uniquely determined by the fibre functor ([fibre-functor mediation](#conj:q-gl-fiber-functor)), the limit $\mathbb{L}_1 = \mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{BD}}$ emerges as the unique equivalence compatible with classical Hecke operators and the identity fibre functor. $\square$
