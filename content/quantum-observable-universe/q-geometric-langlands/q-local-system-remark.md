Classical local systems correspond to representations of $\pi_1(X)$ because their connections satisfy $d^2 = 0$. In a quantum observable universe, the perpetual curvature $d_q^2 \neq 0$ ([non-vanishing of $d_q^2$](#prop:categorical-dq-squared)) obstructs naive flatness. The $q$-monodromy condition absorbs this obstruction. For contractible loops, parallel transport deviates from the identity by a residue proportional to $(q-1)$. 

The Kummer filtration measures this deviation at finite levels. An obstruction class $\kappa_0 = e(\xi) \in H^2(\mathcal{S}, \mathbb{G}_m)$ vanishes modulo $n$ precisely when $n \mid \chi(A)$ ([Kummer filtration](#rem:kummer-filtration-obstruction)). At these levels, the $q$-monodromy becomes $n$-divisible, determining a sublocus of $\mathrm{LocSys}_{G_q}(X)$ with enhanced lifting properties through the brane tower. When $n \nmid \chi(A)$, the obstruction forces a non-trivial $q$-holonomy residue on every $q$-local system. This behavior mirrors the Hecke relation $(T - q)(T + q^{-1}) = 0$, where the symmetric group relation $(T-1)(T+1) = 0$ deforms to split braiding eigenvalues from $± 1$ to $q$ and $-q^{-1}$.
