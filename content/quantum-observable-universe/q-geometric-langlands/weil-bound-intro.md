The explicit formula links the distribution of defects to the spectrum of the Reeb flow. This spectrum must be stable. If the Reeb eigenvalues stray from the critical line, the oscillatory terms grow without bound, triggering catastrophic accumulation or rarefaction of defects.

We prove that the algebraic geometry of Bring's curve unconditionally constrains the eigenvalues to the critical line $\mathrm{Re}(\rho) = 1/2$. This constitutes the Weil bound—the finite-field analog of the Riemann hypothesis—applied directly to the spectral geometry of the substrate.
