By Tannaka--Krein reconstruction, the quantum group $\check{G}_q$ is
recovered as $\mathrm{Aut}^\otimes(\tau)$ where $\tau$ is the fibre
functor on $\mathbf{Rep}(\check{G}_q)$.  A $\check{G}_q$-local system
$\mathcal{L}_q$ is equivalent to a tensor functor
$\mathbf{Rep}(\check{G}_q) \to \mathrm{Vect}_X$, which in turn is
determined by the fibre data $\{V_\lambda^{(q)}(\mathcal{L}_q)_x\}$
over all $\lambda$ and $x$.

The Hecke eigensheaf property provides exactly this data: for each
$(\lambda, x)$ the eigenvalue isomorphism
$\mathbf{T}_{x,\lambda}^{(q)}(\mathcal{M}) \simeq \mathcal{M}
\otimes V_\lambda^{(q)}(\mathcal{L}_q)_x$ extracts the fibre
$V_\lambda^{(q)}(\mathcal{L}_q)_x$ as the multiplicity space.

The tensor compatibility of the Hecke isomorphisms ensures that the
assignment $V_\lambda^{(q)} \mapsto V_\lambda^{(q)}(\mathcal{L}_q)_x$
is a tensor functor, hence defines a point of
$\mathrm{LocSys}_{\check{G}_q}(X)$.  Rigidity of $\tau$
([fibre-functor rigidity](#rem:fibre-functor-rigidity)) guarantees
that any two tensor functors with isomorphic fibre data are uniquely
isomorphic.  $\square$
