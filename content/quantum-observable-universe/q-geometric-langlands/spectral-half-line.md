The spectral coordinate mapping $\alpha_i = q^{\rho_i}$ translates the
Hasse--Weil bound into a statement about the Reeb spectrum. Since
$|\alpha_i| = q^{1/2}$ and $|q^{\rho_i}| = q^{\mathrm{Re}(\rho_i)}$,
we obtain:

$$
\mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = \frac{1}{2} \qquad \text{for all } \rho_i \in \mathrm{Spec}(\xi_q), i = 1, \ldots, 8
$$

All 8 fundamental modes of the Reeb spectrum lie exactly on the critical
line $\mathrm{Re}(\rho) = 1/2$. This is the **finite-field Riemann
hypothesis** expressed in the language of the substrate: the spectral
oscillations in the explicit formula are perfectly balanced, with each
mode contributing fluctuations of amplitude
$|X^{\rho_i}/[\rho_i]_q| = X^{1/2}/|[\rho_i]_q|$ — square-root
cancellation.
