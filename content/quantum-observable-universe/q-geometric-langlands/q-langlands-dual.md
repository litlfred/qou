Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe with quantum gauge group $G_q$ ([quantum gauge group](#def:quantum-gauge-group)). The **$q$-Langlands dual group** $\check{G}_q$ is the quantum group whose representation category relates to $G_q$ by root system duality:

$$
  \check{G}_q = G_{q^{-1}}^{\vee}.
$$

Here, $(-)^\vee$ denotes the classical Langlands dual (interchanging roots and coroots). We also invert the substrate parameter, mapping $q \mapsto q^{-1}$.

For the QOU gauge group $G_q = \mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ ([quantum $\mathrm{SU}(2)$](#def:quantum-su2)):

$$
  \check{G}_q = \mathrm{SU}_{q^{-1}}(2) \cong \mathrm{SO}_{q^{-1}}(3).
$$

The root system of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is self-dual. The Langlands involution acts on the quantum parameter by $q \mapsto q^{-1}$.

The inversion $q \mapsto q^{-1}$ interchanges the two eigenvalues of the braiding: $q \leftrightarrow -q^{-1}$. This exchanges the symmetric and antisymmetric channels of the Iwahori--Hecke algebra. It represents the categorical manifestation of exchanging roots and coroots. In the $\mathbb{G}_m$-family picture, the Langlands involution is the inversion automorphism $\iota \colon \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{G}_m$, given by $\iota(q) = q^{-1}$. This automorphism preserves the torsion subgroups $\mu_n$ and acts as the identity on $\mu_2$.
