**Definition.**
*Let a molecule be assembled from $k$ sub-units carrying strand counts $n_1,\dots,n_k$. The **molecular strand-partition** is the composition*

$$
  \lambda = (n_1,\dots,n_k), \qquad
  |\lambda| = \sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i = N,
$$

*which records how the $N$ strands of the composite braid in $H_N(q)$ are distributed among the sub-units. We write $\ell(\lambda) = k$ for the number of sub-units.*

The strand-partition is the sole datum required for assembly. It is fixed by the topology of the join, requiring no chemical foreknowledge. For three acetylene units (each contributing two carbons), the carbon strand-partition is $\lambda = (2,2,2)$, yielding $N = 6$ and $k = 3$. This is an ordered composition, not an unordered partition. The sub-units are individuated. Reordering them constitutes a separate symmetry, governed by the [assembly projector](#def:molecular-assembly-projector).
