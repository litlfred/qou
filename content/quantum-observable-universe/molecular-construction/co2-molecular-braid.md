**Definition.**
*The **carbon dioxide** braid $CO_2$ joins one carbon atom $C(6, 6)$ and two oxygen atoms $O_1(8, 8)$ and $O_2(8, 8)$, forming two double bonds ($O{=}C{=}O$).*

We construct the atoms prior to assembly: the carbon atomic braid $\mathcal{A}_C \in H_{18}(q)$ has valence 4, while the oxygen atomic braids $\mathcal{A}_{O_1}, \mathcal{A}_{O_2} \in H_{24}(q)$ each have valence 2.

The molecular braid embeds in $H_{66}(q)$ ($18 + 24 + 24 = 66$ strands). The two double bonds exhaust carbon's 4 valence electrons (2 per bond) and both valence electrons of each oxygen. Each $C{=}O$ bond (order 2) consists of one $\sigma$ pair and one $\pi$ pair. Carbon splits its 4 valence electrons equally between $O_1$ and $O_2$, retaining no lone pairs.

We build the molecular braid iteratively. We start with $\mathcal{A}_C$ in normal form. We assemble $\mathcal{A}_{O_1}$ by embedding it, applying the inter-atomic crossings, applying 2 covalent bond operators (4 attractive $\sigma^{-1}$ crossings), and reducing to normal form. We then assemble $\mathcal{A}_{O_2}$ by embedding it, applying all inter-atomic crossings (including $O_1$–$O_2$ interactions), applying the final 2 covalent bond operators, and reducing to normal form again.
