The LP dual for $CO_2$ yields a categorical derivation of Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory. We map VSEPR concepts directly to the braid framework:

| VSEPR concept | Braid framework |
|:-------------|:---------------|
| Electron pair repulsion | $e$–$e$ crossing ($\sigma$, $q$-eigenvalue) increases $\Phi_1$ |
| Bond pair vs lone pair | Shared trefoil (2 attractive crossings) vs retained trefoil (1 attractive crossing) |
| Geometric arrangement | Strand ordering in $H_{N}(q)$ |
| Energy minimisation | $\Phi_1$ minimisation via LP dual |

For $CO_2$, the central carbon has two bond pairs (double bonds) and no lone pairs. VSEPR predicts a linear 180$^\circ$ bond angle. The LP dual confirms this: placing $O_1$ and $O_2$ on opposite sides of $C$ in the strand ordering minimises the $O$–$O$ proton repulsion crossings.

For $H_2O$, the central oxygen has two bond pairs (single bonds) and two lone pairs. VSEPR predicts a bent angle ($\approx 104.5^\circ$). The LP dual reflects this by forcing the two $H$ atoms into a non-symmetric strand arrangement to accommodate the lone pairs.

The braid framework derives geometric preferences directly from the Hecke algebra structure, bypassing the need for postulated repulsion rules. Bond angles emerge as properties of the optimal strand ordering rather than as input parameters.
