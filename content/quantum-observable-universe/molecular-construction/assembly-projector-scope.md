**Scope of the existence proposition.** Existence is pure algebra. It holds for every $\lambda$—bonded or not, stable or not. It makes no claim about energy or stability. A molecule is stable only when the binding linear program selects this symmetric block as its optimum. The binding energy is the trace-weight gap to the next admissible configuration. 

Existence is therefore necessary, as we require the operator to project onto the block, but not sufficient. We record the variational claim (the LP selects $\mu=(N)$) and the energetic claim (the gap sets the binding energy) separately. We do not conflate the free algebraic fact with the computed physical result. The high stability of an aromatic ring follows from the irreducibility of $e_{\lambda,(N)}$ over all $N$ strands, prohibiting the ring from opening one bond at a time. It does not follow from the projector's bare existence.
