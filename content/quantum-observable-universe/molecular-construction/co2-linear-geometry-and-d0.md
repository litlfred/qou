We ask whether the LP dual produces the linear geometry $O{=}C{=}O$, or whether a bent geometry lies lower in $\Phi_1$. In the braid framework, linearity emerges when the optimal strand ordering places $O_1$ and $O_2$ on opposite sides of $C$. This arrangement maximises the distance—the number of intervening strands—between the two oxygens' repulsive $\mathfrak{p}$–$\mathfrak{p}$ crossings. The LP dual selects:
$$
  [\ldots O_1 \ldots, \ldots C \ldots, \ldots O_2 \ldots]
$$
with interleaved covalent electrons mediating each $C{=}O$ bond. It rejects:
$$
  [\ldots C \ldots, \ldots O_1 \ldots, \ldots O_2 \ldots]
$$
because placing the two oxygens adjacent incurs greater repulsion.
