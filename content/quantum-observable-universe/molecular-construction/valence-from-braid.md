*The [shell capacity](#def:electron-shell-braid-capacity) precisely determines the covalent bonding capacity of each atom. An atom shares electrons if and only if the molecular $\Phi_1$ drops below the sum of isolated atomic $\Phi_1$ values. This sharing reduces the total quantum dilogarithm volume.*

*For first-row atoms:*

| Atom | Valence $v$ | Lone pairs | Typical bonds | Example |
|:-----|:------------|:-----------|:-------------|:--------|
| H | 1 | 0 | 1 single | H--H, C--H |
| C | 4 | 0 | 4 single, or 2 double, or 1 triple + 1 single | CH$_4$, CO$_2$, HCN |
| N | 3 | 1 | 3 single, or 1 double + 1 single, or 1 triple | NH$_3$, NO, N$_2$ |
| O | 2 | 2 | 2 single, or 1 double | H$_2$O, CO, CO$_2$ |

*The **octet rule** emerges as a special case. Atoms with $n_{\mathrm{val}} = 2$ minimise $\Phi_1$ when the second shell completes (8 electrons = $2 \times 2^2$). This behaviour is a direct consequence of the Hecke algebra dimension at shell level 2, not an independent axiom.*

*Carbon's bonding versatility (4 bonds across multiple geometries) arises from its position at the half-filling point of the $n = 2$ shell: $Z_{\mathrm{val}} = 4 = \frac{1}{2} \times 8$. Half-filled shells maximize $\Phi_1$ flexibility. This accommodates both $\sigma$-bond (single) and $\pi$-bond (double/triple) configurations.*
