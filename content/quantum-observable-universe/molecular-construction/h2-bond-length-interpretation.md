The covalent channel eigenvalue ratio $\lambda_+/\lambda_- = q/q^{-1} = q^2$ determines the effective bond length. The ratio $q^2 \approx 1.231$ measures the asymmetry of electron sharing between protons:

- $q^2 = 1$: perfectly symmetric sharing, zero bond length.
- $q^2 \to \infty$: electron fully localized on one proton, no covalent bond.
- $q^2 \approx 1.231$: moderate asymmetry, establishing an equilibrium distance where the attractive $\Phi_1$ reduction balances the repulsive proton-proton penalty.

The equilibrium bond length is determined by the competition:

$$
  \frac{\partial \Phi_1^{\mathrm{attract}}}{\partial r}
  + \frac{\partial \Phi_1^{\mathrm{repel}}}{\partial r} = 0
$$

The braid distance $r$ is the number of intervening strands between the two protons. In $H_4(q)$ with interleaved ordering $[\mathfrak{p}_1, e_2, \mathfrak{p}_2, e_1]$, the protons are separated by one strand. The observed $H_2$ bond length is $0.74\;\text{\AA}$.
