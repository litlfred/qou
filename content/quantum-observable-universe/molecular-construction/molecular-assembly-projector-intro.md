A molecule is a composite braid comprising $k$ sub-units (atoms or smaller fragments) assembled into a single word in $H_N(q)$. Here $N$ is the total strand count. We first record the general algebraic structure forced by assembly. This structure holds independently of the specific chemistry.

The $k$ sub-units determine a strand-partition $\lambda = (n_1,\dots,n_k)$ with $\sum_i n_i = N$. The assembly factors through the parabolic (Young) subalgebra $H_\lambda(q) = H_{n_1}(q) \otimes \cdots \otimes H_{n_k}(q) \hookrightarrow H_N(q)$. Every such factorization carries canonical assembly projectors onto the Wedderburn blocks of $H_N(q)$. Projection onto the symmetric block defines delocalization.

Three components remain strictly distinct:

1. Existence: The projector is pure algebra. It exists for every $\lambda$ and carries no energy claim.
2. Optimality: The physics, determined by the binding LP/SDP, selects which block the molecule occupies.
3. Energetics: The binding energy is the trace-weight gap between the selected block and the adjacent configuration.

Benzene appears as the special case $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ (three acetylene sub-units on six carbons). The definitions below apply to arbitrary $\lambda$. The same machinery models $\mathrm{CH_4}$, $\mathrm{CO_2}$, and the other molecules in this chapter.
