**Conjecture.**
*In the quotient atom model $H_{Z+1}(q)$ — where the nuclear
core is a single strand $\mathcal{N}$ and each electron is a
strand $e_k$ — the Gröbner–Shirshov normal form term count
$n(Z)$ satisfies:*

$$
  n(Z) = n(Z - 1)
  \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
  \text{electron } Z \text{ fills a sub-shell pair.}
$$

## Setup

**The quotient atom model.**  Given a neutral atom with $Z$
protons, $N$ neutrons, and $Z$ electrons, quotient the nuclear
core $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ to a single formal variable.  The
resulting element lives in $H_{Z+1}(q)$ with crossing types:

| Pair | Element | Coefficients $(c, d)$ |
|:-----|:--------|:---------------------|
| $\mathcal{N}\text{-}e$ | $Z \cdot \sigma^{-1}$ | $(Z,\; -Z(q - q^{-1}))$ |
| $e\text{-}e$ | $\sigma$ | $(1,\; 0)$ |

The nucleus–electron crossing is $Z$ copies of the attractive
$\sigma^{-1}$ (one per proton); the electron–electron crossing
is the repulsive $\sigma$.

**The NF.** Build the atom by sequential Hopf-linking:
start with $\mathcal{N}$, add $e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_Z$ one at
a time, reducing via Gröbner–Shirshov at each step.

## Computational evidence

The quotient NF term count for $Z = 1$ through $10$:

| $Z$ | Atom | Term count $n(Z)$ | $\Delta n$ | Sub-shell | PAL |
|:----|:-----|:-----------------|:-----------|:----------|:----|
| 1 | H | 2 | — | $1s$ | $\star$ |
| 2 | He | 2 | $0$ | $1s^2$ closes | $\star$ |
| 3 | Li | 6 | $+4$ | $2s$ opens | $\star$ |
| 4 | Be | 6 | $0$ | $2s^2$ closes | $\star$ |
| 5 | B | 23 | $+17$ | $2p$ opens | — |
| 6 | C | 23 | $0$ | $2p^2$ | — |
| 7 | N | 90 | $+67$ | $2p^3$ | — |

| 9 | F | 285 | $+190$ | $2p^5$ | — |
| 10 | Ne | 427 | $+142$ | $2p^6$ closes | — |

The **$\Delta n = 0$ signature** appears at:
- $Z = 2$ ($1s^2$ fills) — the first electron pair closes
- $Z = 4$ ($2s^2$ fills) — the second $s$-pair closes
- $Z = 6$ ($2p^2$, paired) — within the $p$ sub-shell

The term count sequence $\{2, 2, 6, 6, 23, 23, \ldots\}$
directly encodes the shell filling pattern: **plateaus at
each electron pair**.

## Global scaling

All coefficients in the $Z$-electron quotient atom carry a
global prefactor $Z^Z$:

$$
  \text{coeff}(\mathcal{A}(Z)) \in Z^Z \cdot \mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]
$$

For carbon ($Z = 6$): $6^6 = 46656$ appears in every term.

## Algebraic interpretation

The $\Delta n = 0$ condition means:
*adding an electron to an open sub-shell slot does not create
new reduced words in the Hecke algebra*.  The existing NF
basis absorbs the new electron by rescaling coefficients.

Equivalently: the ideal generated by the $(Z{-}1)$-electron
NF words is **stable** under the crossing multiplication
$\sigma_{Z-1} \cdot (\text{NF basis})$.  No new coset
representatives appear.
