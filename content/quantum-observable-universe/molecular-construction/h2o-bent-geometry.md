Oxygen contributes two bonding sites and two lone pairs. In the assembly, the lone pairs occupy two of the four tetrahedral directions around oxygen as non-bonding phantom sites. This extends the methane $T_d$ optimization to $H_2O$. Lone-pair repulsion relaxes the $H$–$O$–$H$ angle from the ideal $109.5^\circ$ toward the observed $\approx 104.5^\circ$.

The four directions — two donor $H$ and two acceptor lone pairs — provide the per-molecule input to the ice lattice. Each water molecule hydrogen-bonds to four neighbors, two as donor and two as acceptor. This tetrahedral coordination tiles into hexagonal ice $I_h$ (space group $P6_3/mmc$). The open tetrahedral network makes ice less dense than liquid water. The choice of which two bonds carry the molecule's hydrogens, subject to the rule that every bond holds exactly one hydrogen, establishes the categorical origin of the Pauling residual entropy of ice ($\approx R\ln(3/2)$). The detailed tiling and symmetry appear in the phase-symmetry model.
