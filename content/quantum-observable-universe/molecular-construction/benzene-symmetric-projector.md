*Benzene constitutes the special case $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ of the [assembly projector](#def:molecular-assembly-projector). Three acetylene sub-units, each contributing two carbons, yield six carbon strands. The projector $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ projects the six $\pi$-electrons onto the symmetric Wedderburn block $\mu = (6)$.*

Projection onto $\mu = (6)$ provides the categorical content of **aromaticity**. The shared $\pi$-content spreads symmetrically over all six carbons rather than localising in three alternating double bonds. The two Kekulé structures are the symmetry-broken, non-projected configurations. Geometrically, $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ realises the [$T(2,6)$ threading link](#def:threading-link), which winds through all six ring atoms simultaneously.

The partition $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ is not an input tuned to benzene. It is forced by the assembly of three two-carbon units. The same construction applied to $\mathrm{CH_4}$ or $\mathrm{CO}$ yields non-aromatic assembly projectors lacking a symmetric ring. The binding LP selects the symmetric block only when the geometry supports delocalisation. This selection, not the bare existence of $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$, distinguishes benzene.
