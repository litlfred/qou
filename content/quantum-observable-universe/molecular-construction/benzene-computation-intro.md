Benzene closes the chapter by introducing a distinct structural feature. As a molecular braid (definition), benzene ($\mathrm{C_6H_6}$) arranges six carbons in a ring on twelve strands, spreading $\pi$-electrons across the cycle rather than localising them on alternating bonds. The threading link $T(2,6)$ emerges as the LP-optimal $\pi$ configuration (proposition). The linear program (LP) dual selects this threading link over any Kekulé alternation. The $D_{6h}$ symmetry of $T(2,6)$ enforces bond equalization (remark), manifesting experimentally as six $\mathrm{C\!-\!C}$ bonds of equal length. The construction does not postulate aromaticity; it forces it as the LP minimum on an $n$-membered ring.
