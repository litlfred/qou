We first determine the bonding capacity of each atom. The electron shell reads structurally as the number of trefoils the atomic braid will admit. The shell-filling rule recovers the aufbau principle by minimising the LP-dual cost $\Phi_1$. The atomic braid directly yields the chemist's valence electron count and the octet rule.

Two combinatorial invariants sharpen this picture. First, the number of Hecke normal-form terms in the quotient atom model detects shell closure. Second, the palindromicity of the normal-form histogram characterises the $s, p, d, f$ orbital types. We take these shell capacities as given before computing the molecular braids for $H_2$, $CO$, $CO_2$, $CH_4$, and $C_6H_6$.
