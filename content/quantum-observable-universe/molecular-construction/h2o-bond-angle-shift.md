*Lean sibling: structural, over $\mathbb{R}$ (the bending energy is archimedean).*

**Proposition.** Near the symmetric tetrahedral value $\theta_0$, the bond-bending energy of [water](#def:h2o-molecular-braid) is harmonic. It features a quadratic restoring term $a(\theta-\theta_0)^2$ with stiffness $a > 0$. Oxygen's two lone pairs introduce a linear perturbation $b\,\theta$. This represents the net angular penalty where lone-pair repulsion exceeds bond-pair repulsion. The energy $a(\theta-\theta_0)^2 + b\,\theta$ is minimized at:
$$ \theta_{\min} \;=\; \theta_0 - \frac{b}{2a}, $$
a linear response of the quadratic minimum. For water $b > 0$, penalizing the wider symmetric angle. Therefore $\theta_{\min} < \theta_0$: the reduction $109.5^\circ \to 104.5^\circ$ is the quadratic well's minimum displaced by the lone-pair term. The sign of the shift is structural. Its magnitude $b/(2a)$ determines the lone-pair repulsion strength relative to the angular stiffness.
