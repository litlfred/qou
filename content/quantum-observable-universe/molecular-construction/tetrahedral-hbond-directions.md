*Lean sibling: explicit Euclidean computation over $\mathbb{R}$.*

**Proposition.** The four hydrogen-bond directions of [a water in ice](#def:h2o-molecular-braid)—two donor, two acceptor—point to the vertices of a regular tetrahedron. By taking the vertex vectors $(\pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1)$ with an even number of minus signs, their sum evaluates to zero. Each vector has squared length $3$. Any two vectors have dot product $-1$. The bond-angle cosine becomes

$$ \cos\theta_0 \;=\; \frac{u_i \cdot u_j}{|u_i|\,|u_j|} \;=\; \frac{-1}{3}, \qquad \theta_0 = \arccos\!\left(-\tfrac13\right) \approx 109.47^\circ. $$

This is the symmetric reference angle $\theta_0$. Oxygen's lone pairs displace the H–O–H angle from this reference to $\approx 104.5^\circ$ via the quadratic-well shift. The Lean sibling proves all four geometric facts exactly over $\mathbb{R}$: balanced sum, squared length $3$, pairwise dot product $-1$, and $\cos\theta_0 = -1/3$.
