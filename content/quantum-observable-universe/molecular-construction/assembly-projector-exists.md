**Proposition.**
*For every [strand-partition](#def:molecular-strand-partition) $\lambda$
of $N$ there exists a symmetric-block
[assembly projector](#def:molecular-assembly-projector) $e_{\lambda,(N)}
\in H_N(q)$ — the $q$-Young symmetriser onto the one-row block
$\mu = (N)$ — and it is idempotent, $e_{\lambda,(N)}^2 = e_{\lambda,(N)}$,
for generic $q$.*

This is the $q$-deformation of the classical Young symmetriser; the
idempotent is Wenzl's projector and exists for all $N$ at generic $q$
\cite{wenzl1988,kassel1995}.

**What this proposition does *not* assert.** Existence is pure algebra:
it holds for *every* $\lambda$ — bonded or not, stable or not — and says
**nothing** about energy or stability.  A molecule is stable only when
the binding LP/SDP *selects* this symmetric block as its optimum, and the
binding energy is then the trace-weight **gap** to the next admissible
configuration.  Existence is therefore *necessary* (one needs the
operator in order to project onto the block) but not *sufficient*; the
variational claim (the LP selects $\mu=(N)$) and the energetic claim (the
gap sets the binding energy) are recorded separately, so that the free
algebraic fact is never conflated with the earned physical one.  In
particular, the high stability of an aromatic ring follows from the
*irreducibility* of $e_{\lambda,(N)}$ over all $N$ strands — the ring
cannot be opened one bond at a time — not from the projector's bare
existence.
