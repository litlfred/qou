**:defterm[Pivotal category]{#pivotal-category}.** A rigid monoidal category equipped with a monoidal natural isomorphism $\delta_X \colon X \xrightarrow{\sim} X^{\vee\vee}$ between each object and its double dual.

The pivotal structure is what makes a *left* trace definable alongside the right one: without $\delta$ there is no way to close the left-hand loop back onto $X$, since evaluation lands on $X^{\vee\vee}$. A pivotal category in which the two traces then **agree** on every object is called **spherical**; pivotal alone does not force that agreement.

**Where the name comes from.** The terminology is geometric, and it is the reason the condition is worth naming rather than merely imposing. Regard $S^2$ as the plane with the point at infinity adjoined. A closed diagram in the plane is then a closed diagram on the sphere, and the isotopy that carries a loop closed on the right to the same loop closed on the left is one that drags it *past the point at infinity* — a motion available on $S^2$ and not in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Adjoining that one move to planar isotopy generates all of the isotopies of the sphere. So a pivotal category evaluates closed diagrams invariantly under isotopy of the *plane*, and a spherical one evaluates them invariantly under isotopy of the *2-sphere* \cite{barrett1993}.

The picture is an interpretation of the condition, not a derivation of it: sliding the loop around the back is *equivalent* to $\mathrm{tr}_L = \mathrm{tr}_R$, not evidence for it. A pivotal category in which the two traces differ is a category whose diagram calculus simply does not descend to $S^2$, and such categories exist — \cite{barrett1993} exhibits one, the quantised enveloping algebra of the Borel subalgebra of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})$, where the two traces are $\mathrm{tr}_L(\theta) = \mathrm{tr}(\theta w)$ and $\mathrm{tr}_R(\theta) = \mathrm{tr}(\theta w^{-1})$ for the pivotal element $w$ and are visibly distinct.

**Mathlib reference.** None — there is no `CategoryTheory.PivotalCategory`. Mathlib's `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Monoidal.Rigid.Basic` lists *"Define pivotal categories (rigid categories equipped with a natural isomorphism `ᘁᘁ ≅ 𝟙 C`)"* under its **Future work** heading, and supplies only the rigid layer: `ExactPairing`, `rightAdjointMate`, `comp_rightAdjointMate`.

The classes this paper needs are therefore built in-repo. The pivotal layer is `PivotalCategory` in [`QOU/MathlibExt/PivotalCategory.lean`](../lean/QOU/MathlibExt/PivotalCategory.lean), stated in the shape Mathlib's own "Future work" list asks for — a natural isomorphism $\mathbb{1}_{\mathbf{C}} \cong {}^{\vee\vee}$.

**How much of the definition above that class currently carries is under active revision, and the difference matters here.** A pivotal structure in the literature — and in the first sentence of this block — is a *monoidal* natural isomorphism. The in-repo class as it stands on `main` carries **naturality only**, omitting the monoidality condition $\delta_{X \otimes Y} = \delta_X \otimes \delta_Y$ (against the comparison $(X \otimes Y)^{\vee\vee} \cong X^{\vee\vee} \otimes Y^{\vee\vee}$); relative to *that* weaker class `pivotalOfBraided` makes every braided right-rigid category pivotal, sorry-free. Whether the monoidality field belongs in the class — and therefore whether braiding alone suffices at all, since the Drinfeld isomorphism is natural but is **not** monoidal — is being revised in [#5856](https://github.com/litlfred/qou/pull/5856), which restores the field and replaces the braided instance with a ribbon one. Do not cite `pivotalOfBraided` as establishing the *literature* notion until that lands; cite it for the natural-isomorphism form it actually proves.

Sphericality is a separate class in either reading, `SphericalStructure` in [`QOU/BraidKnot/KeystonePivotalSpherical.lean`](../lean/QOU/BraidKnot/KeystonePivotalSpherical.lean). They are kept **separate** for the reason above: `leftQDim` is defined with the inverse braiding and is a genuinely different morphism from `qDimObj`, so sphericality is a real hypothesis rather than a consequence of the pivotor.
