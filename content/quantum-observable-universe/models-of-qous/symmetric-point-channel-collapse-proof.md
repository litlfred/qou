At the symmetric point, the two Hecke channels $\sigma$ and $\sigma^{-1}$ become indistinguishable. Their eigenvalues merge. Their symmetric average carries no more information than a single generator. The following computation makes this collapse explicit.

**Proof.** From $s^2 = 1$, the element $s$ is invertible with $s^{-1} = s$. Substituting yields $(s + s^{-1})/2 = (s + s)/2 = s$. $\square$

At $q \neq 1$, the eigenvalues of a Hecke generator are $\{q, -q^{-1}\}$. These distinguish $\sigma$ from $\sigma^{-1}$. At $q = 1$, they degenerate to the $2$-torsion locus $\mu_2 = \{1, -1\}$. The [mass-gap conjecture](#conj:yang-mills-mass-gap) places the vanishing of the gap on this same locus. The proposition forms the algebraic core of every channel degeneration recorded in this section. Any physical data carried by the choice among $\sigma$, $\sigma^{-1}$, and their average remains invisible at the symmetric point. It is restored only at positive order in $h = q - 1$.
