At the symmetric point $q = 1$, the three Hecke channels $\sigma$, $\sigma^{-1}$, and $(\sigma + \sigma^{-1})/2$ degenerate to a single operator. A purely algebraic identity governs this collapse.

**Proposition.** Let $R$ be a field with $2 \neq 0$. Let $s \in R$ satisfy $s^2 = 1$. Then $s^{-1} = s$, and consequently

$$
  \frac{s + s^{-1}}{2} \;=\; s.
$$

**Remark.** Any family of operator assignments drawn from $\{\sigma,\ \sigma^{-1},\ (\sigma + \sigma^{-1})/2\}$ collapses to a single operator under the specialisation $q = 1$. The Hecke relation $(\sigma - q)(\sigma + q^{-1}) = 0$ reduces to $\sigma^2 = 1$ at $q = 1$.
