Enveloping algebras enter because the substrate must be recoverable from
its infinitesimal $q$-commutation data: reconstructing the substrate from
the algebra generated by those infinitesimal relations is the lifting
problem. The concrete anchor is the Heisenberg uncertainty relation, read
here as a $q$-commutator $[\hat{X},\hat{P}]_q = i\hbar_q$; this single
physical relation between position and momentum is the germ that the
enveloping-algebra construction generalizes.

Generalizing from that germ, the tools that make the lift work are the
Lusztig integral form of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ together with
its triangular decomposition, and the Poincare--Birkhoff--Witt (PBW)
basis in its $q$-deformed analogue. The integral form and its triangular
decomposition factor the generators into their component subalgebras,
while the $q$-deformed PBW basis supplies an explicit ordered basis
for the enveloping algebra; together they realize universal enveloping
algebras in their role of lifting the infinitesimal $q$-commutation data
back to the substrate
(cf. [arXiv:math/0409565](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0409565)).
