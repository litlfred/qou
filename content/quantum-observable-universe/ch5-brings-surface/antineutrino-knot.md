The **antineutrino** is the mirror unknot
$\overline{T_{1,0}}$ — the antiparticle of the neutrino.

$$
  \bar{\nu} \;=\; \overline{T_{1,0}} \qquad \text{(mirror unknot, dashed circle ⊙)}
$$

Since the unknot is amphichiral ($T_{1,0} \cong \overline{T_{1,0}}$
as unoriented knots), the antineutrino has the **same** knot
invariants as the neutrino:

- Jones polynomial: $J_{\bar{\nu}}(q) = 1$
- Alexander polynomial: $\Delta_{\bar{\nu}}(t) = 1$
- Hyperbolic volume: $\mathrm{Vol}(\bar{\nu}) = 0$
- Seifert genus: $g = 0$
- Crossing number: $c = 0$

The distinction between $\nu$ and $\bar{\nu}$ is carried by
**orientation** (framing), not by knot type.  In the braid
group, the neutrino is the identity braid $e \in B_1$ with
positive orientation; the antineutrino is $e$ with negative
orientation.

**Visual convention.** The antineutrino is drawn as a
**dashed circle** (⊙), distinguishing it from:
- Solid circle (○) = neutrino (forward time)
- Solid trefoil (●) = electron (forward time)
- Outlined trefoil (○) = positron (backward time)

**Role in beta decay.**

In $\beta^-$ decay, the $L_0^{-}$ smoothing of the $\mathfrak{d} \to \mathfrak{u}$
crossing change produces:

$$
  L_0^{-} \;=\; T_{2,3} \;\sqcup\; \overline{T_{1,0}}
  \quad=\quad e^- \;+\; \bar{\nu}_e
$$

The electron $T_{2,3}$ carries the topological mass
($\mathrm{Vol} = 0$ but crossing energy from the trefoil
structure); the antineutrino $\overline{T_{1,0}}$ carries
zero topological mass and exits as a massless (or
near-massless) particle.

**Lepton number.**  The $L_0^{\pm}$ smoothing always produces
one fermion knot ($T_{2,3}$ or $\overline{T_{2,3}}$) and
one (anti)neutrino ($T_{1,0}$ or $\overline{T_{1,0}}$).
Lepton number is conserved because each smoothing creates
exactly one lepton-antilepton pair.
