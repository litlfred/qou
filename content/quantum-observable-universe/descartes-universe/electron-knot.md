The **electron** is the trefoil $T_{2,3}$ in the lepton sector
— the same knot type as the up quark, but not localized in a
coral tube.

$$
  e^- =  T_{2,3} \qquad \text{(trefoil, solid ●)}
$$

The electron and the up quark are the *same knot* $T_{2,3}$.
What distinguishes them is their **environment**: the up quark
is threaded through a coral tube of the Bring's surface
([color tube coordinates](#def:color-tube-coordinates)),
while the electron orbits the nucleus via Hopf linking
without entering the coral tubes.

**Visual convention.** The electron is drawn as a **solid**
trefoil (forward time direction), identical to the up quark.
Context (coral tube vs. orbital) distinguishes quark from
lepton.

**Caveat on the mass formula.** The trefoil cabling describes
the electron's orbital/linking structure for quantum numbers,
not its contribution to the atomic mass.  In the brane-tower
binding formula
([tower binding energy](#prop:tower-binding-energy)), electrons
enter the mass $\mathcal{M}(Z,N) = Z m_{\mathfrak{p}} + N m_{\mathfrak{n}} + Z m_e - B(Z,N)$
through the free-electron kinetic term $Z m_e$ only.  The
nuclear binding $B(Z,N)$ is a sum over $A - 1$ nucleon-strand
generators; electrons are **not** additional strands in that
sum, because appending $Z$ strands to $H_A(q)$ diverges the
$\prod \hat{V}$ contribution above $q$ (computationally
verified on 3He, 4He).  Atomic binding ($\lesssim 27$ keV at
$Z = 20$) lies below the current prediction-error floor and
is absorbed into the rounding of $Z m_e$.
