**Proposition (Bring-supported force-carrier sector is forced).**
*Bring's surface $\Sigma_4$ has genus $4$
([Bring's surface](#def:brings-surface)).  A torus knot $K$
embeds as a saturating generator of the symplectic homology
$H_1(\Sigma_4, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{2 g} = \mathbb{Z}^8$
only if $g(K) = g(\Sigma_4) = 4$.  Combined with the genus-$4$
classification
([genus-$4$ uniqueness](#prop:torus-knot-genus-four-unique)), the
force-carrier sector of any QOU built on $\Sigma_4$ contains exactly
the two-element set $\{T_{2,9}, T_{3,5}\}$.*

*Proof.* The saturation condition selects torus knots whose Seifert
genus matches the ambient surface's geometric genus.  By the
genus-$4$ uniqueness proposition, the only such torus knots are
$T_{2,9}$ (crossing number $9$) and $T_{3,5}$ (crossing number $10$).
$\square$

**Corollary (categorical complexity of the force-carrier sector).**
*Working over $R = \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/2}, (q - q^{-1})^{-1}]$, the
categorical $q$-dimensions assigned to the two force-carrier objects
are $\dim_q T_{2,9} = [9]_q$ and $\dim_q T_{3,5} = [10]_q$ (the
$q$-deformed crossing numbers).  The total complexity of the
force-carrier sector is*

$$
  \dim_q\bigl(T_{2,9}\bigr)
  \cdot
  \dim_q\bigl(T_{3,5}\bigr)
  \;=\;
  [9]_q \cdot [10]_q \,\in\, R.
$$
