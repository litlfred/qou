The completeness theorem
([completeness](#thm:em-spectral-completeness)) and its corollary
([uniqueness](#cor:gauge-coupling-uniqueness)) together establish
that the singlet channel is the unique one-dimensional invariant
subspace of $A \otimes A$, hence the unique scalar exchange channel.
The symmetric channel $V_+$ has $R$-rank $\dim_q V_+ = [3]_q$ — the
spin-$1$ irrep of $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ — and carries non-trivial spin,
so it is not a scalar gauge coupling.

The categorical $R$-matrix has rank-$2$ endomorphism algebra over
$R$.  Plancherel completeness $w_+ + w_- = 1$ exhausts the Frobenius
mass: no third channel exists and no scalar coupling is missed.  The
singlet weight $w_-$ — modulo the force-carrier complexity
normalisation
([force-carrier uniqueness](#prop:force-carrier-bring-uniqueness)) —
is $\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}$
([EM coupling projection](#prop:em-coupling-projection)).

**Specialisation.**
The categorical statement holds over $R$; numerical evaluation
chooses a ring map $\iota \colon R \to \mathbb{R}$ with $q^{1/2}
\mapsto q_0^{1/2}$ at $q_0 \approx 1.1097$.  The completeness
theorem itself is independent of $\iota$ and survives any
specialisation to $\mathbb{Q}_p$ or $\mathbb{F}_\ell$ admitting
the required units.
