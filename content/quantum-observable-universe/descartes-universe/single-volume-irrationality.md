**Conjecture.**
Each of the three non-zero QOU hyperbolic volumes is irrational:

$$
  \mathrm{Vol}(4_1) = 3\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3) \notin \mathbb{Q},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{Vol}(5_2) \notin \mathbb{Q},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}_3) \notin \mathbb{Q}.
$$

*Remark.* This conjecture is completely open.  No hyperbolic 3-manifold volume has ever been
proved irrational.  The figure-eight volume $3\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$
is a value of the Clausen function at a rational multiple of $\pi$.
Its irrationality would follow from the irrationality of
$\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \sin(k\pi/3)/k^2$ ---
a Dirichlet $L$-value at $s = 2$ for the character
$\chi_{-3}$ --- but even this is unknown.

By contrast, Ap\'ery (1978) proved $\zeta(3) \notin \mathbb{Q}$,
and $\zeta(3)$ equals the hyperbolic volume of the *regular ideal
tetrahedron* (divided by 4).  However, knot complement volumes are
sums of Bloch--Wigner dilogarithm values $D(z_i)$ at algebraic
arguments, and the arithmetic of such sums remains beyond current
methods.

This is the weakest of the chain of conjectures:

$$
  \text{irrationality}
  \;\Longleftarrow\;
  \mathbb{Q}\text{-linear independence (Borel--Zagier)}
  \;\Longleftarrow\;
  \text{algebraic independence.}
$$

Proving any one of them for the QOU volumes would be a breakthrough
in transcendental number theory.
