**Example.**
Deuterium $\mathcal{A}(1,1)$: one proton, one neutron, one electron.

## Derivation

Six $\gamma\gamma$ pair productions:
three for the proton ($\mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{d}$),
three for the neutron ($\mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{d}, \mathfrak{d}$).
Each set of three quarks is confined by $\mathcal{B}_3$
([confinement selection](#prop:confinement-operator-selection)),
then the two nucleons are Hopf-linked via a $p$-$n$ typed crossing
$(\sigma_0 - \tfrac{1}{2}H_A)$.

## Nucleus NF

$$
  \mathrm{NF}(\mathcal{N}(1,1))
  =
  -0.124\gamma - 0.080\sigma_0 + 0.095\sigma_1
  - 0.088 L_+ - 0.104 L_- + 1.004 e^-.
$$

$$
  \mathrm{net}(\mathcal{N}(1,1)) \approx +0.702
$$

The deuteron nucleus has significant demand in $\gamma$, $\sigma_0$,
$L_+$, and $L_-$ — reflecting the loose binding of the only
stable $A = 2$ nucleus.  The dominant term is $e^- \approx +1.004$:
the proton--neutron Hopf link generates a large trefoil component,
which is the nuclear binding expressed in the electron basis.

## Full atom NF (with electron)

$$
  \mathrm{NF}(\mathcal{A}(1,1))
  =
  +0.121\gamma + 0.099\sigma_0 + 1.029\sigma_1
  + 0.126 L_+ + 0.090 L_- - 0.058 e^-.
$$

$$
  \mathrm{net}(\mathcal{A}(1,1)) \approx +1.407
$$

The electron partially fills the nuclear demand, but
$e^- \approx -0.058$ remains negative — deuterium's electron
does not fully close the trefoil channel, consistent with
deuterium's participation in $d + d$ fusion reactions.
