When we apply the [Reeb decomposition](#ex:reeb-decomposition) to a
Descartes universe, we find two classes of fermionic excitations
([fermion](#def:fermion-boson-decomposition)), distinguished by the
degree of their vertical component $\alpha_V$: forms with
$\deg\alpha_V = 1$ give electrons, while $\deg\alpha_V = 2$ gives
muons. More generally, each additional unit of vertical degree
corresponds to a successive lepton generation. The topological
constraint $\chi(\mathcal{S}) = -6$ bounds how many generations can
exist, since the Hodge decomposition of $\mathcal{S}$ admits only
finitely many independent vertical harmonic forms. This
generation-dependent structure leads to testable predictions,
including a $q$-correction to the proton charge radius that differs
between muonic and electronic hydrogen
([Section](#ssec:proton-radius)).
