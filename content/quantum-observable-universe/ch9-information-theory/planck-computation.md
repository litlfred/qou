The number of Reeb operations per Planck unit, $n_R(q) = q/(q-1)$
([Reeb operations per Planck unit](#prop:reeb-operations-per-planck)),
determines the **computational capacity** of a Planck-length
region of the QOU at substrate parameter $q$.

Each operation evaluates $f_q(z) = z^2 + c(q)$ — a polynomial
of degree $2$ — and resolves one additional crossing in the braid
representation of the local link structure.  The total
computational work per Planck unit is therefore $n_R(q)$
polynomial evaluations, each of constant algebraic complexity.

At $q = q_0$: a Planck-length region performs $\sim 10$
degree-$2$ polynomial evaluations per Planck time.  This is the
microscopic computational rate of the universe at our scale —
the clock speed of the exceptional divisor dynamics, coarse-grained
into the smooth Reeb flow that governs observable physics.
