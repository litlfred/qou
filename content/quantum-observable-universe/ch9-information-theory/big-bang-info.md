At $\mathcal{T} = 0$: no Reeb operation has occurred.  The
initial state ([bundle initial conditions](#def:bundle-initial-conditions))
is a formal sum $\mathcal{S}_0 = \sum_i (p_i, H_i)$ specifying
fiber data without horizontal transport.  No information has
propagated; position is known exactly, momentum is entirely
undetermined.

At $\mathcal{T} = 1$: the first iteration of $f_q$ connects
neighboring fibers for the first time.  The $q$-connection
$\nabla_q$ has acted once, producing the first non-trivial
holonomy.  Over $\mathbb{F}_\pp$, this is literally
$z_1 = z_0^2 + c(q)$ — a single polynomial evaluation.  The
Heisenberg commutator $[\hat{X}, \hat{P}]_q = J\hbar_q$ is
near-maximal: the first tick of time is the moment of greatest
uncertainty.

Because $t_P \to 1$ at the Big Bang, the information-theoretic
time $\mathcal{T}$ and the Reeb time coordinate $t$ coincide:
$t = \hbar_q \mathcal{T} \approx \mathcal{T}$.  There is no
coarse-graining, no statistical averaging.  Each braiding on the
exceptional divisor is individually resolved.  The link-length
resolution grows by exactly one crossing per tick, and the
particle spectrum unfolds step by step through the jet
prolongation tower
([Planck steps as jet orders](#rem:jet-planck-steps)).
