**Proposition (what is literature, what is measured, what is calibrated).** Write the sub-leading constant of the volume-conjecture asymptotic as a power law in the Dimofte–Garoufalidis 1-loop invariant,

$$
  c_\sigma \;=\; k \cdot |\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}|^{\,p}.
$$

Then the two factors have different provenance, and conflating them is the error this block exists to prevent.

- **The form is literature.** Dimofte–Gukov–Lenells–Zagier expand the perturbative $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ Chern–Simons partition function as $Z^{(\rho)}(M;\hbar) = \exp\big(S_0/\hbar - \tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{(\rho)}\log\hbar + \sum_{n\ge 0} S_{n+1}\hbar^n\big)$ and identify the one-loop coefficient with the Ray–Singer torsion of $M$ twisted by the flat bundle $E_\rho$: $S_1^{(\rho)} = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(T(M;E_\rho)/2\big)$. The statement is for a **general** $3$-manifold with a flat $G_\mathbb{C}$ bundle — no restriction to knots, to two-bridge knots, or to cusp number. So the dependence $c_\sigma \propto (\text{torsion})^{-1/2}$ is not an assumption of this corpus; it is a cited result. What it is *not* is a theorem: it is a one-loop saddle-point/Feynman-diagram derivation.

- **The exponent is normalisation-invariant, hence measurable.** Rescaling $c_\sigma \mapsto A\,c_\sigma$ by any nonzero constant leaves the two-point exponent $p_{ij} = (\log c_i - \log c_j)/(\log \tau_i - \log \tau_j)$ unchanged (`exponent_normalisation_invariant`). Every convention offset is such an $A$, so no change of convention can move $p$.

- **And $p = -1/2$ was already measured, by checks that were read as measuring something else.** For any *pair* of manifolds, $c_1\sqrt{\tau_1} = c_2\sqrt{\tau_2} \iff p_{12} = -1/2$ (`k_const_iff_exponent_neg_half`). The corpus validated the law by checking that $k_c := c_\sigma|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}|^{1/2}$ agrees across manifolds and recorded the exponent as *assumed*; those are the same statement. The $17$-digit agreement $k_1 = k_2$ between $4_1$ and the Whitehead link **is** the measurement $p = -1/2$ to $17$ digits.

- **The constant is convention, and stays calibrated.** DGLZ carry at least three distinct additive offsets between conventions — the $/2$ inside their logarithm, a $+\log 2$ relating $S_1$ to the colored-Jones coefficient $s_1$, and a $\log\pi$ shift against Gukov–Murakami — and fix their integration constants "by comparison to the asymptotics of the colored Jones polynomial". They calibrate too. Since $c_\sigma$ here is measured from the colored-Jones asymptotic it matches $s_1$ rather than $S_1$, and carrying the $\log 2$ through moves the induced identification of the torsion by a factor of $4$. So $k = 2^{-1/2}$ cannot be lifted from the literature formula; it remains calibrated on $4_1$, and that calibration is not redundant.

**The $N$-power is theorem-backed at two and three cusps.** The extraction $c_\sigma := \lim_N |J'_N|\,N^{-3/2}e^{-\mathrm{Vol}\cdot N/2\pi}$ uses the same power $3/2$ at every cusp number. Van der Veen proves, for the Whitehead-chain family $W_{a,b,c,d}$, that $J_N \sim \exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2\pi}[(\mathrm{Vol}+i\,\mathrm{CS})N + D\log N + E]\big)$ with $D = 3\pi$ whenever $b = 1$ — a value independent of $c$ and $d$, hence of the number of components. The Whitehead link is $W_{0,1,1,0}$ and the Borromean rings are $W_{0,1,1,1}$, both with $b = 1$, so both carry $N^{3/2}$. The cusp-independence of the exponent is therefore proved for exactly the links this corpus uses, and not merely fitted.

**What remains open, stated exactly.** Van der Veen's constant $E$ is the object measured here as $c_\sigma$, and his proof gives it explicitly as a $(c+d+1)$-dimensional Gaussian integral for the whole family; he evaluates it only far enough to show it is nonzero. His closing paragraph then records the conjecture of Gukov–Murakami that $E$ "is determined by the Ray-Singer torsion of the complement twisted by the holonomy representation", and defers it to a subsequent publication. That conjecture *is* the $c_\sigma \leftrightarrow \tau$ question, for links, stated as open in the same paper this corpus reads its Whitehead colored-Jones values from. Evaluating that integral and comparing it to $|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}|^{-1/2}$ is the concrete route from a calibrated constant to a derived one; it requires the constant in van der Veen's Lemma 3 for the maximal term $\tilde S_N$ together with the normalisation between $J$ and $J'$, neither of which is done here.

The distinction the proposition draws also fixes the shape of the residual risk. Any missing cusp-dependent step $g$ in the dictionary between the two normalisations satisfies $k_{\mathrm{meas}}(c) = k_{\mathrm{true}}\,g(c)$, so agreement of the measured constants at two cusp numbers forces $g$ to agree there (`normalisation_step_trivial_of_k_agree`) — which excludes every candidate with $g(1)\neq g(2)$, the retracted per-cusp factor $2^{(c-1)/2}$ among them. The tension lives in the exponent bookkeeping, not in the constant, at the cusp numbers tested.
