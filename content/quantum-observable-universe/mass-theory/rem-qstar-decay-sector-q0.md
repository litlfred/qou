The bar-odd decay observable, taken here in its $c_3$-level normalization $S(q) = c_3(q) + \mathrm{Vol}(4_1)$ (the same object as the preceding remark's $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta/E_0$ up to the ladder rescaling), is a deterministic function of the substrate modulus: $c_3(q)$ is the ladder extractor $c_3 = (\mathfrak{Q}_\beta / Q_{N2} - 1)/u^3$, where $Q_{N2}$ is the $c_2$-level ladder truncation of $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$, $u = b\,\hbar_q^2$, and $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$, $m_e$ are fixed physical constants. Its zero is therefore root-found, not interpolated from anchors:

$$
  q^{*} \approx 1.10997859303152\ldots,
$$

the point where $c_3 = -\mathrm{Vol}(4_1)$ exactly — the pure bar-even substrate point with vanishing decay projection ($\varepsilon = \mathrm{Im}(q) = 0$ in the reading of the interpreted conjecture's candidate (iii)). It lies $\approx 80.3\%$ of the way from the proton anchor toward the muon anchor.

$q^{*}$ is independent of both mass-sector pins — it uses neither $m_\mathfrak{p}$ nor $m_\mu$ — but it is **not** an independent *third determination of $q_0$*, and an earlier version of this remark claimed that it was. The claim is circular: $q^{*}$ is defined above as the root of $S(q) = c_3(q) + \mathrm{Vol}(4_1)$, so locating it presupposes $c_3^{\mathrm{even}} = -\mathrm{Vol}(4_1)$. It is a determination of $q_0$ *conditional on* that hypothesis, which is precisely the hypothesis an independent determination would have to avoid.

The supporting statistics do not rescue it. At $q^{*}$ the muon ratio $m_\mu/m_e$ deviates $\approx 42.7$ ppb from CODATA, versus $\approx 1.4$ ppb at the muon pin and $\approx 211$ ppb at the proton pin; and $q^{*}$ is indeed not the statistical reconciliation of the $\mu$–$\mathfrak{p}$ tension (the precision-weighted mean sits $\approx 0.0007\%$ muon-ward, essentially at the proton pin, which is $\approx 367\times$ more precise, while $q^{*}$ sits $80.3\%$ muon-ward). Both remain true and neither is evidence, because **the $80.3\%$ is a reading of $\mathrm{Vol}(4_1)$'s own value**: every constant $V$ in the window $(-c_3(\mu), -c_3(\mathfrak{p})) = (2.0202,\, 2.0693)$ of width $0.0491$ places a zero between the anchors, and as $V$ sweeps that window the corresponding $q^{*}_V$ sweeps the entire anchor interval. $\mathrm{Vol}(4_1) = 2.0299$ sits $80.30\%$ of the way from the proton end of that window, the same figure. Choosing a different in-window constant relocates $q^{*}$ and would produce a different percentage, equally far from both pins and from their weighted mean.

So the $\mu$–$\mathfrak{p}$ substrate tension is a **two-way** tension between the two mass pins, not a three-way picture.

The residual empirical content must be stated with its error bar, because the error bar dominates it. The extractor amplifies the experimental $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ uncertainty by $1/u^3$, giving $\sigma(c_3) = \sigma(\mathfrak{Q}_\beta)/(Q_{N2}u^3) \approx \pm 1.262$ — about $61\%$ of $|c_3|$. Against that band, $c_3$'s proximity to $-\mathrm{Vol}(4_1)$ is $0.031\sigma$ at the proton pin and $0.008\sigma$ at the muon. These are not close agreements in the evidential sense; they are the absence of a measurement, since every candidate constant in $[0.807,\, 3.331]$ agrees at least as well. That interval is $51\times$ wider than the $0.049$ genericity window quoted above, so the window understates the ambiguity by that factor. Distinguishing $\mathrm{Vol}(4_1)$ from a generic in-band constant requires a neutron $Q$-value sharper by $\approx 32\times$ (proton) or $\approx 130\times$ (muon).

The same arithmetic denies the converse: $0.031\sigma$ is no evidence *against* a $4_1$-volume ingredient either. Whether the identification is structural remains the open question of [`conj:im-q-physical-role`](conj-im-q-physical-role.md), which this remark interprets; it is settled neither by $q^{*}$ nor by the present precision.

Two calibrating facts sharpen how $q^{*}$ may be used. First, the famous hypersensitivity $dc_3/dq \approx 4.7 \times 10^{6}$ is not a singularity of the underlying object: $S'(q^{*}) \approx 2.37 \times (1/u^3)$, a smooth $O(1)$ prefactor magnified by the $1/u^3$ ladder inversion. Second, the precision of $q^{*}$ is limited by the experimental neutron $Q$-value, $dq^{*}/d\mathfrak{Q}_\beta \approx -0.539$. A sharper $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ measurement directly sharpens $q^{*}$, a concrete falsifiable lever that additional mass anchors at the same $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ would not provide.
