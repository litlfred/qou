The confinement LP selects the Borromean link $\mathcal{B}_3$ as the unique optimizer over the $6$-element NF basis of $H_3(q)$. The general LP for knot operator selection in $H_n(q)$ applies for arbitrary strand number $n$; the confinement LP is the special case $n = 3$, $d = 6$.

The objective $\sum_{i} t_i x_i = \mathrm{tr}_M(\Phi)$ is the categorical action. Minimizing it selects the operator of least action among all operators compatible with the Frobenius structure. The Frobenius positivity constraints $Gx \geq 0$ encode the demand–supply balance: $\Phi$ must pair non-negatively with every basis element under the Frobenius inner product. No channel carries unbounded demand.

**LP over motivic shadows, not real numbers.**
The data feeding the LP—Markov traces $t_i = \mathrm{tr}_M(b_i)$ and Frobenius Gram entries $G_{ij} = \mathrm{tr}_M(b_i \cdot b_j)$—are formal elements of $\mathbb{Z}[q^{± 1/2}, (q-q^{-1})^{-1}]$, hence elements of the Habiro completion $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}[q]}$. The selection of the operator $\Phi^* = \sum_i x_i^* b_i$ is a combinatorial problem over the motivic shadows of the motivic substrate (the algebraic data of the Hecke algebra independently of any place-realization). Strong duality is a purely algebraic consequence of the LP. Only after the optimizer is selected does the archimedean realization $\rho_\infty$ project the dual prices and objective value into physical binding energies in $\mathbb{R}$. The non-archimedean realizations $\rho_p$ recover the LP's cyclotomic shadows at each finite place $p$.

The dual variable $y_0$ is the shadow price of the net constraint: the marginal cost (in units of categorical action) of increasing the target mass $n_{\mathrm{target}}$ by one unit. The dual variables $y_i \geq 0$ are the shadow prices of the Frobenius positivity constraints (channel demand prices). A positive $y_i^*$ at optimality indicates that the $i$-th Frobenius constraint is tight, meaning the $i$-th channel is saturated.
