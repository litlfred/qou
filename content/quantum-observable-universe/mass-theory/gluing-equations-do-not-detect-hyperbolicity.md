## What the gluing equations do, and do not, decide

An ideal triangulation with shape parameters $z_1, \dots, z_N$ carries Thurston's gluing equations: one equation per edge class, asserting that the edge parameters around that edge multiply to $1$. A solution is *geometric* when every $z_i$ lies in the upper half plane, and only then does the triangulation describe a hyperbolic structure.

It is tempting to treat the equations themselves as the test — to read "the gluing equations are satisfiable" as "the manifold is hyperbolic". They are not that test.

**Theorem.**
*The trefoil complement is Seifert fibred, hence not hyperbolic. Nevertheless its triangulation admits shape parameters*

$$
  z_0 \;=\; -1, \qquad z_1 \;=\; 2,
$$

*which are **rational**, satisfy **every** gluing equation exactly, are all real — hence flat — and give total volume $0$.*

So a degenerate solution exists and the equations are entirely satisfied by it. What fails is not solvability but **positivity**.

## The separation is structural, not incidental

The two halves of the gluing data behave differently, and the difference is exactly the point:

| condition | needs |
|---|---|
| edge equations $\prod z^{a} (z')^{b} (z'')^{c} = 1$ | $z \neq 0, 1$ only |
| dihedral angles summing to $2\pi$ around each edge | $\operatorname{Im} z > 0$ |

The edge equations are algebraic identities in $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0,1\}$ and descend to flat shapes without complaint. The angle conditions do not: the dihedral angles are the arguments of the edge parameters, and a flat tetrahedron has angles $0$ and $\pi$ rather than a genuine angle triple summing to $\pi$. **Positivity is what detects hyperbolicity; the equations are indifferent to it.**

## Other torus knots behave the same way, over the fields one expects

The pattern is uniform, and the fields are the real cyclotomic subfields the torus type predicts:

| knot | flat shape parameters | field |
|---|---|---|
| $3_1$, the $(2,3)$ torus knot | $-1,\; 2$ | $\mathbb{Q}$ |
| $5_1$, the $(2,5)$ torus knot | $-\tfrac{1+\sqrt 5}{2},\; \tfrac{3-\sqrt 5}{2},\; \tfrac{1-\sqrt 5}{2}$ | $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 5)$ |
| $8_{19}$, the $(3,4)$ torus knot | $1+\sqrt 2,\; -1+\sqrt 2,\; -1$ | $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2)$ |
| $7_1$, the $(2,7)$ torus knot | roots of $x^3 + 3x^2 - 4x + 1$ | real cubic of conductor $7$ |

These are genuine number fields generated by genuine solutions. They are **not** shape fields in the sense of Neumann-Reid, which is the invariant trace field of a hyperbolic structure — these manifolds have none. They are the fields of a degenerate solution, and the distinction is the content of this block.

## Methodological remark

A pipeline that solves the gluing equations and reports the resulting volume will return $0$ here rather than failing, and $0$ is a perfectly good number. Any such pipeline therefore needs the positivity check as a separate gate, and needs a non-hyperbolic control to demonstrate that the gate is doing something. Without one, the pipeline cannot distinguish "detected geometry" from "solved a system of equations".
