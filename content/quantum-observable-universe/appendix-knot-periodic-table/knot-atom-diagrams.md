The following table shows the knot structure of each atom with
$p \leq 4$, with closed sub-Hopf links indicated.

**Visual conventions.**

- **Solid trefoil** (filled): left trefoil $T_{2,3}$ — the
  proton quark constituent.
- **Outline trefoil** (unfilled): right trefoil
  $\sigma(T_{2,3})$ — the electron.
- **Circle**: unknot $K_{0_1}$ — the neutron.
- **Interlocked circles**: Hopf link $\mathcal{L}_H$ — the
  $\bowtie$ operation connecting components.
- **Green border**: closed $1s$ sub-link (2 electrons).
- **Blue border**: closed $2s$ sub-link (2 electrons).
- **Brown border**: nuclear sub-link (protons + neutrons).

| $p$ | Atom | $n$ | Structure | Sub-links | Shells |
|----:|------|----:|-----------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | H | 0 | $[\mathfrak{p}] \bowtie T_{2,3}$ | none | $1s^1$ (open) |
| 2 | He | 2 | $[\alpha] \bowtie \underbrace{(T_{2,3} \bowtie T_{2,3})}_{\text{closed}}$ | $k_\alpha = 1$ | **$1s^2$ (closed)** |
| 3 | Li | 4 | $[3\mathfrak{p}\,4\mathfrak{n}] \bowtie \underbrace{(T_{2,3} \bowtie T_{2,3})}_{\text{closed}} \bowtie T_{2,3}$ | $k_\alpha = 1$ | $1s^2 + 2s^1$ |
| 4 | Be | 5 | $[4\mathfrak{p}\,5\mathfrak{n}] \bowtie \underbrace{(T_{2,3} \bowtie T_{2,3})}_{\text{closed}} \bowtie \underbrace{(T_{2,3} \bowtie T_{2,3})}_{\text{closed}}$ | $k_\alpha = 2$ | $1s^2 + 2s^2$ |

**Observations.**

1. **Hydrogen** ($p = 1$): a single proton $\mathfrak{p}$
   linked to a single electron $T_{2,3}$.  No sub-links — the
   shell is open.

2. **Helium** ($p = 2$): the $\alpha$ particle
   ($2\mathfrak{p} \bowtie 2\mathfrak{n}$) linked to two
   electrons forming a **closed Hopf pair**.  This is the first
   closed sub-link.  Both the nuclear and electronic sub-links
   are closed — helium is doubly stable.

3. **Lithium** ($p = 3$): the $1s^2$ sub-link is closed, but
   the third electron links at a higher level.  The $2s$ shell
   is open — lithium is chemically reactive (one valence
   electron).

4. **Beryllium** ($p = 4$): two closed electron pairs ($1s^2$
   and $2s^2$), both Hopf-linked.  The $2s$ shell is closed
   but the $2p$ sub-shell has not yet started — beryllium has
   two valence electrons.
