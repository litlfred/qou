> *Je pense, donc je suis.*
> — René Descartes, *Discours de la méthode* (1637)

Our starting point is a set of macroscopic physical observations. We see three primary colours. We navigate space where viewpoint does not alter physics. Time flows strictly in one direction. Smooth change prevails across scales, rendering linear approximation (integration and differentiation) effective. The universe has a measurable volume, and measurements pair consistently with instruments.

We formalise these observations mathematically. Following Heisenberg, we do not ask what the universe "is," but rather how its mechanical processes compose and transform. Wigner noted the "unreasonable effectiveness" of mathematics in physics \cite{wigner1960}. We take this unreasonableness not as a mystery, but as a principle to be axiomatised: the observable universe *is* the mathematical structure forced by its own observations. The natural setting for such a structure is category theory, which describes how processes combine without fixing a background ontology.
