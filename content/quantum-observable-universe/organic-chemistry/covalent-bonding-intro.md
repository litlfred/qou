Where metallic bonding shares residual sub-links across an entire lattice, molecular chemistry localises the sharing between two atoms. We make this precise as a **covalent bond** of order $k$ — a shared sub-Hopf link of $k$ trefoils simultaneously linked to one open site in each partner — and read off single, double, and triple bonds as $k = 1, 2, 3$ (def: [covalent bond](#def:covalent-bond-types)). The crossing-energy formula $E_{\mathrm{bond}}(k) = k \cdot \mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}}(T(2,2)) + \binom{k}{2} \Delta E_{\mathrm{inter}}$ then tracks higher bond order as additional rigidity. We close the section by partitioning carbon's electron cloud $6 \cdot T_{2,3}$ into a closed $1s^2$ pair and a residual sub-link of size four (rem: [carbon's four open linking sites](#rem:carbon-valence)), showing that the half-filling $|R|/2n^2 = 1/2$ is what places carbon at the maximally covalent midpoint of the metallic-character spectrum.
