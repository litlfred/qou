Having pinned down chains, rings, and the $\mathrm{C}_6$ case, we sort the standard organic classes by two parameters of the Hopf-chain framework: the bond-order pattern $(k_1, \ldots, k_{N-1})$ along the backbone, and whether the structure is open or closed. The classification recovers alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, cumulenes, and polyynes as distinct distributions of shared trefoils, reads saturation as linking-site occupancy, and writes the hydrogen capacity formula $\mathrm{C}_N \mathrm{H}_{2N+2-2d}$ (open) versus $\mathrm{C}_N \mathrm{H}_{2N-2d}$ (closed) directly from the total degree of unsaturation $d = \sum_i (k_i - 1)$ (rem: [organic chain taxonomy from bond-order patterns](#rem:organic-chain-taxonomy)). Aromatic rings — those with $d = N/2$ — appear as the case where the secondary trefoils themselves close, suggesting Hückel's $4n + 2$ rule is the molecular analogue of the shell capacity $2n^2$.
