Having pinned down chains, rings, and the $\mathrm{C}_6$ case, we now sort the standard organic classes so that the familiar taxonomy — alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, and their relatives, together with their hydrogen counts — falls out of the Hopf-chain framework rather than being imposed by hand. Two parameters do the sorting: the bond-order pattern $(k_1, \ldots, k_{N-1})$ along the backbone, and whether the structure is open or closed. Against these, alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, cumulenes, and polyynes appear as distinct distributions of shared trefoils, and saturation reads as linking-site occupancy. The hydrogen capacity then follows directly: $\mathrm{C}_N \mathrm{H}_{2N+2-2d}$ (open) versus $\mathrm{C}_N \mathrm{H}_{2N-2d}$ (closed), read off from the total degree of unsaturation $d = \sum_i (k_i - 1)$ (rem: [organic chain taxonomy from bond-order patterns](#rem:organic-chain-taxonomy)). Aromatic rings — those with $d = N/2$ — appear as the case where the secondary trefoils themselves close, suggesting Hückel's $4n + 2$ rule is the molecular analogue of the shell capacity $2n^2$.
