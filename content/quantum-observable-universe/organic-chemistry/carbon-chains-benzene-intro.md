We instantiate the chain–ring machinery at $N = 6$, the smallest case where the contrast is experimentally sharp. The **linear $\mathrm{C}_6$ Hopf chain** assembles six carbons with a $6 \times 6$ tridiagonal linking matrix; interior carbons consume two of their four sites for the backbone and the endpoint carbons carry three dangling links each — the soft modes that make linear $\mathrm{C}_6$ flexible as cumulene or carbyne (ex: [linear $\mathrm{C}_6$ Hopf chain](#ex:carbon-chain-c6)). **Benzene** is the closed counterpart: six carbons threaded by $T(2,6)$, whose two components are the two delocalised $\pi$ electrons, and whose Alexander polynomial $q^2 + 1 + q^{-2}$ replaces six pairwise Hopf weights with a single threading invariant — explaining bond-order equalisation and the $\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_6$ formula without further input (ex: [benzene as a molecular Hopf ring](#ex:benzene-ring)). Comparing the two structures, the threaded ring binds one more atom at Alexander weight $3$ versus the chain's $5$, yet the chain persists because pairwise-to-threaded is discontinuous and the geometric reconfiguration sets an activation barrier (rem: [why the C$_6$ chain resists hexagonalisation](#rem:chain-ring-energy)). The section closes with the parameter-free prediction $\Delta E \approx 2 \cdot |q + q^{-1}|_{\mathrm{mol}} \approx 193\text{--}386\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$, matched against the experimental $200\text{--}400\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$ window — the $1/2$ threading factor is the resolution of the pairwise picture's factor-of-two overshoot (prop: [predicted vs. observed $\mathrm{C}_6$ ring-closure barrier](#prop:chain-ring-comparison)).
