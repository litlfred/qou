The universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the associative algebra generated by $e_1, e_2, e_3$ ([SU(2) basis](#def:su2-basis)) subject to the commutation relations. It provides the algebraic scaffolding for every finite-dimensional representation of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. It is the classical limit ($q \to 1$) of the quantum group $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$.

**PBW basis theorem.**
The monomials $\{e_1^{a}e_2^{b}e_3^{c} : a,b,c \geq 0\}$ form a $\mathbb{C}$-basis of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$. In the $q$-deformed setting, the analogous basis is the set of ordered monomials $\{E^{(a)}K^{s}F^{(b)}\}$ in the Lusztig divided-power generators. Ordering is enforced by the Groebner–Shirshov resolution of the $q$-Serre relations.

In this framework, the PBW basis serves two purposes:

1. It provides explicit coordinates for the fibre algebra $\mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)$ at each brane-tower level ([direct limit algebra](#def:direct-limit-algebra) in [Chapter 2](#chap:torsion)).
2. It ensures flatness of the deformation $q \mapsto q_n$. This guarantees the transition maps in the direct limit $\mathcal{A}_\infty = \varinjlim\Gamma(J^1 \mathcal{S}_n, \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2))$ are well-defined algebra homomorphisms.
