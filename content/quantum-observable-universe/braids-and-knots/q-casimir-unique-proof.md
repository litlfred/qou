## Proof

The centre $Z(\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2)))$ is a polynomial ring $R[C_q]$ generated by $C_q$ over the ground ring (Joseph–Letzter, Kassel VI.4.9). Every central element belongs to $R[C_q]$. The only central elements linear in $C_q$ take the form $a + b C_q$.

Fixing the eigenvalue on $V_j$ to $[j]_q[j+1]_q$ forces $b = 1$. Evaluating on the trivial representation $V_0$ yields $[0]_q[1]_q = 0$, forcing $a = 0$. Consequently, $C_q$ is unique. Alternative normalisations, such as the non-symmetric $q$-Casimir $C'_q = C_q + \text{const}$, differ only by a scalar shift absorbed into the ground ring.
