**Proposition (averaging-resonance rule for $c_k$ sectors).**

*Let $\beta \in H_n(q)$ be a typed-$\mathbf{B}_n$ braid word with
$n_\sigma$ pure $\sigma$-generators, $n_{\sigma^{-1}}$ pure
$\sigma^{-1}$-generators, and $n_{\mathrm{avg}}$ averaged crossings
$\sigma_{\mathrm{avg}} = \sigma - h/2$. Let $c_k$ be the rung-$k$
coefficient in the precision-ladder expansion of $\tau_M(\beta)$
about $q = 1$:*

$$
\tau_M(\beta) \;=\; \tau_M(\beta)(1) \,\cdot\, \prod_{k \ge 2} \bigl(1 + c_k \, b^k \hbar_q^{2k} \bigr).
$$

*Then $c_k$ lies in the **bar-invariant fixed-point sector** (i.e.,
picks up a $1/2$ fingerprint at the silver ratio $q = 1+\sqrt 2$)
**iff** the **averaging-resonance condition** is satisfied at order $k$:*

$$
\boxed{\;\;
m_k \;:=\; \max \bigl\{\, m \,\in\, \{0, 1, \ldots, n_{\mathrm{avg}}\} \;:\;
\binom{n_{\mathrm{avg}}}{m} \binom{n_\sigma + n_{\sigma^{-1}}}{k-m}
\ge \binom{n_\sigma + n_{\sigma^{-1}}}{k}
\,\bigr\} \;\ge\; 1
\;\;}
$$

*The integer $m_k$ counts the **dominant** number of averaged
positions in a $k$-fold cyclic substructure of $\beta$. The rung is
**pure-$\sigma$** iff $m_k = 0$ (no averaged-position dominance),
otherwise it is **averaging-resonant**.*

## Definitions

### Pure-$\sigma$ sector

Generated by Hecke generators $\sigma_i$ and their inverses
$\sigma_i^{-1} = \sigma_i - h$. Categorical contributions factor
through $\sigma$-eigenvalue powers $q^a \cdot (-q^{-1})^b$, which at
the silver ratio $q = 1+\sqrt 2$ live in
$\mathbb{Z}[1+\sqrt{2}] \subset \mathbb{R}(\sqrt 2)$. Pell-Lucas
extension; integer Pell-Lucas-clean values at silver ratio for
symmetric combinations.

### Bar-invariant fixed-point sector

Generated by the averaged crossing $\sigma_{\mathrm{avg}} = P_{\mathrm{avg}}(\sigma)
= (\sigma + \sigma^{-1})/2 = \sigma - h/2$, the bar-invariant
projection. Carries the explicit $1/2$ factor as a topological
fingerprint; at silver ratio gives Pell-Lucas-incompatible
rationals (specifically, rationals whose reduced numerator is not
in $\mathbb{Z}[1+\sqrt 2]$).

## Categorical action definition

The framework's categorical action of $H_n(q)$ on $V_{(2,1)} \subset
\mathbb{C}^2$ via the Young seminormal basis $\{T_1, T_2\}$ gives:

$$
\sigma_0 \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} q & 0 \\ 0 & -1/q \end{pmatrix}, \quad
\sigma_1 \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} -1/(q(q^2+1)) & b \\ b & q^3/(q^2+1) \end{pmatrix}
$$

where $b = \sqrt{q^4+q^2+1}/(q^2+1)$ is the Hoefsmit off-diagonal
coupling.

The averaged crossing acts as:
$$
\sigma_{i,\mathrm{avg}} = \sigma_i - \frac{h}{2}\mathbf{1}.
$$

This is the **bar-invariant** part of $\sigma_i$ under the involution
$\sigma_i \leftrightarrow \sigma_i^{-1}$:

$$
P_{\mathrm{avg}}(\sigma_i) = \frac{\sigma_i + \sigma_i^{-1}}{2}
= \frac{\sigma_i + (\sigma_i - h)}{2} = \sigma_i - h/2 = \sigma_{i,\mathrm{avg}}.
$$

## Derivation outline

### Step 1: Markov-trace expansion as polynomial in $h$

For any typed braid $\beta$ with $n_\sigma$, $n_{\sigma^{-1}}$, $n_{\mathrm{avg}}$
crossings, the Markov trace $\tau_M(\beta)$ expands as:

$$
\tau_M(\beta) = \sum_{k \ge 0} P_k(\beta) \cdot h^k
$$

where $P_k(\beta)$ is a polynomial in $q$ (and rational coefficients
depending on the typed-braid recipe).

**Each averaged crossing contributes** $-h/2$ explicitly, so $P_k$
collects terms with various distributions of $-h/2$ factors among
the $n_{\mathrm{avg}}$ averaged positions.

### Step 2: rung-$k$ classification by dominant configuration

The coefficient $c_k$ in the precision-ladder expansion picks up
contributions from $k$-fold cyclic substructures of $\beta$. Each
substructure selects $k$ positions from the $n_\sigma + n_{\sigma^{-1}} + n_{\mathrm{avg}} = n_{\mathrm{total}}$ crossings.

A $k$-position substructure selecting $m$ averaged crossings and
$k - m$ non-averaged crossings has:

- **Pure-$\sigma$ weight**: $\binom{n_\sigma + n_{\sigma^{-1}}}{k - m}$
  ways to choose non-averaged positions
- **Averaging weight**: $\binom{n_{\mathrm{avg}}}{m}$
  ways to choose averaged positions

The **dominant configuration** at order $k$ has $m = m_k$ averaged
positions where $m_k$ maximises $\binom{n_{\mathrm{avg}}}{m} \binom{n_\sigma + n_{\sigma^{-1}}}{k-m}$.

If $m_k \ge 1$, the dominant contribution INCLUDES averaged crossings,
giving the $1/2$ fingerprint. If $m_k = 0$, the dominant contribution
is pure-$\sigma$, giving Pell-Lucas-clean silver-ratio behaviour.

### Step 3: silver-ratio Pell-Lucas test

At $q = 1 + \sqrt{2}$:

- $\sigma$-eigenvalues = $1 \pm \sqrt{2}$ (silver-ratio conjugates)
- Sums $\sigma^a + \sigma^{-a}$ reduce to Pell-Lucas numbers
  ($a_n$: 1, 1, 3, 7, 17, 41, 99, 239, ...)
- $b^2 = 7/8$ exactly
- Pure-$\sigma$ products live in $\mathbb{Z}[1+\sqrt 2]$;
  symmetric combinations give Pell-Lucas integers
- Averaging contributions carry an extra $1/(2^{m_k})$ factor,
  PLUS the symmetric-combination integer — when reduced, the
  denominator $2^{m_k}$ may not divide the integer numerator,
  giving a non-Pell-Lucas-clean rational.

This is the **silver-ratio Pell-Lucas test** for sector classification.

## Refinements to investigate

1. **σ-eigenvalue weighting**: not all $k$-fold substructures
   contribute equally. The $\sigma$-eigenvalues $q$ and $-q^{-1}$
   are unequal, weighting different selections differently. At
   silver ratio, the symmetric combinations $q^a + q^{-a}$ may
   produce non-trivial cancellations that effectively make some
   $m \ne m_k$ configurations dominant.

2. **Cyclic structure constraints**: Markov-trace cyclicity
   $\tau_M(xy) = \tau_M(yx)$ restricts which substructures
   contribute. The $k$-fold substructures must close cyclically,
   reducing the effective count.

3. **Higher-order Wedderburn-block weighting**: the 1D reps
   ($V_{(3)}, V_{(1,1,1)}$) and 2D rep ($V_{(2,1)}$) contribute to
   $\tau_M$ with different per-block prefactors. The dominant
   configuration in each Wedderburn block may differ, with the
   sector outcome determined by the block whose absorption is
   strongest at order $k$.

## Why $k = 5$ specifically? — the half-fill cyclic hypothesis

The involution interpretation ($\sigma_{\mathrm{avg}} = P_{\mathrm{avg}}(\sigma) = \sigma - h/2$) produces the
$1/2$ fingerprint as a single-crossing structural fact, but it does
**not** explain why the fingerprint surfaces specifically at $k = 5$
and not at $k = 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10$. If involution propagated naively
through the Markov-trace expansion, every rung with $m_k \ge 1$ would
inherit the $1/2$ — which is the falsified naive argmax-binomial
rule.

**Stronger candidate hypothesis**: the outlier rung is

$$
\boxed{\;\; k_{\text{outlier}} \;=\; \left\lfloor \frac{n_{\text{total}}}{2} \right\rfloor \;\;}
$$

For the proton/neutron typed-$\mathbf{B}_3$ recipe with
$n_{\text{total}} = n_\sigma + n_{\sigma^{-1}} + n_{\mathrm{avg}} = 11$,
this gives $k_{\text{outlier}} = 5$. ✓

**Geometric motivation**: at $k = \lfloor n_{\text{total}}/2 \rfloor$,
a $k$-fold cyclic substructure covers exactly half the braid word.
This is the **unique** rung where both averaged positions can be
captured by a substructure **without** redundancy — at lower $k$ at
most one is captured (with high combinatorial weight), and at higher
$k$ both are forced to be included plus more (washing the $1/2$
fingerprint out by overlap with pure-$\sigma$ tails).

**Falsifiability**: the half-fill hypothesis predicts
$k_{\text{outlier}} = 5$ for $n_{\text{total}} = 11$ and shifts with
$n_{\text{total}}$. Probing typed-$\mathbf{B}_n$ braids with
$n_{\text{total}} \in \{9, 10, 12, 13\}$ should give
$k_{\text{outlier}} \in \{4, 5, 6, 6\}$ respectively. Either
empirical result confirms or falsifies the rule.

**Status**: this is a working hypothesis, more predictive than the
involution+Leibniz interpretation but not yet derived from the
categorical action. The involution interpretation **supplies the
magnitude** of the fingerprint ($1/2 = h/2$ at silver ratio); the
half-fill hypothesis **supplies the rung**.

## Honest assessment

The averaging-resonance phenomenon is **real** and observed at the
single rung $k = 5$ in the proton typed-$\mathbf{B}_3$ ladder.
Involution + Leibniz explains the $1/2$ magnitude but does **not**
explain rung selection. The naive argmax-binomial rule predicts
the wrong rungs (25% accuracy). The half-fill hypothesis
($k = \lfloor n_{\text{total}}/2 \rfloor$) is the strongest current
candidate for rung selection but remains untested at other
$n_{\text{total}}$.

**Three open R&D directions for closing the rule:**

1. σ-eigenvalue weighting — non-trivial cancellations under the
   silver-ratio reduction may select rungs by parity of substructure
   eigenvalue combinations.
2. Cyclic structure constraints — Markov-trace cyclicity
   $\tau_M(xy) = \tau_M(yx)$ enforces a closure that nontrivially
   restricts substructure counting (the half-fill hypothesis is an
   instance of this).
3. Per-Wedderburn-block weighting — the 1D ($V_{(3)}, V_{(1,1,1)}$)
   and 2D ($V_{(2,1)}$) blocks contribute with different prefactors;
   the dominant configuration may differ per block.

A future Lean theorem would require: (i) explicit symbolic expansion
of $\tau_M(\beta_{\text{typed}})$ to high order in $h$, (ii)
coefficient-by-coefficient classification by $\sigma$-eigenvalue
structure, (iii) proof that the half-fill rung uniquely picks up the
$1/2$ fingerprint.
