The **$q$-Casimir element** is the distinguished central element of the quantum group $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ ([def:quantum-su2](#def:quantum-su2)). Its eigenvalue on the spin-$j$ irreducible encodes the categorical $q$-angular momentum.

**Definition.**
Within $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ generated by $E, F, K, K^{-1}$ with parameter $q^2 \neq 1$, the **(normalised) $q$-Casimir element** is

$$
  C_q  =  F E  +  \frac{q\,K + q^{-1} K^{-1} - (q + q^{-1})}
                       {(q - q^{-1})^2}.
$$

The $(q + q^{-1})$ subtraction in the numerator provides the standard normalisation (Kassel VI.4). It forces the eigenvalue to vanish on the trivial representation and ensures a finite classical limit $q \to 1$. Using $[E, F] = (K - K^{-1})/(q - q^{-1})$, this element rewrites as:

$$
  C_q  =  E F  +  \frac{q^{-1}\,K + q\,K^{-1} - (q + q^{-1})}
                        {(q - q^{-1})^2}.
$$

This element is **central**: it commutes with every generator ($C_q E = E C_q$, $C_q F = F C_q$, $C_q K = K C_q$).

## Action on the spin-$j$ representation

On the $(2j+1)$-dimensional highest-weight module $V_j$, the central element acts as the scalar:

$$
  C_q \big|_{V_j}  =  [j]_q\,[j+1]_q\,\mathrm{id}_{V_j},
$$

where $[n]_q = (q^n - q^{-n})/(q - q^{-1})$ is the symmetric $q$-number. This scalar is the **$q$-Casimir eigenvalue**. The block [def:q-angular-momentum](#def:q-angular-momentum) specialises this to $J_q^2(K) = [j]_q\,[j+1]_q$, defining the $q$-angular momentum of a knot. The sibling block [prop:q-casimir-classical-limit](#prop:q-casimir-classical-limit) recovers the classical limit $j(j+1)$ over $\mathbb{R}$.

## Role in the categorical framework

The centrality of $C_q$ makes it an endomorphism of the identity functor on $\mathbf{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}_q(2))$. It forms a natural transformation $C_q \colon \mathrm{id} \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ whose component at $V_j$ is $[j]_q[j+1]_q \cdot \mathrm{id}_{V_j}$. In the rigid monoidal setting, this functions as the categorical angular-momentum-squared operator.

**Terminology.** The $q$-Casimir *element* generalises Casimir's invariant for $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. It is unrelated to the Casimir *effect* (the vacuum force between conducting boundaries) aside from the eponym. We treat the Casimir effect separately via the $\varepsilon$-vacuum conjecture [`conj:casimir-epsilon-vacuum`](#conj:casimir-epsilon-vacuum).
