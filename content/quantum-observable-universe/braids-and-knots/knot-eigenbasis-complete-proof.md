*Part (1).* When $q$ is not a root of unity, the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_n(R, q)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}[S_n]$ as an abstract algebra (Jimbo's theorem) and is therefore semisimple. The $q$-Schur--Weyl decomposition of $V^{\otimes n}$ is the standard result for $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ (see Chari--Pressley, Chapter 10). The restriction to partitions with at most two parts reflects $\mathrm{rank}(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = 1$: the Young diagrams have at most two rows.

*Part (2).* Alexander's theorem guarantees the surjectivity of the braid closure map $\beta \mapsto \hat{\beta}$. The Markov trace on $H_n(R, q)$ is the unique trace satisfying the Markov property $\mathrm{tr}_M(x \sigma_n^{\pm 1}) = z^{\pm 1}\mathrm{tr}_M(x)$ for $x \in H_n(R, q)$ and a parameter $z$ determined by $q$. On each irreducible summand $S_\lambda$, the trace restricts to a non-zero scalar multiple of the character. Consequently, some braid closure detects every summand.

*Part (3).* The space $V_{\mathrm{phys}} := \varinjlim V^{\otimes n}$ forms the algebraic direct limit. It is the union of finite-dimensional subspaces $V^{\otimes n}$, each completely decomposed by (1). The irreducible summands, indexed by knot types via the closure map, thus span $V_{\mathrm{phys}}$. The construction remains within rigid algebraic categories; we require no metric completion.
