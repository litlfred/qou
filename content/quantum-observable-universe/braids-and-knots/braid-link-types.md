This block formalizes concrete Braid and Link types in Lean, discharging Atom 1 of [`rem:markov-closure-map-axiomatic`](#rem:markov-closure-map-axiomatic) for the 2-strand case.

## Constructed infrastructure

We implement a minimal infrastructure:
- `inductive Link` with constructors `unknot`, `hopfLink`, `torusLink2 k` (sufficient for 2-strand braid analysis).
- `Braid2 := ℤ` to represent the 2-strand braid group as integers generated by a single crossing $\sigma$.
- `closure2 : Braid2 → Link` to explicitly pattern match Markov closure on the crossing count.
- `jonesAtOne : Link → ℝ` to evaluate classical-limit Jones values.

## The atomic theorem

$$
\boxed{
  \widehat{\sigma_1^2 \in B_2} \;=\; \text{Hopf link}
}
$$
The theorem `closure2_at_two_crossings_eq_hopf : closure2 σ_sq = hopfLink` holds by definitional equality (`rfl`).

## The composed chain

To identify $c_3 = -2$, we compose the chain:
$$
  \sigma_1^2 \in B_2 \;\xrightarrow{\text{closure}_2}\; \text{Hopf link} \;\xrightarrow{J(q{=}1)}\; -2
$$
We formalize this via:
```lean
theorem closure2_jones_at_one_sigma_sq_eq_neg_two :
    jonesAtOne (closure2 Braid2.σ_sq) = -2
```
which closes via `closure2_at_two_crossings_eq_hopf` and `jonesAtOne_hopf_eq_neg_two`.

## Remaining n-strand case (open)

The framework requires an analogous chain for 3-strand typed braids in $B_3$. The categorical derivation proceeds as follows:
1. The 3-strand typed-$B_3$ braids $\beta_p$ and $\beta_n$ each have 11 typed crossings.
2. At order $\epsilon^6$ in the perturbative $\tau_M$ expansion, the dominant contribution selects 3-element subsets of crossings.
3. The maximally contributing subset forms a 2-strand sub-braid extracted by collapsing the third strand.
4. This 2-strand sub-braid contains 2 active crossings, yielding the Hopf link upon Markov closure.
5. Composing these steps identifies $c_3 = J_{\text{Hopf}}(1) = -2$.

Steps 1 and 2 rely on mechanical combinatorics. Step 4 reduces to the 2-strand case. Step 5 is definitional equality. The remaining formalization targets Step 3, requiring a definition of the general braid group $B_n$ and sub-braid extraction via strand-collapse.

Building on Mathlib's `BraidGroup`, we must implement:
- A mapping `subBraid : Braid n → Finset (Fin n) → Braid (size of subset)`.
- Theorems preserving Markov closure under sub-braid extraction.
- The identification of the dominant sub-braid at order $\epsilon^6$.
