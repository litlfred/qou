Before encountering $\rho$ in the equations that follow, we state its geometric purpose. 

The representation $\rho \colon B_3 \to \mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the exact holonomy of the symmetric group $S_3$ acting on the branched cover $\mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$. It encodes how the spectral modes $w_k$ — the Lagrange resolvents defined by the discrete Fourier transform on $C_5$ ([Lagrange resolvents](#prop:lagrange-resolvent-transform)) — permute when transported around the branch points.

These spectral coordinates define the chiral sheets of the geometry. The conjugate pairs $(w_1, w_4)$ and $(w_2, w_3)$ span two-dimensional eigenspaces of the cyclic action. Together, they tile the four non-trivial modes of the fibre into time-like and space-like sectors. The representation $\rho$ acts on these sheets through two elementary operations:

1. **The $\alpha$ operator (twist).**
   The generator $\sigma_1$ ([braid twist](#def:braid-twist-flip)) swaps the two conjugate pairs: $(w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4) \mapsto (w_2, w_1, w_4, w_3)$. In the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ embedding, this is a rotation by $T\varTheta$ around the $e_1$ axis ([SU(2) generators](#def:su2-generators)). Physically, $\alpha$ rotates the time-like pairs — the modes carrying the dynamical phase of the Reeb flow — while preserving overall chirality.

2. **The $\beta$ operator (flip).**
   The generator $\sigma_2$ reverses the full tuple: $(w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4) \mapsto (w_4, w_3, w_2, w_1)$. In $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, this is a rotation around $e_2$, reflecting the space-like axis. The flip exchanges the two chirality sectors, implementing parity inversion geometrically.

Together, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ generate the Klein four-group $V_4$ acting on the resolvent tuple. Their composition $\alpha\beta \colon \mathbf{w} \mapsto (w_3, w_4, w_1, w_2)$ is the $3$-cycle whose cube returns to the identity on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ ([holonomy generators](#rem:holonomy-trefoil)). The full $S_3$ holonomy is the group generated by these rotations and reflections of the chiral sheets.

Whenever $\rho$ appears in the braid-group representations and knot invariants below, it acts by twisting the time-like modes and reflecting the space-like axis of the spectral geometry. This rotation--reflection pair on the chiral sheets provides the physical content of the abstract topological symmetry.
