Three mathematically distinct constructions share the name *Markov*. They arise from the same combinatorial source (the braid group $B_n$) but operate at different levels of the Cat$\to$Alg$\to$Geom$\to$Arch$\to$Phys arc.

1. **Markov trace** (Jones--Wenzl). The unique trace functional $\mathrm{tr}_M \colon H_n(R,q) \to R$ satisfying cyclicity, the Markov axiom $\mathrm{tr}_M^{(n)}(x\sigma_{n-1}) = z \cdot \mathrm{tr}_M^{(n-1)}(x)$, and $\mathrm{tr}_M(1)=1$. This algebraic object feeds the mass formula: $\mathfrak{m}(\varrho) = m_e \cdot |\mathrm{tr}_M(\rho(\beta))| \cdot \mathrm{VolDensity}(K,q_0)$. We denote it by $\mathrm{tr}_M$.

2. **Markov chain** (stochastic dynamics). The random walk on braid words induced by Hecke--Markov transition probabilities $p_{w \to w'} = \mathrm{tr}_M(\rho(w')) \cdot \overline{\mathrm{tr}_M(\rho(w'))} / \sum \mathrm{tr}_M(\rho(w'')) \cdot \overline{\mathrm{tr}_M(\rho(w''))}$. The bar denotes the Hecke bar-involution $q \leftrightarrow q^{-1}$; at real $q_0$ the form reduces to $|\mathrm{tr}_M|^2$. This stochastic object yields error bounds on the mass formula via Perron--Frobenius dominant-eigenvalue convergence (see Chapter Stochastic Mechanics).

3. **Markov moves** (topological equivalence). Stabilisation $\beta \mapsto \beta\sigma_n^{\pm 1}$ and conjugation $\beta \mapsto \gamma\beta\gamma^{-1}$ on $B_n$. Two braids represent the same link if and only if they are related by a finite sequence of Markov moves (Markov's theorem, 1935). This topological input makes the Jones polynomial a knot invariant.

These uses are strictly separated: (1) is an algebraic functional, (2) is a probability measure, and (3) is an equivalence relation. Context always disambiguates. When precision matters, we write $\mathrm{tr}_M$ for (1), "Markov chain" for (2), and "Markov move" for (3).
