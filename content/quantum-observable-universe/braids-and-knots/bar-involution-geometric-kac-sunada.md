## Kac-Sunada uniqueness

Distinct manifolds can share a Laplace-Dirichlet spectrum (Kac 1966, Sunada 1985). By analogy, distinct braids can share a Hecke trace $\tau_M(\beta)$ at a fixed $q$, forming isospectral pairs. 

Matching quantities at both $q$ and $q^{-1}$ enforces bar symmetry and restricts this ambiguity. A chiral channel like the Faddeev $\Phi_q$ distinguishes orientation, whereas the symmetric dilogarithm $\mathrm{Li}_2^q$ evaluates the bar-symmetrized spectrum. Thus, the theory restricts to bar-symmetric projections to resolve isospectral equivalence.
