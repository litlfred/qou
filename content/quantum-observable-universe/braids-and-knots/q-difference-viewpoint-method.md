### The $q$-difference / root-of-unity viewpoint

We analyse several $q$-deformed quantities—the $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ Habiro element, the $\alpha_{EM}$ next-order factor ([`prop:alpha-em-habiro-bar-involution`](#prop:alpha-em-habiro-bar-involution)), and the Markov trace—through their behaviour under the **$q$-difference shift** $\sigma_q : q \mapsto q\zeta$ for roots of unity $\zeta$, rather than via power series at $q=1$. This method recurs throughout the categorical derivation.

A Habiro-type element $F(q)$ satisfies a $q$-difference equation $(1 - \sigma_q)\,F = \delta\,(q;q)_\infty$ (Habiro's equation, which restricts the level-1 Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov connection). Three structural features make this tractable:

1. **Fourier diagonalisation.** The discrete Fourier transform over the cyclic group $\langle\zeta\rangle$ diagonalises the shift $\sigma_q$. Its eigenvectors are the root-of-unity evaluations $F(\zeta_d)$.
2. **Witten–Reshetikhin–Turaev truncation.** The $q$-Pochhammer $(u;u)_m = \prod_{k\le m}(1 - u^k)$ vanishes at a primitive $d$-th root of unity whenever $d \le m$. Evaluating a Habiro expansion $F = \sum_m a_m\,(u;u)_m / D$ at $\zeta_d$ truncates the sum to its first $d$ modes, yielding an exact, finite spectrum.
3. **Mode-by-mode solution.** For rational $F$, solving the $q$-difference equation requires partial-fraction decomposition over its poles. Each pole isolates one Fourier mode, extracting exact modular data (residues at $\zeta_d$).

This viewpoint operates intrinsically at the **fibre level**. The variable $q$ remains formal, $\mathrm{Im}(q)$ is unconstrained, and the operative symmetry is the **bar involution** $q \mapsto q^{-1}$ (not a reality assumption). Only the observable component, generated by evaluating at the real archimedean substrate, is strictly real. The root-of-unity spectra and residues constitute exact identities in the appropriate cyclotomic field, completely independent of their eventual archimedean realisation.

The duality between $\alpha_{EM}$ and $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ illustrates this method. Both are Habiro elements obeying identical $q$-difference machinery. A single modular datum distinguishes them: the $\alpha_{EM}$ element $[2]_q(1-h^2\alpha)$ is a bar-involution fixed point with a zero at $\zeta_4$. The $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ trace difference is not a bar fixed point and possesses a pole at $\zeta_4$. The polynomial, bar-fixed nature of $\alpha_{EM}$ allows direct identification of its next-order form as a Habiro element. The $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ analogue requires cancelling a bar-anti-invariant part first.
