**Proposition.**
*The confinement level $\ell(K, q)$ is unique:
$\ell(K, q) = \min\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$ is well-defined
because:*

1. *the filtration $J^\bullet(q)$ is **monotone**:
   $J^0(q) \subseteq J^1(q) \subseteq \cdots \subseteq J^m(q)
   = H_n(R, q)$;*
2. *the set of valid levels $\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$ is
   **upward-closed** in $\{0, 1, \ldots, m\}$ (if $T_w \in
   J^\ell(q)$ and $\ell' \geq \ell$, then $T_w \in J^{\ell'}(q)$);*
3. *the set is **nonempty** (it contains $m$ since $J^m = H_n$).*

*Therefore the minimum exists and is unique.  Tied operators
([confinement level](#def:confinement-level)) enter the filtration
together at a single level, so even with degeneracy the level
remains uniquely defined.*

*Local uniqueness in $q$: by continuity of shadow prices on the
generic locus, $\ell(K, q')$ is locally constant on a neighborhood
of $q$ except at the boundary variety where some $E(\lambda, q)
= 0$.  See $\preceq_q$ ordering ([local shadow ordering](#def:local-shadow-ordering)).*
