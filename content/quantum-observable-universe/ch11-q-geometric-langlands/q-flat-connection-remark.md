The classical flatness condition $\nabla^2 = 0$ is the geometric
incarnation of $d^2 = 0$.  In the QOU, $d_q^2 \neq 0$ introduces a
canonical obstruction to flatness, proportional to $(q-1)$.  The
$q$-flatness equation $\nabla_q^2 = (q-1)\,\mathcal{F}_q$ absorbs
this obstruction: a $q$-flat connection is not truly flat but carries
the *minimal possible curvature* compatible with the $q$-deformation.

This is analogous to the role of the $q$-jacobiator
(Proposition \ref{prop:q-jacobiator}): the failure of the Jacobi
identity at $q \neq 1$ is not an error but a controlled deformation
that encodes the substrate geometry.

From the Hecke perspective, the $q$-curvature $\mathcal{F}_q$
is the categorical shadow of the non-commutativity of Hecke operators
at different places: classically Hecke operators at distinct points
commute, but the $q$-deformation introduces $(q-1)$-proportional
commutators.
