Under the hypotheses of
Conjecture \ref{conj:q-geometric-langlands}, the fibre functor $\tau$
of the QOU determines the $q$-Geometric Langlands equivalence
$\mathbb{L}_q$ uniquely up to natural isomorphism.  More precisely:

$$
  \mathbb{L}_q(\mathcal{M})(\mathcal{L}_q)
  \;\simeq\;
  \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Rep}(\check{G}_q)}
  \big(\tau(-),\;
  \mathrm{Hecke}_q(\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{L}_q)\big),
$$

where $\mathrm{Hecke}_q(\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{L}_q)$ is the tensor
functor $V_\lambda^{(q)} \mapsto
\mathbf{T}_{x,\lambda}^{(q)}(\mathcal{M}) \otimes
\mathcal{L}_q(V_\lambda^{(q)})_x^*$ encoding all Hecke eigenvalue
data simultaneously.

The rigidity of $\tau$
(Proposition \ref{prop:fibre-functor-rigidity}) forces
$\mathbb{L}_q$ to be unique: any two equivalences compatible with the
Hecke eigensheaf structure and the fibre functor are connected by a
unique natural isomorphism.
