In the classical setting a local system on $X$ is equivalent to a
representation of $\pi_1(X)$, which in turn corresponds to a flat
connection.  The flatness condition $d^2 = 0$ ensures that parallel
transport depends only on the homotopy class of the path.

In the QOU the $q$-deformed exterior derivative satisfies $d_q^2 \neq 0$
(Proposition \ref{prop:categorical-dq-squared}), so the naive flatness
condition fails.  The $q$-monodromy condition replaces it: the
deviation from the identity for contractible loops is controlled by
$(q-1)$ and vanishes in the classical limit.

This is the categorified counterpart of the fact that the Hecke
relation $(T - q)(T + q^{-1}) = 0$ deforms the symmetric group
relation $(T-1)(T+1) = 0$.  Just as braiding eigenvalues split from
$\pm 1$ to $q$ and $-q^{-1}$, monodromy splits from trivial to
$q$-parametrised.
