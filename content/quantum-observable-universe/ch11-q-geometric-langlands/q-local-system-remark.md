In the classical setting a local system on $X$ is equivalent to a
representation of $\pi_1(X)$, corresponding to a flat connection with
$d^2 = 0$.  Parallel transport depends only on the homotopy class
of the path.

In the QOU the $q$-deformed exterior derivative satisfies
$d_q^2 \neq 0$
([non-vanishing of $d_q^2$](#prop:categorical-dq-squared)), so the
naive flatness condition fails.  The $q$-monodromy condition
replaces it: the deviation from the identity for contractible loops
is controlled by $(q-1)$ and vanishes in the classical limit.

The Kummer filtration provides a finer invariant.  The Yekutieli
class $\kappa_0 = e(\xi) \in H^2(\mathcal{S}, \mathbb{G}_m)$
has Kummer reduction $\kappa_0 \bmod n = 0$ precisely when
$n \mid \chi(A)$
([Kummer filtration](#prop:kummer-filtration-obstruction)).
At such levels the $q$-monodromy becomes $n$-divisible,
producing a sublocus of $\mathrm{LocSys}_{G_q}(X)$ with
enhanced lifting properties.  At levels $n \nmid \chi(A)$ the
perpetual obstruction forces non-trivial $q$-holonomy residue
on every $q$-local system.

This parallels the Hecke deformation: the Hecke relation
$(T - q)(T + q^{-1}) = 0$ deforms the symmetric group relation
$(T-1)(T+1) = 0$.  Just as braiding eigenvalues split from
$\pm 1$ to $q$ and $-q^{-1}$, monodromy splits from trivial to
$q$-parametrised.
