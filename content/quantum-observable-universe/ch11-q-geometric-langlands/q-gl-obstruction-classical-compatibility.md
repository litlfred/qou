The perpetual obstruction
([Yekutieli obstruction](#prop:q-gl-obstruction-calc)) and the
classical limit
([classical recovery](#prop:q-gl-classical-limit)) are compatible,
but their coexistence reveals that the $q$-Geometric Langlands
correspondence is a **singular deformation** of the classical one.

**The classical limit sits at a degenerate point.**
The point $q = 1$ is the universal torsion point: it belongs to
$\mu_n$ for every $n \geq 1$.  At $q = 1$ the $q$-exterior
derivative reduces to $d$, the obstruction $d_q^2$ vanishes, and
the $q$-curvature $\mathcal{F}_q = 0$.  Classical flatness
$\nabla^2 = 0$ is recovered, and the Beilinson--Drinfeld
equivalence $\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{BD}}$ holds.  However, $q = 1$
lies in $\mu_\infty$, not in
$\mathbb{G}_m^{\mathrm{nt}}$ — the very locus where
$\mathbb{L}_q$ is well-defined.  The classical correspondence is
the *degenerate fibre* of the $\mathbb{G}_m$-family, not a
generic point.

**The perpetual obstruction constrains the non-torsion locus.**
For $q \in \mathbb{G}_m^{\mathrm{nt}}$ and $\chi(A) \neq 0$,
the Yekutieli class $\kappa_0$ is non-trivial at density-$1$
Kummer levels.  This means $q$-flatness is a strictly stronger
condition than classical flatness: fewer connections are
$q$-flat than classically flat.  The moduli
$\mathrm{LocSys}_{\check{G}_q}(X)$ is a proper subspace of
what the classical theory would predict.

**Compatibility.**
There is no contradiction because:

1. The classical limit $q \to 1$ is a limit to a
   *boundary point* of the parameter space
   $\mathbb{G}_m^{\mathrm{nt}}$, not an interior point.
   The $\mathbb{G}_m$-family $\{\mathbb{L}_q\}$ is defined on
   the open complement of $\mu_\infty$, and $q = 1$ lies in the
   closure.

2. As $q \to 1$, the $(q-1)$-factor in the $q$-flatness equation
   $\nabla_q^2 = (q-1)\,\mathcal{F}_q$ forces $\nabla_q^2 \to 0$
   even though $\mathcal{F}_q$ remains non-zero: the curvature is
   *screened* by the vanishing prefactor.  The obstruction does not
   disappear; it becomes invisible.

3. The Kummer constraint (property (4) of the
   [$q$-GL conjecture](#conj:q-geometric-langlands)) encodes this
   singular behaviour: the equivalence degenerates precisely at
   roots of unity, and the classical limit is the universal
   degeneration.

This structure is characteristic of quantum group deformations:
the generic fibre ($q$ non-torsion) carries richer structure than
the special fibre ($q = 1$), and the classical theory is recovered
not by continuity but by specialisation to a singular point.
