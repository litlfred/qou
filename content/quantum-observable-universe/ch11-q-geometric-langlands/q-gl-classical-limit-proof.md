Each component of the $q$-Geometric Langlands correspondence is a
continuous deformation in $q$, and we verify that the $q \to 1$ limit
of each yields the classical counterpart.

**$q$-Local systems**: at $q = 1$ the $q$-monodromy condition
(Definition \ref{def:q-local-system}) becomes
$\mathcal{L}_1(\varrho) = \mathrm{id}$ for contractible $\varrho$,
which is the classical condition that monodromy depends only on
homotopy class.  Hence $\mathrm{LocSys}_{G_1}(X) =
\mathrm{LocSys}_G(X)$.

**$q$-$\mathcal{D}$-modules**: the $q$-flatness equation
$\nabla_q^2 = (q-1)\mathcal{F}_q$ becomes $\nabla^2 = 0$ at $q=1$.
The category $\mathcal{D}_q\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ specialises to the
category of $\mathcal{D}$-modules (modules for the sheaf of
differential operators).

**$q$-Hecke operators**: the eigenvalues $q$ and $-q^{-1}$ degenerate
to $1$ and $-1$.  The $q$-Hecke correspondence degenerates to the
classical Hecke correspondence, and the factorisation defect
$[\mathbf{T}_x, \mathbf{T}_y] = O(q-1)$ vanishes.

**$q$-Opers**: the transversality condition is
$q$-independent and persists at $q = 1$, where $q$-opers become
classical opers in the sense of Beilinson--Drinfeld.

Since each ingredient converges and the equivalence $\mathbb{L}_q$
is uniquely determined by the fibre functor
(Theorem \ref{thm:q-gl-fiber-functor}), the limit $\mathbb{L}_1 =
\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{BD}}$ is the unique equivalence compatible with
classical Hecke operators and the identity fibre functor.  $\square$
