The fibre-functor mediation theorem
(Theorem \ref{thm:q-gl-fiber-functor}) connects the $q$-Geometric
Langlands correspondence to the observability axiom of the QOU
(Definition \ref{def:quantum-observable-universe}):

1. **Observability determines duality.** The observability condition
   $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0$ constrains the
   adjoint representation and thereby the fibre functor $\tau$.  Since
   $\tau$ uniquely determines $\mathbb{L}_q$, the automorphic--spectral
   duality is a *consequence* of observability.

2. **Fibre-functor rigidity = uniqueness of physics.** The rigidity
   of $\tau$ (Proposition \ref{prop:fibre-functor-rigidity}) ensures
   that the Langlands equivalence is unique: there is exactly one way
   to match gauge configurations with matter content, just as there is
   exactly one fibre functor (up to unique isomorphism) on the
   representation category.

3. **$q$-deformation = quantum corrections.** The passage from
   $\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{BD}}$ to $\mathbb{L}_q$ is controlled by
   $(q-1)$, which plays the role of $\hbar_q$ (the $q$-Planck
   constant, Definition \ref{def:quantum-planck-constant}).  The
   $q$-Geometric Langlands correspondence is thus the fully
   quantum-corrected gauge/matter duality, with the classical
   Langlands programme recovered in the semiclassical limit
   $\hbar_q \to 0$.
