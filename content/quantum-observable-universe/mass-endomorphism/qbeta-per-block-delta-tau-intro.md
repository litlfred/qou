The previous section evaluated the Markov trace block by block. We now read these characters through the $T_\beta$ endomorphism. 

The per-block characters $\chi_\lambda(\Delta_{T_\beta})(q)$ over $H_3(q)$ admit closed forms and vanish at the classical point $q = 1$ ([`prop:qbeta-per-block-chi-closed-forms`](#prop:qbeta-per-block-chi-closed-forms)). Once the Markov weights take their canonical values, this vanishing forces the trace difference $\Delta\mathrm{tr}_M$ to carry an explicit $(q^2-1)$ factor ([`prop:qbeta-delta-tau-canonical-conditional-q2-minus-1-factor`](#prop:qbeta-delta-tau-canonical-conditional-q2-minus-1-factor)). We formalise this conditional statement via a class hypothesis (§3b-cond), which we inhabit with an explicit $\sqrt{q}$-bearing instance ([`prop:qbeta-canonical-markov-weights-explicit-instance`](#prop:qbeta-canonical-markov-weights-explicit-instance)).

The low-order Taylor data of the surrogate $\Delta\mathrm{tr}_M$ ([`prop:qbeta-delta-tau-surrogate-taylor-coefficients`](#prop:qbeta-delta-tau-surrogate-taylor-coefficients)) provides the exact per-block input required by the downstream decay-side $c_k$ recursion. In contrast, the downstream extraction itself relies on calibration anchoring.
