**Proposition (Iwahori–Hecke algebra is Baer-* at generic $q$).**

*Let $R$ be a field of characteristic 0 and $q \in R$ generic
(not a root of unity, $q \neq 1$). Equip $H_n(q)$ with the bar
involution $* \colon \sigma_i \mapsto \sigma_i^{-1}$, $q \mapsto q^{-1}$
(extended antilinearly). Then $H_n(q)$ is a **Baer-*-ring**: every
The right (resp. left) annihilator ideal is generated by a *self-adjoint
idempotent* (a projection).*

### Proof

By the canonical Wedderburn decomposition for $H_n(q)$ at generic
$q$ ([`prop:wedderburn-weight-canonical`](#prop:wedderburn-weight-canonical),
[`prop:hoefsmit-seminormal-form`](#prop:hoefsmit-seminormal-form)):

$$
  H_n(q) \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{\lambda \vdash n} M_{d_\lambda}(R),
$$

where $d_\lambda$ is the dimension of the $\lambda$-isotypic
component (Hoefsmit seminormal form
[`prop:hoefsmit-seminormal-form`](#prop:hoefsmit-seminormal-form)).
Let $e_\lambda \in Z(H_n(q))$ be the central primitive idempotent
projecting onto the block $M_{d_\lambda}(R)$.

**Step 1 (idempotents are bar-stable).** The bar involution
preserves the Wedderburn block decomposition because the eigenvalues
$q$ and $-q^{-1}$ of $\sigma_i$ are swapped by $* \colon q \mapsto q^{-1}$,
which permutes the symmetric / antisymmetric eigenchannels but
preserves their joint span. Hence $*(e_\lambda) = e_{\lambda'}$
where $\lambda'$ is the conjugate partition. For self-conjugate
$\lambda = \lambda'$ (e.g. $(2,2)$ for $n = 4$), $e_\lambda$ is
*itself* bar-fixed; for chiral pairs $(\lambda, \lambda')$, the
sum $e_\lambda + e_{\lambda'}$ is bar-fixed.

**Step 2 (annihilators are projection-generated).** Any right
ideal $I \subset H_n(q)$ decomposes as $I = \bigoplus_\lambda I_\lambda$
with $I_\lambda \subset M_{d_\lambda}(R)$. For each $\lambda$, $I_\lambda$
is a right ideal in a matrix algebra $M_{d_\lambda}(R)$, hence
$I_\lambda = p_\lambda M_{d_\lambda}(R)$ for some idempotent
$p_\lambda \in M_{d_\lambda}(R)$ (basic linear algebra). The right
annihilator $\mathrm{Ann}_r(I) = \bigoplus_\lambda (1 - p_\lambda) M_{d_\lambda}(R)$
is generated by the idempotent $\sum_\lambda (1 - p_\lambda)$.

**Step 3 (idempotent self-adjointness).** Each block $M_{d_\lambda}(R)$
inherits a *-structure from $H_n(q)$. Within $M_{d_\lambda}(R)$, every
idempotent is similar (over $R$ algebraically closed) to a
self-adjoint idempotent with respect to a chosen Hermitian form.
The Hoefsmit basis is bar-orthogonal in the Frobenius pairing
[`prop:frobenius-gram-matrix`](#prop:frobenius-gram-matrix), so
the $p_\lambda$ produced in Step 2 can be chosen self-adjoint.

Hence every right annihilator is generated by a self-adjoint
idempotent. Symmetrically for left annihilators. $\square$

### Corollary

The central primitive idempotents $\{e_\lambda\}_{\lambda \vdash n}$
form a **complete system of orthogonal projections** for $H_n(q)$,
indexed by partitions of $n$. They are explicit (see
[`prop:explicit-central-idempotent`](#prop:explicit-central-idempotent))
and bar-aware: self-conjugate $\lambda$ give bar-fixed projections
(real period witnesses); conjugate pairs give bar-swapped projections
(chirality-twisted witnesses).

### Failure locus — explicit characterisation

The Baer-* property fails on the exact locus where $H_n(q)$ ceases
to be semisimple. Concretely:

$$
  H_n(q) \text{ is semisimple}
  \;\;\iff\;\;
  [k]_q \;\neq\; 0 \quad\text{for all } k \in \{2, 3, \ldots, n\},
$$

where $[k]_q := (q^k - q^{-k}) / (q - q^{-1})$ is the symmetric
quantum integer. The failure locus is therefore:

$$
  \boxed{\;
    \mathrm{NonBaer}\bigl(H_n(q)\bigr)
    \;=\;
    \bigl\{\, q \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times \,:\,
      q^{2k} = 1 \text{ for some } k \in \{2, \ldots, n\} \,\bigr\}.
  \;}
$$

Equivalently: $q$ is a primitive $2k$-th root of unity for some
$2 \leq k \leq n$, i.e. $q \in \{\zeta_4, \zeta_6, \zeta_8, \ldots, \zeta_{2n}\}$
(primitive forms only). At each such $q$, the Hoefsmit projector
becomes singular, the Jacobson radical is non-trivial, and the
annihilator structure includes nilpotent elements that are *not*
generated by self-adjoint idempotents.
