**Remark (interpretation).**
The classification of
[prop:s5-equivariant-torus-knots](#prop:s5-equivariant-torus-knots)
has a sharp physical reading.  The Descartes chapter identifies the
three charged lepton generations with torus knots via

$$
  \text{electron} \;=\; T_{2,3},
  \qquad
  \text{muon} \;=\; T_{2,5},
  \qquad
  \text{tau} \;=\; T_{2,7}.
$$

Under pure topological embedding (Seifert genus $\leq 4$) all three
fit on Bring's surface, and the paper adopts this criterion to fill
the lepton generations.  Under the stronger equivariant criterion ---
preserving the knot's intrinsic cyclic symmetry along the embedding ---
the proposition cuts the list to two:

| Particle | Knot | Cyclic symmetry | $\mathbb{Z}_s \hookrightarrow S_5$? | Embedding |
|----------|------|:---------------:|:-----------------------------------:|-----------|
| electron | $T_{2,3}$ | $\mathbb{Z}_3$ | yes (3-cycle) | **symmetry-preserving** |
| muon | $T_{2,5}$ | $\mathbb{Z}_5$ | yes (5-cycle) | **symmetry-preserving** |
| tau | $T_{2,7}$ | $\mathbb{Z}_7$ | **no** (no order-7 element in $S_5$) | symmetry-broken |
| (ghost) | $T_{2,9}$ | $\mathbb{Z}_9$ | **no** (no order-9 element) | symmetry-broken, saturates $g=4$ |
| (ghost) | $T_{2,11}$ | $\mathbb{Z}_{11}$ | **no** | doubly excluded ($g = 5 > 4$) |

**Reading.**  The electron and muon are the only leptons whose
cyclic rotational symmetry is realised by a genuine element of
$\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma_4) = S_5$.  The tau embeds topologically by
virtue of genus fit ($g(T_{2,7}) = 3 \leq 4$) but fails the
equivariance test --- its $\mathbb{Z}_7$ symmetry is not a
subgroup of $S_5$.  Informally, *tau is a lepton in the topological
spectrum but not in the symmetry spectrum.*

**Physical consequence.**  If lepton-generation structure is
generated by the cyclic symmetry of a $\Sigma_4$-embedded torus
knot, the equivariant criterion predicts exactly two generations
of charged leptons, not three.  Experimentally three generations
are observed, matching the paper's topological-embedding criterion.
The equivariant proposition therefore sharpens the three-generation
story: tau carries its mass because the symmetry-broken embedding
still admits a Seifert surface, not because a new $S_5$-compatible
cyclic structure is available.  This is consistent with the
weight-identification picture of
[rem:weight-identification-consequences](#rem:weight-identification-consequences),
which already sees electron and muon as the two leptons whose
representations fit cleanly into the $\mathbb{Z}_5$-graded structure
of the $\mathcal{X}_5$ substrate.

**What it does not claim.**  The proposition does not say tau
"should not exist" --- it exists, and physics confirms it.  It
says tau's existence requires a different mechanism (symmetry-broken
embedding) than electron and muon (symmetry-preserving embedding).
Fourth and higher generations are excluded by the paper's
existing genus saturation at $T_{2,9}$; the equivariant
proposition simply clarifies that three-generation structure is
$2 + 1$, not $3 + 0$, where the $2$ is symmetry-preserving and
the $1$ is symmetry-broken.
