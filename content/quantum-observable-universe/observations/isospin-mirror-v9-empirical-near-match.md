A multi-version probe of the ³H/³He isospin-mirror binding-energy difference
surfaces an empirical near-match. Writing $\mathcal{B}_T$ and $\mathcal{B}_{^3\mathrm{He}}$
for the atomic braids of tritium and helium-3, $y_\lambda$ for the Wenzl
Markov weights at $q_0$, and $\chi^\lambda$ for the atom-side characters, the
*cross-isotope bar-symmetric* combination

$$
S \;=\; \sum_\lambda y_\lambda \cdot
   \frac{\chi^\lambda(\mathcal{B}_T) + \chi^{\lambda^\mathsf{T}}(\mathcal{B}_{^3\mathrm{He}})}{2}
$$

pairs each partition $\lambda$ with its transpose $\lambda^\mathsf{T}$
(chirality) across the $Z\leftrightarrow N$ isospin mirror. Scaled by
$m_\pi\,\hbar_q$, this reproduces the measured difference
$B_T - B_{^3\mathrm{He}} \approx 0.7637\;\mathrm{MeV}$ to about $6\%$.

Multiplying by a further factor $(1+q)/2 \approx 1.0549$ brings the prediction
to $\approx 0.7629\;\mathrm{MeV}$, within $0.11\%$ of experiment.

**The status of each factor is tiered, not uniform.** The character sum $S$
and the substrate scales $m_\pi$ and $\hbar_q$ are built from canonical
infrastructure, and the cross-isotope bar-symmetric pairing is a defensible
structural object (partition transpose realises chirality; the
$Z\leftrightarrow N$ swap realises the isospin mirror). The $(1+q)/2$ factor,
by contrast, is an **empirical correction**: it was selected to close the
residual gap rather than derived from the substrate, and it does not improve
the *absolute* binding energies of D, T, and ³He — applied there it makes the
agreement worse. The near-match is therefore recorded as an exploratory
shape-hint, not as a closure of the mirror difference.

This remark interprets the Option-E isospin-mirror investigation; it makes no
new logical claim. The decision not to promote it to a proposition follows the
$\Delta_\lambda$ gate: the per-partition mirror structure is being probed, but
a fitted scalar correction is not a substrate derivation.
