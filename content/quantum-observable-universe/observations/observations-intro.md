This chapter collects the physical predictions of the Descartes universe and compares them with experimental data. We assume the knot-to-particle mapping and Skein decomposition ([Chapter 9](#chap:brings-surface)), along with the Gröbner--Shirshov reduction engine and Wedderburn mass formula ([Chapter 10](#chap:particle-interactions)). We calibrate the substrate parameter $q_0$ from those constructions. The molecular extension ([Chapter 12](#chap:molecular-construction)) builds on this calibrated $q_0$.

The central result is the substrate consistency window. Multiple independent physical observables (lepton mass ratios, the fine structure constant, proton-to-electron mass ratio, and nuclear binding energies) each determine the substrate parameter $q$. These determinations agree within a narrow interval $q_0 \approx 1.110$. We present particle identification conjectures, CODATA comparisons for fundamental masses, beta decay predictions, and nuclear stability patterns. We propose experimental tests including crystal resonance, proton radius, and Breit--Wheeler pair creation.

Every numerical prediction uses $\approx$ (not $=$) to indicate that values are derived approximations at $q_0$, not exact identities. We tabulate error terms and percentage deviations from experimental data.

**Notation note.** We use plain italic $m_e$, $m_p$, $m_n$ for the CODATA-measured electron, proton, and neutron masses. We treat these as experimental input. They are distinct from the categorical mass $\mathfrak{m}$, which denotes the QOU-derived mass via the topological mass formula $\mathfrak{m}(K) = \mathrm{Vol}(K)/\hbar_q^{2}$. The role of $m_e$ is an external scaling ruler. It fixes the absolute energy scale. All other masses are predicted as ratios $\mathfrak{m}(K)/\mathfrak{m}(4_1) \cdot m_e$. When a comparison table juxtaposes "QOU prediction" against "CODATA value", both are in MeV. The ratio is dimensionless and substrate-derived.
