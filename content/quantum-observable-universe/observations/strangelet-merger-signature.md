Strangelets cannot radiate from a neutron star's surface: the
strangelet core sits at $\rho \gtrsim 2\rho_0$, and strange
quark matter that decompresses below this density converts back
to ordinary hadrons — the Skein polynomial cost is no longer
paid by the Fermi energy.  A neutron star in isolation keeps its
strangelet core locked away.

**Mergers are different.**

When two neutron stars collide, tidal forces decompress the
strangelet cores.  As the density drops below the hyperon
threshold, each strange quark ($T_{2,5}^{\sigma}$,
$|J| = 1.176$) must convert to a down quark
($T_{2,3}^{\sigma}$, $|J| = 0.960$).  The Skein polynomial
cost that was "paid" by compression is now **released** as
conversion energy:

$$
  \delta E_{\mathrm{conv}}
   \approx 
  \frac{|J_{\mathfrak{s}}| - |J_{\mathfrak{d}}|}{|J_{\mathfrak{d}}|}
   \times 
  \frac{V_B}{\hbar_q^2}
   \approx  168  \text{Skein units per strange quark}.
$$

In physical units (scaling by $m_p c^2 \approx 938$ MeV and
$V_B/\hbar_q^2 = 749$):

$$
  \delta E_{\mathrm{conv}}^{\mathrm{phys}}
   \approx 
  \frac{168}{749} \times 938
   \approx  210  \mathrm{MeVperbaryon}.
$$

**The energy budget.**

For a half-solar-mass strangelet core
($N_B \approx 6 \times 10^{56}$ baryons):

| Energy source | Per baryon | Total | Compare to |
|--------------|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Strangelet deconversion | $210$ MeV | $\sim 2 \times 10^{53}$ erg | $200\times$ kilonova |
| CFL gap breaking | $2.5$ MeV | $\sim 2.4 \times 10^{51}$ erg | $2\times$ kilonova |
| Standard kilonova (GW170817) | — | $\sim 10^{51}$ erg | Reference |

The strangelet deconversion energy is **enormous** — two
hundred times the observed kilonova luminosity of GW170817.
Not all of this radiates electromagnetically: most goes into
the kinetic energy and thermal energy of the expanding ejecta,
and a large fraction escapes as neutrinos.  But the sheer
scale means that even a small radiative fraction produces
observable consequences.

**Three signatures.**

1. **Post-merger thermal excess.**  The deconversion energy
   heats the merger remnant beyond what gravitational energy
   and $r$-process radioactivity can explain.  The excess
   thermal energy, radiated over the kilonova timescale
   ($\sim$ days), should produce a **brighter, longer-lasting
   kilonova** than models without a strangelet core predict.
   The luminosity excess scales as the strangelet core mass.

2. **Neutrino burst from CFL gap breaking.**  The CFL
   superconductor ([color superconductivity](#conj:color-superconductivity-borromean))
   stores gap energy
   $\Delta_{\mathrm{CFL}} \approx 2.5$ MeV per baryon.
   When the merger disrupts the superconducting condensate
   (Hopf diquarks break apart), this energy is released as a
   **prompt neutrino burst** — distinct from the thermal
   neutrinos of the merger itself.  The burst energy
   $\sim 2.4 \times 10^{51}$ erg arrives on the
   dynamical timescale ($\sim$ milliseconds), before the
   kilonova.  This is detectable by IceCube, Super-Kamiokande,
   or Hyper-Kamiokande for mergers within $\sim 50$ Mpc.

3. **Anomalous cosmic ray nuclei.**  If any strangelet
   fragments survive decompression (lumps large enough to be
   self-gravitating, $A \gtrsim 10^{40}$), they would
   propagate as cosmic rays with anomalous charge-to-mass
   ratio $Z/A \approx 0$ (electromagnetically neutral per
   baryon number).  No conventional nucleus has $Z/A = 0$ for
   $A > 1$.  Searches for anomalous massive particles in
   cosmic ray detectors (AMS-02, DAMPE) constrain the flux
   of such objects.

**Absence of surface emission.**

An isolated neutron star cannot emit strangelets because:

- The strangelet core is at $\rho > 2\rho_0$, confined by
  the weight of the hadronic mantle.
- Strange matter at the surface ($\rho \sim \rho_0$) would
  immediately convert: the Fermi energy is too low to sustain
  the Jones polynomial cost.
- The Borromean structure reforms during decompression — the
  strange quarks re-pair as ordinary hadrons, not as free
  strangelets.

This is different from the Bodmer–Witten hypothesis, where
strange matter is the absolute ground state at **zero**
pressure, and isolated strangelets would be stable.  In QOU,
strange matter requires high density.  Strangelets are the
cores of neutron stars, not freely propagating objects.

**The merger as a strangelet laboratory.**

A neutron star merger is the only astrophysical event that
**exposes** a strangelet core to decompression.  The
deconversion energy ($\sim 200\times$ kilonova) is released
on a specific timescale set by the hydrodynamic expansion,
with specific spectral signatures set by the $s \to d$
conversion rate (which depends on the Jones polynomial ratio
$|J_{\mathfrak{s}}|/|J_{\mathfrak{d}}| = 1.225$ — a
parameter-free prediction).  Future kilonova observations
with JWST, Rubin Observatory, and Einstein Telescope can
test this against standard $r$-process models.
