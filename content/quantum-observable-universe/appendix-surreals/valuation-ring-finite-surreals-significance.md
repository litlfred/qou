## Significance of this framing

Reading the [standard-part functor](#def:standard-part-functor)
as the valuation-ring quotient
$\mathcal{O} \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m} \cong \mathbb{R}$
([valuation-ring projection](#rem:valuation-ring-finite-surreals))
has a foundational payoff. The appendix's functorial constructions
(point-evaluation of schemes, categorical enrichments,
surreal-coefficient sheaves) all need a **set-sized** coefficient
ring to avoid proper-class pathologies. The valuation ring
$\mathcal{O}$ is set-sized; the ambient $\mathbf{No}$ is a proper
class. By restricting to $\mathcal{O}$ before any point-evaluation,
we stay inside standard set-theoretic foundations.

The infinite surreals $\mathbf{No} \setminus \mathcal{O}$ are **not
in the domain of $\mathrm{st}$**. Any arithmetic involving
$\omega, \omega^2, \omega^\omega$ must be restricted to
$\mathcal{O}$ before projecting. This is not a loss: it is
exactly the archimedean-observability boundary already implicit
in the main paper's treatment of physical observables.
