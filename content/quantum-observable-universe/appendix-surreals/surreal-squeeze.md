**Lemma (conditional on
[`conj:q-aubin-lions`](#conj:q-aubin-lions),
[`conj:ns-counterexample`](#conj:ns-counterexample),
[`conj:critical-exponent`](#conj:critical-exponent),
[`prop:blowup-yekutieli`](#prop:blowup-yekutieli),
and
[`conj:regularity-breakdown`](#conj:regularity-breakdown);
"Surreal squeeze").** *Let $\varepsilon \in \mathrm{Der}(A)$ be the
Reeb infinitesimal
([Reeb infinitesimal](#def:reeb-epsilon))
and let $\delta > 0$ be a standard real number.  Then, inside the
ordered field $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No}$,*

$$
\delta^2 \cdot \varepsilon^{-1}
 > 
\varepsilon^3.
$$

**Proof.** Multiply both sides by the strictly positive element
$\varepsilon$ to obtain the equivalent inequality

$$
\delta^2 > \varepsilon^4.
$$

Since $\varepsilon$ is a positive infinitesimal
([Reeb infinitesimal](#def:reeb-epsilon), positivity and archimedean
smallness), every positive integer multiple $n \cdot \varepsilon^4$ is
still bounded above by any standard real.  In particular
$\varepsilon^4 < \delta^2$.  $\square$
