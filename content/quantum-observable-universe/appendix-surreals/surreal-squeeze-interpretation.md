The surreal squeeze inequality has a uniform reading as a cost-versus-budget comparison underlying the millennium-problem arguments.

## Interpretation of the lemma

The "cost" of a standard-scale deviation $\delta$ from the classical
configuration — whether a zero off the critical line, a singular spike
in the velocity gradient, or a non-trivial Collatz orbit — is
$\delta^2 \cdot \Omega_q \asymp \delta^2 \cdot \varepsilon^{-1}$.
The "budget" available from the mass-gap dissipation is
$\Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3$.  The squeeze states that the cost
**strictly exceeds** the budget.  No passage to the limit is needed:
the inequality is already exact in the ordered field.
