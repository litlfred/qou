The previous section read kinematic data off a single crossing. We now diagonalise the cyclic symmetry that ties crossings together. The relevant cyclic group is $C_5 \subset S_5$ acting on the five branch points of the base surface, and the diagonalisation is the discrete Fourier transform — the Lagrange resolvents. Once $C_5$ is diagonal, the categorical analogues of momentum, angular momentum and energy fall out as the natural eigencoordinates.

**Discrete Fourier transform diagonalises $C_5$ on the base surface** (prop) is the structural backbone: it identifies the Lagrange resolvents $w_0, \dots, w_4$ as the simultaneous eigenvectors of the $C_5$-action on the coordinate ring of $\mathcal{X}_5$, and the accompanying remark **Lagrange resolvents and spectral form** (rem) collects the explicit weight assignments. With the spectral coordinates in place we define **$q$-Momentum from the braid writhe** (def) as the eigenvalue of the writhe operator on the first resolvent, then **$q$-Angular momentum from the $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ Casimir** (def), which extracts the Casimir of the quantum group along the same eigenline, and **$q$-Deformed Energy** (def), which combines the two. The remark **Jones polynomial connection, building block values, and nuclear $q$-angular momentum** (rem) reads the resulting $q$-angular momentum off the building-block knots and lines it up with the Jones polynomial evaluation. The section closes with **Noether's theorem in the $q$-deformed setting** (rem), which packages the conservation laws and anomaly terms for the resolvent symmetries in the form the rest of the chapter consumes.
