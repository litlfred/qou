The previous section extracted a global energy from the $R$-matrix for a closed strand. We now localise that energy to individual crossings. The Hecke relation uniquely forces the sign and weight of each crossing. This yields a per-crossing decomposition that cleanly splits into a strong baseline and an electromagnetic correction.

We assign the weights $(q, -q^{-1})$ to positive and negative crossings, tracing them directly to the eigenvalues of the Hecke generator $T = P \circ R$. A worked example on the trefoil knot illustrates this crossing energy and the resulting binding. In the commutative limit, $i = \sqrt{\lambda_-}$ emerges as the natural complex structure on the antisymmetric eigenspace.

The structural pivot is the Coulomb charge of a knot composite. By extracting the antisymmetric half $Q(\mathfrak{H}) = \tfrac{1}{2}(n_+ - n_-)$ of the crossing count, we obtain the exact decomposition $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}} = \text{strong baseline} + Q \cdot (q - q^{-1})$. Two propositions then confirm that the asymmetric piece transforms correctly under the bar involution $q \mapsto q^{-1}$. The next section takes this Coulomb-charge decomposition as the base for the discrete Fourier transform on the cyclic subgroup $C_5$ to read off kinematic observables.
