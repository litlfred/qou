The preceding construction of the universal $R$-matrix for $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ fixes the braiding on $\mathbf{Rep}(\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2)))$. This braiding yields a numerical invariant of a closed strand: an energy that grows when the strand folds back on itself. We define this energy categorically and confirm it reproduces the classical Möbius energy as $q \to 1$.

The energy of a knot embedding $\varrho \colon S^1 \to \tau(A)$ emerges as a Frobenius double integral built from the Hopf comultiplication and the $q$-Hodge star. In the classical limit, the Frobenius pairing degenerates onto the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Killing form, producing the repulsive $1/r^2$ kernel. At $q \neq 1$, the non-cocommutativity of the Hopf structure makes the energy diverge when two non-adjacent strands approach within the fibre $\tau(A)$. The substrate-side picture replaces $1/r^2$ with a hard kernel, cutting off the divergence at the Planck scale $\hbar_q$. This self-avoidance kernel subsequently drives an energy contribution localised at each crossing.
