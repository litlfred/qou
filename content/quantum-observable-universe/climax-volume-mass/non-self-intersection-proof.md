The universal $R$-matrix $\mathcal{R} \in \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2)) \hat{\otimes} \mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ ([Remark](#rem:r-matrix-braiding)) controls the failure of the cocommutativity square:

$$
  \sigma \circ \Delta  \neq  \Delta,
$$

where $\sigma$ is the tensor swap. At $q = 1$, we have $\mathcal{R} = 1 \otimes 1$ and the comultiplication is cocommutative. Strands may pass through each other freely, since self-intersection carries no energy penalty in the classical regime.

For $q \neq 1$, the classical $1/r^2$ repulsion kernel is replaced by $[2]_q / (\lvert \varrho(s) - \varrho(t) \rvert^2 - \hbar_q^2)$ ([Lemma](#lem:nsi-q-deformed-kernel)). This replacement creates a hard singularity at $\lvert \varrho(s) - \varrho(t) \rvert = \hbar_q$, erecting an energy barrier of infinite height at the quantum scale. $\square$
