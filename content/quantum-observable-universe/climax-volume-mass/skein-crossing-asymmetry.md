The $R$-matrix braiding ([R-matrix](#rem:r-matrix-braiding)) introduces a fundamental asymmetry between positive and negative crossings: the Skein relation

$$
  q  L_+ -  q^{-1} L_-
  =  (q^{1/2} - q^{-1/2}) L_0
$$

weights positive crossings by $q$ and negative crossings by $q^{-1}$. Since $q > 1$, positive crossings contribute more to the Skein polynomial than negative ones. This asymmetry is the topological origin of electromagnetic repulsion.

**Emergent Coulomb repulsion.**
For a hadron $\mathfrak{H}$ with Coulomb charge $Q(\mathfrak{H})$ ([Coulomb charge](#def:coulomb-charge)), the crossing energy ([crossing energy](#def:crossing-energy)) decomposes into a symmetric (strong) baseline and an antisymmetric (Coulomb) correction. The per-crossing repulsive correction between composites of the same charge relative to a mixed-charge baseline is

$$
  \delta_{\mathrm{Coulomb}}
  =  q -  \tfrac{1}{2}(q + q^{-1})
  =  \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})
  > 0.
$$

**Unification of strong and electromagnetic interactions.**
Electromagnetic repulsion is not a separate force in the QOU framework. It is a phase asymmetry in the same Skein polynomial that encodes the strong binding. Both interactions arise from the $R$-matrix braiding of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$, with $q$ controlling the relative strength:

- The strong binding is proportional to $\tfrac{1}{2}(q + q^{-1})$ (the baseline Skein weight).
- The electromagnetic repulsion is proportional to $\tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})$ (the crossing asymmetry).

Their ratio is

$$
  \frac{\text{Coulomb}}{\text{Strong}}
  =  \frac{q - q^{-1}}{q + q^{-1}}
  =  \tanh\!\left(\ln q\right).
$$

At $q = 1$ (classical limit), the asymmetry vanishes and the electromagnetic interaction disappears. At $q \to \infty$ (deep-quantum regime), the ratio approaches $1$ and the two interactions become comparable.

The specific assignment of Coulomb charges to protons and neutrons, and the consequences for nuclear stability, require the Descartes universe ($\chi = -6$) and are developed in Chapter 6.
