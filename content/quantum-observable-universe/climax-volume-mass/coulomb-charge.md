Let $\mathfrak{H}$ be a knot composite with braid word $\beta$. Let $n_+(\beta)$ and $n_-(\beta)$ denote the number of positive and negative crossings in $\beta$. The **Coulomb charge** of $\mathfrak{H}$ is

$$
  Q(\mathfrak{H}) =  \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(n_+(\beta) - n_-(\beta)\bigr).
$$

The crossing energy ([crossing energy](#def:crossing-energy)) decomposes as

$$
  \mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}}(\mathfrak{H})
  = 
  \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}(n_+ + n_-)(q + q^{-1})}_{\text{baseline (strong)}}
  + 
  \underbrace{Q(\mathfrak{H}) \cdot (q - q^{-1})}_{\text{Coulomb correction}}.
$$

The symmetric first term ($q \leftrightarrow q^{-1}$) gives the strong binding. The antisymmetric second term vanishes at $q = 1$. The Coulomb charge $Q(\mathfrak{H})$ dictates the magnitude and sign of this electromagnetic correction:

- $Q(\mathfrak{H}) > 0$: net positive crossings yield a repulsive Coulomb contribution.
- $Q(\mathfrak{H}) = 0$: the composite is electromagnetically neutral.
- $Q(\mathfrak{H}) < 0$: net negative crossings yield an attractive Coulomb contribution.

At $q = q_0$, the ratio of Coulomb to strong contributions is $\tanh(\ln q) \approx 0.104$.
