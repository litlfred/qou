Continuing the [brane tower for SU(2)](#ex:recursive-brane-tower-su2),
we describe the [brane boundary](#def:brane-boundary) at level $0$.

**The singular set.** For $G = \mathrm{SU}(2)$ acting on
$\mathbb{CP}^1$ via the Hopf fibration, the singular set $S$ consists
of the fixed points of the $\mathrm{U}(1)$-action, namely the north and
south poles $\{0, \infty\} \subset \mathbb{CP}^1$. These are the
vortex loci where the fibre degenerates.

**Categorical boundary.** The brane boundary $\partial A_0$ is the
kernel of the restriction $r_0|_S \colon A_0 \to A_0|_S$:

$$
  \partial A_0
  =  \ker\bigl(\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))
  \xrightarrow{r_0|_S} \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{U}(1)_N)
  \oplus \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{U}(1)_S)\bigr),
$$

where $\mathrm{U}(1)_N$ and $\mathrm{U}(1)_S$ are the stabiliser
fibres over the two poles. In Peter--Weyl terms, $\partial A_0$ is the
ideal of functions $f \in \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ that vanish on
both stabiliser circles.

**Archimedean picture.** In the archimedean setting, $\partial \mathcal{S}_0$ is
the boundary of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-frame bundle restricted to the
vortex locus. The frame bundle $F(\mathrm{SU}(2)) \to \mathrm{SU}(2)$
restricts over $S$ to two copies of $\mathrm{U}(1) \times \mathbb{R}^3$,
so $\partial \mathcal{S}_0 \cong \mathrm{U}(1) \times S^2 \sqcup \mathrm{U}(1) \times S^2$,
a disjoint union of two solid tori boundaries.

**Dimension.** Since $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is $3$-dimensional and $S$ is
$1$-dimensional (two circles), $\partial A_0$ has codimension $2$ in
$A_0$, consistent with the vortex being a codimension-$2$ defect.
