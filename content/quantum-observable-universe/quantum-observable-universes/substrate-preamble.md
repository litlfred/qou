Having established the physical observations in the preceding chapter ---
mass spectra, coupling constants, and the spectral predictions that
connect the substrate parameter $q$ to measurable quantities --- we now
turn to the algebraic foundations that underpin these results. The goal
of this chapter is to exhibit, in full categorical detail, the algebraic
object whose internal geometry generates the physics.

The quantum vacuum is formalized as a self-dual Frobenius--Hopf algebra
$A$ living in a rigid :refterm[monoidal category]{#monoidal-category} $\mathbf{C}$. This object
carries three layers of structure. The Frobenius form
$\lambda \colon A \to \mathbf{1}$ and the Hopf comultiplication
$\Delta \colon A \to A \otimes A$ endow $A$ with both an inner product
and a coalgebra structure, related by the Frobenius compatibility
condition. A categorical Reeb derivation
$\xi_q \colon A \to A$ encodes the observer drift --- the intrinsic
time evolution generated by the contact structure on the state bundle
$\mathcal{S}$. The spectral geometry of Bring's curve
$\mathcal{B}$ provides the coordinate fibers: the $2g = 8$ independent
cycles of this genus-$4$ curve determine the fundamental modes of the
Reeb spectrum.

Self-duality is the conceptual keystone. The rigid dualizing
complex $\omega_A^\bullet$ \cite{yekutieli2010} provides a canonical duality theory for the
non-commutative algebra $A$. The Frobenius form induces a
quasi-isomorphism $A \xrightarrow{\sim} \omega_A^\bullet$, establishing
that the observer trace $\Phi = \lambda \circ \mu$ and the observed
state space are intrinsically paired. This self-duality ensures that
the substrate does not require an external arena: it is simultaneously
the algebra of observables and the space on which those observables act.
