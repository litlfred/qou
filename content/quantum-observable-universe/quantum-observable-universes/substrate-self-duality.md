The Frobenius form $\lambda \colon A \to \mathbf{1}$ of the substrate
algebra $A$ induces a quasi-isomorphism between $A$, viewed as a
complex concentrated in degree zero, and its rigid dualizing complex
$\omega_A^\bullet$:

$$
A \xrightarrow{ \sim } \omega_A^\bullet
\quad\text{in } D(A).
$$

More precisely, the non-degenerate Frobenius pairing
$\langle a, b \rangle_F = \lambda(\mu(a \otimes b))$ defines an
$A$-bimodule map $\varphi \colon A \to \omega_A^\bullet$ whose cone is
acyclic, establishing a quasi-isomorphism in the derived category of
$A$-bimodules.

**Corollaries.** The quasi-isomorphism $A \simeq \omega_A^\bullet$
implies:

1. **Serre-type duality**: $\mathrm{Ext}^i_A(M, A) \cong H^{d-i}(M)^\vee$
   for finitely generated $A$-modules $M$, where $d$ is the homological
   dimension.

2. **Canonical trace**: the composition $\varepsilon \circ \mu$ factors
   through the Frobenius form, providing a categorical trace on
   $\mathrm{End}(A)$.

3. **Uniqueness**: since $\omega_A^\bullet$ is rigid, the
   quasi-isomorphism is unique up to unique isomorphism --- there is no
   ambiguity in the self-duality.
