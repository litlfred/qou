We face a topological obstruction: the contact structure prevents
the horizontal distribution $H = \ker\theta$ from being integrable.
Precisely, $d\theta|_{H \times H}$ is non-degenerate, so by the
Frobenius theorem the largest integral submanifolds of $H$ have
dimension at most $n$ (half the rank of $H$). These are the
*Legendrian submanifolds*. A [$q$-torsion leaf](#ex:q-torsion-leaf)
must therefore be Legendrian and invariant under the $q$-deformed
Reeb flow — a stringent geometric constraint.

Existence is not automatic: it depends on the dynamics of $\xi_q$.
In the classical limit $q \to 1$, invariant Legendrian submanifolds
exist whenever the Reeb flow is periodic or integrable (e.g. on
$S^3/\Gamma$). For $q > 1$, the deformation generically breaks
periodicity, and we must appeal to KAM-type persistence results.

In the category of Lie algebroids over $M$, the Frobenius integrability
condition is equivalent to $H$ carrying a Frobenius structure
([quantum universe](#def:quantum-universe)).  Since the Levi form
$d\theta|_{H \times H}$ is non-degenerate, this Frobenius structure
*fails* --- and it is precisely this failure that forces the
dimension bound on integral submanifolds and gives $q$-torsion leaves
their rigidity.
