The quantum ideal gas law of the previous section yields a partition function whose dominant saddle point sits inside one Hecke channel of $H_n(q)$; phase transitions are the loci where that dominance changes. **Phase transitions as $S^3$ boundary actions** (prop) computes the critical temperatures from the restriction of the categorical action to the Hopf base $S^2$ of $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3_q \to S^2$, with $T_q^{(g \to l)}$ and $T_q^{(l \to s)}$ controlled by $[2]_q$ and $[n_K]_q$. **Three phases from Hecke channels and the triple point** (rem) reads gas, liquid, and solid as the symmetric, mixed, and antisymmetric Hecke channels and locates the triple point. **Sublimation from shell-filling representation theory** (prop) then derives the direct solid--gas transition from a non-split extension classified by $\mathrm{Ext}^1_{H_n(q)}$, and **Observational test of the $Z$-parity sublimation rule** (rem) gives the metallic-bonding test that distinguishes it from continuous condensation.
