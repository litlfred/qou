The golden ratio $\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ appears twice in the
Descartes elliptic curve $\mathcal{E}_q$
([golden ratio degeneration](#rem:golden-ratio-degeneration)): once as
a **metric property** (the conformal modulus of the elliptic fiber at
our $q = q_0$) and once as an **algebraic property** (the root of
the degeneration polynomial $s^2 + s - 1 = 0$ at $q_{\mathrm{gold}} = 1/\varphi^2$).  Parallel transport along the $q$-connection
([categorical $q$-connection](#def:categorical-q-connection))
reveals these as the same $\varphi$, seen from opposite ends of a
geodesic in the substrate parameter space.

**Setup.**
The elliptic fiber $\mathcal{E}_q$ at substrate parameter $q$ is a complex
torus $\mathbb{C}/\Lambda(q)$ with period lattice $\Lambda(q)$
generated by $1$ and $\tau(q) \in \mathbb{H}$ (the upper half-plane).
The modular parameter $\tau(q)$ is determined by the $j$-invariant
$j(q) = j(\tau)$.  At $q = q_0$: $j = 129.9$, the real period
$\omega \approx 7.5565$
([computation data](#prop:descartes-elliptic-curve)).

Consider a parallelogram $\Pi(q)$ in the period lattice of $\mathcal{E}_q$
whose diagonal-to-side ratio is $\varphi$.  This is a fundamental
domain of the torus — its shape encodes the conformal structure of
the chiral fiber at that value of $q$.

The left figure is the parallelogram $\Pi(q_0)$ with golden-ratio
diagonal; the right is its image under parallel transport to
$q_{\mathrm{gold}} = 1/\varphi^2$, where one period diverges and
the torus pinches to a nodal rational curve.  The same $\varphi$
appears as a length ratio on the left and as the root of
$s^2 + s - 1 = 0$ controlling the degeneration on the right.

**Transport equations.**
The $q$-connection
$\nabla_q = q^{-1}\nabla + (1 - q^{-1})(\iota_\xi\nabla) \varTheta_A$
has coefficients that depend continuously on $q$.  As $q$ decreases
from $q_0$ to $q_{\mathrm{gold}} = 1/\varphi^2 \approx 0.382$,
the connection weight shifts:

| $q$ | $q^{-1}$ (spatial weight) | $1 - q^{-1}$ (Reeb weight) |
|-----|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| $q_0$ | $0.9011$ | $+0.0989$ |
| $1.000$ | $1.000$ | $0$ (classical) |
| $1/\varphi^2 \approx 0.382$ | $\varphi^2 \approx 2.618$ | $-\varphi \approx -1.618$ |

Below $q = 1$ the Reeb correction **flips sign**: the connection
overshoots the classical value and the Reeb term actively
**unwinds** the internal spin rather than adding to it.

**Degeneration of the parallelogram.**
As $q \to q_{\mathrm{gold}}$, the $j$-invariant diverges:
$j(q) \to \infty$, which forces $\mathrm{Im}(\tau) \to \infty$.
The period lattice stretches: one generator stays finite while the
other diverges.  The parallelogram $\Pi(q)$ is stretched without
bound in one direction:

$$
  \Pi(q) \xrightarrow{q \to q_{\mathrm{gold}}}
   \text{infinite strip}
  \xrightarrow{ \text{pinch}}
   \text{node}.
$$

The golden-ratio diagonal, which at $q = q_0$ encoded a finite
conformal relationship between the two periods, is destroyed as the
torus degenerates into a nodal rational curve.

**The identity: metric $\varphi$ becomes algebraic $\varphi$.**
The golden ratio that was a length ratio in $\Pi(q_0)$ becomes the
root of the polynomial $(s^2 + s - 1)$ that kills the Weierstrass
coefficient $r(q)$.  Explicitly: the factor $s^2 + s - 1$ is the
**minimal polynomial of $1/\varphi$ over $\mathbb{Q}$**, and it
appears in the numerator of $r(q)$ because the Bring's surface is
defined by $\sum x_i = \sum x_i^2 = \sum x_i^3 = 0$ in
$\mathbb{CP}^4$, whose $S_5$ symmetry acts through fifth roots of
unity $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/5}$ satisfying
$\varphi = 2\cos(\pi/5)$.

The shape information of the parallelogram (the ratio $\varphi$ in
the lattice) and the degeneration condition (the root $1/\varphi$ of
$s^2 + s - 1$) are **the same algebraic number** seen at different
points of the $q$-parameter space.  Parallel transport makes this
identity manifest: carrying $\Pi$ from $q = q_0$ to
$q = q_{\mathrm{gold}}$ transforms the geometric content ($\varphi$
as a length ratio) into the algebraic content ($\varphi$ as the
instability threshold).

**Reciprocal asymmetry.**
The inversion $q \to 1/q$ sends $z = \sqrt{q} - 1/\sqrt{q}$ to
$-z$.  At $q_{\mathrm{gold}}$: $z = -1$ and the chiral cubic
factors as $X(X^2+1) = 0$ (degeneration).  At
$q_{\mathrm{gold}}^{-1} = \varphi^2$: $z = +1$ and the cubic
$X^3 + 2X^2 + 3X + 4 = 0$ is irreducible — the fiber is smooth
with $\Delta = -200$, $j = 160$.  The Bring's surface breaks the
$q \leftrightarrow 1/q$ symmetry that quantum groups preserve:
$U_q(\mathfrak{g}) \cong U_{q^{-1}}(\mathfrak{g})$, but
$\mathcal{E}_q \not\cong E_{1/q}$.

**Information-theoretic reading.**
The parallelogram $\Pi(q)$ is a fundamental domain — it tiles the
torus and encodes exactly one period's worth of information about
the chiral fiber.  Transporting it to $q_{\mathrm{gold}}$ reveals
that the **information capacity** of the fiber (the number of
distinguishable states on the torus) diverges along one axis and
collapses along the other.  At the node, the torus has been replaced
by $\mathbb{P}^1$ with a double point: the information that was
distributed around a closed loop is now concentrated at a
singularity.  The golden ratio marks the threshold where the
information geometry of the chiral fiber undergoes a **phase
transition** from distributed (torus) to localised (node).
