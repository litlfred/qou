The preceding sections fixed the Lieb--Robinson velocity $c_q = 1/(q-1)$ and the operational entropy $\eta_q$ of the substrate. We now specialise to the simplest carrier of information: a **one-dimensional channel** — physically, an optical fibre. Throughout, "fibre" means a *physical* one-dimensional waveguide that transmits information, **not** the categorical fibre functor $\tau$; the collision is only nominal.

A 1D QOU channel is a **bounded braid carrier**. The quantum observable universe descends to one dimension as a line along which $n$ strands — the modes of the fibre — propagate while carrying a braid word $\beta \in B_n$. The information placed on the fibre is exactly this braid; what is transmitted and measured is its Markov trace $\mathrm{tr}_M(\beta)$, the same invariant that reads out particle content elsewhere in the framework. The **cladding** is a topological boundary condition: it confines $\beta$ to the strand bundle, so a strand cannot cross the surface of the fibre.

That surface boundary is what makes parallel fibres **non-interacting**. Two cladded fibres laid side by side form a *split* configuration — the boundary suppresses every crossing between a strand of one fibre and a strand of the other — so the joint Hecke algebra factorises as $H_n(q) \otimes H_m(q)$ and the joint Markov trace multiplies, $\mathrm{tr}_M(\beta_A \sqcup \beta_B) = \mathrm{tr}_M(\beta_A)\,\mathrm{tr}_M(\beta_B)$. This is the same structural mechanism as the Borromean composite factorisation — a product over decoupled constituents. The information capacity of a single fibre is then the entropy of the reachable trace image, bounded by the substrate's operational entropy $\eta_q$.

The blocks that follow develop these in turn — the channel object, the non-interaction of parallel fibres, the Markov-trace capacity, and the $q$-deformed dispersion of a propagating mode — and connect the linear, high-effective-$q$ regime of a fibre to the framework's $q$-regime map. _(draft; remaining blocks in progress.)_
