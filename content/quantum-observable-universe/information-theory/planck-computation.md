The number of Reeb operations per Planck unit, $n_R(q) = q/(q-1)$ ([Reeb operations per Planck unit](#prop:reeb-operations-per-planck)), determines the **computational capacity** of a Planck-length region of the QOU at substrate parameter $q$.

Each operation evaluates $f_q(z) = z^2 + c(q)$, a degree-$2$ polynomial. It simultaneously resolves one crossing in the braid representation of the local link structure. The total computational work per Planck unit therefore equals $n_R(q)$ polynomial evaluations. Each evaluation requires constant algebraic complexity.

At $q = q_0$, a Planck-length region performs roughly $10$ degree-$2$ polynomial evaluations per Planck time. This defines the microscopic computational rate of the universe at our scale. It represents the clock speed of the exceptional divisor dynamics, which coarse-grains into the smooth Reeb flow governing observable physics.
