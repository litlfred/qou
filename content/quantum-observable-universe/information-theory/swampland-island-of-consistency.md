Bring's surface enters the framework as a rigid selection. Its
categorical Euler characteristic is fixed,
$$
  \chi(A) = -6
$$
([Euler characteristic](#lem:brings-euler-characteristic)), and Bring's
surface ([Bring's surface](#def:brings-surface)) is the genus-$4$ curve
that realises it. This rigidity is the QOU counterpart of the Swampland
"island of consistency".

**The Swampland statement.**
Within the landscape of effective field theories, only a sparse subset
admits a quantum-gravity completion \cite{vafa2005}; consistency
criteria such as the Weak Gravity Conjecture \cite{arkani-hamed2007}
carve out the admissible region, leaving isolated islands rather than a
continuum.

**QOU translation.**
Demanding an observable volume fixes the categorical Euler
characteristic to $\chi(A) = -6$, and the genus-$4$ hyperbolic
structure that realises this value is rigid: by Mostow rigidity a
finite-volume hyperbolic structure of dimension at least three admits
no continuous deformation, so the topological data — and the masses
read from it — are discrete. The framework occupies an isolated point
rather than a continuous family of vacua.

**Scope.**
The value $\chi(A) = -6$ is derived and Lean-checked, and Bring's
surface as the terminal genus-$4$ object is a categorical result.
The stronger reading — that this geometric rigidity *selects* the
substrate parameter $q_0$ — is motivational and is not a derivation:
$q_0$ is fixed by calibration against the muon-to-electron mass ratio,
not by Mostow rigidity, and the corpus contains no theorem forcing
$q_0$ from $\chi(A) = -6$. Mostow rigidity makes the hyperbolic
geometry discrete; it does not, on its own, determine the substrate
modulus.
