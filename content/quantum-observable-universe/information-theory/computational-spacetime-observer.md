# Computational Spacetime and the Observer

**Time relative to the observer.**
In the QOU formalism, time is not an absolute background parameter, but is strictly relative to the observer. Specifically, the "time" experienced by an observer is precisely the **computational time** required to evaluate the state space leading to or following from that exact point. This discrete sequence of Planck operations acts as the fundamental metric of temporal distance.

**Bring's surface and the emergence of Spacetime.**
This intrinsic relationship between time and distance is spelled out smoothly in the first two coordinates of Bring's surface. Bring's surface natively possesses $2$ complex dimensions. The action of the natural involution $\tau$ upon this surface effectively generates the $4$-dimensional physical spacetime:
$$ \text{2 dim (Complex)} + \text{involution } \tau \implies \text{4 dim (Spacetime)} $$
The first two coordinates of Bring's surface map smoothly into the computational spacetime metric.

**Relative distance as computational cost.**
Because spacetime distance from the observer is derived directly from the coordinates of Bring's surface under involution, physical distance is fundamentally **relative computational time**. The physical separation between two events is simply the number of discrete lattice steps (dependent on the local substrate parameter $q$) that must be computed to connect their state spaces. 

Crucially, while this spacetime computational distance is relative to the observer, it must remain consistent across observers. The algebraic consistency of the state space ensures that the computational distance agrees across different observers, recovering macroscopic symmetries like Lorentz covariance as an emergent property of information-theoretic agreement.
