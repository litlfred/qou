*Interpretation of the [information speed of light](#def:information-speed-of-light).*

The derived, $q$-dependent light speed $c_q = 1/(q-1)$ — together with the $O(1 - q^{-1})$ group-velocity corrections that the $q$-integer phase $[k]_q$ induces on the modes of a bounded channel ([photon modes](#rem:photon-propagation-1d)) — places the framework in the lineage of **doubly special relativity** (DSR) and Planck-scale phenomenology. That literature supplies both a structural precedent for the construction and the sharpest experimental constraints it must clear; this remark records both directions.

**Structural kinship with DSR.** Amelino-Camelia's testable minimum-length scenario \cite{amelinocamelia2001} promotes a second observer-independent scale — a Planckian length — alongside the speed of light, deforming the photon dispersion relation at first order in the ratio of the photon's energy to a quantum-gravity scale $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{QG}}$, while the deformed symmetry is realised by a quantum-group ($\kappa$-Poincaré) structure rather than the classical Poincaré algebra. The parallel to the substrate is direct: the QOU is likewise a quantum-group deformation, with the deformation parameter $q$ in the role DSR assigns to its Planckian invariant, and with macroscopic Lorentz covariance recovered only as an emergent property of the archimedean projection. In both frameworks the photon velocity is energy-dependent at the deformed level — in DSR through the modified dispersion relation, here through $[k]_q$ replacing the classical wavenumber $k$. The DSR precedent settles one conceptual point in the framework's favour: an observer-independent deformation scale is *compatible with* a relativity principle and does not force a preferred frame, which is the correct target for how $q_0$ should enter the archimedean layer — as a second invariant, not as a frame-dependent violation.

**Experimental constraints.** The same programme supplies the bounds. Time-of-flight tests on gamma-ray bursts were proposed as a Planck-scale probe in \cite{amelinocamelia1998}, and the Fermi observation of GRB 090510 \cite{abdo2009} excludes photon dispersion linear in the energy ratio up to a quantum-gravity scale exceeding the Planck energy; the surrounding observational programme — in-vacuo dispersion, Lorentz-invariance-violation versus DSR-deformation scenarios, and their respective bounds — is surveyed in \cite{amelinocamelia2013}. Read against these bounds, the substrate values are prima facie in severe tension: a fractional dispersion of order $\hbar_q = 1 - q^{-1}$ surviving to astrophysical photon propagation would exceed the GRB limits by many orders of magnitude. Consistency therefore requires that the $q$-deformed dispersion be confined to the substrate (fibre) level, with the archimedean projection suppressing its residue at accessible energies. The framework does not currently derive that suppression scale; this is the sharpest falsifiability exposure of the dispersive prediction, and the GRB bounds convert it into a quantitative requirement — any archimedean residue of the $[k]_q$ phase must fall below the GRB 090510 limit \cite{abdo2009} — that an eventual substrate-to-archimedean dispersion calculation must satisfy.
