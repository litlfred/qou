At $\mathcal{T} = 0$, no Reeb operation has occurred. The initial state (see [bundle initial conditions](#def:bundle-initial-conditions)) is a formal sum $\mathcal{S}_0 = \sum_i (p_i, H_i)$. This specifies fiber data without horizontal transport. No information has propagated. Position is known exactly; momentum is entirely undetermined.

At $\mathcal{T} = 1$, the first iteration of $f_q$ connects neighboring fibers for the first time. The $q$-connection $\nabla_q$ acts once, producing the first non-trivial holonomy. Over $\mathbb{F}_\pp$, this reduces to a single polynomial evaluation: $z_1 = z_0^2 + c(q)$. The Heisenberg commutator $[\hat{X}, \hat{P}]_q = J\hbar_q$ is near-maximal. The first tick of time represents the moment of greatest uncertainty.

Because $t_P \to 1$ at the Big Bang, the information-theoretic time $\mathcal{T}$ and the Reeb time coordinate $t$ coincide: $t = \hbar_q \mathcal{T} \approx \mathcal{T}$. There is no coarse-graining or statistical averaging. We resolve each braiding on the exceptional divisor individually. The link-length resolution grows by exactly one crossing per tick. The particle spectrum unfolds step by step through the jet prolongation tower (see [Planck steps as jet orders](#rem:jet-planck-steps)).
