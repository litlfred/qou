**Remark (Relation to Peña et al. and 't Hooft).**
Peña et al. construct a reversible quantum Game of Life using Toffoli-based adder circuits. Their Toffoli gate is the classical limit $q \to 1$ of the $q$-Toffoli, and their construction is recovered in that limit; away from it the two are not comparable, since the $q$-deformed generator is invertible but not unitary. Their unitary tick maps to $3$ Planck steps. We identify the $q$-deformed amplitude $\Phi(q)$ as the physical AND amplitude, with the stability region $q > 1$ and the three operator zeros $q_\star = 1/\varphi^2$ (where $P_{7,7}$ vanishes), $q_\Phi \approx 0.4656$ and $q = 1$ delimiting the sub-physical behaviour.

The relation to 't Hooft's proposal — that quantum mechanics emerges from a deterministic Planck-scale cellular automaton — is more limited, and the limitation is structural rather than a matter of detail. The mechanism 't Hooft relies on is that a deterministic evolution *permutes* a distinguished basis of ontological states, so that the operator it induces on the span of that basis is automatically unitary and generates a Schrödinger flow. That step requires unitarity of the tick, and by the statement's item 4 the $q$-Toffoli is unitary only at $q = 1$.

Consequently the identification holds at $q = 1$ and is not established away from it. At $q = 1$ the tick is a permutation of basis states, the ontological states may be read as Reeb bundle fibres $\mathcal{S}_p$ and the tick as the Planck step $t_P(q)$ — but $q = 1$ is also where the $q$-Toffoli degenerates to the ordinary Toffoli gate, so what the classical limit realizes is the construction of Peña et al. rather than anything the $q$-deformation contributes. At $q \neq 1$ the tick remains invertible, and the automaton remains deterministic and reversible in the sense a cellular automaton requires, but the induced operator is not unitary and 't Hooft's embedding does not apply as stated. Whether a deterministic automaton with an invertible non-unitary tick can support the emergence 't Hooft argues for is not addressed here.

The reading of the Markov trace as a categorical coarse-graining replacing 't Hooft's information loss is likewise not established. It is a suggestion the degeneracy of the trace form makes worth examining — a degenerate form has a radical, and quotienting by a radical is a loss of information rather than an *ad hoc* postulate of one — but no such identification is derived in this paper.
