*Interpretation of the [one-dimensional QOU channel](#def:one-dim-qou-channel).*

The information a single fibre transmits is carried by its braid word $\beta$, and what the receiver reads out is the Markov trace $\mathrm{tr}_M(\beta)$. The **capacity** of the channel is therefore the entropy of the *reachable trace image* — the number of distinguishable values $\mathrm{tr}_M(\beta)$ can take as $\beta$ ranges over the admissible braids on the fibre's $n$ strands. Two braids with the same trace are indistinguishable to the receiver, so capacity counts trace-classes, not braids.

Each elementary crossing contributes at most the operational entropy $\eta_q = (2q-1)/q^3$ of information, so the capacity of an $n$-strand fibre carrying a length-$\ell$ braid is bounded by $\ell\,\eta_q$ $q$-bits — the one-dimensional specialisation of the Bekenstein and Bremermann limits already fixed in this chapter. The non-interaction of parallel fibres then makes total capacity **additive**: because the joint Markov trace of $k$ cladded fibres multiplies, their log-capacities add, and a bundle of $k$ independent fibres carries $k$ times the single-fibre rate — the categorical form of the classical statement that independent channels add their capacities.
