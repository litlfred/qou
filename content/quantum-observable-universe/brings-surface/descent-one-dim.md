By the :refterm[descent]{#descent} theorem ([jet prolongation](#prop\:jet-prolongation)), a
two-complex-dimensional Descartes universe $(\mathbf{C}, \varTheta, G, \mathcal{S})$ with $M = \mathbb{C}^2$
descends to a one-complex-dimensional variety $M_0$.  The stability of the
Euler characteristic under :refterm[descent]{#descent} and lifting
([Euler characteristic stability](#prop\:euler-char-stability)) ensures that the descended
universe retains $\chi(\mathcal{S}) = -6$.  We may therefore study the Descartes
universe in the setting of one-complex-dimensional varieties.
The :refterm[descent]{#descent} may continue further to dimension zero
([:refterm[descent]{#descent} reduction to zero](#rem\:descent-reduction-zero)), where the entire quantum
observable universe is captured by fibre data at a single point.
