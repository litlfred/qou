Choose a well-ordered basis $\{x_i \mid i \in I\}$ for $\mathfrak{g}$.
Then $T\mathfrak{g}$ has a multiplicative monomial basis consisting of
the words of finite length in the $x_i$'s with the graded
lexicographic ordering. The symmetric algebra $S\mathfrak{g}$ has a
basis of words written in non-decreasing order.

The ideal $J$ is generated by

$$
  G := \{g_{i,j} := x_i x_j - x_j x_i - [x_i, x_j]_{\mathfrak{g}}
    \mid x_i > x_j\}.
$$

The argument that $G$ is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis is exactly as in the
classical setting, using
[Buchberger's theorem](#thm:buchberger). As the leading terms
$\mathrm{LT}(g_{i,j}) = x_i x_j$ are monic and the basis is
multiplicative, $G$ is a *unital* Gröbner--Shirshov basis. The corollary then
follows from the
[main decomposition theorem](#thm:unital-groebner-decomposition).
