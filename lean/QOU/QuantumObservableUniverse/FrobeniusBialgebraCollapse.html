    What the Frobenius relation forces on a substrate carrying an antipode #

    QOU.FrobeniusHopfData carries the Frobenius relation μ ≫ Δ = (Δ ▷ A) ≫ α ≫ (A ◁ μ), so its Δ is the Frobenius comultiplication — a map of A-bimodules, hence determined by Δ(1). Since 2026-09-14 the structure also carries the Hopf antipode axioms (bean qou-c64q). This file works out what those two facts force together. The answer is stronger than the question that prompted the file, so it is stated first.

    The antipode axiom alone forces ε(1) · x = ε(x) · 1 #

    scalar_id_eq_counit_unit — no hypothesis beyond the structure's own fields:

    ε ≫ η  =  (λ_A)⁻¹ ≫ ((η ≫ ε) ▷ A) ≫ (λ_A).hom

    In elements, ε(1)·x = ε(x)·1 for every x. The mechanism is two steps: the Frobenius relation forces the convolution S * id to be left multiplication by the single element (S * id)(1) — convolution_eq_left_mul, which uses no antipode axiom at all and is a statement about the convolution whatever S is — and antipode_left then says that same convolution is ε(-)·1. Evaluating at 1 identifies the element as ε(1)·1.

    Consequently:

    • counit_comp_unit_of_unital : if ε(1) = 1 then ε ≫ η = 𝟙_A;
    • isoUnitOfUnital : and then A ≅ 𝟙_C;
    • counit_not_unital_of_nontrivial : contrapositive — a non-trivial substrate has a non-unital counit.

    ε(1) = 1 is Mathlib's BimonObj.one_counit, and is what every bialgebra and every Hopf algebra assumes without comment. So the axiom added on 2026-09-14 does not sit quietly beside the Frobenius relation: the two together leave the substrate one standard normalisation away from being the tensor unit.

    Scope, and it is load-bearing. ε(1) = 1 is not a field of FrobeniusHopfData, and nothing here supplies it. So this is not a proof that the current axioms are inconsistent, nor that A = 𝟙_C. What is proved is the identity ε(1)·x = ε(x)·1 and its contrapositive. Whether the corpus wants a substrate whose counit is non-unital is a question for the owner, not one this file decides; it is recorded in bean qou-aawz.

    The weaker result that prompted the file: the bimonoid axiom is excluded #

    The block's authorNotes recorded the bialgebra (bimonoid) compatibility as merely absent, and left adding it as an open option. It is not one. One of the four bimonoid axioms on its own — Δ ∘ η = η ⊗ η, Mathlib's CategoryTheory.BimonObj.one_comul — collapses the substrate with no appeal to the antipode:

    • comul_eq_of_one_comul : Δ = (λ_A)⁻¹ ≫ (η ▷ A), i.e. Δ(x) = 1 ⊗ x;
    • counit_comp_unit : ε ≫ η = 𝟙_A, so 𝟙_A factors through the tensor unit;
    • isoUnit : with η ≫ ε = 𝟙 (the bimonoid axiom one_counit) this is an isomorphism A ≅ 𝟙_C;
    • not_one_comul_of_nontrivial : contrapositive.

    This half is logically independent of the first: it spends the counit law and the bimonoid unit axiom, where the first spends the antipode axiom and associativity. It is presented second here, but proved first in the file below, because the antipode half reuses its comul_eq_frobenius.

    The two halves together: both unit-side bimonoid axioms are excluded #

    Mathlib's BimonObj has four axioms. Two of them mention only the unit and the counit, and are statable over a bare monoidal category:

    axiom in elements excluded by
    one_comul : η ≫ Δ = (λ_𝟙)⁻¹ ≫ (η ⊗ η) Δ(1) = 1 ⊗ 1 not_one_comul_of_nontrivial
    one_counit : η ≫ ε = 𝟙_𝟙 ε(1) = 1 counit_not_unital_of_nontrivial

    A non-trivial substrate satisfies neither. The second is the sharper statement, because ε(1) = 1 is a normalisation rather than a structural compatibility — it is assumed without comment of every coalgebra with a unit in sight, and it is excluded here by the antipode axiom the definition now carries. The other two bimonoid axioms (mul_comul, mul_counit) need a braiding to state and are not addressed.

    Why none of this is a defect in the definition #

    It is the expected state of affairs, and it is why the name "Frobenius–Hopf" needs reading with care. A finite-dimensional Hopf algebra is Frobenius — but its Frobenius comultiplication is not its Hopf comultiplication. Forcing the two to coincide is what collapses. The substrate takes Δ to be the Frobenius one (that is what frobenius_relation says), so it is a Frobenius monoid with an antipode, not a Hopf monoid in Mathlib's sense, and results about CategoryTheory.HopfObj — antipode_antipode among them — do not transfer to it. That was already recorded in the block's authorNotes; what is new here is that it cannot be fixed by adding the axiom.

    Nothing here argues against a Hopf structure on A borne by a different comultiplication — that is a separate object the corpus does not have.

    Scope #

    Both halves are structural exclusions in the sense of AGENTS.md §"Before declaring refuted": the invariant cited is a proved implication below, not a numerical probe. Each excludes an axiom for a Δ satisfying the Frobenius relation, and says nothing about a Δ that does not.

      🛑 The stronger collapse: the ANTIPODE axiom alone already does it #

      Everything above needs the bimonoid unit axiom as a hypothesis. The results in this section need no extra hypothesis at all beyond the structure's own fields — and they reach nearly the same conclusion.

      antipode_left says S is a left convolution inverse of id_A. Combined with the Frobenius relation, which makes Δ a right A-module map and hence determined by Δ(1), the convolution (S * id) is forced to be left multiplication by the fixed element T = (S * id)(1). The antipode axiom then says that left multiplication equals ε(-)·1, and evaluating at 1 gives T = ε(1)·1. The two together read

      ε(1) · x = ε(x) · 1 for every x,

      which is scalar_id_eq_counit_unit below. As soon as ε(1) = 1 — the standard unitality of a counit, and Mathlib's BimonObj.one_counit — this says x = ε(x)·1: the object is the tensor unit.
