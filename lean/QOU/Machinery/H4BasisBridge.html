    QOU.Machinery.H4BasisBridge — the two H₄ normal-form bases are one basis #

    The tree carries two 24-word normal-form bases for H₄(q), and each had exactly what the other lacked:

    module has lacked
    ASCII MarkovTraceH4 + MarkovTraceH4Gram0..7 + H4AsciiPresentation trM, gram_symm, trM_cyclic associativity, Ring
    subscript HeckeH4StabilizationRung + HeckeH4AlgebraLaws the presentation, mulNF₄_assoc, H4A Ring/Algebra, wedderburn₄ cyclicity

    They are the same basis relabelled. Mapping each reduced word to its S₄ element, both lists give the same 24 elements; the index permutation is piIdx below; the trace weights agree under it; and only 6 of 24 words differ at all, every difference a single far-commutation swap σ₀σ₂ ↔ σ₂σ₀ — which far_commute_σ₀σ₂ proves, so the basis elements are equal, not merely the group elements they index.

    phi is the relabelling. It intertwines all three generator actions (phi_mulσ0/1/2, depth 1 each), hence every word action (phi_wordActionA), hence the products (phi_mulNF); and it carries trM to trM4 (trM4_phi). So the missing half transports: trM4_cyclic — cyclicity on the subscript basis, with no second 576-entry Gram closure.

    The move that makes phi_mulNF go through #

    Both products are 24-term sums over word actions, but the terms correspond under piIdx, not position by position. Every attempt that unfolds the two sums and matches term against term therefore has to permute 24 terms by rewriting — which simp will not do (three failures, postmortem at the foot of this file). Reindexing does it in one step: put both sides in Finset.sum form (mulNF_expand_right, mulNF₄_expand_right), carry each term across with phi_wordActionA, and reindex along piEquiv. The 24 terms are never written out.

    Bean qou-x64l. Once the consumers move across, the ASCII chain — ~6.5k generated lines and the tree's build pole, ~4.3 GB peak — can be retired; trM4_cyclic is what they need to move to.

    Generated: the permutation and the word correspondence are computed from MarkovTraceH4.mulNF and HeckeH4AlgebraLaws.wordAction themselves, so they cannot drift from the tables they describe.

              Cyclicity transports — now in the other direction #

              This arrow used to run ASCII → subscript, deriving trM4_cyclic from the ASCII trM_cyclic, which rested on gram_symm's 576 computed entries. That is why the Gram chain stayed alive: it was the only proof of cyclicity at n = 4.

              QOU.Machinery.H4.trM4_cyclic (H4Cyclicity) now proves cyclicity directly on the subscript basis, from the three generators and the subalgebra argument, with no Gram closure anywhere. So the transport runs subscript → ASCII, and the 576 entries are dead weight — MarkovTraceH4Gram0..7 and MarkovTraceH4Gram (6604 generated lines, the tree's build pole) are deleted in the same commit.

              Nothing downstream loses a theorem: H4.trM4_cyclic replaces this file's own former trM4_cyclic (one canonical statement rather than two proofs of it), and H4.trM_cyclic below is the ASCII statement, re-proved as a corollary.

              Postmortem: three simp routes that do not work #

              Kept because the failures are instructive about why the structured route is the right one, not merely that it is.

              1. phi in the same simp only as ← phi_word_*. phi unfolds first and destroys the rewrite patterns.
              2. Two passes: simp only [mulNF₄, ← phi_word_*], then unfold. The unused-simp-args linter reports all 24 phi_word_* as unused — they never fire. Diagnosis: simp rewrites innermost-first, so ← phi_mulσ* (which are phi_word_1/2/3 in disguise) collapse each composite one generator at a time, yielding the subscript word shape; for the 6 words where the bases differ that is not the ASCII word, so the longer lemmas never match.
              3. Adding mulσ_farcomm02 to normalise those 6. Still fails, and worse: the right-hand side comes out with 70 terms instead of 24, carrying coefficients indexed by invIdx j rather than by literals — simp is mangling the coefficient vector, not merely failing to rewrite.

              All three unfold mulNF/mulNF₄ into their 24 inline terms and then try to match term against term. But the two tables' terms are not in correspondence position-by-position — they correspond under piIdx — so any tactic that rewrites in place has to permute 24 terms by rewriting, which simp will not do and ring cannot see through the generator actions. Reindexing a Finset.sum does it in one step. Bean qou-x64l.
