    The rank matrix of a permutation, and the easy half of Ehresmann's criterion #

    For u a permutation of Fin n, its rank matrix is

    rk u i m = #{x : x < i and u x < m},

    the number of the first i positions carrying a value below m. Ehresmann's tableau criterion (Björner–Brenti, Combinatorics of Coxeter Groups, Thm 2.1.5) says that the Bruhat order on S_n is exactly the pointwise comparison of rank matrices:

    u ≤ w   ⟺   rk w i m ≤ rk u i m   for every i and m.

    This file proves the easy implication, left to right — a subword of a reduced word has a pointwise-larger rank matrix — directly from CoxeterBruhat.BruhatLe's subword definition. The converse (rank criterion ⟹ subword) is not here; it is the one Mathlib-scale piece of the route below.

    Why this file exists #

    Mathas' Proposition 3.7 (Dipper–James) says dominance on row-standard λ-tableaux is the Bruhat order on their d-images. BraidKnot.DominanceIsBruhat.domRel_iff_rankAt proves that the dominance side of that is itself a rank comparison, read only at the reference tableau's row cuts. So 3.7 has no tableau content: both sides compare the rank matrices of the same two permutations, and the whole difference is at the row cuts versus at every index.

    The converse half of 3.7 — d(s) ≤ d(t) → s ⊵ t — is then rk_le_of_bruhatLe here, followed by specialising the inequality to the row cuts and applying domRel_iff_rankAt. No reflection order appears in that chain, so it does not wait on BruhatLe → ReflLe (bean qou-3k66), which the corpus previously recorded as its blocker.

    The two rank steps, and why they are the whole argument #

    Right multiplication by a simple transposition permutes positions, so it can only move the rank matrix at the single cut between the two positions it swaps (rk_mul_swap_of_ne), where it exchanges the two values (rk_mul_swap_cut). The induction on a reduced word from the right then needs exactly two facts, and both were measured over every permutation of S_3…S_5 before being proved:

    • A (rk_mul_swap_le) — if w ascends across the cut then w · s_j has a pointwise-smaller rank matrix.
    • B (rk_mul_swap_mono) — a rank domination survives right multiplication by s_j, provided the dominated permutation ascends across the cut. Nothing is assumed of the other one, which is the part a hand argument gets wrong: the naive case analysis stalls in one sub-case and is rescued by the hypothesis at the cut j + 2, not at j or j + 1.

    QOU.Hecke.Inversions.length_mul_swap_eq_succ_iff supplies the ascent from the Coxeter length, so the two steps are restated as rk_mul_simple_le / rk_mul_simple_mono in the form the induction consumes.

      Interpolation: from the endpoints of a window to its interior #

      Mathas 3.7 compares rank matrices only at the reference tableau's ROW CUTS, while Ehresmann's criterion wants every index. The two are reconciled by the fact that dOf t is increasing across each row block exactly when t is row standard -- so inside a block the values below m come first, and the rank matrix there is pinned by its two endpoints.

      Everything here is stated at the Coxeter level, over an arbitrary window [a, b); the tableau side supplies the windows and the increasing hypothesis.

        The DESCENT direction — what the hard half of Ehresmann's induction needs #

        rk_mul_swap_le / rk_mul_swap_mono above are stated for w ASCENDING across the cut, which is what the easy half consumes. The hard half inducts on a right DESCENT of w, and needs the two facts below. Both were measured before being written (n <= 6, 141939/141939 for the descent-restricted form) and then measured again UNRESTRICTED once the proofs came in without using the w hypothesis at all (n <= 5: 6204/6204 and 8920/8920).

        Only u carries a hypothesis; w is arbitrary. That is not an oversight in the statement -- omega closes both without the w side, and the wider measurement confirms it. It is the same asymmetry as rk_le_of_endpoints further down: in this rank calculus the DOMINATING side (u, the larger) is the one that must be constrained, and the dominated side is free.

        Both proofs turn on the hypothesis at cut j + 2, never at j or j + 1 -- the same place rk_mul_swap_mono's step B is rescued from.
