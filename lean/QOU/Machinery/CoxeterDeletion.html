    The deletion condition, and reduced subwords #

    QOU.Machinery.CoxeterReflectionCocycle proved the exchange property — strong_exchange, for any word and any right inversion — sorry-free at every rank. This file takes the next classical step, which nothing in the corpus had:

    • exists_deletion (the deletion condition, Björner–Brenti Thm 1.4.4): a word that is not reduced has two letters whose deletion leaves the product unchanged.
    • exists_reduced_sublist: hence every word contains a reduced subword with the same product — not merely some reduced word, which is Mathlib's exists_reduced_word', but one sitting inside the given word.

    Why the second one is the interesting half #

    Mathlib already gives a reduced word for any w. The subword strengthening is what the Bruhat order needs: QOU.Machinery.CoxeterBruhat defines u ≤ w by "some reduced word for w has a subword multiplying to u" and records, deliberately, that transitivity, antisymmetry, the subword property and the lifting property are not proved there because "each needs the exchange condition". exists_reduced_sublist is the first of those inputs delivered in the form they are consumed in.

    What this is a step toward, and what it is not #

    The chain to Matsumoto's theorem — any two reduced expressions for the same w are related by braid moves — runs exchange → deletion → Matsumoto. The first was already here; this file supplies the second.

    ✅ The third landed the same day (2026-09-06): QOU.IwahoriHecke.Matsumoto, via CoxeterRankTwo and CoxeterDihedralDescent. It is hypothesis-free for every Coxeter system as of 2026-09-07, CoxeterRankTwo.DihedralExact having been proved in CoxeterDihedralOrder. The paragraph below saying "nothing here closes Matsumoto" is still an accurate statement about this file.

    The conditional that consumes it is QOU.IwahoriHecke.TwMatsumoto.exists_Tw_of_matsumoto. Not BraidWordInvariance.exists_Tw_of_braidEquiv, which quantifies over all words and whose hypothesis is therefore false on a Coxeter group — refuted in the same tranche by TwMatsumoto.not_braidEquiv_hypothesis, since braid moves preserve word length while π [i,i] = π [].

    Nothing here closes Matsumoto, and nothing here should be read as closing it.

    The proof, and why it is short #

    Induction along the word from the right (List.reverseRecOn). Either the prefix is already non-reduced, in which case the inductive deletion survives appending a letter; or the prefix is reduced and the new letter must therefore be a right descent of its product — at which point strong_exchange deletes one letter of the prefix, and the appended letter is the second deletion. No case analysis on the Coxeter matrix, no rank restriction.

    -- Ref: [bjornerbrenti2005] Combinatorics of Coxeter Groups, Thm 1.4.4 (deletion) -- Ref: [bourbaki1968] Lie IV §1 -- Ref: [manuscript] bean qou-06h7

    theorem QOU.Machinery.CoxeterDeletion.exists_deletion {B : Type u_1} {W : Type u_2} [Group W] {M : CoxeterMatrix B} (cs : CoxeterSystem M W) (ω : List B) :
    ¬cs.IsReduced ω → ∃ (a : ℕ) (b : ℕ), a < b ∧ b < ω.length ∧ cs.wordProd ω = cs.wordProd ((ω.eraseIdx b).eraseIdx a)

    The deletion condition. A word that is not reduced has two positions a < b whose deletion leaves the product unchanged.

    This is the classical companion of the exchange property, and it follows from the strong form proved in CoxeterReflectionCocycle with one induction along the word.

    theorem QOU.Machinery.CoxeterDeletion.exists_reduced_sublist {B : Type u_1} {W : Type u_2} [Group W] {M : CoxeterMatrix B} (cs : CoxeterSystem M W) (n : ℕ) (ω : List B) :
    ω.length = n → ∃ (ω' : List B), ω'.Sublist ω ∧ cs.IsReduced ω' ∧ cs.wordProd ω' = cs.wordProd ω

    Every word contains a reduced subword with the same product.

    Stronger than Mathlib's exists_reduced_word', which produces a reduced word for the element; here the reduced word is a List.Sublist of the word one started with. That is the form the Bruhat order's subword criterion consumes.
