    Chord diagrams #

    A chord diagram of degree n is a way to pair up 2n marked points on an oriented circle by n chords. Modelled here as a fixed-point-free involution pairing : Perm (Fin (2 * n)): pairing i is the point joined to i by a chord, pairing (pairing i) = i (involution) and pairing i ≠ i (no chord joins a point to itself).

    Chord diagrams are the target of the symbol map of a finite-type (Vassiliev) invariant, and the space they span — modulo the one- and four-term relations (FourTermRelation.lean) — carries the weight systems (WeightSystem.lean). This file builds only the combinatorial object and its symmetric-group action; no invariant theory lives here.

    Main definitions #

    • ChordDiagram n — a fixed-point-free involution on Fin (2 * n).
    • ChordDiagram.degree — the number of chords, = n by construction.
    • ChordDiagram.conj — the Perm (Fin (2*n))-action by conjugation (relabelling the marked points); the surgeries in the 1T/4T relators are expressed through it.
    • ChordDiagram.oneChord — the unique degree-1 diagram (single chord).

    Type

    A chord diagram of degree n: a fixed-point-free involution on the 2 * n marked points Fin (2 * n). pairing i is the endpoint joined to i; involutivity makes the chords well-defined and fixed-point-freeness forbids a chord from both ending at the same point.

    • pairing : Equiv.Perm (Fin (2 * n))

      The pairing permutation: sends each marked point to its chord partner.

    • involutive : Function.Involutive ⇑self.pairing

      The pairing is an involution: applying it twice returns the point.

    • fixedPointFree (i : Fin (2 * n)) : self.pairing i ≠ i

      No point is its own partner (every chord has two distinct endpoints).

          Symmetric-group action by relabelling #

          Relabelling the 2 n marked points by g : Perm (Fin (2*n)) carries a chord diagram to another chord diagram: the new pairing is the conjugate g ∘ pairing ∘ g⁻¹. Conjugation preserves both the involution and fixed-point-free properties, so this is well-defined. Chord surgeries used in the relators (FourTermRelation.lean) are instances of this action.
