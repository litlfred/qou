    The Euler identity for a regular map #

    source
    theorem QOU.Brings.regularMap_edge_relation {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (p q V E F g : R) (hface : p * F = 2 * E) (hvert : q * V = 2 * E) (heuler : V - E + F = 2 - 2 * g) :
    E * (2 * p + 2 * q - p * q) = p * q * (2 - 2 * g)

    Euler identity for a regular map. For a map on a closed orientable surface of genus g whose faces are all p-gons and whose vertices all have valency q, the incidence counts p * F = 2 * E (each face has p edges, each edge borders two faces) and q * V = 2 * E (each vertex has q edge-ends, each edge has two ends) combine with V - E + F = 2 - 2 * g to give

    E * (2 * p + 2 * q - p * q) = p * q * (2 - 2 * g).

    Stated over an arbitrary commutative ring: the content is the elimination of V and F, which needs no ordering and no division. (§7c -- do not specialise to Real or even Int here; the Bring instance below does that.)

    Bring's surface: the {5,4} map at genus 4 #

    source
    theorem QOU.Brings.bring_VEF (V E F : ℤ) (hface : 4 * F = 2 * E) (hvert : 5 * V = 2 * E) (heuler : V - E + F = 2 - 2 * 4) :
    V = 24 ∧ E = 60 ∧ F = 30

    Bring's map has 24 vertices, 60 edges and 30 quadrilateral faces.

    Kattan-Singerman Theorem 2.1: the unique regular map of type {5,4} of genus 4, with automorphism group S_5. Their convention (stated explicitly in the paper) is that {5,4} means faces are quadrilaterals and vertices have valency 5 -- so p = 4, q = 5.

    Given the two incidence relations and Euler's formula at g = 4, the counts are forced. Note the hypotheses are exactly those of regularMap_edge_relation; nothing about hyperbolic geometry enters.

    theorem QOU.Brings.bring_aut_orientation_preserving :
    2 * 60 = 120 ∧ 4 * 60 = 240 ∧ 120 ≠ 240

    The paper's 120 automorphisms is 2E, not 4E.

    For a regular map the orientation-preserving automorphism group acts simply transitively on the 2E directed edges, so |Aut^+| = 2E. The flag count is 4E, which counts the full group including reflections.

    At E = 60 these are 120 and 240; Kattan-Singerman's |S_5| = 120 is the orientation-preserving count. Both are recorded so the distinction cannot be re-lost -- an earlier draft of the companion probe asserted 4E = 120, which is false, and the probe's own check caught it.

    The cost of manufacturing a triangulation #

    source
    theorem QOU.Brings.diagonalRefinements_card (F : ℕ) :
    Fintype.card (Fin F → Fin 2) = 2 ^ F

    A diagonal-refinement of a quadrangulation is a choice of one of the two diagonals in each face, i.e. a function from the faces to a two-element set; there are 2 ^ F of them.

    This is the step that prices the transfer. The layering theorem wants a triangulated surface; a quadrangulation can be turned into one, but only by making an independent binary choice per face, and this counts those choices.

    source
    theorem QOU.Brings.bring_refinement_count :
    Fintype.card (Fin 30 → Fin 2) = 1073741824

    Bring's map admits 2 ^ 30 = 1073741824 diagonal refinements.

    With F = 30 quadrilateral faces, manufacturing a triangulation to feed the layering machinery costs ~10^9 free binary choices. Selecting among them to reproduce a target is selection against the target -- fitting under the NO-FITTING directive, with no continuous parameter tuned.

    theorem QOU.Brings.universal_map_face_sizes_differ :
    3 ≠ 4

    The face sizes differ, which is the whole obstruction.

    H_3's universal map is triangular (p = 3); H_4's is 4-gonal (p = 4). A diagonal flip -- the move dual to one ideal tetrahedron -- is defined for the former and not the latter. Trivial as arithmetic; recorded because it is the load-bearing step, and because stating it makes the two p values explicit rather than leaving them in prose.
