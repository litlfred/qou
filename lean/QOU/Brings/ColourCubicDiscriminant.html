          16 * (w ^ 3 - 3 * w) + 24 * (w ^ 2 - 2) + 35 * w + 50 = reducedCubic w

          Step 1 — the palindromic reduction. Writing the sextic in terms of the symmetric combinations of z and 1/z collapses it to reducedCubic. This is the identity that makes the reduction legitimate.

          theorem QOU.Brings.colourSextic_eq_cube_mul_reduced {R : Type u_1} [Field R] [CharZero R] (z : R) (hz : z ≠ 0) :
          colourSextic z = z ^ 3 * reducedCubic (z + z⁻¹)

          The reduction is exact on the sextic itself. For z ≠ 0, the sextic equals z³ times the reduced cubic evaluated at w = z + z⁻¹. This is the statement that the roots of the sextic are exactly the z whose z + 1/z solves the cubic — the content of the palindromic reduction, not merely a formal rearrangement.

          theorem QOU.Brings.normSq_eq_one_of_abs_le_two (w : ℝ) (hw : |w| ≤ 2) :
          Complex.normSq { re := w / 2, im := √(4 - w ^ 2) / 2 } = 1

          Why a conjugate pair sits exactly on the unit circle.

          For a real w with |w| ≤ 2, the two solutions of z + 1/z = w are (w ± i√(4−w²))/2, and each has modulus exactly 1. The unit-circle pair of the colour sextic is therefore forced — it is not a numerical coincidence — because the trinomial's real root gives w = (5/4)u − 1/2 with |w| < 2.

          Proved via Complex.normSq, so the conclusion is an exact equality of reals.
