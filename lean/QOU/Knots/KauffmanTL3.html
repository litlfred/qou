    QOU.Knots.KauffmanTL3 — a computable link invariant for 3-braid closures #

    The crossing-minimality half of the Brunnian confinement selection (prop:confinement-operator-selection) needs to recognise when a 3-braid closure is a nontrivial link — and B₃ is non-abelian, so (unlike the 2-sublink triviality, decided by the B₂ ≅ ℤ exponent sum in confinement_operator_selection.lean) this cannot be read off a linking number. This module builds the smallest sufficient machinery: the Temperley–Lieb algebra TL₃ (only 5-dimensional, Catalan 3 = 5) and the Kauffman bracket of a 3-braid closure, as an integer Laurent polynomial in A. The bracket is a computable, decidable link invariant.

    borromean_link_nontrivial then proves by decide (axiom-free) that the Borromean braid (σ₀σ₁⁻¹)³ closes to a link whose bracket differs from the 3-component unlink's — i.e. the Borromean rings are a nontrivial link. Both have writhe 0, so the unnormalised bracket already separates them.

    This is the first brick of the link-invariant library that will close crossing-minimality (enumerate pure 3-braids of length ≤ 6, compute each bracket, confirm the minimal nontrivial Brunnian closure is the Borromean — the "enumerate in Python, assert in Lean" plan; the full enumeration will use native_decide for speed, documented as such). See docs/workplans/2026-06-17-lean-link-invariant-library.md.

                                                              theorem QOU.Knots.TL.borromean_link_nontrivial :
                                                              bracket borromean ≠ bracket unlink3

                                                              The Borromean rings are a nontrivial link (machine-proved, axiom-free): the Kauffman bracket of (σ₀σ₁⁻¹)³'s closure differs from the 3-unlink's. Both closures have writhe 0, so the unnormalised bracket separates them. This is the link-nontriviality invariant the crossing-minimality proof needs (where the B₂ ≅ ℤ exponent-sum test of sublink triviality does not reach, B₃ being non-abelian).

                                                              Brick 2 — crossing-minimality by enumerate-then-assert (native_decide) #

                                                              The last residual of the Brunnian confinement selection: the Borromean is the crossing-minimal nontrivial Brunnian 3-braid. Realising the author's "enumerate in Python, assert in Lean" plan (B₃ is small), we enumerate every pure 3-braid word of length < 6 over {σ₁^±, σ₂^±} and confirm none is simultaneously Brunnian and nontrivial, while the Borromean (σ₁σ₂⁻¹)³ (length 6) is — so 6 is the minimal word-length (= braid crossing number).

                                                              Two decidable invariants combine:

                                                              • Brunnian — every 2-sublink (delete one strand) is the unlink. As in confinement_operator_selection.lean, the surviving 2-braid lives in B₂ ≅ ℤ, trivial iff its exponent sum (b2class) is 0 — necessary and sufficient for the unlink, no general unknotting needed.
                                                              • Nontrivial — the writhe-normalised Kauffman bracket (the Jones invariant (-A³)^(-w)·⟨·⟩, a genuine ambient-isotopy invariant, δ³ on any trivial 3-link) differs from the 3-unlink's δ³. Writhe-normalisation is essential here: the unnormalised bracket (brick 1) shifts by (-A³)^±1 under R1, so a trivial braid of nonzero writhe would spuriously read "nontrivial" — only the Borromean (writhe 0) was safe unnormalised. Jones-nontrivial ⇒ genuinely nontrivial; the converse can fail, which only makes this lower bound safer (it under-counts nontrivial links, never over- counts, so nontrivialBrunnian w = false is never a false negative).

                                                              native_decide is required (not kernel decide): ~1365 words × a Laurent- polynomial bracket each is far beyond kernel reduction (brick 1's single bracket took ~45 s). native_decide compiles the decision procedure, adding the axioms Lean.ofReduceBool + Lean.trustCompiler — trusting the Lean compiler, the same trust class as the Python enumeration oracle (b3_brunnian_minimality_enumeration.py); the two verdicts agree (minimal = 6), which is the audit.
