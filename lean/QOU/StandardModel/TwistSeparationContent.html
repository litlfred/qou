    What the twist "independence" theorem actually says #

    Bean qou-7dzg (V6, follow-up). Companion to ChiralityGradingConstruction.lean.

    The claim under test #

    appendix-standard-model/strand-twist-datum.lean carries

    theorem exists_same_writhe_ne_totalTwist (n : ℕ) (hn : 0 < n) :
    ∃ t₁ t₂ : TwistDatum n, totalTwist t₁ ≠ totalTwist t₂

    whose docstring reads "over any fixed word (hence fixed writhe) there exist framings with distinct total twist", and which conj:sm-hypercharge-invariant leans on: the separation it advertises is what "qualifies the datum as a candidate" for hypercharge.

    But no braid word occurs in that type. Every mention of braid, writhe, or word in that file is in a comment; its imports are Perm.Basic, Fintype.Perm, Tactic — no braid group, no knot theory, no category theory. So the statement proved is "two ℤ-valued functions can have different sums", and whether it supports a claim about braids is exactly what was never checked.

    This file runs that check, by stating the claim with the braid word in the type — the step the original skips.

    Result: the claim is true, and it is braid-free #

    separation_at_fixed_word is the docstring's sentence, formalised: at a fixed word, two framings of that word have different total twist. It is proved for an arbitrary Word type and an arbitrary writhe : Word → ℤ. Nothing about braids, crossings, or the writhe is used or available — the hypotheses are not there to be used.

    separation_holds_with_one_word makes that concrete: instantiate Word := Unit, where there is exactly one word and the writhe is constant. The separation still holds. A statement that survives collapsing the braid group to a point is not evidence about braid groups.

    The reason is structural, not incidental: in this model the framing is Fin n → ℤ, an arbitrary function, so it is by construction unconstrained by whatever the word does. Independence is an artifact of the encoding, not a discovered property of ribbon framings.

    The other horn #

    factoring_twist_cannot_separate: if instead the twist is determined by the crossing content — as a genuine ribbon-category twist is, being canonical rather than freely assignable — then it takes equal values on writhe-degenerate states and separates nothing. This is the argument of chirality_not_a_function_of_crossing (V9) applied to the twist.

    Together the two horns are a dilemma for the hypercharge candidacy, and the corpus needs one of them: either the twist is free — and its separating power is put in by hand, proving nothing about the ribbon structure — or it is canonical, and it cannot separate at all.

    Scope #

    Scope-(a), and narrow. This says what the existing theorem does and does not establish; it does not refute conj:sm-hypercharge-invariant, and it does not touch tr_M, whose framing is unambiguous (the writhe prefactor sits outside the trace). A twist datum derived from the braid word — the open qou-kebu item — is untouched by both horns, because it would be neither free nor a function of the writhe alone.

        theorem QOU.StandardModel.TwistContent.twist_dilemma {Word : Type u_1} (writhe T : Word → ℤ) :
        (∀ (β₁ β₂ : Word), writhe β₁ = writhe β₂ → T β₁ = T β₂) ∨ ∃ (β₁ : Word) (β₂ : Word), writhe β₁ = writhe β₂ ∧ T β₁ ≠ T β₂

        The dilemma, as a single statement: for any candidate twist on words, either it factors through the writhe — and then it is constant on writhe-degenerate pairs, separating nothing — or it does not, in which case it carries information the crossing content does not determine, and that information was supplied by hand rather than derived.
