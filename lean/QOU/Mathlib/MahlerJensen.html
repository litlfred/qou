    theorem Polynomial.mahlerMeasure_X_sub_C (a : ℂ) :
    (X - C a).mahlerMeasure = max 1 ‖a‖

    The Mahler measure of a linear factor: M(X − a) = max(1, ‖a‖).

    This is Jensen's formula for a single root. Mathlib supplies the analytic content (circleAverage_log_norm_sub_const_eq_posLog) but does not connect it to mahlerMeasure.

    The Jensen form #

    source
    theorem Polynomial.mahlerMeasure_eq_leadingCoeff_mul_prod_roots (p : Polynomial ℂ) :
    p.mahlerMeasure = ‖p.leadingCoeff‖ * (Multiset.map (fun (a : ℂ) => max 1 ‖a‖) p.roots).prod

    The Jensen form of the Mahler measure: M(P) = ‖lead P‖ · ∏ max(1, ‖root‖), the product running over the roots of P with multiplicity.

    Assembly rather than analysis, exactly as predicted: mahlerMeasure_X_sub_C for a single factor, Mathlib's prod_mahlerMeasure_eq_mahlerMeasure_prod for the product, and the factorization C (lead P) * ∏ (X − aᵢ) = P which holds over ℂ because it is algebraically closed.

    No P ≠ 0 hypothesis is needed: at P = 0 the left side is 0 by definition and the right side is ‖0‖ * 1 = 0, since (0 : ℂ[X]).roots is the empty multiset.

    theorem Polynomial.mahlerMeasure_C_mul_X_sub_C {q a : ℂ} (hq : q ≠ 0) :
    (C q * X - C a).mahlerMeasure = max ‖q‖ ‖a‖

    M(qX − a) = max(‖q‖, ‖a‖).

    This is the shape the m = 1 case of Roth's lemma measures a rational a/q against, and the bean recorded it as absent. It is the Jensen form at a single root: qX − a is C q * (X − C (a/q)), so its measure is ‖q‖ · max(1, ‖a‖/‖q‖) = max(‖q‖, ‖a‖).

    From the Jensen form to coefficient heights, by Vieta #

    The reverse of the bound in the next section: this one goes up from M(p) to the coefficients, and it is the direction Gelfond applies to each factor.

    source
    theorem Multiset.norm_prod {α : Type u_1} [NormedField α] (t : Multiset α) :
    ‖t.prod‖ = (map (fun (a : α) => ‖a‖) t).prod

    The norm of a multiset product is the product of the norms.

    source
    theorem Multiset.norm_esymm_le (s : Multiset ℂ) (j : ℕ) :
    ‖s.esymm j‖ ≤ ↑(s.card.choose j) * (map (fun (a : ℂ) => max 1 ‖a‖) s).prod

    The j-th elementary symmetric function of a multiset of complex numbers is bounded by C(card, j) times ∏ max(1, ‖a‖).

    Two slacknesses, both deliberate. A subset product ∏_{a ∈ t} ‖a‖ is bounded by ∏_{a ∈ t} max(1,‖a‖) termwise, and then that is extended from t to all of s — legal precisely because every extra factor is ≥ 1, so enlarging the index set only increases the product. With every summand bounded by the same ∏_{a ∈ s} max(1,‖a‖), the sum over powersetCard j s costs a factor card (powersetCard j s) = C(card s, j).

    source
    theorem Polynomial.norm_coeff_le_choose_mul_mahlerMeasure {p : Polynomial ℂ} {k : ℕ} (hk : k ≤ p.natDegree) :
    ‖p.coeff k‖ ≤ ↑(p.natDegree.choose (p.natDegree - k)) * p.mahlerMeasure

    The binomial form of the reverse bridge: ‖coeff k p‖ ≤ C(n, n−k) · M(p), n = deg p.

    Vieta writes coeff k p as lead p times an elementary symmetric function of the roots; the Jensen form writes M(p) as ‖lead p‖ times ∏ max(1,‖root‖). The two share the factor ‖lead p‖, so what is left is exactly Multiset.norm_esymm_le.

    source
    theorem Polynomial.norm_coeff_le_two_pow_mul_mahlerMeasure (p : Polynomial ℂ) (k : ℕ) :
    ‖p.coeff k‖ ≤ 2 ^ p.natDegree * p.mahlerMeasure

    The reverse bridge: H(p) ≤ 2^{deg p} · M(p), one coefficient at a time.

    Since H(p) is the supremum of ‖coeff k p‖ over k, this is the height bound; stating it per coefficient avoids having to fix a definition of H and is the form Gelfond consumes.

    No hypothesis on k: above the degree the coefficient is zero and the bound is free.

    A coefficient bound #

    The direction that turns a Mahler bound into a bound on coefficients. See the ⚠ in the module header: this is not Landau's inequality, only a cruder cousin of it.

    source
    theorem Polynomial.norm_eval_le_sum_norm_coeff {p : Polynomial ℂ} {z : ℂ} (hz : ‖z‖ = 1) :
    ‖eval z p‖ ≤ ∑ i ∈ Finset.range (p.natDegree + 1), ‖p.coeff i‖

    On the unit circle a polynomial is bounded by the sum of the norms of its coefficients.

    source
    theorem Polynomial.mahlerMeasure_le_max_one_sum_norm_coeff (p : Polynomial ℂ) :
    p.mahlerMeasure ≤ max 1 (∑ i ∈ Finset.range (p.natDegree + 1), ‖p.coeff i‖)

    A Landau-type bound: M(p) ≤ max(1, ‖p‖₁).

    ⚠ Weaker than Landau's inequality — see the module header. This is the sup bound on the circle, M(p) = exp ∫ log‖p‖ ≤ sup‖p‖ ≤ ‖p‖₁, not the Parseval one.

    ⚠ The max 1 is not slack, it is forced. The pointwise step is log ‖p(z)‖ ≤ log S with S = ‖p‖₁, and that fails at a zero of p on the unit circle when S < 1: Mathlib's Real.log 0 = 0, which exceeds log S < 0. Bounding by max 1 S makes the pointwise inequality hold at every point of the circle and so avoids an a.e. argument.

    For a non-zero integer polynomial the distinction vanishes, since the leading coefficient alone contributes at least one — that is mahlerMeasure_le_sum_int.

    source
    theorem Polynomial.mahlerMeasure_le_sum_norm_coeff {p : Polynomial ℂ} (h : 1 ≤ ∑ i ∈ Finset.range (p.natDegree + 1), ‖p.coeff i‖) :
    p.mahlerMeasure ≤ ∑ i ∈ Finset.range (p.natDegree + 1), ‖p.coeff i‖

    When the coefficient sum is itself at least one, the max drops away.

    theorem Polynomial.one_le_sum_norm_coeff_map_int {q : Polynomial ℤ} (hq : q ≠ 0) :

    A non-zero integer polynomial has coefficient sum at least one: its leading coefficient is a non-zero integer, hence of absolute value at least one, and every other term of the sum is non-negative.

    source
    theorem Polynomial.mahlerMeasure_le_sum_int {q : Polynomial ℤ} (hq : q ≠ 0) :
    (map (Int.castRingHom ℂ) q).mahlerMeasure ≤ ∑ i ∈ Finset.range ((map (Int.castRingHom ℂ) q).natDegree + 1), ‖(map (Int.castRingHom ℂ) q).coeff i‖

    The coefficient bound over ℤ. For a non-zero integer polynomial, the Mahler measure of its complex image is at most the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients.

    This is the form the Roth's-lemma height bookkeeping consumes: over ℤ the max 1 of mahlerMeasure_le_max_one_sum_norm_coeff disappears. ⚠ Still not Landau — see the module header.

    Next #

    Gelfond's inequality ∏ₖ H(pₖ) ≤ 2^{deg p}(deg p + 1) · H(p) for p = ∏ₖ pₖ over ℤ, and then the height control on the factors φₖ, ψₖ that the induction step of Roth's lemma needs. Every ingredient is now in this file or in Mathlib:

    • mahlerMeasure_mul / prod_mahlerMeasure_eq_mahlerMeasure_prod — M is multiplicative, so ∏ₖ M(pₖ) = M(p) exactly. This is what makes the argument work at all: heights are not multiplicative, Mahler measures are.
    • norm_coeff_le_two_pow_mul_mahlerMeasure — up from each M(pₖ) to H(pₖ).
    • mahlerMeasure_le_sum_int — down from H(p) to M(p).

    ⚠ The remaining work is genuinely bookkeeping, but it is not nothing: it has to move between ℤ[X] and ℂ[X] along Polynomial.map (Int.castRingHom ℂ), and natDegree and coeff must be tracked across that map. natDegree_map_eq_of_injective is the lemma that makes it routine; one_le_sum_norm_coeff_map_int above is a worked example of the pattern.

    Sharpening mahlerMeasure_le_max_one_sum_norm_coeff to Landau proper (M(p) ≤ ‖p‖₂, via Parseval and Jensen's inequality for exp) is optional: the lost √(deg+1) is invisible downstream, so this is worth doing only as an upstream contribution.
