    QOU.Mathlib.LagrangeCornerNodeSum — the corner node sum #

    A third member of the LagrangeCoeffSum family. Where sum_prod_qratio weights each node by 1 and sum_prod_qratio_weighted weights it by the seminormal entry Q·v_i/(Q·v_i − v_b), this one weights it by the constant-numerator ratio (Q − 1)/(Q·v_i − v_b), and the sum is 1:

    ∑_{i ∈ s} (∏_{j ≠ i} (Q·v_i − v_j)/(v_i − v_j)) · (Q − 1)/(Q·v_i − v_b)  =  1      (`v_b = 1`)

    This is the node-coordinate form of the Pieri corner identity — the one identity the Macdonald hook telescope's Pieri step (P) reduces to. In the intended instantiation v_i = Q^{β_i} for the β-numbers of a partition padded with one empty row, so the padded row contributes the node v_b = Q^0 = 1, and i ranges over the rows admitting a box.

    Why v b = 1 is a hypothesis and not decoration #

    The identity is false without a node equal to 1: at |s| = 2 with free nodes the sum is

    (v₀Q² − v₀Q + v₁Q² − v₁Q − Q² + 1)/(v₀v₁Q² − v₀Q − v₁Q + 1),

    which is not 1. The node at 1 is exactly the addable corner in the new bottom row, so the identity is about shapes that may grow a row — dropping that corner from the sum breaks it.

    Where the q-dependence goes #

    The whole Q-dependence sits in the coefficients c₁ … c_{|s|−1} of ∏_{j≠b}(Q·X − v_j), and the Lagrange vanishing annihilates every one of them: only c₀, which is Q-free, survives. That is why the right-hand side is a bare 1 for every Q, and it is the reason the q-deformed statement is no harder than its q = 1 shadow rather than harder.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.sum_prod_qratio_corner {F : Type u_1} [Field F] {ι : Type u_2} [DecidableEq ι] {s : Finset ι} {v : ι → F} (hv : Set.InjOn v ↑s) (hv0 : ∀ i ∈ s, v i ≠ 0) {Q : F} (hQ0 : Q ≠ 0) (hQ1 : Q ≠ 1) {b : ι} (hb : b ∈ s) (hvb : v b = 1) (hne : ∀ i ∈ s, i ≠ b → Q * v i - v b ≠ 0) :
    ∑ i ∈ s, (∏ j ∈ s.erase i, (Q * v i - v j) / (v i - v j)) * ((Q - 1) / (Q * v i - v b)) = 1

    The corner node sum.

    ∑_{i ∈ s} (∏_{j≠i} (Q·v_i − v_j)/(v_i − v_j)) · (Q − 1)/(Q·v_i − v_b) = 1, for a distinguished node b with v b = 1.

    hne is not decoration, exactly as in sum_prod_qratio_weighted: without it a term is 0/0, since Q·v_i = v_b kills the ratio's j = b numerator factor and the weight's denominator at once. In the instantiation v_i = Q^{β_i} it is discharged by addability — β_b = β_i + 1 is precisely what it means for row i not to admit a box.

    i = b needs no special case. For i ≠ b the weight's denominator cancels the j = b factor of the numerator; at i = b that factor is absent and the weight is (Q−1)/(Q·1 − 1) = 1, and the two land on the same expression eval (v i) h / (v i · ∏_{j≠i}(v i − v j)).

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.sum_eq_one_of_corner_ratio_scaling {F : Type u_1} [Field F] {ι : Type u_2} [DecidableEq ι] {s : Finset ι} {v : ι → F} (hv : Set.InjOn v ↑s) (hv0 : ∀ i ∈ s, v i ≠ 0) {Q : F} (hQ0 : Q ≠ 0) (hQ1 : Q ≠ 1) {b : ι} (hb : b ∈ s) (hvb : v b = 1) (hne : ∀ i ∈ s, i ≠ b → Q * v i - v b ≠ 0) (w : ι → F) (hw : ∀ i ∈ s, w i = (∏ j ∈ s.erase i, (Q * v i - v j) / (v i - v j)) * ((Q - 1) / (Q * v i - v b))) :
    ∑ i ∈ s, w i = 1

    The corner identity, for any weight family whose box-addition ratio is the corner q-ratio.

    If w i — the weight attached to the corner in row i — is the corner q-ratio at node i, then ∑_i w i = 1. This is the form in which sum_prod_qratio_corner delivers the Pieri step, and it is the exact analogue of sum_eq_bracket_mul_of_qratio_scaling for the branching sum.

    hw is a genuine sub-hypothesis and not a restatement of the conclusion: it says that adding a box in row i multiplies the weight by the node ratio, which is the q-analogue of the Frame–Robinson–Thrall hook identity in node coordinates and is not proved here. Establishing it is what still stands between this and the general Pieri step (P); the same input is what sum_eq_bracket_mul_of_qratio_scaling names as its own remaining gap. Measured — over 13 shapes at three padding lengths, computations/pieri_corner_node_identity_probe.py fact 2 — the identity it needs is

    ∏_{c ∈ λ} (1 − q^{h(c)})  =  ∏_i (q;q)_{β_i} / ∏_{j<l} (1 − q^{β_j − β_l}),

    whose classical shadow prodHooks_mul_betaProdGap is already proved on the list layer.
