    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedReinsertRow — the re-inserted letter goes back to the row it came from #

    Bricks 28 and 29 of epic qou-nrex (RSK inverse).

    Brick 10 showed the reverse cascade is a right inverse of insertion: re-inserting the ejected letter rebuilds the tableau. It said nothing about where the rebuilt cell landed. This file closes that gap, and then runs it along a whole peel.

    • addCell_removeCorner — the shape-level statement: adding a cell back at a corner you just removed returns the shape you started from. Its proof is the two-case anatomy of removeCorner (removeCorner_eq): away from length one the filter is inert and the operation is a set; at length one the corner is the final row and the filter deletes it.
    • newRowIndex_uninsert — the tableau-level statement: newRowIndex (uninsert T i).1 z = i. It follows from the shape statement because addCell moves exactly one entry, so a shape determines the index that produced it.
    • rowIndices_uninsertSteps — along a run: the recovered word's row-index record is the list of positions the run peeled at, unchanged.

    Why this is the Q side #

    Brick 4 (insertWordRecAux_snd_eq_qAppendSteps) identified the recording tableau as the record of rowIndices. Brick 14 recovered a word whose insertion tableau is P. Neither said the recovered word records the positions it was recovered from — without which the recording tableau of the recovered word is unconstrained. rowIndices_uninsertSteps is that missing step.

    Scope #

    The index bookkeeping only. Nothing here rebuilds a recording tableau from its positions — that is qAppendSteps, and inverting it is the next brick. rskPair bijectivity and RSKBurnsideHolds for general n are past both and are not claimed.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.removeCorner_eq {l : List ℕ} (hy : YoungShape l) {i : ℕ} (hc : SYTEnum.IsCorner l i) :
    2 ≤ l.getD i 0 ∧ SYTEnum.removeCorner l i = l.set i (l.getD i 0 - 1) ∨ l.getD i 0 = 1 ∧ i + 1 = l.length ∧ SYTEnum.removeCorner l i = List.take i l

    The anatomy of removeCorner on a Young shape. Either the row keeps a cell, in which case the zero-filter is inert and the operation is a plain set; or the row had a single cell, in which case the corner condition forces it to be the last row and the filter deletes it.
