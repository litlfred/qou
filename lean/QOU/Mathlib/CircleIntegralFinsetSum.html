    Two Mathlib gaps at our pinned revision #

    Both statements below exist in current Mathlib but not at the revision this project pins (f897ebcf). They are stated here in Mathlib's own shape so that the eventual pin bump deletes this file rather than requiring a rewrite.

    • integral_fun_sum — linearity of the circle integral over a Finset sum. Upstream as circleIntegral.integral_fun_sum.
    • divisor_support_finite_of_subset — a meromorphic function on a compact set has finitely many zeros and poles on any subset. Upstream as MeromorphicOn.divisor_support_finite_of_subset.

    Together with MeromorphicOn.extract_zeros_poles — which is present at the pin — these are what a Finset-indexed argument principle consumes: the factorisation supplies the finitely many factors, this supplies their finiteness, and the first lets the contour integral be taken term by term.

    -- Ref: [manuscript] bean qou-argp — the converse theta zero locus.

    (MeromorphicOn.divisor f V).support.Finite

    If f is meromorphic on a compact U and V ⊆ U, the divisor of f on V has finite support.

    The support is discrete (isolated zeros) and closed in U; a discrete closed subset of a compact set is finite. divisor_restrict transports that from U to V.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.exists_finset_factorisation {V K : Set ℂ} (hV : IsOpen V) (hVconn : IsPreconnected V) (hVne : V.Nonempty) (hK : IsCompact K) (hVK : V ⊆ K) {f : ℂ → ℂ} (hf : AnalyticOnNhd ℂ f K) (hord : ∀ (u : ↑V), meromorphicOrderAt f ↑u ≠ ⊤) :
    ∃ (S : Finset ℂ) (m : ℂ → ℕ) (g : ℂ → ℂ), AnalyticOnNhd ℂ g V ∧ (∀ (u : ↑V), g ↑u ≠ 0) ∧ (∀ a ∈ S, 0 < m a) ∧ (∀ a ∈ S, a ∈ V) ∧ Set.EqOn f (fun (z : ℂ) => (∏ a ∈ S, (z - a) ^ m a) * g z) V

    An analytic function factorises over its zeros on a relatively compact, connected open set.

    extract_zeros_poles gives this modulo a codiscrete set and in finprod/zpow form; the identity theorem upgrades the equality and finprod_zpow_eq_finset_prod_npow normalises the shape.

    The multiplicities are strictly positive and S ⊆ V, so S is exactly the zero set of f on V — without those, a ∈ S would carry no information and the factorisation could not be read backwards.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.analyticOrderAt_eq_of_finset_factorisation {V : Set ℂ} (hV : IsOpen V) {S : Finset ℂ} {m : ℂ → ℕ} {f g : ℂ → ℂ} (hg : AnalyticOnNhd ℂ g V) (hgne : ∀ z ∈ V, g z ≠ 0) (hSV : ∀ a ∈ S, a ∈ V) (hEq : Set.EqOn f (fun (z : ℂ) => (∏ a ∈ S, (z - a) ^ m a) * g z) V) {a : ℂ} (ha : a ∈ S) :
    analyticOrderAt f a = ↑(m a)

    In a Finset factorisation the exponent at a factor is the order of vanishing there.

    Splitting (· − a)^{m a} off the product leaves an analytic function that does not vanish at a — the two remaining kinds of factor, (· − b)^{m b} for b ≠ a and the cofactor g, are both nonzero there — and that is exactly what analyticOrderAt f a = m a says.
