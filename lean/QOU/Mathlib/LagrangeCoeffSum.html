    QOU.Mathlib.LagrangeCoeffSum — coefficient extraction as a sum over interpolation nodes #

    Two facts about Lagrange interpolation that Mathlib states only implicitly.
    coeff_lagrangeBasis the top coefficient of Lagrange.basis s v i is ∏_{j ≠ i} (v i − v j)⁻¹
    sum_eval_div_prod_eq_coeff for deg f < |s|, ∑_i f(v i) / ∏_{j≠i}(v i − v j) = f.coeff (|s| − 1)
    sum_prod_qratio ∑_i ∏_{j≠i} (Q·v i − v j)/(v i − v j) = 1 + Q + ⋯ + Q^{|s|−1}
    sum_eq_bracket_mul_of_qratio_scaling the same, packaged as the branching identity
    sum_prod_qratio_weighted the same sum weighted by Q·v_i/(Q·v_i − v_b), which sums to Q^{|s|}/(Q−1)

    The second is the algebraic form of "the sum of the residues of f/∏(X − v j) over its poles reads off the top coefficient of f". It is the workhorse: with a well-chosen f it turns a sum of products over nodes into a single coefficient.

    ∑ i ∈ s, Polynomial.eval (v i) f / ∏ j ∈ s.erase i, (v i - v j) = f.coeff (s.card - 1)

    Coefficient extraction as a node sum.

    For a polynomial of degree less than the number of nodes,

    ∑_{i ∈ s} f(v i) / ∏_{j ∈ s \ {i}} (v i − v j) = f.coeff (|s| − 1).

    Proof: f equals its own Lagrange interpolant, and the top coefficient of the i-th basis polynomial is ∏_{j ≠ i}(v i − v j)⁻¹.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.sum_prod_qratio {F : Type u_1} [Field F] {ι : Type u_2} [DecidableEq ι] {s : Finset ι} {v : ι → F} (hv : Set.InjOn v ↑s) (hv0 : ∀ i ∈ s, v i ≠ 0) {Q : F} (hQ0 : Q ≠ 0) (hQ1 : Q ≠ 1) :
    ∑ i ∈ s, ∏ j ∈ s.erase i, (Q * v i - v j) / (v i - v j) = ∑ k ∈ Finset.range s.card, Q ^ k

    The q-ratio node sum.

    For pairwise distinct nonzero nodes v i, i ∈ s,

    ∑_{i ∈ s} ∏_{j ≠ i} (Q·v i − v j)/(v i − v j) = 1 + Q + ⋯ + Q^{|s|−1}.

    This is the algebraic heart of Pieri's rule at a principal specialisation. Writing the level-r principal specialisation of s_μ as a Vandermonde ratio in x_i = Q^{μ_i + r − i}, the ratio s_{μ+□ in row i}/s_μ is exactly the i-th summand, and the identity says those ratios sum to [r]_Q — which is the branching identity. It also explains for free why the non-addable rows may be summed over harmlessly: if adding a box to row i would break the partition, then Q·v i = v_{i−1} and that summand is zero.

    Two hypotheses are artefacts of the proof, not of the statement. The identity is a polynomial identity and holds at Q = 1 and at v i = 0 too; hQ1 and hv0 are needed because the argument divides by Q − 1 and by v i. Both hold in the intended application, where v i is a power of Q and Q is not a root of unity.

    Proof: apply sum_eval_div_prod_eq_coeff twice — to divX f and to divX g, where f = ∏_j (X − v j) and g = ∏_j (Q·X − v j). The first pins the node sum of 1/(v i · ∏_{j≠i}(v i − v j)) (the residue at 0); the second produces the wanted sum plus that same correction, and the top coefficients 1 and Q^{|s|} do the rest.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.sum_eq_bracket_mul_of_qratio_scaling {F : Type u_1} [Field F] {ι : Type u_2} [DecidableEq ι] {s : Finset ι} {v : ι → F} (hv : Set.InjOn v ↑s) (hv0 : ∀ i ∈ s, v i ≠ 0) {Q : F} (hQ0 : Q ≠ 0) (hQ1 : Q ≠ 1) (w : ι → F) (W : F) (hw : ∀ i ∈ s, w i = (∏ j ∈ s.erase i, (Q * v i - v j) / (v i - v j)) * W) :
    ∑ i ∈ s, w i = (∑ k ∈ Finset.range s.card, Q ^ k) * W

    Branching, for any weight family whose box-addition ratio is the q-ratio.

    If w i — the weight of "μ with a box added in row i" — is W, the weight of μ, times the i-th q-ratio, then ∑_i w i = [|s|]_Q · W. That is the branching identity, and this is the form in which sum_prod_qratio delivers it.

    The hypothesis hw is the one remaining input, and it is a genuine sub-hypothesis rather than a restatement of the conclusion: it is the classical fact that the level-r principal specialisation is the Vandermonde ratio in the shifted parts x_i = Q^{μ_i + r − i}, from which the box-addition ratio follows because adding a box to row i sends x_i ↦ Q·x_i and fixes every other x_j. Establishing hw for QBeta.WenzlWeight.schurPrincipal is what still stands between this and general-r branching for the corpus weight. It is not stated here as a sorry.

    The weighted node sum #

    The Markov property needs the q-ratio sum weighted by one more factor — the seminormal (Hoefsmit) entry, which in node coordinates is Q·v_i/(Q·v_i − v_b) for a distinguished node b. Remarkably the weighted sum is no harder: the extra factor cancels the j = b term of the ratio's numerator, and what is left is sum_eval_div_prod_eq_coeff applied to a degree |s|−1 polynomial — no divX subtraction, and, unlike the branching case, no separate treatment of i = b.

    source
    theorem QOU.Mathlib.sum_prod_qratio_weighted {F : Type u_1} [Field F] {ι : Type u_2} [DecidableEq ι] {s : Finset ι} {v : ι → F} (hv : Set.InjOn v ↑s) (hv0 : ∀ i ∈ s, v i ≠ 0) {Q : F} (hQ0 : Q ≠ 0) (hQ1 : Q ≠ 1) {b : ι} (hb : b ∈ s) (hne : ∀ i ∈ s, i ≠ b → Q * v i - v b ≠ 0) :
    ∑ i ∈ s, (∏ j ∈ s.erase i, (Q * v i - v j) / (v i - v j)) * (Q * v i / (Q * v i - v b)) = Q ^ s.card / (Q - 1)

    The weighted q-ratio node sum.

    ∑_{i ∈ s} (∏_{j≠i} (Q·v_i − v_j)/(v_i − v_j)) · Q·v_i/(Q·v_i − v_b) = Q^{|s|}/(Q − 1).

    The weight is exactly what the seminormal entry contributes to the Markov condition, and the whole sum collapses to the top coefficient of ∏_{j ≠ b}(Q·t − v_j), which is Q^{|s|−1}.

    i = b needs no special case. For i ≠ b the weight's denominator cancels the j = b factor of the numerator; at i = b that factor is absent but the weight is the constant Q/(Q−1), and the two land on the same expression. I first thought this case broke the uniform shape — it does not, and the appearance that it did came from including the j = i factor, which the ratio's numerator does not have.

    hne is not decoration. Without it a term can be 0/0: Q·v_i = v_b makes the ratio's numerator vanish and the weight's denominator vanish, and the two sides then disagree. In the intended application s indexes the rows that admit a box, where β_b = β_i + 1 is impossible precisely because row i admits one — so the hypothesis is discharged by addability, and the degenerate case is the one the corner identity excludes by summing over addable cells only.
