    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedWordRecovery — recovering the word (RSK inverse, brick 3) #

    Brick 2 undid one insertion. This file iterates it: given the final insertion tableau and the list of rows where each step placed its new cell, the whole word comes back.

    • rowIndices T w — the row indices, one per letter, in insertion order. Built by running the same recursion insertWord runs, so it is a record of the fold, not a search.
    • uninsertSteps T is — undo the steps of is from the final tableau T, recovering the word. The recursion is structural in the forward index list: uninsertSteps T (i :: rest) undoes the later steps first (rest) and only then step i, which is what makes it a plain structural recursion rather than a fold over a reversed list.
    • uninsertSteps_foldl — the inversion law, and uninsertSteps_insertWord — its specialisation at the empty tableau: uninsertSteps (insertWord w) (rowIndices [] w) = ([], w).

    So the insertion tableau together with the row-index list determines the word. That is the whole content of the RSK inverse on the P side; what remains is that the recording tableau encodes exactly that index list, which is brick 4.

    Scope #

    The P side only, and conditional on being handed the index list. Nothing here says the recording tableau supplies it, nothing here proves rskPair bijective, and RSKBurnsideHolds for general n stays open. (It is proved for n ≤ 12 by an unrelated route — PartitionsOfBridge.rskBurnsideHolds_le12 — with no RSK in it.)

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean.

      def QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.uninsertSteps :
      List (List ℕ) → List ℕ → List (List ℕ) × List ℕ

      Undo a run of insertions. uninsertSteps T is treats T as the tableau after all the steps recorded in is (in insertion order) and peels them off, returning the starting tableau and the recovered word.

      Note the order: i :: rest undoes rest — the later steps — before i. That is what keeps this a structural recursion on the forward list.

        The word-recovery law. Running the insertions of w into a well-formed T and then undoing them at their recorded rows returns T and w exactly.

        The induction is the mirror of insertWord's fold: the outer step of w is undone last, which is where uninsertSteps' order pays off — the inductive hypothesis applies to the inner fold unchanged, and brick 2's uninsert_insert closes the outer step.
