          Part 4 — the matrix entries are small #

          The last input Siegel needs. translateMatrix α i n is Roth's binom(n,i)·α^{n−i}, and the bound is proved without the closed form: peel one variable occurrence at a time from monomial n 1 = X_j · monomial n' 1, so each step multiplies by a single linear factor X_j + C α_j and costs a factor 2B — the 2 from its two terms, the B from the constant one. The multivariate binomial theorem is never needed.

          ⚠ The bound is stated for an arbitrary submultiplicative seminorm, not for house, and the reason is a measurement rather than a taste for generality.

          Neither Siegel module ships in this project's built Mathlib closure, and both are cheap to add: Mathlib.NumberTheory.SiegelsLemma (over ℤ) is 3 modules and about half a minute; Mathlib.NumberTheory.NumberField.House (over a number field) is 40 modules and about four. Both are verified usable at this pin — Int.Matrix.exists_ne_zero_int_vec_norm_le with attribute [local instance] Matrix.seminormedAddCommGroup and open Matrix, and house in QOU/Mathlib/RothHouseBound.lean.

          ⚠⚠ An earlier version of this paragraph said House was 470 of 3322 imports away and framed the next step as a fork between that build and re-deriving the integral-basis bookkeeping by hand. Both numbers were wrong and there was no fork — see RothHouseBound for the cause (an import-cone script that matched import inside comments) and for what it says about reporting an unvalidated measurement as a measurement.

          ⚠ .lake is a build artifact, so a fresh container has neither module.

          The bound below is nonetheless stated for an arbitrary submultiplicative seminorm, and that remains the right shape: it instantiates at house (RothHouseBound.house_translateMatrix_le) and at |·| on ℤ (abs_translateMatrix_le) with no duplicated proof.

            Part 5 — the bridge to index's own language #

            Parts 2 and 3 count multi-indices as Fin m → ℕ, because that is the shape the product-space lemmas want. index and idxWeight speak σ →₀ ℕ. Finsupp.equivFunOnFinite carries one to the other, and the two weights differ by exactly the mean:

            centredWeight r (equivFunOnFinite i) = idxWeight r i − m/2

            idxWeight sums over the support while centredWeight sums over all coordinates; they agree because the missing terms are 0/d = 0, which is idxWeight_eq_sum_univ.

            The endpoint is fintypeCard_idxWeight_lt: Fintype.card of the equation subtype is less than Fintype.card of the unknown subtype — p < q in exactly the form Int.Matrix.exists_ne_zero_int_vec_norm_le asks for it.

            source
