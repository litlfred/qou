    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedWordSurjectivity — the recovered word rebuilds the tableau (brick 14) #

    Brick 3 (SchenstedWordRecovery) proved that running the insertions of a word and then undoing them at their recorded rows returns the word: uninsertSteps ∘ insertWord = id. That direction supports injectivity, and it is the one bricks 7 and 8 already consumed.

    This file proves the other direction at the word level: run the reverse cascade at a list of corner positions, collect what falls out, and re-inserting that word rebuilds the tableau you started from.

    foldl_insert_uninsertSteps — (uninsertSteps T is).2.foldl insert (uninsertSteps T is).1 = T

    and, when the run empties the tableau, the form surjectivity actually wants:

    insertWord_uninsertSteps — insertWord (uninsertSteps T is).2 = T.

    a single reverse step is invertible by insert brick 10, insert_uninsert

    Brick 10 takes the three invariants as hypotheses on the tableau it is handed. Iterating it needs them at every intermediate tableau, which is what bricks 11 and 13 supply, and why they had to come first.

    CornersAlong, and why the corner condition lives on the run #

    ColStrict preservation needs the removed cell to be a corner (brick 11 located that, with a counterexample). At the level of a whole run, the condition is that every step removes a corner of the tableau as it stands at that step — not of the original. CornersAlong says exactly that, by the same recursion uninsertSteps itself uses.

    This is not a hypothesis a caller has to invent. In the RSK inverse the positions come from the recording tableau, and the cell carrying the largest remaining entry of a standard tableau is a corner of the current shape. CornersAlong is where that fact is consumed; supplying it is the Q-side's job, one level up.

    Scope #

    The P side, at the word level, conditional on the run's positions being corners and (for the second form) on the run emptying the tableau. Both are facts about the recording tableau and are proved elsewhere or not yet — nothing here claims them. RSKBurnsideHolds for general n remains open; it is proved for n ≤ 12 by an unrelated route (PartitionsOfBridge.rskBurnsideHolds_le12, no RSK in it).

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean.

    def QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.CornersAlong :

    Every step of the run removes a corner — of the tableau as it stands at that step, which is what ColStrict preservation needs. Defined by the same recursion as uninsertSteps: the later steps of rest happen first, so the corner condition for i is checked against the tableau they leave behind.

      The mirror of brick 3's uninsertSteps_foldl. That one runs insertions and undoes them; this one undoes and re-inserts. Together they make insertWord and uninsertSteps mutually inverse on column-strict tableaux, given the positions.

      The induction is one step of brick 10 (insert_uninsert) wrapped around the inductive hypothesis, with bricks 11 and 13 supplying the invariants at the intermediate tableau and brick 13 also ruling out the none branch.

      The form surjectivity wants. When the run empties the tableau, the word it recovered is a word whose insertion tableau is exactly T.

      insertWord is foldl insert [], so this is foldl_insert_uninsertSteps with the starting tableau known to be empty. Whether a given run empties the tableau is a fact about the positions — supplied by the recording tableau, not by anything here.
