    QOU.Mathlib.YoungLatticeUpOperator — the up-operator identity on SYT counts #

    The target is

    ∑_{λ = μ + one addable cell}  f^λ  =  (|μ| + 1) · f^μ,                      (UP)

    the up half of Young's lattice being a 1-differential poset. QOU.Mathlib.SYTBranchingRule supplies the down half (numStandardYoungTableaux_branching), and QOU.Mathlib.YoungLatticeCornerCount shows that (UP) is exactly what is still needed to close

    ∑_{λ ⊢ n} f^λ = I(n)                                (the involution numbers, bean `qou-jlam`).

    The route, and why it is not the operator-algebra one #

    The textbook derivation puts D U = U D + I on the free module ℚ[Young] and reads (UP) off D U^{m+1} ∅ = (m+1) U^m ∅. That needs the off-diagonal of DU − UD = I — a statement about pairs of diagrams — plus the free-module setup. This file avoids both: it proves (UP) directly, by strong induction on |μ|, from three ingredients that are already in the corpus or are pure row arithmetic.

    Writing A(μ) for the addable cells, C(μ) for the removable corners, a(μ) = |A(μ)|, r(μ) = |C(μ)|, and expanding each f^{μ+s} by the branching rule:

    upSum μ  =  ∑_{s ∈ A(μ)} f^{μ+s}
         =  ∑_{s ∈ A(μ)}  ∑_{c ∈ C(μ+s)} f^{(μ+s) ∖ c}
         =  a(μ)·f^μ  +  ∑_{s ∈ A(μ)} ∑_{c ∈ C(μ+s), c ≠ s} f^{(μ ∖ c) + s}

    — the split is by whether the corner removed is the cell just added. The double sum is then transposed, which is what isCornerCell_addCell_iff below is for: it identifies the two index sets

    {(s, c) : s ∈ A(μ), c ∈ C(μ + s), c ≠ s}  =  {(s, c) : c ∈ C(μ), s ∈ A(μ ∖ c), s ≠ c}.

    Applying the induction hypothesis to each μ ∖ c (which has |μ| − 1 cells) and the branching rule once more to ∑_{c ∈ C(μ)} f^{μ∖c} = f^μ gives

    upSum μ + r(μ)·f^μ  =  a(μ)·f^μ + |μ|·f^μ,

    and a(μ) = r(μ) + 1 (card_addableCells_eq_card_cornerCells_succ) turns that into (UP) by cancelling r(μ)·f^μ. The identity is arranged so that no ℕ subtraction appears anywhere in the argument.

    Everything reduces to row lengths #

    corner_iff and addable_iff (in YoungDiagramAddable) characterise both notions by the row-length sequence. So the two structural facts this file needs about insertion and deletion are

    (μ + (a,b)).rowLen i = μ.rowLen i + [i = a],      (μ ∖ (p,q)).rowLen i = μ.rowLen i − [i = p],

    after which isCornerCell_addCell_iff is a three-case split (a = p, a = p+1, neither) closed by omega. That is the whole mathematical content: a corner c = (p,q) is destroyed by inserting s exactly when s sits directly right of c or directly below it, and those are the same two cases in which s fails to be addable to μ ∖ c.

    Pure combinatorics; nothing archimedean (§7c), no fit, no ℕ-subtraction in the counting identity.

    The corner-survival correspondence #

    This is the transposition lemma for the double sum, and the only place where the geometry of the Young lattice actually enters.

    source
    theorem QOU.Mathlib.isCornerCell_addCell_iff (μ : YoungDiagram) (a b p q : ℕ) (hs : IsAddableCell μ (a, b)) (hc : IsCornerCell μ (p, q)) :
    IsCornerCell (addCell μ (a, b) hs) (p, q) ↔ IsAddableCell (eraseCorner μ (p, q) hc) (a, b)

    A corner survives an insertion exactly when the inserted cell was addable to the diagram with that corner removed.

    Concretely: inserting s destroys the corner c precisely when s is c's right neighbour or c's lower neighbour, and those are exactly the two positions at which s fails to be addable to μ ∖ c. The proof is the three-case split a = p / a = p + 1 / neither on the row indices.

    The two one-directional transfers #

    isCornerCell_addCell_iff is stated for s addable to μ and c a corner of μ. These two lemmas supply the hypotheses from the other side, so that the two index sets of the double sum are literally the same subset of A(μ) ×ˢ C(μ).

          theorem QOU.Mathlib.upSum_eq (n : ℕ) (μ : YoungDiagram) :
          μ.cells.card = n → upSum μ = (n + 1) * numStandardYoungTableaux μ

          The up-operator identity. ∑_{λ = μ + one addable cell} f^λ = (|μ| + 1) · f^μ.

          Strong induction on |μ|. Each summand is expanded by the branching rule (numSYT_addCell_eq), the double sum is transposed by Finset.sum_comm, and the inner sum is identified with upSum (μ ∖ c) (sum_pairTerm_add), to which the induction hypothesis applies. card_addableCells_eq_card_cornerCells_succ then cancels the corner count off both sides.
