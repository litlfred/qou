    Step 2's index lower bound over 𝓞 K. Every low-weight translated coefficient that is small at φ₀ and bounded at the other conjugates vanishes, and enough vanishing is a lower bound on the index.

    The hypothesis is a pair per coefficient, unlike the ℤ case's single bound: M at the other d−1 conjugates and smallness at φ₀ measured against M^{d−1}. That asymmetry is the whole difference between |N(x)| ≥ 1 and |z| ≥ 1.

    theorem MvPolynomial.submultSeminorm_norm_complex :
    SubmultSeminorm norm

    ‖·‖ on ℂ is a submultiplicative seminorm. So every estimate in RothTaylorBound applies at a complex embedding of a number field — which is exactly what the 𝓞 K case needs, since there the smallness is measured at one embedding while the others carry only the house bound. Generalising those estimates from |·| on ℝ to an arbitrary SubmultSeminorm was cheaper than a second copy of the peeling induction, and this is the whole cost of the ℂ case.

    theorem MvPolynomial.embedding_coeff_translate {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [NumberField K] {m : ℕ} (φ : K →+* ℂ) (β : Fin m → ℚ) (P : MvPolynomial (Fin m) (NumberField.RingOfIntegers K)) (i : Fin m →₀ ℕ) :
    φ (coeff i (translate (fun (h : Fin m) => (algebraMap ℚ K) (β h)) ((map (algebraMap (NumberField.RingOfIntegers K) K)) P))) = coeff i (translate (fun (h : Fin m) => ↑(β h)) ((map φ) ((map (algebraMap (NumberField.RingOfIntegers K) K)) P)))

    An embedding commutes with translation at a rational point. φ fixes ℚ, so the point does not move under φ; only the coefficients do. That is why the same rational β can be used at every conjugate, and why the d−1 "other" bounds are about the same polynomial evaluated the same way.

    ‖φ₀ (coeff i (translate (fun (h : Fin m) => (algebraMap ℚ K) (↑(pn h) / ↑(qd h))) ((map (algebraMap (NumberField.RingOfIntegers K) K)) P)))‖ ≤ ↑(∏ h : Fin m, (r h + 1)) * (↑(∏ h : Fin m, (r h + 1)) * (H * (2 * B) ^ ∑ h : Fin m, r h) * ((2 ^ ∑ h : Fin m, r h) * S))

    The Taylor bound at the distinguished embedding. This is the composition that makes the assembly honest: hidx is step 1's index at α, and it is what restricts the sum, so the estimate genuinely consumes step 1 instead of taking its consequence for granted.

    An assembly that instead took the smallness as a hypothesis would type-check and would never use step 1 at all — the polynomial's defining property would be inert.
