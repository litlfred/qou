    noncomputable def QOU.Mathlib.QGevreyTaming.qBorelCoeff (q : ℝ) (e : ℕ → ℕ) (a : ℕ → ℝ) (n : ℕ) :
    ℝ

    The q-Borel coefficient for a general exponent sequence e: aₙ / q^{e n}.

    Taking e n = n² gives the divisor matching deg_q(cₙ) = n²; taking e = qBorelExp d gives the RSZ level-d kernel.

      q-Gevrey taming, general exponent. If the coefficients are dominated by C·Aⁿ·q^{e n}, then dividing by q^{e n} leaves them geometrically bounded.

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.QGevreyTaming.qBorelCoeff_compose (q : ℝ) (e f : ℕ → ℕ) (a : ℕ → ℝ) (n : ℕ) :
      qBorelCoeff q f (qBorelCoeff q e a) n = qBorelCoeff q (fun (k : ℕ) => e k + f k) a n

      Composing two q-Borel divisions adds their exponents — the q-analogue of borelCoeff_compose (there, applying the single-Borel divisor twice gives the double-Borel divisor; here the exponents add rather than the factorials squaring). This is the formal-series face of q-acceleration transitivity.

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.QGevreyTaming.qBorelCoeff_dilate (q : ℝ) (e : ℕ → ℕ) (a : ℕ → ℝ) (n : ℕ) :
      qBorelCoeff q e (fun (k : ℕ) => q ^ k * a k) n = q ^ n * qBorelCoeff q e a n

      The q-Borel transform intertwines the two dilations. Dilating the argument by q multiplies the n-th coefficient by qⁿ; the transform divides by a kernel that does not depend on the coefficients, so the transformed sequence is dilated in exactly the same way:

      B(qⁿ·aₙ) = qⁿ · B(aₙ).

      This is the reason the dilation on x induces a dilation on the Borel variable, hence why a Borel-plane singularity at ω moves to qω — the shift field of QAlienBridge.TwistedQBridgeSystem, and in turn why the index set of the q-bridge has to be the spiral quotient E_q = ℂ*/q^ℤ rather than ℂ*. Recorded here as a lemma rather than left as prose in that file: it is the one step of that argument that is a computation.
