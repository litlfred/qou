    QOU.Mathlib.HomotopyLifting — a fibration predicate Mathlib does not have #

    Bean qou-de7r, item 2 of qou-pan0. QOU/Mathlib/LongExactSequence.lean supplies the algebraic long exact sequence. This file and QOU/Mathlib/HomotopyGroupFunctor.lean / QOU/Mathlib/FibrationExactness.lean supply the beginning of the topological side that would feed it.

    What Mathlib has, checked rather than guessed #

    The pinned Mathlib (f897ebcf, Lean 4.24.0) was surveyed by enumerating the declarations each olean actually contains, not by recalling names:

    • Mathlib.Topology.Homotopy.HomotopyGroup — GenLoop, HomotopyGroup, Cube.boundary, the Group/CommGroup instances. So π_n exists.
    • Mathlib.Topology.Homotopy.Basic — ContinuousMap.Homotopy, HomotopyWith, HomotopyRel, and HomotopicRel.comp_continuousMap.
    • Mathlib.Topology.Homotopy.Lifting — lifting theorems for covering maps and local homeomorphisms only (IsCoveringMap.liftHomotopy, IsCoveringMap.liftPath, IsLocalHomeomorph.monodromy_theorem). A covering map is a fibration with discrete fibres, and its lifts are unique; every proof there runs on that uniqueness, so none of it generalises.

    There is no fibration. Fibration appears in the olean tree only in Order/UpperLower/Fibration (an order-theoretic notion) and in the abstract CategoryTheory model-category / simplicial-set files, none of which is connected to HomotopyGroup on topological spaces. So the predicate below is new, and it is written to match how HomotopyGroup is built — over the cubes N → I — because a predicate that does not connect to those is worthless here.

    What is defined #

    HasHomotopyLiftingProperty p Y is the ordinary HLP: a homotopy into B indexed by Y, together with a lift of its start, extends to a homotopy into E. IsHurewiczFibration quantifies over all Y; IsSerreFibration over the cubes, which is the version π_n needs.

    HasHomotopyLiftingPropertyRel p Y A is the stationary relative HLP: if the homotopy downstairs never moves on A, the lift can be chosen never to move on A either. This is the special case of the homotopy lifting property for the pair (Y, A) in which the prescribed lift over A × I is constant — and it is the only case the exactness argument in FibrationExactness.lean uses.

    The gap this file does not close #

    Classically the two are equivalent for cubes: (Iⁿ × I, Iⁿ × {0} ∪ ∂Iⁿ × I) is homeomorphic to (Iⁿ × I, Iⁿ × {0}), so a Serre fibration automatically has the relative property with respect to (Iⁿ, ∂Iⁿ). That homeomorphism — radial projection from a point above the cube — is a genuine piece of geometry and is not proved here. It is named as SerreCubePairBridge so that the hole has an address rather than being silently absorbed: nothing in this development proves it, and nothing in this development uses it in a proof.

    Consequently the exactness theorem downstream takes HasHomotopyLiftingPropertyRel as a hypothesis directly. That hypothesis is not vacuous: Prod.hasHomotopyLiftingPropertyRel_fst proves it, for every Y and every A, for the projection of a trivial bundle B × F → B.

    def QOU.Mathlib.HomotopyLifting.HasHomotopyLiftingProperty {E : Type u} {B : Type v} [TopologicalSpace E] [TopologicalSpace B] (p : C(E, B)) (Y : Type w) [TopologicalSpace Y] :
    Prop

    The homotopy lifting property of p : E → B with respect to a space Y.

    Given a homotopy H : I × Y → B and a map f : Y → E lifting its start, there is a homotopy H' : I × Y → E lifting all of H and starting at f.

    The argument order I × Y (rather than Y × I) is deliberate: it is the order ContinuousMap.Homotopy uses, so a Mathlib homotopy can be fed to this predicate with no shuffling. Nothing about the definition depends on it, and everything about the usability does.

      Prop

      The stationary relative homotopy lifting property with respect to a pair (Y, A).

      Same data as HasHomotopyLiftingProperty, with one hypothesis and one conclusion added: if H is constant in t on A, the lift may be chosen constant in t on A too.

      This is what the fibration exactness argument needs and the plain HLP does not give. Lifting a nullhomotopy of p ∘ g produces a map Iⁿ → E into the fibre, but with no control on ∂Iⁿ the result is not a based sphere at all — the boundary drifts away from the basepoint along the lifted homotopy, and the class it would represent does not exist. Pinning the boundary is not a convenience; without it there is no theorem.

            HasHomotopyLiftingPropertyRel ContinuousMap.fst Y A

            A trivial bundle is a fibration, and it satisfies the relative property as well, for every pair (Y, A).

            This is the theorem that makes everything downstream non-vacuous. The relative HLP is taken as a hypothesis by the exactness result, and a hypothesis nothing satisfies proves nothing; here it is discharged outright. The lift is the only available one — carry the homotopy in the base coordinate and hold the fibre coordinate fixed at whatever f chose — and holding the fibre coordinate fixed is exactly why the boundary does not drift.

            def QOU.Mathlib.HomotopyLifting.SerreCubePairBridge {E : Type u} {B : Type v} [TopologicalSpace E] [TopologicalSpace B] (p : C(E, B)) :
            Prop

            The unproved bridge, stated so that it has a name.

            For a fibration, the plain homotopy lifting property against a cube implies the stationary relative property against that cube and its boundary. The proof is the pair homeomorphism (Iⁿ × I, Iⁿ × {0} ∪ ∂Iⁿ × I) ≅ (Iⁿ × I, Iⁿ × {0}) obtained by projecting radially from a point above the cube (Hatcher, §4.2). It is real geometry, it is not proved here, and no result in this development invokes it — FibrationExactness assumes the relative property directly instead.

            Recorded as a Prop-valued definition rather than a sorryed theorem on purpose: a sorry would leak sorryAx into every downstream axiom check and make the honest results indistinguishable from the assumed one.
