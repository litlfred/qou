      The q-bridge system #

      source
      structure QOU.Mathlib.QAlienBridge.QBridgeSystem (R : Type u_4) (A : Type u_5) (Ω : Type u_6) [CommSemiring R] [CommRing A] [Algebra R A] :
      Type (max (max u_4 u_5) u_6)

      A q-native bridge system: a dilation automorphism σ, a family of alien derivations Δ_ω, a multiplicative weight c_ω, a unipotent correction, and the relation

      σ ∘ Δ_ω ∘ σ⁻¹ = c_ω · Δ_ω + N_ω.

      The relation is a field, i.e. a hypothesis — nothing in the data determines it, and axiomatising it would not establish it (AGENTS.md §"Axiomatization is NOT a Proof").

      The unipotent field is what makes this the general "combined" form: the adjoint action of σ on the Δ_ω need not be diagonalisable, and the Levi decomposition of a local q-difference Galois group is precisely a torus of multiplicative characters extended by a unipotent part ([vanderput2006] §5 (1), (5); the Δ_ω themselves are [ramis-sauloy2006] Thm 3.5). Setting unipotent = 0 (IsSemisimple) recovers the pure multiplicative case.

      • dil : A ≃ₐ[R] A

        The dilation automorphism σ (classically x ↦ qx).

      • alien : Ω → Derivation R A A

        The alien derivation at the singularity ω.

      • weight : Ω → R

        The multiplicative weight — the semisimple part of the adjoint action.

      • unipotent : Ω → Derivation R A A

        The nilpotent part of the adjoint action (zero in the diagonalisable case).

      • bridge (ω : Ω) (f : A) : (conjDeriv self.dil (self.alien ω)) f = self.weight ω • (self.alien ω) f + (self.unipotent ω) f

          The hypothesis has teeth. If σ commutes with Δ_ω (the conjugation is trivial) and the system is semisimple, then every value of Δ_ω is fixed by scaling by c_ω. So whenever c_ω fixes nothing but 0, commutation forces Δ_ω = 0.

          The q-Euler model, and the exact additive ↔ multiplicative link #

          source
          noncomputable def QOU.Mathlib.QAlienBridge.qEulerSystem {R : Type u_1} {A : Type u_2} [CommSemiring R] [CommRing A] [Algebra R A] (σ : A ≃ₐ[R] A) (E : Derivation R A A) {t : A} {c : R} (hσt : σ t = c • t) (hcomm : ∀ (g : A), σ (E g) = E (σ g)) :
          QBridgeSystem R A ℕ

          The q-Euler bridge system. Given a dilation σ with σ t = c · t and a derivation E commuting with σ (the Euler / degree operator), the family Δ_n := tⁿ·E satisfies the q-bridge with multiplicative weight cⁿ and no unipotent part.

          The weight comes out multiplicative for a completely explicit reason: σ is an algebra map, so it passes through the product tⁿ · E(−) and multiplies by σ(t)ⁿ = cⁿ. A derivation would instead have differentiated the product and produced n additively.

            The answer, stated as one theorem. Suppose the same (A, E, t) carries both structures — E t = t (so AlienBridge.eulerSystem applies) and σ t = c·t with σ commuting with E (so qEulerSystem applies). Then:

            • the two systems have the same alien family Δ_n = tⁿ·E, indexed by the same n;
            • the classical weight is n — additive;
            • the q-weight is cⁿ — multiplicative.

            So additive and multiplicative are not rival conventions and not a modelling choice. There is one grading, indexed by n; the classical weight is that index, and the q-weight is the multiplicative character c^{(−)} evaluated on it. Which one you see is determined by whether the operator you conjugate by is a derivation (differentiates the product ⇒ n appears additively) or an automorphism (passes through the product ⇒ cⁿ appears multiplicatively).

            The general case is neither: QBridgeSystem carries a unipotent field, and qweight_shift shows the eigenvalue is then multiplied by the semisimple part and shifted by the nilpotent one — a Jordan block, the matrix-representation form. Both models here are semisimple; a mixed example needs the q-logarithm, which is not built.

                  A genuinely MIXED model — the unipotent block #

                  Both models above are semisimple, which would leave `QBridgeSystem`'s
`unipotent` field untested and the "combined" case decorative.  This section
exhibits a system where the semisimple part is trivial (`c = 1`) and the
whole content is unipotent.

The hypothesis `σ t = t + 1` is not invented for the purpose: it is the
defining property of the **q-logarithm** `ℓ_q`, for which `σ_q ℓ_q = ℓ_q + 1`.
In q-difference Galois theory `ℓ_q` generates exactly the unipotent part of
the local Galois group, the part the torus of characters `qⁿ` is extended by.
So this is the unipotent block, not a toy.

                        The twist: why the index set must be E_q, proved rather than asserted #

                        `QBridgeSystem` above assumes `σ ∘ Δ_ω ∘ σ⁻¹` is a multiple of **`Δ_ω` itself**
— the *same* index.  For q-difference equations that is an over-restriction,
and the reason is geometric: the dilation acts on the Borel plane too, so it
moves a singularity at `ω` to one at `qω`.  The honest general form carries a
**shift** on the index set,

`σ ∘ Δ_ω ∘ σ⁻¹  =  c_ω · Δ_{shift ω}  +  N_ω`,

and `QBridgeSystem` is the `shift = id` case (`ofUntwisted` / `untwist`).

Two facts make this precise, and both are theorems here rather than prose:

* `shift_mul_eq_id_iff` — on a group of singularity locations with
  `shift ω = q·ω`, the shift is the identity **iff `q = 1`**.  So for a genuine
  dilation the untwisted form is simply unavailable on `ℂ*`.
* `untwist` — the twist disappears exactly when the alien family is invariant
  under the shift, i.e. **constant along q-spirals**, i.e. factors through
  `ℂ*/q^ℤ = E_q`.

Together: `E_q` is not a convenient choice of base, it is forced — it is
precisely the quotient on which the bridge relation can be index-preserving.
That is independent corroboration of the corpus's `E_q` framing, where the
third singular site `x = q⁻²` was found to be the identity orbit `[q⁻²] = [1]`
([`2026-08-08-rsz-c1-infinity-route-closed.md`](../../../../../docs/audits/2026-08-08-rsz-c1-infinity-route-closed.md)).

`conjDeriv_iterate` is the content of the twist when it is *not* removed:
iterating the conjugation walks the orbit and **accumulates the product** of
the weights along it.
