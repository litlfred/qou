    noncomputable def MvPolynomial.rothDegrees {m : ℕ} (H : Fin m → ℕ) (N : ℕ) :
    Fin m → ℕ

    The degree vector Roth's setup produces from a height vector.

    Every H h ^ d h is pinned to the same scale N — Mahler's normalisation r_h·log H_h ≈ const, written without logarithms. The first index takes the floor and the rest the ceiling, which is what makes clause 2 (H₁^{r₁} smallest) hold on the nose rather than up to rounding.

      Γ_m, produced. Given heights that grow rapidly enough and a common scale N, the normalised degrees satisfy every clause of RothGammaFull.

      ⚠ This is the first construction of Γ_m in the arc that is not a transport (rename_equiv), a descent (rothGammaFull_descend), or a hand-built literal. Clauses 1 and 2 come from the normalisation; 3a, 3b and 4 are conditions on F and b that the caller supplies, because they constrain data this construction does not choose.

      Γ_3 at t = 1/2, produced #

      The arc's Γ witnesses were all at m = 2 and all hand-built, and the one attached to index_le_rothBound sat at t = 1, where the bound it exhibits is trivial. This one is at m = 3 and t = 1/2, and every clause comes out of rothGammaFull_of_heights rather than out of a structure literal.

      The numbers are forced, not chosen. Clause 3a reads q·(m−1)m(2m+1) ≤ F·p, which at m = 3, p = 1, q = 2 is 84 ≤ F; clause 3b then demands 2^84 ≤ H₁. The rapid-growth hypothesis H_j^{2q} ≤ H_k^p is H_j^4 ≤ H_k, so the heights are

      H = (2^84, 2^336, 2^1344),   N = 2^6720,   d = (80, 21, 6)

      and N is a common power of all three heights, which is what makes the three Nat.logs exact. ⚠ d is decreasing and H^d is increasing — 2^6720 < 2^7056 < 2^8064 — which is clauses 1 and 2 pulling in opposite directions and both holding. That is the joint satisfiability that had never been exhibited above m = 2.

          The loop, closed #

          index_le_rothBound is Roth's Lemma; gamma_three is a Γ_3 nothing assumed. Composing them is the first statement in the arc where both sides are discharged: the hypothesis is produced from height data, and the conclusion is a bound on an actual index.

          At m = 3 the constant is rothConstant 3 = 2⁴ − 3 = 13, so the bound reads Θ⁴ ≤ 13⁴ · ½, i.e. Θ ≤ 13/2^{1/4} ≈ 10.9.

          ⚠ That number is not small, and saying so matters more than the theorem reads. Θ_m ≤ m holds outright (IsRothTheta.le_card), so at m = 3 the trivial bound is 3 and this one is 10.9: Roth's Lemma is weaker than the trivial bound here. It has to be — the strength is asymptotic in t, and t = ½ is nowhere near the regime the theorem is for. What this witness establishes is that the machine runs end to end on data it produced itself, not that the number it emits is useful at these parameters. Both are worth having; only the first was missing.
