    Grouping on the other side — and why the length depends on which #

    exists_separated_representation_monomial groups the monomials of P by their x-part, so the representation has one term per x-monomial. Mahler's bound is n ≤ r_m + 1: one term per y-monomial, with y the single split-off variable. Those are wildly different counts — ∏_{h<m}(r_h+1) against r_m+1 — and only the second is what the recursion's error term t = r/D₁ can afford.

    ⚠ So the side you group on is not a presentation choice. The results below mirror the grouping and, unlike the originals, state the index set explicitly rather than existentially — the whole point is to count it.

    The mirror of exists_separated_representation_monomial: group by the y-part.

    Now the right factors are bare monomials and the left ones collect the coefficients, so the count is the number of distinct y-parts.

    The grouped coefficients inherit P's x-degrees. φ j is a sum of monomials whose exponents are the x-parts of P's own support, so every exponent occurring in φ j occurs in P.

    ⚠⚠ That clause was missing, and without it hres cannot be discharged at all.     Factor degrees are bounded by the polynomial's #

    ⚠ Family-uniform data, which is what hres needs. The induction step's input quantifies over the families realising a separated representation, so every hypothesis it carries must be statable before the family is known. The degree bound below is: it bounds the factors' degrees by P's own, which does not vary.

    And it is false without the independence hypothesis. Take P = x₀y₀ and the length-2 representation φ = ![x₀ + x₀⁵, −x₀⁵], ψ = ![y₀, y₀]: the sum telescopes back to P, but φ 0 has degree 5. What rules that out is ψ being dependent — and after the tenth correction hres already excludes dependent families, since a dependent family's Wronskians all vanish.

    degreeOf j (φ k) ≤ degreeOf (Sum.inl j) P

    The factors of a separated representation have degrees bounded by the polynomial's, provided the other family is linearly independent.

    The proof is the disjointness identity again: a non-zero coefficient of φ k gives a non-zero vector c, independence of ψ makes ∑ cₗψₗ non-zero, and any non-zero coefficient of that is a coefficient of P at a multi-index whose σ-part is the one we started with.

    Mahler's n ≤ r_m + 1. For a single split-off variable of degree at most d, a minimum separated representation has at most d + 1 terms.

    This is the bound the recursion's row cost needs: with t = r/D₁ and r ≤ d + 1, the growth condition (RothGrowth, which makes the split-off variable's degree the smallest) turns t into something of order 1/ω.

    Next #

    exists_separated_representation_indep is what the induction step wants: Fin r-indexed, both families independent. exists_separated_representation_indep_le_degreeOf adds Mahler's length bound n ≤ r_m + 1, which is what the recursion's row cost t = r/D₁ needs.

    ⚠ The length bound is entirely a matter of which side the grouping is on, and the two sides are not close: the x-side gives one term per x-monomial (a product of degrees), the y-side one term per y-monomial (for a single split-off variable, d + 1). Both were easy; only one is usable.

    The induction step can now be fed: exists_separated_representation_indep produces the representation, det_separated_wronskian factors its Wronskian, BoundedOrderWronskian turns the two independences into non-vanishing Wronskians, and RothInductionStep splits the index and the height.

    ⚠ The other entry the audit came back empty on is ω(m, ε) — the growth constant in RothGrowth. Nothing supplies or constrains it, and nothing can until the recursion is written, because ω is whatever the recursion's error terms force it to be. That is the one open item that is genuinely mathematical rather than mechanical.
