    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedCornersAlong — insertion always creates a corner (brick 15) #

    Brick 14 (SchenstedWordSurjectivity) proved that a reverse run rebuilds the tableau it was peeled from, under two hypotheses about the positions:

    • CornersAlong T is — every step removes a corner of the tableau as it stands at that step;
    • (uninsertSteps T is).1 = [] — the run empties the tableau.

    Both were stated as the interface to the recording tableau, and left to be discharged one level up. This file discharges both for every insertion-built run, which is what shows the hypotheses are satisfiable rather than an empty class.

    The load-bearing fact turns out not to be about the recording tableau at all:

    insert_isCorner — the cell insert T x adds is always a corner of the result.

    Why: if the new cell lands at the end of row i (making it length L), then before the insertion row i had length L - 1, and ColStrict's length half gave row i + 1 at most that. So row i + 1 is at most L - 1 < L — a corner. The row below being as long would have violated the Young shape of the tableau we started from.

    From there, cornersAlong_rowIndices follows by induction, with brick 3's uninsertSteps_foldl supplying the bridge: after undoing the later steps, the tableau standing at step k is exactly the tableau after k + 1 insertions, so the corner condition at that step is insert_isCorner.

    What this does and does not settle #

    It settles that brick 14's hypotheses hold for every P that arises from a word — which is the anti-vacuity check, and it makes insertWord_uninsertSteps applicable rather than merely true.

    It does not settle surjectivity of rskPair. That needs CornersAlong P is for an arbitrary standard pair (P, Q), where is is read off Q rather than off an insertion history. The argument there is different — the cell carrying the largest remaining entry of a standard tableau is a corner, because a cell below it would carry a larger one — and it is a later brick of epic qou-nrex. Nothing here claims it, and RSKBurnsideHolds for general n stays open.

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.insert_isCorner (T : List (List ℕ)) :
    ColStrict T → ∀ (x : ℕ), IsCorner (insert T x) (newRowIndex T x)

    Insertion always creates a corner. The cell insert T x adds sits at the end of its row with the row below strictly shorter.

    The no-bump case is the whole content: the row grows from L - 1 to L, and ColStrict had already bounded the row below by L - 1. Had the row below been as long, the tableau we started from would not have been a Young shape. The bumping case is the recursion.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.cornersAlong_rowIndices (w : List ℕ) (T : List (List ℕ)) :
    RowsSorted T → ColStrict T → NoEmptyRows T → CornersAlong (List.foldl insert T w) (rowIndices T w)

    Every insertion-built run removes corners. The corner condition at step k is checked against the tableau after the later steps are undone, which brick 3's uninsertSteps_foldl says is the tableau after k + 1 insertions — so it is exactly insert_isCorner.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.insertWord_uninsertSteps_insertWord (w : List ℕ) :
    insertWord (uninsertSteps (insertWord w) (rowIndices [] w)).2 = insertWord w

    Brick 14's hypotheses are satisfiable. Both discharged, for every word.

    The conclusion is weaker than brick 3's uninsertSteps_insertWord (which pins the recovered word on the nose, not merely its insertion tableau), and that is the point: this is a consistency check on brick 14, not a new result about insertWord. What it buys is that insertWord_uninsertSteps is a theorem with inhabited hypotheses.
