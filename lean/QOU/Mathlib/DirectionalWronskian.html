      The directional generalized Wronskian: det ((Dᶜ)ⁱ fⱼ).

      Its rows have order 0, 1, …, m-1 by construction, which is exactly the bound Roth's lemma asks for — the bound is inherited from Dᶜ rather than engineered.

        The base point drops out of the problem: what is left to decide is a question about the direction alone.

        What remains for qou-sq3h, in order:

        1. A good direction exists. f independent over K ⟹ ∃ c, dirGenWronskian c f ≠ 0. By the iff above this is equivalent to finding one line whose restrictions stay independent. ⚠ Genuinely open: a genericity statement over the space of directions, and the naive per-element argument does not close it because span f is infinite.
        2. Multilinear expansion. (Dᶜ)ⁱ is a linear combination of monomial operators of order i; expanding det over those exhibits a single bounded-order mvGenWronskian. The useful direction is the contrapositive — if every bounded-order generalized Wronskian vanishes then dirGenWronskian c f = 0 for every c — which needs only multilinearity of det in its rows, not a multinomial theorem.
