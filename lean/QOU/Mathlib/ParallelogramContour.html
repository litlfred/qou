      Cauchy for the parallelogram — proved, not axiomatised #

      This was an axiom, and the comment above it read:

      BLOCKED: Proving Cauchy-Goursat for parallelograms from scratch in Lean 4 is currently infeasible. Mathlib only has integral_boundary_rect_eq_zero_of_differentiable_on_off_countable which applies strictly to axis-aligned rectangles. We cannot use an affine transformation to pull back a generic parallelogram to an axis-aligned rectangle because such a transformation is ℝ-linear, not ℂ-linear … the standard proof involves decomposing the parallelogram into two triangles and proving Goursat's lemma for triangles. However, Mathlib currently completely lacks integrals over triangles and Goursat's lemma for simplices.

      Every sentence of that is true and the conclusion does not follow, because it answers a harder question than the one the statement asks. Goursat is what you need to prove Cauchy from scratch for a merely-differentiable function. The 2026-08-24 soundness fix had already added Differentiable ℂ f — entire — and an entire function has a primitive. With a primitive the contour integral is a telescoping sum and the shape of the contour is irrelevant: no triangles, no Goursat, no rectangles, no affine pullback.

      Concretely, if F' = f then each edge integrates by the fundamental theorem of calculus,

      ∫₀¹ ω · f(a + tω) dt = F(a + ω) − F(a),

      and the four edges of the parallelogram telescope:

      [F(z₀+ω₁) − F(z₀)] + [F(z₀+ω₁+ω₂) − F(z₀+ω₁)] − [F(z₀+ω₂+ω₁) − F(z₀+ω₂)] − [F(z₀+ω₂) − F(z₀)] = 0.

      The primitive comes from Mathlib's DifferentiableOn.isExactOn_ball (Morera for a disk, Mathlib/Analysis/Complex/HasPrimitives.lean), applied on any ball large enough to contain the four edges — ‖z₀‖ + ‖ω₁‖ + ‖ω₂‖ + 1 does.

      The one thing genuinely given up is sharpness, and it was given up in 2026-08-24 rather than here: Cauchy needs only differentiability on a neighbourhood of the closed parallelogram, and Differentiable is strictly stronger. Sharpening the hypothesis still wants Goursat, so that half of the comment above stands and is left open.
