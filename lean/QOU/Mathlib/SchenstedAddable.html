    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedAddable — addable-cell sequences peel back as corners (brick 20) #

    Brick 19 reduced the P-side run condition to ShapeCornersAlong, a statement about shapes alone. What remains for surjectivity is to produce that condition from a standard recording tableau. This file does the half of it that needs no tableau at all.

    A standard tableau is the same data as a sequence of addable cells: entry 0 goes somewhere addable in the empty shape, entry 1 somewhere addable in what that leaves, and so on. Peeled back in reverse, such a sequence is exactly a corner sequence:

    shapeCornersAlong_addCellSteps — if is adds cells addably to l, then ShapeCornersAlong (addCellSteps l is) is, and peeling those corners returns l.

    Everything here is on List ℕ shapes — no tableau, no Q, no insertion. That is the point: it isolates the combinatorial content, leaving brick 21 with only the bridge "a standard Q gives an addable sequence".

    The three single-step facts #

    • youngShape_addCell — adding an addable cell keeps the shape weakly decreasing and positive;
    • isCorner_addCell — the added cell is a corner of the result. This is the shape-level mirror of brick 15's insert_isCorner, which said the same for Schensted insertion, and it has the same one-line reason: the row below was already bounded by the old length.
    • removeCorner_addCell — and peeling it returns the shape you started from.

    The third is what makes the run-level statement an exact inverse rather than merely a corner condition, and it is why removeCornerSteps … = l falls out of the same induction.

    Scope #

    Shapes only. Producing an AddableAlong sequence from a standard recording tableau is brick 21; nothing here claims it, nothing here claims rskPair is bijective, and RSKBurnsideHolds for general n stays open (proved for n ≤ 12 by an unrelated route, PartitionsOfBridge.rskBurnsideHolds_le12, with no RSK correspondence in it).

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean.
