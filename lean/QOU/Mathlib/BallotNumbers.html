    Ballot numbers m_j(n) = C(n,j) − C(n,j−1) #

    Bean qou-0cyu, items G1/G2/G5. Mathlib infrastructure: at the pinned rev Mathlib has Nat.choose and catalan but no ballot numbers, no pattern-avoidance theory, and no RSK. This file supplies the piece that both open identities need.

    Why this number #

    m_j(n) is the number of standard Young tableaux of the two-row shape (n − j, j). It is the multiplicity with which the irreducible (n − j, j) occurs in the n-fold tensor power of the 2-dimensional representation, and the two open statements in qou-0cyu are both statements about it:

    • G2 [2]_qⁿ = Σ_j m_j(n) · [n − 2j + 1]_q — the q-dimension of V^⊗n.
    • G5 Σ_j m_j(n)² = catalan n — by RSK, "321-avoiding permutations are Catalan". Proved for general n below (ballot_sq_sum_catalan), and not by RSK: see the section heading before choose_sq_sum for why the induction route does not close and what replaces it.

    The engine, and why it is the whole content #

    Everything reduces to one recursion:

    m_{j+1}(n+1) = m_{j+1}(n) + m_j(n)

    This is Pascal applied twice, and it is exactly the shape needed to run the induction for G2 against the q-integer three-term recursion [m+2] + [m] = [2]·[m+1] already proved in QOU.QBeta.TemperleyLiebTower.qint_three_term. Matching the two recursions term by term is what makes G2 an induction rather than a computation:

    [2]ⁿ⁺¹ = [2]·Σ_j m_j(n)[n−2j+1] = Σ_j m_j(n)([n−2j+2] + [n−2j])

    and collecting the coefficient of [n−2j+2] on the right gives m_j(n) + m_{j−1}(n), which ballot_succ_succ says is m_j(n+1). The two recursions are the same recursion.

    Indexing #

    ballot n 0 = 1 by definition rather than by the formula, because the formula would need C(n, −1) = 0 and ℕ subtraction silently gives C(n, 0) instead — the classic off-by-one that makes m_0 come out 0 rather than 1. Values are ℤ so that m_j(n) can be written as a difference without truncation, even though every value in range is a nonnegative integer.

    def QOU.Mathlib.ballot (n : ℕ) :
    ℕ → ℤ

    Ballot number m_j(n) = C(n,j) − C(n,j−1), the number of standard Young tableaux of shape (n − j, j).

    The j = 0 case is given directly rather than by the difference: C(n, 0−1) would be C(n, 0) under ℕ subtraction, making m_0(n) = 0 instead of 1.

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.ballot_succ_succ (n j : ℕ) :
      ballot (n + 1) (j + 1) = ballot n (j + 1) + ballot n j

      The ballot recursion — the engine. m_{j+1}(n+1) = m_{j+1}(n) + m_j(n).

      Two cases, each Pascal. The j = 0 case is the one that would break under a naive uniform definition: m_1(n+1) = n while m_1(n) + m_0(n) = (C(n,1) − 1) + 1, and those agree only because m_0 was pinned to 1 rather than computed.

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.ballot_boundary_zero (j : ℕ) :
      ballot (2 * j + 1) (j + 1) = 0

      The boundary vanishing: m_{j+1}(2j+1) = 0.

      C(2j+1, j+1) = C(2j+1, j) by binomial symmetry, so the ballot number just past the diagonal is zero. Small, and it is precisely what makes G2's induction close in the even case.

      Why it is the crux. Running [2]ⁿ⁺¹ = [2]·Σ_j m_j(n)[n−2j+1] through the three-term recursion and reindexing produces a sum over j = 0 … ⌊n/2⌋ + 1, while the target for n+1 runs to ⌊(n+1)/2⌋. Those agree when n is odd. When n is even, say n = 2m, the derived sum has one term too many — at j = m + 1, with coefficient m_{m+1}(2m+1). This lemma says that coefficient is 0, so the extra term disappears and the two sums coincide.

      So the parity split in G2 is not an artefact of a clumsy index choice; it is real, and this is the fact that resolves it.

      ballot 3 0 ^ 2 + ballot 3 1 ^ 2 = ↑(catalan 3) ∧ ballot 4 0 ^ 2 + ballot 4 1 ^ 2 + ballot 4 2 ^ 2 = ↑(catalan 4) ∧ ballot 5 0 ^ 2 + ballot 5 1 ^ 2 + ballot 5 2 ^ 2 = ↑(catalan 5)

      G5 for general n: Σ_j m_j(n)² = catalan n #

      Bean qou-0cyu item G5, closed. The bean priced this as needing RSK ("321-avoiding permutations are Catalan"), noted that Mathlib has neither RSK nor pattern avoidance, and offered two routes: (a) formalise enough RSK — LARGE; (b) a direct induction on ballot_succ_succ.

      Neither is what closes it. Route (b) does not in fact close: the ballot recursion m_{j+1}(n+1) = m_{j+1}(n) + m_j(n) is linear, while the Catalan recursion C_{n+1} = Σ_i C_i C_{n-i} is a convolution, and squaring the former produces cross terms 2 Σ_j m_j m_{j-1} that the latter has no place for. The two recursions do not match, which is exactly why G2 (linear in the m_j) succumbs to induction and G5 does not.

      What closes it is a third route with no induction at all: reflection plus Vandermonde. The three moving parts:

      1. Run the sum over the full range k = 0 … n+1 rather than k = 0 … ⌊n/2⌋. Over the full range the summand is a difference of two binomials, and Vandermonde evaluates each of the three resulting sums in closed form: Σ_k m_k(n)² = 2·C(2n,n) − 2·C(2n,n+1) (ballot_sq_sum_full).

      2. catalan n = C(2n,n) − C(2n,n+1) (catalan_eq_choose_sub), from Mathlib's succ_mul_catalan_eq_centralBinom together with Nat.choose_succ_right_eq, by cancelling n+1.

      3. Fold the full range in half. m_{n+1−k}(n) = −m_k(n) (ballot_reflect), so the squares are symmetric about k = (n+1)/2 and the full sum is exactly twice the half sum (ballot_sq_sum_fold). The fold has a fixed point precisely when n is odd, and there the summand vanishes — which is ballot_boundary_zero above, already proved for G2's parity split. The same lemma resolves both.

      So G5 is a finite binomial identity, not a combinatorial bijection, and every ingredient is in Mathlib at the pinned rev. Nothing here needs a tableau.

      Why this matters downstream. The Gram form of the Markov trace is diagonal in the Wedderburn basis with entries proportional to y_λ, and y_λ = 0 ↔ ℓ(λ) > r at Wenzl level r. Convention D is level r = 2, so

      dim radical = n! − Σ_{ℓ(λ) ≤ 2} (dim V_λ)²

      and the two-row Specht dimension is f^{(n-k,k)} = C(n,k) − C(n,k−1) = m_k(n). ballot_sq_sum_catalan therefore turns corank(n) = n! − catalan n from four measured values (n = 3,4,5,6 → 1, 10, 78, 588) into a general-n statement. See QOU.QBeta.MarkovGramDegenerate for the wiring, and computations/probes/two_row_ballot_catalan_reduction.py for the numerical check of the reduction (n ≤ 14, hook length formula against full partition enumeration).

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.choose_sq_sum (n : ℕ) :
      ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (n + 1), n.choose k * n.choose k = (2 * n).choose n

      Vandermonde, range form. Σ_{k ≤ n} C(n,k)² = C(2n,n).

      Nat.add_choose_eq gives this over Finset.antidiagonal; the work is transporting it to Finset.range and applying Nat.choose_symm to turn C(n, n−k) into C(n,k).

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.choose_mul_succ_sum (n : ℕ) :
      ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (n + 1), n.choose (j + 1) * n.choose j = (2 * n).choose (n + 1)

      Vandermonde, off-diagonal range form. Σ_{j ≤ n} C(n,j+1)·C(n,j) = C(2n,n+1).

      Same transport as choose_sq_sum, with one extra step: the k = 0 term of the antidiagonal sum is C(n,0)·C(n,n+1) = 0, and peeling it is what shifts the index so that C(n, n+1−k) becomes C(n, j) against C(n, j+1).

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.choose_sq_sum_shift (n : ℕ) :
      ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (n + 1), n.choose (j + 1) * n.choose (j + 1) + 1 = (2 * n).choose n

      The shifted square-sum, Σ_{j ≤ n} C(n,j+1)² + 1 = C(2n,n).

      Both sides are the same range-(n+2) sum read two ways: peeling the last term (C(n,n+1)² = 0) reduces it to choose_sq_sum, and peeling the first (C(n,0)² = 1) reduces it to the shifted sum. Stated in ℕ with the + 1 on the left so that no truncated subtraction appears.

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.catalan_eq_choose_sub (n : ℕ) :
      ↑(catalan n) = ↑((2 * n).choose n) - ↑((2 * n).choose (n + 1))

      catalan n = C(2n,n) − C(2n,n+1), over ℤ.

      Written with C(2n, n+1) rather than the more familiar C(2n, n−1). The two are equal by binomial symmetry, but n+1 involves no truncated ℕ subtraction, so this form is correct at n = 0 as well (1 − 0 = 1 = catalan 0) and needs no side condition. The n−1 spelling would give C(0,0) − C(0,0) = 0 there — the same off-by-one that ballot itself is defined to avoid.

      Proof: (n+1)·catalan n = C(2n,n) is Mathlib's succ_mul_catalan_eq_centralBinom, and (n+1)·C(2n,n+1) = n·C(2n,n) is Nat.choose_succ_right_eq at k = n. Subtract and cancel n+1 ≠ 0.

      theorem QOU.Mathlib.ballot_sq_sum_full (n : ℕ) :
      ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (n + 2), ballot n k ^ 2 = 2 * ↑(catalan n)

      The full-range square-sum: Σ_{k=0}^{n+1} m_k(n)² = 2·catalan n.

      This is the step that makes G5 a closed-form evaluation rather than an induction. Over the full range the summand expands as C(n,k+1)² − 2·C(n,k+1)·C(n,k) + C(n,k)², and Vandermonde evaluates all three pieces: C(2n,n) − 1, C(2n,n+1), and C(2n,n). The stray −1 is cancelled exactly by the k = 0 term m_0(n)² = 1, which is peeled off separately because m_0 is pinned rather than computed. What remains is 2·(C(2n,n) − C(2n,n+1)), and catalan_eq_choose_sub identifies that as 2·catalan n.

      source
      theorem QOU.Mathlib.ballot_reflect (n k : ℕ) (hk : k ≤ n + 1) :
      ballot n (n + 1 - k) = -ballot n k

      The reflection: m_{n+1−k}(n) = −m_k(n) for k ≤ n+1.

      Binomial symmetry, read on the difference: C(n,n+1−k) − C(n,n−k) is C(n,k−1) − C(n,k). The two boundary cases are the ones the uniform argument misses — k = 0 (where m_0 = 1 is pinned and m_{n+1} = −C(n,n) = −1 is computed) and k = n+1 (the mirror of the same pair) — so both are discharged separately.

      This is what halves the full-range sum. It also explains the shape of ballot_boundary_zero: an odd n puts a fixed point at k = (n+1)/2, where the reflection forces m_k = −m_k.

      source
      theorem QOU.Mathlib.ballot_sq_sum_fold (n : ℕ) :
      ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (n + 2), ballot n k ^ 2 = 2 * ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (n / 2 + 1), ballot n k ^ 2

      The fold: the full-range sum is exactly twice the half-range sum.

      Split range (n+2) at ⌊n/2⌋ + 1, reindex the tail to start at 0, reverse it with Finset.sum_range_reflect, and apply ballot_reflect term by term: the tail becomes Σ_{j < n+1−⌊n/2⌋} m_j(n)², a head sum again.

      The parity split is where the two cases genuinely differ, and it is not an artefact of the index choice:

      • n = 2m: the tail has length m + 1 = ⌊n/2⌋ + 1, matching the head exactly.
      • n = 2m+1: the tail has length m + 2, one term too long. The excess term is m_{m+1}(2m+1)², and ballot_boundary_zero says it vanishes.

      So both cases give tail = head, hence full = 2·head.

      source
      theorem QOU.Mathlib.ballot_sq_sum_catalan (n : ℕ) :
      ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (n / 2 + 1), ballot n k ^ 2 = ↑(catalan n)

      G5, general n: Σ_{j ≤ ⌊n/2⌋} m_j(n)² = catalan n.

      The two-row Specht dimensions squared sum to the Catalan number, for every n, against Mathlib's own catalan. Equivalently — though the proof never uses the equivalence — 321-avoiding permutations of n are counted by C_n.

      Combined with the Wedderburn diagonalisation of the Markov-trace Gram form and the Wenzl vanishing y_λ = 0 ↔ ℓ(λ) > 2 in convention D, this gives corank(n) = n! − catalan n for all n, superseding the four measured values. The corank statement itself remains conditional on the Wenzl weight identification, which is carried as a Lean class and is therefore invisible to #print axioms (bean qou-fpjy); this theorem is unconditional.
