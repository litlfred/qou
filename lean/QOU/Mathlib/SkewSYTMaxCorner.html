    QOU.Mathlib.SkewSYTMaxCorner — the erase-max step of the skew branching rule #

    Bean qou-b21r reduces RSKIdentitiesContext.branching_two_one to ONE open input: the skew branching rule

    f^{λ/μ} = ∑_{c a corner of λ with c ∉ μ}  f^{(λ ∖ c)/μ},

    i.e. "remove the largest entry of a skew standard Young tableau". Its other two inputs are in hand — QOU.Mathlib.upSum_eq is proved, and the transposition is the Finset.sum_comm manoeuvre pairTerm already performs there.

    This file supplies both directions' constructions, sorry-free — the corner extraction (exists_max_corner), the diagram-level consequences, and the insertion (skewInsertMax). It does not close the branching rule: proving the two are mutually inverse, and the sum itself, are still to do.

    Why the skew case is not a transliteration of the straight one #

    QOU/Mathlib/SYTEraseMax.lean can lean on StandardYoungTableau.bijOn, which hands over injectivity and surjectivity as separate directly-usable facts. SkewStandardYoungTableau instead certifies its filling with a Finset image equality

    (outer.cells \ inner.cells).image entry = Finset.range (outer.cells \ inner.cells).card

    from which both halves must be re-derived. skew_injOn, skew_mem_range and skew_exists_of_lt do that once, so the rest of the development can cite them the way the straight case cites bijOn.

    Note also the skew entries are 0-based (range n) where the straight ones are 1-based (Icc 1 n); index arithmetic does not port.

    The one genuinely new obligation #

    exists_max_corner must show the maximal cell is a corner of outer and lies outside inner — the second half has no counterpart in the straight case, and it is exactly what makes inner ≤ outer ∖ c hold, so that the smaller skew shape is well-formed.

    Pure combinatorics; nothing archimedean (§7c).

      theorem QOU.BraidKnot.YoungTableauStandard.exists_max_corner {outer inner : YoungDiagram} (T : SkewStandardYoungTableau outer inner) (n : ℕ) (hn : (skewCellsOf outer inner).card = n + 1) :
      ∃ c ∈ skewCellsOf outer inner, T.entry c.1 c.2 = n ∧ (c.1 + 1, c.2) ∉ outer ∧ (c.1, c.2 + 1) ∉ outer

      The maximal entry sits on a removable corner of outer that is outside inner.

      This is the erase-max step of the skew branching rule, and the one place its geometry differs from the straight case: the corner must additionally be shown to lie outside inner, which is exactly what makes outer ∖ c still contain inner.

      Both neighbours are ruled out the same way — if the neighbour were in outer it would be a skew cell (skew_right_mem / skew_down_mem), its entry would strictly exceed the maximum by row_strict / col_strict, and yet be bounded by it via skew_mem_range.

      The insert direction #

      skewInsertMax is the mirror of the erase step: put the new maximum at the corner and keep S elsewhere. Together with exists_max_corner these are the two halves of the branching bijection — the bijection PROOF (that they are mutually inverse) and the resulting sum are still to do.

      The content field is where the skew case costs more than the straight one: it must show image entry (skew outer inner) = range (N+1) from S.content : image = range N, by splitting the skew region as insert c (skew (outer ∖ c) inner). skewCellsOf is an abbrev, so it unfolds in the goal and rw cannot see it — the split is stated in the raw outer.cells \ inner.cells form for that reason.
