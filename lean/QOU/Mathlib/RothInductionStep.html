      ⚠⚠ This is where a problem with the previous docstring's claim becomes visible, and it is worth stating rather than hiding.

      r is the length of a minimal separated representation, so r ≤ (number of distinct x-monomials of P) ≤ d₁ + 1. Then r²/D₁ ≈ d₁, and the error term is of the same order as the trivial bound on the index itself. In that regime the inequality is true but useless.

      The classical proof does not have this problem, because it measures the Wronskian's index against the Wronskian's own degree vector — W has degree ≤ r·d₁ in x, so the weights used for index W are r·d rather than d. Every statement in this arc uses one degree vector d throughout, index_sepW included.

      ⚠ So: this inequality is proved and correct, and it is not established that it is the form Roth's lemma needs.

      ═══ ⚠ THIS CONFIRMS THE SIXTH CORRECTION RATHER THAN RESOLVING IT ═══

      The error term is now r/D₁ + r/D₂, down a factor r from the unscaled sum_index_le_index_sepW_concrete. It is still too large.

      r is the length of a minimal separated representation, so it is bounded by the number of distinct monomials in the split-off variable group — of order D₁. Then r/D₁ is of order 1, and Roth's lemma needs a conclusion of the form index ≤ m·ε with ε small. An error of order 1 swamps it. The r/D₂ term is worse: under the growth condition the two degree scales differ by a factor ω, so r/D₂ is of order ω.

      ⚠ Two things to be clear about in that estimate:

      • the bound r ≲ D₁ is not proved here — it is a plausible bound on the length of a minimal representation, and the magnitude argument rests on it;
      • the theorem below is correct regardless; what is in question is whether it is strong enough, which is a statement about constants, not about validity.

      So the honest status is unchanged from the sixth correction: the arc has a proved inequality that is not established to yield Roth's lemma, and applying the rescaling did not close that. Reconstructing the correct constant bookkeeping needs the actual argument, not more lemmas — every ingredient here is tight given only "operators have order at most r", so the loss is structural and must be repaired upstream of this statement.

      The square-root conclusion — what the sixth correction was missing #

      index_le_of_rescaled_factor_bounds above is the linear shape: it divides the summed inequality through by r and reads off θ ≤ εU + εV + C. The sixth correction recorded that this is not strong enough, and the reading of Mahler §13 says why: dividing through linearly throws away the fact that the per-column cost grows with the column.

      Mahler's argument instead splits on the cut-off N = ⌊(J(A) − t)·r⌋ + 1 — the column past which t + ν/r already exceeds the index, so the column contributes nothing. Keeping the truncation gives a sum of r terms of which only the first N are positive, and estimating that gives a bound on (θ − t)², not on θ − t. The square root is the whole gain.

      sq_index_le_of_det_bound is that conclusion, assembled from two halves proved separately:

      • sum_max_le_index_det (QOU/Mathlib/WronskianIndex.lean) — index side, WithTop ℚ, turns index_det_ge plus a growing per-column cost into ∑_{ν<r} max(0, θ−t−ν/rr);
      • RothArith.sq_mul_le_two_mul_of_sum_le (QOU/Mathlib/RothRecursionArith.lean) — pure rational arithmetic, turns a bound on that truncated sum into (θ−t)² · rr ≤ 2S.

      ⚠ Provenance caveat. The constants in RothRecursionArith were reconstructed from a partly illegible scan of Mahler §13, and the numeric bookkeeping should still be re-derived against a clean copy. The shape — truncated sum, square-root conclusion — is what the source shows and what is proved here.

      The two downstream constants this used to defer are no longer open: §11 gives ω = 1/t and §14 gives c_m = 2^{m+1} − 3 with conclusion Θ_m ≤ c_m·t^{2^{−(m−1)}}, both read from the page scans. ⚠ The value recorded here, 2^m − 3, was wrong.

      ⚠ This does not yet make the recursion run. It replaces a shape that was known to be too weak with one that is the right shape; whether the resulting constants close the induction is the next thing to check, and is a question about ω, not about these two halves.

      ⚠ Corrections eight and nine — the second one corrects the first #

      Correction 8, as recorded: §13's combined display, transcribed as θ ≤ t + 2·max S q, says nothing, since 2·max S q ≥ 2S ≥ S makes it a consequence of index_le_add_of_det_bound (θ ≤ t + S). max_form_implied_by_case_one below is that one-line proof and it stands.

      Correction 9: the transcription was wrong, and so was the conclusion drawn from it. The source's first branch is not J(W) but J(W)/n — the combined display reads

      J(A) < t + 2 max( J(W)/n , √(J(W)/r_m) ).

      ⚠⚠ Correction 14 corrects that line. §13's body has since been read, and the second branch is √(J(W)/n) — both branches over the same quantity (1/n)J(W), not r_m in the radicand:

      J(A) ≤ t + 2 max( (1/n)·J(W) , √( (1/n)·J(W) ) ).

      Everything correction 9 says about the 1/n stands; only the subscript in the radicand was wrong. But that one subscript is what put rr = r_m into index_le_two_mul_max_of_det_bound and so created its side condition S ≤ q²·rr — which at the source's rr = n is automatic. See RothLemmaStatement's correction-14 section for what collapses as a result.

      Correction 8 concluded from the vacuous form that "Mahler's max is an artefact of his case split" and that the strong branch should be extracted alone. That was wrong. With the 1/n restored:

      • the combined statement is not implied by θ ≤ t + S — 2S/n can be far smaller than S, and in the application it is: S is of size n·Φ_m, so 2S/n is of size Φ_m;
      • case (1) is genuinely useful, so the max is combining two useful bounds rather than hedging a useless one against a strong one;
      • and the two branches are complementary by construction, so the combination needs no side condition at all.

      The cost of the error was a real one: index_le_of_det_bound below carries the hypothesis S·rr ≤ r, introduced to force the strong branch because case (1) looked worthless. In the recursion's regime that condition reads Φ_m·r_m ≤ 1, which is false — Φ_m is small but r_m is a large degree. So the theorem is sound and its hypothesis is unsatisfiable where it matters. index_le_two_mul_max_of_det_bound replaces it and assumes nothing of the kind.

      ⚠ How this was caught: by taking the next step seriously — "check S·rr ≤ r against the parameter conditions" — instead of carrying it as a caveat. The check failed, and tracing why found the mis-transcription. A side condition nobody tests is indistinguishable from a bug.

      ⚠ And what did not catch it: lake build, #print axioms, and the non-vacuity witnesses. The witnesses were all at r = 1 or r = 2, where S·rr ≤ r is satisfiable; the hypothesis only becomes unsatisfiable in the asymptotic regime, which no finite witness reaches.

      §13 is complete. index_le_of_det_bound is its conclusion in a form the recursion can apply: given a matrix of derivatives of P whose per-column cost is t + j/rr, an upper bound S on the determinant's index with S·rr ≤ r, and a rational q ≥ √(S/rr), the index of P is at most t + 2q. Every hypothesis is checkable without knowing the index of P.

      What that leaves is not more inequalities. It is two constructions and one check:

      1. The operator family. §13's matrix is A_{μν} = Δ_{j_{μ1},…,j_{μ,m−1}, ν−1} with ∑_{h<m} j_{μh} ≤ μ−1 and order exactly ν−1 in the last variable. Building that as a D : Fin r → Fin r → List σ and proving hc for it is a construction the arc does not have. Note it is t + j/rr with the cost uniform in the row — which is what makes the permutation step in sum_max_le_index_det sound, so this is not a free choice.
      2. The determinant is non-zero. hS is unsatisfiable when det = 0 (index = ⊤), so the generalized Wronskian's non-vanishing — BoundedOrderWronskian's business — is a real precondition, not bookkeeping.
      3. S·rr ≤ r in the recursion's regime. With S = n·Φ_m, rr = r_m, r = n this is Φ_m · r_m ≤ 1. ⚠⚠ Correction 14: rr = r_m is itself the error — the source's radicand is (1/n)J(W), so rr = n, and the condition reads Φ_m ≤ Ψ_m, i.e. Θ₁ ≤ t, which §13 proves from Γ_m (RothLemmaStatement.theta_one_le_of_gamma).
