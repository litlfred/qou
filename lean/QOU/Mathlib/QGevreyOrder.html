    def QOU.Mathlib.QGevreyOrder.IsQGevreyOrder {K : Type u_1} [NormedField K] (q s : ℝ) (a : ℕ → K) :
    Prop

    The q-Gevrey order of a coefficient sequence a : ℕ → K, measured against a base q (q > 1). a has order s when ‖a n‖ ∼ C^n · q^{s·n²/2}, made precise as the limit of the leading Gaussian coefficient (2·log_q ‖a n‖)/n² → s. The atTop filter and the n² denominator wash out the C^n prefactor and the finitely many n = 0,1 terms.

      MI-2 — the analytic convolution (Cauchy-product) law #

      The q-Gevrey order under the CAUCHY product of power series
`(a ⋆ b)_n = ∑_{k≤n} a_k · b_{n-k}` is governed by the Newton polygon
(dominant-balance of the Gaussian exponents), NOT by the elementary
log-additivity used for the pointwise product above.

**CORRECTED LAW (MI-2).**  MI-1's stub asserted the additive law
`order(a ⋆ b) = s + t`.  That is WRONG.  The RSZ dominant balance of
`‖(a⋆b)_n‖ ∼ ∑_{k=0}^n q^{s k²/2} · q^{t (n-k)²/2}` is a Laplace/saddle sum:
writing `θ = k/n ∈ [0,1]`, the leading `n²`-coefficient is the ENVELOPE

    `E(s,t) := sup_{θ∈[0,1]} ( s·θ² + t·(1−θ)² )`.

The quadratic `g(θ) = s θ² + t(1−θ)²` has `g''(θ) = 2(s+t)`, so:

* **Convex (not both ≤ 0)** — the sup is at an endpoint and
  `E(s,t) = max s t`.  The interior critical point `θ* = t/(s+t)` is a
  *minimum*; the sum is dominated by the boundary terms `k = 0` or `k = n`.
  (Growing/divergent q-Gevrey coefficients, `s` or `t > 0`.)
* **Concave (both s,t ≤ 0)** — the sup is the interior saddle
  `θ* = t/(s+t)`, giving `E(s,t) = s·t/(s+t)` (the HARMONIC combination, not
  `s+t`).  This is the genuine convergent-q-Gevrey regime of RSZ.

Numerically verified (mpmath, base `q = 2`, `n = 400`, positive/no-cancel
coefficients): `(-1,-1)↦-0.5=st/(s+t)`; `(-2,-1)↦-0.667=st/(s+t)`;
`(2,1)↦+2.0=max`; `(1,3)↦+3.0=max`; `(-3,1)↦+1.0=max` (note `s+t=-2<0` yet
`max`, since `t>0`); `(-0.5,-2)↦-0.4=st/(s+t)`.  So the split is
**both ≤ 0 → `st/(s+t)`, otherwise → `max s t`**, NOT the sign of `s+t`, and
never the additive `s+t`.

**SCOPE (honest).**  The two analytic ingredients of the `≤` (upper)
direction are proved sorry-free below: (i) `cauchyMul_norm_le_sum`, the
triangle bound `‖(a⋆b)_n‖ ≤ ∑ ‖a_k‖‖b_{n-k}‖`; (ii) `exponent_le_envelope`,
the exact dominant-balance inequality
`s k² + t j² ≤ E(s,t)·(k+j)²` (`k,j ≥ 0`) — the mathematical heart, an
identity-plus-sign completing-the-square argument in each regime.  The
`ε`-`δ` assembly into the order LIMIT (upper bound) and the matching lower
bound are now both **proved sorry-free**: the positive-coefficient case in
`cauchyMul_order_of_pos` (interior saddle included), and the general
normed-field case in `cauchyMul_order`, which reduces to it by a
factor-`(n+1)` squeeze under a non-cancellation hypothesis plus everywhere-
nonzero factors.  `NoCancellation` alone is insufficient — see
`cauchyMul_order_noCancellation_insufficient`.

          theorem QOU.Mathlib.QGevreyOrder.envelope_shift_le (s t δ : ℝ) (hδ : 0 ≤ δ) :
          cauchyEnvelope (s + δ) (t + δ) ≤ cauchyEnvelope s t + δ

          [MI-2, sorry-free] Envelope shift bound. Raising both slopes by the same δ ≥ 0 raises the envelope by at most δ.

          This is the algebraic core of the ε-δ step: passing from the limit form to a bound costs an ε in each slope, and this lemma says that costs at most ε in the envelope — so the two forms have the same envelope in the limit. Conceptually it is sup_θ((s+δ)θ² + (t+δ)(1−θ)²) ≤ sup_θ(sθ² + t(1−θ)²) + δ, since θ² + (1−θ)² ≤ 1 on [0,1]; proved here directly from the closed form, which is why it is a four-way case split rather than one line.

              MI-3 — bound-form extraction and the θ = ½·C(m,2) payoff bridge #

              Two consumptions of the MI-1/MI-2 infra, both proved sorry-free.

(a) **Bound-form extraction.**  The limit-form `IsQGevreyOrder` yields a
    two-sided *eventual bound* on the leading Gaussian coefficient — the
    `ε`-`δ` step the `≤` direction of `cauchyMul_order` explicitly cites.
    This is only the LIMIT ⟹ BOUND direction; the reverse (BOUND ⟹ LIMIT)
    is genuinely FALSE (a bound controls only the `limsup`/`liminf`, not the
    limit), so there is no full equivalence to state — recorded honestly,
    not axiomatised.

(b) **θ = ½·C(m,2) payoff bridge (Tier-2 #4).**  The Route-B binding kernel
    has proved growth `q^{m²/4}` (docs/audits/2026-07-20-theta-jet-gb-
    filtration.md).  Since `IsQGevreyOrder q s a` reads `‖a n‖ ∼ q^{s n²/2}`,
    that growth is order `s = ½` (`routeB_gaussian_order_half`).  The GS
    cumulative degree `C(m,2) = m(m−1)/2` (corpus `gs_cumulative_degree`,
    `QOU.Mass.ThetaSlopeArithmetic`) has leading Gaussian coefficient `½`
    (`gs_degree_leading_coeff_half`).  The **single value `½`** is thus
    simultaneously the Route-B q-Gevrey order and the GS-degree leading
    coefficient (`theta_eq_half_gs_bridge`) — and `½·(½) = ¼` is exactly the
    θ-coefficient of the `q^{m²/4}` exponent, closing the numerical
    `θ = ½·C(m,2)` identity at the level of leading coefficients.  What
    stays probe-only is that the kernel's *actual* coefficient sequence
    realises this order (the RSZ slope=order theorem, MI-2's harder
    direction); this bridge compiles the leading-coefficient arithmetic, not
    that identification.

              q-Gevrey LEVEL — the reciprocal notion, and the one RSZ ties to slopes #

              Ramis–Sauloy–Zhang, Astérisque 355 §1.3.2, verbatim:

              we introduce the space of formal series of q-Gevrey order s: C[[z]]_{q;s} := { Σ aₙzⁿ | ∃A > 0 : aₙ = O(Aⁿ q^{s n²/2}) }. We also say that f ∈ C[[z]]_{q;s} is of q-Gevrey level 1/s.

              So IsQGevreyOrder above is RSZ's order, on the nose. The level is its reciprocal, and the level is what RSZ attaches to the Newton polygon: §2.3.2 calls the two-slope case the "one level case, because the q-Gevrey level µ₂ − µ₁ is the fundamental parameter", and Example 3.3.3 writes d := µ₂ − µ₁ for "the level", with the q-Borel kernel tₙ of "order of growth q^{n²/2d}" — which is exactly order 1/d.

              Both words are therefore in play, they are reciprocal, and the corpus has used "q-Gevrey ½" for both. This section makes the level a definition so the two numbers stop travelling as prose.

                    MI-3(d): the Newton slope is the q-Gevrey LEVEL, not the order #

                    Bean qou-slpd item 5. IsQGevreyOrder and IsQGevreyLevel are reciprocal (RSZ §1.3.2), and this corpus carries one ½ of each kind — the Route-B kernel's order and the C1 ∞-side's level. Which one the Newton polygon attaches to was the last step in the C1 chain carried by prose.

                    RSZ's own touchstone cannot settle it. The q-Euler / Tshakaloff equation zσf = f has Newton slope 1 and solution c_m = q^{m(m-1)/2}, i.e. order 1 and level 1. At slope 1 the two readings agree, which is exactly why a ½ could sit unexamined here: the one example everyone checks against is the one case that cannot tell them apart. slopeKCoeff_order_one below is that case.

                    The slope-k family separates them. L_k = z^kσ − 1 has Newton points (0,0), (1,k), hence slope k; recursion c_m = q^{m−k}c_{m−k}; nonzero coefficients c_{jk} = q^{k j(j−1)/2}; and therefore order 1/k, level k. It is the level that tracks the slope, and slopeKCoeff_order proves this for every k ≥ 1, not just for sampled instances. slopeKCoeff_order_half (k = 2) is the first case where the two readings differ. computations/qbeta/c3_w2_newton_slope_is_qgevrey_level.py (4/4) additionally checks the polygon and the recursion symbolically for k = 1, 2, 3 — the two inputs the Lean takes as given.

                    Consequence for C1, and it is forced rather than chosen. The computed Newton slope at ∞ is ½ (ThetaSlopeArithmetic.newtonSlopes_c1). Read as a level it predicts order 2, i.e. growth q^{n²} — which is exactly c1InfCoeff_order_two and C1FormalSolutionGrowth.natDegree_c1CoeffPoly. Read as an order it would predict q^{n²/4}, off by a factor 4 in the exponent and inconsistent with the corpus's own measured growth. So theta_eq_half_gs_bridge below reaches the right number by the wrong route: it grounds c1Slope = ½ in the Route-B order, where the correct grounding is the ∞-side level. The number, and every downstream conclusion, is unchanged — Door-i needs only that the two slope multisets differ, which is ThetaSlopeArithmetic.newtonSlopes_c1_ne_ggm.

                      2·log_q(a_{kₙ}·b_{n−kₙ}) / n²
