    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedShapeRun — CornersAlong reduces to a shape-level condition (brick 18) #

    Brick 14 left two hypotheses as the interface between the P side of the RSK inverse and the recording tableau; brick 15 showed they are inhabited; bricks 16 and 17 supplied the corner argument and the shape bridge. This file spends them.

    uninsertSteps_shape_and_corners — from a condition stated entirely on shapes, both CornersAlong T is and the shape of the tableau the run returns follow.

    The point is the decoupling. CornersAlong T is mentions the tableau T at every step, so discharging it looked like it would need P and Q reasoned about together. It does not: IsCorner is determined by the shape (isCorner_iff_shape), and brick 17 says the cascade acts on shapes as removeCorner. So the whole run-level corner condition factors through tableauShape T — and what remains for surjectivity is a statement about Q's shape alone, with P no longer mentioned.

    Why the four conclusions are proved together #

    CornersAlong and the shape identity are mutually dependent along the induction: knowing the shape at step k needs the corner condition to have held at every later step (or the cascade may not even have succeeded), and checking the corner condition at step k needs the shape at step k. Brick 14's uninsertSteps_invariants is no help on its own — it takes CornersAlong as a hypothesis, which is what we are trying to produce. One simultaneous induction resolves all of it.

    Scope #

    The reduction, and nothing beyond it. Producing ShapeCornersAlong from a standard recording tableau — reading the positions off Q and showing each step peels the corner carrying the largest remaining entry — is the next brick of epic qou-nrex. Nothing here claims it, nothing here claims rskPair is bijective, and RSKBurnsideHolds for general n stays open (proved for n ≤ 12 by an unrelated route, PartitionsOfBridge.rskBurnsideHolds_le12, with no RSK correspondence in it).

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean.

    Scope note superseded (2026-08-23). The scope paragraph above records the state of the arc when this brick landed. RSKBurnsideHolds is now proved for every n (QOU.Mathlib.rskBurnsideHolds, brick 42), and SnIsotypicSpechtSplit is inhabited, so any statement here that they "remain open" or hold "only for n ≤ 12" describes history rather than the corpus.         Every hypothesis is now about T's shape and the position list — P itself appears only in the conclusion. That is the whole point of the reduction: what remains for surjectivity is to produce ShapeCornersAlong and the emptying condition from a standard recording tableau, with the insertion tableau no longer involved.

        The converse, and what it buys #

        Brick 18 goes shape → tableau. The converse also holds, and is worth having because it turns the reduction into an iff: the tableau-level and shape-level run conditions are the same condition.

        There is no circularity. The induction's own hypothesis supplies ShapeCornersAlong for the later steps, and tableauShape_uninsertSteps then converts it into the shape at that point — so the shape is available before the corner at the current step is checked.
