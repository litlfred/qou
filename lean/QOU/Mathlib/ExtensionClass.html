    QOU.Mathlib.ExtensionClass — the topological charge as a splitting obstruction #

    Beans qou-fngs, qou-zut2. The corpus reaches its topological integer two ways, neither of them homotopy theory. A third candidate was on the books; this file supplies the algebra that removes it.

    • Residue. conj:topological-charge-quantization gives Res_q(ω, 1) = Φ(Res_ξ(ω)) = 1, called there "the algebraic counterpart of the Gauss–Bonnet theorem". Its lean.ref is a stub and resolves to no declaration.
    • Winding. winding-parity-sign-transport reads the exponent n in (σ₁σ₂⁻¹)ⁿ as a winding number.
    • Extension class — withdrawn 2026-08-24. phase-transition-boundary stated that the mixed-channel Hecke representation is a non-split extension 0 → V_sym → V_mixed → V_asym → 0 classified by Ext¹_{H_n(q)}(V_asym, V_sym) ≅ ℤ. There is no such class.

    Why the homological route is gone #

    Three of the reasons are algebra this file now carries.

    isSplit_of_isSemisimpleRing — over a semisimple algebra every short exact sequence of modules splits, so Ext¹ vanishes on every pair and there is nothing left to classify. prop:knot-eigenbasis-complete(1) puts H_n(q₀) exactly there: Jimbo's theorem at a q₀ that the substrate axiom (def:substrate-parameter) forbids from being a root of unity.

    isCompl_ker_quadratic — the same splitting from the Hecke quadratic alone, with no semisimplicity input. rem:hecke-algebra-from-braiding already names the two channels as the q and −q⁻¹ eigenspaces of σ on S²V ⊕ Λ²V; those eigenspaces are complementary as soon as [2]_q = q + q⁻¹ is a unit, which is the very quantity phase-transition-boundary divides its critical temperatures by. The block presupposed the splitting it went on to deny.

    The third reason needs no Lean: Ext¹_A(M, N) for a k-algebra A is a k-module, and this block's ground ring is pinned to ℝ by its own .ts note and its own Lean sibling (abbrev R_q := ℝ). A real vector space is divisible and torsion-free, so it is never ≅ ℤ unless it is 0. Relocating to a root of unity would not have helped — there the group is a ℂ-vector space.

    What is proved here #

    The charge is the failure to split, and the two ways of saying "it splits" agree.

    hasRetraction_of_hasSection is the splitting lemma over any ring, in the direction actually needed here: a section produces a retraction. The converse holds too and is not proved — nothing below uses it, and claiming an iff I did not prove is the defect this session spent its time removing.

    isCompl_of_isSplit is the payoff: a split sequence exhibits range incl as a direct summand, so the middle term is determined by its ends and there is nothing left for a class in Ext¹ to record. isSplit_of_isCompl is its converse and the engine of both splitting theorems below.

    The relation to the surviving two routes #

    selfDual_isSplit: the ⋆_q eigenspace sequence 0 → Ω⁻ → Ω² → Ω²/Ω⁻ → 0 splits whenever 2 is invertible. So it carries no charge. That is not a disappointment, it is the point — it says the topological charge does not live in the self-duality decomposition, and looking for it there is looking in the wrong sequence.

    The sym/asym sequence is now in the same position, for a sharper reason: heckeSES_isSplit shows it splits on the Hecke quadratic alone. So neither eigenspace sequence the corpus writes down carries a class, and the integer has to come from the residue or the winding route or not at all.

    One asymmetry between the two is worth keeping. Over R_q = ℤ[q, q⁻¹] the number 2 is not invertible (R_q = ℤ[ℤ] has units ± qⁿ), so selfDual_isSplit does not apply over the ground ring at all — exactly the boundary rem:self-dual-ses crosses between its two sentences. [2]_q has no such problem: it is a unit in the archimedean realisation for every q₀ > 0, which is why the Hecke splitting needs no wall-crossing.

    Submodule.exists_isCompl needs a DivisionRing, and that gap is real: complements are not automatic over a ring, which is why Ext¹ can be nonzero somewhere. It is just not nonzero here.

    structure QOU.Mathlib.ExtensionClass.SES (R : Type u) [Ring R] (C B A : Type v) [AddCommGroup C] [Module R C] [AddCommGroup B] [Module R B] [AddCommGroup A] [Module R A] :
    Type v

    A short exact sequence 0 → C → B → A → 0 of R-modules.

    Bundled because the splitting question is about the sequence, not about any one of its maps: B can be a direct sum in more than one way, and it is the compatibility with incl and proj that an extension class records.

    • incl : C →ₗ[R] B

      The inclusion of the sub-object.

    • proj : B →ₗ[R] A

      The projection to the quotient.

    • incl_injective : Function.Injective ⇑self.incl

      Exactness on the left.

    • proj_surjective : Function.Surjective ⇑self.proj

      Exactness on the right.

    • exact : Function.Exact ⇑self.incl ⇑self.proj

      Exactness in the middle: range incl = ker proj.

              The sym/asym sequence carries no charge either #

              rem:hecke-algebra-from-braiding gives the braiding σ = τ(c_{V,V}) the quadratic (σ − q)(σ + q⁻¹) = 0 and names its two eigenvalues as the weights of S²V and Λ²V. Those two eigenspaces are complementary the moment [2]_q = q + q⁻¹ is a unit — no semisimplicity, no root-of-unity hypothesis, no statement about the base ring beyond that one inverse.

              theorem QOU.Mathlib.ExtensionClass.isCompl_ker_quadratic {R : Type u} [CommRing R] {M : Type v} [AddCommGroup M] [Module R M] (f : M →ₗ[R] M) (a b : R) (hquad : (f - a • LinearMap.id) ∘ₗ (f + b • LinearMap.id) = 0) (hab : IsUnit (a + b)) :
              IsCompl (LinearMap.ker (f - a • LinearMap.id)) (LinearMap.ker (f + b • LinearMap.id))

              A quadratic with invertible eigenvalue gap splits its eigenspaces.

              With a = q and b = q⁻¹ this is the Hecke relation, and a + b = [2]_q. The splitting is explicit: x = u·(f x + b·x) + u·(a·x − f x) for u = [2]_q⁻¹, the first summand killed by f − a and the second by f + b. Nothing else is used, so the conclusion holds over any commutative ring in which [2]_q is invertible — in particular over ℝ at every q₀ > 0, and over ℤ[q, q⁻¹] it does not, which is the only place the question could still have been open.

                Where the eigenvalue gap can vanish — and where it cannot #

                QOU/Mathlib/HeckeExtOne.lean computes the classification of these extensions as R / (α − β), so everything turns on the difference of the two eigenvalues. Bean qou-oyty records that the corpus carries two Hecke normalisations and that the difference is not invariant under the rescaling between them. The two lemmas below locate the degenerate locus for the normalisation phase-transition-boundary actually uses.

                source
                theorem QOU.Mathlib.ExtensionClass.twoQ_eq_zero_iff {R : Type u} [CommRing R] {q u : R} (hu : q * u = 1) :
                q + u = 0 ↔ q * q + 1 = 0

                [2]_q = q + q⁻¹ vanishes exactly on q² + 1 = 0.

                The inverse is carried as a hypothesis q * u = 1 rather than written q⁻¹, because R_q = ℤ[q, q⁻¹] is a ring with no Inv instance even though q is invertible in it (QOU.GroundRing).

                In the corpus's normalisation (σ − q)(σ + q⁻¹) = 0 (rem:hecke-algebra-from-braiding) the eigenvalue difference is q − (−q⁻¹) = q + q⁻¹, so this is the locus where the symmetric and antisymmetric channels collide — q = ±i, not q = −1.

                theorem QOU.Mathlib.ExtensionClass.twoQ_ne_zero_of_ordered {R : Type u} [Field R] [LinearOrder R] [IsStrictOrderedRing R] {q : R} (hq : q ≠ 0) :

                The gap never vanishes over an ordered field. q² + 1 ≥ 1 > 0.

                So the collision locus of the corpus's normalisation is not reached by any real substrate value — root of unity or not. Relocating a non-split extension to a degenerate q is therefore unavailable on the archimedean side of the §7c wall, where phase-transition-boundary lives.
