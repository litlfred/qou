    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedUninsertInvariants — what the reverse cascade preserves (RSK inverse, brick 11) #

    Brick 10 proved the cascade right inverse one step at a time, taking RowsSorted, ColStrict and NoEmptyRows as hypotheses on the tableau it is handed. Iterating it — which is what word-level surjectivity needs — requires knowing that the tableau uninsert returns still carries those invariants, so the next step can be taken. That is this file.

    Proved here, sorry-free:

    • uninsert_rowsSorted — the reverse cascade keeps rows weakly increasing;
    • uninsert_noEmptyRows — and keeps every row non-empty.

    Both hold at every row index, with no side condition.

    Why ColStrict is not here, and where the corner condition actually belongs #

    Column-strictness is a different matter, and measuring it located the corner condition I had been looking for in the wrong place.

    ColStrict is List.IsChain StrictBelow, and StrictBelow upper lower asserts both lower.length ≤ upper.length and upper[j] < lower[j]. Now take the keeping branch at row 0: the result is r.dropLast :: T, so the first comparison becomes StrictBelow r.dropLast row₁, whose length half reads

    row₁.length ≤ r.length - 1.

    The hypothesis only supplies row₁.length ≤ r.length. The two differ exactly when row 1 is as long as row 0 — that is, when the removed cell was not a corner of the shape. So:

    • the right-inverse identity (brick 10) needs no corner condition — see that file's docstring;
    • preserving ColStrict needs one, and cannot hold without it.

    The failure is machine-checked, not asserted: cornerWitness_colStrict and cornerWitness_not_colStrict exhibit T = [[1,3],[2,4]], column-strict with a non-corner row 0, whose reverse cascade at row 0 is not column-strict. cornerWitness_rowsSorted_survives and cornerWitness_noEmptyRows_survives show the split is real on that same witness — the two invariants proved here survive the very step that breaks the third.

    That is not a gap in the RSK inverse: the reverse cascade is always run at the cell carrying the largest entry of the recording tableau, which is a corner. The corner condition is supplied by Q, one level up, and belongs in the statement that consumes it rather than here. Stating the two invariants that hold unconditionally, and saying plainly what the third costs, is the point of this file.

    Scope #

    Two invariants, unconditionally. ColStrict preservation under a corner hypothesis, word-level surjectivity, and RSKBurnsideHolds for general n are the remainder of epic qou-nrex. Nothing here claims them.

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.uninsert_rowsSorted (T : List (List ℕ)) :
    RowsSorted T → ∀ (i : ℕ), RowsSorted (uninsert T i).1

    The reverse cascade keeps rows weakly increasing. Each row it touches changes either by dropLast (a sublist) or by rowUninsert, which brick 1's rowUninsert_sorted covers; the rest are carried through unchanged.

    source
    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.uninsert_noEmptyRows (T : List (List ℕ)) :
    NoEmptyRows T → ∀ (i : ℕ), NoEmptyRows (uninsert T i).1

    The reverse cascade never leaves an empty row. A row shortened by rowUninsert keeps its length (rowUninsert_length); the one row that loses a cell is deleted outright when that would empty it, which is the whole reason uninsert tests r.length ≤ 1.
