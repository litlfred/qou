    theorem MvPolynomial.rothDegrees_log_bounds_den {m : ℕ} (H qd : Fin m → ℕ) (N : ℕ) (hN : N ≠ 0) (hH : ∀ (h : Fin m), 2 ≤ H h) (hq1 : ∀ (h : Fin m), 1 ≤ qd h) (hqH : ∀ (h : Fin m), qd h ≤ H h) (h : Fin m) :
    ↑(rothDegrees H N h) * Real.log ↑(qd h) ≤ Real.log ↑N + Real.log ↑(H h)

    The normalisation in the denominators' currency, and what does not transfer. Step 4 wants bounds on r_h·log q_h, but rothDegrees is built from the heights H_h = max(|p_h|, q_h). The upper half transfers for free, because q_h ≤ H_h.

    The lower half does not, and the asymmetry is real rather than an artefact: H_h ≤ q_h fails whenever the numerator dominates, and then r_h·log q_h sits arbitrarily far below r_h·log H_h. height_le_den below identifies exactly what recovers it — |p_h| ≤ q_h, which in Roth's setting is the harmless consequence of approximating a number of absolute value at most 1. It is a hypothesis on the approximations, not a consequence of the degree choice, and stating which one is the point of separating these two lemmas.

    theorem MvPolynomial.height_le_den {p : ℤ} {q : ℕ} (hq : q ≠ 0) (hpq : p.natAbs ≤ q) :
    max (↑p / ↑q).num.natAbs (↑p / ↑q).den ≤ q

    What recovers the lower half. If the numerator does not exceed the denominator then the height of the reduced fraction is at most q, so log H_h ≤ log q_h and the normalisation's lower bound transfers from heights to denominators.

    Note it is the reduced fraction's height: 2/4 has height 2, not 4, which is why this is an inequality rather than the equality H_h = q_h one might expect.

    Step 4 from the normalisation. roth_contradiction with its additive hypothesis replaced by data a parameter choice can actually be checked against: an upper normalisation r_h·log q_h ≤ L', a per-coordinate size condition, and one comparison of constants.

    The remaining hfin is Roth's inequality with everything named: (1+θ)·m·L' on the left — the denominators' total size inflated by the polynomial's θ-share — against K·(μ−ν) on the right, the approximation exponent times the index gap.

    Discharging the last inequality: what "take m sufficiently large" actually needs #

    Substituting the standard relations — K = (2+δ)L from an approximation exponent 2+δ, L' = (1+η)L from a normalisation tight to within η, and μ = m(½−ε) from step 1 — turns (1+θ)·m·L' < K(μ−ν) into

    (2+δ)·ν  <  m · [ (2+δ)(½−ε) − (1+θ)(1+η) ],

    so everything hinges on the bracket being positive, and slack_pos says exactly when:

    (2+δ)·ε + θ + η + θ·η  <  δ/2.

    The approximation excess δ/2 must exceed the total slack, and the three slacks are of different origin — ε is how far step 1's index falls short of m/2, η is how far the normalisation is from exact, θ is the auxiliary polynomial's share of the denominators' size. Only after that is m free: exists_m_of_slack is then pure Archimedes.

    Writing this out is what makes the four constants comparable. Before it, η in particular had no budget at all — it entered only in the last commit, when rothDegrees turned out to be two-sided, and nothing said how tight the two sides had to be relative to δ. Now δ/2 pays for it.

    theorem MvPolynomial.slack_pos {δ η θ ε : ℝ} (hslack : (2 + δ) * ε + θ + η + θ * η < δ / 2) :
    0 < (2 + δ) * (1 / 2 - ε) - (1 + θ) * (1 + η)

    When the slack budget balances. The bracket that has to be positive before enlarging m helps at all.

    theorem MvPolynomial.exists_m_of_slack {δ η θ ε ν : ℝ} (hslack : (2 + δ) * ε + θ + η + θ * η < δ / 2) :
    ∃ (m : ℕ), (2 + δ) * ν < ↑m * ((2 + δ) * (1 / 2 - ε) - (1 + θ) * (1 + η))

    Take m large. Once the slack budget balances, an m making the inequality strict exists — and this is the only place in Roth's theorem where "sufficiently large" is used for its Archimedean content rather than as shorthand for a counting bound.

    No sign condition on ν is needed; I wrote one and the linter reported it unused, which is right — exists_nat_gt does not care. The stronger statement is kept.
