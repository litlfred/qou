    Cost is weight #

    The bridge that makes the rest of the module short: the "cost" appearing in index_le_index_pderivList_add and in index_le_of_det_bound's hc is nothing but the idxWeight of the operator's multi-index. Once that is said, bounding a cost is bounding a weight, and idxWeight's additivity does the work.

    (List.map (fun (k : σ) => 1 / ↑(d k)) l).sum = idxWeight d (multiIndexOf l)

    The cost of an operator list is the weight of its multi-index.

    source
    theorem MvPolynomial.idxWeight_le_of_sum_le {σ : Type u_1} {D N : ℕ} (hD : D ≠ 0) (d : σ → ℕ) (hd : ∀ (k : σ), D ≤ d k) (e : σ →₀ ℕ) (he : (e.sum fun (x : σ) (n : ℕ) => n) ≤ N) :
    idxWeight d e ≤ ↑N / ↑D

    A multi-index of total order at most N, on variables of degree at least D, has weight at most N/D.

    This is what turns Mahler's ∑_{h<m} j_{μh} ≤ μ − 1 into a cost bound: the row constraint is on the total order, and the degree floor converts it to a weight.

      The determinant is non-zero #

      ⚠ This was the arc's last unmet input, and §6 of the source is where the answer is — not §7, and not in any new inequality. Every piece was already here; what was missing was the composition.

      Mahler's §6: write A = ∑ₖ Pₖ(x₁,…,x_{m−1}) Σₖ(x_m) in a minimum representation. Both families are then linearly independent, so Lemma 1 gives bounded-order Wronskians U* and V** that do not vanish. Differentiating the representation with the combined operators D_{μν} = D*_μ D**_ν gives a matrix identity, and by the multiplication law for determinants

      det (D_{μν} A)  =  U*(x₁,…,x_{m−1}) · V**(x_m).

      A product of two non-zero polynomials over a domain is non-zero. That is the whole argument.

      Mapped onto this arc: exists_separated_representation_indep is the minimum representation, exists_bounded_order_mvGenWronskian_ne_zero is Lemma 1, sepW_eq (det_separated_wronskian) is the multiplication law, and MvPolynomial over a field is a domain.

      ⚠ The tenth correction: "for every bounded-order family" is unsatisfiable #

      hfac and hres below quantify over the families realising a separated representation of P, and an earlier version quantified over all of them. That version is vacuous.

      Take Dx i = [] for every i. The order condition |Dx i| ≤ i holds, since 0 ≤ i; the family realises the same representation, because it does not touch φ or ψ; and for r ≥ 2 its Wronskian matrix has all rows equal, so the determinant is 0 and the index is ⊤ — below no finite bound. mvGenWronskian_nil_eq_zero (QOU/Mathlib/MvGenWronskian.lean) is that computation. And every P admits a length-2 representation (pad a shorter one with zeros), so this is not an edge case: the hypothesis was false for every P.

      The repair is to carry mvGenWronskian Dx φ ≠ 0 and mvGenWronskian Dy ψ ≠ 0 into hfac, and it costs nothing, because the proof only ever applies hfac to the family exists_sepW_ne_zero_le_degreeOf produces — whose Wronskians are non-zero by construction. The hypothesis had simply been stated more strongly than the argument used.

      ⚠ How this was found, and what did not find it. It surfaced when the two halves of hres were finally supplied and the non-vanishing hypotheses they need had nowhere to come from. Neither lake build nor #print axioms could have caught it, and neither could a witness at small r — at r = 1 a one-column determinant is φ 0, which need not vanish, so the hypothesis is satisfiable there. That is correction 9's lesson exactly: a hypothesis nobody has had to discharge is indistinguishable from a false one, and the discharging is what tests it.

      What the induction hypothesis supplies #

      exists_index_le_of_rothGrowth leaves S — the bound on the determinant's index — existential and unconstrained. That is honest but not usable: the recursion has to know S is small, and it knows that because index_sepW splits the determinant's index into the two factors' indices, which the induction hypothesis (for the x-side) and the base case (for the single split-off variable) bound.

      ⚠ Note the factor r in hfac. It is not slack: the source's J(W) ≤ n·Φ_m carries it, and it is what makes S/r ≤ Φ — which is the quantity correction 9 showed the conclusion actually depends on.
