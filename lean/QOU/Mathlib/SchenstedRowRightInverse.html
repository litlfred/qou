    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedRowRightInverse — the other row-level inverse law (brick 9) #

    Brick 1 proved rowUninsert ∘ rowInsert = id on the bumping branch. Surjectivity of RSK needs the other composite: running a reverse insertion and then re-inserting the value it displaced must give the original row back.

    • mem_of_rowUninsert_some — the displaced value came from the row.
    • rowInsert_rowUninsert — if rowUninsert r y = (r', some z) for a weakly-increasing r, then rowInsert r' z = (r, some y).

    Together with brick 1 the two maps are mutually inverse on their bumping branches, which is what turns "the inverse machinery exists" into "the forward map is onto".

    Why it works #

    rowUninsert replaces the rightmost entry z < y by y. In the resulting row everything strictly before that position is ≤ z (the row was sorted and z sat there), and the position itself now holds y > z. So when rowInsert looks for the leftmost entry > z it lands on exactly that position — the two rules are looking for the same cell from opposite ends, which is why the composite is the identity and not merely close to it.

    Scope #

    One row, one step. The cascade-level right inverse (insert ∘ uninsert = id under a corner condition) and the word-level surjectivity are later bricks of epic qou-nrex; nothing here claims them, and RSKBurnsideHolds for general n remains open.

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.rowInsert_rowUninsert (r : List ℕ) :
    List.Sorted (fun (x1 x2 : ℕ) => x1 ≤ x2) r → ∀ (y z : ℕ), (rowUninsert r y).2 = some z → rowInsert (rowUninsert r y).1 z = (r, some y)

    The other inversion law. Re-inserting the displaced value into the reverse-inserted row recovers the original row, and bumps back the value that was pushed in.

    This is the mirror of rowUninsert_rowInsert: brick 1 undid an insertion, this redoes an undoing.
