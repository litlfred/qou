        Eₚ'(z) = -z^p · exp(Sₚ(z)).

        The telescoping (1 - z)·∑_{k<p} z^k = 1 - z^p is the whole content: it cancels the -1 coming from differentiating the linear factor, leaving a single monomial -z^p. This is why Eₚ is "flat to order p" at the origin and hence why the canonical product converges.

            Eₚ(1) = 0 on ℝ. This single value is what makes the main constant exactly 1: the total mass of the integral below is 1 - Eₚ(1) = 1.

            source
            theorem QOU.Mathlib.WeierstrassFactorEstimate.integral_pow_mul_exp_expSumR (p : ℕ) :
            ∫ (t : ℝ) in 0..1, t ^ p * Real.exp (expSumR p t) = 1

            The total mass is exactly 1. ∫₀¹ t^p·exp(Sₚ(t)) dt = 1.

            This is the sharp step. Bounding the integrand crudely by exp(H_p) would give e^{H_p}/(p+1) > 1 for p ≥ 1 — too weak. Here the integral is evaluated EXACTLY by the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to 1 - Eₚ, whose endpoint values are 0 and 1 - Eₚ(1) = 1.
