    The ε-reduction (boundary argument, load-bearing step). If two functions agree on every shifted closed half-plane {re ≥ ε} for ε > 0, they agree on the open half-plane {re > 0}. This is the ε → 0 collapse of the Watson boundary limiting argument: it reduces uniqueness on {re > 0} (whose boundary point 0 breaks DiffContOnCl after transport) to uniqueness on the shifted half-planes (which avoid that puncture). Pure re-order logic — take ε = re z.
