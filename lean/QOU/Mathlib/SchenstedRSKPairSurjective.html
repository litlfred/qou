    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedRSKPairSurjective — rskPair hits every standard pair #

    Brick 38 of epic qou-nrex (RSK inverse), and the end of the arc that began at brick 10.

    exists_rskPair_eq — for any Young diagram μ and any two standard Young tableaux of shape μ, some permutation of Fin |μ| has that pair as its RSK image.

    Everything it needs is proved: brick 32 recovers the word, brick 35 says the list representation is faithful, brick 36 turns the word into a permutation, brick 37 supplies the shape hypothesis from "the pair shares a shape". What is left is the plumbing, and the plumbing is dependent-type work: rskPairWord is a Σ whose second component has already been transported along recordingShape_eq_pShape, so comparing it with ⟨μ, S_P, S_Q⟩ means moving three things at once.

    sigma_pair_ext_entry does it in the only way that stays honest: substitute the diagram equality, then compare entries — and entry_cast says the transport does not move any entry, which is what makes the second component comparable at all. Under the transport the recording tableau is shiftT of the 0-based one, so shiftT_unshiftT is what closes that leg — the down-shift of brick 34 undone, on entries the content fact already knows are positive.

    Scope #

    Surjectivity. Not bijectivity: injectivity is SchenstedRSKInjective, stated for words, and combining the two into an Equiv — and thence RSKBurnsideHolds for general n — is not done here. RSKBurnsideHolds remains proved only for n ≤ 12, by rskBurnsideHolds_le12, a route with no RSK correspondence in it.

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean; no fit.

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.exists_rskPair_eq {μ : YoungDiagram} (SP SQ : StandardYoungTableau μ) :
    ∃ (σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin μ.cells.card)), rskPair σ = ⟨μ, (SP, SQ)⟩

    rskPair is surjective onto standard pairs. Every pair of standard Young tableaux of a common shape μ is the RSK image of a permutation of Fin |μ|.
