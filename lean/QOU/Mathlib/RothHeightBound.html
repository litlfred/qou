    The m = 1 height bound for Roth's lemma, proved elementarily.

    If (qX − p)^k divides P in ℤ[X] and P ≠ 0, then q^k ≤ |lead P|, hence q^k ≤ H(P) for any height that dominates the leading coefficient.

    This is exactly what the base case of Roth's lemma consumes: with the hypothesis H(P) ≤ q^{εd} it gives q^{θd} ≤ q^{εd}, so the index θ is at most ε.

    Moving the divisibility from ℚ[X] into ℤ[X] #

    What "P vanishes to order k at p/q" gives is divisibility over ℚ. The bound above needs it over ℤ. Gauss's lemma bridges the two — but Mathlib's form wants both polynomials primitive, and the auxiliary polynomial of Roth's lemma is not.

    The m = 1 base case of Roth's lemma, complete.

    If P ∈ ℤ[X] vanishes to order k at p/q — every coefficient of the translate below k is zero — with p, q coprime and q > 0, then q^k ≤ |lead P|.

    With Roth's hypothesis H(P) ≤ q^{εd} this gives q^{θd} ≤ q^{εd}, i.e. the index θ ≤ ε. No Mahler measure anywhere.

    §10's other half — the lowest non-zero coefficient #

    Mahler, §10, verbatim:

    A(x) = (Qx − P)^j B(x) where B(x) is a certain polynomial with integral coefficients. Hence the lowest and the highest non-zero coefficients of A(x) are divisible by P^j and Q^j, respectively. It follows that H^j ≤ |A| ≤ a.

    Everything above proves the Q^j half, and an earlier note in this file called that "the m = 1 base case … complete". It is complete as a statement about the leading coefficient; it is not §10's bound. H is max(|P|, |Q|), and |Q|^j ≤ |A| says nothing whenever the height is carried by the numerator: κ = 99/1 has H = 99 and Q = 1, so the leading-coefficient bound degenerates to 1 ≤ |A|. The trailing half is the whole source of the H^j.

    ⚠ The two halves are not symmetric in Lean the way they read on the page. q > 0 comes free — Lean's ℚ keeps a positive denominator — but p ≠ 0 does not, and at p = 0 Mahler's sentence is vacuous rather than false: P^j = 0 divides only 0. So the trailing lemmas below carry p ≠ 0 and the assembled bound discharges κ = 0 separately, where coprimality forces q = 1 and H^j = 1. Reading "the lowest and the highest … respectively" as one symmetric statement is what hides that case.

    theorem Polynomial.max_abs_pow_le_max_abs_trailingCoeff_leadingCoeff {p q : ℤ} (hq : 0 < q) (hco : IsCoprime p q) {P : Polynomial ℤ} (hP : P ≠ 0) (k : ℕ) (h : ∀ d < k, ((taylor (↑p / ↑q)) (map (algebraMap ℤ ℚ) P)).coeff d = 0) :
    max |p| |q| ^ k ≤ max |P.trailingCoeff| |P.leadingCoeff|

    §10's H^j, both halves assembled.

    H = max(|p|, |q|) is the height of κ = p/q, and each of the two coefficients §10 names is one of P's coefficients, so the max of their absolute values is bounded by any bound on all of them. This is the inequality §10 turns into Θ₁ ≤ log a / (r log H).

    The p = 0 corner is genuine mathematics, not bookkeeping: κ = 0 offers no P^j divisibility at all, and the bound survives only because coprimality then forces q = 1, so H = 1.

      ⚠⚠ This item used to read "the m = 1 base case of Roth's lemma is pow_le_abs_leadingCoeff_of_taylor_coeff_eq_zero, complete". That was wrong — the seventeenth correction in this arc. That theorem is the Q^j half of §10 and bounds |q|^k, but §10's height is H = max(|p|, |q|), so the leading half alone proves nothing whenever the numerator is the larger — which is the typical case. The failure shape is the arc's most persistent one: a true theorem was labelled with the name of the statement it was a step toward, and the label was then read back as a completion. Same species as corrections 12 and 14, and it survived precisely because the Lean type-checks: nothing in a green build objects to a docstring.

    4. The induction step — the remaining work, and where Gelfond / Mahler is genuinely needed: bounding the heights of the factors φₖ, ψₖ in terms of H(P). The factorization itself is proved (QOU.Mathlib.SeparatedWronskian, det_separated_wronskian); it is the height control on the factors that is missing.

    ⚠ The index-side hypothesis of (2) is stated as "every coefficient of the translate below k vanishes", which is what QOU.Mathlib.RothIndex's index/translate machinery produces in one variable. Wiring the two together is bookkeeping, not new mathematics, and is deliberately left until the multivariate statement of Roth's lemma fixes the shape it should take.
