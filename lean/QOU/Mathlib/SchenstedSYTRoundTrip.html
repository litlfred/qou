    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedSYTRoundTrip — the bridge is an inverse #

    Brick 35 of epic qou-nrex (RSK inverse).

    Brick 10's listStandardYT turns a list tableau into a standard Young tableau; bricks 33–34 turn one back. This file proves the composite is the identity:

    sigma_listStandardYT_toListTableau — ⟨_, listStandardYT (toListTableau S) …⟩ = ⟨μ, S⟩.

    Why the statement is a Sigma #

    listStandardYT lands on the diagram ofRowLens (tableauShape T) …, which is only propositionally equal to μ. So the two tableaux do not have the same type and cannot be compared directly. Packing the diagram alongside the tableau is what makes the equation statable, and it is the form rskPair's codomain takes anyway.

    The dependent-type work is confined to two small lemmas — ofRowLens_congr (equal row-length lists give equal diagrams, by subst, the sortedness proofs being irrelevant) and sigma_ext_entry (substitute the diagram, then compare entries). Everything else is entrywise on ℕ and has no cast in it: getD_getD_toListTableau_all says the list tableau reads back the tableau's entries at every index, on the diagram and off it — off it both sides are 0, one by getD past the end and the other by the SSYT's own zeros.

    Scope #

    The bridge only. Two gaps remain between here and rskPair surjectivity, both named on the epic bean: threading the Σ-equality through rskPair's definition, and the fact that a recovered word of content {1, …, n} comes from a permutation (∃ σ, wordOfPerm σ = w). rskPair bijectivity is not claimed and RSKBurnsideHolds for general n remains open.

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean; no fit.

    Scope note superseded (2026-08-23). The scope paragraph above records the state of the arc when this brick landed. The per-brick scope is otherwise unchanged and still accurate.

    content (toListTableau S) = Multiset.map (fun (x : ℕ) => x + 1) ↑(List.range μ.cells.card)

    theorem QOU.Mathlib.Schensted.getD_getD_toListTableau_all {μ : YoungDiagram} (S : StandardYoungTableau μ) (i j : ℕ) :
    ((toListTableau S).getD i []).getD j 0 = S.entry i j

    The list tableau reads back every entry — on the diagram by construction, off it because both sides are 0: the list runs out, and a semistandard tableau vanishes off its shape.
