    QOU.Mathlib.SchenstedRSKInjective — the RSK map is injective on words (brick 7) #

    Assembly. Bricks 1–6 built the inverse machinery; this file composes them into the statement they were for:

    insertWordRec is injective on words of a fixed length — the pair (P(w), Q(w)) determines w.

    The chain, each link already proved:

    1. brick 4 — Q(w) is qAppendSteps [] (rowIndices [] w) 0;
    2. brick 6 — reading timestamp k off that recording tableau returns (rowIndices [] w).getD k 0;
    3. so equal Qs (of equal length) force equal rowIndices;
    4. brick 3 — equal insertWord and equal rowIndices force equal words.

    Why the length hypothesis #

    qRowOf locates timestamps 0, …, |w|-1; nothing it says constrains indices past the end. Two words of different lengths can therefore agree on every timestamp the shorter one has. In the RSK application the length is fixed (|w| = n for a permutation of Fin n), so the hypothesis costs nothing there — but it is a real hypothesis and is stated rather than hidden.

    Scope #

    Injectivity on the raw List ℕ layer. Lifting it to SchenstedPairingMap.rskPair (which is typed in StandardYoungTableau) and proving surjectivity — and thence RSKBurnsideHolds — are the remaining bricks of epic qou-nrex. Nothing here claims them.

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ; nothing archimedean.
