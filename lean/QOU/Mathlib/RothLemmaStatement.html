      What the growth condition buys: the row cost #

      RothGrowth is the one parameter condition the arc carries, and until now nothing used it. Here is what it is for.

      The induction step splits off the last variable in the ordering and its row cost is

      t = d_last / D₁,     D₁ a lower bound on the *other* variables' degrees.

      RothGrowth makes the degrees decrease along the ordering, so every earlier degree is at least ω times the last one, and t ≤ 1/ω. Choosing ω ≥ 1/ε gives t ≤ ε.

      ⚠ The numerator was d_last + 1 until correction 24, which cost a factor 2 here and nowhere in §13. See RothOperatorFamily.cost_sepW_le.

      This was the arc's own reconstruction, and §11 has since confirmed it. Property Γ_m's first clause reads max(s₂/s₁, …, s_M/s_{M−1}) ≤ t, i.e. s_h ≥ s_{h+1}/t — which is RothGrowth o ω d with ω = 1/t. So the facts proved below are facts about the source's condition, not only about this arc's definition of it, and the caveat this paragraph used to carry is withdrawn. (The reading is recorded under "the eleventh correction" below; §11 is garbled in the PDF's OCR layer and had to be read from the page scans.)

      Θ_m — the quantity Roth's lemma is actually about #

      ⚠ Everything in this arc up to here bounds the index of one polynomial. Roth's lemma is about Θ_m, which §8 defines as the least upper bound of J(A; r₁,…,r_m; κ₁,…,κ_m) taken over two sets:

      • R(a; r₁,…,r_m) — non-zero integer polynomials of degree ≤ rₕ in xₕ and height ≤ a;
      • Q(H₁,…,H_m) — systems of m rationals κₕ = Pₕ/Qₕ in lowest terms with max(|Pₕ|,|Qₕ|) ≤ Hₕ.

      §9's content is that both sets are finite, so the lub is attained; that Θ_m is non-decreasing in a; and that 0 ≤ Θ_m ≤ m.

      ⚠ This arc previously recorded §9 as "Siegel's lemma plus a counting argument, the biggest unbuilt piece". That is wrong — Siegel's lemma is nowhere in §9. What §9 needs is finiteness of two explicitly bounded sets and attainment of a maximum over them, which is elementary. The mis-estimate came from reading a section title ("The upper bound Θ_m(…)") and inferring its content.

      IsRothTheta below characterises Θ_m rather than constructing it: the value is pinned (IsRothTheta.unique), so anything satisfying the predicate is the Θ_m.

      Existence is proved — exists_isRothTheta, from finite_rothPolySet and finite_rothFracSet plus non-emptiness. An earlier version of this paragraph called it an undischarged obligation; it no longer is.
