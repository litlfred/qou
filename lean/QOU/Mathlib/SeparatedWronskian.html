    Separation: a derivative in the first group of variables passes through a product of a first-group factor with a second-group factor, touching only the first.

    This is what makes the Wronskian of a separated polynomial factor: the two operator families act on disjoint variable groups, so Δ Δ' (φ ψ) = (Δφ)(Δ'ψ) with no cross terms.

    theorem MvPolynomial.det_separated_wronskian {σ : Type u_1} {τ : Type u_2} {K : Type u_3} [CommRing K] {r : ℕ} (Dx : Fin r → List σ) (Dy : Fin r → List τ) (φ : Fin r → MvPolynomial σ K) (ψ : Fin r → MvPolynomial τ K) :
    (Matrix.of fun (i j : Fin r) => (pderivList (List.map Sum.inl (Dx i) ++ List.map Sum.inr (Dy j))) (∑ k : Fin r, (rename Sum.inl) (φ k) * (rename Sum.inr) (ψ k))).det = (rename Sum.inl) (mvGenWronskian Dx φ) * (rename Sum.inr) (mvGenWronskian Dy ψ)

    The generalized Wronskian of a separated polynomial factors.

    For P = ∑ₖ φₖ(x) ψₖ(y) with the two variable groups disjoint, the mixed Wronskian is the product of a Wronskian in x and one in y.

    ⚠ This is the engine of Roth's lemma's induction on the number of variables: it is what reduces m variables to 1 and m−1. The bounded-order criterion supplies the two factors; this identity is what lets them be used separately.
