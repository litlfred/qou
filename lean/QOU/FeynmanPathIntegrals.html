    Feynman Path Integrals as a Special Case of Categorical Trace #

    Formalizes the correspondence between the Feynman path integral formalism and the QOU categorical trace, showing that the transition amplitude on the knot-type eigenbasis equals the Markov trace on the Hecke algebra.

    Structure #

    1. Feynman transition amplitude — finite sum over irreducible summands
    2. Equivalence with Markov trace — the central proposition
    3. Classical limit — q → 1 degeneration to k[S_n]
    4. Feynman–QOU dictionary — structural correspondence

    References #

    • [feynman1948] R. Feynman, "Space-time approach to non-relativistic quantum mechanics", Reviews of Modern Physics 20 (1948), 367–387.
    • [jones1987] V.F.R. Jones, "Hecke algebra representations of braid groups and link polynomials", Annals of Mathematics 126 (1987), 335–388.
    • [chari1994guide] V. Chari & A. Pressley, A Guide to Quantum Groups, Ch. 10.

          structure QOU.FeynmanPathIntegrals.FeynmanCategoricalTrace :
          Type (max (max (max (max (max (u_1 + 1) (u_2 + 1)) (u_3 + 1)) (u_4 + 1)) (u_5 + 1)) (u_6 + 1))

          Feynman transition amplitude on the knot-type eigenbasis.

          Given a q-Schur–Weyl decomposition V^{⊗n} ≅ ⊕_λ V_λ ⊗ S_λ and a braid representative β ∈ B_n, the transition amplitude restricted to the eigenbasis is the Markov-weighted sum of traces on irreducible Hecke modules.

          The Markov weights w_λ are determined by the Markov property tr_M(x T_n^{±1}) = z^{±1} tr_M(x). The weighted sum is exactly the Jones–Markov trace:

          Σ_λ w_λ · tr_{S_λ}(ρ(β)) = tr_M(ρ(β))

          This replaces the continuum path integral ∫Dφ e^{iS/ℏ} with a finite algebraic expression.

            source
            theorem QOU.FeynmanPathIntegrals.jones_trace_generator_at_one (markov : PathIntegrals.MarkovTrace) (hT_sq : ∀ (i : Fin (markov.hecke.n - 1)), markov.hecke.mul (markov.hecke.T i) (markov.hecke.T i) = markov.hecke.one) (i : Fin (markov.hecke.n - 1)) :
            markov.tr (markov.hecke.mul (markov.hecke.T i) (markov.hecke.T i)) = markov.tr markov.hecke.one

            At q = 1, the Hecke quadratic relation T² - (q-q⁻¹)T - 1 = 0 degenerates to T² = 1. Applying the Markov trace (which is linear and satisfies tr(ab) = tr(ba)) to T² = 1 gives tr(T²) = tr(1). Since tr(T²) = tr(T·T) and T is invertible with T⁻¹ = T (from T² = 1), we get tr(T) = tr(T⁻¹). Combined with the Markov property tr(xT±1) = z±1 tr(x) at z = 0 (since z → 0 as q → 1), this gives tr(T) = tr(1) for each generator.

            The full J_K(1) = 1 result follows from the fact that every element of H_n(1) ≅ k[S_n] is a product of generators T_i, and tr is multiplicative on the augmentation ideal at z = 0.
