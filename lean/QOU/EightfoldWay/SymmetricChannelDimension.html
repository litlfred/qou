    The symmetric channel has dimension C(N + n - 1, n) — the decuplet at N = n = 3 #

    prop:borromean-decuplet states that the fully symmetric channel of V^{⊗3} at dim V = 3 is 10-dimensional, via binom(N+n-1, n) = binom(5,3) = 10, and that this is conditional on N = 3: at N = 2 the same partition yields only a 4-dimensional quadruplet.

    What is actually being formalised (and what is not) #

    The temptation is to "formalise" the proposition as Nat.choose 5 3 = 10. That is arithmetic wearing a theorem's clothes — it is a closed numeral computation that says nothing about any vector space, and it would satisfy a coverage checker while proving nothing. Per the strict Lean discipline, it is not a theorem.

    The genuine content is the dimension formula itself, for general N and n:

    dim_K { degree-n forms in N variables }  =  C(N + n - 1, n)

    The space of degree-n homogeneous forms in N variables is the n-th symmetric power of the dual of an N-dimensional space, so this is the symmetric channel's dimension, not a stand-in for it. The proof goes through the object the manuscript's table actually displays — the weight diagram, i.e. the exponent vectors (n_u, n_d, n_s) of total degree n:

    • wdEquivSym — the weight diagram is in bijection with multisets of n flavours. This is the "Content" column of the table (uuu, uud, …, sss) made precise: a decuplet state is a multiset of three quark flavours.
    • card_weightDiagram — that set has C(N + n - 1, n) elements (stars and bars).
    • homogeneousSubmodule_eq_restrictSupport — the degree-n forms are exactly the polynomials supported on the weight diagram, so the diagram indexes a basis.
    • finrank_homogeneousSubmodule — the dimension formula, over any commutative semiring satisfying the strong rank condition (in particular over any field).

    The numerals come last and are derived:

    • decuplet_dim — N = 3, n = 3 gives 10;
    • quadruplet_dim — N = 2, n = 3 gives 4;
    • decuplet_ne_quadruplet — the two differ.

    That last one is the point of the exercise. The block's anti-fitting guard is the claim that the decuplet is conditional on N = 3 and would be a quadruplet at N = 2. Deriving both numbers from one general theorem makes that conditional machine-checked rather than asserted, and it is what makes the statement falsifiable: were the dimension N-independent, decuplet_ne_quadruplet would fail.

    Honest scope #

    Three things are not formalised here, and none of them should be read into the above.

    1. The symmetrizer. The manuscript realises the symmetric channel as the image of the Hecke symmetrizer e_(3) = Σ_{σ ∈ S₃} T_σ acting on V^{⊗3}. Identifying that image with the symmetric power requires n! to be invertible — e_(3) composed with itself is n! · e_(3), hence over a field of characteristic 2 or 3 it is not a projector at all, and the identification fails. What is proved here is the dimension of the symmetric power; the char-0 (or char > n) hypothesis that ties it to the symmetrizer image is not discharged.
    2. The SU(3)-structure. That the 10 basis vectors carry the weights listed in the table, and that the space is the irreducible (3,0) representation rather than merely a complement of the correct dimension, needs representation theory Mathlib does not carry usably. Only the indexing by quark-count coordinates is proved (wdEquivSym).
    3. The physical identification. That the spin-3/2 baryon decuplet is this channel is a spin–flavour–colour selection-rule argument the chapter does not derive; it is already recorded as an open item in sec:eightfold-open-problems, and nothing here closes it.

        The N = 2 quartet is the isospin-3/2 ladder #

        quadruplet_dim says the symmetric channel at N = 2 is four-dimensional. That is a count, and a count does not say the four states are the Δ(1232) isospin quartet. This section supplies the label: the third isospin component, and the four facts that make it a faithful one.

        With index 0 = u and 1 = d (the convention weightDiagram's own docstring fixes), u carries I₃ = +1/2 and d carries I₃ = -1/2, so a degree-3 monomial carries I₃ = (n_u - n_d)/2. Everything below is stated on the doubled value 2I₃ = n_u - n_d, to stay in ℤ and keep the statements free of division.

        Nothing here is archimedean and nothing needs the base ring, so these are ℕ/ℤ statements (§7c).

        The contrast with N = 3 is deliberate and is NOT proved here. At three flavours I₃ alone is not a faithful label — strangeness is a second independent coordinate, and Σ*⁰ and Λ-like states share an I₃. That is the decuplet's business and is gated on N = 3 existing at all.
