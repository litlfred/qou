    The envelope, wired: d/dφ V(x φ, y φ) = ∂V/∂x · x′ along the curve #

    Bean qou-trns. FiveTwoEnvelopeAssembly proved the envelope theorem abstractly — if ∂F/∂y = 0 the total derivative collapses to the x-part — and FiveTwoEnvelopeSum proved what the surviving terms sum to. What was never written is the instantiation at the actual potential

    V(x,y) = Li₂(y) + Li₂(x·y) + Li₂(y/x) + log(−y)² .

    This file writes it.

    Route #

    The abstract theorem is stated for a Fréchet derivative on ℂ × ℂ. Getting one for V means assembling four two-variable derivatives and then doing continuous -linear-map algebra. Differentiating along the path instead needs only the one-variable chain rule, four times, and produces the same statement:

    d/dφ V(x φ, y φ) = ∂V/∂x · x′ + ∂V/∂y · y′       (`hasDerivAt_deformedV_path`)

    with ∂V/∂y = 0 then killing the second term (hasDerivAt_deformedV_envelope). The abstract theorem remains the conceptual statement — and it is not used here, which is worth saying plainly rather than implying a dependency that does not exist.

    The collection step, and a trap it avoids #

    Adding the four chain-rule outputs is a rational identity, but the terms contain logarithms and field_simp will happily rewrite 1 − y/x to (x−y)/x inside one — changing the branch and silently breaking the whole argument. That happened once already in this lane. dV_collect therefore takes the four logarithms as opaque variables, so no tactic ever sees a log while denominators are cleared. Same discipline as sum_div_of_pinned.

    ⚠ The envelope hypothesis is NOT free on the curve #

    dVdy x y = 0 unfolds to (1−y)(1−x·y)(1−y/x) = y², which after clearing x is x·y² = (1−y)(1−x·y)(x−y) — the deformed saddle, and by deformed_saddle_iff_curve that is the curve (dVdy_eq_zero_iff_exp, curve_eq_zero_of_saddle). An earlier draft of this docstring concluded from that "so the envelope hypothesis is free on the curve". That is false, and Route 30 G2 caught it:

    root dVdy
    the two roots other than the positive real one 0
    the positive real root t 2πi/t — measured to 2e-40, not 0

    The exponentiated saddle holds at all three roots (to 1e-41); what fails at t is the logarithmic identity, because −t is a negative real and sits on log's cut, so log(−t) = log t + iπ and the 2·log(−y) term contributes a bare 2πi. Exponentiating is necessary, not sufficient: the branch integers have to line up too.

    The transport survives, and for a reason worth stating. S(φ) = Σⱼ Re Vⱼ takes real parts, and the leak is 2πi·t′/t with t and t′ real, hence purely imaginary, hence killed by Re. Route 30 measures Re(dV/dφ) = Re(∂V/∂x·x′) at all roots to 4.6e-41, while the unreduced difference reaches 2.47. So hasDerivAt_deformedV_envelope below is a true theorem whose hypothesis fails at one of the three roots, and hasDerivAt_re_deformedV_envelope is the one that actually applies.

    Side conditions #

    The Li₂ derivative needs each argument off the cut [1,∞) and nonzero. These are stated as hypotheses, not assumed: they are what FiveTwoCutAvoidance is for, and pretending otherwise is how a gap gets buried.

    The log(−y) derivative is taken as a hypothesis, not derived from −y ∈ slitPlane. An earlier draft did derive it that way, and Route 31 G4 showed the result: on this arc one of the three roots is real and positive at every φ, so −y is a negative real, so slitPlane fails, so the theorem was vacuous at that root — at all 199 sampled points. Two routes now supply the hypothesis, with the same value Y′/Y: hasDerivAt_log_neg_of_slitPlane off the cut, and hasDerivAt_log_neg_ofReal on it via log(−t) = log t + iπ.

    The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms.

          5. ∂V/∂y on the curve is 2πi·(integer)/y — Route 30's correction, structurally #

          Route 30 G2 refuted "the envelope hypothesis is free on the curve": ∂V/∂y = 0 holds at two of the three roots, but at the positive real root it is 2πi/t, because −t sits on log's cut.

          The general statement behind that is cleaner than either case. On the curve the exponentiated saddle holds exactly, so the logarithmic one holds up to 2πi, and therefore

          ∂V/∂y · y  ∈  2πi·ℤ

          at every point of the curve. k = 0 recovers the two easy roots; k = ∓1 is the real root's leak. Nothing has to be known in advance about which root is which.
