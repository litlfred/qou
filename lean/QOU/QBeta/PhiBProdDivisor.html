    phiBProd's products have no spurious zeros (divisor-rigidity narrowing) #

    Formalize-first narrowing of the sole remaining keystone input (qou-7xvy). Input 1 (#4296) closed MeromorphicOn (phiBProd b) via the infinite-product-analyticity route. The remaining open input is the divisor rigidity of the physical Φ_b: that the ratio Ψ / phiBProd of two solutions is entire (hr_entire) / bounded (hbdd) — which needs the state-integral growth estimate.

    This file supplies the phiBProd-side half of that divisor computation, unconditionally on the branch Im b² > 0: the two infinite products are nonzero wherever all their factors are nonzero — a convergent product ∏(1 + fₖ) with ∑‖fₖ‖ < ∞ and no vanishing factor does not vanish (tprod_one_add_ne_zero_of_summable). Combined with FaddeevQuantumDilogarithm.phi_b_zero (each vanishing factor is a genuine zero of Φ_b), this pins phiBProd's zero/pole divisor to exactly the explicit shifted lattices where numFactor / denFactor vanish — no hidden zeros, no hidden poles.

    This concretizes the abstract divisor-rigidity hypothesis: the remaining hr_entire input is now "Ψ vanishes exactly on phiBProd's explicit b-lattice and has poles exactly on the dual lattice", a checkable statement about Ψ, rather than an abstract order match. It does not close the input (the Ψ-side divisor still needs the growth estimate); it is the honest, formalizable narrowing.

    Verified lean-direct against the pinned Mathlib Log.Summable cone + PhiBProd. Sorry-free.

    phiBProd is nonzero off the union of the two lattices. Where every numerator and denominator factor is nonzero, phiBProd b z = (∏ num)/(∏ den) ≠ 0: it has neither a zero (numerator nonvanishing) nor a pole (denominator nonvanishing) there. This is the phiBProd-side divisor computation the divisor-rigidity input consumes.
