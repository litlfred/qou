    The writhe normalisation of the GS coefficient sum is forced, not chosen #

    Deriving the correction factor from the two stabilisation rules.

    gb_winding_parity_wire_probe.py (GAP-2) found that the Gröbner–Shirshov reduction certificate's total coefficient sum S(β) = ∑_w c_w(q) reads off the winding parity from the reduction output rather than from the wⁿ presentation — but that it is a framed quantity, not yet a knot invariant. Under Markov stabilisation it transforms as

    positive β ↦ β σ_n : S ↦ S (exponent sum e ↦ e+1, strands n ↦ n+1) negative β ↦ β σ_n⁻¹ : S ↦ (1−h)·S (exponent sum e ↦ e−1, strands n ↦ n+1)

    with h = q − q⁻¹. The probe's [G3] therefore said the readout must be "writhe-normalised first" for a general braid, without saying by what.

    This file derives the factor instead of positing one. Seek a correction of the monomial form A^e · B^n in the exponent sum e and strand count n. Markov invariance of S · A^e B^n under the two moves is exactly

    A * B = 1 (positive stabilisation: e and n both rise by one) A = (1 − h) * B (negative stabilisation: e falls, n rises)

    and writhe_factor_sq shows these two force

    A² = 1 − h, equivalently A^e B^n = A^(e − n) with A² = 1 − h,

    i.e. the normalisation is the familiar (1−h)^{(e−n)/2} — uniquely determined up to sign (writhe_factor_unique_up_to_sign), with no freedom left to tune. no_normalisation_at_h_eq_one records the boundary: at h = 1 the two constraints are contradictory, so no monomial normalisation exists at all. (Only this necessary condition is proved. h ≠ 1 is not sufficient — existence also needs 1 − h to be a square in the base ring — and no sufficiency claim is made here.)

    This is also why [G3]'s escape clause held: on the canonical family wⁿ = (σ₁σ₂⁻¹)ⁿ the exponent sum is 0 and the strand count is 3 for every n, so the correction is the single constant B³ across the whole family and cannot change any relative sign — hence the parity readout was already stable there without normalisation. That is an arithmetic fact about the word, recorded here rather than formalised, since it needs the braid word itself.

    Base ring: generic [CommRing R] (§7c); the invertibility lemma adds a [Field R]. A enters only through its defining relation A * B = 1, never as a chosen square root, so nothing depends on a branch choice — the same discipline as QOU.QBeta.WindingLatticeNormForm.

    Companion probe: computations/gb_writhe_normalised_readout_probe.py.

    Scope. This determines the unique monomial normalisation compatible with the two measured stabilisation rules. It does not prove that the normalised readout is a knot invariant (that needs the Markov theorem's conjugation move too, untouched here), and it does not deliver the full winding class (m,ℓ).

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.WritheNormalisation.writhe_factor_sq {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (A B h : R) (hAB : A * B = 1) (hA : A = (1 - h) * B) :
    A ^ 2 = 1 - h

    The normalisation factor is forced. If A^e B^n corrects the coefficient sum to a Markov-stabilisation invariant — A * B = 1 from the positive move, A = (1−h) * B from the negative one — then A² = 1 − h. There is no remaining freedom: the exponent (e − n)/2 and the base 1 − h are both derived.

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.WritheNormalisation.writhe_factor_B_sq {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (A B h : R) (hAB : A * B = 1) (hA : A = (1 - h) * B) :
    (1 - h) * B ^ 2 = 1

    The companion relation on B: it is the inverse square root of 1 − h.

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.WritheNormalisation.factor_collapses {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (A B : R) (hAB : A * B = 1) (k : ℕ) :
    A ^ k * B ^ k = 1

    A^e B^n collapses to a power of A alone: since B = A⁻¹, the correction depends only on e − n, which is why the normalisation is written (1−h)^{(e−n)/2}. Stated in the n = e case, where it is the bare statement that the correction is trivial exactly on zero-(e−n) braids.

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.WritheNormalisation.writhe_factor_unique_up_to_sign {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (A B A' B' h : R) (hAB : A * B = 1) (hA : A = (1 - h) * B) (hAB' : A' * B' = 1) (hA' : A' = (1 - h) * B') :
    A ^ 2 = A' ^ 2

    Uniqueness up to sign. Two normalisations satisfying the same two stabilisation constraints have equal squares, so over an integral domain they differ only by a sign. No tuning is possible.

    theorem QOU.QBeta.WritheNormalisation.no_normalisation_at_h_eq_one {K : Type u_2} [Field K] (A B : K) (hAB : A * B = 1) (hA : A = (1 - 1) * B) :
    False

    The boundary of the construction. At h = 1 the two stabilisation constraints are contradictory, so no monomial normalisation exists. This is the honest boundary of the construction, not an edge case to be waved away.
