    theorem QOU.QBeta.BarEven.deriv_at_one_eq_zero_of_barEven {f : ℝ → ℝ} (hf : DifferentiableAt ℝ f 1) (hsym : (fun (x : ℝ) => f x⁻¹) =ᶠ[nhds 1] f) :
    deriv f 1 = 0

    Bar-evenness forces stationarity at the bar fixed point.

    If f agrees with x ↦ f x⁻¹ near 1 and is differentiable at 1, then deriv f 1 = 0.

    The proof is the one-line differentiation of f (q⁻¹) = f q at q = 1: the chain rule contributes the factor d/dq (q⁻¹) = −1 there, giving −f'(1) = f'(1). Nothing about f beyond differentiability is used — the statement is about the involution, not the function.

    ℝ

    The Markov parameter of convention C, in the square-root variable u = √q: z_C = u/(u²+1). Written this way it is manifestly invariant under u ↦ u⁻¹, which is the bar involution.

        The contrast, stated together. z_C is bar-even hence stationary at the bar fixed point; z_D is not bar-even and is not stationary. Since α's t² coefficient is 1/4 + z'(1) and the c·ln q prediction is 1/4, this is exactly why C passes the holonomy t² test and D fails it — and therefore why that failure is not independent evidence against convention D.
