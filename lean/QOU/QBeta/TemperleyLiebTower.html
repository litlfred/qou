    Convention D is Temperley–Lieb, and that is what H₄ buys #

    Bean qou-4g4a / qou-o3ta. Answers the owner's "h4" — option C5, enlarge the arena from H₃(q) to H₄(q).

    The measurement that reframes everything #

    Computing the Wedderburn weights of the SU(2) Markov trace (convention D, z = q³/(q²+1)) at q₀ for n = 2 … 7, with y_λ from the Wenzl formula:

    n   partitions with y_λ ≠ 0        Σ dim²   dim Hₙ   Catalan(n)
2   (2), (1,1)                          2        2        2
3   (3), (2,1)                          5        6        5
4   (4), (3,1), (2,2)                  14       24       14
5   (5), (4,1), (3,2)                  42      120       42
6   (6), (5,1), (4,2), (3,3)          132      720      132
7   (7), (6,1), (5,2), (4,3)          429     5040      429

    Two facts, both exact and both measured to 1.3e−51:

    1. y_λ = 0 exactly when λ has three or more rows. Not "small", not "vanishing at q₀" — identically zero, because SU(2)'s fundamental is 2-dimensional and there is no antisymmetriser on three strands in it.
    2. Σ dim² over the surviving blocks = Catalan(n) — 2, 5, 14, 42, 132, 429.

    Together: Hₙ(q) / rad ≅ TLₙ(q), the Temperley–Lieb algebra.

    Why that matters more than the H₄ question itself #

    The metric blocker (GramSignature.lip_kernel_forces_unbounded) says a degenerate Gram form makes the Connes distance unbounded, and the repair was to quotient by the radical. That looked like an ad-hoc amputation. It is not: the radical is the Temperley–Lieb ideal — generated by the three-strand q-antisymmetriser — and the quotient is a canonical, named, well-studied algebra with a diagrammatic calculus and a standard bilinear form. "Quotient by the radical" and "work in TLₙ" are the same instruction.

    So the degeneracy is not a defect introduced by convention D. It is what convention D means. Under C the framework was doing full-Hecke; under D it has been doing Temperley–Lieb all along without saying so.

    The closed form, and the one identity behind all of it #

    For a two-row partition λ = (n − k, k):

    y₍ₙ₋ₖ,ₖ₎ = [n − 2k + 1]_q / [2]_q ⁿ,        y_λ = 0 for ≥ 3 rows.

    Verified against y_lambda_su_n for every partition of n = 2 … 7; worst residual 1.34e−51 at 50 dps. Under it, everything reduces to the three-term q-integer recursion

    [m + 2] + [m] = [2] · [m + 1],

    which qint_three_term_pair below proves in one linear_combination. That single identity is:

    • the branching coherence of the Markov trace on the whole tower — the H₂ ⊂ H₃ case proved in GramSignature.mu_triplet_eq_branch / mu_singlet_eq_branch is one instance of it, and markov_branching_general is the general statement;
    • hence the reason μ₊ = y₍₃₎ + y₍₂,₁₎ and μ₋ = y₍₂,₁₎ + y₍₁³₎;
    • hence, at the next level, y₍₃₎[H₃] = y₍₄₎ + y₍₃,₁₎ and y₍₂,₁₎[H₃] = y₍₃,₁₎ + y₍₂,₂₎ — checked numerically to 50 dps.

    A pleasant corollary that fell out of the same table: y₍₂,₂₎[H₄] = y₍₂,₁₎[H₃]² = μ₋² = 1/[2]_q⁴, and the isospin weights themselves are just the n = 2 weights: μ₊ = y₍₂₎[H₂], μ₋ = y₍₁,₁₎[H₂]. The two numbers the NN sector runs on are the Wedderburn weights of the two-strand algebra.

    What H₄ actually buys — and what it risks #

    TL₃ has blocks of dimension (1, 2), so its largest matrix block is M₂ (4-dimensional). TL₄ has blocks (1, 3, 2), largest M₃ (9-dimensional). For the interface that is a real widening:

    • H₃ window — one quark either side of the junction. Interface algebra span{1, σ₂}: 2 parameters, a 2-plane in a 4-dimensional block. This is the colour-weight blocker of qou-17gn.
    • H₄ window — two quarks either side. Interface algebra ⟨σ₂, σ₃⟩ ≅ H₃, whose image in TL₄ is TL₃: 5 parameters, inside a 14-dimensional algebra whose largest block is 9-dimensional.

    2 → 5 reachable dimensions. That is the honest size of the C5 payoff.

    And the honest risk, stated first rather than discovered later. The owner's standard is "more accurate to experiment and/or backed by canonical derivation". Three extra parameters is neither, by itself. qou-4g4a proved an impossibility — the interface band lies strictly between the two isospin weights, missing by 63 % and 23 % — and the danger of C5 is that it converts a clean impossibility into a fit with room to hide. A 5-parameter interface that reaches the deuteron proves nothing unless the 5 parameters are themselves derived. The gain here is structural (the arena is now a named algebra with a known form); it is not yet a prediction, and nothing below claims otherwise.

    theorem QOU.QBeta.TemperleyLiebTower.qint_three_term_pair {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (q r X Y : R) (hqr : q * r = 1) :
    X * q ^ 2 - Y * r ^ 2 + (X - Y) = (q + r) * (X * q - Y * r)

    The three-term q-integer recursion, [m+2] + [m] = [2]·[m+1].

    Stated on a formal inverse pair q · r = 1 with X = qᵐ, Y = rᵐ abstracted, so there is no natural-number subtraction, no q⁻¹, and no division: the whole content is one linear_combination against hqr. Every branching identity in this file and in GramSignature is an instance of it.

    Reading the substitution: [m]_q = (qᵐ − q⁻ᵐ)/(q − q⁻¹), so with X = qᵐ and Y = rᵐ the numerator of [m+2] is X q² − Y r², of [m] is X − Y, and of [m+1] is X q − Y r. The common denominator q − q⁻¹ cancels, which is why it never appears.

    theorem QOU.QBeta.TemperleyLiebTower.markov_branching_general {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (q r X Y D P : R) (hqr : q * r = 1) (hD : D = q + r) (hDne : D ≠ 0) (hP : P ≠ 0) :
    (X * q ^ 2 - Y * r ^ 2 + (X - Y)) / (D * P) = (X * q - Y * r) / P

    Branching coherence of the Markov trace, at every level of the tower.

    y₍ₙ₊₁₋ₖ,ₖ₎ + y₍ₙ₋ₖ,ₖ₊₁₎ = y₍ₙ₋ₖ,ₖ₎ — the two ways of adding a box to a two-row Young diagram, summing to the weight one level down. With the closed form y = [m]/[2]ⁿ and m = n − 2k + 1 this is exactly the recursion above divided through by [2]ⁿ⁺¹.

    Stated with D = [2] = q + r and the three numerators abstracted, so it is visibly the same identity: nothing about SU(2), about q₀, or about the convention enters.

    D ≠ 0 is genuinely needed, not defensive: q · r = 1 together with q + r = 0 forces q² = −1, which no ordered field admits but ℂ does, and there the statement is false (0 = (Xq − Yr)/P). At the physical q₀ > 0, D = q + q⁻¹ ≥ 2.

    Convention D is the unique non-trivial trace that kills the antisymmetriser #

    Everything above took y₍₁³₎ = 0 as measured. This section derives it, and in both directions, which turns "convention D is Temperley–Lieb" from an observation into a characterisation.

    Write the Markov trace on H₃(q) in the Wedderburn basis and impose the three normalisations it must satisfy, with r a formal inverse for q:

    tr(1)     = y₍₃₎ + 2y₍₂,₁₎ + y₍₁³₎              = 1
tr(σ)     = q y₍₃₎ + (q − r) y₍₂,₁₎ − r y₍₁³₎   = z
tr(σ₀σ₁)  = q² y₍₃₎ − y₍₂,₁₎ + r² y₍₁³₎         = z²

    Three linear equations, three unknowns. Solving and factorising the antisymmetric weight gives

    y₍₁³₎ · (q² + 1)(q⁴ + q² + 1)  =  q² (z − q) (z(q² + 1) − q³),

    so for q > 0 the antisymmetriser dies at exactly two values of z:

    z = q                    the trivial branch
z = q³/(q² + 1)          CONVENTION D

    and the first is not a competitor: z_eq_q_forces_trivial shows it collapses the whole trace to y₍₃₎ = 1, y₍₂,₁₎ = y₍₁³₎ = 0 — the one-dimensional character, which carries no mixed-symmetry block and therefore no isospin structure at all. So convention D is the unique non-trivial Markov trace on H₃(q) whose radical contains the three-strand antisymmetriser.

    Convention C is thereby excluded by derivation rather than by ruling: z = 1/(q^{1/2} + q^{-1/2}) is neither root, which is exactly why y₍₁³₎ = 0.0227449 ≠ 0 there and why C's Gram form is non-singular. (CONVENTION-C: KEEP_HISTORICAL -- that number is the evidence excluding C, not a stale production value. Do not flip.)

    Why this matters beyond bookkeeping #

    QOU.Hecke.MarkovTraceH2 (sibling branch claude/post-drain-audit-8hj2, PR #4857) observes that its z cannot be pinned at n = 2 — the class constrains z only through trace_gen, so markovTraceH2 is a def rather than an instance because "an instance would have to invent a value". That is correct at two strands: H₂(q) is commutative, has one generator, and every z is admissible.

    This section supplies the missing pinning, and it first becomes available at three strands. z is free at n = 2 and forced at n = 3, the moment there is a three-row partition whose weight can be asked to vanish. The two results compose: their instance supplies the algebra, this supplies the parameter.

    The corollaries also recover, now derived rather than measured, the closed forms used throughout GramSignature: y₍₂,₁₎ = q²/(q²+1)² = 1/[2]_q², which is μ₋, and y₍₃₎ = (q⁴+1)/(q²+1)² = [4]_q/[2]_q³.

      The vanishing propagates up the tower — Wenzl becomes a corollary, not a citation #

      ConventionPinning derives y₍₁³₎ = 0 at three strands. qou-8iru observed y_λ = 0 for every λ with ≥ 3 rows at n = 2 … 7, and attributed it to Wenzl. This section shows the higher levels follow from the n = 3 case, so the citation is not load-bearing.

            One factor cancelled against a power — the tower's recurring shape.

            c · x / c^(n+1) = x / c^n. Every level of the tower divides by [2]_qⁿ, and every branching step produces one extra factor of [2]_q upstairs, so this is the cancellation that appears each time.

            Do not reach for field_simp here. Under a variable exponent it rewrites the base — (q + q⁻¹)^n became (q²q⁻¹ + q⁻¹)^n — after which ring cannot reconcile two different bases raised to the same n, and the goal is unprovable-looking rather than merely unproved.

            A named lemma rather than a tactic macro, deliberately: the hand-rolled version was rw [pow_succ, mul_comm] followed by mul_div_mul_left, and the bare mul_comm rewrites whichever multiplication it meets first — fine in a two-factor goal, wrong in a larger one. The lemma applies at a named position and cannot pick the wrong redex.
