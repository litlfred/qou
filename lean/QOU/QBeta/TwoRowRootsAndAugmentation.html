    The two-row condition has two roots, and one of them is the augmentation #

    Companion to computations/qbeta/qbeta_admissible_functionals_bracket_probe.py (19/19), on beans qou-99qc and qou-cds1.

    Under convention D the corpus places the decay channel in the Temperley–Lieb quotient (prf:algebraic-gns-embedding), and a functional descends to that quotient exactly when it annihilates the (1,1,1) block. For the Ocneanu trace on H₃(q) that condition is y_(1,1,1)(z) = 0, whose numerator is

    P(z) = -q²·(q - z)·(q²z + z - q³).

    Two facts about it are ring identities and are pinned here. Neither is the identification of P with the Markov weight — that is done in the probe, by solving the defining linear system; these are the statements about P and about the trace values that the probe's conclusion rests on.

    What is proved #

    • markovTrace_longest_at_q — the load-bearing one. The Ocneanu trace sends the longest basis element to z·(h·z + 1); at z = q with h = q - q⁻¹ that is exactly q³, which is the augmentation's value there. It needs h·q = q² - 1, i.e. it is where the Hecke parameter enters.
    • traceValues_eq_augmentationValues_at_q — all four trace values agree with the augmentation's at z = q, so at that root the Markov trace is the augmentation rather than a third functional.
    • twoRow_numerator_eq_zero_iff — over a domain, P(z) = 0 exactly at the two named roots. This is what makes the admissible set complete rather than a sample.
    • two_roots_distinct — the roots are genuinely two, for q ≠ 0.

    Base ring (AGENTS.md §7c) #

    Generic [CommRing R], with q : Rˣ carrying the invertibility that q⁻¹ presupposes. twoRow_numerator_eq_zero_iff additionally assumes [IsDomain R], which is what "a product vanishes iff a factor does" needs — still not archimedean: no ordering, no Real.*.

          twoRowNumerator q z = 0 ↔ z = q ∨ (q ^ 2 + 1) * z = q ^ 3

          The two-row condition has exactly two roots.

          Over a domain and for q ≠ 0, P(z) = 0 iff z = q or (q²+1)·z = q³ — the second being z_D = q³/(q²+1) written division-free. This is what upgrades the bracket from "the two functionals I enumerated" to a completeness statement about the admissible set.
