    1. The algebraic core of NZ (52): the gluing relation forces oddness #

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.NeumannZagierCuspParity.gluing_swap_inverse {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (z w : R) (h : z * w * (z - 1) * (w - 1) = 1) :
    z * (1 - w) * (w * (1 - z)) = 1

    The reciprocal-argument identity.

    If z, w lie on the 4₁ gluing variety z w (z-1)(w-1) = 1, then the two logarithm arguments produced by v ↦ -v (which swaps z ↔ w) are reciprocal:

    (z(1-w)) · (w(1-z)) = 1.

    Hence u(-v) = log(z(1-w)) = -log(w(1-z)) = -u(v): the meridian log-holonomy is an ODD function of the longitude one, which is NZ eq (52) for this knot. Stated over a generic commutative ring because the identity is algebraic — it uses only (1-w)(1-z) = (w-1)(z-1) and needs no analysis, no branch choice, and no series.

    theorem QOU.QBeta.NeumannZagierCuspParity.gluing_symm {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (z w : R) (h : z * w * (z - 1) * (w - 1) = 1) :
    w * z * (w - 1) * (z - 1) = 1

    The swap is an involution on the gluing variety: if (z, w) is a solution then so is (w, z). This is what makes v ↦ -v a symmetry of the deformation rather than merely a reparametrisation.

    2. Odd numerator ⇒ even cusp shape ⇒ the correction is second order #

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.NeumannZagierCuspParity.oddFun_div_even {v : ℝ → ℝ} (hodd : ∀ (x : ℝ), v (-x) = -v x) (u : ℝ) :
    v (-u) / -u = v u / u

    An odd v gives an even τ(u) = v(u)/u.

    The cusp shape is the ratio of the longitude to the meridian log-holonomy. When the numerator is odd — which §1 establishes for 4₁ — the ratio is even, so its expansion about u = 0 has no linear term and the leading correction to τ₀ is O(u²).

    This is the structural half of the probe's finding that the real modulus drift is second order: the quadratic suppression is forced by parity, not observed in the numbers.

    The even-ness of τ in the form actually used downstream: the first-order coefficient of an even function vanishes, so writing τ(u) = c₁ + c₃u² omits no u¹ term. Stated as the parity identity for the quadratic shape.

        Two independent sources describe one lattice. The corpus proves the 4₁ cusp lattice is the hexagonal ℤ⟨1, ω⟩; NZ give the cusp SHAPE as 2√-3. This identity says NZ's meridian-longitude lattice ℤ⟨1, τ₀⟩ is a SUBLATTICE of it, generated by 1 and -2 + 4ω.
