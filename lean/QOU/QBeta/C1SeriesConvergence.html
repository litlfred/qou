    Where the C1 series solutions converge #

    Bean qou-c1ln row 3. The connection matrix P = Y₀⁻¹Y_∞ of the ramified C1 operator is assembled from two series (computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_pole_divisor.py):

    • the 0-side ∑_k g_k z^k, with g₀ = 1, g₁ = 0 and

      g_{k+2} = t²p^{2(k+2)} / (t²p^{2(k+2)} + (q−1)tp^{k+2} + (q−1)) · g_k

    • the ∞-side ∑_k h_k z^{−k}, with h₀ = 1 and a two-term recursion whose coefficients carry p^{−k} and p^{−2k} against a denominator tending to the constant 1 − p².

    Everything downstream — Y₀, Y_∞, the inverse, the divisor — presupposes that these converge where they are used. That was numerical. This file derives it.

    What this file proves #

    • summable_zeroSideCoeff — the 0-side series converges for every ‖z‖ < 1. The ratio tends to 1 like 1 + O(‖p‖^{−k}), so the coefficients are bounded, which is all that radius ≥ 1 needs.
    • summable_infSideCoeff — the ∞-side series is entire: it converges for every u. The two recursion coefficients vanish geometrically while the denominator tends to a nonzero constant.

    Reusable infrastructure #

    Three of the lemmas are about sequences and say nothing about C1; they are stated and proved in the general form because Mathlib has none of them:

    • norm_le_of_ratio_le_one_add_geometric — a two-step recursion whose ratio is 1 + O(geometric) has bounded terms. The telescoped product ∏(1 + Cρᵏ) is controlled by exp (C ∑ ρᵏ) ≤ exp (C/(1−ρ)).
    • summable_of_norm_coeff_le — bounded coefficients ⇒ convergence on the open unit disc.
    • summable_of_two_step_geometric — a two-step recursion whose coefficients vanish geometrically gives an entire series. The majorant is M ∏_{j<k} (cσʲ) with σ = √ρ: the two-step lag costs exactly a square root, and σ² = ρ is what lets the induction close with a constant c instead of a k-dependent one.

    All three take the recursion hypothesis only for k ≥ K, since in both C1 applications the estimate only holds once the geometric tail has overtaken the constant term — for the ∞-side that is also what keeps the denominator away from zero.

    What this file does NOT prove — stated so it is not read for more #

    • Sharpness of the 0-side radius. Proved here is radius ≥ 1; that it is exactly 1 needs the mirror-image estimate ‖ratio‖ ≥ 1 − Cρᵏ and hence ∏(1 − Cρᵏ) > 0, giving a positive lower bound on the even coefficients and so divergence for ‖z‖ > 1. The route is the same shape as the upper bound (1 − x ≥ exp(−2x) for 0 ≤ x ≤ ½, from exp(2x) ≥ 1 + 2x), and is not taken here. Nothing downstream uses sharpness: the singularity's location is pinned by the θ_p zeros, not by this radius.
    • That these series solve the q-difference equation. Partly closed 2026-08-08: both recursions are now derived from the operator — see the section at the bottom of this file (zeroCoeffAt_eq_zero_iff, infCoeffAt_eq_zero_iff), with the coefficient collections machine-checked symbolically. What is still open is that these series, times their theta prefactors, are the canonical solutions at 0 and ∞ — the normalisation and uniqueness, not the recursion. That is row 4.
    • Y₀, Y_∞, the inverse, meromorphy — bean qou-c1ln row 4, untouched.

              The two recursions, derived from the operator #

              Added 2026-08-08 (bean qou-slvl item 2). Above, zeroSideCoeff and infSideCoeff were defined to match c3_w2_c1_pole_divisor.py, so the summability theorems certified convergence of stated sequences rather than of solutions. Both recursions are now consequences of

              L = (p⁴z² − 1)τ² − (p²−1)τ − (p²−1),   τF(z) = F(pz),  q = p².

              Neither side is a bare power series, and the theta prefactors are what make the brackets polynomial — via the functional equation θ_p(pw) = w·θ_p(w):

              • 0-side y₀ = e_t(z)·G(z), e_t = θ_p(z)/θ_p(z/t), G = ∑ g_k z^k. Then τe_t = t·e_t, so L y₀ / e_t = (p⁴z²−1)t²τ²G − (p²−1)t·τG − (p²−1)G, whose z^{k+2} coefficient is zeroCoeffAt.
              • ∞-side y_∞ = H(z)/θ_p(z/T), H = ∑ h_k z^{-k}. Then τ[1/θ_p(z/T)] = (T/z)/θ_p(z/T) and τ²[…] = T²/(pz²)·/θ_p(z/T), and in u = 1/z the bracket's u^{k+2} coefficient is infCoeffAt.

              That these collections really are those coefficients, and that solving them returns gc() and hc() verbatim, is machine-checked symbolically in computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_side_recursions_derived.py (4/4). The two theorems below prove, in general, that each collection vanishes iff its recursion holds.

              Still not established: that these series, times their theta prefactors, are the canonical solutions at 0 and ∞ — that is qou-c1ln row 4. What is established is that the recursions are forced by the operator.
