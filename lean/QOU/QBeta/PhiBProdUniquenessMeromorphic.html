    phiBProd uniqueness for a merely-meromorphic, continuous second solution (broadening) #

    PhiBProdLociDensity.phiBProd_uniqueUpToScalar_of_normalForm requires the second solution Φ₁ to be in meromorphic normal form (MeromorphicNFOn Φ₁). That hypothesis is load-bearing, not cosmetic: a general MeromorphicOn function may be discontinuous at an isolated removable point, and the pointwise proportionality ∀ z, … → Φ₁ z = c·phiBProd z genuinely fails there.

    This file gives the honest broadening: MeromorphicOn Φ₁ plus continuity on the nonzero locus (∀ z, phiBProd z ≠ 0 → ContinuousAt Φ₁ z) — a strictly weaker, more elementary hypothesis than MeromorphicNFOn (which implies both). The key is MeromorphicAt.analyticAt: a meromorphic function that is continuous at z is analytic there, discharging the Φ₁ analyticity that hr_ratio needs without the normal-form machinery.

    phiBProd_uniqueUpToScalar_of_meromorphic — the resulting statement. The dense periodicity loci are reconstructed from the merged countability lemmas (PhiBProdLociDensity), so the only inputs are MeromorphicOn Φ₁ and continuity-where-finite — exactly what "the physical Φ_b is a genuine meromorphic function" means.

    Verified lean-direct against the merged phiBProd chain. Sorry-free.

    hr_ratio for a continuous meromorphic second solution. If Φ₁ is meromorphic on ℂ and continuous at z (where phiBProd b z ≠ 0), then Φ₁ is analytic at z (MeromorphicAt.analyticAt), so the ratio Φ₁/phiBProd is analytic — hence its normal form agrees with the pointwise ratio. No normal-form hypothesis on Φ₁.
