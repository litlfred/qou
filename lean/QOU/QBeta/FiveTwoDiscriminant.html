    The GGM curve's discriminant vanishes on |x| = 1 #

    Bean qou-trns; the Lean form of Route 16 (computations/probes/qbeta_5two_route16_discriminant_vanishes_probe.py).

    What this refutes #

    qou-trns recorded the transport's sub-gap 1 as closed — "k = 0 is pinned by the base point" — with a step that reads

    "The three branches yⱼ(x) are continuous and never collide on the arc (min |disc| = 0.348), so g is continuous."

    and stated the envelope theorem's implicit-function hypothesis as satisfied, on the same constant. Both are false. The discriminant has a zero on the unit circle, so two branches collide there.

    0.348 was a grid minimum from an ~360-point scan of [0, π]; the dip is narrow (|disc'| ≈ 87), so refining the scan gives 0.068, then 0.0068, then 0.

    Why this is Lean-sized #

    The refutation needs no analysis of the branches at all. Three ingredients:

    1. disc is the standard cubic discriminant of the GGM curve — a ring identity (disc_eq_cubic_formula).
    2. disc is palindromic, so disc x = x⁴ · Q (x + 1/x) with Q u = u⁴ − 6u³ + 7u² + 6u − 31 — a field_simp; ring identity (disc_eq_Q).
    3. Q (−2) = 49 > 0 > −23 = Q (−1), so Q has a real root in (−2,−1) by the intermediate value theorem. Points of |x| = 1 are exactly those with x + 1/x real in [−2,2], and (−2,−1) is inside it.

    So the whole thing is polynomial algebra plus IVT, and Q_neg_two/Q_neg_one are norm_num.

    Scope #

    This refutes the argument. It does not endanger the transport's conclusion: Route 17 shows S(e^{iφ}) − S(1) = φ² holds to 4e-40 through the branch point, because S sums over all roots and is therefore symmetric — a collision permutes roots, and a symmetric function cannot notice a permutation. Only the per-root labelling dies, and only the argument used labels.

    The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms.

            From a root of Q to a point of the unit circle #

            For u ∈ [−2,2] the point x = u/2 + i·√(1 − u²/4) has ‖x‖ = 1 and x + 1/x = u. That is the correspondence the reduction trades on.
