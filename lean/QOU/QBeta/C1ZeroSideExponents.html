    C1 at 0 — the Fuchsian exponents #

    The 0-side of the ramified C1 operator

    L = (p⁴z² − 1)τ² − (p²−1)τ − (p²−1),   τF(z) = F(pz),  q = p²

    is regular singular: at z = 0 the coefficient a₂ is −1, so the indicial equation is

    t² + (q−1)·t + (q−1) = 0

    (t the eigenvalue of τ). This file records what that equation forces.

    What is here #

    • zeroSideIndicial and Vieta: the two exponents multiply to q − 1 and sum to −(q − 1).
    • discriminant_eq — the discriminant is (q−1)(q−5), which factors. So the exponents are a conjugate pair exactly for 1 < q < 5 (conjugate_pair_iff), and the substrate q₀ ≈ 1.10998 is comfortably inside that window — the 0-side exponents there are genuinely complex, not real.
    • abs_sq_eq_q_sub_one — for a conjugate pair, |t|² = q − 1, so |t| = √(q−1). Measured at the substrate as 0.33163018974662244642 against √(q−1) = 0.33163018974662244642 (20 digits).

    Why this is the whole of it #

    The point of the 0-side is to supply one half of the connection matrix P = Y₀⁻¹ Y_∞. That matrix is now obtained (computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_connection_matrix.py), though the Stokes multipliers still are not — see below.

    Correction (2026-08-07): the "degenerate basis" negative was WRONG #

    An earlier revision of this file recorded that a first numerical attempt found the 0-side basis degenerate — normalised determinant |det|/max² of 2.4e-18 at the matching point — and blamed the normalisation e_t(z) = θ_p(z)/θ_p(z/t) for "placing the two conjugate exponents ~17 orders of magnitude apart". That diagnosis is impossible, and has been refuted. Three separate errors:

    • p = √q is real, so θ_p has real coefficients and θ_p(conj w) = conj θ_p(w). The exponents are a conjugate pair (conjugate_pair_iff below), so for real z, |θ_p(z/t₂)| = |θ_p(conj(z/t₁))| = |θ_p(z/t₁)|, hence |e_{t₁}(z)| = |e_{t₂}(z)| exactly. Measured ratio is 1.0. A 1e17 disparity between the two columns cannot occur.
    • |det|/max² is not a conditioning measure. The Casoratian satisfies W(pz) = −(q−1)·W(z)/(p⁴z²−1) exactly (verified to 1e-150), so it decays under outward propagation for purely analytic reasons while the columns stay independent. The scale-invariant measure |det|/(‖col₀‖·‖col₁‖) = |sin∠| comes out 0.59 … 0.72 across the disc.
    • The bases do share a domain. h being entire in 1/z means it converges for every z ≠ 0; with the 0-side radius 1 the two overlap on the whole punctured disc and match directly. The propagation that lost the conditioning was never required.

    Also worth recording: P(pz) = P(z) is automatic for any two fundamental matrices of one system (Y₀(pz) = A(z)Y₀(z) and Y_∞(pz) = A(z)Y_∞(z), so A cancels). It is a correctness check on the numerics and was never evidence of anything — including in the attempt that read its "passing" as meaningful.

    What the connection matrix looks like #

    P = [[c₊, c₋], [conj c₊, conj c₋]], elliptic per entry to 1e-27 … 1e-55, with c₋ carrying simple poles exactly on z ∈ T·p^ℤ (the zeros of θ_p(−z/T)).

    Still open, and not claimed: c₊ is not an exact constant — its z-variation is precision-independent and grows with q, so "connection constant" is a q → 1 statement only; the law behind that variation is unidentified; and the Stokes multipliers are still not extracted. Having the connection matrix is a prerequisite for them, not the same thing.

    So: the exponents below are solid, the geometry of the indicial equation is settled, and the connection matrix exists. The multipliers are the open work.

    The pole divisor #

    c₊ has simple poles at [−1] and [−T]; c₋ at [−1] and [+T], where T = √(q−1)/q^{3/4}. All of them are zeros of a θ_p, whose zero set is the single orbit −p^ℤ — see QOU/QBeta/C1ConnectionPoles.lean (sorry-free, ⊇ direction) and computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_pole_divisor.py. The [−1] pole comes from the common numerator θ_p(z) of the character e_t above, so it sits in both columns and is an artifact of this normalisation, not connection data.
