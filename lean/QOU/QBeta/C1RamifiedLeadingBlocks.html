    C1 at ∞ — ramification, and the two leading blocks #

    The W2 ∞-side of the c₃ investigation. The q-difference operator is

    L₁ = (q²x − 1)·σ² − (q−1)·σ − (q−1)        (C1, RSZ route-A)

    and the question is the Stokes data of its solutions at ∞.

    Why ramification is forced, not chosen #

    At ∞ the valuation is v_∞(a) = −deg(a), so the three coefficients sit at

    (0, 0),  (1, 0),  (2, −1).

    The middle point lies strictly above the chord (0,0)–(2,−1) (middle_strictly_above_chord), so it is not a vertex: the Newton polygon is the single edge (0,0)–(2,−1) of slope −1/2. A non-integral slope makes the companion matrix nilpotent at ∞, and one cannot read a leading block off a nilpotent limit. That nilpotency is not an obstacle to route around — it is what a non-integral slope looks like in the companion frame.

    RSZ (Astérisque 355 §3.3) prescribes the fix: a slope with denominator d is resolved by ramifying with index d. Here d = 2, via x = z², q = p², τF(z) = F(pz). After it the hull is (0,0), (1,0), (2,−2), slope −1, integral (ramified_slope_is_integral), and the leading data is readable.

    CORRECTION 2026-08-07 — the edge polynomial carries a theta twist #

    An earlier version of this file stated the edge polynomial as p⁴T² − p² + 1, with roots ±√(q−1)/q, and called those roots the leading blocks. That was the differential-equation convention applied to a q-difference operator, and it is wrong.

    The canonical solutions here are theta-normalised: y = θ_p(z/T)⁻¹ h(z), with θ_p(pw) = w·θ_p(w). Iterating that gives

    θ_p(pⁱ w) = p^{i(i−1)/2} · wⁱ · θ_p(w)

    a quadratic factor in i. So the aᵢ(z)τⁱ term contributes Aᵢ · p^{−i(i−1)/2} · Tⁱ, and the edge polynomial is

    Σᵢ Aᵢ · p^{−i(i−1)/2} · Tⁱ = 0

    With the twist it is the indicial equation of the theta ansatz. Without it the i = 2 term was too large by p, moving the roots by q^{1/4}:

    edge polynomial root
    untwisted (old, wrong) p⁴T² − p² + 1 ±√(q−1)/q
    twisted (correct) p³T² − p² + 1 ±√(q−1)/q^{3/4}

    This is not a matter of taste between conventions. The untwisted root provably fails the indicial equation — numerically −5.6e-3, not roundoff (computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_theta_canonical.py, which also builds both canonical solutions explicitly and verifies them by substitution to ~1e-62). untwisted_forces_trivial_p below is the algebraic form of that: the two roots agree only at p = 1, so the error is invisible exactly in the classical limit and nowhere else.

    The arithmetic that the old version proved — that p⁴T² − p² + 1 has roots ±√(q−1)/q — was true about that polynomial. What was wrong was calling that polynomial the edge polynomial, and its roots the blocks that index the Stokes sectors. Same failure mode as bean qou-64jx: an object computed correctly, then given a name whose meaning it does not have.

    Scope — what is NOT proved here #

    This is the leading exponential data that indexes the two Stokes sectors. It is not the two Stokes multipliers, which are the connection data between sectors and need the connection matrix of the theta-normalised solutions at 0 and ∞. Prerequisite, not closure. Nothing is wired to production.

    The base-ring split follows §7c: the edge algebra is stated over a generic CommRing/Field, and only the square-root extraction descends to ℝ.

        Part 2 — the theta twist #

        θ_p(pw) = w·θ_p(w) iterated gives θ_p(pⁱw) = p^{i(i−1)/2} wⁱ θ_p(w). The exponent i(i−1)/2 is the triangular number, and it is what the τⁱ term of the edge polynomial must be divided by. Only i ≤ 2 is needed for a rank-2 operator, so the concrete values are recorded rather than the general recursion.

          Part 3 — the (twisted) edge polynomial #

          Generic algebra: no ordering, no square roots.

          source
          theorem QOU.QBeta.C1Ramified.edge_eq_iff {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (p T : R) :
          p ^ 3 * T ^ 2 - p ^ 2 + 1 = 0 ↔ p ^ 3 * T ^ 2 = p ^ 2 - 1

          The edge equation. With the theta twist, the i = 2 coefficient p⁴ becomes p⁴·p^{−1} = p³, so the edge polynomial is p³T² − p² + 1 and the edge equation is p³T² = p² − 1.

          source
          theorem QOU.QBeta.C1Ramified.untwisted_forces_trivial_p {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (p S : K) (hp : p ≠ 0) (hS : S ≠ 0) (huntwisted : p ^ 4 * S ^ 2 = p ^ 2 - 1) (htwisted : p ^ 3 * S ^ 2 = p ^ 2 - 1) :
          p = 1

          The untwisted root is not merely a different convention — it is wrong except in the classical limit.

          If some S ≠ 0 satisfied both the untwisted edge equation p⁴S² = p²−1 and the correct one p³S² = p²−1, then p = 1. So the two disagree at every p ≠ 1, and the old answer cannot be recovered by renaming.

          Part 4 — solving over ℝ #

          The square root is the one archimedean step (§7c).

          source
          theorem QOU.QBeta.C1Ramified.leading_blocks (p T : ℝ) (hp : 0 < p) (hp1 : 1 ≤ p ^ 2) :
          p ^ 3 * T ^ 2 = p ^ 2 - 1 ↔ T = √((p ^ 2 - 1) / p ^ 3) ∨ T = -√((p ^ 2 - 1) / p ^ 3)

          The leading blocks. For p > 0 with p² ≥ 1, the solutions of the twisted edge equation are B₁,₂ = ±√((p²−1)/p³). In the unramified variable q = p² that is ±√(q−1)/q^{3/4} — not ±√(q−1)/q.

          source
          theorem QOU.QBeta.C1Ramified.blocks_distinct_iff (p : ℝ) (hp : 0 < p) (hp1 : 1 ≤ p ^ 2) :
          √((p ^ 2 - 1) / p ^ 3) ≠ -√((p ^ 2 - 1) / p ^ 3) ↔ p ≠ 1

          The two sectors are distinct exactly when p ≠ 1. At p = 1 the blocks collide — the classical, non-q-deformed limit, which is also the only place the untwisted convention would have agreed.

          source
          theorem QOU.QBeta.C1Ramified.stokes_sector_count (p : ℝ) (hp : 0 < p) (hp1 : 1 < p) :
          {√((p ^ 2 - 1) / p ^ 3), -√((p ^ 2 - 1) / p ^ 3)}.card = 2

          The Stokes space is two-dimensional at p ≠ 1: exactly two roots, hence exactly two sectors.
