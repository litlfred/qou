    QOU.QBeta.TraceNondegeneracyInsufficient — nondegeneracy alone is not enough #

    Brick 4 of qou-s7z2, and it is a negative one. It was reached by trying to assemble bricks 2 and 3 into a WenzlTowerStep instance at the Markov trace, and finding that the assembly cannot close — for a reason worth recording rather than working around.

    The shape of the problem #

    WenzlTowerStep.semisimple_ascend reads

    IsSemisimpleRing Hn →
(∀ y ≠ 0, ∃ z, trn1 (y * z) ≠ 0) →
IsSemisimpleRing Hn1

    Brick 1 (MarkovTraceQuotient.quot_pairing_nondegenerate) makes the second hypothesis free on the quotient: after quotienting by the radical, every nonzero class is separated by the pairing, by construction. So one might hope the class becomes easy over TL_n.

    It does not, and the reason is not a missing lemma. Nondegeneracy of a trace does not imply semisimplicity. The corpus's semisimplicity engine (MathlibExt.isSemisimpleRing_of_regTraceForm_nondegenerate) is about the regular trace form x, y ↦ trace(L_{xy}) — the trace of left multiplication, an intrinsic invariant — not about an arbitrary linear functional that happens to pair non-degenerately. Those are different hypotheses, and only the former is strong enough.

    The counterexample #

    The dual numbers ℚ[ε]/(ε²) carry the functional τ(a + bε) = b. It is linear, cyclic (the ring is commutative), and non-degenerate: given a + bε ≠ 0, pair with ε if a ≠ 0, and with 1 if a = 0. Yet ℚ[ε] is not semisimple — ε is a nonzero nilpotent, so the ring is not reduced.

    So an instance of WenzlTowerStep at a general trace cannot be obtained from its own nondegeneracy hypothesis. Something must supply the regular trace form's nondegeneracy, and in the Hecke setting that is exactly HeckeGramFactorial's open input — the general T_w-basis Gram determinant, the classical Schur-element product formula. n = 2 is done; no fabricated instance may stand in for the general value.

    What this does and does not say #

    Scope (b), a structural exclusion, in the sense of AGENTS.md's refutation rule: it is not "this route did not work numerically", it is a proved counterexample to the implication nondegenerate trace ⇒ semisimple. Cite it as such.

    It does not say WenzlTowerStep is wrong, unprovable, or that the TL-quotient route is dead. It says the class's own nondegeneracy hypothesis is not what discharges it, so effort aimed at strengthening or re-deriving that hypothesis is misdirected — the binding constraint is the Gram determinant. Bricks 1–3 are unaffected and remain what they claim to be.
