    QOU.QBeta.TLTowerRadicalDescent — the tower inclusion and the two radicals #

    Brick 3 of qou-s7z2. Bricks 1 and 2 built the quotient A / rad τ and showed the Hecke generators descend to it. What neither does — and what a WenzlTowerStep instance over TL_n still needs — is a relation between the radicals at consecutive ranks. They are different ideals in different algebras, and nothing so far connects them.

    The hypothesis that connects them #

    A bare inclusion ι : Hn →ₐ Hn1 says nothing about the two radicals: membership in rad τn1 quantifies over all of Hn1, not over ι '' Hn, so no amount of compatibility between τn and τn1 on the subalgebra constrains it.

    What does connect them is a conditional expectation ε : Hn1 →ₗ Hn together with the level-n+1 trace being the induced one, τn1 = τn ∘ ε (MarkovTowerBridge.towerTrace). Then the bimodule law at z = 1 (epsilon_inclusion_mul, ε (ι y * x) = y * ε x) moves any test element of Hn1 back down to Hn, and the two radicals become comparable.

    What comes out — reflection, not just preservation #

    The expected statement is that ι carries rad τn into rad τn1. That holds, and is the easy direction. The converse also holds, because ε retracts ι and is therefore surjective, so a test element of Hn can always be lifted: ι a ∈ rad τn1 ↔ a ∈ rad τn.

    That is worth more than preservation alone. Preservation alone would give a map of quotients; reflection makes it injective, i.e. TL_n ↪ TL_{n+1}, which is what makes the quotients a tower rather than a sequence of unrelated algebras.

    Scope #

    This is still not a WenzlTowerStep instance at the Markov trace. It supplies the tower's injectivity; what remains is assembling that with brick 2's presentation descent into the class, and — untouched by any of bricks 1–3 — HeckeGramFactorial's open input, the general T_w-basis determinant.

    Note also that HeckeConditionalExpectation is itself an OPEN §3b conjecture class with no instance. Everything here is therefore conditional on it, in the §3b-cond sense: the implication is proved, the premise is not established.

    The inclusion carries the lower radical into the upper one.

    If a pairs to zero against all of Hn, then ι a pairs to zero against all of Hn1 under the induced trace — because ε moves the test element back down: τn1 (ι a * x) = τn (ε (ι a * x)) = τn (a * ε x) = 0.

    The conditional expectation is doing the work. Without it the statement is not merely unproved but unstateable: nothing would relate a test element of Hn1 to one of Hn.

    ι a ∈ TemperleyLiebTower.traceRadical (Machinery.MarkovTowerBridge.towerTrace trn ε) ↔ a ∈ TemperleyLiebTower.traceRadical trn

    The tower inclusion detects the radical exactly.

    Both directions together. This is the statement that makes the quotients a tower: the induced map Hn / rad τn → Hn1 / rad τn1 is injective, so TL_n sits inside TL_{n+1} rather than merely mapping to it.

    Non-vacuity #

    The three statements above would be worth nothing if traceRadical trn were always everything or always {0} in the situations they apply to. The guard is the same one brick 1 uses, transported up the tower: when the lower trace does not kill the unit, the unit is outside both radicals, so neither is everything.

    Non-vacuity: the unit escapes both radicals when τn 1 ≠ 0. The upper statement is the interesting one — it says the induced trace on Hn1 is not identically zero on the image, which is what stops map_mem_traceRadical from being a statement about the whole algebra.
