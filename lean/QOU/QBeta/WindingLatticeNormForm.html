    The winding lattice of a braid-word geodesic: two quadratic orders, one parity #

    Why only one of the two orders can carry a sign flip.

    A braid word's particle/vortex path closes to a geodesic on the cusp torus, a class (m, ℓ) in the cusp lattice ℤ⟨1, ω⟩. With the meridian log-holonomy ln q₀ (meridian-holonomy-boost, proved) and the 4₁ cusp shape ω = e^{iπ/3} (cusp-shape-bar-parity, proved), the imaginary part of the complex volume collapses onto an integer binary quadratic form. This file formalises the two facts that decide which order can produce a sign flip:

    • disc 12 — the cusp-torus order ℤ[√3]. Its fundamental unit 2 + √3 has norm +1, so every power has norm +1 (pell3_norm). A parity-driven sign flip is structurally unavailable here.
    • disc 5 — Bring's golden order ℤ[φ], φ = (1+√5)/2. Its fundamental unit φ has norm −1, so the norm of φⁿ alternates: (−1)ⁿ (goldenPow_norm). A parity-driven sign flip exists here.

    The one-line reason is normPhi_step versus norm3_step: the golden recursion (a,b) ↦ (b, a+b) negates the norm at each step, while the silver recursion (a,b) ↦ (2a+3b, a+2b) preserves it.

    Everything here is ring-level algebra over a generic CommRing (§7c) or over ℤ; no archimedean structure, no ordering, no ℝ. The elements s and the basis vector φ appear only through their defining relations (s * s = 3; φ² = φ + 1), so nothing depends on a choice of real square root.

    Companion probes: computations/qbeta/qbeta_cusp_lattice_geodesic_probe.py (the disc-12 identity, 5 assertions) and computations/qbeta/qbeta_disc5_parity_probe.py (the parity contrast, 4 assertions).

    Scope. These are theorems about the two quadratic orders. They do not establish that the physical S-channel sign is given by the winding parity: that link is derived only on the cusp torus and is transported to Bring's surface by analogy in the companion probe, where it is labelled conjectural.

    theorem QOU.QBeta.WindingLatticeNormForm.winding_lorentz_form {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (c s m l : R) (hs : s * s = 3) :
    (c * (2 * m + l)) ^ 2 - (c * l * s) ^ 2 = c ^ 2 * ((2 * m + l) ^ 2 - 3 * l ^ 2)

    The winding Lorentz form (disc 12). For the geodesic u = c·(m + ℓω) with cusp shape ω = (1 + s)/2 where s * s = 3 (i.e. s stands for √3, ω = e^{iπ/3}), the doubled real and imaginary parts are c(2m+ℓ) and c·ℓ·s, and the indefinite pairing Re² − Im² collapses onto the integer form (2m+ℓ)² − 3ℓ² — the norm form of ℤ[√3].

    Stated over a generic CommRing with s given only by s * s = 3, so it holds for any base ring carrying a square root of 3; no ordering is used.

            The parity flip on Bring's golden order. N(φⁿ) = (−1)ⁿ: the norm of the n-th power alternates with the parity of n. Contrast pell3_norm, where the norm is constantly +1.

            The structural contrast, in one statement. On the cusp-torus order the norm of every unit power is +1; on Bring's golden order it is (−1)ⁿ. Hence a sign that alternates with the winding number is available on the disc-5 order and structurally unavailable on the disc-12 one — which is exactly why the sign flip must live on Bring's surface rather than on the cusp torus.

            source
            theorem QOU.QBeta.WindingLatticeNormForm.norm3_ne_neg_one (a b : ℤ) :
            norm3 (a, b) ≠ -1

            Structural exclusion (mod-3 obstruction). The value −1 is not represented by the disc-12 norm form at all — by any lattice point, not merely by unit powers: reducing a² − 3b² = −1 mod 3 demands a² ≡ 2 (mod 3), and 2 is not a square mod 3. This is the negative-Pell insolvability for D = 3, the cited invariant behind pell3_norm's "no parity flip": the norm (Kummer/Galois norm of ℚ(√3)/ℚ) simply never takes the value −1 on the cusp-torus order, while on the golden order it does (goldenPow_norm_alternates, normPhi (goldenPow 3) = −1).
