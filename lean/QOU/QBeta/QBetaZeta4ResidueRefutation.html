          §4. C(y) is a degree-3 obstruction: no rational root, missed by every #

             quadratic substrate anchor

          C(y) = y³ − 4y² + 3y − 2 is monic with constant term −2, so by the rational root theorem any rational root is an integer dividing 2, i.e. y ∈ {±1, ±2}. The four evaluations below are all nonzero, so C has no rational root and (being a monic integer cubic) is irreducible over ℚ — its root generates a genuine degree-3 field (S₃ splitting, disc −152).

          Consequence for the c_3 odd channel: every substrate quadratic anchor sits at a rational trace value y = q²+q⁻² — golden φ ↦ y = 3, silver 1+√2 ↦ y = 6, classical q = 1 ↦ y = 2 — and C is nonzero at each. A degree-≤2 substrate number can never be a root of an irreducible cubic, so no quadratic-anchor / real-q closure can resolve the odd carrier; this is the structural reason those closures (KC-refuted class) keep failing. (Numerical aside, NOT a structural claim: C(3) = −2 coincides with the c_3 → −2 q=1 shadow value; flagged as a coincidence pending any derivation.)

          §5. Knot-sector decomposition: the degree-3 field is the Hopf sector #

          Where does the cubic field come from? The bar-odd part of the net decay-width carrier Δ_net = Δ(neutron) − Δ(proton) on the H₃ normal-form basis {𝟏, σ₀, σ₁, σ₀σ₁, σ₁σ₀, σ₀σ₁σ₀} (knots unknot / virtual / Hopf / trefoil) decomposes exactly by knot sector (reproducible exact Laurent arithmetic, probe qbeta_odd_carrier_sector_decomposition.py, from free-neutron-decay.witness.json):

          • virtual (σ₀, σ₁; 1 crossing) → 0 and trefoil (σ₀σ₁σ₀; 3 crossings) → 0: the odd-crossing sectors are bar-EVEN, contributing nothing to the bar-odd carrier.
          • unknot (𝟏; identity) → a quadratic C_unknot(y) = 2y² − 7y + 4 (disc 17, field ℚ(√17)), in y = q²+q⁻².
          • Hopf (σ₀σ₁+σ₁σ₀; 2 crossings) → the cubic C_Hopf(y) = y³ − 6y² + 10y − 6 (irreducible over ℚ, disc −76 = −2²·19).

          with C = C_Hopf + C_unknot. So the degree-3 field of the odd channel is sourced entirely by the Hopf-link (double-crossing) sector; the identity adds only a quadratic, and the odd-crossing sectors vanish. It is NOT a 3-dim representation (H₃'s Wedderburn blocks are 1,2,1 — none is 3-dim) nor a single knot invariant — it is specifically the 2-crossing amplitude in the isospin difference (refines the KC18 identification of the odd channel with the Markov crossing term). The discriminant prime 19 is shared by C_Hopf (−76) and the total C (−152 = −2³·19) — the arithmetic fingerprint of the sector.

              §6. Classical (q=1) shadow: the Hopf cubic reproduces the c_3 shadow #

              At the classical trace point q = 1 the trace variable is y = q²+q⁻² = 2. The c_3 q=1 shadow is −2 (registry KC10, = J_Hopf(q=1)). The Hopf-sector cubic reproduces it exactly — a carrier-side confirmation that the c_3→−2 shadow lives in the Hopf (double-crossing) sector (KC20/§5).

              Scope (STRICT). This is a shadow-level (q=1) identity only. It does NOT extend to the substrate: C_Hopf at the substrate trace value is anchor-rigid while the physical c_3 swings 2.375% across anchors, so the cubic does not determine c_3 off q=1 — the swing lives in the crossing weight w(q) (registry KC19). See docs/audits/2026-07-07-hopf-cubic-vs-crossing-weight-shadow-bridge.md. The total carrier gives C(2) = −4 = C_Hopf(2) + C_unknot(2); the shadow −2 matches the per-sector Hopf value J_Hopf(1), not the total.
