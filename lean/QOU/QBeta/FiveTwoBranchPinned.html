    The branch pinning, end to end: Σⱼ log(1 − x·yⱼ) = log x #

    Bean qou-trns. FiveTwoEnvelopeSum proved exp(Σⱼ log(1 − x·yⱼ) − log x) = 1 from Vieta alone, and FiveTwoArgSector proved that the branch integer vanishes given a bound on Σⱼ arg(1 − x·yⱼ). Between them sat a step nobody had written: taking imaginary parts. This file writes it, and closes the pinning.

    The step #

    Complex.exp_eq_one_iff turns exp(A − log x) = 1 into A − log x = n·(2πi) for an integer n. Its imaginary part is

    Im A = arg x + 2π·n,

    and Im(log w) = arg w makes the left side Σⱼ arg(1 − x·yⱼ) — exactly the shape branchInt_eq_zero_of_sector consumes. Once n = 0, the original equation gives A = log x outright: no need to recover the real part separately, which is the small trick that makes this clean.

    What it costs #

    Nothing beyond Mathlib. The content was already there on both sides; the gap was that neither end had been connected to the other, and a chain of proved lemmas with an unwritten join is not a proof. Recorded here rather than folded silently into a larger file, because "assembly of proved parts" is exactly the phrase under which a missing step hides.

    The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms.
