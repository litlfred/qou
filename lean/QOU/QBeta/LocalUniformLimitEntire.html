    Local-uniform limit of analytic functions is analytic (infinite-product-analyticity infra) #

    The second half of the infinite-product-analyticity gap for phiBProd (input 1). The first half — each factor entire, every finite partial product entire — is PhiBProdFactorAnalytic (merged). This file supplies the analytic-limit step:

    • analyticOnNhd_of_tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn — a sequence of functions analytic on an open set s ⊆ ℂ that converges locally uniformly to g has analytic limit g. Proof: the limit is holomorphic (TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn.differentiableOn, the Weierstrass convergence theorem) and holomorphic-on-open ⇒ analytic (DifferentiableOn.analyticOnNhd, the Cauchy-integral bridge).

    Applied to phiBProd: the partial products ∏_{k<n} numFactor b · k are entire (PhiBProdFactorAnalytic.analyticOnNhd_numPartial), so if they converge locally uniformly to fun z => ∏' k, numFactor b z k, that infinite product is entire — discharging the AnalyticOnNhd hypothesis of PhiBProdMeromorphic.phiBProd_meromorphicOn_of_analytic (input 1). The sole remaining input is that local-uniform convergence (phiBProd_num_analytic_of_tendsto, ..._den_...), available in principle from PhiBProd's geometric Multipliable bounds for Im b² > 0.

    Verified lean-direct against the Mathlib LocallyUniformLimit + Complex.Analytic cones (built from pinned source) + PhiBProd. Sorry-free.
