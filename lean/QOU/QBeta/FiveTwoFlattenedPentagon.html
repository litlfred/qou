        Why (y₁, ȳ₁) is out of reach: it is ON the cut #

        The region hypothesis is not a technicality to be discharged later, and it is not that the 5₂ conjugate pair lies in some awkward corner. It lies on the boundary of the domain, exactly:

        y₃·(1 − y₁y₂) = y₃ − 1        [from `y₁y₂y₃ = 1`, one line]

        so 1 − y₁ȳ₁ = (y₃−1)/y₃, and y₃ = 0.4301597… ∈ (0,1) makes that a negative real — numerically −1.3247179…, the negated plastic number, which is Route 55's map showing up again. A negative real is not in slitPlane, so PentagonRegular fails at that configuration: log(1 − xy) sits on its branch cut.

        That is the whole obstruction, and it is sharper than "no region was found":

        • the straight segment from any real a ∈ (0,1) to y₁, with ȳ₁ fixed, is regular at every interior point — only the endpoint fails, and only in the one argument 1 − wy;
        • the approach is from the upper half-plane (Im(1 − wȳ₁) = a(1−t)·Im y₁ > 0), which is the side on which the principal Complex.log is continuous.

        So what is missing is a boundary-continuity argument, not a region. Measured in computations/probes/qbeta_5two_route57_on_the_cut_probe.py.

        The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms.

        The flattened sum is symmetric in its two variables, like the unflattened one: x ↔ y permutes the five triples (argument, Λ, Λ') rather than changing them (1 ↔ 2, 3 ↔ 5, 4 fixed).
