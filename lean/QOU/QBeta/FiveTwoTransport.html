    S(φ) in Lean, and S(π) − S(0) = π² #

    Bean qou-trns. Everything the envelope step needs was proved; what was missing was the object it is about. S(φ) = Σⱼ Re V(x φ, yⱼ φ) had never been written down as a Lean function, so envelope_integrate had nothing to be applied to. This file writes it and applies it.

    What is defined #

    arcX φ = exp(iφ), and

    envelopeS Y₁ Y₂ Y₃ φ = Σⱼ Re (V (arcX φ, Yⱼ φ)) .

    The three root-paths are parameters, not constructed. Constructing a global differentiable labelling is a monodromy question — the discriminant vanishes at φ₀ and two branches collide there — and exists_hasDerivAt_root gives one only locally. Taking the labelling as a hypothesis is the honest factoring: it is what the IFT supplies on each side of φ₀, and envelope_integrate is built to tolerate exactly one exceptional point.

    Why one exceptional point is enough, and not a fudge. S is a symmetric function of the roots, so as a function of φ it needs no labelling at all — it is well defined and continuous across φ₀, where the individual branches collide and swap. Route 31 measures this: the symmetric sum satisfies S(φ) − S(0) = φ² on both sides of φ₀ and at φ₀ itself, and dS/dφ = 2φ to working precision. The labelling is needed only to differentiate term by term, which is a local operation — hence a hypothesis, and hence one exceptional point.

    The chain, and where each link comes from #

    step theorem
    d/dφ Re V(x φ, Yⱼ φ) = Re(∂V/∂x · i·x) hasDerivAt_re_deformedV_envelope
    Σⱼ ∂V/∂x = −2·log x / x sum_dVdx_of_pinned, fed by logSumA_eq_log and logSumB_eq_neg_log
    (−2 log x/x)·(i·x) = 2φ envelope_value, using log(exp(iφ)) = iφ
    S′ = 2φ off one point ⟹ S φ = S 0 + φ² envelope_integrate

    so S(π) − S(0) = π², which is what the transport wants.

    ⚠ Two hypotheses that were nearly fatal #

    Both were caught by Route 31 G4, the non-vacuity gate, and both would have made this file's main theorem true-but-empty.

    −y ∈ slitPlane is not in VSide. An earlier draft had it, and it can never hold at the positive real root — −t is a negative real. On this arc one of the three roots is real and positive at every φ, so the theorem would have been vacuous at all 199 sampled points. The log(−y) derivative is now passed in, by hasDerivAt_log_neg_of_slitPlane off the cut or hasDerivAt_log_neg_ofReal on it.

    ⚠ Read the hypothesis on ∂V/∂y carefully #

    It is Re(∂V/∂y · Yⱼ′) = 0, not ∂V/∂y = 0. The stronger form is false at the positive real root, where ∂V/∂y = 2πi/t because −t sits on log's cut — see FiveTwoEnvelopeWiring's docstring and Route 30 G2. The weaker form holds at all three roots, and real parts are all S ever sees.

    The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms.

          Every root of the curve on |x| = 1 lies in notCut — the real/complex case split, discharged.

          notCut = {w | Im w ≠ 0 ∨ Re w < 1}. A root with Im ≠ 0 takes the first disjunct outright. A real root takes the second: on |x| = 1 the y-cubic is unitCubic (Re x) with real coefficients, and no real root of that reaches 1 when Re x ≤ 1 (unitCubic_root_lt_one).

          ⚠ Both branches are live on the arc, so neither is defensive. Route 36 G5: the arc crosses the discriminant at c* = −0.74221766588, giving three real roots at 46 of 199 sampled points and one real plus a conjugate pair at the other 153. That is also why the real branch leans on unitCubic_root_lt_one — which bounds every real root — rather than on exists_pinning_data's distinguished t ∈ (0,1), which would leave two of the three unhandled at those 46 points.

          ⚠ And the disjunct attribution here is the exact one, not the numerical one. polyroots returns |Im| ~ 2.6e-41 for the real root at one arc point, so a split driven by a floating Im ≠ 0 test would route that root through the first disjunct — which is false in exact arithmetic, and is the branch this proof cannot take. Route 36 G2 classifies by root type for that reason.

          ⚠ hx is load-bearing — do not weaken it. The statement reads as though it might hold for general x, and it does not. The curve is quadratic in x, so fixing a bad y and solving produces a counterexample outright: at y = 2 it is 2x² − 9x + 2 = 0, giving x = (9 ± √65)/4, both real and neither on the unit circle, with y = 2 real and Re y ≥ 1 — so y ∉ notCut. Route 36 G6 carries it exactly. hx is used twice and essentially: once for curve_eq_unitCubic, once for Re x ≤ 1.
