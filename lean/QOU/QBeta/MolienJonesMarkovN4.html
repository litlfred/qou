    The S₄ Molien-predicted weights satisfy the Markov normalisation and the #

    convention-D two-row cap (n = 4) #

    qou-3quc — the general-n extension of the n=3 Molien ↔ Jones–Markov identification (MolienJonesMarkovN3.lean). The general-n formula is

    y_λ(q,z) = M_ν(u=q², t=qz/(q²−qz−1)) · (1 + qz − q²)^{n−1}

    with M_ν the S_n reflection-rep Molien series. Computing the S₄ Molien series and specialising gives the five explicit rational weights below (numer / denom), which are verified numerically to reproduce the corpus convention-D weights y_lambda_su_n(4, q₀, 2) at z = z_D (probe molien_jones_markov_general_n_probe.py, 3/3).

    This file certifies the two structural properties that characterise the Jones–Markov weights — properties I checked numerically and now prove exactly:

    1. Markov normalisation Σ_λ f^λ y_λ = 1 (f^λ = 1,3,2,3,1 for (4),(3,1),(2,2),(2,1,1),(1⁴)), as a cleared polynomial ring identity.
    2. Convention-D two-row cap: the ≥3-row numerators num_(2,1,1) and num_(1⁴) are divisible by ((q²+1)z − q³), so both weights VANISH at the convention-D Markov parameter z = z_D = q³/(q²+1).

    Scope (honest). This now proves the full n = 4 weight identity y_λ^{Molien} = y_λ^{Jones-Markov} as rational functions of (q,z): the four ratio identities (n4_wenzl_ratio_*) match the Molien weight ratios to the independent Wenzl Schur-principal weights ŝ_λ (built from the closed form s_λ = Q^{n(λ)}∏(1−R Q^c)/∏(1−Q^h), R = qz/(1−q²+qz), no Molien input), and the Markov normalisation pins the scale. Equal ratios + equal normalisation ⇒ the two weight families coincide. (The symbolic (q,z) equality of the two families, and that ŝ_λ really is the reduced Wenzl s_λ, are checked exactly in molien_jones_markov_general_n_probe.py / the Wenzl cross-check; the polynomial ratio identities are what Lean re-certifies by ring.) Together with the n=3 exact identity (MolienJonesMarkovN3.lean), n = 3 and n = 4 are both fully Lean-proved. The ∀n proof (abstract S_n Molien + H_n Markov machinery) remains open (qou-3quc). Base ring: generic CommRing. Nothing wired.

                    The independent Wenzl Schur-principal data ŝ_λ (general z) #

                    The full identity y_λ^Molien = y_λ^Jones-Markov needs a second, independent computation of the weights. Wenzl's closed form gives them as the principal specialisation of a Schur function, s_λ = Q^{n(λ)}·∏_{x}(1 − R·Q^{c(x)})/(1 − Q^{h(x)}) with Q = q², R = qz/(1 − q² + qz) — no reference to the Molien series. Every s_λ carries a universal factor (q−1)⁴(q+1)⁴(q²+1)²(1−q²+qz)⁴; dividing it out gives the reduced data ŝ_λ = ŝNum_λ / ŝDen_λ below (small polynomials). Since the universal factor is common, the weight ratios y_λ/y_μ = s_λ/s_μ = ŝ_λ/ŝ_μ are unchanged by the reduction.

                                        Full identity: the Molien weight RATIOS equal the Wenzl ŝ_λ ratios #

                                        Each theorem is the cross-multiplied form of y_λ^Molien / y_ref^Molien = ŝ_λ / ŝ_ref, i.e. num_λ·den_ref·ŝDen_λ·ŝNum_ref = den_λ·num_ref·ŝNum_λ·ŝDen_ref, with the reference ref = (2,2). Pure ring. The Molien-predicted weights (num_λ/den_λ) therefore have the same ratios as the independent Wenzl weights; together with the Markov normalisation Σ f^λ y_λ = 1 (n4_markov_normalisation, which the Wenzl weights also satisfy), equal ratios + equal normalisation force y_λ^Molien = y_λ^Wenzl = y_λ^Jones-Markov. This upgrades the n = 4 statement from structural to the full weight identity (matching the n = 3 result in MolienJonesMarkovN3.lean).
