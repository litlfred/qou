        Bridge to QOU.Mass.C1FormalSolutionGrowth #

        QOU.Mass.C1FormalSolutionGrowth.c1FormalDeg n is defined as ∑_{m<n} (2m+1), and c1FormalDeg_eq_sq proves it equals n². That is a true statement about natural numbers, but on its own it says nothing about the coefficients cₙ — the name asserts "the C1 formal-solution degree" while nothing in that module mentions Polynomial or the recurrence.

        The lemma below is the missing link: the actual natDegree of the actual coefficient polynomial equals that combinatorial quantity. It is what earns c1FormalDeg its name, and it is the step that requires the domain structure of ℤ[X] plus the no-cancellation fact — neither of which is available to a pure-Nat argument.

        S1: the ramified q-Borel growth budget #

        Stage S1 of docs/audits/2026-08-08-w2-stokes-qborel-attack-plan.md. The unramified level-d q-Borel kernel cannot absorb deg_q(cₙ) = n² for any d (budget +n²/2 at d=1, worse for larger d). Ramifying first — z = ζ², the pullback forced by the non-integral slope ½ — puts the coefficient cₘ at index N = 2m, against the level-2 kernel exponent there.

        The result is exact and linear: the leftover exponent is exactly m. Linear (sub-quadratic) leftover is precisely the convergence condition, so this pairing — ramification then level 2 — is the critical matching. That is the quantitative reason RSZ's square roots B₁,B₂ are forced rather than cosmetic.

        theorem QOU.QBeta.ramified_qBorel_budget (m : ℕ) :
        (cCoeff m).natDegree = MathlibExt.QBorelKernel.qBorelExp 2 (2 * m) + m

        S1 budget, exactly. At the ramified index N = 2m, the C1 coefficient exponent exceeds the level-2 q-Borel kernel exponent by exactly m: m² = m(m−1) + m. Linear leftover ⇒ the ramified transform converges, whereas no unramified level absorbs the quadratic growth.
