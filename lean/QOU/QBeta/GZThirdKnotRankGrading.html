    QOU.QBeta.GZThirdKnotRankGrading — the third knot's rank grading: a GZ erratum, an object-identity guard, and an S₃ block decomposition #

    Why this file exists #

    Route C M3 (AdjointIndexSetM3) left one live question: H_Ad claims the knot-independent core of the DT index set is the multiplicity-free 𝔰𝔩₂ adjoint weight set, s(x) = x + 1 + x⁻¹. The stated way to break it was a rank-6 knot whose grading is not multiplicity-free.

    Garoufalidis–Zagier, Knots and Their Related q-Series (SIGMA 19 (2023) 082, library/arxiv-2111.06645/2304.09377v2.pdf) — the sequel [44] that library/arxiv-2111.06645v3/2111.06645v3.pdf announces — supplies exactly such a knot: the (−2,3,7) pretzel, where §3.5 reports "a new phenomenon… the weight spaces have higher multiplicity."

    That looks like the refutation. It is not, and Part B below is the guard that says why. Recording the near-miss is the point: the two gradings agree on 4₁'s total only by coincidence, and conflating them would have retired a hypothesis on the strength of a different object.

    -- Ref: [manuscript] library/arxiv-2111.06645/2304.09377v2.pdf §3.4, §3.5 (Garoufalidis–Zagier) -- Ref: [manuscript] library/arxiv-2111.06645v3/2111.06645v3.pdf §2.1 (Garoufalidis–Zagier) -- Ref: [manuscript] docs/audits/2026-08-11-route-c-bps-cohomology-scoping.md §6

    Part A — the (−2,3,7) weight data, and an internal contradiction in the source #

    Three statements in §3.5, quoted:

    1. Prose: "there are weights 0, 1 and 2 with dimensions 1, 4 and 1, respectively."
    2. Explicit decomposition: ⟨h⟩ = W₀ ⊕ W₁ ⊕ W₂ with W₀ = ⟨H₀, H₃, H₄, H₅⟩, W₁ = ⟨H₁⟩, W₂ = ⟨H₂⟩.
    3. ε-deformation degrees: (δ₀,…,δ₅) = (0,1,2,0,0,0); and the companion weight vector (−1, 0, 1, −1, −1, −1) on h = (h_j).

    Statements 2 and 3 agree with each other and give (4, 1, 1). Statement 1 gives (1, 4, 1). Two independent internal statements against one: the prose has its first two dimensions transposed. Adjudicated below rather than guessed.

                      Part E — the absent sign block is FORCED, not observed #

                      Part D recorded the missing sign representation as an observation. It is more than that: given GZ's own decomposition it is a theorem, and the reason is a general fact about permutation modules.

                      Step 1 — the level restricts where the question can even be asked. GZ introduces the level as "a new phenomenon" arising at (−2,3,7), which has N = 2. The other two knots have N = 1, so ρ factors through the trivial quotient: there is no finite group acting at 4₁ or 5₂, hence no sign block to be absent. The observation is single-instance among these three knots by construction, and Part D's phrasing understated that.

                      Step 2 — but at (−2,3,7) the absence is forced. S₃-characters are determined on the three classes e, transpositions, 3-cycles (sizes 1, 3, 2). GZ gives W₀ = standard ⊕ trivial², whose character is [4, 2, 1]. That is exactly [3, 1, 0] + [1, 1, 1] — the character of the permutation module on 3 points, plus one trivial. Characters determine complex representations up to isomorphism, so

                      W₀ ≅ ℂ[X] ⊕ ℂ, X a 3-element S₃-set.

                      And a permutation module on 3 points never contains the sign representation: ⟨χ_perm, χ_sign⟩ = (1·3·1 + 3·1·(−1) + 2·0·1)/6 = 0. Adding trivial summands cannot introduce one. So the sign block's absence is a consequence of W₀ being permutation-type, not a coincidence to be explained.

                      What stays interpretive. That the 3-element set is geometrically the three cusps of Γ(2) (equivalently the three non-zero 2-torsion classes, the three index-2 subgroups) is the standard Γ/Γ(2) ≅ S₃ picture, but GZ does not say W₀ realizes it, and this file does not claim so. The character identity is proved; the geometric identification is a reading.

                      Inner products are carried as 6⟨·,·⟩ to stay in Int.

                                                                          Part I — 9₁₂ breaks the tetrahedron coincidence, and the rule survives it #

                                                                          A pattern worth noticing, and then killing. Across the three knots Parts F–H used, the ideal-tetrahedron count happens to equal the trace-field degree:

                                                                          knot ideal tetrahedra (DG §6) trace field degree r
                                                                          4₁ 2 2 2
                                                                          5₂ 3 3 3
                                                                          6₁ 4 4 4

                                                                          Three for three. If the rank rule were secretly riding on that, everything in Parts F–H would be an artifact of a triangulation count rather than a statement about a number field — and nothing so far distinguishes the two readings.

                                                                          DG §6.5 settles it. The 9₁₂ knot has 10 ideal tetrahedra and trace field

                                                                          x¹⁷ − 8x¹⁶ + 32x¹⁵ − 89x¹⁴ + 195x¹³ − 353x¹² + 542x¹¹ − 719x¹⁰ + 834x⁹ − 851x⁸ + 764x⁷ − 605x⁶ + 421x⁵ − 253x⁴ + 130x³ − 55x² + 18x − 3,

                                                                          irreducible, of type [1, 8] — degree 17. Measured here by exact Sturm sequence: exactly 1 real root, so [r₁, r₂] = [1, 8] and r₁ + 2r₂ = 17, matching DG's stated type.

                                                                          10 ≠ 17. The coincidence dies at the fourth knot, and the rank rule — which reads the signature, never the triangulation — is untouched. That is the point of recording it: a rule that agreed with a coincidence on three points had to be separated from it before the agreements could count as evidence.

                                                                          Sharper than the corpus's existing guard. docs/audits/ already carries a refutation of "trace-field degree ≈ tetrahedron count" (PR #5277) — but its counterexamples are the Whitehead link and Borromean rings, both links, so a reader could have rescued the heuristic by restricting it to knots. 9₁₂ is a knot, and it kills the knot-restricted version too.

                                                                          A new prediction, and a large one. Under the standing hypothesis A_K^red = F_K: r(9₁₂) = 17, |P_K| = 18. Far more falsifiable than the small cases — a wrong rule has 17 ways to miss here rather than 4.

                                                                          Not verified, and said plainly: (−2,3,9)'s predicted r = 5 from Part F remains unconfirmed. Census identification needs SnapPy (absent in this sandbox) and no saddle data for it is in the corpus, so neither the Part-G nor the Part-H route is available. Recording 9₁₂ here does not substitute for it.
