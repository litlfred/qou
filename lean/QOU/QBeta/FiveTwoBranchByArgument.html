    Pinning the branch integer WITHOUT continuity — an argument count #

    Bean qou-trns. After FiveTwoEnvelopeAssembly, hcont is the single open input to the envelope step. hcont is used for exactly one thing: to force the branch integer k in

    Σⱼ log(1 − x·yⱼ) = log x + 2πi·k

    to be 0. This file replaces that topological argument with an arithmetic one, trading an analytic hypothesis for two real-algebraic ones.

    The idea #

    exp(Σⱼ log(1 − x·yⱼ) − log x) = 1 is already proved from Vieta alone (exp_logSumA_sub_log_eq_one), so k exists. Taking imaginary parts on x = e^{iφ},

    Σⱼ arg(1 − x·yⱼ) = φ + 2π·k .

    Now k is pinned by bounds: if the argument sum lies in (−π, 2π) and φ ∈ [0, π), then |2πk| < 2π, so k = 0. No continuity, no root tracking, no base point — branchInt_eq_zero_of_bounds below.

    What the bounds need, and what they cost #

    sum_bounds_of_arg_structure derives (−π, 2π) from three facts about the individual arguments, all measured on the open arc at 40 dps (Route 27):

    fact measured
    each arg(1 − x·yⱼ) > −π/2 min over the arc is −1.4077…, and −π/2 = −1.5708
    at most one exceeds π/2 max count is 1; second-largest peaks at 1.3099 < π/2
    at least one is ≥ 0 at most two are negative

    These are real-algebraic statements on |x| = 1 — the same shape as xy_neg, which this branch already proves by resultant elimination. They are not proved here; they are the new obligations, and they replace hcont.

    Whether that is a good trade is a judgement, not a theorem. In its favour: they are algebraic rather than analytic, they are finite (three inequalities), and the lane has working machinery for exactly this shape. Against: the margins are not generous — −1.4077 against −π/2 is a 10% margin, and 1.3099 against π/2 is 17% — so the eliminations will be tighter than xy_neg's was.

    A structural simplification found along the way #

    On the open arc the two log-sums are complex conjugates,

    Σⱼ log(1 − yⱼ/x) = conj( Σⱼ log(1 − x·yⱼ) )    (Route 27, to 1e-40)

    because the roots are closed under conjugation (the cubic has real coefficients on |x| = 1) and x⁻¹ = conj x. Hence k₂ = −k₁: there is only one branch integer, not two. It fails at φ = π exactly because 1 − x·y₁ sits on the cut there and log(conj z) ≠ conj(log z).

    The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms. This file does not close hcont; it exchanges it for a different, arguably more tractable set of obligations, and says so.

    Non-vacuity #

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoDeformedSaddle.branchInt_hypotheses_satisfiable :
    ∃ (a₁ : ℝ) (a₂ : ℝ) (a₃ : ℝ) (φ : ℝ) (_ : -(Real.pi / 2) < a₁) (_ : a₁ < Real.pi), a₁ + a₂ + a₃ = φ + 2 * Real.pi * ↑0

    Non-vacuity. The hypotheses of branchInt_eq_zero_of_arg_structure are satisfiable, with the measured arc values: taking a₁ = 1.4, a₂ = a₃ = 0 and φ = 1.4 gives a genuine instance, so the theorem is not about an empty set.

    The check with real content is Route 27, which measures the three structural facts along the arc and finds the margins −1.4077 vs −π/2 and 1.3099 vs π/2.
