    L4 — Cyclic-dilogarithm pentagon + Faddeev–Volkov uniqueness skeleton #

    Result L4 of the Φ_b combinatorial (cyclotomic / tetraplectic) fibre workplan (docs/workplans/2026-07-13-phib-combinatorial-fibre-lean-workplan.md, bean qou-evo7). The pentagon / five-term relation is the standard functional identity of the quantum dilogarithm; L4 uses it as the uniqueness lever: pentagon + inversion + unitarity + modular invariance pin Φ_b uniquely (Faddeev–Volkov rigidity), converting the keystone from an existence hunt into a rigidity statement — the datum is forced, not fitted.

    Honest scope — outcome (B), with a fully-proved finite/lattice rigidity core #

    The full operator pentagon Φ(𝗉)Φ(𝗊) = Φ(𝗊)Φ(𝗉+𝗊)Φ(𝗉) is an identity in the finite Weyl algebra at ζ_N (non-commuting 𝗉, 𝗊 with 𝗉𝗊 = ζ² 𝗊𝗉); a genuine scalar/commutative "pentagon" identity for Φ : ℂ → ℂ would be false, so this file does not fabricate one. Instead it delivers, honestly:

    • (A-core, fully proved, sorry-free) the finite cyclic Faddeev–Volkov rigidity — the finite-lattice heart of the uniqueness statement. A solution of the finite N-periodic multiplicative shift recursion (the cyclic quantum dilogarithm at ζ_N, CyclotomicDilogCollapse/L1 → CyclicQuantumDilog/L2) is determined by its seed (solution_unique_of_seed), equals the explicit partial product (solution_eq_prod), and — given the pentagon closure (∏ over a full period = 1, the cyclotomic-collapse shadow of the pentagon, carried as an honest hypothesis in PentagonClosure) — is N-periodic (PentagonClosure.solution_periodic). No open input; pure finite algebra over a generic CommRing.

    • (A-core, fully proved) the analytic ambiguity is a periodic function: the ratio of any two FaddeevPhi solutions is invariant under the full ib-shift (ratio_ib_periodic) — the concrete step showing the only freedom between two solutions is doubly-periodic, which is exactly what the pentagon's Liouville rigidity then kills.

    • (B, §3b-cond conditional theorem) the global Faddeev–Volkov uniqueness. PentagonRigidity carries, as an honest open hypothesis field (uniqueUpToScalar), the analytic rigidity the pentagon buys per Faddeev–Kashaev — every FaddeevPhi solution is a constant multiple of a fixed one — which requires the doubly-periodic-⇒-constant (Liouville) step not available in Mathlib. faddeevPhi_unique_of_pentagon then derives genuine equality of two solutions from that hypothesis plus a normalization point (pinning the scalar). This is the §3b-cond pattern: the theorem proves the implication (rigidity-up-to-scalar + normalization ⇒ equality); the pentagon is honestly marked as the open input, not laundered as proved.

    No c_k is produced or wired; registry untouched; scope-(a) throughout.

    1. Finite cyclic recursion rigidity (fully proved, generic CommRing) #

    Model a solution of the finite cyclic quantum dilogarithm on the shift lattice as f : ℕ → R obeying the first-order multiplicative recursion f (k+1) = w k · f k with shift multipliers w : ℕ → R (at ζ_N these are the N-periodic factor family 1 + ζ^{2k+1} x). The recursion determines f from its seed f 0, so two solutions agreeing at the seed agree everywhere — the finite Faddeev–Volkov rigidity.

      3. Analytic ambiguity is ib-periodic (fully proved) #

      The concrete analytic step underlying Faddeev–Volkov rigidity: two FaddeevPhi solutions at the same modular parameter b solve the same first-order quasi-period recursion Ψ z = P z · Ψ (z + ib) (phi_b_quasiperiod), so the shared prefactor P cancels in the ratio and Φ₁/Φ₂ is ib-periodic. The only freedom is thus a doubly-periodic function; the pentagon's Liouville rigidity (§4) is exactly what forces it constant.
