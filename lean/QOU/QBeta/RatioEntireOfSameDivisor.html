    Brick (c): same divisor ⇒ the ratio extends to an entire function #

    The genuine content of hr_entire — the divisor-rigidity input of the Faddeev uniqueness leg (FaddeevPhiUniquenessAssembly.faddeevPhi_proportional_of_ratio_entire takes exactly this). Two meromorphic solutions Φ, Ψ of the same first-order q-difference equation are not forced to share a divisor by the equation alone — their ratio is an elliptic function that may be nonconstant (Weierstrass ℘). But given that they have the same divisor (the analytic rigidity fact that Φ_b's explicit PhiBProd zero/pole lattice supplies), the ratio's normal-form representative is entire.

    The construction that dissolves the 0/0 junk at the shared zeros is Mathlib's meromorphic normal form toMeromorphicNFOn: it agrees with Ψ/Φ on a punctured neighborhood of every point, its meromorphicOrderAt equals that of Ψ/Φ (which is ord Ψ − ord Φ = 0 under same-divisor), and a normal-form function of order ≥ 0 is analytic (MeromorphicNFAt.meromorphicOrderAt_nonneg_iff_analyticAt). So the representative is analytic at every point, i.e. entire.

    This is the honest §3b-cond input: same-divisor ⇒ entire is proved here; the same-divisor premise itself (for the concrete Φ_b) is the remaining PhiBProd content. Verified lean-direct against the Meromorphic cone built from pinned mathlib source (Mathlib.Analysis.Meromorphic.{Order,NormalForm}).

    Same divisor ⇒ entire ratio. If Φ, Ψ are meromorphic on ℂ, Φ is nowhere locally zero (meromorphicOrderAt Φ ≠ ⊤ everywhere), and they have the same divisor (meromorphicOrderAt Ψ = meromorphicOrderAt Φ at every point), then the normal-form representative r := toMeromorphicNFOn (Ψ/Φ) univ is entire (Differentiable ℂ r) and agrees with Ψ/Φ on a punctured neighbourhood of every point. This is the hr_entire divisor-rigidity input of the Faddeev uniqueness leg.
