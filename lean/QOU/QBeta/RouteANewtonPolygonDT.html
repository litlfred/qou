    QOU.QBeta.RouteANewtonPolygonDT — the Route-A Newton-polygon readings of the DT spectrum, and the cross-knot refutation #

    Lean formalization of the assertions of the Route-A probe (computations/probes/qbeta_g3_aomega_route_a_newton_polygon.py; audit docs/audits/2026-08-11-aomega-internal-derivation-attack-options.md §5). Everything here is decidable integer lattice geometry — the Newton polygons in question are 3- and 4-vertex integer polygons, so the probe's numerics are promoted to proofs rather than merely re-run.

    What is proved #

    For the figure-eight 4₁, from the A-polynomial A_{4₁}(M,L) = −M⁴ + L(1 − M² − 2M⁴ − M⁶ + M⁸) − L²M⁴ (geometric component):

    • a41_edge_slopes_pm_four — every non-vertical Newton-polygon edge has slope ±4 (the boundary slopes of 4₁);
    • a41_full_L_degree_eq_three — the full A-polynomial (geometric × the abelian (L−1)) has L-degree 3 = the number of boundary-parabolic SL₂(ℂ) connections {0, g, ḡ}.

    Both 3 and 4 are members of the certified DT spectrum {3,4} (StokesQZeroDTCount, FiveTwoDTSpectrumSplit), which is what made the Newton polygon look like the internal handle. Two negative results decide the matter:

    • c1_slope_ne_a41_slope — the QOU C1 binding carrier [m+2]_q b_{m+2} = b_{m+1} + b_m has Newton slopes ±1/2, not ±4, so C1 is not the quantum A-polynomial of 4₁; Route A needs the true colored-Jones recursion Â_K.
    • r2_slope_reading_refuted — the slope reading (DT integer = boundary slope) fails the 5₂ cross-knot gate: 5₂'s maximal boundary slope is 10 ∉ {3,4}. The 4₁ coincidence 4 = |slope| is single-knot only.
    • r1_connection_reading_survives — the L-degree / connection-count reading survives both knots (4₁ ↦ 3, 5₂ ↦ 4, both in {3,4}).

    The surviving reading R1 is an Euler-characteristic-type datum, which is why the audit pivots to Route C; and by FiveTwoDTSpectrumSplit.symmetric_scalar_blind_to_chirality even R1, being a scalar, cannot equal the chirality-split datum. Nothing here derives A_ω.

    Caveat retired 2026-09-07. The 5₂ boundary-slope datum ({0,−4,−10}) and connection count (4) were literature-recalled defs (bean qou-52sn). They are now derived from the corpus's own A_{5₂} (#6985, Farey word RL): a52_edge_slopes_in_0_m4_m10, fiveTwoMaxAbsSlope_derived, fiveTwoConnections_eq_four. The section The mirror proves both Route-A inputs mirror-invariant, so the derived frame's sign information — the one thing #6985 adds beyond the recalled table — is invisible to Route A by construction.

                  Transcription check — the A-polynomial reciprocal symmetry #

                  The A_{4₁} monomial data is typed in (it is a literature value, not something Lean can read off a knot). It is not unchecked, however: A-polynomials satisfy a reciprocal symmetry (M,L) ↦ (M⁻¹, L⁻¹), which on the Newton polygon is the central point-reflection about the polygon's centre — here (4,1), i.e. (m,l) ↦ (8−m, 2−l). A transcription slip in any exponent would generically break it, so the two theorems below are a genuine (if partial) integrity check on the input, in the same spirit as the det S± = 1 and S⁺ᵀS⁻ = 1 identities that certify the Stokes matrices in FiveTwoStokesMatrixIdentities.

                                              The mirror, and what the Newton polygon cannot see #

                                              The mirror image K̄ has A_{K̄}(L, M) = A_K(L, M⁻¹) up to a unit, so on exponents it is (m, l) ↦ (−m, l): every boundary slope changes sign, and the L-profile is untouched. The two Route-A inputs — the maximal absolute slope (R2) and the L-degree (R1) — are therefore invariant under the mirror. The 5₂ DT datum is the ordered pair (4 ∈ S⁺, 3 ∈ S⁻) whose bar-odd part is nonzero (FiveTwoDTSpectrumSplit.chirality_odd_datum_ne_zero); a quantity that cannot tell 5₂ from its mirror cannot carry it. This sharpens FiveTwoDTSpectrumSplit.symmetric_scalar_blind_to_chirality from "no symmetric scalar" to "no Newton-polygon slope-magnitude or degree datum", and it is the reason the derived frame (#6985, which fixes the sign of the slopes) adds nothing to Route A: the sign is exactly what Route A discards.
