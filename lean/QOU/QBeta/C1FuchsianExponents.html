    noncomputable def QOU.QBeta.indicialDiscriminant :
    Polynomial ℚ

    The discriminant of C1's indicial equation at the Fuchsian point 0: (q−1)² − 4(q−1) = q² − 6q + 5.

      theorem QOU.QBeta.not_isSquare_indicialDiscriminant :
      ¬∃ (f : Polynomial ℚ), f * f = indicialDiscriminant

      C1's two weights at 0 are irrational over ℚ(q). The indicial discriminant q² − 6q + 5 is not a square in ℚ[q], so the roots t = (−(q−1) ± √(q²−6q+5))/2 of the indicial equation are not rational functions of q.

      Proof by multiplicity, not degree parity (the degree is even, so parity is silent): f·f = p forces f(1)² = p(1) = 0, hence f(1) = 0; differentiating, p' = f'·f + f·f', so p'(1) = 0. But p'(1) = −4.

      theorem QOU.QBeta.both_ends_irrational :
      ¬IsSquare indicialDiscriminant ∧ ¬IsSquare newtonEdgeConstant

      Both ends of C1 are quadratically irrational, for different reasons. Recorded together because the contrast is the point: at 0 the weights come from an indicial equation and are computable by pure algebra; at ∞ they come from Stokes data and are not. That the two ends need different arguments (multiplicity here, degree parity there) reflects that difference rather than being an accident of the proofs.
