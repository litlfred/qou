    Parity-modulated geometric sequences — why a one-step ratio test lies #

    The mechanism behind bean qou-64jx. A q-Borel radius estimate for the C1 recurrence was read off the one-step ratio c_{m+1}/c_m at a single m, and came out branch-dependent at 3.7e-4. That was labelled "two nearby singularities … consistent with the two-sector structure". It is not: the parity-free estimator is branch-independent to 3.1e-25 (computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_parity_artifact.py).

    This file isolates the algebra that makes that happen, in a model with no asymptotics in it. The numerics establish that the actual C1 Borel coefficients fit this model to 25 digits; what is proved here is the model's behaviour, and that is what explains the reading.

    The model #

    parityGeom γ α β m = γ ^ m * (if Even m then α else β)

    a geometric sequence whose amplitude is modulated by the parity of the index. The two-step decoupling of a recurrence that couples m to m + 2 produces exactly this shape, with α and β fixed by the initial conditions — i.e. α/β is the branch datum.

    What the three theorems say #

    • twoStepRatio_eq — the two-step ratio is γ ^ 2, for every m, with no hypothesis relating α and β. The branch datum cancels identically. This is why the parity-free estimator is branch-independent, and it is exact rather than asymptotic.
    • oneStepRatio_even / oneStepRatio_odd — the one-step ratio is γ * (β/α) at even m and γ * (α/β) at odd m. So it is 2-periodic, and each phase carries the branch datum. Sampling one parity therefore reports a branch-dependent number no matter how far out in m you go — the estimator is not converging slowly, it is converging to the wrong thing.
    • oneStepRatio_mul_eq — the two phases multiply to γ ^ 2. The branch datum appears as β/α in one and α/β in the other and cancels in the product. This is the punchline: the geometric mean of the two phases recovers γ exactly, which is why that is the canonical repair rather than an ad-hoc one.

    oneStepRatio_ne_iff records when the 2-cycle is visible at all: precisely when α ^ 2 ≠ β ^ 2. At α = ±β the modulation is invisible to the one-step ratio and no spurious branch-dependence appears — consistent with the observed amplitude vanishing as the branch approaches that balance.

    Scope #

    This is a statement about parityGeom, not about the C1 recurrence. It does not prove that the C1 Borel coefficients have this form; that is the numerical finding, and it is recorded as such in the witness. What this file does is remove any doubt about the interpretation of that finding: given the shape, branch-dependence of the one-step ratio is forced and carries no information about singularity count.

      theorem QOU.QBeta.ParityRatio.oneStepRatio_mul_eq {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (γ α β : R) (hα : α ≠ 0) (hβ : β ≠ 0) :
      γ * (β / α) * (γ * (α / β)) = γ ^ 2

      The punchline. The two phases of the one-step ratio multiply to γ ^ 2: the branch datum enters one as β/α and the other as α/β, and cancels.

      So the geometric mean of the two phases recovers γ exactly, for every branch. That is why averaging the parities is the canonical repair of the estimator rather than an ad-hoc patch — and why a single-parity sample cannot be fixed by going to larger m.

      theorem QOU.QBeta.ParityRatio.oneStepRatio_eq_iff {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (γ α β : R) (hγ : γ ≠ 0) (hα : α ≠ 0) (hβ : β ≠ 0) :
      γ * (β / α) = γ * (α / β) ↔ α ^ 2 = β ^ 2

      The 2-cycle is visible exactly when α ^ 2 ≠ β ^ 2. At α = ±β the modulation is invisible to the one-step ratio and no spurious branch-dependence appears at all — the estimator is only misleading off that balance.

      A one-step ratio that is constant in m forces the modulation away.

      Contrapositive of the above, and the sanity check on the whole diagnosis: if the one-step ratio really had settled to a single branch-independent limit, the parity amplitude would have to be trivial. Observing a stable 2-cycle is therefore incompatible with "the estimator converged", however stable consecutive same-parity samples look.
