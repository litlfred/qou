    The Pauli-lock closure is carried entirely by its two hypotheses #

    What this file establishes. QOU.QBeta.InterfaceScalar.separable_interface_degenerate is the theorem the two-nucleon channel-asymmetry closure rests on. Its statement is

    theorem separable_interface_degenerate
    {Sp Is Sp3 : Type*} (fSpin : Sp → R) (fIso : Is → R) (fSpace : Sp3 → R)
    (s1 s0 : Sp) (t1 t0 : Is) (ell : Sp3)
    (hplus : fSpin s1 = fIso t1) (hminus : fSpin s0 = fIso t0) :
    fSpin s1 * fIso t0 * fSpace ell = fSpin s0 * fIso t1 * fSpace ell

    and its proof is rw [hplus, hminus, mul_comm (fIso t1) (fIso t0)]. Three facts about that shape, each machine-checked below:

    1. The hypotheses do work (separability_hypotheses_have_content). Drop them and the conclusion is false — exhibited on a two-element index type over ℝ. So the closure is not a tautology about products.
    2. The residual mathematics is commutativity (degeneracy_is_commutativity). Once hplus/hminus are in force the conclusion is mul_comm. Everything the theorem asserts about physics is asserted by its antecedent.
    3. Releasing the hypotheses releases the conclusion completely (asymmetry_attainable_with_distinct_scalars, distinct_positive_scalars_reach_any_ratio). If the isospin SU(2) carries scalars ν± different from the spin SU(2)'s μ±, the channel ratio is any element of the field — with positivity preserved over ℝ, so the escape is not an artefact of admitting unphysical negative weights. In particular the measured two-nucleon asymmetry 33.7055 is attainable (measured_asymmetry_attainable).

    Why this is worth stating. Bean qou-brng (2026-09-04) records the equal-scalars hypothesis as "the residue" of the channel-asymmetry question and names its next step as "a witness for D9 would make the equal-scalars question decidable for the first time." Two shape facts bear on that, and both are checkable rather than argued:

    • fSpin and fIso are arbitrary functions on arbitrary types. The projector scalars μ₊ = (z + q⁻¹)/(q + q⁻¹), μ₋ = (q − z)/(q + q⁻¹) appear in the theorem's docstring, never in its statement. Nothing in the type ties either function to the corpus's H₂ Wedderburn weights y₂, y₁₁ — that identification lives in a different section of the same file (weight_eq_proj_scalar, WeightsDetermineZ), about different variables.

    • The theorem has no application site. Measured over every tracked file, with Lean comments stripped (nested block comments included) before the name is searched for. Quoted against origin/main as of 2026-09-06, which is the tree the claim is about — not against this branch, which cites the name in four files of its own and so cannot be its own denominator:

    So hplus/hminus are not a claim about the corpus that a witness could settle; they are the antecedent of a statement universally quantified over every possible pair of scalar assignments. Making them a claim about the corpus requires instantiating the theorem at the corpus's own constructions — which nothing does.

    And that instantiation is not merely absent — one of its two arguments does not exist. Measured 2026-09-06 over every .lean in the tree:

    • fIso IS available. The isospin scalars are μ₊ = (z + q⁻¹)/(q + q⁻¹) and μ₋ = (q − z)/(q + q⁻¹) (InterfaceScalar.mu_sum_eq_one, mu_singlet_lt_mu_triplet). At the proton pin under convention D they evaluate to 0.75270211325196540159377935162 and 0.24729788674803459840622064838 — which are, to all 30 digits, the corpus's own Wedderburn weights y₍₂₎ and y₍₁,₁₎ at n = 2 (y-lambda-at-q0.witness.json), exactly as weight_eq_proj_scalar says they must be. This is a re-measurement, not a discovery: bean qou-kj4v recorded the same identity at 50 dps (agreement to 3.3e-52) on 2026-08-09. Cited because the agreement is worth having twice from two directions, not because it is new here.
    • fSpin is NOT. There is no spin-side projector-scalar pair anywhere. The two spin-side carriers assert something else entirely: BringFiberAction (D10) builds A5Irrep → IcosahedralRotation with an injectivity field, and SpinFromSo3Fiber constrains levelCoeff : ℕ → R, the h^(2n) Wedderburn coefficients of the three-strand Borromean trace (levelCoeff 1 = 0, levelCoeff 2 = −3/4). Neither is a two-element scalar on the spin channel. Read from the carriers' own field lists, not from their names.

    🛑 "un-instantiable" would be too strong, and this paragraph said it before the beans path of the corpus-grep came back. Someone has instantiated it: bean qou-5d6k (2026-08-25) evaluates the Pauli-locked interface at 50 dps by using μ± for both SU(2)s and gets

    μ(³S₁) = μ₊^spin · μ₋^iso = 0.18614164195799085217
μ(¹S₀) = μ₋^spin · μ₊^iso = 0.18614164195799085217

    — separable_interface_degenerate's conclusion, measured, to the last digit. So the statement is not that the identification cannot be written down; it is that the only way anyone has written it down is by positing μ± on the spin side, which assumes hplus/hminus rather than checking them. That is why "instantiate it at the corpus's own constructions" is empty as a next step: no construction supplies fSpin, and the one instantiation that exists supplies it by assumption.

    The distinction matters because the two readings send a reader to different places. "It cannot be instantiated" invites building a spin scalar. "It has only ever been instantiated by assuming the answer" says the missing thing is a derivation of the spin projector weights from the D10 side — which is a statement about BringFiberAction, not about this theorem.

    A route that this observation might seem to open is already closed — do not walk into it. The tempting next move is to drop the missing spin factor and read the channel asymmetry off the isospin scalars alone. Their log-ratio at the proton pin is B(T=0)/B(T=1) = 4.9181, against a measured 33.7055 — but the shortfall is beside the point, because the whole class is excluded by a stronger argument that needs no candidate: the total B(T=1) + B(T=0) = −α·log(μ₊μ₋) is invariant under every T-odd rescaling (μ₊, μ₋) ↦ (μ₊t, μ₋/t), and μ₊ + μ₋ = 1 floors it at α·log 4 = 5.8030 against nature's 2.1510. Route 7 of 2026-08-16-two-nucleon-sector-final-state-eight-closures.md, Lean, scope-(b), landed 2026-08-16. The 4.9181 is recorded here only so the next reader does not re-derive it as though it were news.

    There is a second, independent reason, and it is sharper: bean qou-4g4a proves the admissible interface band [z − h, z] = [0.40362773835082593616, 0.61268803714646525160] lies strictly between the two isospin weights — μ₋ is 63.22 % below the floor and μ₊ 22.85 % above the ceiling, with a 7.56 % margin on the tighter of the two q conditions, so it is not a knife-edge and not fixable by a better q. Using an isospin projector as the interface is therefore not merely unmotivated but unavailable: the two mechanisms compute on disjoint sets. (That separation on the lower side is a convention-D phenomenon; under C, μ₋ = 0.303675 sits inside the band.)

    What this does NOT say. It does not refute separable_interface_degenerate, which is correct and sorry-free, nor the physics reading of the Pauli lock, nor anything about whether the two SU(2)s in fact carry equal scalars — that stays exactly as unestablished as its converse. It is scope-(a) throughout: a statement about what the Lean asserts, with the invariant named where it becomes (b) — see asymmetry_attainable_with_distinct_scalars, whose content is that the conclusion is unconstrained once the antecedent is dropped.

    Base ring (§7c). Everything here is generic — CommRing, CommMonoid, Field — with the non-degeneracy (5 : R) ≠ 6 carried as a hypothesis rather than obtained from an ordering. The archimedean half (positivity of the projector weights, and the measured asymmetry as a real numeral) lives in the sibling SeparableInterfaceHypothesesHaveContentReal.lean and consumes these results at R := ℝ, per §7c's rule that the specialisation goes in its own file.

    1. The hypotheses do work #

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.SeparableInterfaceContent.separability_hypotheses_have_content {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (hne : 5 ≠ 6) :
    ∃ (fSpin : Bool → R) (fIso : Bool → R) (fSpace : Unit → R), fSpin true * fIso false * fSpace () ≠ fSpin false * fIso true * fSpace ()

    The separability conclusion is false without hplus/hminus.

    Index the two channels by Bool. With fSpin = (2, 1), fIso = (5, 3) (reading true ↦ the first slot) and a unit space factor, the two sides are 5 and 6.

    The only thing the ring must supply is that those two are different — carried as the hypothesis (5 : R) ≠ 6 rather than deduced from an ordering, so the statement stays on the generic side of the §7c wall. Its R := ℝ instance is in the sibling file.

    This is the gate_has_content pattern this arc uses elsewhere: a theorem whose hypotheses could be dropped without changing the conclusion asserts nothing, and the cheapest way to know which kind you have is to exhibit a failure.

    2. With the hypotheses, the residual mathematics is commutativity #

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.SeparableInterfaceContent.degeneracy_is_commutativity {R : Type u_1} [CommMonoid R] (a b : R) :
    a * b = b * a

    What separable_interface_degenerate proves, once its antecedent is applied.

    Rewriting by hplus and hminus replaces both spin scalars by isospin ones, and the two sides then differ by the order of a product. So the closure's mathematical content is mul_comm; its physical content is entirely in the identification the hypotheses make. Stated here separately so that this is visible without reading a proof term.

    3. Releasing the identification releases the conclusion #

    source
    theorem QOU.QBeta.SeparableInterfaceContent.asymmetry_attainable_with_distinct_scalars {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (μp μm r : R) (hμp : μp ≠ 0) :
    ∃ (νp : R) (νm : R), μp * νm = r * (μm * νp)

    Distinct scalars reach every ratio.

    If the spin SU(2) carries (μ₊, μ₋) and the isospin SU(2) carries a different pair (ν₊, ν₋), the channel ratio r defined by μ₊ ν₋ = r · (μ₋ ν₊) is unconstrained: for every r there is such a pair. So no bound on the achievable asymmetry survives the loss of hplus/hminus — not a weakened bound, none at all.

    Scope (b) on the conjunction {separable interface, distinct scalar pairs}, invariant named: a field has no nontrivial multiplicative constraint between two free elements.
