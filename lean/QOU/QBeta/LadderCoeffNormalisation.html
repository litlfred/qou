        theorem QOU.QBeta.deriv_deriv_ladderModel_zero (A c : ℝ) :
        deriv (deriv (ladderModel A c)) 0 = Real.cos A * (2 * c - 1 / 4)

        The model's second derivative at 0 is cos A · (2c − 1/4).

        The −1/4 is the cosine factor's own contribution and the 2c is the explicit factor's; they are separate terms, which is exactly why reading the same model at two normalisations gives two different "quadratic coefficients".

        noncomputable def QOU.QBeta.normalisedQuadCoeff (A c : ℝ) :
        ℝ

        The normalised quadratic (Taylor) coefficient of the model: the second derivative at 0, divided by 2! and by the model's own value at 0. This is the c̃₂ of the naming ruling.

          c̃₂ = c₂ − 1/8 — the exact offset between the two readings.

          Independent of A: the phase cancels in the normalisation, so the offset is a clean rational rather than an anchor-dependent quantity. This is what makes c₂ and c̃₂ two names for one model rather than a contradiction.
