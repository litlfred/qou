    The q-theta zero locus — where the C1 connection matrix has its poles #

    The connection matrix P = Y₀⁻¹ Y_∞ of the ramified C1 operator

    L = (p⁴z² − 1)τ² − (p²−1)τ − (p²−1),   τF(z) = F(pz),  q = p²

    is p-elliptic, and every one of its poles sits where some θ_p in the construction vanishes (computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_connection_matrix.py):

    pole on ℂ*/p^ℤ locus source intrinsic?
    [−1] z ∈ −p^ℤ θ_p(z), the common numerator of the 0-side character e_t(z) = θ_p(z)/θ_p(z/t) no — normalisation artifact
    [−T] z ∈ −T·p^ℤ θ_p(z/T₊) yes
    [+T] z ∈ +T·p^ℤ θ_p(−z/T) yes

    with T = √((p²−1)/p³) = √(q−1)/q^{3/4} the theta-normalised canonical parameter. So all three loci are single p-orbits of the zero set of θ_p, and the whole question reduces to: where does θ_p vanish?

    What this file proves #

    • qTheta_neg_zpow_eq_zero — θ_p(w) = 0 for every w ∈ −p^ℤ, the ⊇ direction of the zero locus, which is exactly what places the poles above. The converse — that these are the only zeros, each simple — is QOU/QBeta/C1ThetaZeroLocus.lean, built on the contour identity below.

    • summable_qThetaTerm — the series converges for P > 1, w ≠ 0.

    • qTheta_functional_equation — θ_P(P²w) = w·θ_P(w), i.e. θ_p(pw) = w θ_p(w).

    • qThetaC_conj / norm_qThetaC_conj_pair — θ_P commutes with conjugation, so the two conjugate 0-side exponents give exactly equal magnitudes on the real axis. This is the formal core of the refutation the whole C1 arc restarted from: a basis putting them "~17 orders of magnitude apart" is impossible, not merely unobserved.

    • differentiableOn_qThetaC — θ_P is holomorphic on ℂ* (bean qou-c1ln step 1): Weierstrass M-test on annuli plus TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn.differentiableOn. Makes θ_P a genuine analytic object, the prerequisite for it having a divisor at all.

    • qThetaC_functional_equation / deriv_qThetaC_functional_equation / logDeriv_qThetaC_functional_equation — the functional equation over ℂ, differentiated, and in logarithmic-derivative form p·θ'(pw)/θ(pw) = 1/w + θ'(w)/θ(w). That last one is the +1 per period: it is the entire zero-counting mechanism the converse zero locus runs on.

    • circleIntegral_scale / winding_step / qTheta_winding_step — the same +1, carried up to the contour level:

      ∮_{|z| = pρ} θ'/θ dz  =  2πi  +  ∮_{|z| = ρ} θ'/θ dz

      for any ρ > 0 on whose circle θ_P does not vanish. This is the form the argument principle consumes. circleIntegral_scale (change of variables for circleIntegral, absent from Mathlib) and winding_step (1/w in the integrand ⇒ 2πi in the integral) are stated θ-free and are reusable.

    • qTheta_inversion — θ_P(1/w) = w·θ_P(w), Morita's inversion formula (arXiv:1105.1998 §2.1) in our normalisation.

    • thetaRatio_multiplier — θ_P(cz)/θ_P(z) is a q-character with multiplier c: the building block of every connection coefficient in the literature (the pseudo-constants of Ramis–Sauloy–Zhang).

    • connRatio_reflect — numerator and denominator of the closed form swap under conjugation, which is why |χ| = 1 on ℝ* and zeros pair with conjugate poles.

    • connRatio_elliptic — the closed form found for the connection invariant (a ratio of four θ_p's) is p-elliptic, straight from the functional equation. Proves the shape is right; not that χ equals it.

    summable_qThetaTerm, differentiableOn_qThetaC and qTheta_functional_equation are the analytic foundation for the converse. The vanishing theorem itself needs none of them (see below), but every route to "these are the only zeros" needs θ_p to be a genuine analytic object, and the functional equation is what forces the zero set to be a union of p-orbits.

    The proof is an involution, and needs no analysis at all. For each k, the map n ↦ 2k−1−n on ℤ

    • leaves the exponent −n(n+1) + 2kn invariant (pure ring), and
    • flips (−1)ⁿ, since n + (2k−1−n) = 2k−1 is odd,

    so the series is its own negative and therefore vanishes. Crucially Equiv.tsum_eq and tsum_neg are both unconditional in Mathlib, so no summability hypothesis is required — which is what makes this cheap.

    Reparametrisation #

    Everything is stated in P with p = P², so that the exponent is −n(n+1) with no integer division. θ_P(P²w) = w·θ_P(w) is then the usual θ_p(pw) = w·θ_p(w). This is purely to keep the arithmetic division-free; nothing mathematical turns on it.

    What this file does NOT prove — stated so it is not read for more #

    • The converse. That −p^ℤ are the only zeros of θ_p is the Jacobi triple product, which is not proved here. Without it one gets "these points are zeros", not "there are no others".

      Mathlib cannot supply it (source inspected at HEAD, 2026-08-07): Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/JacobiTheta/ has OneVariable, TwoVariable, Bounds, Manifold, defining the additive two-variable theta jacobiTheta₂ z τ = ∑' n : ℤ, … — but there is no Jacobi triple product anywhere (the "triple product" matches are Jordan algebras, the Hermitian functional calculus, and the vector cross product) and no zero-locus or vanishing results for theta at all.

      What Mathlib does have, and what a future attempt should build on: HasProd/Multipliable/tprod (full infinite-product infrastructure), Analysis/Meromorphic/{Divisor, IsolatedZeros, Order}, and the full circleIntegral API including Complex.circleIntegral_eq_of_differentiable_on_annulus_off_countable. It does not have the argument principle, a winding number, or a complex residue (name scan at HEAD, 2026-08-07: zero hits for argumentPrinciple, windingNumber, countZeros; every residue hit is IsLocalRing).

      But JTP is not on the critical path, and that is worth stating plainly because this file previously implied it was. The two routes are not equally costly:

      (a) zero-counting. θ_p holomorphic on ℂ* + θ_p(pw) = wθ_p(w) forces the winding number on |w| = pρ to exceed the one on |w| = ρ by exactly one. That is no longer a sketch: it is qTheta_winding_step below, proved. The argument principle then reads the difference off as exactly one zero, with multiplicity, in ρ < |z| < pρ, which combined with qTheta_neg_zpow_eq_zero pins it at −p^k, makes it simple, and excludes any other. Every input to (a) other than the argument principle itself is now proved in this file, up to and including the contour identity it would be applied to. (b) JTP via the product side — define ∏(1−xᵐ)(1+xᵐw)(1+xᵐ⁻¹w⁻¹), show it satisfies the same functional equation, conclude the ratio is elliptic and bounded hence constant. This additionally needs Liouville on ℂ*/p^ℤ, i.e. strictly more than (a).

      So the honest remaining gap is one named Mathlib-scale lemma, the argument principle on an annulus, not a theory build-out. That is still not a small job — but it is a different size of job than "formalise JTP", and the distinction was being lost. Recorded so this is neither re-scoped as cheap nor written off as hopeless.

      Independently: the statement itself is checked to hold at our substrate in computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_theta_zero_count.py, so what is missing is a formalisation gap and not a mathematical doubt.

    • That these zeros are poles of P. That needs the analytic construction — convergent 0-side series, entire ∞-side series, the matrix inverse and its meromorphy — none of which is formalised.

    • Divisor completeness. The residue-theorem argument (each entry has exactly two simple poles per fundamental domain, so residues cancel) lives on the multiplicative torus ℂ*/p^ℤ and needs elliptic-function theory that Mathlib does not have.

    Numerically all of the above is checked in the witness computations/qbeta/c3-w2-c1-connection-matrix.witness.json (7/7).

          Analytic infrastructure #

          The vanishing theorem below needs none of this — Equiv.tsum_eq and tsum_neg are unconditional, so it goes through even if qTheta were junk. But any route to the converse (Jacobi triple product, or zero-counting via the argument principle) needs qTheta to be a genuine analytic object first. These two are that foundation.

          theorem QOU.QBeta.C1Poles.summable_qThetaTerm (P w : ℝ) (hP : 1 < P) (hw : w ≠ 0) :
          Summable fun (n : ℤ) => qThetaTerm P w n

          The q-theta series is summable for P > 1, w ≠ 0.

          |term n| = exp(−L n² + (M−L) n) with L = log P > 0. Since −L t² + B t ≤ B²/(4L) for every real t (one completed square), the series is dominated by C·e^{−|n|} with C = exp(B²/(4L)), B = |M−L| + 1.

            The magnitudes at a conjugate pair of exponents agree EXACTLY.

            The 0-side exponents are a conjugate pair t, conj t (C1ZeroSideExponents.conjugate_pair_iff). For real z, z / conj t = conj (z / t), so by qThetaC_conj the two theta values are conjugates and their magnitudes are equal — hence so are |e_t(z)| for the two exponents, since they share the numerator θ_p(z).

            The connection invariant's closed form #

            Numerically, the basis-invariant connection datum χ = P₀₀P₁₁/(P₀₁P₁₀) equals

            χ(z) = θ_p(z/α)·θ_p(−z/ᾱ) / [ θ_p(−z/α)·θ_p(z/ᾱ) ],   α = t₂/T

            with constant exactly 1 and |α| = q^{3/4} — worst relative deviation 5.3e-63 over twelve independent points (computations/qbeta/c3_w2_c1_connection_invariant.py and successors). The theorem below proves that this shape has the required invariance, straight from the functional equation. It does not prove that χ equals it — that needs the analytic construction, which is not formalised.

            qTheta P (P ^ 2 * z / a) * qTheta P (-(P ^ 2 * z) / b) * (qTheta P (-z / a) * qTheta P (z / b)) = qTheta P (-(P ^ 2 * z) / a) * qTheta P (P ^ 2 * z / b) * (qTheta P (z / a) * qTheta P (-z / b))

            theorem QOU.QBeta.C1Poles.thetaRatio_multiplier (P c z : ℝ) (hP : 0 < P) (hc : c ≠ 0) (hz : z ≠ 0) :
            qTheta P (P ^ 2 * (c * z)) * qTheta P z = c * (qTheta P (c * z) * qTheta P (P ^ 2 * z))

            The theta quotient E_c(z) = θ_P(cz)/θ_P(z) is a q-character with multiplier c.

            Division-free form of E_c(P²z) = c · E_c(z).               theorem QOU.QBeta.C1Poles.logDeriv_qThetaC_functional_equation (P : ℝ) (hP : 1 < P) (w : ℂ) (hw : w ≠ 0) (hθ : qThetaC P w ≠ 0) :

              The +1. The logarithmic derivative gains exactly 1/w per period:

              p·θ'(pw)/θ(pw)  =  1/w + θ'(w)/θ(w),   p = P².

              This is the whole zero-counting mechanism in one line. The argument principle reads that off as exactly one zero, counted with multiplicity, in every fundamental annulus, which with qTheta_neg_zpow_eq_zero pins that zero at −p^k and makes it simple.               Argument principle on an annulus, for a single zero of known order.

              If f = (· − a)^m · u with u analytic and non-vanishing on an open A containing the closed annulus r ≤ ‖z‖ ≤ R, and the zero a lies strictly inside the annulus, then the winding difference is exactly m:

              ∮_{|z|=R} f'/f  −  ∮_{|z|=r} f'/f  =  m · 2πi.

              The u'/u part cancels by the annulus Cauchy theorem; the m/(z−a) part contributes 2πi on the outer circle (Cauchy) and 0 on the inner (holomorphic in the disc).

              The argument principle on an annulus, Finset-indexed, with the factors allowed to sit anywhere off the two contours.

              For F = (∏ a ∈ S, (·−a)^{m a})·u with u analytic and non-vanishing on a neighbourhood of the closed annulus, the winding difference counts exactly the factors between the two circles, with multiplicity. A factor inside the inner circle contributes 2πi to both integrals and cancels; one outside the outer circle contributes to neither.

              The weaker hypothesis is what the θ instantiation needs. Counting with a filter removes the need entirely.
