    QOU.LiftingDescent.tm_transient_localisation #

    Canonical Lean for content block lem:topological-mass-transient-localisation: Transient terms are bounded by exceptional-divisor contributions.

    Block statement. The transient component α − τ̂ₙ(α) has support in disjoint discs Dᵢ centred at the blow-up points, and the norm of its multiplicative integral is bounded by a sum over the exceptional divisors Eᵢ:

    ‖MI∫_Σ (α − τ̂ₙ(α))‖ ≤ Σᵢ ‖MI∫_{Eᵢ} (∇_A q, L ∧ F⁺)‖.

    The block stated equality until 2026-10-10, when it was restated as the inequality on owner ruling (bean tmvac); the equality is the open conjecture class below.

    What is derived, and what is not #

    Everything is a parameter. The multiplicative integrals take values in an arbitrary (possibly non-commutative) seminormed group G, so the disc product is an ordered List.prod. The proof block's two geometric steps are explicit hypotheses:

    • hsplit — the transient integral is the ordered product of the disc integrals (support in the disjoint discs Dᵢ);
    • hsupp — each disc integral equals the exceptional-divisor integral (the integrand is supported on Eᵢ ⊂ Dᵢ).

    From these, the inequality ‖MI∫_Σ‖ ≤ Σᵢ ‖MI∫_{Eᵢ}‖ is derived (tm_transient_localisation) from the triangle inequality of a group seminorm.

    The equality the block formerly stated is not derivable from these hypotheses: the proof block's step "the norm of the product equals the sum of the norms" is false for a general group norm, and norm_additivity_not_forced exhibits data satisfying both hypotheses (in Multiplicative ℝ) for which equality fails. The equality is therefore recorded as the content-bearing conjecture class TransientLocalisationNormEquality, with no instance. Closing it needs a further structural input (e.g. that the disc integrals lie on a common ray on which the norm is additive — the proof block's appeal to commutation modulo the central band does not by itself give this).

    An earlier revision carried both geometric steps and their composite as fields of a data class that the all-zero instance satisfies, so the "localisation" said nothing; one earlier still defined transientMItotal := 0. Bean tmvac.

    Transient-term localisation, derived half (inequality).

    Given that the transient multiplicative integral over Σ is the ordered product of the disc integrals (hsplit) and that each disc integral is the exceptional-divisor integral (hsupp), its norm is at most the sum of the exceptional-divisor norms.

    The equality is not forced by the split. There is data in the seminormed group Multiplicative ℝ satisfying both geometric hypotheses (hsplit with N = 2, and hsupp trivially with div = disc) for which ‖MI∫_Σ‖ ≠ Σᵢ ‖MI∫_{Eᵢ}‖. Scope (a): this shows the hypotheses alone do not yield the block's equality; it says nothing about the specific multiplicative integral of the transient integrand.
