      def QOU.Interactions.inductiveGBReduction [ctx : InductiveGBReductionContext] :
      Prop

      Proposition statement (from manuscript):

      Let $\mathcal{N}_{\leq k}$ denote the Gröbner--Shirshov normal form of the first $k$ nucleons in $H_k(q)$. The inductive step*

      $$ \mathcal{N}_{\leq k+1} #

      r!\bigl( \mathcal{N}{\leq k} \cdot C(\mathfrak{N}k, \mathfrak{N}{k+1}; \sigma_g) \cdot \mathfrak{N}{k+1},; G \bigr) $$

      produces a normal form with at most $\binom{A-1}{\lfloor(A-1)/2\rfloor}$ terms, where $r(\cdot, G)$ is the Gröbner--Shirshov division algorithm (nuclear Gröbner reduction).

      The total computation for $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ requires $O(A^2)$ multiplications and $O(A^3)$ Hecke reduction steps.

      Proof #

      At each inductive step, the product $\mathcal{N}_{\leq k} \cdot C \cdot \mathfrak{N}_{k+1}$ has at most $|\mathcal{N}_{\leq k}| \times 2 \times 6$ terms (the crossing $C$ has at most 2 terms, and the nucleon NF has 6 terms). The Gröbner--Shirshov reduction eliminates all $\sigma_i^2$ via the Hecke relation and sorts via Yang--Baxter, producing a normal form of bounded size.

      The key identity enabling this is the nuclear Gröbner reduction.

      We name the resulting bound (pattern C of the vacuity-fix playbook) as a Prop over the context's primitive step-count data, rather than projecting it from self-assuming h_* fields: the normal form has at most $\binom{A-1}{\lfloor(A-1)/2\rfloor}$ terms, with $O(A^2)$ multiplications and $O(A^3)$ Hecke reduction steps. An instance of this proposition is the open complexity claim.
