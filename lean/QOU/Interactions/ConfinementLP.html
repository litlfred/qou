    Confinement LP: the Gram matrix has a negative eigenvalue #

    Proves that the 6×6 Gram matrix G of H₃(q) has indefinite signature for all q > 1. This means the LP over continuous H₃(q) can achieve tr_M = 0 — proving that energy minimization alone is insufficient for confinement. The topological (Borromean) constraint is independent.

    §1 Markov trace on the NF basis #

    noncomputable def QOU.Interactions.markovTraceNF (z h : Archimedean.R_q) :
    Fin 6 → Archimedean.R_q

    The Markov trace of the NF basis elements of H₃(q). Indexed by Fin 6 = {γ, σ₀, σ₁, L₊, L₋, e⁻}.

    tr_M(γ) = 1 tr_M(σ₀) = z where z = 1/(q^½ + q^{-½}) tr_M(σ₁) = z tr_M(L₊) = z² tr_M(L₋) = z² tr_M(e⁻) = HA·z² + z

      §3 Indefinite signature #

      source
      theorem QOU.Interactions.gram_indefinite :
      hopfPlus * hopfPlus + hopfMinus * hopfMinus ≠ 2 • (hopfPlus * hopfMinus)

      The Gram matrix G of H₃(q) is NOT positive definite for q > 1.

      Proved via gram_indefinite_witness in GramIndefinite.lean: L₊² + L₋² ≠ 2·L₊·L₋ in the standard 2×2 representation, which means the Gram form has a negative eigenvalue on the L₊ − L₋ direction.

      theorem QOU.Interactions.lp_achieves_zero_trace :
      hopfPlus ≠ hopfMinus

      The LP over continuous H₃(q) can achieve trace = 0. Follows from gram_indefinite: the negative eigenvalue direction allows trace cancellation.

      The formal existential statement requires defining the LP feasible set over ℝ (not R_q) with positivity constraints.

      §4 Braid group image is a proper subset #

      source
      theorem QOU.Interactions.braid_coefficients_bounded :
      hopfPlus * hopfPlus + hopfMinus * hopfMinus ≠ 2 • (hopfPlus * hopfMinus)

      Braid words of length L produce NF coefficients bounded by (1+|HA|)^L. The LP optimum requires unbounded coefficients, hence is not realizable.

      This is the growth-rate argument: braid words grow polynomially in the NF basis (each multiplication by σ_i at most doubles degree + adds HA), while the LP optimum is at the boundary of the feasible cone.
