    The classification theorem has two layers:

    • the geometric input — that the minimal genus of a hyperelliptic Riemann surface carrying a faithful ℤ₅ automorphism commuting with the hyperelliptic involution is g = 2 (the bar φ-orbit / Möbius- normalisation argument in the manuscript). This is the single open categorical/geometric fact, carried here as a hypothesis hg : g = 2;
    • the arithmetic consequence — that the branch divisor then has |Branch(π)| = 2g + 2 = 6 by Riemann–Hurwitz. This is a genuine computation and is derived (by/omega), not projected from an opaque context field.

    This is pattern A of the vacuity-fix playbook: isolate the lone open geometric input (g = 2) as a hypothesis and mechanically derive the remaining numerical claims. The previous encoding posited the whole conjunction as a single class field and projected it — a vacuous self-assuming projection — and is removed.

      theorem QOU.HyperellipticQuintic.minimal_z5_classification {g branch_count : ℕ} (hg : g = 2) (hbranch : branch_count = branchCount g) :
      g = 2 ∧ branch_count = 2 * g + 2 ∧ branch_count = 6

      Minimal ℤ₅-hyperelliptic classification (Riemann–Hurwitz numerics) — [manuscript theorem].

      Let X be a smooth projective Riemann surface of minimal genus that is hyperelliptic (degree-2 cover π : X → ℙ¹ with involution ι) and admits a faithful ℤ₅ automorphism φ commuting with ι. The manuscript shows by the bar φ-orbit / Möbius-normalisation argument that the minimal such genus is g = 2; this is the lone open geometric input, carried as the hypothesis hg : g = 2.

      Given g = 2, the branch-divisor cardinality is derived: |Branch(π)| = 2g + 2 = 6. (The full classification — X ≅ 𝒳₅ : y² = x⁵ − 1 — concerns the curve isomorphism and is tracked separately.)
