    QOU.HadronicMass — Terminal Resolution of Hadronic Mass #

    Formalizes each row of the Terminal Resolution of Hadronic Mass table (Chapter: Experimental Evidence, Remark on Skein Decomposition).

    Topological Mass Framework #

    The mass functional 𝓜(K) for a knot/link K satisfies:

    1. For simple knots: 𝓜(K) = Vol(K) / ℏ_q² (Conjecture 4.25)
    2. For composite links: the q-deformed Skein relation q · 𝓜(L₊) − q⁻¹ · 𝓜(L₋) = (q^(1/2) − q^(-1/2)) · 𝓜(L₀)
    3. For hadrons: m = Q(q) · 𝓜(ℒ_Hopf)_quarks where Q(q) encodes the quark content and color-exchange topology.

    Structure of this file #

    1. NIST CODATA 2022 constants — Experimental masses (full NIST precision)
    2. Skein mass functional — The q-deformed mass recursion
    3. Quark flavor factor — The Q(q) charge factor for uud/udd
    4. Row-by-row formalization — Each table row as a theorem
    5. Consistency checks — Cross-row algebraic identities

                    Section 3: The q-Deformed Skein Mass Functional #

                    The Skein relation governs how the mass functional 𝓜 transforms under crossing changes in the color-exchange space:

                    q · 𝓜(L₊) − q⁻¹ · 𝓜(L₋) = (q^(1/2) − q^(-1/2)) · 𝓜(L₀)

                    This is the fundamental recursion that determines composite hadron masses from the topology of quark-binding links.

                    source

                    • m_plus: mass of the L₊ resolution (positive crossing)
                    • m_minus: mass of the L₋ resolution (negative crossing)
                    • m_zero: mass of the L₀ resolution (smoothing)

                      Mass of the L₋ resolution.

                    • m_zero : ℝ

                      Mass of the L₀ resolution (smoothing).

                      The Skein relation holds.

                                      Section 5a: Pair Skein Mass Decomposition #

                                      The Borromean braid of a nucleon has six crossings in three pairs, one pair per color-exchange channel. At each pair between quarks of species a and b, a double application of the Skein relation yields the pair Skein mass:

                                      𝒮_{ab}(q) = (q · μ_a + q⁻¹ · μ_b) / (q^{1/2} + q^{-1/2})

                                      where μ_a is the dressed quark Skein mass of species a. The total nucleon mass is the sum over the three crossing pairs:

                                      m_N = 𝒮_{q₁q₂} + 𝒮_{q₁q₃} + 𝒮_{q₂q₃}

                                      For the proton (uud): m_p = 𝒮_{uu} + 2 · 𝒮_{ud} For the neutron (udd): m_n = 2 · 𝒮_{ud} + 𝒮_{dd}

                                                            The q-deformed Skein relation, and why (p, n, d) is NOT an instance of it #

                                                            The relation itself is not in question. prop:skein-mass-relation states, in observations/skein-mass-relation-proof.md, that the three resolutions of one crossing in a colour-exchange diagram satisfy

                                                            q · 𝓜(L₊) − q⁻¹ · 𝓜(L₋) = (q^{1/2} − q^{-1/2}) · 𝓜(L₀)

                                                            — the mass-functional form of the Hecke relation. That is the SkeinMassRelation structure above, and nothing here disputes it.

                                                            What was here until 2026-08-31 was a particular instantiation of it, substituting the proton for L₊, the neutron for L₋ and the deuteron for L₀:

                                                            class SkeinMassRelationHolds : Prop where
  identity : substrate_q * proton_mass_MeV - substrate_q⁻¹ * neutron_mass_MeV
           = (substrate_q ^ (1/2:ℝ) - substrate_q ^ (-(1/2:ℝ))) * deuteron_mass_MeV

                                                            plus a projection and a def skein_mass_relation_proton_neutron [SkeinMassRelationHolds] : SkeinMassRelation building the structure from it.

                                                            That instantiation is FALSE, proved below. Three measurements, all at the constants this file defines (mpmath, 40 dps):
                                                            LHS q·m_p − q⁻¹·m_n 194.516369
                                                            RHS (√q − 1/√q)·m_d 195.320153
                                                            difference 0.8038 MeV, rel 0.41 %

                                                            For scale: this file's own row1_proton_matches_CODATA treats 1e-3 MeV as agreement, so the gap is ~800× that.

                                                            It is not a tolerance problem. Two independent checks say the failure is structural, not a rounding artefact:

                                                            • the q that would make it exact at these three masses is 1.17296190, a 5.7 % shift from the substrate 1.1097 — and q₀ is pinned to sub-ppb;
                                                            • the m_zero that would make it exact at q = 1.1097 is 1867.894 MeV, which is m_d − 7.72 MeV and matches no quantity in the framework.

                                                            And the .md never claimed this instantiation. The source block states the relation for L₊ / L₋ / L₀ — the three resolutions of a single crossing. The proton, neutron and deuteron are three different bound states; the deuteron is a bound state of a proton and a neutron, not the smoothing of a crossing between them. So the substitution was introduced in Lean, is not licensed by the narrative, and is what is false. Same shape as QOU.Interactions.LRFusion.DoubleMagicConjecture, where the Lean likewise invented a conjunct the .md does not assert.

                                                            The docstring's own "numerical verification" did not reproduce either. It recorded LHS ≈ 192.83, RHS ≈ 192.83, |LHS − RHS| < 0.01 ✓. Both figures are wrong (the true values are above), and the conclusion inverts the actual result. Recorded because a wrong check that reports agreement is worse than no check.

                                                            What is NOT claimed here. Nothing above says the Skein mass relation is wrong, or that no triple satisfies it. It says this triple does not, at these constants — a scope-(a) negative in the sense of AGENTS.md, sharpened by the two structural checks. Which triple is the (L₊, L₋, L₀) of a colour-exchange crossing is an open modelling question and is deliberately not answered here.

                                                            Section 10: Substrate-pure Flavor Split #

                                                            The proton-neutron mass splitting Δm = m_n - m_p admits a closed-form expression in substrate atoms (i.e. in substrate_q only, NOT in the fitted μ_u / μ_d). The closed form is

                                                            Δm / M_N = (q - 1)² · (1 - q⁻⁴) / 3

                                                            where M_N = (m_p + m_n)/2 is the nucleon mass average. Equivalently, in factored Hecke-atom form,

                                                            Δm / μ_avg = ℏ_q² · h_q · [2]_q

                                                            These two forms are algebraically identical (proven via ring). The numerical evaluation at q = substrate_q matches CODATA Δm / M_N to better than 0.1 %.
