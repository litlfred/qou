      What a trace-factoring functional can carry #

      qou-qb7p's surviving objection to the (2,1) clause of unified-iterative-correction-proof.md is that the Wedderburn embedding has the wrong shape: it assembles blocks into one matrix, while the clause needs one block's scalar C_{(2,1)} split into three coefficients (volume, surface, Coulomb). The probe accompanying that bean records why no Lean theorem was offered — the tempting statement, "three independent coefficients cannot come from one scalar", is false as stated, since set-theoretically a single real encodes three, and manufacturing a hypothesis to rescue it would be the same overreach the probe documents.

      There is a hypothesis that does not have to be manufactured: linearity, which this corpus already commits to for the Markov trace (markovTrace_add, markovTrace_smul, markovTrace_linear in Common.lean). Under it the statement becomes true and provable, and that is what this section does.

      markovTraceOnWedderburn factors through the block traces — one real per partition, not the whole block. A functional of that form supported at a single λ is exactly c λ · trace (x λ): one coefficient against one number. Three such functionals at one partition are therefore linearly dependent, with an explicit witness.

      What this does and does not settle. It does not show the (2,1) clause is false; the claim may well be true. It rules out one route to it — the route the clause itself names — whenever that route is linear, factors through the Markov trace, and is localised at (2,1). Three independent coefficients need more than one dimension, and that is all the trace supplies.

      It also says where the room is, which is the useful half. For λ = (2,1) ⊢ 3, f^λ = 2, so the block is M₂(ℝ) — four real dimensions, of which the trace reads one. The information the clause wants is present in the block and destroyed by taking its trace. A derivation that reads the block rather than its trace is not blocked by anything here.

        The room, stated as a theorem rather than asserted #

        The section above says the information a three-coefficient derivation needs "is present in the block and destroyed by taking its trace". That was an assertion. Here it is a theorem: whenever a block has dimension at least 2 there are distinct decompositions on which every block trace agrees — so every trace-factoring functional, the Markov trace included, takes the same value on both. The witness is an off-diagonal matrix unit, which is traceless.

        So the trace is not merely one-dimensional per partition (previous section); it is genuinely lossy, and the lost directions are where a derivation reading the block rather than its trace would live.

        The (2,1) instance — gap closed 2026-09-05. Instantiating this needs 2 ≤ f^{(2,1)}. When first written, no declaration in this corpus computed seminormalDim at a named partition, so the (2,1) reading was conditional on a classical fact the Lean side could not supply. QOU/HeckeAlgebra/SeminormalDimSmall.lean now supplies it — f^{(2,1)} = 2 via the Frame–Robinson–Thrall hook-length formula (QOU.Mathlib.dimSYT_eq_dimHLF, pointwise and axiom-clean) — and carries trace_does_not_separate_p21 and indistinguishable_p21 unconditionally.

        The theorems here keep the bound as a hypothesis, which is the right shape: they are about any block of dimension ≥ 2, and (2,1) is one instance. Note the boundary is real — f^{(1,1)} = 1, also proved there, so at the charge shape the block is its trace and these results say nothing.
