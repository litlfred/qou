    QOU.OneDim.TLIsXXZChain — the production trace's algebra is the 1D XXZ chain #

    What this settles. The corpus already proves that the production Markov trace is the SU_q(2) / Temperley–Lieb one: FibreDimensionForcesTL.lean derives z · [N]_q = q ^ N from a fibre of quantum dimension [N]_q, which at N = 2 is convention D (z_D = q³/(q²+1)), and MarkovTraceTLUniqueness.lean closes the converse. Neither says what the Temperley–Lieb algebra is as a mechanics.

    This file supplies that step. Writing e for a Temperley–Lieb generator on two neighbouring sites of (R²)^{⊗n}, the operator identity

    -2 • e = (XX + YY + Δ • ZZ) - Δ • I - ((q - q⁻¹)/2) • (sz₁ - sz₂)

    holds with Δ = (q + q⁻¹)/2; summing over i and telescoping the boundary term gives the open U_q(sl₂)-symmetric XXZ chain on n sites. So the algebra the shipped pipeline computes in is a one-dimensional spin chain.

    What is proved here, and what is delegated. In the basis (↑↑, ↑↓, ↓↑, ↓↓) that matrix identity has five entries, three of which are 0 = 0. The two that carry content are the scalar identities twoMul_eq / twoInv_eq below — the statement that the anisotropy and the Hecke parameter h = q - q⁻¹ recombine to 2q and 2q⁻¹ on the two singlet-channel diagonal entries. Those are the file's algebraic content and are proved here, over any field in which 2 is invertible (AGENTS.md §7c — no ℝ, no ordering, nothing archimedean).

    The hypothesis (2 : R) ≠ 0 is not a technicality to be optimised away: the anisotropy is a half-sum, so a field where 2 = 0 is one where halving is not defined. Mathlib's 0⁻¹ = 0 convention makes that failure silent rather than loud — in characteristic 2 every x / 2 here collapses to 0 and all three identities become false, not merely unprovable. They were first stated without the hypothesis and lean-direct caught it; the negations are witnessed over GaloisField 2 2 and, for tlLoop_add_heckeH, already in ZMod 2 at q = 1. Do not remove the binder.

    The matrix assembly itself is verified numerically at mp.dps = 60, at both documented q₀ pins, in computations/onedim/tl_is_the_xxz_chain_probe.py (17/17), which also carries the provenance caution: the boundary sign was recalled wrong on first attempt, and the coefficients are solved from the matrix entries rather than remembered.

    The regime claim (one_lt_anisotropy) needs an ordering and so is stated over ℝ, after the substrate→archimedean wall, in its own section.

          The regime claim #

          This section needs an ordering, so it is stated over ℝ and sits after the substrate→archimedean wall (AGENTS.md §7c).

          source
          theorem QOU.OneDim.one_lt_anisotropy {q : ℝ} (hpos : 0 < q) (hne : q ≠ 1) :
          1 < xxzAnisotropy q

          The substrate is in the massive (Ising-like) regime. For real q > 0 with q ≠ 1, the XXZ anisotropy exceeds 1.

          This is what makes Bethe strings stable bound states rather than scattering states: Δ > 1 is the gapped, Ising-like phase of the XXZ chain, whereas |Δ| < 1 is the massless phase where no such bound states survive.

          It carries no calibration input and no free parameter: the conclusion follows from q ≠ 1 alone, so it is forced by the substrate being a genuine deformation rather than fitted to reproduce anything. The numerical value at q₀ is Δ - 1 ≈ 5.448e-3, agreeing between the two documented pins to ~1.2 ppb (measured in the probe cited above) — so the verdict does not depend on which pin is used.

          source
          theorem QOU.OneDim.eq_one_of_anisotropy_le {q : ℝ} (hpos : 0 < q) (h : xxzAnisotropy q ≤ 1) :
          q = 1

          Contrapositive form: an anisotropy at or below 1 forces q = 1, i.e. the undeformed (classical) point. There is no real q ≠ 1 giving a massless chain, so the massless phase is unreachable from a real substrate parameter — it requires |q| = 1, which the corpus's q₀ ≈ 1.10998 is not.
