    The bound on Σ|eig(M_λ)| — the Cauchy–Schwarz step, formalized (qou-o6ov) #

    Context. #6556 established that the b(λ)-decay of |tr_λ(ρΩ₂^P)| is carried by the magnitude factor Σ_i |eig_i(M_λ)|, with M_λ = ρ_λ(ρ)·ρ_λ(Ω₂^P), and that ‖M_λ‖_F² = tr_λ(X* X) for X = ρ·Ω₂^P — a positive trace of a Hecke algebra element, whose hook-column closed form in the reduced Burau is derived there. What was missing was a bound on Σ|eig| itself. The derivation is three steps:

    (i)   Σ_i |eig_i(M)|  ≤ √(dim · Σ_i |eig_i(M)|²)      Cauchy–Schwarz on the moduli vector
(ii)  Σ_i |eig_i(M)|² ≤ ‖M‖_F²                        Schur's inequality
(iii) ‖M_λ‖_F²        = tr_λ(X* X)                    anti-involution identity (#6556)
      ⟹  Σ_i |eig_i(M_λ)| ≤ √(dim_λ · tr_λ(X* X))                                    (B)

    What is formalized here is step (i), and step (i) only — the one step that is pure inequality over an arbitrary finite index set, with no matrix theory in it. sum_le_sqrt_card_mul_sum_sq is Cauchy–Schwarz in the form the derivation uses; sum_le_sqrt_card_mul_of_sq_le composes it with a hypothesis Σ aᵢ² ≤ F, which is exactly the shape step (ii) supplies, and yields (B)'s right-hand side.

    What is NOT formalized, and is carried as a hypothesis rather than asserted: Schur's inequality (ii) is a classical theorem about the eigenvalues of a complex matrix (via Schur triangularization) and is not proved here; sum_le_sqrt_card_mul_of_sq_le takes it as the hypothesis hF. The identity (iii) is the measured anti-involution fact of #6556. The Hecke-specific content — the seminormal construction, the atom word, the reduced-Burau closed form for tr_λ(X*X) — is outside Mathlib, as in the sibling files.

    Honest note on strength. Step (i) costs a factor up to √dim and is tight only when all |eig_i| are equal. Measured on this corpus the resulting bound gives away a factor 2–6 (tightness Σ|eig| / √(dim·tr(X*X)) runs 0.16–0.48). It is a usable bound — it holds and it decays with b at controlled dimension — not a sharp one.

    This is the archimedean side (Real.sqrt, an order), kept in its own file per §7c so the algebraic siblings stay over a bare CommRing.

    source
    theorem QOU.MassTheory.sum_le_sqrt_card_mul_sum_sq {ι : Type u_1} [Fintype ι] (a : ι → ℝ) (ha : ∀ (i : ι), 0 ≤ a i) :
    ∑ i : ι, a i ≤ √(↑(Fintype.card ι) * ∑ i : ι, a i ^ 2)

    Cauchy–Schwarz, in the form the Σ|eig| derivation uses. For non-negative a, Σ aᵢ ≤ √(card ι · Σ aᵢ²). This is step (i) of the chain: it is what converts control of the squared moduli (which Schur's inequality supplies, and which the Frobenius norm computes) into control of Σ|eig| itself.

    source
    theorem QOU.MassTheory.sum_le_sqrt_card_mul_of_sq_le {ι : Type u_1} [Fintype ι] (a : ι → ℝ) (ha : ∀ (i : ι), 0 ≤ a i) (F : ℝ) (hF : ∑ i : ι, a i ^ 2 ≤ F) :
    ∑ i : ι, a i ≤ √(↑(Fintype.card ι) * F)

    The composed bound (B). Given non-negative a (the eigenvalue moduli of M_λ) and any F dominating Σ aᵢ² — the role played by Schur's inequality together with ‖M_λ‖_F² = tr_λ(X* X) — we get Σ aᵢ ≤ √(card ι · F). With F = tr_λ(X* X) this is exactly

    Σ_i |eig_i(M_λ)| ≤ √(dim_λ · tr_λ(X* X)),

    whose right-hand side is a positive trace of an algebra element, explicit in the reduced Burau on the hook column (#6556). Hypothesis-carrying by design: Schur's inequality is the classical input and is not proved here (_of_ naming per local/formalizer).
