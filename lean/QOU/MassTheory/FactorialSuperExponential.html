    Factorial growth is super-exponential — the bridge #6101 withdrew #

    QOU.Mass.ConstantKernelGrowthBound (landed in #6101) proves not_isConstKernelRec_of_superExponential: no constant-kernel recurrence has a SuperExponential solution, where

    SuperExponential a  :=  ∀ M K : ℝ, ∃ n, M * K ^ n < |a n|.

    That is the right generality. But the corpus's actual growth statement is not phrased that way: SWPeriodSuperExponential.factorial_le_involution_two_mul (PR #6052) gives n ! ≤ I (2n) — a factorial lower bound on the even subsequence. Without a bridge, the general theorem cannot be applied to the one result that would trigger it.

    #6101's author attempted that bridge and withdrew it rather than half-prove it, recording in their docstring that they could not locate the Mathlib limit lemma in this toolchain and that their first attempt mis-indexed M·Kⁿ at 2n. Their theorems are unaffected — nothing in their file needs it — and they noted it "reads best" next to a factorial statement. This file supplies it.

    What is proved #

    • superExponential_of_factorial_le — if n ! ≤ a (2n) for every n, then a is SuperExponential. The index 2n is what makes it work for every real K, negative included: K ^ (2n) = (K²)ⁿ ≥ 0, so the sign case on M is clean and no positivity hypothesis on K is needed.
    • not_isConstKernelRec_of_factorial_le — hence no constant-kernel recurrence admits such an a, by composition with #6101's theorem.

    The limit input is Real.summable_pow_div_factorial: (K²)ⁿ / n ! is summable, so its terms tend to zero, so the factorial outruns every geometric. tendsto_pow_div_factorial_atTop does not exist under that name in this toolchain, which is what stalled the original attempt.

    Scope #

    This is plumbing between two landed results, not a new finding. It decides nothing about which premise of prop:prolonged-periods-fixed-depth is false — the identification a_D^(n) = I(n)/n, the fixed-finite-rank operator, or the n-independence of the kernel. See beans qou-9yqb and #6052.

    Base ring ℝ: the content is an order/limit statement, so the archimedean setting is genuinely required (AGENTS.md §7c).
