    QOU.MassTheory.InvolutionSelectionRule — what an invariance argument does and does not localise #

    Bean qou-bn8e. The corpus argues in several places that a mass splitting must be sourced by one particular sector, because the splitting breaks an involution that every other sector respects. The clearest instance is rem:isospin-mass-split-em-localisation (folio/quantum-observable-universe/mass-theory/), which concludes that the mirror-isobar split m(³H) − m(³He) ≈ 0.529 MeV "must therefore be entirely sourced by the Coulomb term", on the grounds that "the trace tower, co-Casimir, chirality, and shell-correction functionals all respect these structural involutions."

    The valid core of that argument is here, and so is its load-bearing hypothesis. The core is a one-line cancellation. What is doing the real work is the enumeration — the claim that the listed sectors are all of them, and that each is invariant. This file makes the enumeration a List in the type, so that the arity of the claim cannot be lost between the proposition and the prose (AGENTS.md §"Grep the claim SHAPE, not only the topic").

    What is proved #

    declaration content
    split_invariantSector_eq_zero an ι-invariant sector contributes zero to the ι-split
    split_sum_eq_split_remainder a sum of invariant sectors plus a remainder splits exactly like the remainder
    omitted_sector_breaks_localisation the enumeration is load-bearing: omit one non-invariant sector and the conclusion is false

    What this does NOT establish #

    Three things, and the third is why bean qou-bn8e stays open.

    1. Exhaustiveness. split_sum_eq_split_remainder localises the split into G only for the decomposition it is handed. Nothing here says the corpus's four named sectors plus Coulomb exhaust the mass functional, and omitted_sector_breaks_localisation shows that gap is not cosmetic.
    2. The invariance of each named sector. Only the shell-correction sector has a proved sign-flip invariance in this corpus (prop:crystal-isospin-degeneracy, and that one is itself conditional on CrystalEigenlineSignBlind). Surveying the Lean tree turned up no theorem asserting sign-flip invariance of the trace tower, the co-Casimir or the chirality functional. The nearest chirality result, QOU.Resurgence.chiral_sign_cancels, does not help and is worth reading before anyone cites it here: it concerns a different involution (diagram conjugation, not the generator sign-flip) and states anti-invariance, sign (conj μ) = − sign μ. A sector that is anti-invariant is the one that survives a split, not one that drops out of it. So the remark's "all respect these structural involutions" is, at present, one proved case generalised to four — and the generalisation crosses at least two distinct involutions, which is its own hazard (AGENTS.md §da-arity-conflation).
    3. Any magnitude whatsoever. A selection rule says where a quantity may live, never how big it is. E_EM is not derived by this file and is not derived anywhere in this corpus.

    Scope, per the (a)/(b) discipline. Nothing here refutes the localisation claim — it is very plausibly true. The claim is scope-(a): as currently supported it does not follow, because two of its premises are unproved. Fixing that means proving the two missing invariances, not weakening the remark.

        split ι (fun (y : X) => (List.map (fun (s : InvariantSector ι R) => s.F y) ss).sum + G y) x = split ι G x

        Localisation, stated with its enumeration explicit. If the mass functional decomposes as a sum over a given list of invariant sectors plus a remainder G, then the split is the split of G alone.

        The list is the hypothesis. Read ss as "the trace tower, the co-Casimir, the chirality and the shell correction" and G as "the Coulomb term", and this is the remark's argument — with the two things it assumes now visible in the statement rather than in the prose.

        source
        theorem QOU.MassTheory.InvolutionSelectionRule.omitted_sector_breaks_localisation :
        ∃ (ι : ℤ → ℤ) (F : ℤ → ℤ) (G : ℤ → ℤ) (x : ℤ), Function.Involutive ι ∧ split ι G x = 0 ∧ split ι (fun (y : ℤ) => F y + G y) x ≠ 0

        The enumeration is load-bearing, not bookkeeping. If a sector that is not invariant is left out of the list, the localisation conclusion is false: here the remainder G has zero split, so the argument would report "the split is entirely G's, hence zero", while the true split is 2.

        Concretely ι = (· * -1) on ℤ, the omitted sector is the identity, and G is zero. This is why point 2 above matters: an unproved invariance is indistinguishable, to this argument, from an omitted sector.

        Sectors that factor through an invariant of the state #

        The practical way a sector turns out to be invariant is rarely a computation: it is that the sector never sees the data the involution moves. The Frobenius co-Casimir term of the unified mass formula (thm:knot-mass-formula-unified) is the case in point. By thm:frobenius-codim-universal,

        τ(C_dim_q) = Σ_{λ ∈ Â} 1 / dim_q(λ),

        a sum over the Wedderburn blocks of H_n(R, q). It is a function of (n, q) alone and takes no braid word as an argument at all, so a generator sign flip cannot move it: it factors through word ↦ (n, q), which the flip fixes.

          The Frobenius co-Casimir sector is invariant, and the reason is arity. Modelled with Y = ℕ × R for the pair (n, q): whatever G computes from the block data, the generator sign flip leaves (n, q) alone, so the term drops out of the split. Both mirror isobars sit at A = 3, hence at the same n, so it cancels between them exactly rather than approximately.

          This supplies one of the two premises rem:isospin-mass-split-em-localisation was missing. The other, the trace tower, is false at the substrate — QOU.MassTheory.TraceSignFlipRegime.
