    ≤ 2 rows and ≤ 2 columns are different families, and nothing in this corpus said so #

    Bean qou-cds1. Two halves of the Lean corpus quantify over Temperley–Lieb survivors in opposite conventions, and until this file nothing related them:

    module survivor condition in Mathlib terms
    QOU.QBeta.TemperleyLiebTower, QOU.QBeta.TwoRowNormalisation at most two rows μ.colLen 0 ≤ 2
    QOU.MathlibExt.WenzlSchurElement, QOU.MassTheory.SelfConjugateStratumVanishes at most two columns μ.rowLen 0 ≤ 2

    WenzlSchurElement's own docstring warns that "anyone wiring schurElement_tl_eq_zero_iff to that block must transpose". This file supplies the transposition as theorems, so that warning can be discharged rather than merely read.

    Why it is a trap and not just a difference #

    The two families are equinumerous — transposeEquiv below is an explicit bijection between them at every fixed size — while being different sets (atMostTwoRows_iff_atMostTwoCols_false). At n = 3:

    convention surviving shapes Σ dim²
    ≤ 2 rows (3), (2,1) 1² + 2² = 5
    ≤ 2 columns (2,1), (1,1,1) 2² + 1² = 5

    Disjoint but for (2,1), and both sum to catalan 3 = 5. So every count-based and dimension-based check agrees under either reading, and a proof transported from one to the other by matching numbers rather than by transposing is wrong without ever producing a wrong number. That is the failure mode this file exists to make impossible: transposeEquiv is the only honest transport, and it is not the identity.

    Scope — what is proved here and what is not #

    Proved: the shape-level statements. The transpose is a size-preserving involution, it carries each family onto the other, and the two families are genuinely distinct.

    Proved, at general n: the dimension half as well. dimHLF_two_col_eq_two_row gives dim λ' = dim λ for exactly this family — the two-column shape 2^k 1^{n−2k} has the same Frame–Robinson–Thrall dimension as the two-row shape (n−k, k) it transposes to — and dimHLF_two_col_sq_sum carries Σ dim² = catalan n across to the column family, which QOU.Mathlib.TwoRowDimCatalan had only for the row family.

    So the Σ dim² = 5 column of the table above is a theorem, at every n, not hand arithmetic. The proof needs no factorials: both hook products factor into the same three products, termwise. For (a, b) with a = k+m, b = k, against its transpose 2^k 1^m,

    two-row:     ∏_{j<b}(a−j+1) · ∏_{b≤j<a}(a−j) · ∏_{j<b}(b−j)
two-column:  ∏_{i<k}(k+m−i+1) · ∏_{k≤i<k+m}(k+m−i) · ∏_{i<k}(k−i)

    are the same three factors in a different association, so prodHooks agrees on the nose and n!/prodHooks follows. That is why this is ~120 lines rather than a hook-length development.

    Still not proved: dim λ = dim λ' for a general partition. Nothing here touches shapes with three or more rows and three or more columns; the argument above is specific to this family and does not generalise as written. That is a Mathlib-shaped statement and remains open — but it is no longer what stands between the two halves of this corpus.

            The dimension half — and it lives in a different encoding #

            Everything above is about YoungDiagram. The corpus's standard-tableau count, QOU.BraidKnot.IdentityI.dimHLF (Frame–Robinson–Thrall, n!/∏ h_c), is about List ℕ. That is a third encoding alongside Nat.Partition, and the gap between them is part of why the two conventions were never related: the transpose is cheap on YoungDiagram (Mathlib has it), the dimensions are cheap on List ℕ (this corpus has them), and no file held both.

            colLen is deliberately not opened. YoungDiagram.colLen and QOU.BraidKnot.IdentityI.colLen are both in scope in this file and Lean reports the bare name as ambiguous — the encoding straddle described above, biting inside the file that documents it. Written out in full below rather than disambiguated by an alias, so the reader can see which of the two is meant at every use.
