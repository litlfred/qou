          Convexity is sufficient and strictly unnecessary. There is an aggregator through the origin that is not convex on Ici 0 and still has a strictly positive 3-body cumulant — so a search for b₃ > 0 restricted to convex aggregators is searching a strict subset of what the cumulant admits.

          The scaling route — where a derived curvature could come from #

          sign_carries_no_information_beyond_the_secant says the convexity route is a derivation only if the secant condition comes from something that does not already know b₃. Scaling is such a something. A homogeneity degree is a structural property of an assembly law, fixed by how the law behaves under rescaling the bond weight — not a curvature chosen to fix a sign.

          Suggested by Aishwarya–Rotem, New Brunn–Minkowski and functional inequalities via convexity of entropy (arXiv:2311.05446v3), whose Brunn–Minkowski results hold for log-concave measures with a p-homogeneous potential. The relevance here is the shape of the hypothesis, not the inequalities: it is a scaling assumption doing the work that a convexity assumption would otherwise do.

          The scaling identity. If the aggregator scales by a factor c across the ratio 3, the cumulant is (c − 3) Φ g — no convexity, no differentiability, no hypothesis on Φ anywhere else.

          theorem QOU.MassTheory.B3AggSecant.b3Agg_pos_iff_exponent_gt_one (Φ : ℝ → ℝ) {g p : ℝ} (hhom : Φ (3 * g) = 3 ^ p * Φ g) (hpos : 0 < Φ g) :
          0 < b3Agg Φ g ↔ 1 < p

          The power-law reading. A p-homogeneous aggregator scales by 3 ^ p, so b₃ > 0 iff p > 1: the assembly must be superlinear in the bond count. p = 1 is additive superposition and gives exactly zero; p < 1 — which is where a concave law such as a logarithm sits — gives a negative cumulant.

          So the open question "why should the aggregator curve upward?" becomes the sharper and more answerable "is the assembly law superlinear in the bond count, and why?" That is a question about scaling, which the framework can in principle settle without reference to the measured sign.

          theorem QOU.MassTheory.B3AggSecant.sign_carries_no_information_beyond_the_secant (Φ : ℝ → ℝ) {g : ℝ} (hg : 0 < g) :
          0 ≤ b3Agg Φ g ↔ originSecant Φ g ≤ originSecant Φ (3 * g)

          The fit statement, as a theorem rather than a caution. For g > 0 the predicate "Φ has a non-negative 3-body cumulant at g" is logically equivalent to a property of Φ alone — its origin-secant slope not falling across (g, 3g). The two are one proposition, so exhibiting an aggregator with the second is not independent evidence for the first.
