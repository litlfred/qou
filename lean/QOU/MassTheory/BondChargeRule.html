        3. Summed over a nucleus, the degree differs #

        For Z protons of charge 1 and N neutrons of charge 0, over the complete graph on A = Z + N nucleons. Both totals are stated as closed forms and are what the combinatorial sums evaluate to.

        def QOU.MassTheory.BondChargeRule.sumRuleTotal {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (A Z : R) :
        R

        The sum rule totals to (A-1)·Z — each nucleon appears in A-1 bonds, so Σ_{i<j} (Qᵢ + Qⱼ) = (A-1)·Σᵢ Qᵢ = (A-1)Z. Linear in Z.

            4. The mirror difference — right multiplicity, wrong scaling #

            source
            theorem QOU.MassTheory.BondChargeRule.prodRule_mirror_difference {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (Z W : R) :
            prodRuleTotalDoubled Z - prodRuleTotalDoubled W = (Z - W) * (Z + W - 1)

            Under the product rule a mirror pair (Z, ·) vs (W, ·) at fixed A differs by (Z - W)(Z + W - 1), i.e. (Z-W)(A-1) for a mirror pair where Z + W = A. Linear in A. The observed Coulomb displacement energy goes as (A-1)/A^{1/3}, so the product rule fixes the multiplicity and still lacks the 1/R of a Coulomb energy.

            source
            theorem QOU.MassTheory.BondChargeRule.sumRule_mirror_difference {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (A Z W : R) :
            sumRuleTotal A Z - sumRuleTotal A W = (A - 1) * (Z - W)

            The sum rule's mirror difference at fixed A is (A-1)(Z - W) — also nonzero, which is why the model has a mirror splitting at all, and flat in the sense that its Z-dependence carries no extra factor of A.
