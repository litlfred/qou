    The Tjon line in simplex form: saturation alone forces a negative μ₄ #

    Why a large negative four-body moment is not evidence of a four-body force.

    Pionless EFT explains the empirical Tjon correlation between B(³H) and B(⁴He) by the absence of a four-nucleon force at leading order: one SU(4)-symmetric three-nucleon parameter suffices to renormalise the four-nucleon system (Platter–Hammer–Meißner, [platter2005tjon], nucl-th/0409040; there B_d = 2.22 and B_t = 8.48 MeV give B_α = 29.5 MeV against the Coulomb-corrected experimental 29.0 ± 0.1). This file turns that statement into arithmetic on the residual-simplex ansatz M(A) = ∑ₖ μₖ · C(A,k) of QOU.MassTheory.UrsellSlotAlignment, and draws the consequence the Δ_λ programme needs.

    The central identity is tjon_deviation_eq_mu4:

    M(4) − (4·M(3) − 6·M(2)) = μ₄

    so 4·M(3) − 6·M(2) is exactly the two-point ("no four-body parameter") prediction for M(4), and μ₄ is precisely the deviation from it. This is the Tjon line written in the simplex basis: mu4_eq_zero_iff_tjon says the prediction is exact iff μ₄ = 0.

    The consequence is mu4_neg_of_below_tjon: if the four-body point falls below the two-point prediction — which is what saturation does — then μ₄ < 0, with no four-body interaction anywhere in the derivation. So a negative μ₄ is what a saturating, non-pairwise-additive binding curve produces when it is expanded in a C(A,k) basis that has no saturation mechanism of its own. Its sign carries no four-body information.

    additive_higher_moments_vanish is the contrapositive baseline: exact pairwise additivity forces μ₃ = μ₄ = 0, so every nonzero high moment measures non-additivity, not a k-body force.

    On the name. Throughout this file μ₄ is called the "four-body moment" because that is the standing name for the k = 4 simplex coefficient, and the docstrings are careful to deny the physical reading each time. It is worth saying once, plainly, why the denial is needed: with three moments fitted to three anchors (A = 2, 3, 4) the system is exactly determined, so μ₄ := M(4) − 4M(3) + 6M(2) is a definition carrying zero residual freedom. Writing μ₄ is another way of writing "the amount by which M(4) exceeds the two-point prediction" and nothing more; it acquires no physical content from the index it is written under. This is the corpus rule defined residual ≠ derived decomposition applied to this file's own vocabulary. Narrative twin: rem:tjon-universality-external.

    mu4_target_exceeds_n3lo_bound certifies the numeric claim behind the GAP-9 probe: the corpus target μ₄ = −4.591 (in units of B(D) = 2.224566 MeV) exceeds 34 × the N³LO four-nucleon estimate of 0.300 MeV (Jurgenson–Navrátil–Furnstahl, [jurgenson2009srg], arXiv:0905.1873, which measures the SRG-induced four-body force at λ = 2 fm⁻¹ as "about 50 keV net" and quotes the N³LO long-range estimate as "of order a few hundred keV").

    §UniversalLimitCrossCheck at the foot of the file adds the external check that the framing above needs: the field's own parameter-free four-body answer ([hammer2007fourbody]) is not on the μ₄ = 0 baseline, and the simplex basis assigns it a μ₄ larger than the nuclear one.

    Base ring: generic [CommRing R] (§7c) for the identities; the sign lemma adds only an ordering. The one numeric certification is over ℝ, as it must be.

    Companion probes: computations/deltalambda/mu4_vs_nuclear_4n_force_bound_probe.py and computations/deltalambda/gap10_tjon_correlation_probe.py.

    Scope. These are theorems about the simplex bookkeeping and one certified numeric inequality. They do not show ρ_grad is wrong, they do not show the block-trace operator is right, and they do not establish that nuclear binding saturates — that is an empirical input, cited, not proved here.

        μ₄ = 0 and "no four-body force" are not the same statement #

        The file's framing above is that a large μ₄ is a simplex-basis artifact rather than four-body physics. That framing is strengthened, not merely illustrated, by the field's own parameter-free answer, and this section proves the point rather than asserting it.

        Hammer–Platter solve the four-boson Yakubovsky equations with no four-body parameter at all and report that, away from threshold, the four-body ground state is related to the deepest three-body state by a factor of 5 ([hammer2007fourbody] §III.B). So M(4) = 5·M(3) is a ratio produced by a calculation that contains no four-body input.

        Say it that way and no further. "No four-body parameter is needed to renormalise at leading order" is not "no four-body force exists": [jurgenson2009srg] puts the four-nucleon force at tens to a few hundred keV — small, not zero. And the authors themselves flag that their universality rests on the RG analysis of their ref. [12], which Yamashita et al. dispute (§I, p. 3) — though that dispute explicitly exempts the nuclear case, "⁴He atoms and nucleons (where this sensitivity is absent)", which is the case at issue here. The argument below needs only the weak reading, so it takes only the weak reading.

        universal_ratio_forces_mu4 computes what the simplex basis assigns to that calculation: μ₄ = 9·μ₂ + μ₃, which is positive and large — at the nuclear (μ₂, μ₃) = (1, 0.8128) it is 9.81, some 2.6 × the value the nuclear data themselves produce. A quantity that is large in a calculation carrying no four-body input at all is not measuring a four-body input.

        tjon_baseline_lt_four_ratio sharpens this into an exclusion: on the μ₄ = 0 baseline the ratio M(4)/M(3) is strictly below 4 for any positive μ₂, so the universal ratio 5 is outside the baseline's reachable range entirely (universal_ratio_not_in_tjon_baseline). The two notions of "no four-body parameter" — the EFT's (no new coupling in a nonlinear Yakubovsky solution) and the simplex's (μ₄ = 0 in a linear inclusion–exclusion form) — are therefore provably inequivalent, and the external result may be cited as motivation for the baseline but never as a derivation of it.

        Companion probe: computations/deltalambda/tjon_external_cross_check_probe.py, which also re-derives the AME2020 inputs from library/mass_1.mas20.txt rather than from transcribed literals.
