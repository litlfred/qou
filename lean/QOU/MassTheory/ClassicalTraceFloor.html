    The classical floor of the categorical binding trace #

    Bean qou-zrx2. Probes: computations/probes/hbar_q_order_of_binding_observables_probe.py (the discovery) and classical_floor_derivation_probe.py (the derivation).

    What this is about #

    The corpus reads nuclear binding off the signed categorical trace difference

    Δtr(Z,N) = tr_M(W_free) − tr_M(W_atom)

    at H_{3A} (binding_canonical, derive_binding_chain_cooperative). Every energy in the framework vanishes as q → 1, where the Hecke algebra degenerates to ℂ[S_n] and no deformation is left — Δ(net) = h(h−2)/2 does, and so does the Plancherel block sum. Δtr does not. Measured along q = exp η, it converges to a nonzero limit:

    A = 2  →  1/8          A = 3  →  3/32
A = 4  →  7/128        A = 5  →  15/512

    so Δtr = c₀(A) + c₁·ℏ_q + O(ℏ_q²), and at q₀ the classical part is 22.46 % of it for the deuteron, 4.88 % for ⁴He. That fraction of any binding read off this observable is present with the braiding switched off.

    This file records the arithmetic core of the derivation of c₀(A) — the part that is genuinely proved rather than computed.

    The derivation, and where it is and is not formal #

    1. Schur–Weyl at q = 1 gives tr_M(β)|_{q=1} = 2^{c(σ_β) − n}, with c(σ) the cycle count of the permutation underlying β. The Markov weights are y_λ = s_λ(1,Q)/(1+Q)^n; at Q = 1, s_λ(1,1) = dim V_λ^{GL(2)} (zero for ℓ(λ) > 2 — the corpus's row cap), and Σ_λ dim V_λ · χ^λ(σ) is the character of σ permuting the factors of (ℂ²)^{⊗n}, which is 2^{c(σ)} since σ fixes exactly the basis vectors constant on each cycle. NOT formal here. Mathlib has no Schur–Weyl duality. It enters classical_floor_of_cycle_counts as the two explicit hypotheses hSWfree and hSWatom — visible in the statement rather than hidden in a class with no instance, because it is a published theorem the corpus is entitled to assume, not an open conjecture.
    2. The cycle counts. Every letter (c, d, g) of atom_braid_word_3A has d ∈ {0, −h, −h/2} with h = q − 1/q, so at q = 1 every letter degenerates to the transposition s_g and the permutation is q-independent. Counting: c(σ_free) = 2A and c(σ_atom) = A + 1, verified for A = 2 … 20 directly from the words — no trace evaluation, so it holds far past A = 5 where the Markov trace stops being computable. NOT formal here. It is a computation on a concrete word; it enters as hfree and hmerge.
    3. The arithmetic. Given 1 and 2, c₀(A) = 2^{−A} − 2^{1−2A}. This is what the file proves.

    The split is deliberate. Assuming step 1 assumes a published theorem; assuming step 2 assumes an 18-point computation; step 3 is then not numerics at all, and stating it here is what stops the closed form from resting on the Richardson extrapolation it was originally read off.

    Why hmerge is the right way to state step 2 #

    c(σ_free) − c(σ_atom) = A − 1 says each of the A−1 inter-nucleon interfaces merges exactly one pair of cycles. That is one clean fact, and it is the same (A−1)-bond chain adjacency derive_binding_chain_cooperative sums over — arrived at from the permutation rather than from the interface list. So the floor's A-dependence is the chain structure, and phrasing the hypothesis this way makes that visible instead of tabulating two opaque counts.

    noncomputable def QOU.MassTheory.classicalTraceFloor (cFree cAtom n : ℤ) :
    ℚ

    The classical (q = 1) trace difference, as it comes out of the two cycle counts: 2^{c_free − n} − 2^{c_atom − n}.

    Integer exponents throughout — c_atom − n is negative in every case of interest, so zpow is the honest type.

      theorem QOU.MassTheory.classical_floor_of_cycle_counts {A cFree cAtom : ℤ} {trFree trAtom : ℚ} (hfree : cFree = 2 * A) (hmerge : cFree = cAtom + (A - 1)) (hSWfree : trFree = 2 ^ (cFree - 3 * A)) (hSWatom : trAtom = 2 ^ (cAtom - 3 * A)) :
      trFree - trAtom = 2 ^ (-A) - 2 ^ (1 - 2 * A)

      The floor, end to end.

      hfree and hmerge are step 2 (the cycle counts, computed from the braid words); hSWfree and hSWatom are step 1 (Schur–Weyl at q = 1, a published theorem Mathlib does not carry). Everything between them is derived.

      The conclusion is exactly the form the probes measured — 1/8 at A = 2, 7/128 at A = 4 — but here it is not extrapolated.

      The contamination dies exponentially #

      c₀(A) = 2^{−A} − 2^{1−2A} is positive and strictly below 2^{−A} for A ≥ 2 (it vanishes at A = 1, where the exponents coincide), so the classical part of Δtr is bounded by 2^{−A} while Δtr(q₀) itself grows with A. That is the formal content of the measured decay — 22.46 % at the deuteron, 10.55 % at tritium, 4.88 % at ⁴He, 2.16 % at ⁵Li, halving per nucleon.

      It matters for reading the corpus's own results: the deuteron's apparent agreement under convention A was floor-driven and does not survive the subtraction, while the ⁴He 4-body α-residual is essentially untouched by it.

      theorem QOU.MassTheory.classicalTraceFloor_pos {A : ℤ} (hA : 2 ≤ A) :
      0 < 2 ^ (-A) - 2 ^ (1 - 2 * A)

      The floor is strictly positive for A ≥ 2.

      A ≥ 2, not A ≥ 1: at A = 1 the two exponents coincide (1 − 2 = −1) and the floor is exactly 0. Every nucleus has A ≥ 2, so the bound covers the whole range of interest, but the boundary case is real and is why the hypothesis is not 1 ≤ A.
