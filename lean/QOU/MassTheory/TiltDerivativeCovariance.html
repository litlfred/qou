    The tilt derivative IS the covariance, and the vanishing mean is what makes it so #

    Bean qou-k5r5 (gap: M₁ = Cov_Planch(Σ□h, ω₃) > 0 for all n), epic qou-x631.

    What this file proves, and why it is not bookkeeping #

    The probe wlambda_covariance_epic_M1_sigmah_tilt_path.py reformulates M₁ by tilting Plancherel measure with the statistic Σ□h itself: with x a formal tilt variable,

    N(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ · fᵢ · x^{hᵢ},   D(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ · x^{hᵢ},   R(x) = N(x)/D(x)

    and R'(1) = M₁. That identity was checked numerically at every n ≤ 22. Here it is proved, for an arbitrary finite family over an arbitrary field — so it is a fact about the construction, not about the range that was sampled.

    The mathematical content is not the quotient rule. It is that the derivative of the tilted mean at x = 1 is a covariance rather than merely a weighted moment, and that this holds precisely because the tilted statistic has vanishing weighted mean (wMean s w f = 0). In the application f = ω₃ and that hypothesis is the exact fact E_Planch[ω₃] = 0 — the same fact that qou-ujil uses to eliminate limit-shape arguments. So the hypothesis of tilt_deriv_eq_wCov is the obstruction, re-used as an asset rather than suffered as one.

    Anti-vacuity #

    hf is load-bearing, not decorative: tilt_deriv_ne_wCov_of_mean_ne_zero exhibits a two-point family with wMean ≠ 0 where the conclusion fails. Without that, a reader could not tell whether the hypothesis was doing any work.

    Scope #

    This proves the reformulation, not the gap. M₁ > 0 remains OPEN — nothing here bounds R'(1) below, and the file makes no claim about the sign.

            The tilt derivative is the covariance.

            R'(1) = Cov_w(h, f), given only that f has vanishing w-weighted mean. In the application f = ω₃, h = Σ□h and w is the Plancherel weight (f^λ)², so this says M₁ is the slope at x = 1 of the Σ□h-tilted mean of ω₃ — a function that vanishes at x = 1, which is exactly why the limit-shape evaluation there is uninformative and the derivative is not.
