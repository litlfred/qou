    The 3-body cumulant sign is a convexity statement about the aggregator #

    Two assemblies have now been machine-checked to miss the measured 3-body Ursell cumulant, in opposite directions:

    • the multiplicative / Mayer assembly W(K) = 1 − ∏ f_ij gives b₃ < 0 (QOU.MassTheory.MayerThreeBodySign.b3_neg_of_le, and the same sign in QOU.MassTheory.Mu3SlotExclusion.thirdDiff_multiplicative);
    • the bond-functional additive assembly gives b₃ = 0 exactly (QOU.Mass.PairwiseCumulantVanishing.b3_eq_zero_ofBondFunctional, and the 4-body analogue QOU.MassTheory.BodyOrderCumulant.fourthDiff_pairwise);
    • AME2020 requires b₃ = +1.4262225 > 0 (QOU.MassTheory.AmeBindingNotPairwise.ame_b3_eq).

    Read as two separate failures, the natural next move is to try a third assembly. This file says that is the wrong move, because both failures are the same failure, and names the one property that decides the sign.

    The statement #

    Write the binding of a symmetric A-body system as a function of its total bond weight: B(A) = Φ(m_A · g) where m_A is the bond count and g the common per-bond weight. The deuteron carries one bond and the three-body system three, so with Φ 0 = 0 the Ursell inversion b₃ = B(3) − 3·B(2) is

    b₃ = Φ(3g) − 3·Φ(g) =: b3Agg Φ g

    which is a secant comparison on the ray through the origin, and nothing else. Jensen at the two points 0 and 3g with weights (2/3, 1/3) gives, with no further hypotheses,

    • Φ concave through the origin ⇒ b₃ ≤ 0 (b3Agg_nonpos_of_concaveOn)
    • Φ convex through the origin ⇒ b₃ ≥ 0 (b3Agg_nonneg_of_convexOn)

    so the contrapositive not_concaveOn_of_b3Agg_pos is the result the programme needs: the measured positive b₃ excludes every concave-through-the-origin aggregator, for every bond weight and every bond set.

    Why this subsumes both prior negatives #

    The additive assembly is Φ = (a • ·), the boundary case — affine is concave, so it saturates the bound at b₃ = 0 exactly (b3Agg_linear_eq_zero, which recovers b3_eq_zero_ofBondFunctional's value independently and by a different route; this is a numerical agreement, not a formal reduction of that theorem).

    The multiplicative assembly with a common bond factor is 1 − f^n in the bond count, and its cumulant factors exactly (wMult_b3_factor):

    b₃ = (1 − f³) − 3(1 − f) = −(f − 1)²·(f + 2)

    a negated perfect square times a positive factor. So on f > −2 it is ≤ 0 unconditionally and vanishes only at f = 1, i.e. only when the bonds do not couple at all (wMult_b3_eq_zero_iff). That is strictly sharper than MayerThreeBodySign.b3_neg_of_le, which needed the measured threshold f_pn ≤ 2/3 to conclude; in the equal-bond case no threshold is needed, and the (f − 1)² says the negative sign is second order in the coupling, not an artefact of where the measured f_pn happens to sit.

    Scope, and what this does NOT exclude #

    This is a scope-(b) structural exclusion in the sense of AGENTS.md §"Before declaring refuted": the invariant is Jensen's inequality, cited and proved here, and the excluded class is concave-through-the-origin aggregators of the total bond weight.

    It excludes nothing else, and in particular the convex branch is open and reachable — b3Agg_sq_pos exhibits a convex Φ with b₃ = 6 > 0. The programme's orientation therefore inverts: the aggregator must be strictly convex at small argument, i.e. binding must be superadditive in bond count rather than saturating in it. Two live constraints bound that search from the other side and are not addressed here:

    • saturation (QOU.Mass.CoordinationSaturation) needs B/A bounded, which for convex Φ through the origin means Φ x / x must be bounded — so Φ has to be strictly convex at small argument and asymptotically linear. That is a narrow but non-empty class (x²/(1+x) is in it); whether the framework's Hecke-side quantities produce such a Φ is exactly the open question.
    • the connected 3-body sign is not the only target: b₄ = +9.243387 must come out too, and b₄'s sign is not decided by concavity — √· is concave and yields a positive b₄ — so b₃ is the sharp discriminator of this family and b₄ is not. Do not read a b₄ agreement as evidence about the aggregator.

    Reference for the cumulant conventions and the AME2020 inputs: docs/coordination/2026-08-29-bk-crossing-mass-binding-arc-reassessment.md.

      The multiplicative assembly, exactly #

      With a common bond factor f, the Mayer weight of an n-bond system is 1 − f^n.
