            §1. The repair class ε = q·δ·E/4 #

            source
            theorem QOU.MassTheory.PnOffset.weightForm_barOdd {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] (q δ E : K) (hq : q ≠ 0) :
            q⁻¹ * (-q ^ 2 * δ) * E / 4 = -(q * δ * E / 4)

            Absorbing the weight makes the offset bar-odd, for every bar-even E.

            bar q = q⁻¹, bar δ = -q²·δ (PnInterfaceBarStructure, ClosedForm.flavorDelta_bar) and bar E = E by hypothesis, so the bar image of ε = q·δ·E/4 is q⁻¹·(-q²·δ)·E/4 = -ε.

            The bar images are supplied as the substituted values; the statement is the resulting identity. Note there is no hypothesis on E beyond bar-evenness having already been used to write E on both sides: the criterion of pnWeighted_barEven_iff selects nothing within this class.

            source
            theorem QOU.MassTheory.PnOffset.weightForm_cubic_factor {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] (q E : K) (hq : q ≠ 0) :
            q * flavorDelta q * E / 4 = (q - 1) ^ 3 * (q * (q + 1) * (q ^ 2 + 1) * E / (12 * q ^ 4))

            Every member of the class with E regular at q = 1 vanishes to third order there, because δ does.

            δ carries the factor (q-1)² explicitly and a further (q-1) inside 1 - q⁻⁴ = (q⁴-1)/q⁴; pulling all three out leaves a cofactor in which q = 1 may be substituted freely. Consequently a regular-E repair moves f_pn from the strict-averaged z - h/2 by O((q-1)³) — the same order as the canonical δ/4 it is meant to replace. This is what excludes the geometric-mean candidate of §3 from the class.

              §3. The second derived candidate, and why the class misses it #

              source
              theorem QOU.MassTheory.PnOffset.geomMean_sq {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] (q : K) (hq : q ≠ 0) (hq2 : q ^ 2 + 1 ≠ 0) :

It is therefore an admissible p-n factor by the criterion — a second structurally-derived candidate alongside the arithmetic mean z - h/2.

              §4. Arithmetic or geometric? The product-over-bonds argument is unsound #

              source
              theorem QOU.MassTheory.PnOffset.fPP_mul_fNN_eq {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] (q : K) (hq : q ≠ 0) (hq2 : q ^ 2 + 1 ≠ 0) :
              markovZ q * (markovZ q - heckeH q) = (q / (q ^ 2 + 1)) ^ 2

              The product of the two like-flavour factors, in closed form.

              f_pp · f_nn = (q/(q²+1))², which is (z/q²)² — consistent with geomMean_sq, and the reason the geometric mean is z/q² in the first place.

              source
              theorem QOU.MassTheory.PnOffset.one_sub_fPP_mul_fNN {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] (q : K) (hq2 : q ^ 2 + 1 ≠ 0) :
              1 - markovZ q * (markovZ q - heckeH q) = ((q ^ 2 - 1) / (2 * (q ^ 2 + 1))) ^ 2 + 3 / 4

              The product of the FACTORS is not the trace of the product of the BONDS, and misses it by at least 3/4 at every q.

              This is the statement that decides the arithmetic-vs-geometric question, so it is worth being explicit about what the two sides are.

              • The two like-flavour bonds are the Hecke elements σ (p-p, d = 0) and σ - h·1 = σ⁻¹ (n-n, d = -h) — see bondD and QOU.Hecke.ClosedForm.interfaceFactor_eq_bond_expectation. They are mutually inverse, so their product in the algebra is the identity, whose Markov-trace expectation is τ(1) = 1 (the normalisation that f_k = z + d_k already assumes).
              • Their two factors are f_pp and f_nn, and this theorem says 1 - f_pp·f_nn is a square plus 3/4. So over any ordered field f_pp·f_nn ≤ 1/4, never 1, and the two sides differ by a factor of at least four.

              Hence the Markov trace is not multiplicative on these bonds — it could not be, since a bond and its inverse would have to have reciprocal traces — and the closed form's product over bonds is a product of traces, not the trace of a product.

              The consequence for the fork. The case for the geometric mean was that the closed form is a product, so the multiplicative midpoint of two neighbouring factors is the natural interpolation. That argument takes a geometric mean at the level of factors while borrowing its motivation from a multiplicative structure that exists only at the level of elements — and the two levels are not connected here, by this theorem. At the element level the multiplicative midpoint of σ and σ⁻¹ is the identity, with trace 1, which is not the geometric mean z/q² of the factors (0.497 at the proton pin) nor anything else in the family.

              The arithmetic mean has no such gap: the Markov trace is linear, so (f_pp + f_nn)/2 is exactly the trace of (σ + σ⁻¹)/2, the skein symmetriser that QOU.BraidKnot.interface_knot_markov_z_bar_symmetric is already stated about, and arithMean_bondConst_eq_midpoint records that its bond constant is the midpoint of the other two.

              Scope. This is a statement about which of the two candidate interpolations has algebraic backing, not a derivation of the physical offset. It removes the structural argument for the geometric mean; it does not establish that ε = 0 is correct, and the colour-tube correction δ/4 sits on top of the arithmetic mean either way.
