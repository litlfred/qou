    Orbit counting for permutations: the additive core #

    Bean qou-zrx2 follow-up (5), reopened by qou-pdg9 / qou-qqm4.

    Why this file exists #

    ClassicalTraceFloor derives the classical floor c₀(A) = 2^{−A} − 2^{1−2A} of the Markov trace difference from two cycle counts of the canonical braid word,

    c(σ_free) = 2A,        c(σ_atom) = A + 1,

    and LeadingOrderTraceCoefficient derives c₁ from the same counts with one letter deleted. Both take those counts as stated hypotheses, verified by an 18-point computation rather than proved, because Mathlib has Equiv.Perm.cycleType, SameCycle and Disjoint.cycleType_mul but no orbit-count API and no lemma for how an orbit count moves when a permutation is multiplied by a transposition.

    This file supplies the API and the additive half of what those counts need.

    The right object is the DEFECT, not the count #

    The number of orbits (cycles including fixed points) of σ on a fintype α is

    orbitCount σ = (fixed points) + (number of nontrivial cycles)
             = card α − #support σ + card (cycleType σ)

    which subtracts, so it is awkward in ℕ and not additive. Its complement

    orbitDefect σ = #support σ − card (cycleType σ) = Σ_{c ∈ cycleType σ} (c − 1)

    is additive over disjoint permutations, is 0 for the identity, and is 1 for a transposition. orbitCount σ = card α − orbitDefect σ recovers the count.

    What is proved here #

    • orbitDefect_one, orbitDefect_swap, and card_cycleType_le_card_support (so the ℕ subtraction in the definition is honest, not truncating).
    • orbitDefect_mul_of_disjoint — the additive core.
    • orbitDefect_prod_of_pairwise_disjoint — additivity over a list, which is what a braid word built one disjoint nucleon block at a time needs.
    • orbitCount_prod_of_disjoint_unit_defect — the free-word half of ClassicalTraceFloor's hypotheses, provable now. The free word is a disjoint product of one identical block per nucleon, each of defect one, so on 3A strands it leaves 3A − A = 2A orbits: exactly the c(σ_free) = 2A that file assumes.
    • sign_eq_neg_one_pow_orbitDefect — the defect's parity is the sign, so multiplying by any transposition flips it: orbitDefect_swap_mul_odd.
    • sameCycle_swap_mul_iff — a transposition exactly toggles whether two points share a cycle.
    • The transposition step itself, both directions, sorry-free: orbitDefect_swap_mul_of_sameCycle (split: the defect drops by exactly one) and orbitDefect_swap_mul_of_not_sameCycle (merge: it rises by exactly one).

    The transposition step, and how it is proved #

    Multiplying by a transposition changes the orbit count by exactly one — up when the two points lay in different cycles, down when they lay in the same one. The parity comes free from the sign (orbitDefect_swap_mul_odd); the magnitude is the content, and Mathlib supplies none of it — there is no cycleType_swap_mul lemma in the library at all. It is proved here from one base case, reached twice per induction step.

    • (S-adj) orbitDefect_swap_apply_mul — the adjacent case b = f a. Split f = cycleOf f a * (f * (cycleOf f a)⁻¹) into disjoint halves (disjoint_mul_inv_of_mem_cycleFactorsFinset); the transposition cannot leave the first, so orbitDefect_mul_of_disjoint reduces to a single cycle, where Mathlib's IsCycle.swap_mul and support_swap_mul_eq give a cycle on one fewer point — or, when the cycle is that transposition, the identity.
    • (S) orbitDefect_swap_mul_of_sameCycle — the general case. b = σ^k a for a least k ≥ 1, and orbitDefect_swap_mul_aux walks that arc down to k = 1: each step peels the first letter off the front of the arc with (S-adj) and puts it back at the far end with (S-adj) again, glued by the three-cycle identity swap (σa) b · swap a (σa) = swap a (σa) · swap a b.
    • (R) sameCycle_swap_mul_of_not_sameCycle and (R2) not_sameCycle_swap_mul_of_sameCycle — the dynamics: a transposition toggles whether a and b share a cycle (sameCycle_swap_mul_iff). Both are the same walk along σ's orbit of a; (R) needs no a ≠ b at all.
    • The merge is derived from (S) and (R) rather than proved again, since swap a b is an involution.

    🛑 What this does and does not settle for ClassicalTraceFloor #

    It closes the free half outright (orbitCount_prod_of_disjoint_unit_defect), and it supplies the general step the atom half needs. It does not by itself discharge that file's stated counts: knowing that each letter moves the count by ±1 leaves the bookkeeping of which sign each letter of the canonical word carries — i.e. whether that letter's two strands already lay in one orbit. That is a statement about the specific word, not about permutations in general, and it is not in this file.

    So the general cycle-count gap those two files name is closed; the word-specific accounting is not. Do not cite this file as verifying c(σ_atom) = A + 1.

        theorem QOU.MassTheory.orbitDefect_swap_mul_odd {α : Type u_1} [Fintype α] [DecidableEq α] {σ : Equiv.Perm α} {a b : α} (h : a ≠ b) :
        ¬orbitDefect (Equiv.swap a b * σ) % 2 = orbitDefect σ % 2

        Half of the ±1 step, free. Multiplying by a transposition flips the defect's parity — so the change is odd, hence nonzero, and in particular swap a b * σ ≠ σ in defect. What this does NOT give is that the change is exactly one.

        The transposition step #

        orbitDefect_swap_mul_odd above gives the parity of the step. The magnitude |Δ| = 1 is the content, and it comes in two halves:

        • (S) the split, orbitDefect_swap_mul_of_sameCycle — within one cycle, the transposition splits it and the defect drops. Proved below via the adjacent case orbitDefect_swap_apply_mul, which is where Equiv.Perm.IsCycle.swap_mul (removing a point from a cycle leaves a cycle) does the work.
        • (R) reachability, sameCycle_swap_mul_of_not_sameCycle — joining two different cycles puts a and b in the same cycle of the product. Elementary, and the argument is short: iterating swap a b * σ from b follows σ's cycle (τ x = σ x whenever σ x ∉ {a, b}) until it reaches b's predecessor x with σ x = b, where τ x = swap a b b = a. So a is reached from b.

        The merge case is derived, not assumed — orbitDefect_swap_mul_of_not_sameCycle below is proved from (S) and (R), because swap a b is an involution: applying (S) to swap a b * σ, whose a and b are in one cycle by (R), and using swap a b * (swap a b * σ) = σ, gives the merge statement directly.

        Everything in this section is sorry-free.

        source
        theorem QOU.MassTheory.sameCycle_swap_mul_of_not_sameCycle {α : Type u_1} [Fintype α] [DecidableEq α] {σ : Equiv.Perm α} {a b : α} (hnc : ¬σ.SameCycle a b) :
        (Equiv.swap a b * σ).SameCycle a b

        (R) Joining two different cycles with a transposition puts the two points in one cycle of the product. Proved.

        Iterating τ = swap a b * σ from a agrees with σ (because τ x = σ x whenever σ x ∉ {a, b}) all the way to the first return σ^m a = a, where the swap finally fires and sends a to b. Nothing on a's orbit is b, or a and b would already share a cycle; and nothing before step m is a, by minimality.

        Note it needs no a ≠ b: at a = b the hypothesis is already false, SameCycle being reflexive.

        source
        theorem QOU.MassTheory.not_sameCycle_swap_mul_of_sameCycle {α : Type u_1} [Fintype α] [DecidableEq α] {σ : Equiv.Perm α} {a b : α} (h : a ≠ b) (hc : σ.SameCycle a b) :
        ¬(Equiv.swap a b * σ).SameCycle a b

        (R2) The converse of (R), and the separation half of the split: if a and b already share a cycle, the transposition puts them in different cycles of the product. Proved, by the same walk.

        Take k least positive with σ^k a ∈ {a, b}. If σ^k a = a then b never appears on a's orbit, contradicting the hypothesis; so σ^k a = b, and then τ = swap a b * σ follows σ up to step k−1 and closes back to a at step k — τ^k a = a, with the whole τ-orbit of a sitting inside {σ^j a : j < k}, which avoids b.

        This is the arc-separation fact any counting proof of (S) needs.

        theorem QOU.MassTheory.sameCycle_swap_mul_iff {α : Type u_1} [Fintype α] [DecidableEq α] {σ : Equiv.Perm α} {a b : α} (h : a ≠ b) :
        (Equiv.swap a b * σ).SameCycle a b ↔ ¬σ.SameCycle a b

        (R) and (R2) together: a transposition exactly toggles whether the two points share a cycle. The complete dynamical picture at {a, b}, and all the dynamical input a counting proof of (S) needs — what remains there is the counting itself.

        theorem QOU.MassTheory.orbitDefect_swap_mul_of_not_sameCycle {α : Type u_1} [Fintype α] [DecidableEq α] {σ : Equiv.Perm α} {a b : α} (h : a ≠ b) (hnc : ¬σ.SameCycle a b) :

        The merge case, DERIVED from (S) and (R). When a and b lie in different cycles of σ, those two orbits merge and the defect rises by exactly one.
