    The curvature trace is a commutator-with-ρ sum, in every irrep (qou-o6ov) #

    Context. The projector curvature Ω₂^P = (4/[2]_q²)·Σ_a [σ_a, σ_{a+1}] (#6445) is a genuine Hecke-algebra element. Every previous handle on its trace was family-specific: the hook column via the reduced Burau (#6464, #6480, #6520), the two-row column via R-matrix weight sectors (#6525). This file records the identity that is family-independent — it holds in every irrep and follows from trace cyclicity alone:

    tr (ρ · Σ_a [σ_a, σ_{a+1}]) = Σ_a tr ([ρ, σ_a] · σ_{a+1}).            (★)

    Derivation, one commutator at a time: tr(ρσ_aσ_{a+1}) − tr(ρσ_{a+1}σ_a) = tr(ρσ_aσ_{a+1}) − tr(σ_aρσ_{a+1}) = tr([ρ,σ_a]·σ_{a+1}), the middle step being cyclicity applied to ρ·σ_{a+1} and σ_a.

    Why it matters. (★) puts the decay object on [ρ, σ_a] — the arc's original single-window object — and says the whole thing is governed by how the braid word fails to commute with each generator: if ρ commuted with every σ_a, the trace would vanish identically in every irrep.

    What it does NOT give: the decay is CANCELLATION, not size (settled by the conjugate-pair control, magnus_conjugate_pair_cancellation_probe.py). (★) says what the decay object is, not why it falls with b(λ). The control is conjugate partitions: dim λ = dim λ' exactly while b flips, so a pair is two irreps of identical dimension at very different b — the dimension confound removed by a theorem rather than by normalisation. Measured at A = 2 and 3 (exact mpf), ratios being higher-b partner ÷ lower-b:

    • 9 of 9 pairs: commutator-norm ratio ≥ 1 (1.13–1.31, clustered near 1.25) while the trace ratio is 0.003–0.27. Sharpest: (8,1) vs (2,1⁷), both dimension 8 — ‖[ρ,σ_a]‖ rises 0.755 → 0.955 while |tr|/dim falls 33.65 → 0.115.

    So at equal dimension the high-b irrep has larger commutators and a far smaller trace. Consequently no bound of the form |tr| ≤ Σ_a ‖[ρ,σ_a]‖·‖σ_{a+1}‖ can produce the decay — it runs the wrong way on every pair measured. This is scope-(a): the plain norm route as-is is excluded, not every norm-flavoured argument. It also explains why the hook bound (#6520) had to go through the spectrum (e_j of the Burau moduli); that was the route available, not merely the route taken, and a general-λ bound should be spectral in the same sense.

    A raw b-scan cannot see this: the normalised norm is U-shaped in b (falls to a mid-b minimum, then rises), so a scan truncated before the tail reads as "the norm falls with b" and inverts the answer. An earlier version of this docstring asserted the conclusion from A = 2 alone, was then retracted to "open" on half-measured A = 3 data, and is now restored on the control that actually establishes it — see docs/audits/2026-09-04-magnus-b-decay-is-cancellation-not-size.md.

    Scope. Sorry-free and unconditional, over any R-algebra A with an R-linear functional tr satisfying cyclicity tr (x*y) = tr (y*x) — the hypothesis is carried explicitly, since a general linear functional is not a trace. The Hecke-specific content (the seminormal construction, the atom word) is outside Mathlib, as in the sibling files.
