      theorem QOU.MassTheory.BindingResidual.g2_affine {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (E Ep hsq : R) (hEp : Ep ≠ 0) (hh : hsq ≠ 0) :
      g2 E Ep hsq = E * (1 / (Ep * hsq)) - 1 / hsq

      g₂ is affine in the binding E: g₂ = E · (1/(Ep·hsq)) − 1/hsq. The slope is 1/(Ep·hsq); there is no higher-order dependence on E.

      theorem QOU.MassTheory.BindingResidual.g2_amplification {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (E Ep hsq δ : R) (hEp : Ep ≠ 0) (hh : hsq ≠ 0) :
      g2 (E * (1 + δ)) Ep hsq - g2 E Ep hsq = δ * E / (Ep * hsq)

      The amplification identity. A relative perturbation δ of the measured binding (E ↦ E·(1+δ)) shifts g₂ by exactly δ · E / (Ep · hsq). The coefficient E/(Ep·hsq) is the amplification factor (≈ 23.9 at q₀): the 1/h² magnifies the binding's relative error into g₂.

      theorem QOU.MassTheory.BindingResidual.r_amplification {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (E Ep hsq δ : R) (hEp : Ep ≠ 0) (hh : hsq ≠ 0) :
      g2 (E * (1 + δ)) Ep hsq - 1 - (g2 E Ep hsq - 1) = δ * E / (Ep * hsq)

      r = g₂ − 1 inherits the same amplification (subtracting the constant 1 does not change the sensitivity): the residual moves by δ·E/(Ep·hsq) under a relative binding perturbation δ. So resolving r to absolute precision η requires the binding to relative precision η·Ep·hsq/E.
