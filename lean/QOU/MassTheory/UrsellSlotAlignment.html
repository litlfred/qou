    Bond order is not particle count: the Ursell slot alignment #

    Why the triple bond difference belongs in the 3-body slot, not the 4-body one.

    The Δ_λ binding programme carries two ladders that look alike and are indexed differently, and conflating them mis-slots every comparison:

    • the moment ladder μ₂, μ₃, μ₄, … of the residual simplex, indexed by particle count — μ_k is the coefficient of C(A,k) in M(A) = ∑_k μ_k · C(A,k);
    • the mixed-difference ladder Δ¹g, Δ²g, Δ³g, … of a subset trace, indexed by bond count — Δᵐg is the m-th mixed difference over m chosen interface bonds.

    For three particles the bonds a = 0↔1, b = 1↔2, c = 0↔2 form a triangle: three bonds, but only three vertices. So Δ³g — a bond-order-3 object — is still a 3-body quantity. It is not a 4-body term, and pairing it against μ₄ is an index error. A genuine μ₄ needs a fourth particle (hence A = 4, H₁₂, and C(4,2) = 6 bonds).

    triangle_moment_decomp gives the correct assembly: the 3-body moment is the sum of all three pair differences plus the triple, not either piece alone. moment_eq_triple_iff says exactly when the shortcut of reading Δ³g as the moment is legitimate — precisely when the pair differences cancel — and moment_pos_of_pairs_dominate is the measured configuration, in which they do not: three positive pair differences outweigh a negative triple, so the assembled moment keeps the pair differences' sign.

    The consequence for the programme is a robustness result, not a new door: the sign obstruction found for the pair difference survives the slot correction. It does not survive it because the correction is small — the correction is large (it roughly doubles the magnitude) — but because the triple enters with the same sign as the assembled moment demands.

    mu_inversion_three / mu_inversion_four verify, over a generic commutative ring, that the recursive form used by the residual-simplex compute (mu3 = M₃ − 3μ₂, mu4 = M₄ − 6μ₂ − 4μ₃) is the binomial inversion of M(A) = ∑ μ_k C(A,k) at A = 2,3,4, and mu4_closed_form that it agrees with the closed alternating form M₄ − 4M₃ + 6M₂.

    Base ring: generic [CommRing R] (§7c) for the identities; the sign lemma adds only an ordering. No ℝ, no analysis — these are ring-level facts about inclusion–exclusion.

    Companion probe: computations/deltalambda/ursell_slot_alignment_probe.py.

    Scope. These are theorems about the inclusion–exclusion bookkeeping. They do not assert that the block trace g is the right operator, nor that its assembled 3-body moment matches the derived μ₃; the probe reports that comparison separately, and it misses.

          theorem QOU.MassTheory.UrsellSlotAlignment.triangle_moment_decomp {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (e a b c ab ac bc abc : R) :
          bodyMoment3 e a b c abc = pairDiff e a b ab + pairDiff e a c ac + pairDiff e b c bc + tripleDiff e a b c ab ac bc abc

          The slot-alignment identity. The 3-body moment of the triangle is the sum of the three pair differences plus the triple difference — neither piece alone. This is the statement that fixes which measured quantity is the μ₃ comparand.

          theorem QOU.MassTheory.UrsellSlotAlignment.moment_eq_triple_iff {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (e a b c ab ac bc abc : R) :
          bodyMoment3 e a b c abc = tripleDiff e a b c ab ac bc abc ↔ pairDiff e a b ab + pairDiff e a c ac + pairDiff e b c bc = 0

          When reading Δ³g as the moment is legitimate. The triple difference equals the 3-body moment exactly when the three pair differences cancel. Since they are measured strictly positive (and hence do not cancel), the shortcut is invalid for this operator.

          theorem QOU.MassTheory.UrsellSlotAlignment.moment_pos_of_triple_dominated {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] [LinearOrder R] [IsStrictOrderedRing R] {e a b c ab ac bc abc : R} (h : -(pairDiff e a b ab + pairDiff e a c ac + pairDiff e b c bc) < tripleDiff e a b c ab ac bc abc) :
          0 < bodyMoment3 e a b c abc

          The measured configuration: the sign obstruction survives the slot correction. If the triple difference exceeds minus the total of the three pair differences, the assembled 3-body moment is strictly positive. With the three pair differences measured positive (total ≈ 1.23) and the triple measured ≈ -0.33, the hypothesis holds comfortably, so the assembled moment carries the pair differences' sign — correcting the slot from Δ²g to ∑Δ²g + Δ³g cannot flip the sign verdict.

          The decomposition is load-bearing here: the conclusion is about bodyMoment3, which is only connected to the hypothesis through triangle_moment_decomp.

          theorem QOU.MassTheory.UrsellSlotAlignment.triple_must_dominate_of_moment_nonpos {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] [LinearOrder R] [IsStrictOrderedRing R] {e a b c ab ac bc abc : R} (h : bodyMoment3 e a b c abc ≤ 0) :
          tripleDiff e a b c ab ac bc abc ≤ -(pairDiff e a b ab + pairDiff e a c ac + pairDiff e b c bc)

          The contrapositive, as the programme uses it: to get a non-positive assembled moment, the triple must be at least as large in magnitude as the whole pair total. A triple that is merely negative does not suffice — which is exactly why the measured Δ³g < 0 does not open the sign door.

            theorem QOU.MassTheory.UrsellSlotAlignment.mu4_unconstrained_below_four {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (μ₂ μ₃ μ₄ μ₄' : R) {A : ℕ} (hA : A < 4) :
            Mval μ₂ μ₃ μ₄ A = Mval μ₂ μ₃ μ₄' A

            No 3-particle measurement determines μ₄. Two simplex ansätze differing only in their 4-body moment agree on M(A) for every A ≤ 3, because the C(A,4) column vanishes there. Hence nothing measured on a 3-particle cluster — at any bond order, Δ²g, Δ³g, or higher — constrains μ₄; a genuine μ₄ comparand requires A = 4.
