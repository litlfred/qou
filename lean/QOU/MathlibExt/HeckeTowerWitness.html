    The infinite Hecke tower as a MarkovTrace — a non-constant filtration with h ≠ 0 #

    Bean qou-cins (step S1 of the wiring plan qou-0fmb, epic qou-08a2).

    OcneanuTrace.MarkovTrace bundles a filtration (H, H_mono, g, ginv) with a trace (tr, z, markov). Every existing witness is degenerate in one of the two ways that matter:

    • OcneanuTrace.unitMarkovTrace u — the quadratic scalar h = u - u⁻¹ is genuinely non-zero, but the carrier is R itself, every generator is the same element, and the filtration is constant (H = ⊤).
    • SymmetricGroupTowerWitness — the filtration grows, but the generators are involutions, so h = 0: that is the q = 1 specialisation, not a Hecke tower.

    This file supplies a witness in the missing quadrant: the carrier is the presented infinite Iwahori-Hecke algebra H_∞, the generators σ₀, σ₁, … are distinct free generators, the filtration is the tower's own Algebra.adjoin filtration, and h is a free parameter of the construction, so h ≠ 0 is available.

    The construction #

    HeckeTower R h is FreeAlgebra R ℕ quotiented (via RingQuot) by the three defining relations of H_n(q) — with h = q - q⁻¹:

    quad   σ_m * σ_m = h • σ_m + 1
braid  σ_{m+1} * σ_m * σ_{m+1} = σ_m * σ_{m+1} * σ_m
far    σ_m * σ_k = σ_k * σ_m                          (k + 1 < m)

    Because they are imposed, the three relations hold by construction (twGen_quad, twGen_braid, twGen_far) rather than being hypotheses to discharge. The inverse generator is σ_m - h·1, forced by quad, so the two-sided-inverse fields are proved, not assumed.

    The trace, and what it is NOT #

    The trace is obtained by collapsing every generator onto the single generator of the rank-2 algebra HeckeRankTwoMarkov.Hecke2 R h and composing with its Ocneanu trace. The Markov axioms then hold exactly when z is a root of the Hecke quadratic z² = h·z + 1 (ocneanuTrace_markov_gen) — the same quadratic that HeckeRankTwoMarkov.ocneanuTrace_mul_defect identifies. For h = q - q⁻¹ the root is z = q.

    🛑 This trace is NOT the Ocneanu trace of H_∞(q), and is not faithful. It factors through a rank-2 commutative quotient, so it cannot separate elements of the tower. Constructing the faithful Markov trace on H_∞(q) is Jones' theorem and remains open here (bean qou-g8i1, step S2). Nothing below claims otherwise: MarkovTrace never requires faithfulness, and the theorems that consume a MarkovTrace's filtration (TowerSpanReduction, DisjointBlockTraceLaw) do not touch the trace at all.

    What is proved about the filtration, and what is not #

    Proved. twH_zero_lt_one : H 0 < H 1 whenever R is non-trivial. This is the structural separation from unitMarkovTrace: the filtration is not constant, and in particular not ⊤. The proof is by the collapse map — if H 0 = H 1 then σ₀ is a scalar, hence σ is a scalar in Hecke2, hence 0 = 1 in R.

    🛑 NOT proved. H n < H (n+1) for n ≥ 1. The collapse map sends every generator to the same element, so it cannot separate H 1 from H 2. Do not read twH_mono as strict growth at every level — it is ≤.

    And the obvious repair does not work — this is proved below, not merely suspected. The corpus does hold a rank-6 separating candidate with its basis established (QOU.SchurWeyl.sixBasis, QOU.SchurWeyl.sixWords_indep), so it is tempting to map the tower there and send the tail g m, m ≥ 2, to a central scalar. scalar_tail_forces_central shows that any such map forces the adjacent generator to be central, which σ₁ is not — and tail_scalar_is_automatically_a_unit closes the one apparent escape, so the whole "tail-to-scalars" family is excluded, not just one member. What strict growth actually needs is the Iwahori-Hecke coset basis, or the direct limit of SchurWeyl.HeckeInclusion.heckeIncl with injectivity. The upper half of that basis theorem is in the corpus (finrank_le_factorial, bean qou-uoal); the n! lower bound is the open half. Tracked as bean qou-p8yg.

                      🛑 Why the cheap finite separator cannot give strict growth #

                      The obvious route to H 1 < H 2 is to map the tower into a small algebra in which g 0 and g 1 stay independent — the corpus has a rank-6 candidate with its basis proved (QOU.SchurWeyl.sixBasis, QOU.SchurWeyl.sixWords_indep) — sending the tail g m, m ≥ 2, to a central scalar so that far is satisfied for free.

                      That route is structurally blocked, and the obstruction is the braid relation rather than anything about the target. The three results below are the proof.

                      The same genre of argument — braid plus a degeneracy hypothesis forcing the generators to collapse — appears in QOU.Eightfold.braid_forces_eq (for a commutative image) and in braid_forces_eq_of_commuting_involutions of open PR #7153, which rules out the involution witness. That second name is not yet on main; it is cited here as forthcoming sibling work, not as a corpus declaration, and will not resolve until #7153 lands.

                      The moral for the next attempt: a tail assignment checked only at the boundary (far, and braid at the bottom index) looks complete and is not — the interior braid relation, between the last non-scalar generator and the first scalar one, is what fails.

                      🛑 No finite stage receives the tower at all #

                      The two results above exclude sending the tail to scalars. The question they leave open — bean qou-p8yg — is whether any assignment of the tail into a finite H_n(q) exists. It does not, and the reason is one identity.

                      braid_central_forces_eq says that braiding with a central element forces equality. Centrality therefore does not stay put: it propagates down the tower, collapsing each generator onto the one above it (central_forces_below, central_tail_collapses_all).

                      That settles the question. In any finite target the far relations force every sufficiently high generator to commute with all the low ones — i.e. to be central — and the collapse then drags every generator onto that one element. So no such map separates g 0 from g 1, whatever the target, and strict growth of the filtration cannot be obtained this way at all. It has to come from the Iwahori-Hecke coset basis, or from the direct limit of SchurWeyl.HeckeInclusion.heckeIncl with injectivity.

                      These results subsume scalar_tail_forces_central above, which is the special case where the central element is a scalar — and are strictly stronger, concluding x = y rather than merely that x is central. That theorem is kept because it is cited.

                      The answer to qou-p8yg's open question: no tail assignment exists.

                      If any generator of the tower maps to a central element, every generator below it maps to that same element. In a finite target the far relations make the high generators central, so the entire image collapses to one element and g 0 cannot be separated from g 1.
