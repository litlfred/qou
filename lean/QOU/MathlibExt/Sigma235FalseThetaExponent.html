    def QOU.MathlibExt.Sigma235Support (n : ℤ) :
    Prop

    The eight residues mod 60 on which the Σ(2,3,5) character χ is nonzero: {1, 11, 19, 29} where χ = +1, and their partners 60 − r where χ = −1.

      theorem QOU.MathlibExt.sigma235_sq_eq_120_mul_add_one (n : ℤ) (h : Sigma235Support n) :
      ∃ (m : ℤ), n ^ 2 = 120 * m + 1

      The exponent is integral. On the support of χ, n² is 1 more than a multiple of 120; equivalently (n²−1)/120 ∈ ℤ.

      This is the fact that makes the Jacoby–Lawrence normalisation q^{(n²−1)/120} a genuine power series, and pins the discrepancy with q^{n²/120} to the single constant q^{1/120} rather than to any n-dependent difference.

      theorem QOU.MathlibExt.sigma235_exponent_low :
      (1 ^ 2 - 1) / 120 = 0 ∧ (11 ^ 2 - 1) / 120 = 1 ∧ (19 ^ 2 - 1) / 120 = 3 ∧ (29 ^ 2 - 1) / 120 = 7

      The four exponents Jacoby–Lawrence's series actually carries at the four positive support residues: 0, 1, 3, 7. These are the numbers a reader can check against p. 55, and they are integers exactly because of sigma235_sq_eq_120_mul_add_one.

      theorem QOU.MathlibExt.sigma235_lz_exponent_not_integral :
      ¬120 ∣ 1 ^ 2

      The n²/120 normalisation is not integral: at n = 1 the exponent numerator n² leaves remainder 1 on division by 120. Stated as the sharp negative so the two normalisations cannot be silently identified.
