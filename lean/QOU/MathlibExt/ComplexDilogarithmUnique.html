    cLiTwoExt is the analytic continuation — uniqueness #

    Bean qou-clx8. ComplexDilogarithmDeriv.lean proved cLiTwoExt analytic on ℂ ∖ [1,∞), and cLiTwoExt_eq_cLiTwo had already given agreement with the series on the open disc. Together those are what "analytic continuation" means — but the bean's own phrasing asks for more:

    cLiTwoExt(y₁) is not identified with the Li₂(y₁) the sum rule refers to

    which is uniqueness: any analytic g on ℂ ∖ [1,∞) agreeing with the series on the disc is cLiTwoExt. That is the identity theorem, and it needs the domain to be preconnected. This file supplies that and draws the conclusion.

    The Mathlib gap that was actually missing #

    Mathlib has all three moving parts —

    • Complex.starConvex_one_slitPlane (ℂ ∖ (−∞,0] is star-convex about 1)
    • StarConvex.isPathConnected
    • AnalyticOnNhd.eqOn_of_preconnected_of_frequently_eq (the identity theorem)

    — but not the star-convexity of ℂ ∖ [1,∞), which is the set this development lives on. starConvex_zero_notCut below fills it: ℂ ∖ [1,∞) is star-convex about 0, for the reason the picture suggests — if t·w lies on the cut for some t ∈ (0,1], then t·w is real and ≥ 1, so w is real and w = (t·w)/t ≥ 1/t ≥ 1, i.e. w was on the cut already.

    notCut_eq_preimage_slitPlane records the relation to Mathlib's set: ℂ ∖ [1,∞) = (1 − ·)⁻¹(ℂ ∖ (−∞,0]), so the two are exchanged by w ↦ 1 − w, and the star centres 0 and 1 correspond. The direct proof is shorter than transporting star-convexity across that map, so the transport is recorded as an equation rather than used.

    What this closes #

    eqOn_cLiTwoExt_of_eqOn_ball : any analytic `g` on `ℂ ∖ [1,∞)` agreeing with
                              `cLiTwo` on the disc equals `cLiTwoExt` there

    So cLiTwoExt is not merely an analytic function extending the series — it is the one, and cLiTwoExt y₁ at ‖y₁‖ = 1.52 is the value the sum rule's Li₂(y₁) refers to, with no choice left open.

    The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms. qou-clx8 is explicit that closing it does not move the sum rule; Route 5 is a restatement, and even the whole chain leaves an unevaluated real integral.

    Uniqueness of the continuation #

    source
    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.eqOn_cLiTwoExt_of_eqOn_ball {g : ℂ → ℂ} (hg : AnalyticOnNhd ℂ g notCut) (hagree : ∀ (z : ℂ), ‖z‖ < 1 → g z = cLiTwo z) :
    Set.EqOn g cLiTwoExt notCut

    cLiTwoExt is the unique analytic extension of the series.

    If g is analytic on ℂ ∖ [1,∞) and agrees with cLiTwo on the open unit disc, then g = cLiTwoExt on all of ℂ ∖ [1,∞).

    The identity theorem, with z₀ = 0: agreement on a whole disc is far more than the accumulation it needs.

    source
    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.eqOn_cLiTwoExt_hypotheses_satisfiable :
    AnalyticOnNhd ℂ cLiTwoExt notCut ∧ ∀ (z : ℂ), ‖z‖ < 1 → cLiTwoExt z = cLiTwo z

    Non-vacuity. eqOn_cLiTwoExt_of_eqOn_ball is universally quantified over g, and a uniqueness theorem whose hypotheses nothing satisfies proves nothing. They are satisfiable: cLiTwoExt itself meets them.

    The check with real bite is numerical and lives in Route 23 G1: mpmath's polylog(2, ·) is an independently implemented analytic continuation (functional equations and series acceleration, nothing to do with this integral), so uniqueness predicts it must agree with cLiTwoExt everywhere off the cut. It does — to 30 dps at ‖y‖ = 40, far outside the disc where the series diverges.
