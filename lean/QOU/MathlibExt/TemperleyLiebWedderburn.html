    Wedderburn decompositions of TL₂(δ) and TL₃(δ) #

    Bean qou-cds1. QOU.MathlibExt.HeckeToTemperleyLieb proved TL_n(δ) ≅ H_n(v²)/⟨relators⟩ and left exactly one thing open:

    ⟨ U_i U_j U_i − v²·U_i ⟩  =  traceRadical tr_M

    whose hard half — that the trace sees everything outside the ideal — needs TL_n(δ) semisimple. QOU.MathlibExt.TraceRadicalNondegenerate proved everything downstream of a Wedderburn decomposition, as a theorem about whatever satisfies the hypothesis; nothing was known to satisfy it.

    This file supplies the decomposition itself at n = 2 and n = 3:

    TL₂(δ) ≃ₐ[R] R × R                                 (δ a unit)
TL₃(δ) ≃ₐ[R] Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) R × R          (δ and δ² − 1 units)

    so at those two levels the hypothesis is discharged rather than assumed, and tl3_traceRadical reads the open item's conclusion off it: the weighted trace on TL₃(δ) has zero radical.

    Where the maps come from — nothing new is constructed #

    Both are representations the corpus already had. tl2Rep is in TemperleyLiebAlgebra; tl3Rep5 is in TemperleyLiebSpanning, built there to prove linear independence. What is new is that each is bijective, and the proof of that is the same three steps twice:

    1. the images of the basis are linearly independent — already the content of the independence lemma, refactored out;
    2. they span, which is where the unit hypotheses enter and is the only new computation;
    3. so they are a basis of the target, and a linear map sending a basis to a basis is an isomorphism (Module.Basis.equiv plus Module.Basis.ext).

    Why δ² − 1 must be a unit and not merely a non-zero-divisor #

    TemperleyLiebSpanning.tl3Basis needs δ² − 1 only to be a non-zero-divisor — enough for injectivity. Surjectivity is strictly stronger over a ring: an injective endomorphism of a free module of finite rank need not be onto (×2 : ℤ → ℤ). The determinant of the five images against the standard basis of Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) R × R is

    det = (δ² − 1)²

    so the map is onto exactly when δ² − 1 is invertible. That is not an artefact of the chosen basis: at δ = ±1 the algebra genuinely degenerates.

    The inverse is polynomial in c = δ⁻¹ and w = (δ² − 1)⁻¹, both carried as primitive data with polynomial hypotheses c * δ = 1 and w * (δ² − 1) = 1. So §7c is satisfied for free — nothing here is over ℝ, nothing needs an ordering, and no square root or division appears.

    At the substrate δ = q + q⁻¹ ≈ 2.01 with q ≈ 1.11, so δ² − 1 ≈ 3.05; both hypotheses hold with room.

    What this does NOT do #

    n = 2 and n = 3 are not n. The decompositions are exhibited by hand, one map each, and give no general theorem — TL_n(δ) is still not proved semisimple, and the ideal equality above is still open for n ≥ 4. General n needs the planar diagram basis, which is what everything in this arc keeps arriving at from a different side.

    -- Ref: [jones1985] https://doi.org/10.2307/1971403
