    Re Li₂ is continuous across the cut (1,∞) — Route 36 G11, closed #

    Bean qou-glbl, obligation (2). envelope_integrate wants ContinuousOn S (Icc 0 π) — the closed interval — and at φ = π two of the three Li₂ arguments land exactly on the cut, at w₀ = 1 + 2cos(2π/7) = 2.2469796. So the transport needs Li₂ continuous at a point of its own cut.

    continuousAt_re_cLiTwoExt (hx : 1 < x) :
  ContinuousAt (fun w : ℂ => (cLiTwoExt w).re) (x : ℂ)

    ⚠ The recorded next step was not merely unpriced — it could not work #

    G11 left the residue as "dominated convergence alone — a uniform integrable bound for ‖log(1−wt)/t‖ near w₀". No such bound exists. The integrand's logarithmic singularity sits at t = 1/w and moves with w: for every t near 1/w₀ the point w = 1/t is real, lies in the approach set, and makes |log(1−wt)| infinite. So the pointwise supremum over any neighbourhood is +∞ at a whole interval of t, and a w-independent dominating function is not available at any strength. Vitali / uniform integrability would do it; Mathlib does not have it in a usable shape.

    Route 37 G11-successor records this. It is the difference between "not yet priced" and "priced wrong": the previous revision named a technique, and the technique was the thing that was false.

    What replaces it, and why it is cheap #

    The inversion formula on ℂ ∖ [0,∞):

    Li₂(w) + Li₂(1/w) + ½·log(−w)² = −π²/6

    Read as Li₂(w) = −π²/6 − Li₂(1/w) − ½·log(−w)², every term on the right is harmless at w₀:

    • 1/w₀ = 0.445 is an interior point of ℂ ∖ [1,∞), where cLiTwoExt is already known analytic (analyticOnNhd_cLiTwoExt), hence continuous;
    • log(−w)² is elementary, and its only discontinuity at w₀ is in the imaginary part.

    envelopeS takes .re of every term, and Re log(−w)² is continuous across the positive reals — arg(−w) jumps by 2π there but

    Re(log(−w)²) = (log‖w‖)² − (|arg w| − π)²                 `re_log_neg_sq`

    and |arg w| does not jump. That single rewrite is what carries the theorem.

    The real-part restriction is not a convenience. cLiTwoExt jumps by 2πi·log x across the cut (Route 37 G4 measures it, ≈ 9.45 at x = 4.5), so a statement about cLiTwoExt unqualified would be false.

    ⚠ Which boundary value Lean's branch computes #

    Lean's Complex.log takes arg = +π on the negative reals, and for real x > 1 the integrand's 1 − x·t is a negative real once t > 1/x. So cLiTwoExt x is the boundary value from below, Li₂(x − i0), with Im = −π·log x. Route 37 G3 pins this against the independently known jump Li₂(x+i0) − Li₂(x−i0) = 2πi·log x; getting it backwards flips the sign of the log(−w)² correction while leaving every other check self-consistent.

    Route 37, 11 gates, all pass. G0 is a regression gate on the quadrature split (the reference integral is wrong without it, and this lane has shipped a partial version of that fix before). G4 and G8 are gated on the ratio gap/ε over six decades, not on a small residual at the finest sample.

    The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms.

      source
      theorem QOU.MathlibExt.log_one_sub_inv_add_log_neg {w : ℂ} (hw : w ∈ invDomain) :
      Complex.log (1 - w⁻¹) + Complex.log (-w) = Complex.log (1 - w)

      The additive law for the two logarithms in the inversion derivative.

      (1 − 1/w)·(−w) = 1 − w is algebra; that the principal logarithms add is not. It needs arg (1 − 1/w) + arg (−w) ∈ (−π, π], and the two arguments have strictly opposite signs throughout ℂ ∖ [0,∞) — Im (1 − 1/w) = Im w/‖w‖² against Im (−w) = −Im w — so the sum is trapped between them.

      ⚠ The hypothesis is load-bearing. On (0,1) both arguments are +π and the sum is 2π: Route 37 G5 checks that the law fails there, so dropping invDomain gives a false lemma rather than a harder one.
