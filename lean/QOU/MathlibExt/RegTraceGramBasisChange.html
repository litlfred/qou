    QOU.MathlibExt.RegTraceGramBasisChange — transporting the Gram determinant between bases #

    Bean qou-kwoa, the determinant's value: the transport step.

    RegTraceGramProduct computes the Gram determinant of a semisimple algebra in a product basis, deriving the integer constant ∏_i d_i^(d_i²). That left one gap, stated there and in PR #6552 as the thing not claimed: a Hecke algebra's Gram is taken in its T_w basis, and the two bases differ. A Gram matrix transforms as Pᵀ G P, so the determinant picks up (det P)² — the constant was pinned only up to a square.

    This file closes the transport in general. It does not evaluate det P for the Hecke case; see What is still NOT claimed.

    Contents #

    • bilin_toMatrix_det_basis_change — the square. Gram determinants in two bases of the same space differ by exactly (det (b.toMatrix c))². Holds for any bilinear form; nothing about the regular trace enters.
    • bilin_toMatrix_det_reindex — relabelling a basis does not move the determinant, because it permutes rows and columns by the same permutation. This is what lets a product basis indexed by Σ λ, κ λ be compared with a T_w basis indexed by permutations.
    • regTraceGram_toMatrix_map — pulling a basis back along an algebra equivalence leaves the regular-trace Gram matrix equal, not merely congruent. The regular trace form is an algebra invariant (regTraceForm_algEquiv, #6517), so the transported Gram is the same matrix and contributes no factor at all.
    • regTraceGram_det_eq_sq_mul_prod_matrix — the three composed: for any algebra carrying a Wedderburn isomorphism to a product of matrix algebras, and any basis of it, the Gram determinant is a square times the derived integer constant.

    Why the square cannot be removed, and what that means for the constant #

    (det P)² is not an artifact of a clumsy route — a Gram determinant is a genuine invariant only up to squares of units, because congruence G ↦ PᵀGP is the transformation law of a bilinear form. So no choice of argument makes a basis-independent integer appear. What regTraceGram_det_eq_sq_mul_prod_matrix says is the sharp version: the constant ∏_i d_i^(d_i²) is exactly the square class of the Gram determinant, and the T_w basis contributes the representative.

    Two consequences worth keeping straight:

    • Non-vanishing transports for free, since a square is nonzero iff its root is. That is consistent with RegTraceGramNondegenerate, which reaches det(twGram) ≠ 0 without any of this — and is a check on it.
    • The integer value does not transport for free. Pinning it needs det P for the change of basis from T_w to the pulled-back matrix-unit basis, which is a statement about the Wedderburn isomorphism's matrix and is not computed here.

    What is still NOT claimed #

    det P is not evaluated, for the Hecke case or any other. In particular this does not say the Hecke Gram determinant equals ∏_λ d_λ^(d_λ²) — it says the two differ by a square, which is the correct statement and is weaker. Nothing here touches the q-part, where the Schur elements live, so this is not a proof of Geck–Pfeiffer 7.2.6.

    A Gram determinant is basis-dependent exactly up to a square.

    BilinForm.toMatrix_mul_basis_toMatrix gives the congruence Pᵀ G P; taking determinants turns the two copies of P into the square. This is the reason a Gram determinant pins an integer only up to (det P)², and it holds for every bilinear form — no algebra structure and no trace is involved.

    Relabelling a basis leaves the Gram determinant alone.

    Reindexing permutes rows and columns by the same map, so the determinant is untouched — in contrast to a genuine change of basis, which contributes the square above. This is what makes it legitimate to compare a product basis indexed by Σ λ, κ λ with a T_w basis indexed by permutations.

    IsUnit (b.toMatrix ⇑c).det

    A change-of-basis determinant is a unit.

    b.toMatrix c and c.toMatrix b are mutually inverse, so their determinants multiply to 1. This is what upgrades the square in bilin_toMatrix_det_basis_change from "some factor" to "a factor that cannot vanish" — without it the transport says nothing about non-vanishing.

    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.regTraceGram_toMatrix_map {K : Type u_1} {A : Type u_2} {B : Type u_3} [Field K] [Ring A] [Ring B] [Algebra K A] [Algebra K B] [FiniteDimensional K A] [FiniteDimensional K B] {ι : Type u_4} [Fintype ι] [DecidableEq ι] (e : A ≃ₐ[K] B) (b : Module.Basis ι K B) :
    (BilinForm.toMatrix (b.map e.symm.toLinearEquiv)) (regTraceBilin K A) = (BilinForm.toMatrix b) (regTraceBilin K B)

    Pulling a basis back along an algebra equivalence leaves the regular-trace Gram matrix literally equal — not merely congruent to it.

    The regular trace form is an algebra invariant (regTraceForm_algEquiv), so transporting the basis along e.symm transports the form with it and the matrix of entries is unchanged. This is why the Wedderburn isomorphism contributes no factor to the determinant: the only square in the whole transport comes from the change of basis within the algebra.

    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.regTraceGram_det_eq_sq_mul_prod_matrix {K : Type u_1} {A : Type u_2} [Field K] [Ring A] [Algebra K A] [FiniteDimensional K A] {ι : Type u_3} [Fintype ι] [DecidableEq ι] {ι' : Type u_4} [Fintype ι'] [DecidableEq ι'] {J : ι' → Type u_5} [(i : ι') → Fintype (J i)] [(i : ι') → DecidableEq (J i)] (E : A ≃ₐ[K] (i : ι') → Matrix (J i) (J i) K) (c : Module.Basis ι K A) (f : (i : ι') × J i × J i ≃ ι) :
    ((BilinForm.toMatrix c) (regTraceBilin K A)).det = ((((Pi.basis fun (i : ι') => Matrix.stdBasis K (J i) (J i)).map E.symm.toLinearEquiv).reindex f).toMatrix ⇑c).det ^ 2 * ∏ i : ι', ↑↑(Equiv.Perm.sign (Equiv.prodComm (J i) (J i))) * ↑(Fintype.card (J i)) ^ (Fintype.card (J i) * Fintype.card (J i))

    The transport, composed: any Gram determinant of a Wedderburn-decomposable algebra is a square times the derived integer constant.

    Given a Wedderburn isomorphism E to a product of matrix algebras and any basis c of the algebra, the Gram determinant of the regular trace form in c is

    (det P)² · ∏ᵢ ± d_i^(d_i²)

    where P is the change of basis from the pulled-back matrix-unit basis to c. The product is regTraceGram_pi_matrix_det from RegTraceGramProduct; the square is bilin_toMatrix_det_basis_change; the Wedderburn isomorphism itself contributes nothing, by regTraceGram_toMatrix_map.

    Specialised to a Hecke algebra with c = basisTwOfField, the left side is (twGram …).det. det P is not evaluated — see the module docstring.
