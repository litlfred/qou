    Block structure of a normal-form word — and a cost of the (I,J) encoding #

    Bean qou-cds1. TemperleyLiebClosure proved the mechanism behind the measured δ rule:

    e_k · (pre ++ k :: post)  =  δ · (pre ++ k :: post)      when pre is far from k

    and left one thing to do — instantiate it at the normal-form index, i.e. derive "pre is far from k" from the measured condition k ∈ I ∧ k-1 ∉ I.

    This file takes the list-level steps that instantiation needs, and records a gap that attempting it exposed.

    🛑 The gap, which is a cost of a choice made two tranches back #

    NormalIdx states domination as a threshold count

    ∀ x,  |I ∩ [0,x]|  ≤  |J ∩ [0,x]|

    rather than pointwise j_r ≤ i_r. That was deliberate and is documented in TemperleyLiebNormalForm: the threshold form needs no sorting, is decidable, and is what makes Fintype.card fall to decide — seven cardinalities came out of it, up to catalan 7 = 429.

    The cost was not priced. descRun_eq_cons — that a block's word begins with its top index — needs j_r ≤ i_r, the pointwise form. The two are equivalent (checked for every pair at m ≤ 7 before the encoding was adopted), but that equivalence is not proved in Lean, and the word construction cannot proceed without the direction threshold ⟹ pointwise.

    So the encoding that made the count cheap makes the word expensive, and the missing bridge is a theorem, not a definition unfolding. Recorded because the trade was made knowingly in one direction and its other side only appeared when the word was needed.

    The bridge to prove: for equal-size I, J, the threshold condition gives |J ∩ [0, i_r]| ≥ r+1 (since |I ∩ [0, i_r]| = r+1), hence j_r ≤ i_r.

    What is proved here #

    • mem_elems — elems lists exactly the members.
    • elems_sorted — and lists them strictly increasing, inherited from List.finRange by Sublist. No sorting algorithm and no sortedness proof of its own, which is the point of defining elems by filter.
    • descRun_le — every letter of a block is at most its top index. This is the half of the block structure the δ rule actually consumes: it is what makes an earlier block "far from k" once its top is known to be ≤ k-2.
    • descRun_eq_cons — a block's word begins with its top index, given j ≤ i.

    descRun_le is independent of the gap above. descRun_eq_cons takes j ≤ i as a hypothesis, so it is usable the moment the bridge lands, and is not blocked on it.

    -- Ref: [jones1985] https://doi.org/10.2307/1971403

    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.TemperleyLiebWordBlocks.descRun_le {m : ℕ} {i j t : Fin m} (h : t ∈ TemperleyLiebNormalForm.descRun i j) :
    t ≤ i

    Every letter of a block is at most the block's top index.

    This is the half of the block structure the δ rule consumes: an earlier block whose top is ≤ k-2 has all its letters ≤ k-2, hence all far from k.

    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.TemperleyLiebWordBlocks.descRun_eq_cons {m : ℕ} {i j : Fin m} (h : j ≤ i) :
    ∃ (rest : List (Fin m)), TemperleyLiebNormalForm.descRun i j = i :: rest

    A block's word begins with its top index.

    Takes j ≤ i as a hypothesis rather than deriving it, so it is usable the moment the threshold-to-pointwise bridge lands and is not blocked on it.
