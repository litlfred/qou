    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.integral_cexp_two_pi_sq_shift (s : ℝ) :
    ∫ (v : ℝ), Complex.exp (-(2 * ↑Real.pi) * (↑v + ↑s) ^ 2) = ↑(√2)⁻¹

    ∫_ℝ e^{−2π(v+s)²} dv = 2^{−1/2}, for every real shift s.

    The complete Gaussian at covariance 2π is 2^{−1/2} and translation does not move it. Independence of s is the structural fact behind the v-integrals in Step 4: it is why the n transverse directions contribute a bare 2^{−n/2} and leave the u-integral untouched.

    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.integral_cexp_neg_pi_mul_div_two {A : ℂ} (hA : 0 < A.re) :
    ∫ (u : ℝ), Complex.exp (-(↑Real.pi * A / 2) * ↑u ^ 2) = (2 / A) ^ (1 / 2)

    ∫_ℝ e^{−πAu²/2} du = (2/A)^{1/2} for any complex A with Re A > 0.

    Re A = c+d ≥ 1 on the Whitehead chains, so the hypothesis is never binding there; it is exactly the condition that makes the Gaussian converge.

    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.integral_vdv_step4 (n : ℕ) {A : ℂ} (hA : 0 < A.re) :
    ∫ (u : ℝ) (v : Fin n → ℝ), Complex.exp (-↑Real.pi * (A * ↑u ^ 2 / 2 + 2 * ∑ j : Fin n, (↑(v j) + ↑u / 2) ^ 2)) = ↑(√2)⁻¹ ^ n * (2 / A) ^ (1 / 2)

    The Step-4 Gaussian: 2^{−n/2}·(2/A)^{1/2}, for every n and every complex A with Re A > 0.

    Stated as the iterated integral (v first, then u) — see the file header on what that does and does not claim. Note there is no rank restriction and no erfc: with the signs of the Taylor expansion restored, the transverse integrals are complete Gaussians and the whole thing is elementary.
