    Compact Quantum Dilogarithm — formal power series backbone #

    The compact quantum dilogarithm (Faddeev–Kashaev) is the reciprocal infinite product $$\Phi_q(x) \;=\; \prod_{n=0}^{\infty} \bigl(1 + q^{2n+1}\,x\bigr)^{-1}.$$

    This file develops the algebraic (formal power series) properties first, deliberately deferring the analytic/convergence layer. The core object is the finite denominator product $$P_N(q, x) \;=\; \prod_{n=0}^{N-1} \bigl(1 + q^{2n+1}\,x\bigr),$$ the finite q-Pochhammer symbol $(-qx; q^2)_N$, of which $\Phi_q(x)$ is the reciprocal of the $N \to \infty$ limit.

    Everything here is stated over a generic [CommRing R] (AGENTS.md §7c: pure algebra, no archimedean structure). Because PowerSeries S is itself a CommRing, every lemma specialises to the formal-power-series statement by taking R := PowerSeries S and x : PowerSeries S — no dedicated PowerSeries infinite-product machinery is needed for the finite-truncation algebra. In particular the functional equation qPochProd_functional is the discrete precursor of the q-difference equation $$\Phi_q(q^2 x) \;=\; (1 + q x)\,\Phi_q(x)$$ that pins the quantum dilogarithm uniquely.

    Consumers #

    • QOU.NeutronProtonSplitting — the hard-coded quarkPhi "volumes" are evaluations of $\Phi_q$ at the up/down quark orderings; this backbone lets them be defined rather than pinned as literals.
    • QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolumeIndependence — the hyperbolic volumes enter through the Rogers/Faddeev dilogarithm, whose finite q-analogue is $P_N$.

      Index additivity (cocycle identity). Splitting the product after the first M factors and re-indexing the tail rescales the argument by q^{2M}: $$P_{M+N}(q, x) \;=\; P_M(q, x)\,\cdot\,P_N(q,\; q^{2M} x).$$ This is the finite backbone from which both the functional equation (M = 1, below) and — in the limit — the multiplicative cocycle of $\Phi_q$ under the q-shift descend.

      @[simp]
      theorem QOU.MathlibExt.QuantumDilogarithm.qPochProd_q_zero {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (x : R) (N : ℕ) :
      qPochProd 0 x N = 1

      At q = 0 every factor collapses to 1 — since 2n+1 ≥ 1 we have 0 ^ (2n+1) = 0, so 1 + 0 ^ (2n+1) * x = 1 for every n (the first factor included) — hence $P_N(0, x) = 1$ for all N (the classical, undeformed truncation is trivial).

      Infinite limit — realized in QuantumDilogarithmInfinite (bi-formal R[[q]][[x]]) #

      The genuine compact quantum dilogarithm is the reciprocal of the N → ∞ limit, $$\Phi_q(x) \;=\; \Bigl(\lim_{N\to\infty} P_N(q, x)\Bigr)^{-1},$$ which is not formalised here (this file is the finite-truncation algebra). It is now formalised in the sibling module QOU.MathlibExt.QuantumDilogarithmInfinite via the bi-formal R[[q]][[x]] route (target T1): both q and x are treated as formal variables, so the object is defined by its q-difference coefficient recurrence and the functional equation Φ_q(q²x)⁻¹ = (1 + q x)·Φ_q(x)⁻¹ is proved sorry-free — sidestepping the analytic obstacles noted below (which is why the analytic route was deferred):

      1. Convergence is genuinely analytic. The coefficient of x^k in P_N is the elementary symmetric polynomial e_k(q, q^3, …, q^{2N-1}), which grows with N; it stabilises only after summing the geometric q-series, so the limit lives in a completed ring (X-adic PowerSeries, or |q| < 1 analytic), not in the finite-truncation algebra above. Formalising it needs Multipliable in the X-adic topology (Mathlib RingTheory.PowerSeries.Inverse + a completion argument), which is the topological-ring layer this tranche defers.
      2. The q-shift cocycle survives to the limit — qPochProd_add is the finite backbone; the limiting functional equation Φ_q(q² x) = (1 + q x)·Φ_q(x) is its shadow.
