    QOU.MathlibExt.TwistedQuantumDimension — the quantum dimension the ribbon literature means #

    QOU.MathlibExt.qDimWith is the twistless trace η ≫ β ≫ ε. The classical quantum dimension of a ribbon category is not that: it carries the twist,

    tr_q(f) = ε ∘ β ∘ ((θ_X ∘ f) ⊗ id) ∘ η

    and qDimWith is exactly its θ = 𝟙 specialisation. That specialisation is correct in the symmetric case and nowhere else, which is why ribbon ⇒ spherical — a theorem about the twisted trace — cannot be stated against qDimWith.

    This file supplies the twisted version, qDimTwistedWith, together with the two structural facts qDimWith already has. The interesting outcome is where the ribbon axiom is and is not needed:

    • qDimTwistedWith_congr_dual — independence of the choice of dual holds on a balanced category alone. The twist rides on A, not on the dual B, so it never meets the comparison map; one interchange step is the whole difference from the twistless proof.
    • qDimTwistedWith_rightDual — conjugation symmetry dim(Xᘁ) = dim(X) does need the ribbon axiom, and needs it exactly once. RibbonCategory.twist_dual converts θ_{Xᘁ} into (θ_X)ᘁ, and rightAdjointMate_comp_evaluation at f = θ_X then carries it across the evaluation.

    So moving from the twistless dimension to the twisted one relocates conjugation symmetry from "free" to "one axiom you already have", and costs nothing else.

    A display hazard, recorded because it cost real time #

    ᘁ is overloaded: postfix on an object it is the right dual, on a morphism it is rightAdjointMate. They print identically, so RibbonCategory.twist_dual renders as twist Xᘁ = twist Xᘁ — a tautology on screen, a real rewrite in the elaborator. A rw with it looks like a no-op and is not. Do not "simplify it away".

      noncomputable def QOU.MathlibExt.qDimTwistedLeftWith {C : Type u_1} [CategoryTheory.Category.{u_2, u_1} C] [CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategory C] [CategoryTheory.BraidedCategory C] [BalancedCategory C] (A B : C) [CategoryTheory.ExactPairing A B] :
      CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategoryStruct.tensorUnit C ⟶ CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategoryStruct.tensorUnit C

      The twisted LEFT quantum dimension: the same strand closed on the other side, carrying the inverse twist.

      The inverse is load-bearing, so it needs a justification that is not "it makes the theorem come out". qDimTwistedLeftWith_eq_of_cap works precisely because θ_A meets θ_A⁻¹ and cancels; with θ_A on both sides one is left with θ_A², and the identity would require θ² = 𝟙, which is false in general. So the exponent is not cosmetic and cannot be waved through.

      Two things justify it, and it is worth separating them:

      1. The opposite-exponent shape is the standard one. Barrett–Westbury, Spherical Categories, Prop. 3.6, give the two traces of a spherical Hopf algebra as tr_L(θ) = tr(θw) and tr_R(θ) = tr(θw⁻¹) — the same element in the two traces, with opposite exponents.
      2. That shape does not smuggle in sphericality. This is the non-circularity check, and it is the more important of the two: Barrett–Westbury's Example 3.5 — the quantised enveloping algebra of the Borel of SL₂(ℂ) — uses this very convention to exhibit a category satisfying every spherical axiom except that "the left and right traces are distinct". A convention under which sphericality is still refutable is not one that assumes it.

      What this does NOT establish, stated so it is not read as more than it is. Barrett–Westbury's w is the pivotal element (γ²(b) = w b w⁻¹), not the ribbon twist θ. The two are related in a ribbon category but are not the same object. So the citation supports the shape of this definition and its non-circularity; it is not a citation that this particular θ/θ⁻¹ pairing is the standard ribbon left trace. Establishing that identification would need Turaev's construction of the left duality from the right one, which is not formalised here.

      -- Ref: [barrett1993] Barrett, Westbury, Spherical Categories, Prop. 3.6 and Example 3.5.

        qDimTwistedLeftWith A B = qDimTwistedWith A B

        Sphericality, reduced to one identity at the cap.

        Left trace = right trace as soon as the two twists agree across the evaluation,

        (θ_B ▷ A) ≫ ε  =  (B ◁ θ_A) ≫ ε.

        Everything else is the balancing axiom. Read as a decomposition this says exactly where the content of sphericality sits: the Joyal–Street axiom θ_{A⊗B} = β_{A,B} ≫ β_{B,A} ≫ (θ_A ⊗ θ_B) does all of the geometric work — it converts the left closure into the right one by feeding the double braiding through the coevaluation — and leaves behind precisely one residue, the twist that ended up on the dual leg. A braided category alone cannot discharge that residue, because nothing relates θ_B to θ_A. That is the job of the ribbon axiom, and it is the whole job.

        Ribbon ⇒ spherical: in a ribbon category the left and right twisted quantum dimensions of every object agree.

        This is the statement that is unavailable for the twistless trace qDimWith, and the reason is visible in the proof: the cap identity is discharged by RibbonCategory.twist_dual together with rightAdjointMate_comp_evaluation at f = θ_X, and with θ = 𝟙 both sides of the cap identity collapse to ε — true, but then qDimTwistedLeftWith and qDimTwistedWith are two different twistless closures ((β_ B A).inv versus (β_ A B).hom) whose difference is the monodromy, and no axiom of a braided rigid category forces that to vanish. So the twistless pair is not made spherical by this argument at any twist, including the trivial one; it is the twist that carries the statement.

            theorem QOU.MathlibExt.qTrWith_tensor_monodromy {C : Type u_1} [CategoryTheory.Category.{u_2, u_1} C] [CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategory C] [CategoryTheory.BraidedCategory C] (X X' Y Y' : C) [CategoryTheory.ExactPairing X X'] [CategoryTheory.ExactPairing Y Y'] (g : X ⟶ X) (h : Y ⟶ Y) :
            qTrWith (CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategoryStruct.tensorObj X Y) (CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategoryStruct.tensorObj Y' X') (CategoryTheory.CategoryStruct.comp (β_ X Y).hom (CategoryTheory.CategoryStruct.comp (β_ Y X).hom (CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategoryStruct.tensorHom g h))) = CategoryTheory.CategoryStruct.comp (qTrWith X X' g) (qTrWith Y Y' h)

            The monodromy factorisation for the nested closure (bean qou-l64i).

            Closing the 2-cable X ⊗ Y against the nested dual Y' ⊗ X' of tensorExactPairing, with a monodromy β_{X,Y} ≫ β_{Y,X} inserted in front of g ⊗ h, splits into the two separate closures.

            The monodromy is not decoration: tensorExactPairing nests the two dual pairs in opposite orders at its two ends — the cup is X, Y, Y', X' (X outside) while the cap is Y', X', X, Y (Y outside) — so the nested closure links the X-loop with the Y-loop. The monodromy is exactly the crossing that unlinks them, which is why the statement is false without it. This is also why the earlier attempt to route the proof through a partial trace (ptrWith) could not work: no trace-of-partial-trace factorisation respects two opposite nestings at once.

            Proof shape: the hexagon splits β_{X⊗Y, Y'⊗X'} into the X- and Y-halves; braiding naturality detaches the X-strand onto the cup and then carries g and h back to the two loops; what is left is a single strand crossing a cap, discharged by braiding_slide_cap_right.

            Multiplicativity, CONDITIONAL on the monodromy factorisation.

            The hypothesis hmono is qTrWith_tensor_monodromy above, proved unconditionally as of bean qou-l64i; the unconditional form is qDimTwistedWith_tensor below. This conditional form is kept because it isolates where the balancing axiom is used. Closing the 2-cable X ⊗ Y with a monodromy β_{X,Y} ≫ β_{Y,X} on the input splits into the two separate closures. Given it, the twisted quantum dimension is multiplicative, and the derivation uses the balancing axiom exactly once — twist_tensor is precisely what puts the required monodromy in front of θ_X ⊗ θ_Y.

            It was stated conditionally on purpose. I had asserted in the bean that multiplicativity "is then a corollary"; that assertion is what this theorem replaces with a machine-checked implication, after an earlier assertion of the same kind (that the nested closure factors through ptrWith) turned out to be false. A named hypothesis discharged by rw is worth more than a claim that a step will go through.
