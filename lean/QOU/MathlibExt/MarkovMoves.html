    Markov moves on a tower of braid-like groups, algebraically #

    Mathlib has neither braid groups nor Markov moves. Measured against the vendored tree (commit f897ebcf72cd16f89ab4577d0c826cd14afaafc7, 6996 modules): a word-boundary search for Markov move, stabilization/stabilisation in the braid sense, braid closure, Ocneanu, Markov trace and Jones polynomial returns nothing, and find -iname '*braid*' returns only CategoryTheory files about the monoidal braiding. Two near-misses a bare grep would report: Mathlib.CategoryTheory.MarkovCategory is categorical probability (copy–discard categories) and Mathlib.MeasureTheory…Markov is Markov's inequality — neither is this.

    This file supplies the equivalence relation generated by the two Markov moves, and the descent theorem that is its reason for existing.

    The setting: a tower, not diagrams #

    The moves are formulated on an abstract tower

    B 0 → B 1 → B 2 → ⋯,

    a family of groups with a stabilisation homomorphism incl n : B n →* B (n+1) and a distinguished new generator gen n : B (n+1) (the classical σₙ). BraidTower is exactly that data — nothing here assumes B n is the Artin braid group, which is why the results apply verbatim to it, to its abelianisation, and to any Hecke-type quotient.

    Working on the disjoint union of all levels, Σ n, B n, is what makes stabilisation expressible at all: the move changes the strand number, so no fixed-n relation can carry it.

    What is a DEFINITION here #

    • BraidTower — the tower data (two fields, no axioms: it is data, not a claim).
    • Move — the two Markov moves as a one-step relation on Σ n, B n:
      • (M1) conjugation ⟨n, a * b⟩ ~ ⟨n, b * a⟩ (equivalently ⟨n, u * x * u⁻¹⟩ ~ ⟨n, x⟩, derived below as markovEquiv_conj);
      • (M2) stabilisation ⟨n, x⟩ ~ ⟨n+1, incl x * gen n⟩ and its negative form with (gen n)⁻¹.
    • MarkovEquiv — the equivalence closure Relation.EqvGen (Move T).
    • IsMarkovInvariant, IsScaledMarkov, Framing, normalise — the descent interface.

    What is a THEOREM here #

    • markovEquiv_equivalence — MarkovEquiv is an equivalence relation.
    • IsMarkovInvariant.descends — the payoff. A functional respecting both moves is constant on Markov classes. Packaged as IsMarkovInvariant.lift on Quot (Move T) with lift_classOf, and classOf_eq_iff shows Quot (Move T) is exactly the quotient by MarkovEquiv (no coarser).
    • IsScaledMarkov.normalise_isMarkovInvariant — the Jones normalisation, abstractly. A trace does not satisfy (M2) on the nose: it scales, tr (incl x * gen n) = z · tr x. Given a framing homomorphism (the writhe) and scalars c, d solving c·d·a = 1 and d·b = c, the rescaled functional c^{writhe} · d^{level} · f does satisfy both moves, hence descends. In the classical case those two equations are what force the square roots in Jones' normalising factor; here their solvability is a hypothesis, and the descent is the theorem.

    What is NOT proved here — read this before citing the file #

    Markov's theorem is not proved here and is not provable here. The statement that Markov equivalence classes of braids correspond exactly to isotopy classes of links is a topological theorem (Markov 1936; Birman, Braids, Links, and Mapping Class Groups; Kassel–Turaev, Braid Groups, Thm. 2.8). Proving it requires link diagrams, Reidemeister moves and a braiding algorithm — none of which exist in Mathlib and none of which appear below. Outside this disclaimer, no declaration or docstring below mentions a link, a diagram, or an isotopy: every object here is a group element, and no statement about knots or links is made or implied.

    What is proved is the algebraic descent: given the moves as stated, an invariant functional is well defined on the quotient. That is the half a link invariant needs from this side; the other half — that the quotient is the set of links — is the cited topological input.

    Four smaller things are also not established here, and each is load-bearing if the file is cited:

    • BraidTower is data. Nothing proves that any B n is the Artin braid group, that incl is the strand-adding inclusion, or that gen n is a braid generator. The moves are defined relative to whatever tower is supplied, and the only tower constructed below is the abelianisation.
    • No BraidTower on the Artin groups exists in this corpus. The stabilisation homomorphism BraidGroup n →* BraidGroup (n+1) it would need is not defined anywhere, so Framing is not instantiable there either — see the Framing docstring.
    • No Markov trace is constructed here, and nothing claims the Iwahori–Hecke tower carries one; that is the Jones/Ocneanu theorem, cited and not formalised. IsScaledMarkov is a hypothesis about a given f.
    • The solvability of c·d·a = 1 and d·b = c is a hypothesis of IsScaledMarkov.normalise_isMarkovInvariant, not a theorem. Eliminating d gives c² = b·a⁻¹, which over a general CommRing need not have a root — the classical square root in Jones' normalising factor is exactly this obstruction.

    Ground types: a generic family {B : ℕ → Type*} [∀ n, Group (B n)] and, for the normalised descent, a generic [CommRing K] with the scalars taken in Kˣ (§7c — this is pure algebra; no ordering, no Real.sqrt, no archimedean content anywhere).

    Non-vacuity #

    writheTower (§Non-vacuity) is the abelianised tower B n = Multiplicative ℤ, and it is not a toy: writheParity_isMarkovInvariant exhibits a genuine ZMod 2 Markov invariant on it, from which not_markovEquiv_of_writheParity_ne proves MarkovEquiv is a proper relation — ⟨0, 1⟩ and ⟨0, σ⟩ are not equivalent. Together with writheTower_markovEquiv_cross_level (which relates two elements at different levels) this pins the relation strictly between the identity and the total relation, so neither the relation nor the descent theorem is vacuous. writheScaled_isScaledMarkov does the same for the scaled/normalised interface, with non-trivial scalars a = A * D, b = A⁻¹ * D.

    One limit of the witness, stated because it is easy to overread. Running the normalisation chain end to end on this tower yields the constant functional 1 — writheScaled_normalise_eq_one, proved, not conceded. So writheScaled_normalise_isMarkovInvariant shows the hypotheses of IsScaledMarkov.normalise_isMarkovInvariant are satisfiable; it does not exhibit a non-trivial normalised invariant, and no file below constructs one. The non-constant invariant here is writheParity, which is unnormalised.

        The two Markov moves #

        inductive QOU.MathlibExt.MarkovMoves.Move {B : ℕ → Type u} [(n : ℕ) → Group (B n)] (T : BraidTower B) :
        Tot B → Tot B → Prop

        The Markov moves, as a one-step relation.

        • conj — Markov move I: a * b ~ b * a at a fixed level.
        • stab — Markov move II (positive): x ~ incl x * gen n, raising the level by one.
        • stabInv — Markov move II (negative), with (gen n)⁻¹.

        The relation is deliberately not symmetric or reflexive; MarkovEquiv is its equivalence closure.

                    The Jones normalisation: descent up to a scaling factor #

                    A Markov trace does not satisfy move II on the nose — it scales by the Markov parameter. The classical repair multiplies by a power of the writhe and a power of the level. This section carries out that repair abstractly.

d * b = c            (negative stabilisation is cancelled)

                            then c^{writhe} · d^{level} · f is a genuine Markov invariant, hence descends to Markov classes by IsMarkovInvariant.descends.

                            The two equations are not decoration: eliminating d gives c² = b * a⁻¹, which is exactly why the classical Jones normalising factor carries a square root. Their solvability is a hypothesis here; the descent is the theorem.

                            Non-vacuity #

                            Everything above is stated for an arbitrary tower, so it is worth exhibiting one and checking that the relation is neither trivial nor total, and that the scaled interface has a non-degenerate solution. The tower used is the abelianised braid tower B n = Multiplicative ℤ, whose element is the writhe: incl is the identity and gen n is σ, of writhe one.
