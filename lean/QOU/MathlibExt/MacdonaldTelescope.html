    QOU.MathlibExt.MacdonaldTelescope — the hook telescope, proved at low rank #

    The Macdonald hook telescope is

    Σ_{λ ⊢ n} f_λ · s_λ(Q, X) = ((1 − X)/(1 − Q))^n

    with f_λ the number of standard Young tableaux of shape λ and s_λ the Wenzl Schur element (WenzlSchurElement.schurElement). It is the identity the y_λ closed form reduces to, and it is currently carried in the corpus as an axiom (mass-theory/macdonald-hook-telescope.lean).

    Why it is true, and why the general case is not proved here #

    s_λ(Q, X) with X = Q^r is the principal specialisation s_λ(1, Q, …, Q^{r−1}) of the Schur function, since 1 − X·Q^{c(x)} = 1 − Q^{r+c(x)}. The identity is then the Frobenius/Cauchy identity for the regular representation

    Σ_λ f_λ · s_λ = p₁ⁿ

    specialised at (1, Q, …, Q^{r−1}), where p₁ = 1 + Q + ⋯ + Q^{r−1} = (1 − X)/(1 − Q).

    Both halves — the Frobenius identity and the principal-specialisation product formula — are symmetric-function infrastructure Mathlib does not have. That is the honest reason the general n is not proved here, and it is a real gap rather than a hard step: the mathematics is classical (Stanley EC2 Cor. 7.12.5; Macdonald I.3 ex. 1), the Lean substrate is missing.

    What IS proved here, unconditionally #

    The identity at n = 1 and n = 2, computed from the concrete schurElement — not from an axiom, not from a restatement. At n = 2 it is a genuine cancellation: the two partitions contribute

    s₍₂₎ = (1 − X)(1 − XQ) / ((1 − Q²)(1 − Q)), s₍₁,₁₎ = Q · (1 − X)(1 − X·Q⁻¹) / ((1 − Q²)(1 − Q)),

    and the bracket (1 − XQ) + Q(1 − X·Q⁻¹) = (1 + Q)(1 − X) is what makes the (1 + Q) cancel against 1 − Q², leaving ((1 − X)/(1 − Q))². Nothing here is true by construction; the cancellation is the content.

    These are the base cases any induction would need, and they are the first non-vacuous check that the corrected n(λ) prefactor is the right one — with the old contentSum prefactor, telescope_two below is false.

    norm_num will not unroll List.range at a numeral on its own, and the schurElement folds are over exactly that. These two rewrites are all it needs; without them the inner column-fold survives every tactic below.

      n = 3 — the first case where the SYT multiplicity does any work #

      n = 1 and n = 2 both have f_λ = 1 for every λ, so the multiplicity in Σ_λ f_λ · s_λ was never exercised: those cases would hold equally for an unweighted sum. n = 3 is the first that distinguishes them —

      λ = (3) f = 1, n(λ) = 0, hooks 3,2,1 λ = (2,1) f = 2, n(λ) = 1, hooks 3,1,1 λ = (1,1,1) f = 1, n(λ) = 3, hooks 3,2,1

      and the 2 is load-bearing: drop it and the identity fails. So this is the base case that actually tests the shape of the telescope, and the one an induction would need to trust.

      The reduction: general n does NOT need symmetric-function theory #

      A correction to what this file said above, and to what I told the author repeatedly. The general case was described as resting on symmetric-function infrastructure Mathlib lacks — the Frobenius identity and the principal-specialisation product formula. That is one route, and it is not the cheapest. The telescope follows by induction on n from two elementary statements, neither of which mentions a symmetric function:

      • (P) Pieri, specialised. Σ_{λ = μ + box} s_λ(Q, X) = ((1 − X)/(1 − Q)) · s_μ(Q, X), a rational-function identity in Q, X over the cells of μ and its addable corners. Verified symbolically with X free (not just at X = Q^r) for μ = ∅, (1), (2), (1,1), (2,1), (3), (2,2), (3,1).
      • (F) the SYT recursion. f_λ = Σ_{μ = λ − box} f_μ.

      Given those, the step is three rewrites:

      Σ_{λ⊢n+1} f_λ s_λ = Σ_{λ⊢n+1} (Σ_{μ=λ−box} f_μ) s_λ (F) = Σ_{μ⊢n} f_μ (Σ_{λ=μ+box} s_λ) (reindex) = ((1−X)/(1−Q)) · Σ_{μ⊢n} f_μ s_μ (P)

      Both inputs are Mathlib-shaped and elementary; Nat.Partition n (with its Fintype) and YoungDiagram already exist, so the enumeration and the box-addition maps are available. The remaining work is real but it is combinatorics, not symmetric-function theory — a materially smaller and differently-shaped gap than the one previously recorded here.

      The theorems below are not another base case. They exercise the engine: pieri_one is (P) at μ = (1), and telescope_two_via_pieri derives n = 2 from n = 1 through it, showing the induction step is real rather than merely checked numerically.

      (P) at a shape that exercises the whole engine #

      pieri_one is (P) at λ = (1), whose two corners are both trivial: one is a single row, the other a single column, and neither has a cell above or below the new box. It cannot distinguish the three row cases from each other.

      λ = (2,1) is the first shape that does. It has three addable corners:

      corner what it exercises
      (3,1) a box in row 0, with row 1 below it — rowFactor_addBoxRow_row_below
      (2,2) a box in row 1, with row 0 above it in the changed column — the interior case, the one needing product surgery
      (2,1,1) a new bottom row, which addBoxRow cannot express at all (§ addBoxNewRow)

      So this is the (P) analogue of telescope_three: the first case where the statement's moving parts are all in play at once, and the one an induction would have to trust. Verified symbolically with X free beforehand in computations/y_lambda_pieri_step_probe.py.

      (P) stated in general #

      QPartitionCombinatorics.addableCorners enumerates the shapes one box larger. With it, (P) is a single equation about one shape, and the two cases proved above are instances of it rather than statements that merely resemble it — which is what pieriStep_one and pieriStep_two_one below establish.

      Stating it does not prove it. The general case is the induction over corners, and it is open; nothing here asserts otherwise. What this buys is that the open goal is now a named proposition with verified instances, so the next attempt starts from a statement that has been checked against the probe rather than from prose.

          Markov-trace normalisation tr_M(𝟙) = 1 at general rank n, conditional on the Macdonald hook telescope.

          Given any list parts of (multiplicity, partition) pairs whose partitions all have size n, IF the multiplicity-weighted Schur-element sum equals the Wenzl normalisation Z_n = ((1−X)/(1−Q))^n (the general telescope, htel — the one identity Mathlib's missing symmetric-function infrastructure prevents proving outright, see the file header), THEN the same multiplicities weight the concrete Jones–Markov coefficients y_λ = jonesMarkovCoeff to exactly one:

          Σ_p mult_p · s_{λ_p} = Z_n ⟹ Σ_p mult_p · y_{λ_p} = 1.

          This is the honest conditional M1: it is the jonesMarkov_normalisation_three argument (divide the telescope by the common Z_n) lifted off the three fixed partitions of 3 to an arbitrary rank and partition list. It carries the open general telescope as an explicit hypothesis rather than assuming it — instantiate htel with a proved telescope (as telescope_three does at n = 3) and the normalisation follows unconditionally. Nonzero denominator needs only Q ≠ 1, X ≠ 1.
