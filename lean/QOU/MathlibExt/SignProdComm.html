    QOU.MathlibExt.SignProdComm — the signature of the coordinate swap on ι × ι #

    Bean qou-kwoa. QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.twGram_det_eq_sq_mul_prod states the Gram determinant of the T_w basis as a square times ∏_λ sign(prodComm) · d_λ^(d_λ²), leaving the sign as the abstract Equiv.Perm.sign (Equiv.prodComm ι ι). This evaluates it:

    sign (prodComm ι ι) = (-1) ^ (d * (d - 1) / 2),    d = card ι

    so the per-block sign of that determinant is explicit rather than a black box.

    The argument, and the step that is easy to miss #

    prodComm is an involution, so its cycle type is a multiset of 2s. Equiv.Perm.sign_of_cycleType gives sign = (-1) ^ (cycleType.sum + card cycleType), and for an involution with support of size s that is (-1) ^ (s + s / 2). The support here is the off-diagonal — prodComm fixes exactly the pairs (a, a) — so s = d² - d = d(d-1).

    That s is even, being a product of consecutive integers, is doing real work: it is what kills the first summand and collapses (-1)^(s + s/2) to (-1)^(s/2). Without it the formula does not simplify to the half-count alone.

    A Lean note worth keeping #

    Writing (Equiv.prodComm ι ι : Equiv.Perm (ι × ι)).support does not work: the ascription is unfolded before dot-notation is resolved, so Lean looks for Equiv.support and fails. Hence the named prodSwap below — its declared type has head constant Equiv.Perm, so .support, .cycleType and .sign resolve. prodSwap_eq transfers the result back.

    Cross-check against measurement #

    computations/hecke/tw_gram_square_class_crosscheck.py uses exactly this formula to predict the sign of det(twGram), and it matches the determinants tw_gram_determinant_probe.py measured at n = 1, 2, 3: block dimensions (1,1), (1,2,1) and (1,3,2,3,1) give +1, -1, -1 against measured +, -, -. The Lean here and that measurement are independent.

      Concrete checks #

      decide evaluates the signature by brute force, without going through the cycle-type argument above. d = 1 gives 1; d = 2 and d = 3 both give -1, matching (-1)^(d(d-1)/2) at (-1)^0, (-1)^1 and (-1)^3. These are genuinely decidable finite computations, not a native_decide escape.
