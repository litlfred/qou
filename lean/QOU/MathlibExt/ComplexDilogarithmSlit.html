    cLiTwoExt on all of ℂ ∖ [1,∞), and its derivative along rays #

    Bean qou-clx8. That bean records two things this file acts on.

    First, the side condition in the existing bricks is sufficient but not necessary:

    intervalIntegrable_log_one_sub_div requires Re(1 − yz) > 0 on [0,1], i.e. Re(1 − y) > 0. That fails at e.g. y = 1.5 + 0.3i and y = 3 + 0.001i while the identity still holds. Do not formalize Re(1−y) > 0 as if it were the real hypothesis — it needlessly excludes most of ℂ ∖ [1,∞).

    and it names the hypothesis that is right:

    For Im y ≠ 0, the segment {1 − yz : z ∈ [0,1]} meets the real axis only at z = 0, because Im(1 − yz) = −z·Im y vanishes on [0,1] only at z = 0.

    mem_slitPlane_one_sub_mul below is that, and it covers all of ℂ ∖ [1,∞) in one statement: y.im ≠ 0 ∨ y.re < 1 is exactly the complement of the cut, since [1,∞) ⊆ ℂ is {y : y.im = 0 ∧ 1 ≤ y.re}. The two cases are disjointly easy — off the real axis the imaginary part does it, on the real axis below 1 the real part does. ComplexDilogarithm.intervalIntegrable_log_one_sub_div has been generalised to take slitPlane membership directly, with the old right-half-plane form kept as a corollary.

    Consequence: cLiTwoExt y is given by a genuinely convergent integral for every y off the cut — including ‖y₁‖ = 1.52, the 5₂ root the series cannot reach, which is the identification qou-0yqu lists as missing.

    Second, the bean's remaining obstruction is analyticity in y, and both routes it lists go through differentiation under the integral sign, which it calls "not a Mathlib one-liner". This file takes a third route for the radial direction, where the parameter can be moved into the limit of integration instead of the integrand:

    cLiTwoExt (t·y) = −∫₀ᵗ log(1 − y·u)/u du        (`cLiTwoExt_ofReal_mul`)

    by the substitution u = t·z — Mathlib's integral_comp_mul_left. Once the parameter is a limit, the derivative is the fundamental theorem of calculus, not differentiation under an integral:

    d/dt cLiTwoExt (t·y) = −log(1 − y·t)/t          (`hasDerivAt_cLiTwoExt_radial`)

    which is d/dw Li₂(w) = −log(1−w)/w restricted to the ray through y, since the chain rule reads cLiTwoExt'(ty)·y = −log(1−ty)/t.

    What this does and does not settle #

    It gives the derivative along rays from the origin. Full analyticity on ℂ ∖ [1,∞) needs the derivative in every direction, and that is still open — a primitive-of-a-holomorphic-function argument on the simply-connected slit plane is the natural next route, and it is not written here. So "the analytic continuation" remains a name plus agreement-on-the-disc plus this radial derivative, not yet a theorem.

    qou-clx8 is also explicit that closing it does not move the sum rule: Route 5 is a restatement, and even the whole chain leaves an unevaluated real integral. This file is taken for the Mathlib gap, as that bean instructs.

    The 5₂ sum rule is PROVED: QOU.QBeta.FiveTwoComplexPentagon.fiveTwo_saddle_sum_rule and fiveTwoSaddleSumRule_holds, sorry-free, standard axioms.

    Off the real axis the imaginary part does the work — Im(1 − yz) = −z·(Im y), which is non-zero for z > 0, and at z = 0 the point is 1. On the real axis below 1 the real part does it: Re(1 − yz) = 1 − z·(Re y) ≥ 1 − max(Re y, 0) > 0 for z ∈ [0,1].
