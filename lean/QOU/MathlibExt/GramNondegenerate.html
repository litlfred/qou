    An invertible Gram matrix makes a bilinear form nondegenerate on the span #

    For a bilinear form B and a finite family v : ι → V, if the Gram matrix (B (v i) (v j)) has nonzero determinant then

    span k (range v) ⊓ (span k (range v))ᗮ = ⊥.

    Mathlib does not have this. It has LinearMap.BilinForm.Nondegenerate and Submodule.orthogonalBilin, and it has the Gram matrix of a basis (LinearMap.BilinForm.toMatrix), but nothing connecting the determinant of the Gram matrix of an arbitrary spanning family to triviality of the radical.

    Why it is wanted here #

    This is the last link of the chain that closes specht_form_nondegenerate:

    • MathlibExt.RankMinor turns linear independence into a nonzero minor;
    • MathlibExt.CauchyBinet turns a nonzero minor into det (M Mᵀ) ≠ 0 over any characteristic-zero field;
    • this file turns det Gram ≠ 0 into W ⊓ Wᗮ = ⊥.

    Nothing in it mentions Specht modules, tabloids, or ℤ — it is the general statement, and the Specht application supplies v and the determinant.

    Note on which side the form is evaluated #

    Submodule.orthogonalBilin N B is the left complement: x ∈ Nᗮ means B n x = 0 for every n ∈ N (Mathlib flags the chirality explicitly). The proof below therefore reads the Gram matrix rows-first — gramMatrix B v j i is B (v j) (v i) — so that orthogonality gives mulVec, not vecMul. For a symmetric form the distinction is invisible, but the theorem does not assume symmetry and this is the side that works without it.

      Diagonal Gram ⇒ linear independence #

      The other thing a Gram matrix is good for. Nondegeneracy above needs the whole matrix invertible; independence needs only that it is diagonal with nonzero diagonal, which is what an orthogonality relation delivers without any normalisation of the diagonal entries.

      This is the step that lets the Specht completeness count avoid [IsAlgClosed k]: FDRep.char_orthonormal pins the diagonal to 1 and needs algebraic closure to do it, but a bound only needs the diagonal nonzero.
