    def QOU.MathlibExt.MatrixSemisimpleGeneral.matrixPiRingEquiv {m : Type u_1} [Fintype m] {ι : Type u_2} (S : ι → Type u_3) [(i : ι) → Ring (S i)] :
    Matrix m m ((i : ι) → S i) ≃+* ((i : ι) → Matrix m m (S i))

    Matrices over a product ring are the product of the matrix rings.

    Matrix m m (Π i, S i) ≃+* Π i, Matrix m m (S i). A transposition of the two outer indices; every field is rfl except multiplicativity. Mathlib has Matrix.compRingEquiv for matrices of matrices but nothing for matrices over a Pi.

    The Matrix.of in toFun is load-bearing, not decoration. Matrix m m α is a type synonym for m → m → α, so a bare fun a b => … elaborates with the Pi multiplication (instHMul on m → m → S i) even under a : Matrix m m (S i) ascription — and then Matrix.mul_apply does not fire, while Pi.mul_apply fires and rewrites the product pointwise, which is the wrong lemma. Matrix.of pins the matrix structure.

      IsSemisimpleRing (Matrix m m R) for a general semisimple base — Mathlib's proof_wanted IsSemisimpleRing.matrix, in full.

      MatrixSemisimple.instIsSemisimpleRing_matrix covers the division-ring base. This lifts it to any semisimple R, by composing pieces that all already exist:

      1. Artin–Wedderburn (exists_ringEquiv_pi_matrix_divisionRing) writes R ≃+* Π i, Matrix (Fin (d i)) (Fin (d i)) (D i) over division rings D i;
      2. RingEquiv.mapMatrix carries that inside Matrix m m;
      3. matrixPiRingEquiv pushes the product outwards;
      4. Matrix.compRingEquiv flattens each Matrix m m (Matrix _ _ (D i)) to Matrix (m × Fin (d i)) (m × Fin (d i)) (D i), semisimple by the division-ring case;
      5. a finite product of semisimple rings is semisimple, and RingEquiv.isSemisimpleRing transports back along the composite.
