    QOU.MathlibExt.RegTraceMatrix — the regular trace form of a matrix algebra #

    Bean qou-kwoa, the determinant's value.

    RegTraceGramNondegenerate proves det(twGram) ≠ 0 without computing the determinant, and gets the characteristic hypothesis it needs from regTraceForm_one_one (B(1,1) = finrank). That is enough for non-vanishing, but it is not the sharp statement. The sharp statement is

    regTraceForm K (Matrix ι ι K) x y = (card ι) * (x * y).trace

    — the regular trace is d times the ordinary matrix trace. B(1,1) = d² is its diagonal corollary (tr 1 = d), and the whole characteristic story reads off it: the form is d · tr(xy), and tr is nondegenerate over any field, so the form degenerates exactly when d = 0 in K. docs/audits/2026-08-30-tw-gram-determinant-measured.md asserts that equivalence as the premise of its scope-(b) exclusion; this file proves it.

    Why the proof is short #

    Left multiplication by M acts on each column of X independently — column j of M * X is M *ᵥ (column j of X) — so lmul M is literally block-diagonal, with card ι identical blocks M.mulVecLin. That is exactly the shape QOU.MathlibExt.trace_piDiag (TracePi.lean) was written for, so no basis computation is needed. Matrix.stdBasis exposes no repr simp lemma, which makes the direct route painful; this one avoids it.

    The block Gram determinant #

    In the matrix-unit basis the Gram matrix is d • the identity, row-permuted by the transpose involution (i,j) ↦ (j,i) — because tr(E_ij · E_kl) = δ_{jk} δ_{il}. So its determinant is sign(prodComm) · d^(d²), and the per-block factor d^(d²) of the integer constant ∏_λ d_λ^(d_λ²) measured by the probe is derived rather than observed.

    Numerical cross-check against the probe. Assembling d^(d²) over the Wedderburn blocks of H_{n+1} — i.e. over the partitions of n+1, with d_λ the SYT count — reproduces the probe's measured integer constant exactly at every rank it computed:

    n block dims d_λ ∏_λ d_λ^(d_λ²) probe's constant
    1 1, 1 1 1
    2 1, 2, 1 16 2⁴
    3 1, 3, 2, 3, 1 6198727824 2⁴·3¹⁸

    The probe obtained its constants by factoring exact symbolic determinants and did not use this lemma; the agreement is therefore a check on both. The assembly itself is not yet formalised — that needs the Gram of a product form in a product basis to be block diagonal, plus the change-of-basis square relating the matrix-unit basis to T_w. Both are the natural next steps and neither is claimed here.

    What this is a step toward, and what it is NOT #

    Geck–Pfeiffer 7.2.6 gives det(Gram) = ∏_λ (Schur_λ)^{f^λ}. The measured values (computations/hecke/tw_gram_determinant_probe.py, exact arithmetic) factor as an integer constant ∏_λ d_λ^(d_λ²) times a q-part. This file supplies the block form that the integer constant comes from. It says nothing about the q-part, which is where the Schur elements live, and it is not a proof of Geck–Pfeiffer.

      theorem QOU.MathlibExt.regTraceGram_stdBasis_det_ne_zero_iff (K : Type u_3) [Field K] (ι : Type u_4) [Fintype ι] [DecidableEq ι] [Nonempty ι] :

      The block Gram determinant vanishes exactly in the bad characteristic.

      d^(d²) = 0 in K iff d = 0 in K, so this is the sharp form of the obstruction that docs/audits/2026-08-30-tw-gram-determinant-measured.md states — and the reason the determinant route can never certify semisimplicity when char K ∣ d.
