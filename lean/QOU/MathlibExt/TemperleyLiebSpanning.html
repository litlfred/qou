    Upper bounds for TL_n(δ) — the gap TemperleyLiebAlgebra names about itself #

    Bean qou-cds1. QOU.MathlibExt.TemperleyLiebAlgebra says, in its own non-vacuity section:

    This does not claim TL₂(δ) ≅ R × R: no upper bound on TL₂(δ) is proved anywhere in this file.

    That is the structural gap, and it is not cosmetic. Everything in that file bounds TL from below — representations separating generators, showing elements are nonzero and distinct. Nothing bounds it from above, so TL_n(δ) could a priori be enormous, and no dimension count, Wedderburn decomposition or semisimplicity statement can even be attempted until it is finite over the expected spanning set.

    This file supplies the upper bounds at n = 2 and n = 3:

    TL₂(δ) = span_R {1, e₀}                      (2 = catalan 2)
TL₃(δ) = span_R {1, e₀, e₁, e₀e₁, e₁e₀}      (5 = catalan 3)

    and the matching lower bounds, so both are exact:

    dim TL₂(δ) = 2 = catalan 2                   (`tl2Basis`)
dim TL₃(δ) = 5 = catalan 3                   (`tl3Basis`)

    At n = 2 the lower bound is TemperleyLiebAlgebra.tl2_linearIndependent, already in the corpus. At n = 3 it was not — that file's tl3Rep lands in 2 × 2 matrices, and a 4-dimensional target cannot separate five elements. tl3Rep5 fixes this by summing the same family with the trivial representation e_i ↦ 0; five dimensions is exactly enough, and the separation goes through as soon as δ is a unit and δ² − 1 is a non-zero-divisor. Both hold at the substrate (δ = q + q⁻¹ ≈ 2.01, δ² − 1 ≈ 3.05).

    These are the first two instances of dim TL_n = catalan n in this corpus proved as algebra facts rather than as partition counts.

    Why this is the right next step for the Wedderburn direction #

    The open item left by QOU.MathlibExt.HeckeToTemperleyLieb is ⟨relators⟩ = traceRadical tr_M, whose hard half needs TL_n(δ) semisimple. QOU.MathlibExt.TraceRadicalNondegenerate closed everything downstream of the Wedderburn decomposition. The decomposition itself needs, at minimum, that the algebra is finite-dimensional with the expected basis — which is exactly what an upper bound gives and what was missing.

    The method, which is uniform and generalises #

    Algebra.adjoin R (Set.range e) = ⊤ — proved in HeckeToTemperleyLieb — says the generators generate. So it suffices to exhibit a submodule containing them, containing 1, and closed under multiplication: Submodule.toSubalgebra turns it into a subalgebra, and Algebra.adjoin_le then forces it to be everything.

    Closure under multiplication reduces, via Submodule.span_mul_span, to checking the finitely many products of spanning elements — 4 at n = 2, 25 at n = 3. Each is a one-line consequence of e_sq, e_adjacent or e_farCommute.

    What does not generalise for free is the spanning set. At n = 2, 3 it is small enough to write down and close by hand. General n needs the planar diagram basis (catalan n of them) and the reduction of an arbitrary word to a diagram — that is the real content of dim TL_n = catalan n, and it is not done here.

    The lower bounds do not generalise for free either, and for a different reason: each is a representation exhibited by hand, of exactly the right dimension. General n wants the diagram basis again, from the other side.

            n = 3: the matching lower bound, and freeness #

            tl3_span_eq_top used to end by saying no lower bound was available at n = 3, because TemperleyLiebAlgebra's tl3Rep lands in 2 × 2 matrices — a 4-dimensional target, which cannot witness independence of five elements however it is chosen. That reading of the obstruction was right and the conclusion drawn from it was too strong: the fix is not a different 2 × 2 family but the same one carried into a target one dimension larger.

            TL₃(δ) has a one-dimensional representation e_i ↦ 0 — the trivial one — and the direct sum of it with tl3Rep is 5-dimensional. That is exactly enough, and it separates the five spanning elements the moment δ is a unit and δ² − 1 is a non-zero-divisor.

            Both hypotheses are used, and neither is decoration:

            • δ a unit is what makes e₀e₁e₀ = e₀ hold in the 2 × 2 block at all (tl3Fam_adjacent already needs it);
            • δ² − 1 a non-zero-divisor is genuine. At δ = ±1 the algebra really does degenerate — δ = 1 collapses M₀M₁ and M₁M₀ onto one another — so no argument can drop it.

            At the substrate the relevant δ = q + q⁻¹ with q ≈ 1.11, so δ ≈ 2.01 and δ² − 1 ≈ 3.05: both hypotheses hold, and hold with room.
