    QOU.MathlibExt.BraidedTensorDual — where the monodromy comes from #

    In a braided category with two exact pairings X ⊣ X' and Y ⊣ Y' there are two ways to exhibit X ⊗ Y as a right dual of Y' ⊗ X':

    • swap-then-tensor — build the tensor pairing X ⊗ Y ⊣ Y' ⊗ X' (QOU.MathlibExt.tensorExactPairing) and turn it around with CategoryTheory.BraidedCategory.exactPairing_swap;
    • tensor-then-swap — turn each pairing around first, then tensor.

    They are not the same pairing, and the discrepancy is the monodromy (β_ X Y).hom ≫ (β_ Y X).hom. That is the obstruction to QOU.MathlibExt.drinfeldIso being monoidal, and hence the reason a braided right-rigid category is not pivotal in the monoidal sense — see the module docstring of QOU.MathlibExt.PivotalCategory.

    The mechanism #

    Expanding the block braiding β_{X ⊗ Y, Y' ⊗ X'} by the two hexagons gives four crossings. Two of them — Y over Y' and X over X' — pair off with the two evaluations and reproduce the componentwise comparisons. The surviving two are X over Y' and Y over X': each crosses only one leg of a dual pair, and braiding_slide_cap_right_inv says such a crossing can be pushed off only at the cost of an inverse braiding past the other leg. The two leftovers are (β_ Y X).inv and (β_ X Y).inv, whose composite is the inverse monodromy.

    evaluation_swap_tensor_step below carries out the first of the two pushes.

    The proof is: two hexagons to expand the block braiding, one interchange to bring the crossing next to the evaluation it must cross, and the inverse slide.

    The second push, and the monodromy it leaves.

    Three strands: X crossing the closed Y-pair. Pushing the X-over-Y' crossing off the Y-cap leaves the inverse monodromy ((β_ X Y).hom ≫ (β_ Y X).hom)⁻¹ acting on X ⊗ Y at the top.

    The proof is three moves and no diagram chase. Write (β_ Y X).inv = M⁻¹ ≫ (β_ X Y).hom, which splits the crossing into a monodromy and an honest braiding. Recognise the two leading crossings as the block braiding β_{X ⊗ Y, Y'} (braiding_tensor_left_hom), so that braiding_naturality_left carries the monodromy from the middle to the top. What is left is exactly braiding_slide_cap_right at the Y-cap.

    Swap-then-tensor and tensor-then-swap are compared by the inverse monodromy.

    The dual-uniqueness isomorphism between the two pairings of Y' ⊗ X' with X ⊗ Y is not the identity: it is ((β_ X Y).hom ≫ (β_ Y X).hom)⁻¹. In a symmetric category the monodromy is trivial and the two pairings agree; in a genuinely braided one they do not.

    The ribbon repair #

    The monodromy is exactly what a balancing cancels, so while drinfeldIso is not monoidal, its twisted form θ ≫ drinfeldIso is. That is the classical statement — a ribbon element v makes uv⁻¹ grouplike — and twist_drinfeld_monoidal below is its categorical form.

            The ribbon repair: θ ≫ drinfeldIso IS monoidal.

            drinfeldIso misses monoidality by the inverse monodromy (pivotorTensor_hom); the balancing axiom θ_{X ⊗ Y} = β_{X,Y} ≫ β_{Y,X} ≫ (θ_X ⊗ θ_Y) supplies exactly the monodromy, and the two cancel. The step that makes the cancellation go through is monodromy_inv_comm: θ_X ⊗ θ_Y commutes past the monodromy by braiding naturality, so the β β from the balancing axiom meets its own inverse.

            This is the categorical form of the classical fact that a ribbon element v makes uv⁻¹ grouplike, and it is why ribbon, not merely braided, is the hypothesis under which a rigid category is pivotal in the monoidal sense.
