    Rectangular Cauchy–Binet #

    det (A * B) = ∑_f det (A_f) * det (B^f), the sum running over the strictly monotone f : Fin r → Fin n — i.e. over the r-element column subsets of A and the matching row subsets of B.

    Mathlib does not have this. At the pinned revision there is no file matching Cauchy-Binet / cauchy_binet / CauchyBinet, and no lemma writing a determinant as a sum over column subsets; det_mul covers only the square case. This file builds it.

    Why it is wanted here #

    sp01 — specht_form_nondegenerate, the critical path into the Specht cluster — names exactly this as one of its two routes, and rules out the other: an ℝ-only positive-definiteness shortcut was declined because it would weaken the generic [Field k] [CharZero k] hypothesis to ℝ.

    Cauchy–Binet avoids that. For an integer Gram matrix G = M Mᵀ,

    det G = ∑_f (det M_f)²

    is a sum of squares of integers, so it is positive as soon as one minor is nonzero — and that conclusion transfers to every characteristic-zero field without specializing to ℝ.

    Status #

    Complete and sorry-free. det_mul_eq_sum_minors is the general theorem; det_gram_map_ne_zero is the corollary sp01 consumes. Every declaration in this file checks with #print axioms reporting only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

    Proof route #

    Leibniz, then interchange the product and sum:

    det (A * B) = ∑_σ sgn σ ∏_i ∑_k A (σ i) k * B k i = ∑_{φ : Fin r → Fin n} ∑_σ sgn σ ∏_i A (σ i) (φ i) * B (φ i) i

    then group by φ. Two facts finish it:

    1. a non-injective φ contributes 0 — the inner alternating sum cancels in pairs under the transposition swapping two indices with φ i = φ j;
    2. an injective φ factors uniquely as a strictly monotone f composed with a permutation of Fin r, and that permutation's sign factors out.

          Step 1 — Leibniz, with the inner sum pulled out #

          det (A * B) expands by Matrix.det_apply into a signed sum over permutations of a product of matrix entries, and each entry of A * B is itself a sum. Finset.prod_sum turns the product-of-sums into a sum over functions φ : Fin r → Fin n, which is the indexing the whole proof runs on.

          Step 3 — a non-injective φ contributes nothing #

          The textbook argument pairs each σ with σ ∘ (swap i j) and cancels. That involution is fiddly here, because the B factor carries the row index and does not survive the reindexing unchanged.

          Cleaner: the σ-sum is a determinant. Factoring the σ-independent B product out leaves exactly Matrix.det_apply for A.submatrix id φ, and a non-injective φ repeats a column of that submatrix — which Mathlib already knows kills the determinant. No hand-rolled involution needed.

          Step 4 — an injective φ sorts into Selector ∘ permutation #

          This is the piece with no Mathlib support. An injective φ : Fin r → Fin n has an r-element image; enumerating that image in increasing order gives the strictly monotone f, and the permutation π records how φ shuffles it.

          Finset.orderEmbOfFin supplies the sorted enumeration; what has to be built is the bijection onto the image and the resulting Equiv.Perm.

                      Corollary — the Gram determinant is a sum of squares #

                      This is the form sp01 (specht_form_nondegenerate) asks for, and the reason the general theorem was worth building. Specialising B := Aᵀ makes the two minors transposes of each other, so each term of the Cauchy–Binet sum is a square:

                      det (A Aᵀ) = ∑_f (det A_f)²

                      Over an ordered ring that is immediately ≥ 0, and > 0 as soon as a single minor is nonzero — no positive-definiteness machinery, no rank argument.

                      The last lemma is the transfer that made this route preferable to the ℝ-only shortcut sp01 declined: run the positivity over ℤ, where the sum of squares lives, then push the nonzero conclusion along ℤ → k for any characteristic-zero field. The hypothesis stays [Field k] [CharZero k] rather than collapsing to ℝ.
