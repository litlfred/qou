    The radical of a Markov trace, and what faithfulness means #

    OcneanuTrace.MarkovTrace axiomatises a trace on a tower but says nothing about whether that trace sees the algebra. This file supplies the missing notion and its elementary theory, so that "is the Ocneanu trace faithful?" becomes a statement one can prove or refute rather than a phrase.

    The definition, and why this one #

    For a trace tr on A the associated bilinear form is ⟨a, b⟩ = tr (a * b), and its radical

    rad T = {a | ∀ b, tr (a * b) = 0}

    is what faithfulness is about: tr is faithful exactly when rad T = ⊥. Note what is not meant — tr is a functional into R, so it is never injective on an algebra of dimension > 1, and "faithful" never refers to injectivity of tr itself.

    What is proved #

    • rad is a two-sided ideal — mul_mem_rad_left needs the trace property tr (x * y) = tr (y * x) and associativity, nothing else.
    • one_notMem_rad — the radical is proper, from normalisation tr 1 = 1. So the question is never vacuous in the degenerate direction.
    • tr_mul_gen_sub_z_of_mem — the Markov axiom is an orthogonality statement: g n - z • 1 is orthogonal to the whole of H n. This is (T3) read as a fact about the form rather than about stabilisation, and it is the reason the radical is the natural object here: each new generator arrives with a prescribed component orthogonal to everything below it.
    • rad_mono_of_towerSpan_step — the level-wise radicals increase: an element of H n orthogonal to a subalgebra B ⊆ H n stays orthogonal when B is extended by sandwiches v * g n * w. Cyclicity turns the sandwich inside out, Markov strips the generator, and the result lands back in B. This is what makes a single low-level computation decide faithfulness at every level, and it is used in OcneanuTraceNotFaithful.

    What is NOT proved here #

    Nothing about any particular trace. Whether rad is zero depends on the Markov parameter z, and that computation is OcneanuTraceNotFaithful. Nothing here asserts or assumes faithfulness either way.

        The lifting step, and the reusable content of this file.

        If a ∈ H n is orthogonal to every element of a subalgebra B ⊆ H n, then it is orthogonal to every sandwich v * g n * w with v, w ∈ B as well.

        Cyclicity moves w to the front, Markov strips g n, and v * w is back in B. So the radical relative to a stage of the filtration only ever grows — one computation at one level settles every level above it.

        One filtration step, without a conditional expectation #

        QBeta.TLTowerRadicalDescent.map_mem_traceRadical reaches the same conclusion as tr_mul_eq_zero_of_step_span below, but from a HeckeConditionalExpectation — an open §3b class with no instance, so everything there is conditional in the §3b-cond sense. That file argues a bare inclusion cannot do better:

        A bare inclusion ι : Hn →ₐ Hn1 says nothing about the two radicals: membership in rad τn1 quantifies over all of Hn1, not over ι '' Hn, so no amount of compatibility between τn and τn1 on the subalgebra constrains it.

        That is right about a bare inclusion and too strong about a tower. A test element of H (n+1) is not arbitrary: if the step is spanned by the previous stage together with single sandwiches, the element reduces to test elements of H n, and then cyclicity and Markov finish it. What the conditional expectation buys is the converse (mem_traceRadical_of_map, which spends inst.retract); the forward direction needs only a spanning property.
