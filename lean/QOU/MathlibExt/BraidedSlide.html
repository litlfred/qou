    QOU.MathlibExt.BraidedSlide — sliding a strand across a cap or a cup #

    In a braided category, a morphism c : A ⊗ B ⟶ 𝟙_ C behaves like a cap joining A to B, and u : 𝟙_ C ⟶ A ⊗ B like a cup. The four lemmas here say that a strand W passing across such a cap or cup may be pushed off it, at the cost of braiding W past each of the two legs separately:

       W over the cap        =        W past A, then past B, then the cap

    Nothing is assumed of c and u beyond their type — no rigidity, no exact pairing, not even that they are related to each other. Each lemma is the corresponding braiding_naturality_* at the cap or cup, with the hexagon braiding_tensor_*_hom expanding β at the tensor A ⊗ B.

    Why these are worth naming #

    They are the mechanism by which the monodromy appears in rigid braided categories. When A and B are a dual pair and c is the evaluation, a strand crossing one leg cannot simply be deleted: pushing it off supplies a braiding past the other leg as well, and after the cap closes, that second braiding is a crossing of W with the original object. Two such pushes — one for each of a pair of dual legs — produce β ≫ β, which is precisely the discrepancy that obstructs monoidality of the Drinfeld isomorphism and that a ribbon twist cancels. See QOU.MathlibExt.PivotalCategory for that argument.

    Naming #

    W slides "right" when it starts on the left of the cap and ends on its right, matching braiding_naturality_right; "left" is the mirror image, from braiding_naturality_left.

    The inverse form of braiding_slide_cap_right — the one an assembly actually uses.

    Read left to right: a strand W that crosses only the second leg B before the cap can still be pushed off, but it leaves behind an inverse braiding past the first leg A. That leftover is where a monodromy comes from: apply this twice, once for each leg of a dual pair, and the two leftovers compose to a double braid.
