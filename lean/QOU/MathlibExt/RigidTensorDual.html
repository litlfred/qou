    QOU.MathlibExt.RigidTensorDual — duals of a tensor product #

    Mathlib's Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Monoidal/Rigid/Basic.lean opens with a "Future work" list. This module supplies its first entry, quoted verbatim:

    • Show that X ⊗ Y and Yᘁ ⊗ Xᘁ form an exact pairing.

    Nothing in Mathlib implements it: there is no ExactPairing (X ⊗ Y) _, and no HasRightDual instance for a tensor product. Duals reversing order is the fact every downstream monoidal statement needs — in particular one cannot even state that a pivotal structure is monoidal without it, since that statement compares a_{X ⊗ Y} against a_X ⊗ a_Y through the comparison isomorphism (X ⊗ Y)ᘁᘁ ≅ Xᘁᘁ ⊗ Yᘁᘁ built from this pairing.

    Imports only Mathlib, so it lifts upstream verbatim. Companions: Ribbon.lean, PivotalCategory.lean, BraidedQuantumDimension.lean.

    Main definition #

    • tensorExactPairing — ExactPairing (X ⊗ Y) (Y' ⊗ X') from ExactPairing X X' and ExactPairing Y Y'. The coevaluation nests η_Y inside η_X; the evaluation runs ε_X on the inner pair and then ε_Y.

    The proof, and why the obvious routes fail #

    Both snake identities are genuine string-diagram arguments. Neither is reachable by cat_disch (the class's own autoparam), simp, monoidal, or aesop_cat; roughly thirty-five automated tactic placements were tried and none closes either field.

    The reason is worth recording, because it is not obvious from the goal. The composite fires in the order η_X, η_Y, ε_X, ε_Y, and in that order neither snake is contiguous — η_Y sits between η_X and ε_X. Moving it out of the way is the interchange law, not coherence, so monoidal can never reach the two-snake form no matter how the intermediate is phrased.

    What works is the idiom of Mathlib's own coevaluation_evaluation_braided': pad every calc term with 𝟙 _ ⊗≫ … ⊗≫ 𝟙 _, parenthesise the interesting part as a single ⊗≫ group, and regroup until a bare W ◁ g ≫ f ▷ Z appears with no compound whiskering around it — that bare shape is what whisker_exchange matches, and the compound forms hide it. Once the exchange fires the two snakes are contiguous and coevaluation_evaluation'' / evaluation_coevaluation'' finish the job.

    The coevaluation creates the X-pair and nests the Y-pair inside it; the evaluation contracts the inner X-pair first and then the outer Y-pair. Duals reverse order, which is why the right-hand object is Y' ⊗ X' and not X' ⊗ Y'.

              (CategoryTheory.rightDualIso (tensorExactPairing X X₁ Y Y₁) (tensorExactPairing X X₂ Y Y₂)).hom = CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategoryStruct.tensorHom (CategoryTheory.rightDualIso r₁ r₂).hom (CategoryTheory.rightDualIso p₁ p₂).hom

              The dual comparison of a tensor product is the tensor of the dual comparisons.

              Both tensorExactPairing X X₁ Y Y₁ and tensorExactPairing X X₂ Y Y₂ exhibit a right dual of X ⊗ Y, so rightDualIso compares them; the claim is that this comparison factors as the tensor of the two componentwise comparisons. Note the order reversal on the right: the dual of a tensor is the tensor of the duals in the opposite order, so the Y-comparison comes first.

              Proved by uniqueness (rightDualIso_hom_unique): it suffices that the right-hand side satisfies the same evaluation identity, and that is the two componentwise identities plus one application of the interchange law. Purely formal — no braiding, no rigidity beyond the two pairings.
