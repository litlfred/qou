    QOU.MathlibExt.RegTraceGramNondegenerate — the CONVERSE of the Gram criterion #

    Bean qou-kwoa.

    QOU/MathlibExt/RegTraceGram.lean proves one direction:

    det (Gram of regTraceBilin in a basis) ≠ 0   ⟹   A is semisimple

    and its docstring records the intended next step as computing that determinant — det = [n]_q!, i.e. Geck–Pfeiffer 7.2.6, det(Gram) = ∏_λ (Schur_λ)^{f^λ}. That is the hard half, and bean qou-0y6h was closed against it.

    The other direction makes the computation unnecessary for non-vanishing. Semisimplicity of H_n(q) is now proved independently — via the Wedderburn decomposition (QOU.SchurWeyl.isSemisimpleRing_hecke_generic), which owes the Gram determinant nothing. So det ≠ 0 follows from semisimplicity rather than the reverse, and the Schur-element product is needed only for the determinant's value.

    This file supplies the missing implication. It is not a corollary of the forward one, and it is not in Mathlib: Algebra.traceForm_nondegenerate is about separable field extensions, whereas regTraceForm x y = trace (L_{xy}) is the regular trace of a possibly-noncommutative algebra.

    Where the characteristic hypothesis enters, and why it is not removable #

    On a block Matrix (Fin d) (Fin d) K the regular trace is d · the ordinary matrix trace, because left multiplication acts on d copies of the column space. So the form is d · tr(xy), and it degenerates exactly when d = 0 in K. That is why the statement carries a characteristic assumption and why the [CharZero K] on the Hecke side is load-bearing rather than inherited noise: at char p ∣ d_λ the Gram determinant of a genuinely semisimple algebra vanishes.

    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.regTraceForm_mul_assoc {K : Type u_1} {A : Type u_2} [Field K] [Ring A] [Algebra K A] [FiniteDimensional K A] (x y z : A) :
    regTraceForm K A (x * y) z = regTraceForm K A x (y * z)

    Associativity of the regular trace form. Both sides are trace (L_z) for the same z, by mul_assoc. This is what makes the form's radical a two-sided ideal rather than merely a subspace.

    theorem QOU.MathlibExt.regTraceForm_one_one {K : Type u_1} {A : Type u_2} [Field K] [Ring A] [Algebra K A] [FiniteDimensional K A] :
    regTraceForm K A 1 1 = ↑(Module.finrank K A)

    B(1, 1) is the dimension. L_1 = id, so the trace is finrank K A in K.

    This is the whole of the characteristic input: the radical argument below excludes the degenerate case by B(1,1) ≠ 0, and B(1,1) is a natural number cast into K. In characteristic p ∣ finrank K A it vanishes and the argument genuinely fails — as it must, since the Gram determinant of a semisimple algebra really does vanish there.
