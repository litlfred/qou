    QOU.SchurWeyl.OcneanuWeightAddCell — the weight's two halves under addCell #

    HookLengthAddCell supplies how a hook moves when a cell is added. This file applies it to ocneanuWeight itself, splitting the weight into its numerator and denominator products and showing how each responds. It is the second brick toward the arbitrary-z corner identity (bean qou-j7ln); the convention-D case is closed and merged (91bf8fcb4).
    ocneanuNumProd, ocneanuDenProd the two products, as objects
    ocneanuNumProd_addCell the numerator gains exactly one factor
    ocneanuDenProd_addCell the denominator gains q² − 1, and every old factor shifts by one step iff its cell shares the new cell's row or column

    The asymmetry, which is the whole point #

    The numerator's proof is Finset.prod_insert and nothing else, because each factor depends only on its own cell's (i, j) and not on the diagram. The denominator cannot be done that way at all: hookLength is diagram-dependent, so every surviving factor may move. That asymmetry is why arbitrary z is harder than convention D, where the ≥3-row weights vanish, only twoRow survives, and schurPrincipal_twoRow telescopes the entire hook-content product.

    Naming — cousins, not synonyms #

    The corpus already has ocneanuNumFactor_eq_zero_of_content_neg_two and ocneanuNumFactor_conventionD. Those are about one cell's factor; the definitions here are the products over all cells. Hence …Prod, so the two families cannot be mistaken for each other.

    What remains #

    The ratio w_{ν+s} / w_ν still needs the arm/leg telescoping of the shifted factors — the if in ocneanuDenProd_addCell has to be turned into a product over s's row and column. That is the next brick and it has no two-row counterpart.

    Scope (§7c) #

    Generic [Field R] — no ordering, nothing archimedean, no ℝ.
