    QOU.SchurWeyl.HeckeCoset — the coset spanning bound dim H_{n+1}(q) ≤ (n+1)! (bean qou-uoal) #

    The Iwahori–Hecke basis theorem dim_K H_n(q) = n! has two halves; this file establishes the spanning half in general n (upper bound), via the tower coset decomposition. With the QOU indexing Hecke K q qi n = H_{n+1}(q) (the algebra on n generators σ_0,…,σ_{n-1}), the statement proved here is

    Module.finrank K (Hecke K q qi n) ≤ (n+1)!.

    Strategy — coset decomposition of the tower #

    H_{n+1}(q) = ⨁_{k} H_n(q) · r_k over the n+2 (classically n+2 = [H_{n+2}:H_{n+1}]) coset representatives

    r_0 = 1, r_j = σ_n σ_{n-1} ⋯ σ_{n-j+1} (j = 1,…,n+1),

    i.e. r_j is the descending product of the top j generators. Right multiplication by any generator preserves the subspace Σ_k ι(H_n)·r_k (ι = heckeIncl, the tower inclusion H_n ↪ H_{n+1}), so that subspace is a right ideal containing 1, hence all of H_{n+1}. Iterating gives a spanning family indexed by cosetIndex n of cardinality (n+1)!.

    Handedness. The family carries the subalgebra on the left (ι(H_n)·r_j), so the induction must be taken on the right — right multiplication acts on the r_j factor, where the Hecke relations apply. An earlier left-handed version of the closure lemma left the top generator open as a sorry, because σ_n · ι(w) · r_j offers no relation with which to move σ_n past the opaque ι(w). On the correct side the same statement is a four-case computation; see span_mul_gen_mem. Nothing deeper was needed (bean qou-j6f9).

    What is delivered here #

    • descProd m j — the descending product σ_m σ_{m-1} ⋯ σ_{m-j+1} of length j in H_{m+1}(q) = Hecke K q qi (m+1) (the coset representative r_j).
    • descProd_mul_σ_of_add_lt / _of_add_eq / _of_add_eq_succ / _of_le_add — the four cases of the bubble: r_j σ_i equals σ_i r_j (far commutation), r_{j+1} (lengthening), (q-qi)·r_j + (q·qi)·r_{j-1} (quadratic), or σ_{i-1} r_j (braid), selected by comparing i + j with m. All sorry-free.
    • cosetIndex m, cosetFam m — the recursively-built spanning family and its finite index type, with Fintype.card (cosetIndex m) = (m+1)! (card_cosetIndex, sorry-free).
    • one_mem_span_cosetFam — 1 is in the span of the family (sorry-free).
    • finrank_le_card_of_span — the generic finite-spanning ⇒ finrank ≤ card reduction (sorry-free).
    • incl_mul_descProd_mem — coset absorption: ι(w)·r_k stays in the span.
    • span_mul_gen_mem — right-multiplication by a generator preserves the span (sorry-free). This is the general-n form of the twelve explicit HeckeDim3.gen_mul_mem reductions at n = 3.
    • span_cosetFam_eq_top — the spanning statement, from span_mul_gen_mem via the adjoin = span-of-submonoid route (Submonoid.closure_induction_right).
    • finrank_le_factorial — the headline bound finrank K (Hecke K q qi n) ≤ (n+1)!, proved from span_cosetFam_eq_top. Sorry-free; #print axioms gives [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound].

    The whole file is sorry-free: this settles the spanning half of the Iwahori–Hecke basis theorem in general n. The complementary linear-independence half (dim ≥ n!) is not claimed here — at n = 3 it is HeckeDim3Indep.

    Base ring: generic [CommRing K] for the algebraic data; [Nontrivial K] only for the finrank bound (StrongRankCondition), per §7c.

      Right multiplication of a coset representative by a generator #

      These four lemmas are the entire content of the coset "bubble". Writing r_j = descProd m j = σ_m σ_{m-1} ⋯ σ_{m-j+1} (so r_j occupies generator indices [m-j+1, m], and r_0 = 1), the product r_j σ_i re-expresses as

      case condition r_j · σ_i
      far commutation i + j + 1 ≤ m σ_i · r_j
      lengthening i + j = m r_{j+1}
      quadratic i + j = m + 1 (q-qi)·r_j + (q·qi)·r_{j-1}
      braid m + 2 ≤ i + j σ_{i-1} · r_j

      In every case the result is (a generator, or a scalar) · r_k — i.e. it lands back in Σ_k ι(H_m)·r_k, which is exactly what closes the span argument.

      Handedness is the crux. The spanning family is ι(H_m)·r_j, with the subalgebra on the left; so it is right multiplication by a generator that re-enters the family, and the induction must be taken on the right (Submonoid.closure_induction_right). Left multiplication by the top generator does not reduce this way — σ_m · ι(w) · r_j offers no way to move σ_m past ι(w) — which is why the left-handed formulation of this lemma looked like the deep half of the basis theorem.

          theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.HeckeCoset.span_mul_gen_mem {K : Type u_1} [CommRing K] {q qi : K} (m : ℕ) (i : Fin m) (b : Hecke K q qi m) :
          b ∈ Submodule.span K (Set.range (cosetFam m)) → b * Hecke.σ K q qi m i ∈ Submodule.span K (Set.range (cosetFam m))

          The coset-span closure lemma (the crux) — right-handed. Right multiplication by any generator σ_i of H_{m+1}(q) = Hecke K q qi m preserves the K-span of the coset family cosetFam m.

          This is the general-n form of the twelve explicit HeckeDim3.gen_mul_mem reductions at n = 3, and it is sorry-free: each family element ι(w)·r_j meets σ_i in exactly one of the four cases of the bubble (descProd_mul_σ_of_add_lt / _of_add_eq / _of_add_eq_succ / _of_le_add, selected by comparing i + j with m), and every case returns either a family element or ι(w·σ_k)·r_j with the level-m statement available by recursion.

          Why right and not left. The family is ι(H_m)·r_j, with the subalgebra on the left. Right multiplication therefore acts on the r_j factor, where the Hecke relations apply; left multiplication by the top generator would have to move σ_m past the opaque ι(w), which no relation does. Taking the induction on the correct side is what turns this from "the deep half of the basis theorem" into a four-case computation.
