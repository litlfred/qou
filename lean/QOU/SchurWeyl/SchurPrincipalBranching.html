    QOU.SchurWeyl.SchurPrincipalBranching — the box-addition ratio for schurPrincipal #

    The last assembly. schurPrincipal_mul_const writes

    s_μ · C(r) = Q^{n(μ)} · P_μ, P_μ = ∏_{i<j<r}(1 − Q^{β_i−β_j}),

    with C(r) independent of μ. Adding a box at row a changes P only in the pairs involving a, and the two boundary factors are exactly the q-ratio's (QRatioBetaFactors). Putting those together gives the box-addition ratio.
    betaPairProd Q r μ P_μ, in interval indexing
    levelConst Q r C(r), the μ-independent constant
    rowColProd Q r a μ the row-a and column-a factors of P_μ
    betaPairProd_addCell P_ν · (row·col)_μ = P_μ · (row·col)_ν
    schurPrincipal_addCell_mul the box-addition ratio, cross-multiplied
    qratio_prod_eq_rowColProd the q-ratio is Q^a · (row·col)_ν/(row·col)_μ
    schurPrincipal_addCell_eq s_ν = (q-ratio) · s_μ — the branching hypothesis itself
    qratio_prod_eq_zero_of_not_addable a row that cannot take a box contributes 0
    isAddableCell_rowEnd_iff row a takes a box ⟺ a = 0 or μ_a < μ_{a−1}

    The headline is stated division-free on purpose: as a cross-multiplied equation it needs no non-vanishing hypothesis beyond the two the closed form already carries, so it stays true at the roots of unity where the quotient form is 0/0. The quotient form is a corollary for whoever supplies the genericity.

          The q-ratio is Q^a times the row-column ratio.

          ∏_{j ≠ a} (Q·x_a − x_j)/(x_a − x_j) = Q^a · (row·col)_ν / (row·col)_μ, x_i = Q^{β_i}.

          Unconditional beyond Q ≠ 0: a product of quotients is the quotient of the products in any field, and the two QRatioBetaFactors lemmas supply the factorwise identity — j > a exactly, j < a with one Q. The a copies of Q from the rows above are what becomes Q^a.

          s_{μ+□ at row a}(1,…,Q^{r−1}) = (∏_{j ≠ a} (Q·x_a − x_j)/(x_a − x_j)) · s_μ(1,…,Q^{r−1}), x_i = Q^{β_i}.

          The two extra hypotheses over the cross-multiplied form are the price of dividing: levelConst and the row-column product must be invertible.           theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.qratio_prod_eq_zero_of_not_addable {F : Type u_1} [Field F] (Q : F) {r a : ℕ} {μ : YoungDiagram} (hpos : 0 < a) (ha : a < r) (heq : μ.rowLen (a - 1) = μ.rowLen a) :
          ∏ j ∈ (Finset.range r).erase a, (Q * Q ^ betaR r μ a - Q ^ betaR r μ j) = 0

          A row that cannot take a box contributes nothing.

          Row a > 0 is addable exactly when μ_{a−1} > μ_a. When instead μ_{a−1} = μ_a, the β-numbers satisfy β_{a−1} = β_a + 1, so Q·x_a = x_{a−1} and the j = a−1 factor of the q-ratio's numerator is zero.

          This is what lets a sum over all r rows stand in for a sum over the addable ones — the schurPrincipal-level counterpart of QOU.Mathlib.vandPairProd_update_eq_zero, and the reason the r = 2 chain's rowLen_two_addCell_row2 needs no general-r analogue.
