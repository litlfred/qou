    QOU.SchurWeyl.SchurPrincipalBetaForm — both sides of schurPrincipal in β-form #

    ContentProductRows and SYTBetaGapVandermonde express the numerator and the denominator of QBeta.WenzlWeight.schurPrincipal in terms of descFactF at the β-numbers. They index differently — the hook side by a List over range |l|, the content side by a Finset over range r — and they measure β against different things. This file lands both on one common form, betaR.
    paddedRowLens r μ μ.rowLens extended with empty rows to length r
    betaR r μ i μ_i + (r − 1 − i), the β-number at r rows
    betaNum_paddedRowLens betaNum (paddedRowLens r μ) i = betaR r μ i
    hookSide_betaR ∏_c f(h(c)) · ∏_{i<j<r} f(β_i − β_j) = ∏_{i<r} descFactF f β_i
    contentSide_betaR ∏_c f(r − c₁ + c₂) · ∏_{i<r} descFactF f (r−1−i) = ∏_{i<r} descFactF f β_i
    contentProd_mul_descFactF_eq the two, equated — the ∏_i descFactF f β_i cancels
    schurPrincipal_mul_const s_μ · C = Q^{n(μ)} · ∏_{i<j<r}(1 − Q^{β_i−β_j}), C independent of μ
    betaR_lt the β-numbers strictly decrease across the r rows
    nPart_addCell n(μ + □) = n(μ) + row index

    The last row is the point: the right-hand sides are the same product, so the numerator over the denominator is the Vandermonde ∏_{i<j} f(β_i − β_j) over the μ-independent constant ∏_{i<r} descFactF f (r−1−i). In a ratio of two diagrams that constant cancels outright.

        The closed form #

        source
        theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.schurPrincipal_mul_const {R : Type u_2} [Field R] (Q : R) {r : ℕ} {μ : YoungDiagram} (hr : μ.colLen 0 ≤ r) (hD : ∏ c ∈ μ.cells, (1 - Q ^ QBeta.WenzlWeight.hook μ c) ≠ 0) :
        QBeta.WenzlWeight.schurPrincipal r Q μ * ∏ i ∈ Finset.range r, Mathlib.SYTBeta.descFactF (fun (n : ℕ) => 1 - Q ^ n) (r - 1 - i) = Q ^ QBeta.WenzlWeight.nPart μ * ∏ i ∈ Finset.range r, ∏ k ∈ Finset.range (r - (i + 1)), (1 - Q ^ (betaR r μ i - betaR r μ (i + 1 + k)))

        schurPrincipal in Vandermonde form, division-free.

        s_μ(1,Q,…,Q^{r−1}) · ∏_{i<r} descFactF (1−Qⁿ) (r−1−i) = Q^{n(μ)} · ∏_{i<j<r} (1 − Q^{β_i−β_j}).

        The left-hand constant does not depend on μ — it is the empty-partition contribution — so in a ratio of two diagrams it cancels outright, leaving Q^{n(ν)−n(μ)} times a ratio of Vandermondes. That is the branching ratio, and the remaining work is to identify ∏_{i<j}(1 − Q^{β_i−β_j}) with vandPairProd r (fun i => Q^{β_i}) up to a monomial.

        The only hypothesis beyond μ having at most r rows is that the hook product is nonzero — the same genericity schurPrincipal_eq_zero_iff already carries, and exactly what makes the schurPrincipal quotient well-posed in the first place.

        Two facts for the branching ratio #

        With schurPrincipal_mul_const in hand the branching ratio is

        s_ν/s_μ = Q^{n(ν)−n(μ)} · P(β')/P(β), P(β) = ∏_{i<j<r}(1 − Q^{β_i−β_j}), β' = β + e_a,

        and the μ-independent C has already cancelled. Two ingredients remain, and both are here.

        No monomial bookkeeping is needed on this route. Going through vandPairProd would introduce (−1)^{C(r,2)} and ∏_{i<j} x_i; comparing P(β')/P(β) against the q-ratio directly does not. Per pair, with x_i = Q^{β_i}:

        • j > a (so β_j < β_a): (Q·x_a − x_j)/(x_a − x_j) = (1 − Q^{β_a+1−β_j})/(1 − Q^{β_a−β_j}) after cancelling Q^{β_j} — an exact match, no leftover factor;
        • j < a (so β_j > β_a): the same cancellation of Q^{β_a} leaves an extra Q.

        So the q-ratio is Q^{#{j < a}} = Q^a times P(β')/P(β) — and Q^a is exactly what nPart_addCell supplies.
