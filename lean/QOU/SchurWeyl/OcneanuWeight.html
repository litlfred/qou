    QOU.SchurWeyl.OcneanuWeight — the Ocneanu/Markov weight at general n #

    HeckeWedderburnData realises the Markov trace on H_n(q) as a weighted character sum tr_M = Σ_λ w_λ χ_λ. HeckeMarkov3 proves the three weights at n = 3 (markovWeight0/1/2, derive-then-check against the normalisation and the two Markov relations); HeckeWedderburnDataGeneral supplies the block decomposition at every n but leaves the weight family an explicit parameter, because what makes a family the Ocneanu one is the Markov condition, and that is proved only at n = 3.

    This file supplies the missing ingredient's definition: a single closed form ocneanuWeight q z μ, one formula for every Young diagram. That it reproduces markovWeight0/1/2 exactly at n = 3 is proved in OcneanuWeightMarkov3, which is a separate file because those comparisons are ℝ-valued (§7c below) while everything here is generic.

    The formula #

    For a Young diagram μ,

    ocneanuWeight q z μ  =  ∏_{(i,j) ∈ μ} ( q^{2i}·(q² − 1 − q·z) + q^{2j+1}·z )
                      / ∏_{(i,j) ∈ μ} ( q^{2·h(i,j)} − 1 )

    with h the hook length. Numerator: one factor per cell, affine in the Markov parameter z, with a framing monomial in the row index. Denominator: the q²-hook product. Pulling the row index out of the numerator gives the equivalent form q^{2n(λ)}·∏_x ((q²−1) + zq(q^{2c(x)}−1)) / ∏_x (q^{2h(x)}−1), with c the cell content and n(λ) = Σ_i i·λ_i.

    Provenance — derived, not recalled #

    The corpus already carried a candidate general-n weight (computations/mass_at_3A_proper.py::jones_wenzl_weight). It is wrong: it fails to reproduce the proved n = 3 weights under any of 160 convention combinations, and never sums to 1 (computations/schurweyl/ocneanu_weight_validation.py). The formula above was therefore derived rather than recalled, in exact ℚ(q,z) (computations/schurweyl/ocneanu_weight_general_n.py): solve n = 2 from tr(1) = 1 and tr(σ₁) = z; read n ≤ 3 as a per-cell content product over the hook denominator; then solve n = 4, 5 from scratch to pin the framing monomial, which n ≤ 3 cannot see — every partition of n ≤ 3 has distinct cell contents, and the first repeat is the two content-0 cells of (2,2).

    Numerically validated in exact arithmetic: Σ_λ d_λ w_λ = 1 and Σ_λ w_λ χ^λ(σ₁) = z through n = 6, and the branching identity w_μ^{(n)} = Σ_{λ = μ + □} w_λ^{(n+1)} through n = 5 → 6.

    What is NOT claimed here #

    OcneanuWeightMarkov3's n = 3 specialisations are the only Lean-proved content about this formula. The Markov property at general n is open. Nothing in either file instantiates HeckeWedderburnData with ocneanuWeight — doing so would let a downstream consumer read the resulting functional as a Markov trace on the strength of a numerical check, which is exactly the laundering HeckeWedderburnDataGeneral's docstring warns against. The general-n Markov property has to be proved before that instantiation is honest.

    Base ring (§7c) #

    ocneanuWeight is stated over a generic [Field R]: it is a rational function of q and z with no ordering, no Real.sqrt, no archimedean content — this file mentions ℝ nowhere. The specialisation to ℝ is confined to OcneanuWeightMarkov3, and is forced only because markovWeight0/1/2 are ℝ-valued.

    References: Jones (1987) [jones1987], Wenzl (1988) [wenzl1988], Ram (1997) [ram1997].

        The convention-D Markov parameter truncates the support #

        Setting the per-cell numerator factor of a cell of content c to zero and solving for z gives z = q^{2k+1} / (1 + q² + ⋯ + q^{2k}) for c = −k (and no solution at c = 0, where the factor is the constant q² − 1). The k = 2 member of that family is z = q³/(q²+1) — which is exactly the Markov parameter of convention D, made canonical by the owner ruling of 2026-08-15.

        So at convention D every Young diagram with three or more rows carries weight zero: such a diagram contains the cell (2,0), of content −2. The k = 1 member z = q likewise kills every diagram with two or more rows. Convention C's z = √q/(q+1) is not a member of the family at any integer content, and truncates nothing.

        Vanishing on the partitions of three or more rows is the condition for a trace on H_n(q) to factor through its Temperley–Lieb quotient; that reading is standard (Jones (1983), Wenzl (1988)) and is not proved here. What is proved here is the vanishing itself.

        This DUPLICATES existing corpus content, and is kept for one specific reason. QOU.QBeta.WenzlWeight.schurPrincipal_eq_zero_iff (sorry-free, general n, general level r) already proves the vanishing criterion, and wenzlLevel_conventionD already proves that convention D is level r = 2. I derived the statement below independently and only then grepped the corpus — the wrong order, and the corpus-grep discipline exists precisely to catch it. The theorem is retained because its hypotheses are strictly weaker: it needs no root-of-unity condition on q, where the iff does. The sharper iff, and the identification of the two weight formulas that makes the duplication visible, are in OcneanuWeightWenzl: ocneanuWeight q (q³/(q²+1)) μ = schurPrincipal 2 (q²) μ / (q²+1)^{|μ|}.

        Cross-checked against computations/ocneanu_weights.py::omega_lambda_via_inversion, an independent p(n) × p(n) linear solve at the live q₀ and this z: at n = 2 … 5 exactly the diagrams with three or more rows come back zero, and every other weight agrees with the closed form to the limit of the float64 arithmetic that path uses (worst 1.5e-16, which cannot distinguish agreement from a discrepancy below that scale).

        At the convention-D Markov parameter z = q³/(q²+1), every partition with three or more rows has weight zero.

        The hypothesis 0 < μ.rowLen 2 says the third row is nonempty, i.e. μ has at least three rows; equivalently (2,0) ∈ μ, a cell of content −2, whose numerator factor vanishes at this z.

        The z-dependence lives only off the diagonal #

        The numerator factor of a cell is affine in z; its z-coefficient is q^{2j+1} − q^{2i+1}, which vanishes identically on the diagonal i = j — with no hypothesis on q whatever, since the two z terms cancel term-for-term. So a diagonal cell contributes a constant, and the whole z-dependence of ocneanuWeight is carried by the off-diagonal cells.

        Counting them gives the degree: a Young diagram has d(μ) diagonal cells (its Durfee square size), so the numerator has z-degree at most |μ| − d(μ). Since every non-empty diagram contains (0,0), d ≥ 1 always, and the naive bound |μ| from |μ| affine factors is never attained.

        Why this matters, and where it is used. The span of the Markov traces over the whole Ocneanu family {z_N} is bounded by that degree plus one: writing W(z) = Σ_k z^k c_k, the family lies in the span of the coefficient vectors c_k. Measured against p(n) — the number of Wedderburn blocks, i.e. the dimension of the full space of trace functionals — this is a strong restriction, and it is what forbids recovering an individual block's character by any subtraction over the family.

        computations/probes/markov_trace_family_rank_probe.py measures the consequences: deg_z = |μ| − d(μ) for every partition at n = 2..6 (exact, sympy over ℚ(q)), a resulting family rank of exactly n against p(n) blocks, and — at n = 6 — only the two one-dimensional blocks individually recoverable, (4,2) not among them despite being visible to convention D.

        What is proved here is the mechanism, not the degree count. The statement below confines the z-dependence to the off-diagonal cells, which is the content; turning that into deg_z ≤ |μ| − d(μ) needs the polynomial-degree argument over the product and is not done here. Nor is rank = n proved — it is measured at n = 2..7, so the exclusion it supports is scope-(a).

        theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.mem_cells_diag_zero (mu : YoungDiagram) (h : 0 < mu.rowLen 0) :
        (0, 0) ∈ mu.cells

        Every non-empty diagram has a diagonal cell, namely (0,0). So d(μ) ≥ 1 and the top z-power z^{|μ|} always dies — the naive degree bound from |μ| affine factors is never attained.

        The degree bound, as a polynomial in z #

        Packaging the numerator as an element of R[X] turns the split above into an actual degree statement: the product's degree is at most the sum of its factors' degrees, each diagonal factor is a constant, and each off-diagonal factor is linear. So

        deg_z (numerator)  ≤  #{cells off the diagonal}  =  |μ| − d(μ)

        with d(μ) the Durfee square size. This is the ≤ direction, which is the one the span argument needs: W(z) = Σ_{k ≤ deg} z^k c_k lies in the span of deg + 1 fixed coefficient vectors, so the whole Ocneanu family spans at most that many dimensions of the p(n)-dimensional space of trace functionals.

        Equality (deg_z = |μ| − d(μ) exactly) is not proved here — it is measured, exactly and symbolically over ℚ(q), at n = 2..6 in computations/probes/markov_trace_family_rank_probe.py, along with the resulting family rank of exactly n. Neither equality nor rank = n is a theorem for all n.

          The span bound — why the degree bound restricts the whole family #

          The degree bound is what limits the family. A trace functional in the Ocneanu family is the weight vector W(z) read at one z; if each component is a polynomial of degree ≤ d, then W(z) = Σ_{k ≤ d} z^k c_k for d+1 fixed coefficient vectors, so every member of the family — at every z, not merely the sampled ones — lies in the span of those d+1 vectors.

          Combined with deg_z ≤ |μ| − d(μ) ≤ |μ| − 1, the family at level n spans at most n dimensions, against the p(n) dimensions of the full space of trace functionals. That gap is what forbids isolating an individual block's character by subtraction over the family.

          The first two lemmas are stated for an arbitrary finite family of polynomials, since nothing about the Ocneanu weight is used. ocneanuFamily_finrank_le is the application.

          The lower bound: the n hooks are linearly independent, so rank = n #

          ocneanuFamily_finrank_le gives rank ≤ n. This section supplies ≥ n by exhibiting n members of the family that are linearly independent — the hook diagrams (n−k, 1^k), k = 0..n−1. Each hook has one diagonal cell, n−k−1 arm cells and k leg cells, so its numerator is (q²−1)·∏ arm_j·∏ leg_i, a product of n−1 linear factors with known roots.

          The mechanism is that the leg roots are the Ocneanu family itself: the i-th leg factor vanishes at z_i = q^{2i}(q²−1)/(q(q^{2i}−1)) = q^i/[i]_q. So hook k vanishes at z_m exactly when m ≤ k — which is the row cap, read as "a diagram with k+1 rows is invisible at every level ≤ k" — and evaluating hooks 0..n−1 at z_1..z_n gives a triangular system with nonzero diagonal. The row cap and the rank are one phenomenon.

          The genericity conditions on q (leg roots distinct; arm roots off the leg roots; q² ≠ 1) are stated as hypotheses, not proved: they hold when q is not a root of unity of small order, and hooks_linearIndependent is exactly as conditional as they are. Measured exactly over ℚ(q) at n = 2..6 in markov_trace_family_rank_probe.py; the leg-root/Ocneanu identification was verified there symbolically before this was written.

          hookNum is the hook numerator written out directly. Its identification with ocneanuNumPoly q μ for the hook diagram μ is not proved here — it requires computing YoungDiagram.cells of a specific shape and is a separate plumbing step.
