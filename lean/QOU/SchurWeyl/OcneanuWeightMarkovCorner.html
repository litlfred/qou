    QOU.SchurWeyl.OcneanuWeightMarkovCorner — the Markov condition's arithmetic core #

    The general-n Markov condition tr(x·σ_n) = z·tr(x) is the one statement standing between this corpus and its last external citation (see OcneanuWeightWenzl). It reduces to a finite identity in the corner contents of a single partition, and this file proves that identity at convention D.

    The reduction, and what it costs #

    For x ∈ H_n decompose V_λ = ⊕_{μ = λ − □} V_μ and set A_μ = P_μ σ_n P_μ. Then A_μ commutes with H_{n−1}; Hecke branching is multiplicity-free, so the commutant of H_{n−1} in End(V_μ) is the Gelfand–Tsetlin algebra and A_μ is diagonal in the seminormal basis, with (T,T) entry the Hoefsmit parameter seminormalParam q (c(λ/μ) − c_T(n)). Since H_n = ⊕_μ End(V_μ) the diagonal entries x_{TT} range freely, so coefficients may be matched pointwise; and n being T's largest entry, its cell is a removable corner of μ. The condition collapses to

    for every μ ⊢ n and every removable corner r of μ: ∑_{s addable corner of μ} w_{μ+s} · a(c(s) − c(r)) = z · w_μ.

    Update — the structural half of that reduction IS now formalised, in SeminormalBlockTrace, and by a much shorter route than the one described above: genMat has exactly two nonzero entries per column, so σ_k's off-diagonal crossing blocks is immediate and needs no Okounkov–Vershik argument at all. Read the justification below as the heavier route it is; SeminormalBlockTrace's docstring records the correction.

    Update 2 — the assembly is now formalised too, in MarkovAssembly: markov_of_corner_identity proves the Markov condition for the weighted seminormal trace from the per-corner identity, taken as an explicit hypothesis, at every m. So the reduction described below is no longer paper-only — what remains outside Lean is the corner identity at general n (this file proves it at convention D for the ≤2-row diagrams). Read the caveat below with that narrowing: it was written when the whole reduction was on paper.

    🛑 That reduction is NOT formalised, and this file does not formalise it. It is derived on paper (computations/schurweyl/ocneanu_markov_corner_reduction.py, which also tests the resulting identity exactly at symbolic z for every partition of n ≤ 6). Steps (i) and (ii) — multiplicity-freeness of branching, and the Hoefsmit entry — are what seminormalRep encodes, but neither is proved in Lean. So the theorems below do not prove the Markov condition. They prove its arithmetic core; the remaining gap is representation-theoretic, not arithmetic, and that is a different and more tractable shape than the original statement. Bean qou-uer7.

    What is proved here #

    At convention D the trace lives on the ≤2-row diagrams, and μ = twoRow m k has at most two removable corners — (0,m−1) of content m−1 (present iff m > k) and (1,k−1) of content k−2 (present iff k > 0) — and at most two addable corners of nonzero weight, (0,m) of content m and (1,k) of content k−1; the third, (2,0), has weight zero. So the identity is two equations:

    removable corner statement
    markov_corner_row0 (0,m−1), needs k < m w_{(m+1,k)}·a(1) + w_{(m,k+1)}·a(k−m) = z_D·w_{(m,k)}
    markov_corner_row1 (1,k−1), corner needs k > 0 w_{(m+1,k)}·a(m−k+2) + w_{(m,k+1)}·a(1) = z_D·w_{(m,k)}
    markov_corner_row1_diag as above at k = m one term; twoRow m (m+1) is not a partition

    markov_corner_row1 is stated without 0 < k because the algebra does not need it — but at k = 0 row 1 has no removable corner, so there the identity corresponds to no corner and carries no Markov content.

    Both collapse, against the proved bracket form schurPrincipal_twoRow, to elementary geometric-sum facts: row 0 to [d+2] − 1 = x·[d+1] and row 1 to [d+1] = [d] + x^d.
