    QOU.SchurWeyl.HeckeMatsumoto — T_w does not depend on the reduced word #

    HeckeStandardBasis defines T_w := stdElt m w as the product of the generators along one particular reduced word for w ∈ S_{m+1}. This file proves Matsumoto's theorem for the Iwahori–Hecke algebra: the product along any reduced word for w is T_w (prod_σ_of_reduced), so two reduced words for the same permutation give the same element (matsumoto).

    Proof #

    The key step is stdElt_mul_σ_of_ascent:

    T_w · σ_i = T_{w s_i}   whenever ninv (w s_i) = ninv w + 1.

    It is proved on the coset family, by induction on m, with the four cases of the coset bubble (HeckeCoset.descProd_mul_σ_*). For w = ι(a) · c_j (with c_j the descending coset representative):

    • far commutation and braid: c_j s_i = s_k c_j with s_k in the smaller group, so the ascent passes to a at k and the induction hypothesis applies;
    • lengthening: c_j s_i = c_{j+1}, which is again a coset representative;
    • quadratic: c_j s_i = c_{j-1}, which loses an inversion, so this case cannot be an ascent.

    The permutation identities needed are obtained from the Hecke ones by specialising at q = qi = 1: Hecke.lift sends σ_i ↦ s_i into the group algebra ℤ[S_{m+1}] (specialise), and MonoidAlgebra.of is injective. So nothing about permutations is re-proved except the braid and commutation relations of the adjacent transpositions.

    Then prod_σ_of_reduced is induction along the word: a prefix of a reduced word is reduced, and each appended letter is an ascent.

    Base ring (§7c) #

    Generic [CommRing K]; the specialisation uses ℤ, on the permutation side only.
