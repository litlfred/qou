    QOU.SchurWeyl.SeminormalInequivalence — pairwise inequivalence of the ρ_λ (q-deformed) #

    The second half of SW-5 (bean qou-jfr8). Irreducibility of each ρ_λ is the range statement AlgHom.range (seminormalRep …) = ⊤; this file starts the companion claim that the family is pairwise inequivalent — ρ_λ ≇ ρ_ν for λ ≠ ν — which is what makes {ρ_λ : λ ⊢ n} the list of irreducibles rather than merely a supply of them.

    This is the q-deformed statement, and it is not the one already in the corpus #

    QOU.BraidKnot.SpechtClassification.specht_pairwise_noniso proves pairwise non-isomorphism for the Specht modules over k[S_n] at q = 1, as Representation k (Perm (Fin n)), by the dominance-order route. The object here is different: an algebra map Hecke ℝ q q⁻¹ n →ₐ[ℝ] Matrix (SYT μ) (SYT μ) ℝ at generic q. Neither statement implies the other, and no dominance order is needed on this side — the q-Jucys–Murphy diagonalisation (seminormalRep_qJM) exposes the shape directly in the spectrum.

    The q = 1 file is still the precedent for the Lean shape: it declares its own intertwiner predicate rather than reaching for FDRep, to avoid Module.Finite plumbing before any mathematical content is reachable. MatRepEquiv below is the algebra-hom analogue.

    What is proved here, and what it does not give #

    seminormalRep_not_matRepEquiv_of_contentCoshSum_ne is a conditional separation: two shapes whose content-cosh sums differ carry inequivalent representations. It is exact and cheap — the bar-symmetrised sum Θ is central, ρ_λ(Θ) is the scalar cellContentCoshSum λ q (seminormalRep_barSymJMSum), and a scalar is a similarity invariant.

    It is not the unconditional statement. At a fixed real q the cosh-sum is a single number, and nothing here rules out two distinct shapes colliding on it. As Laurent polynomials in q the sums differ — distinct shapes have distinct content multisets and the coefficients are positive — so they can agree for at most finitely many q; but "at most finitely many" is not "not at this one".

    contentCount_eq_of_matRepEquiv closes that gap: an equivalence forces the two shapes to have the same number of cells of every content. The route is not the one the plan anticipated — no per-generator eigenvalue multiset, no characteristic polynomial, no Newton identities. Summing over k first makes the diagonal constant (the cell sweep), so Σ_k L_k^m acts by a scalar for every m and the same scalar_eq applies verbatim; the family of scalars is then a polynomial identity in disguise.

    youngDiagram_eq_of_contentCount_eq then closes it, and needs neither Frobenius coordinates nor an induction on the diagram: each diagonal of a Young diagram is downward-closed, so its length already decides membership, and the diagonal lengths are exactly the content multiplicities.

    So seminormalRep_not_matRepEquiv_of_ne is unconditional: distinct shapes of the same size carry inequivalent representations, with no hypothesis beyond μ ≠ ν and the genericity seminormalRep already requires. The conditional seminormalRep_not_matRepEquiv_of_contentCoshSum_ne is kept as the cheap criterion it is — one scalar, checkable per pair — but it is no longer the strongest statement here.

    The unconditional claim is nonetheless true: H_n(q) over ℝ is semisimple whenever q is not a root of unity of order ≤ n, which q ≠ 0 with q ^ 2 ≠ 1 supplies, and the ρ_λ are then a complete pairwise-inequivalent family. What is open is the formalisation, not the fact.

    Refs:

    • [ram1997] Ram, Seminormal representations of Weyl groups and Iwahori–Hecke algebras, https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024611597000373
    • [wenzl1988] Wenzl, Hecke algebras of type A_n and subfactors — semisimplicity away from roots of unity.

    The seminormal application #

    source
    theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.seminormalRep_not_matRepEquiv_of_contentCoshSum_ne {μ ν : YoungDiagram} {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] (q : K) (hq0 : q ≠ 0) (hq2 : GenericParameter.NotRootOfUnity q) (n : ℕ) (hμ : μ.cells.card = n + 1) (hν : ν.cells.card = n + 1) (hne : ∑ c ∈ ν.cells, (q ^ (2 * cellContent c) + q ^ (-(2 * cellContent c))) ≠ ∑ c ∈ μ.cells, (q ^ (2 * cellContent c) + q ^ (-(2 * cellContent c)))) :
    ¬MatRepEquiv (SeminormalRep.seminormalRep q hq0 hq2 n hμ) (SeminormalRep.seminormalRep q hq0 hq2 n hν)

    Shapes with different content-cosh sums carry inequivalent representations.

    Θ = barSymJMSum is central, and seminormalRep_barSymJMSum evaluates it to the scalar Σ_{c ∈ μ} (q^{2c} + q^{−2c}). By MatRepEquiv.scalar_eq an equivalence would force the two sums to agree.

    Read the hypothesis, not the name. This separates exactly the pairs of shapes whose sums differ at this q. It is not μ ≠ ν → ρ_μ ≇ ρ_ν; see the module docstring for why one scalar cannot deliver that, and what does.

    Stage 2 — the whole power-sum family, not one scalar #

    scalar_eq above compares a single number and is therefore conditional. What removes the condition is that the same argument runs for every power m: the element Σ_k L_k^m is again central-acting (its diagonal is constant, by the cell sweep), so an equivalence forces the content power sums to agree at every m.

    That family is what pins the shape. Writing t = q^{2m}, the identity says the two content generating functions Σ_{c ∈ μ} t^c and Σ_{c ∈ ν} t^c agree at every point of {q^2, q^4, q^6, …}. Because q ≠ 0 and q^2 ≠ 1, those points are pairwise distinct — q^2 is positive and ≠ 1, so its powers are strictly monotone — so the difference is a Laurent polynomial with infinitely many roots, hence identically zero, hence the two content multisets coincide. This is the step that a single scalar cannot reach, and it uses the genericity hypothesis for something other than zpow injectivity.

      What remains between this and μ = ν is the classical fact that a Young diagram is determined by its content multiset — stated in the module header as the one open step, and deliberately not assumed here.

      Content multiset determines the diagram #

      The last step, and it does not need Frobenius coordinates or an induction on the diagram. The observation that collapses it: each diagonal of a Young diagram is downward-closed, so its length already decides membership. Writing diagIndex (i,j) = min i j for the position of a cell along its own diagonal, a cell lies in μ exactly when its diagonal index is below the length of that diagonal — and the diagonal lengths are precisely the content multiplicities.

      So equal content counts give equal diagonal lengths, which give the same membership predicate, which is the same diagram.

          The unconditional statement #

          Pairwise inequivalence of the q-deformed seminormal representations — unconditional.

          Distinct shapes of the same size carry inequivalent representations. No hypothesis on the shapes beyond μ ≠ ν, and none on q beyond the genericity seminormalRep already requires.

          The chain, all sorry-free: an equivalence makes Σ_k L_k^m act by the same scalar for every m (contentPowSum_eq_of_matRepEquiv); that family of coincidences is a polynomial identity in disguise, because q ^ 2 ≠ 1 makes the evaluation points q^2, q^4, … distinct (contentCount_eq_of_matRepEquiv); and a Young diagram is determined by its content multiset, because its diagonals are downward-closed (youngDiagram_eq_of_contentCount_eq).

          Why the bar-symmetrised sum was never going to be the separating element #

          seminormalRep_not_matRepEquiv_of_contentCoshSum_ne above is a real criterion, but it is strictly weaker than the content-count route that closes SW-5, and the reason is worth having as a theorem rather than as a note: the bar-even bracket cannot distinguish a partition from its transpose, at any q.

          source
          theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.coshSum_transpose {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (q : K) (μ : YoungDiagram) :
          ∑ c ∈ μ.transpose.cells, (q ^ (2 * cellContent c) + q ^ (-(2 * cellContent c))) = ∑ c ∈ μ.cells, (q ^ (2 * cellContent c) + q ^ (-(2 * cellContent c)))

          The bar-symmetrised content sum is transpose-invariant. Transposing a Young diagram negates every content, and q^{2c} + q^{−2c} is even in c, so the sum is unchanged — at every q.

          Consequence: barSymJMSum acts by the same scalar on ρ_λ and ρ_{λ'}, so the cosh criterion is vacuous on every transpose pair. That is why the unconditional argument goes through the whole power-sum family and the content counts rather than through this single scalar.

          Measured in exact rational arithmetic, the collapse is total: at n = 7 the 15 partitions give only 8 distinct values of the cosh sum, and every collision is exactly a transpose pair.

          This also sharpens what cellContentCoshSum_eq_bracket records (that the bar-even brackets are not independent spectral degrees of freedom): the invariant is not merely dependent on the others, it is strictly coarser than the partition itself.
