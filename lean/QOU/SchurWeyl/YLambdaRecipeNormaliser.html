    QOU.SchurWeyl.YLambdaRecipeNormaliser — the y_λ recipe's Z_n, in closed form #

    prop:y-lambda-recipe-from-partition (y-lambda-recipe-from-partition.md) computes the Jones–Markov central coefficient in four steps. Step 3 forms the Schur principal specialisation, and step 4 divides by

    Z_n = ∑_{μ ⊢ n} f^μ · s_{μ,r}(Q),

    a sum over all partitions of n — p(n) terms, which is what makes the recipe's cost O(p(n) · max hooks).

    This file gives Z_n in closed form on the Wenzl lattice:

    Z_n = [r]_Q ^ n.

    So step 4 needs no sum at all, and y_λ is wenzlWeight r Q λ on the nose — QOU.SchurWeyl.wenzlWeight is exactly the recipe's output, not merely something shaped like it.
    sum_dim_schurPrincipal ∑_{|ν|=n} f^ν s_ν = [r]_Q^n — the recipe's Z_n
    wenzlWeight_eq_recipe y_λ = s_λ / Z_n is wenzlWeight r Q λ

    Scope — this is the ON-LATTICE half, and the recipe is OFF it #

    r is the Wenzl level and is a positive integer; Q^r = z_W/(1 − Q + z_W) pins the lattice. The recipe's own substitution is not at an integer r. It fixes

    X := q^{3/2} / (1 + q − q² − q³ + q^{3/2})

    in place of Q^r, and inverting the lattice relation gives, exactly,

    X = z_W/(1 − Q + z_W) at Q = q² with z_W = q·z_C = q^{3/2}/(q+1)

    — the convention-C parameter, Wenzl-normalised. At the substrate anchor that is an effective level r ≈ 2.5995, not an integer. So the theorems below do not apply to the recipe as written; they apply to its integer-level counterpart. Two consequences, neither of them closed here:

    • the recipe is convention-C frame, and the 2026-08-15 owner ruling makes D canonical — so it needs re-derivation, not a z-swap (bean qou-cds1 records why substituting z_D into a C-frame ladder is not a valid move);
    • the closed form appears to hold off the lattice too — ∑_{μ⊢n} f^μ s_μ(X) = ((1−X)/(1−Q))^n for a general X, symbolically verified for n = 1…6, which specialises to [r]_Q^n at X = Q^r. That general statement is not proved here: the proof below runs on the r-row β-coordinates and the r-element Lagrange sum, so it is tied to integer r and a general-X version needs a different argument. Tracked as a bean.

    theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.sum_dim_schurPrincipal {F : Type u_1} [Field F] (Q : F) (hQ0 : Q ≠ 0) (hgen : ∀ (k : ℤ), k ≠ 0 → Q ^ k ≠ 1) {r : ℕ} (hbr : levelBracket r Q ≠ 0) (hQ1 : Q ≠ 1) (n : ℕ) :
    ∑ ν : { ν : YoungDiagram // ν.cells.card = n }, ↑(Mathlib.numStandardYoungTableaux ↑ν) * QBeta.WenzlWeight.schurPrincipal r Q ↑ν = levelBracket r Q ^ n

    The recipe's Z_n, in closed form.

    ∑_{μ ⊢ n} f^μ · s_μ(1, Q, …, Q^{r−1}) = [r]_Q ^ n.

    Step 4 of prop:y-lambda-recipe-from-partition sums over every partition of n to build its normaliser; on the Wenzl lattice that sum is [r]_Q ^ n.

    This is sum_dim_wenzlWeight_eq_one with the normalisation cleared: the weight divides by [r]_Q ^ |μ| per diagram, and every diagram in the sum has |μ| = n, so the whole sum scales by the single factor [r]_Q ^ n.

    source
    theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.wenzlWeight_eq_recipe {F : Type u_1} [Field F] (Q : F) {r n : ℕ} (hbr : levelBracket r Q ≠ 0) {ν : YoungDiagram} (hν : ν.cells.card = n) :
    wenzlWeight r Q ν = QBeta.WenzlWeight.schurPrincipal r Q ν / levelBracket r Q ^ n

    The recipe's output is wenzlWeight.

    y_λ = s_λ / Z_n — step 4 of the recipe — is wenzlWeight r Q λ, once Z_n is the closed form above. So the y_lambda field of QOU.Hecke.WedderburnDecomp, whose docstring gives precisely this recipe, has a proved closed form on the Wenzl lattice rather than only a prose one.

    Stated per-diagram; hν is what ties the recipe's n to the diagram.
