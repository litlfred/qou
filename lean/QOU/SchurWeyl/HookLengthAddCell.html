    QOU.SchurWeyl.HookLengthAddCell — how hook lengths move when a cell is added #

    The first brick for the arbitrary-z corner identity (bean qou-j7ln; the convention-D case is closed, MarkovConventionD). Read off ocneanuWeight's definition, the weight is

    ∏_{x∈μ} [q^{2i}(q²−1−qz) + q^{2j+1}z] / ∏_{x∈μ} [q^{2h(x)} − 1],

    and the two halves behave completely differently when a cell is added:

    • the numerator gains exactly one factor, because each factor depends only on that cell's (i, j) and not on the diagram;
    • the denominator does not, because hookLength is diagram-dependent.

    This file is that dependence, in closed form.
    rowLen_of_isAddableCell, colLen_of_isAddableCell an addable cell sits at the end of its row and the bottom of its column
    hookLength_addCell_self the added cell's own hook is 1
    hookLength_addCell_of_mem every other cell gains 1 iff it shares the new cell's row or column, and 0 otherwise

    Why the statement is this clean #

    The naive expectation is a case split over "arm" and "leg" of s. It collapses, because for x ∈ ν the order conditions come free: if x shares s's row then x.2 < rowLen ν x.1 = s.2 automatically, and symmetrically for the column. So the hook grows by 1 on exactly the union of s's row and column, and those two sets meet only at s itself — which is not in ν.

    not_mem_of_isAddableCell_le is the one-line reason both filters vanish in hookLength_addCell_self: any cell of ν weakly below-right of s would drag s into ν by up_left_mem.

    Scope #

    Pure ℕ × ℕ combinatorics — no ring, nothing archimedean, no z and no q. What it does not do is assemble the weight ratio; that is the next brick, and the arm/leg telescoping it needs has no counterpart in the two-row case, where schurPrincipal_twoRow collapsed the whole product.
