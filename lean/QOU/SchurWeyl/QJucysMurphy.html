    QOU.SchurWeyl.QJucysMurphy — q-deformed Jucys–Murphy elements (bean SW-10 / qou-ck0g) #

    The Murphy elements (q-deformed Jucys–Murphy family) L_0, L_1, …, L_n ∈ H_{n+1}(q), defined recursively by conjugation with the Hecke generators:

    L_0 = 1, L_{k+1} = σ_k · L_k · σ_k.

    In the Hoefsmit seminormal representation ρ_λ, the element L_k acts diagonally on the basis vector indexed by a standard Young tableau T with eigenvalue q^{2·cellContent(T(k))}, where T(k) is the cell of shape λ occupied by entry k.

    What is delivered here #

    • Definition (qJM): the Murphy elements as a recursive σ-conjugation in Hecke K q qi n, generic over [CommRing K] (AGENTS.md §7c). Sorry-free.

    • Inverse Murphy elements (qJMInv): the inverse family, built from σInv. Sorry-free (definition only; the inverse proof is a Ref'd gap).

    • Per-cell algebraic identity (cosh_eq_sq_bracket_add_two): for any a, b with a·b = 1 (i.e. a = q^c, b = q^{−c}), a² + b² = (a − b)² + 2, which is the cleared-denominators form of q^{2c} + q^{−2c} = (q − q⁻¹)² · [c]_q² + 2. Sorry-free. This identity shows the two candidate bar-even brackets for g_λ coincide up to a bar-even scalar and a λ-independent shift (see bean qou-8vt2).

    • Seminormal eigenvalue — GAP: L_k acts with eigenvalue q^{2c(k)} on the seminormal basis. Ref: [ram1997] Theorem 4.1; [murphy1992]; [hoefsmit1974].

    • Commutativity — the Murphy elements commute pairwise, generating the Gelfand–Tsetlin subalgebra. Proved (qJM_commute, via qJM_commute_proved) from the braid and far-commutation relations alone. Ref: [murphy1992] §2; [murphy1981] for the q = 1 case.

    • Bar-symmetrised sum (barSymJMSum): Σ_{k=0}^{n} (L_k + L_k⁻¹), the bar-even central element whose block eigenvalue is Σ_{c ∈ λ} (q^{2c} + q^{−2c}). Definition sorry-free; centrality proved given q · qi = 1 (barSymJMSum_central); the block eigenvalue is the one remaining GAP in this file, and it is stated at generic q (0 < q, q ≠ 1) — see its docstring for why the roots-of-unity regime is excluded rather than deferred.

    Convention #

    We follow the QOU Hecke–skein convention (AGENTS.md §7a): σ_i² = (q − q⁻¹)·σ_i + 1, with eigenvalues {q, −q⁻¹}. The Murphy-element eigenvalue convention is q^{2c} (matching Ram 1997, after normalisation to the skein form). This convention is NOT q^c — the factor of 2 in the exponent comes from the skein normalisation where the quadratic has roots q, −q⁻¹ (not q, −1).

    Cite the convention — it is not stated elsewhere in this corpus, and conventions differ by q ↔ q² and by sign across the literature.

    noncomputable def QOU.SchurWeyl.qJM (K : Type u_1) [CommRing K] (q qi : K) (n a✝ : ℕ) :
    Hecke K q qi n

    The Murphy elements L_k ∈ H_{n+1}(q) (= Hecke K q qi n), defined recursively: L_0 = 1, L_{k+1} = σ_k · L_k · σ_k.

    Hecke K q qi n has n generators σ_0, …, σ_{n−1}, corresponding to the Hecke algebra H_{n+1}(q) (the symmetric group S_{n+1} has n adjacent transpositions). There are n + 1 Murphy elements L_0, …, L_n. For indices beyond n, the recursion freezes (no generator σ_k exists); those elements are semantically meaningless.

    The recursion L_{k+1} = σ_k L_k σ_k is the q-analogue of the classical Jucys–Murphy recursion in MonoidAlgebra k (Equiv.Perm (Fin n)) (see QOU.BraidKnot.jucysMurphy).

        Algebraic identity: the cosh–bracket relation (sorry-free) #

        The key identity connecting the "cosh" form q^{2c} + q^{−2c} of the bar-symmetrised JM eigenvalue to the "bracket" form (q − q⁻¹)² · [c]_q² + 2.

        In cleared-denominators form with a = q^c, b = q^{−c} (so a · b = 1):

        a² + b² = (a − b)² + 2.

        The two bar-even candidates for the per-mode rate g_λ — the cosh sum Σ_{c ∈ λ} (q^{2c} + q^{-2c}) and the bracket sum (q − q⁻¹)² · Σ [c]_q² + 2n — therefore differ only by a bar-even overall scalar (q − q⁻¹)² and a λ-independent shift 2n. The scalar is normalisation; the shift is an overall heat-trace factor. Neither is a spectral degree of freedom.

        source
        theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.cosh_eq_sq_bracket_add_two {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (a b : R) (hab : a * b = 1) :
        a ^ 2 + b ^ 2 = (a - b) ^ 2 + 2

        Cleared-denominators cosh–bracket identity (sorry-free).

        For any a, b ∈ R with a · b = 1: a² + b² = (a − b)² + 2.

        Instantiate with a := q^c, b := q^{−c} to obtain the per-cell identity q^{2c} + q^{−2c} = (q^c − q^{−c})² + 2 = (q − q⁻¹)² · [c]_q² + 2, the last step by [c]_q = (q^c − q^{−c})/(q − q⁻¹).

        Seminormal eigenvalue and commutativity (GAPs — Ref'd sorry) #

        The deep results from the Hoefsmit/Murphy/Ram theory. These cannot be proved without the general seminormal representation (itself a GAP in HoefsmitRep.lean, bean SW-4 residual). They are stated here as sorry-gapped theorems with full citations so that downstream consumers (qou-8vt2) can depend on the API.

        Eigenvalue statement (informal) #

        In the seminormal representation ρ_λ : H_{n+1}(q) →ₐ M_{d_λ}, the Murphy element L_k (= qJM K q qi n k) acts diagonally on the basis indexed by SYT of shape λ. The eigenvalue on the basis vector indexed by SYT T is

        q^{2 · cellContent(T(k))},

        where T(k) is the cell (row, col) occupied by entry k in T, and cellContent (row, col) = col − row.

        Convention alert. The q^{2c} (not q^c) arises from the QOU/skein normalisation σ² = (q − q⁻¹)σ + 1 with eigenvalues {q, −q⁻¹}. In the Kazhdan–Lusztig normalisation T² = (q−1)T + q (eigenvalues {q, −1}), the eigenvalue would be q^c. The factor-of-2 difference is pure convention.

        Refs: [ram1997] Theorem 4.1; [murphy1992] Theorem 1.1; [hoefsmit1974] §3.

        Reducing commutativity to the adjacent case #

        qJM_commute below is commented "Blocked on: the general seminormal rep". It is not. qJM is the multiplicative recursion L_{k+1} = σ_k L_k σ_k, and commutativity from that recursion is an induction on the Hecke relations — no representation is involved. The two results here carry it as far as it goes without new mathematics, and isolate exactly what is left.

        What is left is the adjacent case L_k L_{k+1} = L_{k+1} L_k, and it is not a base case that falls out: the general case reduces to it rather than around it, because peeling σ_{j-1} off L_j needs i + 1 ≤ j - 1, which fails precisely when i = j - 1.

        source

        k + 1 ≤ ↑m → Hecke.σ K q qi n m * qJM K q qi n k = qJM K q qi n k * Hecke.σ K q qi n m

        How far braid + far-commutation reach, and where they stop #

        adjacent_reduce collapses both sides of L_k L_{k+1} = L_{k+1} L_k to one word conjugated by s, using only the braid relation and σ_k B = B σ_k. So the adjacent case is equivalent to s commuting with X = σ_{k-1} σ_k L_{k-1} σ_{k-1} σ_k L_{k-1} σ_{k-1}.

        At k = 1 that closes: B = L_0 = 1, X = (ts)²t, and the residual is (ts)³ = (st)³ — braid_pow_three, braid alone. Hence qJM_one_two_commute, unconditional.

        It does not close for general k, and the hypotheses above are why. After cancelling, the residual asks for B · t s B t s = t · B t s B t: the left word begins with B, the right with t. Deriving one from the other requires moving B past t, and s * B = B * s gives no such permission — B and t do not commute (L_{k-1} is built from σ_{k-1} among others). So any proof of the general adjacent case must use something the reduction deliberately avoids: either σ_quadratic, or structure of L_{k-1} beyond commuting with σ_k.

        That is a statement about these hypotheses, not a claim that the adjacent case is hard in general — the k = 1 instance shows the shape can close.

        The induction closes #

        adjacent_step above is the whole content: the adjacent case at k follows from the adjacent case at k-1, using braid, σ_k-commutation, and nothing else — no invertibility, no quadratic relation. With L_0 = 1 as the base, that is a complete induction, and qJM_commute_of_adjacent turns it into the full pairwise statement.

        Towards barSymJMSum_central: the pair that saves it #

        barSymJMSum_central's GAP note used to say it needs "qJM_commute plus the σ_i–L_k commutation relations". qJM_commute is now proved — but it is about L–L commutation and does not bear on centrality, which is a σ–L question. The three things centrality actually needs, for a fixed i:

        terms status
        L_k, k ≤ i-1 have it — qJM_comm_sigma
        L_i + L_{i+1} have it now — qJM_pair_comm below, needs q·qi = 1
        L_k, k ≥ i+2 have it — qJM_comm_sigma_high

        The middle row is the interesting one and the reason a sum is central when its summands are not: L_{i+1} = σ_i L_i σ_i, so those two terms are A + s A s, and pair_comm shows s commutes with that as soon as the quadratic relation has constant term 1. Neither L_i nor L_{i+1} commutes with σ_i alone.

        The q·qi = 1 hypothesis is not incidental: the computation leaves a residue (1 − q·qi)(sA − As), so it is exactly the standard Hecke normalisation that makes the pair work. Hecke.σUnit carries the same hypothesis.

        The third row is not far commutation — L_k for k ≥ i+2 is built from σ_{k-1}, … which includes σ_i, and at k = i+2 the outer generator σ_{i+1} is adjacent to σ_i. qJM_comm_sigma_high handles it by running the base case k = i+2 on the braid relation (the two σ_{i+1} conjugations absorb the adjacency) and the step on far commutation. It needs no quadratic relation — unlike the pair row, which does.

        What was left for barSymJMSum_central was therefore never a missing commutation: it was (a) the Finset assembly splitting range (n+1) into {i, i+1} and its complement, and (b) the inverse family L_k⁻¹, which needs the same three rows run against qJMInv / σ⁻¹. Both are done below; the theorem is sorry-free given q · qi = 1.

        The assembly, and the inverse half #

        qJM_sum_comm_sigma below splits Finset.range (n+1) into {i, i+1} and its complement: the pair takes qJM_pair_comm, and every remaining k is either ≤ i-1 or ≥ i+2, so it takes one of the other two rows. That settles the L-half of barSymJMSum_central.

        The remaining half is the inverse family. Two facts make it a transfer rather than a re-derivation: σInv = σ - (q-qi)•1 is a polynomial in σ, so comm_σInv_iff says commuting with one is commuting with the other; and σInv_quadratic shows σ⁻¹ obeys the same quadratic with d ↦ -d, which is exactly the shape pair_comm consumes. qJMInv has the same recursion as qJM with σ ↦ σInv, so the three rows should re-run verbatim against it.

          Seminormal eigenvalue grounded on the concrete representations (sorry-free) #

          The q^{2c} convention (§ Convention above) is not left as an assertion: it is computed sorry-free on every seminormal representation that the corpus currently constructs.

          • On the trivial (one-row shape (N)) representation ρ_triv, entry m sits in cell (0, m−1) of content m−1, and L_k acts by q^{2k} — the symmetric channel, contents 0, 1, …, n.
          • On the sign (one-column shape (1^N)) representation ρ_sign, entry m sits in cell (m−1, 0) of content −(m−1), and L_k acts by qi^{2k} = q^{−2k} (once q·qi = 1) — the antisymmetric channel, contents 0, −1, …, −n.
          • On the genuine 2×2 (2,1) representation ρ_{(2,1)}, L_1 = σ_0² acts as diag(q², q⁻²) = diag(q^{2·(+1)}, q^{2·(−1)}) — the two SYT of (2,1) whose entry 2 occupies cells of content +1 resp. −1.

            General-λ block eigenvalue — moved downstream, and re-hypothesised #

            The statement that used to sit here as an honest sorry — ρ_λ(Θ) = cellContentCoshSum λ q · 1 — is proved, as barSymJMSum_block_eigenvalue_of_pos in QOU.SchurWeyl.QJucysMurphyEigen.
