    QOU.SchurWeyl.HoefsmitRep — Hoefsmit seminormal representations of H_n(q) (bean SW-4) #

    The representations ρ_λ : H_n(q) → M_{d_λ}(K) of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra, built on the universal property Hecke.lift (bean SW-1) and the Specht dimension dLambda λ = #SYT(λ) (bean SW-2).

    What is delivered here (honest status) #

    • The two 1-dimensional representations — fully sorry-free, generic [CommRing K].

      • rhoTriv (index/trivial rep): σ_i ↦ q;
      • rhoSign (sign rep): σ_i ↦ -qi (= -q⁻¹ once q·qi = 1). Both are genuine Hecke →ₐ[K] K algebra maps produced through the Hecke.lift interface, with lift_σ-based computation lemmas. Their quadratic relations hold identically over any commutative ring — no q·qi = 1 hypothesis is needed (the q·qi cross-terms cancel; see rhoTriv / rhoSign proofs).

    • The n = 3 (i.e. Hecke K q qi 2), shape λ = (2,1), d_λ = 2 seminormal representation — fully sorry-free over ℝ, including the braid relation. seminormalRep21 is an honest Hecke ℝ q q⁻¹ 2 →ₐ[ℝ] Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℝ; the two generator matrices S0 = diag(q, -q⁻¹) and the derived seminormal matrix S1 satisfy the quadratic relation (both) and the single braid relation S0 S1 S0 = S1 S0 S1 by explicit 2×2 matrix computation. This is the first concrete genuinely-2×2 seminormal braid identity of the program, verified for all real q ≠ 0.

    • Axial-distance infrastructure — sorry-free. cellContent, axialDistance on Young-diagram cells: the content difference that indexes Young's seminormal / orthogonal form.

    • The generic axial-distance seminormal block — sorry-free, generic field. seminormalBlock q a is the 2×2 building block of every size-2 SYT-orbit of the general construction (top-right 1, trace q - q⁻¹, determinant -1, one free parameter a). seminormalBlock_quadratic proves its Hecke quadratic relation for all q ≠ 0 and all a; seminormalBlock_ne_scalar proves its non-degeneracy (off-diagonal 1 ≠ 0); S1_eq_seminormalBlock recovers the concrete (2,1) matrix as seminormalBlock q (-1/(q³+q)).

    • The λ = (2,2), d_λ = 2, N = 4 seminormal representation — sorry-free over ℝ. seminormalRep22 : Hecke ℝ q q⁻¹ 3 →ₐ[ℝ] M_2(ℝ), generators ![S0, S1, S0]. The program's first 4-strand rep and its first non-vacuous far-commutation (σ_0 σ_2 = σ_2 σ_0); every relation reduces to the (2,1) lemmas. seminormalRep22_nonScalar is a concrete witness of the general non-degeneracy statement.

    • General λ seminormal representation — PROVED, and it lives elsewhere. hoefsmit_seminormal_rep is in QOU.SchurWeyl.HoefsmitRepGeneral, sorry-free, for every λ ⊢ N at any real q ≠ 0 that is not a root of unity. It is built from SeminormalRep.seminormalRepFin — the actual SYT-indexed axial-distance construction — not assumed. It could not stay in this file: that proof consumes SeminormalRep, which imports TableauCells, which imports this file, so stating it here and proving it there would be circular. See the closing section for the full note.

    The two one-dimensional representations (generic [CommRing K], sorry-free) #

    A one-dimensional representation is an algebra map H_n(q) →ₐ[K] K (the 1×1 matrices are K itself). Building one through Hecke.lift amounts to giving a single scalar g for every generator and checking the three Hecke relations degenerate to ring identities.

    source
    noncomputable def QOU.SchurWeyl.rhoTriv (K : Type u_1) [CommRing K] (q qi : K) (n : ℕ) :
    Hecke K q qi n →ₐ[K] K

    Trivial / index representation ρ_triv : H_n(q) →ₐ[K] K, σ_i ↦ q.

    The quadratic relation q·q = (q - qi)·q + (q·qi)·1 is an identity over any commutative ring (the q·qi terms cancel: (q-qi)q + q·qi = q² - qi·q + q·qi = q²), so no q·qi = 1 hypothesis is required. Braid and far-commutation are trivial because scalars commute.

            The λ = (2,1), d_λ = 2 seminormal representation over ℝ (sorry-free) #

            Hecke ℝ q q⁻¹ 2 is H_3(q) (two generators σ_0, σ_1, one braid relation σ_0 σ_1 σ_0 = σ_1 σ_0 σ_1). Its 2-dimensional irreducible (the shape (2,1) Specht module) is afforded by the seminormal matrices

            • S0 = diag(q, -q⁻¹) — σ_0 fixes the two SYT [[1,2],[3]], [[1,3],[2]] (entries 1,2 share a row resp. a column), with eigenvalues q resp. -q⁻¹;
            • S1 — the square-root-free seminormal (not orthogonal) matrix for σ_1, which exchanges the two SYT (it swaps 2 ↔ 3), with the axial-distance–determined entries below.

            Both have spectrum {q, -q⁻¹} (trace q - q⁻¹, determinant -1), so each satisfies the quadratic relation by Cayley–Hamilton; the braid relation is the genuine 2×2 identity verified here for all q ≠ 0. This crosses the substrate→archimedean wall (§7c), so we specialise to K := ℝ.

                  The generic axial-distance seminormal block (sorry-free, generic field) #

                  Every size-2 orbit block of the general Hoefsmit matrices has one shape: a 2×2 matrix with top-right entry 1, trace q - q⁻¹, and determinant -1 — hence spectrum {q, -q⁻¹} — carrying a single free parameter, its top-left entry a (an axial-distance rational function of q), the other two entries being forced by the trace and the determinant. This is the reusable building block from which every generator matrix ρ_λ(σ_i) of the general seminormal representation is assembled block-diagonally over the s_i-orbits of SYT λ. We isolate it here and prove its quadratic relation once and for all, for every q ≠ 0 and every parameter a, over a generic field (§7c: no archimedean order is used). The concrete (2,1) matrix S1 is recovered as seminormalBlock q (-1/(q³+q)) (see S1_eq_seminormalBlock).

                  Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) K

Every size-2 orbit block of the general Hoefsmit matrices is of this form (with a an axial-distance function of q).

                    theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.seminormalBlock_quadratic {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (q a : K) (hq : q ≠ 0) :
                    seminormalBlock q a * seminormalBlock q a = (q - q⁻¹) • seminormalBlock q a + (q * q⁻¹) • 1

                    Quadratic relation for the generic block (sorry-free, every q ≠ 0, every a): B(a)² = (q - q⁻¹)·B(a) + (q·q⁻¹)·1. Because the block's trace is q - q⁻¹ and its determinant is -1 identically, Cayley–Hamilton gives B(a)² = (q - q⁻¹)·B(a) + 1; q ≠ 0 is used only to identify the constant 1 = q·q⁻¹ matching the Hecke normalisation qi := q⁻¹.

                    source
                    theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.seminormalBlock_ne_scalar {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (q a s : K) :
                    seminormalBlock q a ≠ s • 1

                    The generic block is non-scalar (sorry-free): B(a) ≠ s·1 for every scalar s, because its top-right entry is 1 ≠ 0 while every scalar matrix s·1 has off-diagonal entries 0. This is the crux of the non-degeneracy that distinguishes the seminormal representation from the trivial q·1 rep, isolated for the 2×2 building block.

                    The shape λ = (2,2), d_λ = 2 seminormal representation over ℝ (sorry-free) #

                    Hecke ℝ q q⁻¹ 3 is H_4(q) (three generators σ_0, σ_1, σ_2, two braid relations and one non-vacuous far-commutation σ_0 σ_2 = σ_2 σ_0). Its 2-dimensional irreducible — the shape (2,2) Specht module, with the two SYT [[1,2],[3,4]], [[1,3],[2,4]] — is afforded by

                    • σ_0 ↦ S0 = diag(q, -q⁻¹) — entries 1,2 share a row in the first SYT (eigenvalue q), a column in the second (eigenvalue -q⁻¹);
                    • σ_1 ↦ S1 — the axial-distance block (the same content difference 2 as the (2,1) case) that swaps the two SYT;
                    • σ_2 ↦ S0 = diag(q, -q⁻¹) — entries 3,4 share a row in the first SYT, a column in the second.

                    Every relation reduces to the already-proved (2,1) lemmas: both braid relations are S0_S1_braid (one directly, one by symmetry), the two quadratics are S0_quadratic / S1_quadratic, and the far-commutation σ_0 σ_2 = σ_2 σ_0 is S0 · S0 = S0 · S0 (rfl). This is the program's first 4-strand seminormal representation and its first non-vacuous far-commutation verification.

                      General λ seminormal representation — PROVED, and it moved #

                      hoefsmit_seminormal_rep used to be stated here with a -- Ref:-cited sorry. It is now proved, in QOU.SchurWeyl.HoefsmitRepGeneral, and has been removed from this file rather than left standing beside a proved twin.

                      It could not stay: the proof consumes SeminormalRep.seminormalRepFin, and SeminormalRep imports TableauCells, which imports this file — so stating it here and proving it there would be circular. The move is an import-order consequence, not a reorganisation.

                      The concrete cases above are unchanged and remain the small-λ witnesses of the general statement: rhoTriv / rhoSign (d_λ = 1), seminormalRep21 (λ = (2,1), the smallest genuine 2×2), and seminormalRep22 (λ = (2,2), the first 4-strand case) with seminormalRep22_nonScalar.
