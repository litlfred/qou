    QOU.SchurWeyl.PiBlockSeparation — a subalgebra that separates blocks and covers each is everything #

    The algebraic crux of SW-7 (qou-0h5k), with no Hecke, matrix or tableau content, so it can be checked on its own terms.

    Let A be a subalgebra of a finite product of R-algebras ∏ i, B i. Suppose

    • A contains a family of scalar tuples (c i v)ᵢ whose values separate the index set, and
    • each block projection A → B i is surjective.

    Then A = ⊤.

    The point is what this argument does not need. The classical Wedderburn route runs through Jacobson density or a bicommutant theorem, neither of which is in Mathlib; the simplicity of the blocks is never used here. Separation puts every block idempotent Pi.single i 1 into A by finite Lagrange interpolation, and then x = Σᵢ (Pi.single i 1) · aᵢ reassembles any target from per-block preimages. Both steps are elementary.

    Two Lean-shape notes carried over from DiagonalSeparation, which proves the single-block analogue:

    • ∏ i, B i is not commutative, so Finset.prod cannot be taken over it even though the interpolation factors do commute. The product is taken in ι → R — which is commutative — and pushed across by the ring hom p ↦ fun i => algebraMap R (B i) (p i), with membership recovered by induction on the index set.
    • Separation is the entire content. A family of scalar tuples agreeing on two indices generates a subalgebra containing neither block idempotent, however large the family.

      Separating scalar tuples put every block idempotent in the subalgebra. Finite Lagrange interpolation: for each j ≠ i pick a tuple separating i from j, and multiply the normalised differences. The product is 1 in block i and 0 in every other block.
