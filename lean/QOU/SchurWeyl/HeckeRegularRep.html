    QOU.SchurWeyl.HeckeRegularRep — the T_w are linearly independent over any ring #

    Over an arbitrary commutative ring K, with q, qi : K free, the elements T_w = stdElt m w (w ∈ S_{m+1}) are linearly independent in H_{m+1}(q), so they form a basis (stdBasisAny). HeckeStandardBasisIndep proves the same only at generic q over a characteristic-zero field, by counting dimensions.

    Proof: the regular representation #

    Let E be the free K-module on S_{m+1}, with basis e_w. Put c = q − qi, d = q·qi, and let L_i, R_j : E → E be

    L_i e_w = e_{s_i w}                  if w⁻¹(i) < w⁻¹(i+1),   else  c e_w + d e_{s_i w},
R_j e_w = e_{w s_j}                  if w(j) < w(j+1),       else  c e_w + d e_{w s_j}.
    1. L_i and R_j commute (Lop_comm_Rop). If {w(j), w(j+1)} ≠ {i, i+1}, then left- multiplying by s_i does not change the right test at j, and right-multiplying by s_j does not change the left test at i, so the two orders agree by direct expansion. Otherwise s_i w = w s_j, and the four expansions are checked by hand.
    2. Any operator commuting with every R_j is determined by its value on e_1 (eq_of_comm_Rop): e_w is e_1 pushed through the R_j along a reduced word for w.
    3. So the Hecke relations hold for the L_i: the quadratic relation by direct expansion, and the braid and commutation relations because both sides commute with all R_j and agree on e_1. This gives ρ : H_{m+1}(q) → End_K E with σ_i ↦ L_i (regRep).
    4. ρ(T_w) e_1 = e_w (regRep_stdElt_one), so a linear relation among the T_w becomes one among the e_w, which are independent.

    Base ring (§7c) #

    Generic [CommRing K], no hypotheses on q, qi. Nothing archimedean.

    LaTeX label: thm:hecke-standard-basis
