    QOU.SchurWeyl.HeckeSymmetricAlgebra — H_{m+1}(q) is a symmetric algebra over any ring #

    Over an arbitrary commutative ring K, with q, qi : K free, let τ = symTrace be the coefficient of T_1 in the basis {T_w} (stdBasisAny). Then

    • τ(T_w T_v) = (q·qi)^{ℓ(w)} if v = w⁻¹, and 0 otherwise (symTrace_stdElt_mul_stdElt);
    • τ(x y) = τ(y x) (symTrace_mul_comm);
    • when q·qi is a unit, the form (x, y) ↦ τ(x y) is nondegenerate (eq_zero_of_symTrace_mul_eq_zero), so H_{m+1}(q) is a symmetric algebra, with dual basis (q·qi)^{-ℓ(w)} T_{w⁻¹};
    • every Markov trace with parameter z = 0 equals τ (MarkovTraceFamily.tr_eq_symTrace).

    Proof of the product formula #

    Induct along a reduced word u for w, peeling the last letter: w = w' s_i with ℓ(w) = ℓ(w') + 1, so T_w = T_{w'} σ_i, and expand σ_i T_v by the left rule.

    • If v⁻¹(i) < v⁻¹(i+1) then σ_i T_v = T_{s_i v} and ℓ(s_i v) = ℓ(v) + 1. Both sides vanish: v = w⁻¹ would give s_i v = w'⁻¹, of length ℓ(w) − 1 = ℓ(v) − 1.
    • Otherwise σ_i T_v = (q − qi) T_v + q·qi T_{s_i v}. The first term vanishes, since v = w'⁻¹ would make i a right descent of w', contradicting ℓ(w' s_i) = ℓ(w') + 1. The second is q·qi · (q·qi)^{ℓ(w')} exactly when s_i v = w'⁻¹, i.e. v = w⁻¹.

    Base ring (§7c) #

    Generic [CommRing K], no hypotheses on q, qi except IsUnit (q * qi) for nondegeneracy. Nothing archimedean.
