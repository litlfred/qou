    QOU.SchurWeyl.WenzlMarkovTraceInstance — the level-r Markov trace, and its uniqueness #

    MarkovTraceInstance closes the convention-D chain: the Ocneanu weight defines a Markov trace, and markovTrace_unique identifies every Markov trace at that parameter with it. #5662 generalised the three weight properties — vanishing, branching, normalisation — and the Markov condition itself to every integer level r. This file carries the identification across.

    The one thing that was not r = 2-specific #

    HeckeCoset.markovTrace_unique never mentions a level. It is stated for two MarkovTraceFamily K q qi z at a common z, over a generic [CommRing K], and its proof runs on the coset spanning family alone. So "uniqueness at r = 2" was not a level-2 theorem with a level-2 proof; it was a level-free theorem applied to the one level for which an instance existed. What this file adds is the missing instances — one per level — not a new uniqueness argument.

    That is worth stating plainly, because "generalise uniqueness" invites the assumption that the uniqueness proof needs work. It does not. The r-dependence lived entirely in ocneanuMarkovTrace.
    markovTraceOfWeight any weight with normalisation + branching + Markov is a MarkovTraceFamily
    wenzlMarkovTrace the level-r instance, at z_r = Q^r / (q · [r]_Q), Q = q²
    eq_wenzlWtrace_of_markov uniqueness at every integer level r
    wenzlMarkovTrace_two_tr conservativity: at r = 2 the trace is wtrace
    MarkovTraceFamily.param_eq the parameter is recoverable from the family: z = tr₁(σ₀)
    level_unique_of_wenzlMarkovTrace_eq distinct levels give distinct traces
    realGen_of_sq_ne_one over ℝ the genericity hypothesis follows from q² ≠ 1
    wtraceW_ne_of_level_ne the same, as an inequality, needing only q ≠ 0 and q² ≠ 1

    The two uniqueness questions are different, and both are needed #

    They are easy to conflate, so:

    • Uniqueness of the trace, given the parameter — markovTrace_unique. Fixing z pins the functional tr on every H_n(q). Nothing there is about levels; z ranges over the whole ring.
    • Uniqueness of the level, given the parameter — QBeta.WenzlWeight.level_unique_of_markovParameter. Fixing z pins the integer r, for Q not a root of unity. Nothing there is about traces.

    Neither implies the other: the first says a z has at most one trace, the second says a z is hit by at most one level. level_unique_of_wenzlMarkovTrace_eq is what you get by running them together in the one direction that composes — tr determines z (param_eq), z determines r — and it is a statement about neither alone: the level is a functional invariant of the trace, readable off a single value tr₁(σ₀).

    Scope #

    • Genericity hgen: Q = q² is not a root of unity. At a root of unity H_n(q) is not semisimple and partitions stop indexing the irreducibles; the statement would need a different formulation, not a better proof. Over ℝ this excludes nothing beyond q² ≠ 1 — see realGen_of_sq_ne_one, which discharges it.
    • r ≠ 0. At r = 0 the bracket [0]_Q is the empty sum 0 and the normalisation w_μ = s_μ / [r]_Q^{|μ|} is undefined.
    • z_r is a point of the Wenzl lattice. Nothing here says a Markov trace exists at an arbitrary z off it — only that if one does and it sits at some z_r, it is this one.

    Base ring (§7c) #

    Generic [Field K], except the ℝ corollaries at the end. The genericity hypothesis hgen of the level-r chain follows from NotRootOfUnity q over any field (gen_of_notRootOfUnity), so it is never a separate restriction. Related generic content lives elsewhere: ne_one_of_gen, levelBracket_ne_zero_of_gen, wenzlMarkovParam_spec and level_unique_of_wenzlMarkovParam_eq are generic [Field R] in WenzlWeightLevelR, and MarkovTraceFamily.param_eq is generic [CommRing K] in MarkovTraceUnique. Note the last of those is stronger for having moved: stated over ℝ it looked like a fact about this trace, and it is a fact about any Markov trace family over any commutative ring.

          theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.level_unique_of_wenzlMarkovTrace_eq {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (q : K) (hq0 : q ≠ 0) (hq2 : GenericParameter.NotRootOfUnity q) (hgen : ∀ (k : ℤ), k ≠ 0 → (q ^ 2) ^ k ≠ 1) {r s : ℕ} (hr : r ≠ 0) (hs : s ≠ 0) (h : (SeminormalRep.wtraceW q hq0 hq2 (wenzlWeight r (q ^ 2)) 1) (Hecke.σ K q q⁻¹ 1 (Fin.last 0)) = (SeminormalRep.wtraceW q hq0 hq2 (wenzlWeight s (q ^ 2)) 1) (Hecke.σ K q q⁻¹ 1 (Fin.last 0))) :
          r = s

          Distinct levels give distinct traces.

          If the level-r and level-s weighted seminormal traces agree on every H_n(q) — indeed, if they agree at the single element σ₀ ∈ H_1(q) — then r = s.

          This is the composition the two uniqueness statements admit: param_eq reads the parameter off the trace, and level_unique_of_markovParameter reads the level off the parameter. Neither alone says this. In particular the level is not extra bookkeeping attached to the trace; it is recoverable from one value of it.

          Over ℝ the genericity hypothesis is not a restriction #

          source
          theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.realGen_of_sq_ne_one {q : ℝ} (hq0 : q ≠ 0) (hq2 : GenericParameter.NotRootOfUnity q) (k : ℤ) :
          k ≠ 0 → (q ^ 2) ^ k ≠ 1

          hgen over ℝ — the ℝ case of gen_of_notRootOfUnity, kept under its old name.

          Over ℝ, NotRootOfUnity q is itself equivalent to q ≠ 0 ∧ q² ≠ 1 (a real root of unity is ±1), so the hypothesis carried through the level-r chain adds nothing over ℝ beyond q² ≠ 1.

          This is worth stating because the scope notes on the level-r files read as though genericity were a live restriction — "at a root of unity H_n(q) is not semisimple and partitions stop indexing the irreducibles". That is true of the general theory, and it is exactly why the hypothesis is there; but over the real base ring the excluded locus is {q² = 1} and nothing else. In particular it does not exclude the substrate value: q₀ ≈ 1.10998 has q₀² ≈ 1.232 ≠ 1, so the level-r Markov trace exists there for every r ≥ 1.

          Reading this the other way is the caution: it does not say the general-Q statements are stronger than they look. Over ℂ, or over a cyclotomic field, hgen is a real hypothesis and the level-r weight genuinely fails without it. What is vacuous is the specialisation to ℝ.
