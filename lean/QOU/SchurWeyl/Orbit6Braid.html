    QOU.SchurWeyl.Orbit6Braid — the generic size-6 S₃-orbit braid identity #

    (bean qou-gjg6, step (iii))

    The braid relation for the general-λ Hoefsmit seminormal representation reduces to one identity per ⟨s_i, s_{i+1}⟩-orbit of SYT λ. Orbits of size 1, 2 and 3 reduce to the 2 × 2 template S0_S1_braid; the generic orbit has size 6 and is a genuine 6-dimensional identity, not reducible to 2 × 2 blocks — because σ_i and σ_{i+1} pair the six tableaux differently. That identity is proved here, sorry-free.

    The set-up #

    Index the six tableaux by (x, y, z), the contents of the cells holding the three consecutive entries i, i+1, i+2; σ_i swaps the first two coordinates and σ_{i+1} the last two, with axial distances y - x and z - y. In the basis order

    (c₁,c₂,c₃), (c₁,c₃,c₂), (c₂,c₁,c₃), (c₂,c₃,c₁), (c₃,c₁,c₂), (c₃,c₂,c₁)

    σ_i pairs indices {0,2}, {1,4}, {3,5} and σ_{i+1} pairs {0,1}, {2,3}, {4,5}. The two pairings differ; that is the whole difficulty.

    Why the statement is polynomial #

    Writing c = q - q⁻¹ and p = c₂ - c₁, r = c₃ - c₂, the three axial distances that occur are p, r and p + r, with seminormal parameters (SeminormalParam.lean)

    a₁ = a(p) = c/(1-u), a₂ = a(r) = c/(1-v), a₃ = a(p+r) = c/(1-uv)

    for u = q^{-2p}, v = q^{-2r}. These are not independent: eliminating u and v gives the single relation

    a₃ · (a₁ + a₂ - c) = a₁ · a₂ (seminormalParam_mul_rel)

    and that relation is exactly what the braid identity needs — the theorem below is stated for arbitrary reals satisfying it, with no q, u, v and no integer distances. The relation is also necessary: perturbing a₃ away from it breaks the identity.

    Everything else about the parameters — in particular that a(-d) = c - a(d), so the partner entry of each block is c - aᵢ — is already proved in SeminormalParam.lean.

    The orientation convention #

    Each s-pair {T, sT} must designate one member as "first" — the one whose column carries the plain 1 rather than 1 + a·a' — because seminormalBlock is the square-root-free (seminormal, not orthogonal) form and is not symmetric under exchanging the pair. Here "first" is the member with negative axial distance, matching the verified (2,1) instance: in [[1,2],[3]] entry 2 has content 1 and 3 has content -1, so σ₁ acts at d = -2 < 0 on the first basis vector, which is S1's layout.

    What matters is global consistency, not which sign is chosen. The theorem below proves the negative-distance convention; the positive-distance one is the same statement with the orbit transposed. Mixing the two — negative-first for σ_i, positive-first for σ_{i+1} — appears to break the identity, so the convention is a real constraint rather than a cosmetic choice; that is why it is stated here rather than left implicit.

    Evidence for the mixed case is numerical, not formal: an exact-rational search over the four orientation combinations (both-negative, both-positive, and the two mixed) found the braid identity holding for the two consistent choices and failing for both mixed ones, with ABA - BAB carrying six nonzero entries. Only the consistent case is formalised here.

    Scope #

    This closes step (iii) — the braid identity — of the general-λ construction, in the generic size-6 case. It does not by itself close hoefsmit_seminormal_rep. Step (i) (the tableau layer: cellOf, the axial distance, the s_k-action, far commutation) is complete in TableauCells.lean; step (ii) — classifying the ⟨s_k, s_{k+1}⟩-orbits of SYT λ and assembling the block-diagonal generators, which includes mapping each orbit onto the explicit index ordering its identity is stated in — remains open (bean qou-gjg6).

    Refs:

    • [hoefsmit1974] Hoefsmit, PhD thesis (UBC 1974) — the seminormal form.
    • [ram1997] Ram, Seminormal representations of Weyl groups and Iwahori–Hecke algebras, https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024611597000373 — the axial-distance formulas.

    Matrix (Fin 6) (Fin 6) ℝ

    ρ(σ_i) on a size-6 orbit: pairs {0,2}, {1,4}, {3,5} with parameters a₁, a₃, a₂. The partner entry of a block is c - a, per seminormalParam_add_neg; the lower-left entry is 1 + a·(c-a), per seminormalBlock.

    Defined by a match on the index values rather than as a !![…] literal: entry lookup is then a single iota reduction, where the literal form forces simp through a chain of vecCons unfoldings and blows up (the 6 × 6 triple product has 1296 scalar terms).

        theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.Orbit6.genA_quadratic (c a₁ a₂ a₃ : ℝ) :
        genA c a₁ a₂ a₃ * genA c a₁ a₂ a₃ = c • genA c a₁ a₂ a₃ + 1

        The Hecke quadratic relation on the orbit for ρ(σ_i). Unlike the braid this is pair-local — it needs no relation among the parameters, and indeed holds for every c, since each block has trace c and determinant -1 and Cayley–Hamilton does the rest. Instantiating c = q - q⁻¹ (with q * q⁻¹ = 1) gives the Hecke relation σ² = (q - q⁻¹)σ + (q·q⁻¹)·1 in the normalisation of Hecke.σ_quadratic. (genB satisfies the same statement with the pairing permuted.)

        Tying the abstract parameters back to the seminormal form #

        source
        theorem QOU.SchurWeyl.Orbit6.seminormalParam_mul_rel (q x y : ℝ) (hx : x ≠ 1) (hy : y ≠ 1) (hxy : x * y ≠ 1) :
        (q - q⁻¹) / (1 - x * y) * ((q - q⁻¹) / (1 - x) + (q - q⁻¹) / (1 - y) - (q - q⁻¹)) = (q - q⁻¹) / (1 - x) * ((q - q⁻¹) / (1 - y))

        The seminormal parameters satisfy the braid relation's hypothesis. With a₁ = a(p), a₂ = a(r), a₃ = a(p+r) — the three axial distances of a size-6 orbit, written multiplicatively as x = q^{-2p}, y = q^{-2r}, so that the third is at the product x*y — the relation a₃ (a₁ + a₂ - c) = a₁ a₂ holds identically.

        This is what makes genA_genB_braid a statement about the actual Hoefsmit matrices rather than about abstract 6 × 6 arrays.
