    A standard tableau admitting an adjacent swap, whenever the shape has two of them #

    SeminormalRep.genMat_ne_scalar shows the generator matrix σ_k is non-scalar as soon as some tableau is SwapAdmissible at k — with no hypothesis on q. What it does not supply, and what hoefsmit_seminormal_rep's non-degeneracy payload needs, is that a shape with #SYT μ ≥ 2 has such a tableau at all. This file closes that step (bean SW-5, the second of the two gaps named in qou-im9l).

    The argument, and why it is not the one the bean predicted #

    The bean's planned route was geometric: #SYT μ ≥ 2 forces μ to have two rows and two columns, then the row-reading tableau is admissible at k = λ₁, where the last cell of row 0 and the first of row 1 differ in both coordinates. That route needs (a) a proof that a single row or single column has exactly one SYT, and (b) an explicit construction of a standard tableau — both real work.

    Neither is needed. Run the contradiction on the anti-diagonal i + j instead:

    • If k and k+1 are not swap-admissible in T, they share a row or a column, and sameRow_adjacent / sameCol_adjacent (already in TableauCells) put them in adjacent cells. Either way the anti-diagonal goes up by exactly one (antiDiag_cellOf_succ).
    • So if T is admissible nowhere, antiDiag (cellOf T k) = k - 1 for every entry — an induction whose base case is cellOf T 1 = (0,0) (cellOf_one).
    • That reads backwards as T.entry i j = i + j + 1: the filling is determined by the shape. Two tableaux admissible nowhere therefore agree on every cell, hence are equal.

    So #SYT μ ≥ 2 and "no tableau is ever admissible" are contradictory, which is exactly the claim. The shape never has to be classified and no tableau is ever constructed.

    Scope. This gives one admissible pair somewhere. It is not the connectivity of SYT μ under admissible transpositions (the stronger, standard fact); nothing here needs it, and it is not claimed. Nor does it say where the admissible index is: for μ = (2,1) and T = [[1,2],[3]] the witness is k = 2 and k = 1 fails, so the index genuinely depends on the tableau — which is why the argument locates a tableau by contradiction rather than naming an index in advance.

    Refs: no external result is used; [ram1997] and [hoefsmit1974] are cited at the consumer.
