    QOU.SchurWeyl.SeminormalRep — the generator matrices on all of SYT μ (bean qou-gjg6) #

    Step (ii) of the general-λ construction, taking route (c) of the three recorded in bean qou-7xt3: rather than building an object for "the ⟨s_k, s_{k+1}⟩-orbit" and assembling block-diagonally, define the generator matrix on all of SYT μ at once, with the per-generator trichotomy (axialDist_trichotomy) supplying the case split, and use the orbit-local identities (Orbit3Braid, Orbit6Braid, seminormalBlock) only as the algebraic content of the relations.

    Why (c) rather than (a) or (b): the s_k-action swapTableau is partial — defined only under SwapAdmissible — so an "orbit" is a connected component under partial moves, not a group orbit, and formalising that notion is itself the work. Routes (a) and (b) both force one to build the per-orbit basis correspondence that makes Orbit3Braid/Orbit6Braid's explicit index orderings usable. Indexing the matrix by SYT μ directly sidesteps the correspondence: there is no re-indexing to justify.

    What is delivered here #

    • offDiag q d — the off-diagonal entry of a size-2 orbit pair, carrying the orientation convention: the member with negative axial distance is "first" and its column gets 1 + a(d)·a(-d); the other gets the plain 1. This is the asymmetry of the square-root-free (seminormal, not orthogonal) form, and it is the convention fixed by the verified (2,1) instance and used throughout Orbit3Braid / Orbit6Braid.
    • genMat q k — the matrix of σ_k, indexed by SYT μ on both sides:
      • the diagonal is a(axialDist T k) uniformly, which is what makes the trichotomy a case split on block size only: at axial distance ±1 (a size-1 orbit) a is the scalar q resp. -q⁻¹, by seminormalParam_one / _neg_one;
      • the entry at (s_k·T, T) is offDiag, when the swap is admissible;
      • everything else is 0.
    • genMat_diag, genMat_of_not_admissible, genMat_apply_of_ne — the computation rules.
    • genMat_quadratic — the Hecke quadratic relation σ² = (q - q⁻¹)σ + 1, on all of SYT μ at once, for arbitrary shape. This is what route (c) was chosen for: the relation is proved globally, not one orbit at a time and then glued.

    Scope #

    All three Hecke relations are proved on all of SYT μ, unconditionally — genMat_quadratic, genMat_far_comm, and genMat_braid. No hypothesis on the orbit: the braid dispatches on three admissibility flags to seven shape theorems (sizes 1, 2 in two orientations, 3 in three positions, and the generic 6), with every further hypothesis those need derived through the classification iffs in TableauCells.

    What remains for hoefsmit_seminormal_rep is packaging only — feeding the three relations to Hecke.lift, which is an index translation between k : ℕ here and Fin n there. The -- Ref:-cited sorry stands until that lands.

    The quadratic is the easy one of the three, and it is worth saying why: it is column-local. Each column of genMat has support in {T, s_k·T} (sum_mul_genMat_column), so (M²)_{·,T} never sees a third tableau and the identity reduces to a 2 × 2 Cayley–Hamilton — trace q - q⁻¹ by seminormalParam_add_neg, determinant -1 by offDiag_mul_neg. The braid relation is not column-local: it mixes σ_k and σ_{k+1}, whose supports differ, and that is exactly the content of Orbit3Braid / Orbit6Braid.

    Refs:

    • [hoefsmit1974] Hoefsmit, PhD thesis (UBC 1974) — the seminormal form.
    • [ram1997] Ram, Seminormal representations of Weyl groups and Iwahori–Hecke algebras, https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024611597000373 — the axial-distance formulas.

    The off-diagonal entry of a size-2 orbit pair, at axial distance d as measured in the source tableau. The negative-distance member is the "first" of the pair and its column carries 1 + a(d)·a(-d); the other carries the plain 1.

        genMat q k hk hkn * genMat q k hk hkn = (q - q⁻¹) • genMat q k hk hkn + 1

        The Hecke quadratic relation, on all of SYT μ at once.

        ρ(σ_k)² = (q - q⁻¹)·ρ(σ_k) + 1 — the relation Hecke.σ_quadratic imposes, in the normalisation σ² = (q - q⁻¹)σ + (q·q⁻¹)·1.

        The proof is a two-case split on SwapAdmissible, which is the trichotomy of TableauCells:

        • σ_k fixes T (axial distance ±1). The column is the single entry a(±1), which seminormalParam_one / _neg_one evaluate to q resp. -q⁻¹ — the two roots of X² - (q - q⁻¹)X - 1.
        • σ_k moves T. The column is {T, s_k·T}, and the 2 × 2 block it spans has trace a(d) + a(-d) = q - q⁻¹ (seminormalParam_add_neg, using axialDist_swapTableau) and determinant -1 (offDiag_mul_neg). Cayley–Hamilton does the rest.

        No block-diagonal decomposition is constructed or needed: the block structure is read off the entry rules column by column.

        Far commutation #

        σ_k σ_l = σ_l σ_k for |k - l| ≥ 2, again on all of SYT μ at once. Like the quadratic and unlike the braid, this relation is column-local: a far swap at l moves no cell that σ_k looks at (cellOf_swapTableau_of_ne), so every input to σ_k's row rule — the parameter, the admissibility, the partner — survives it untouched (axialDist_swapTableau_of_far, swapAdmissible_far_iff).

        Note what is not needed: no hypothesis on q at all. The quadratic had to know that a(d) and a(-d) sum to q - q⁻¹; far commutation never combines two parameters, it only observes that both sides pair the same four entries.

        Towards the braid #

        The braid is the one relation that is not column-local: σ_k and σ_{k+1} have different supports, so no single-column argument reaches it. What replaces the column is the ⟨s_k, s_{k+1}⟩-orbit, and the two lemmas below carry the triple product down to entries at named tableaux of that orbit, with every coefficient already identified.

        Writing p = axialDist T k and r = axialDist T (k+1), the third parameter that appears is a(p + r) — not a new unknown, by axialDist_succ_swapTableau / axialDist_swapTableau_succ. That is exactly the a₃ of Orbit6Braid, whose one relation a₃(a₁ + a₂ - c) = a₁a₂ (seminormalParam_mul_rel) is what the braid needs.

        After both expansions the identity to prove is, for every row T',

          ρ_k[T',T]·a(r)·a(p) + ρ_k[T',s_{k+1}T]·off(r)·a(p)
    + ρ_k[T',s_kT]·a(p+r)·off(p) + ρ_k[T',s_{k+1}s_kT]·off(p+r)·off(p)
=
  ρ_{k+1}[T',T]·a(p)·a(r) + ρ_{k+1}[T',s_kT]·off(p)·a(r)
    + ρ_{k+1}[T',s_{k+1}T]·a(p+r)·off(r) + ρ_{k+1}[T',s_ks_{k+1}T]·off(p+r)·off(r)

        which is pure algebra in the orbit once each ρ entry is resolved. What is still missing is the orbit-size classification (bean qou-7xt3): the row cases depend on which of s_kT, s_{k+1}T, s_{k+1}s_kT, s_ks_{k+1}T exist and which coincide, and s_{k+1}s_kT ≠ s_ks_{k+1}T is precisely what distinguishes the size-6 orbit from the smaller ones.

        Size-3 orbits, and why there are three of them #

        A size-3 orbit is a domino plus a disjoint cell (Orbit3Braid), and T can sit at any of its three positions. Each position gives a different admissibility pattern, so the size-3 case is three theorems, not one:

        T's position hka hla further
        the σ_k-fixed end ✗ ✓ s_{k+1}·T is k-admissible
        the middle ✓ ✓ neither s_k·T nor s_{k+1}·T extends
        the σ_{k+1}-fixed end ✓ ✗ s_k·T is (k+1)-admissible

            A generator that moves any tableau is not a scalar matrix.

            Needs no hypothesis on q whatever. The reason is that offDiag takes the plain value 1 on the non-negative branch, so of the two off-diagonal entries (s_k·T, T) and (T, s_k·T) — which sit at distances d and -d — at least one is exactly 1, whichever sign d has. A scalar matrix has both equal to 0.

            Note this does not go through offDiag ≠ 0 at the moved entry: that can genuinely vanish, since offDiag q (-1) = 0. It is the pair that cannot both vanish.
