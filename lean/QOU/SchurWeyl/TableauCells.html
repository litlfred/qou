    QOU.SchurWeyl.TableauCells — inverting the entry map, and the axial distance of #

    consecutive entries (bean qou-gjg6, step (i) infrastructure)

    The general-λ Hoefsmit construction indexes a basis by SYT λ and, for each generator σ_i, reads off the axial distance between the cells holding the entries i and i+1. That requires going backwards along the filling — from an entry to its cell — which the StandardYoungTableau structure supports (its bijOn field says the entry map is a bijection from cells onto {1,…,|μ|}) but does not expose. This file exposes it.

    What is delivered #

    • cellOf T k — the cell of T holding entry k, with cellOf_mem, entry_cellOf (the round trip on entries) and cellOf_unique (the round trip on cells). Total: off-range k is sent to (0,0), so no subtype bookkeeping propagates into the construction.
    • axialDist T k = axialDistance (cellOf T k) (cellOf T (k+1)) — the parameter that selects the seminormal block of σ_k.
    • axialDist_of_sameRow / axialDist_of_sameCol — the two size-1 orbit cases: consecutive entries sharing a row have axial distance +1, and sharing a column, -1. Via seminormalParam_one / seminormalParam_neg_one these are exactly the scalar eigenvalues q and -q⁻¹.

    The proofs of the last two are the reason they are worth recording: sharing a row forces the two cells to be adjacent, because a cell strictly between them would carry an entry strictly between two consecutive integers. That is where standardness (as opposed to mere semistandardness) does the work.

    • swapTableau T k — the s_k-action: the tableau with the entries k and k+1 exchanged, proved again standard under SwapAdmissible (the two cells share neither a row nor a column, which by the adjacency lemmas is exactly the complement of the two size-1 cases). swapAdmissible_swapTableau and swapTableau_involutive make {T, s_k·T} a genuine two-element orbit with no side hypotheses — this is the size-2 orbit of the construction.
    • swapTableau_comm — far commutation: the s_k- and s_l-actions commute for |k - l| ≥ 2, the tableau-level form of σ_k σ_l = σ_l σ_k. With cellOf_swapTableau_of_ne (a far swap moves no cell outside {l, l+1}) and swapAdmissible_of_far, which derives the admissibility the use site needs.

    Scope #

    Step (ii) — assembling the block-diagonal generator matrices from the orbits (an enumeration Fin (dLambda λ) → SYT λ, the orbit trichotomy as a decidable case split, and the assembly itself) — is not here and remains open under bean qou-gjg6, and with it the -- Ref:-cited sorry in hoefsmit_seminormal_rep. What this file supplies is everything that step needs about individual tableaux: the entry→cell inverse, the axial distance, the two scalar cases, and the size-2 orbit partner.

    Refs:

    • [hoefsmit1974] Hoefsmit, PhD thesis (UBC 1974) — the seminormal form.
    • [ram1997] Ram, Seminormal representations of Weyl groups and Iwahori–Hecke algebras, https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024611597000373 — the axial-distance formulas.

                  Adjacent generators: the third parameter #

                  Far swaps leave the other generator's distance alone (next section). Adjacent ones do not — and what replaces it is not a fourth independent quantity but the sum. With cell contents x, y, z at the entries k, k+1, k+2, the two distances are y - x and z - y; applying either generator produces the third distance z - x.

                  That is the whole reason a ⟨s_k, s_{k+1}⟩-orbit is governed by three parameters a(p), a(r), a(p+r) satisfying one relation (Orbit6Braid.seminormalParam_mul_rel) rather than by two free ones — and it is why the braid cannot be read off a single column.

                  The six words, pinned by two entries #

                  Every element of a ⟨s_k, s_{k+1}⟩-orbit is a word in the two swaps, and is determined by what it carries in the two cells cellOf T k and cellOf T (k+1):

                  at cellOf T k at cellOf T (k+1)
                  T k k+1
                  s_k·T k+1 k
                  s_{k+1}·T k k+2
                  s_{k+1}s_k·T k+2 k

                  Adjacent generators that both fix T fix it with the same scalar.

                  If neither σ_k nor σ_{k+1} moves T, then axialDist T k = axialDist T (k+1) — both +1 (two cells in a row) or both -1 (two in a column). The mixed configurations are impossible, not merely unobserved.

                  That impossibility is what makes the braid relation hold on a size-1 orbit at all. There both sides collapse to a(d_k)²·a(d_l) and a(d_l)²·a(d_k), whose difference is a(d_k)a(d_l)(a(d_k) - a(d_l)); with a(±1) ∈ {q, -q⁻¹} that vanishes exactly when the two distances agree, and is -(q + q⁻¹) ≠ 0 when they differ.                   The impossibility argument: suppose k, k+1 share a row and k+1, k+2 a column. Adjacency (sameRow_adjacent / sameCol_adjacent) forces the cells to be (i,j), (i,j+1), (i+1,j+1), and then the corner (i+1,j) lies in μ and is squeezed to carry k+1 — which (i,j+1) already carries. The column-then-row case is the mirror.

                  The orbit shape is decided by three pairwise conditions #

                  Write c₁, c₂, c₃ for the cells of T holding k, k+1, k+2. Every admissibility that arises anywhere in a ⟨s_k, s_{k+1}⟩-orbit is one of just three conditions — (c₁,c₂), (c₂,c₃), (c₁,c₃) sharing neither a row nor a column — because each word merely permutes which entry sits in which of the three cells:

                  word its k-condition its (k+1)-condition
                  T (c₁,c₂) (c₂,c₃)
                  s_k·T (c₁,c₂) (c₁,c₃)
                  s_{k+1}·T (c₁,c₃) (c₂,c₃)
                  s_{k+1}s_k·T (c₂,c₃) —
                  s_k s_{k+1}·T — (c₁,c₂)

                  Two consequences settle the case analysis outright. s_k·T is (k+1)-admissible iff s_{k+1}·T is k-admissible — both are the (c₁,c₃) condition — so the flag patterns where exactly one of them holds are vacuous.
