    QOU.SchurWeyl.DiagonalSeparation — separating diagonals generate the diagonal matrix units #

    A purely algebraic brick, with no Hecke or tableau content: if a subalgebra A of Matrix ι ι R contains a family of diagonal matrices whose entry-tuples separate the index set, then A contains every diagonal matrix unit single i i 1.

    This is finite-set Lagrange interpolation, written as a product rather than a sum: for each j ≠ i pick a member of the family whose i- and j-entries differ, and multiply the corresponding normalised differences. The product is 1 at i — every factor is — and 0 at any other U, because the factor indexed by j = U vanishes there.

    Why the separation hypothesis is the whole content. Diagonal alone gives nothing: a family of diagonal matrices all of whose entry-tuples agree on two indices can never distinguish them, and the subalgebra it generates cannot contain either matrix unit. Separation is exactly the missing ingredient, and it is what SeminormalSimpleSpectrum.seminormalRep_qJM_spectrum_injective supplies for the q-Jucys–Murphy family.

    Stated over a Field R because the normalisation divides by d i v − d j v; the index type is a Fintype with DecidableEq, and the family index V need only be nonempty.

    Matrix.single i i 1 ∈ A

    Separating diagonals generate the diagonal matrix units. If A contains diagonal (d · v) for every v, and the tuples d i · separate the indices, then single i i 1 ∈ A for every i.
