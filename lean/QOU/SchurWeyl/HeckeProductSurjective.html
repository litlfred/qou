    QOU.SchurWeyl.HeckeProductSurjective — the block product map is surjective #

    SW-7 (qou-0h5k), the step that needed an argument rather than an assembly.

    Φ = ⊕_{λ ⊢ n+1} ρ_λ : H_{n+1}(q) → ∏_λ M_{#SYT λ}(ℝ) is surjective.

    Why this is not the classical route #

    BraidKnot.SpechtWedderburnGeneral settles the q = 1 case by injectivity + equal finrank. That does not transfer. On the q-side the corpus has HeckeCoset.finrank_le_factorial (dim H ≤ (n+1)!) but not the matching lower bound — that is the open Gröbner–Shirshov item qou-auq3 — and injectivity together with dim source ≤ dim target proves nothing at all.

    Surjectivity is what the q-side actually has, from two results that landed separately:

    • seminormalRep_range_eq_top — each block is surjective;
    • youngDiagram_eq_of_contentPowSum_eq — the central Murphy power sums separate the shapes.

    Why no density theorem is needed #

    Mathlib has no Jacobson density and no bicommutant, and the usual proof wants one. It is avoidable: PiBlockSeparation.eq_top_of_separating_of_blockSurj gets A = ⊤ from separation plus per-block surjectivity by finite Lagrange interpolation and nothing else — block simplicity is never used. Separation supplies the block idempotents; per-block surjectivity supplies the entries.

    Once this lands, the Wedderburn isomorphism follows by counting: surjective, with dim H ≤ (n+1)! and dim (∏_λ M_{d_λ}) = Σ_λ d_λ² = (n+1)! (SW-3, now available for every n), forces bijectivity — and yields dim H_{n+1}(q) = (n+1)! as a corollary, the Iwahori–Hecke basis theorem's lower bound, without the Gröbner–Shirshov route.
