      twoPointExponent t₁ (A * c₁) t₂ (A * c₂) = twoPointExponent t₁ c₁ t₂ c₂

      The exponent is invariant under an overall rescaling of c.

      Rescaling c ↦ A·c is exactly what a change of normalisation convention does to the observable, and it cancels in the numerator: log (A·c₁) − log (A·c₂) = log c₁ − log c₂. So a convention offset can move the prefactor k freely and can never move p. No positivity is needed on t₁, t₂ — the denominator is untouched.

          The saddle condition, and what it forces #

          The knot-side derivation runs Laplace's method on Kashaev's one-variable sum ⟨4₁⟩_N = Σ_k |(q;q)_k|², whose logarithm expands as

          log g_k = −(2N/π)·Λ(θ) + log(2N sin θ) + O(1/N),      θ = kπ/N

          with Λ the Lobachevsky function. Three quantities come out of the saddle:

          • the SADDLE CONDITION Λ'(θ) = −log(2 sin θ) = 0, i.e. 2 sin θ* = 1;
          • the STIRLING factor 2 sin θ*, from the subleading term;
          • the HESSIAN |Λ''(θ*)| = |cot θ*|, from the Gaussian integral,

          giving c_σ = 2 sin θ* · |cot θ*|^{−1/2} and |τ_DG| = |cot θ*| / 2.

          WHAT IS AND IS NOT PROVED HERE. The algebra downstream of the saddle is proved here; the saddle itself still enters as hypotheses hc and hτ. The two analytic inputs those hypotheses stand for are now BOTH formalised elsewhere in this corpus (bean qou-mlgp), so the reading "not formalised, Mathlib has no usable form" — which this header carried until 2026-08-09 — is out of date:

          • the expansion, QOU.Knot.FigureEightVolume.kashaev_log_sum_asymptotic. With kashaev_factor_eq_sin_sq giving log g_k = 2·∑_{j≤k} log(2 sin jπ/N), it says exactly the display above with the O(1/N) made explicit: log g_k = −(2N/π)Λ(θ) + log(2N sin θ) + (π/6N)cot θ + O(1/N²);
          • Laplace's method for the sum, QOU.MathlibExt.hasSum_gaussian_theta and …_tilt — an EXACT Jacobi-theta identity for the Gaussian sum, so sum/integral = 1 + O(e^{−π²/a}).

          WHAT IS STILL ASSUMED, as of 2026-08-09, is hτ ONLY. hc is now DERIVED, in QOU.Knot.FigureEightVolume.cSigma_sq_mul_abs_cot: the asymptotics produce the constant in the shape c_σ(θ) = (2 sin θ)·|cot θ|^{−1/2} — Stirling factor over square-rooted Hessian — and squaring that and multiplying by |cot θ| is hc. cSigma_saddle_pow_four and k_sq_eq_half below are re-proved there with hc supplied rather than assumed, so the chain from the Kashaev sum to c_σ = 3^{−1/4} no longer passes through an assumption.

          The text here read "nobody has yet derived hc and hτ" until that date; that was true when written and is now half out of date. hτ remains open.

          What is proved here is the algebra, which is where the whole surprise lives:

          • saddle_forces_cot — the saddle condition plus θ ∈ (π/2, π) pins cot θ* = −√3. Nothing about 4₁ is used; one summation variable is the only input.
          • c_sq_sqrt_three_of_saddle and c_pow_four_eq_third — hence c_σ⁴ = 1/3. In one variable the saddle condition IS 2 sin θ* = 1, so the Stirling factor is forced to 1 and c_σ = 3^{−1/4} with no freedom at all. 3^{−1/4} is a property of "one summation variable", not of 4₁.
          • k_sq_eq_half_of_saddle — the sharpest of the three, and the reason this section exists. k := c_σ·|τ|^{1/2} satisfies k² = 1/2 using only the saddle condition: the Hessian cancels between c_σ and τ before it is ever evaluated, so k = 2^{−1/2} never depended on cot θ* = −√3, on the trace field, or on which manifold this is. The corpus calibrated k on 4₁ for a long time; it was forced by the shape of the saddle.
