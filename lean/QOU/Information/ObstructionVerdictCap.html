    QOU.Information.ObstructionVerdictCap — the single-verdict reading, and its counter-model #

    Companion to ObstructionGenericity and content block thm:qou-obstruction-reduction.

    The question this answers #

    thm:qou-obstruction-reduction computes the classical-limit obstruction space as one-dimensional, HH²(𝒰_q, Ad) ≅ k, with class χ(A) = -6 ≠ 0; and the corpus attaches several Millennium reformulations to restrictions of that one class. It is tempting to read this as: one scalar, therefore one verdict, therefore the legs stand or fall together. Call that the single-verdict reading.

    This file makes the reading precise, shows what it would cost, and then exhibits the corpus's own counter-model to it. The conclusion is the opposite of the tempting one: the restrictions are independent, the unification is weaker than it reads, and no leg constrains another through the shared class alone.

    Why the reading is not the corpus's #

    Three facts, each checkable in the corpus, defeat any attempt to model "the obstruction vanishes" as a single Prop:

    1. Vanishing is level-indexed, not a proposition. qou-obstruction-reduction.md part (c) reads "the obstruction vanishes at Kummer level n iff n ∣ χ(A)", carried in Lean as kappa_vanishes : ℕ → Prop with kappa_vanishes_iff_chi a family over n (QOU.AlgebraicSubstrate.qou_obstruction_reduction). There is no single Prop to be faithful to.

    2. The evaluation maps are explicitly different. conj:qou-p-neq-np states the shared-class corollary as "their evaluation maps differing but the underlying class remaining identical", and scopes proportionality to "RH, NS, and BSD" plus P-vs-NP — q-Collatz is not among them.

    3. The restrictions land in three different groups: χ(A) ∈ HH²(𝒰_q, Ad), κ_C ∈ H²(𝒮_{C_q}, 𝔾_m), NS(A) ∈ H³_loc(A, Θ). Classes in different groups may vanish independently by construction.

    The counter-model (the punchline) #

    The corpus proves both of the following at χ(A) = -6, with no inconsistency:

    • kappa_vanishes 2 is true — since 2 ∣ -6, the q-Collatz even-branch [2]_q Kummer divisor is removable (QOU.AlgebraicSubstrate.qcollatz_kummer_removable_of_chi_neg_six, proved);
    • res_ℛ(χ(A)) ≠ 0 — because q₀ is strictly non-torsion, no finite Kummer level applies (QouPNeqNp.chiResidual_ne_zero).

    A vanishing verdict on one leg and a non-vanishing verdict on another, from the same class, simultaneously. That pair is a counter-model to the single-verdict hypothesis: single_verdict_refuted_by_counter_model below turns it into a refutation. So the collision results in this file are conditional on a hypothesis the corpus itself falsifies — which is exactly why the "one scalar, three problems" intuition must not be used to transfer results between legs.

    Correction record (2026-08-15) #

    An earlier revision of this file asserted the opposite: that the three faithfulness claims plus proportionality were "not jointly tenable" and that the package "predicts q-Collatz is false, contradicting conj:q-collatz". That was wrong, for the three reasons above — it collapsed a level-indexed family to one Prop, and applied a proportionality claim to a leg that claim excludes. The error was caught by adversarial review before merge. The present file keeps the formal skeleton (which was sound) and replaces the interpretation with the counter-model. Recorded per AGENTS.md §"Report honest negatives".

    Scope #

    Every hypothesis is a named argument — no axiom, no instance, nothing instantiated. Collatz, NSRegular, PNeqNP are opaque Props throughout, exactly as in QouPNeqNp. Nothing here proves or refutes any conjecture; the only thing refuted is the single-verdict reading, and it is refuted by corpus facts supplied as explicit hypotheses.

    What one verdict would buy #

    source
    theorem QOU.Information.verdict_cap {v A B : Prop} (hA : v ↔ A) (hB : v ↔ B) :
    A ↔ B

    Verdict cap. If one verdict v is faithful to two predicates, those predicates are equivalent. This is the whole content of "one scalar cannot answer two questions differently" — and it is Iff algebra, which is the point: the mathematics is trivial, so all the weight sits on whether the hypotheses hold. Per the counter-model below, they do not.

    source
    theorem QOU.Information.opposite_polarity {z A B : Prop} (hA : z ↔ A) (hB : ¬z ↔ B) :
    A ↔ ¬B

    Opposite polarity. A predicate faithful to a verdict and a predicate faithful to its negation are negations of one another.

    The single-verdict hypothesis, named rather than assumed #

    The three restrictions get three distinct propositions, as the corpus has them. SingleVerdict is the extra assumption that collapses them — stated explicitly so it can be pointed at, and, below, falsified.

    source
    def QOU.Information.SingleVerdict (VanC VanNS VanR : Prop) :
    Prop

    The single-verdict hypothesis. The Collatz, Navier–Stokes and resolution-tree restrictions of κ₀ agree: the first two vanish together, and the third's vanishing is the same event. This is what "one class, one verdict" would require. It is not a corpus claim.

      The counter-model: the corpus falsifies the hypothesis #

      source
      theorem QOU.Information.single_verdict_refuted_by_counter_model {VanC VanNS VanR : Prop} (hCvanishes : VanC) (hRnonzero : ¬VanR) :
      ¬SingleVerdict VanC VanNS VanR

      The single-verdict hypothesis is refuted by the corpus's own arithmetic. Feed it the two facts the corpus proves at χ(A) = -6 — the Collatz-side level-2 restriction vanishes (2 ∣ -6, so the [2]_q divisor is removable), while the resolution-tree restriction does not (q₀ strictly non-torsion, so no finite Kummer level applies) — and it is inconsistent.

      Hence no collision result above applies to the corpus, and the shared class transfers nothing between legs.

      source
      theorem QOU.Information.legs_independent_of_counter_model {VanC VanNS VanR : Prop} (hCvanishes : VanC) (hRnonzero : ¬VanR) :
      ¬SingleVerdict VanC VanNS VanR ∧ VanC ∧ ¬VanR

      Restated as the corpus's disposition. Given the same two facts, the legs are not forced together: the collision hypothesis fails, so Collatz and PNeqNP may both hold. This is the honest reading of "one class, several restrictions" — the class is shared, the verdicts are not.
