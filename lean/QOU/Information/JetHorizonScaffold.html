    QOU.Information.JetHorizonScaffold — structure behind the transfer #

    Scaffolding for JetHorizonTransfer (bean qou-iv9f). It makes the transfer's antecedent concrete — grounding it in the degree-graded observation trace — while keeping the one genuinely open step (the degree↔complexity dictionary) an explicit, auditable hypothesis. Nothing here fabricates the transfer instance; it exposes exactly what such an instance would have to supply.

    Taking a trace is the observation step #

    In the QOU an observable result is obtained by taking a trace (the observation frame τ / Markov trace, AGENTS.md canonical chain): the categorical fibre A is collapsed to a scalar τ(a) ∈ k. The observed value splits by Taylor/jet degree, τ(a) = ⟨degree-0 part⟩ + ⟨degree-≥1 part⟩, and the two parts play different computational roles (below).

    Why NP-witness ≡ degree-≥1 (not P-witness) #

    A recurring question: since degree-≥1 data is the accessible (leaking) layer, why call it an NP-witness rather than a P-witness? Because a witness is a defining feature of NP, not P:

    • NP = "a short certificate exists and is verifiable in poly time." The degree-≥1 data that leaks across the horizon is exactly such a certificate — you receive and verify it. That is the NP notion.
    • P = "the answer is computed directly in poly time." P has no external certificate; it produces the degree-0 answer itself. Calling degree-≥1 a "P-witness" conflates the verifier's certificate (NP) with the solver's computation (P) — a category error.

    So the horizon picture is: degree-≥1 certificates are poly-verifiable (in reach), the degree-0 answer is poly-inaccessible (blocked) ⇒ P ≠ NP. But the assignment is a modelling choice: a different dictionary (e.g. degree-≥1 read as P-computable partial data) yields a different conclusion. That non-uniqueness is precisely why the transfer is conjectural — it is encoded below as an explicit ComplexityDictionary with no instance.

    The observation frame / trace grading. An observable result τ(a) splits into its degree-0 part (the decision answer) and its degree-≥1 part (the witness / Hawking-leakage data).

    • τ : A → k

      The observation trace τ : A → k (Markov trace / observation frame).

    • answer : A → k

      Degree-0 component of the observed value: the decision answer.

    • witness : A → k

      Degree-≥1 component: the verifiable witness data that leaks.

    • degree_split (a : A) : self.τ a = self.answer a + self.witness a

      The observable splits by degree.

      The degree↔complexity dictionary (the open bridge, §3b-cond). The interpretive identification the transfer rests on. NPVerify and PDecide are opaque Props (no Mathlib complexity theory); the fields state the chosen reading — degree-≥1 certificates are NP-verifiable, the degree-0 answer being blocked obstructs P — and derive the QOU separation. No instance is declared: this map is the irreducible conjectural input (anti-laundering).

      • answerBlocked : Prop

        The decision answer (degree-0) is poly-time inaccessible when the obstruction blocks it.

      • witnessVerifiable : Prop

        Degree-≥1 certificates are poly-verifiable (the NP side).

      • separation_of_blocked : answerBlocked decisionCost → witnessVerifiable decisionCost → PNeqNPInQOU decisionCost

        The conjectural transfer: a blocked degree-0 answer with verifiable degree-≥1 witnesses is exactly the P ≠ NP separation in the QOU.

      • witnesses_leak : witnessVerifiable decisionCost

        Degree-≥1 witnesses are always available (they leak across 𝓗).
