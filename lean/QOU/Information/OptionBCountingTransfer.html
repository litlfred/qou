    QOU.Information.OptionBCountingTransfer — the non-fiat transfer, analysed #

    Option B (the native reading): the observation trace computes the #P count J_L(q₀). We get counting-hardness for free — but the question is whether any route to P ≠ NP exists that is not by fiat (not just assuming the degree-0 = decision identification).

    The decisive fact, formalised here: the generic transfer "counting-hard ⟹ decision-hard" is FALSE. The witness is the permanent / perfect matching:

    • counting perfect matchings is #P-complete (Valiant 1979), yet
    • deciding whether a perfect matching exists is in P (König / Hopcroft–Karp).

    So a #P-hard count can sit on top of a poly-time decision. Any transfer from "the QOU trace is #P-hard" to "P ≠ NP" therefore cannot be a generic principle — it must exploit QOU-specific structure that the matching problem lacks. That is exactly what "not by fiat" would require, and no such structure is known.

    What a non-fiat transfer would actually need #

    To get P ≠ NP honestly from Option B you would need a decision lower bound on a specific QOU-encoded NP-complete problem L:

    1. Encode an NP-complete L as a family of links β_{L,n} so that deciding L reduces to reading J_{β_{L,n}}(q₀) — this exists (JVW-style reductions of NP-hard problems into Jones/Tutte evaluations).
    2. Prove L has no poly-time decider — i.e. that deciding L genuinely requires the degree-0 obstruction, with no shortcut of the matching kind. This step is a circuit/TM lower bound; it is P vs NP itself, and the Razborov–Rudich natural-proofs barrier blocks any uniform/relativizing argument (which a "works at every non-torsion q₀" argument is).

    Step 1 is real and reusable; step 2 is the wall, and it is not the trace's counting-hardness — that is step 0, already free.

    The one physically-motivated non-fiat candidate (weaker than P vs NP) #

    The corpus's Bekenstein / bandwidth bound (𝔅(q) = qη_q/(q-1)² q-bits per Planck step; the computational horizon is where the effective tree exceeds the polynomial bandwidth budget) is an information lower bound. It could give a genuine, non-fiat lower bound — but only in a bandwidth-limited (physical) model of computation, not for abstract Turing machines. It would prove a physical Church–Turing-style statement ("no bandwidth-limited device decides L in poly time"), which is strictly weaker than abstract P ≠ NP. That is the honest ceiling of the non-fiat route.

        theorem QOU.Information.generic_transfer_false [M : MatchingFacts] :
        ¬GenericTransfer

        The generic counting→decision transfer is FALSE (sorry-free). Perfect matching has a #P-hard count and a poly-time decision, so counting-hardness cannot imply decision-hardness in general. Hence any QOU route from "trace is #P-hard" to "P ≠ NP" must use QOU-specific structure — it can never be the generic principle. This is why Option A's degree-0 identification, read as a general law, is refuted, and why a non-fiat transfer needs a genuine decision lower bound.
