    QOU.Information.CountingModuliGrade — #P as an ω-graded section over moduli #

    This file formalizes the picture behind the question "how do we describe #P over a moduli space of problems / Turing machines / QOU objects, with the initial photonic condition = the Turing state?".

    The moduli picture. A problem is a point m of a moduli space M. In the QOU dictionary a moduli point is a categorical object (a braid / Hecke representation) equipped with an initial photonic condition — the contact / Reeb initial data of the state bundle — and that initial condition is the Turing configuration (tape + head + state). The computation is the evolution (the braid word / Reeb flow); the counting answer #P(m) is the terminal Markov trace. So #P is a section over M, and its cost is a family of growth functions cost : M → ℕ → ℕ — one growth function per moduli point, graded fiberwise by the surreal ω-grade of SurrealBudgetGrade.

    What is proved here (the counting axis). The hard stratum {m | SuperPolynomial (cost m)} is where counting is ω-super-polynomial. A single hard point excludes a uniform (one-algorithm-for-all-moduli) polynomial bound over the whole space (not_familyPolyBounded_of_hardPoint), and the QOU boundary family lands such a point unconditionally — the physical 3^{3n} resolution-tree cost sits in the hard stratum (physical_point_hard, via qou_physical_intractability_unconditional). So: over the moduli space of QOU counting problems, no uniform polynomial counting algorithm exists — proved, sorry-free.

    What stays open (the decision axis = the wall). Crossing from counting-hard to decision-hard is the res ≠ 0 ⟹ P≠NP wall. It is packaged honestly as the §3b conditional class PhotonicDecisionHard with no instance: an instance would exhibit a decision-hard moduli point, i.e. prove P≠NP. The gap between the (proved) counting hard point and the (absent) decision hard point is exactly the counting/decision axis boundary of MagnitudeAxisBoundary.

    Base ring not applicable (ℕ-valued costs / finite combinatorics).
