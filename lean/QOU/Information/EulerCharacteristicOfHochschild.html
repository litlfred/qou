    The Euler characteristic of Hochschild homology is the Hodge one #

    The 2026-09-15 separation of the four "Euler characteristics" (rem:three-euler-characteristics) reads the −6 of Bring's curve as the alternating sum of the Hochschild homology of the base curve, Σ_n (−1)^n dim HH_n(M₀) = 2 − 2g. That reading has two cited inputs and one identity, and this file proves the identity.

    • HKR at the level of dimensions (Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg; Loday 3.4.4): for a smooth projective variety X of dimension d, HH_n(X) ≅ ⊕_{j − i = n} H^i(X, Ω^j), so dim HH_n = Σ_{j − i = n} h^{i,j}. Encoded as the definition hhDim.
    • Hodge decomposition (cited, not proved here): Σ_{i,j} (−1)^{i+j} h^{i,j} is the topological Euler characteristic χ_eu(X). Encoded as the definition hodgeEuler; the identification with χ_eu is not a claim of this file.
    • The identity proved here (hhEuler_eq_hodgeEuler): Σ_n (−1)^n dim HH_n = Σ_{i,j} (−1)^{i+j} h^{i,j}. The sign (−1)^{j−i} equals (−1)^{i+j} because the exponents differ by 2i (neg_one_zpow_sub); re-indexing the double sum over the Hodge square is the rest.

    For a curve (d = 1; h^{0,0} = h^{1,1} = 1, h^{1,0} = h^{0,1} = g) the sum is 2 − 2g (hhEuler_curve), and −6 at g = 4 (hhEuler_bring). The three Hochschild dimensions of a curve are 2, g, g (hhDim_curve_*), which is the ledger rem:three-euler-characteristics quotes: HH_0 alone has two components and neither is −6.

    None of this involves the quantum dimension dim_q, which is a different object. Dimensions are integers; no ℝ, no numerics, no sorry.

    -- Ref: [loday-1998] https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-11389-9 -- Ref: [hkr-1962] https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1962-0142598-8
