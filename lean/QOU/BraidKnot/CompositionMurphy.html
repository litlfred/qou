    x_μ for a composition, and its eigenvector property #

    Mathas' Lemma 3.9 — the foundation of the Murphy-basis spanning argument — is stated for a composition μ, not a partition. QOU.BraidKnot.HeckeMurphy.murphySum builds x_S for an arbitrary Finset of permutations and proves it is a q-eigenvector for any subgroup, so the composition case costs nothing once the subgroup exists — which is what CompositionYoung.youngOfComp supplies.

    What this file adds is the translation of the eigenvector hypothesis into the composition's own terms. HeckeMurphy.σ_mul_murphySum_subgroup asks for sw i ∈ H, an opaque condition on a permutation; mem_youngOfComp_sw turns it into the statement it should be — i and i+1 lie in the same block of μ — which is how Mathas states it and the only form usable when μ is given by its parts.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.CompositionMurphy.mem_youngOfComp_sw {n : ℕ} {c : Composition (n + 1)} {i : Fin n} :
    HeckeMurphy.sw i ∈ CompositionYoung.youngOfComp c ↔ c.index i.castSucc = c.index i.succ

    Membership of a simple transposition in a composition's Young subgroup is exactly the statement that the two labels it swaps share a block.

    The reverse direction is where the content is: a transposition fixes the block function everywhere outside the two points it moves, so agreement at those two points is all that is needed.

      The eigenvector property, in the composition's own terms.

      σ_i · x_μ = q · x_μ whenever i and i+1 share a block of μ — i.e. for every simple reflection inside a part, which is the generating set of S_μ.

      The algebra is entirely HeckeMurphy.σ_mul_murphySum_subgroup; what this supplies is the hypothesis in a form that can be checked from the parts of μ.
