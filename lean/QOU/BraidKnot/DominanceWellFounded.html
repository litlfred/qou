    Strict dominance on tabloids is well-founded — 3.14's inner induction measure #

    PartitionLexWellFounded supplies the outer axis of Mathas' Lemma 3.14: the lex order across shapes. This file supplies the inner one — dominance within a fixed shape, the order 3.14's case (ii) inducts along (ν = λ with u ⊵ s, v ⊵ t).

    Both ingredients were already here and, once again, neither was built for this:

    • SpechtDominanceCore.domRel_refl / _trans / _antisymm — dominance is a genuine partial order on tabloids, proved sorry-free;
    • Specht.Tabloid.instFintype — tabloids of a fixed shape are a Fintype.

    The strict part of a partial order on a finite type is well-founded, so the two compose, exactly as they did on the outer axis.

    Direction — and it matters #

    domRel a b reads "a is dominated by b". 3.14's case (ii) gives the induction hypothesis for tableaux that dominate the one at hand, so the induction runs up the order, and domGtOn a b below is "a strictly dominates b". That mirrors lexGtOn, where the hypothesis is available for lex-greater shapes. Reversing either would give a recursor whose hypothesis is the wrong way round — well-founded either way, since the order is finite, and useless for the theorem.

    Scope #

    With this and PartitionLexWellFounded.lexGtOn_wellFounded, both of 3.14's axes have a well-founded measure. That does not make 3.14 proved, or even stated: the statement also needs the two axes combined into a single measure (a lexicographic product), and then the mathematics of the Garnir step itself. What is settled is that neither axis can fail to terminate.

    Note also that dominance here is on tabloids — row-equivalence classes — which is the standard object for the dominance order; a tableau's dominance is that of its class.

    🛑 RESOLVED — read this, not the doubt it replaced. The doubt was: the corpus retired a tabloid-dominance measure for Garnir straightening at q = 1 on an exact-arithmetic counterexample (μ=(3,2), t=[[1,2,3],[5,4]]), concluding "Garnir produces terms whose tabloids are dominance-incomparable to {t}", hence "no tabloid-level measure orders them" (SpechtStandardBasisCore.lean §"Measure correction (2026-07-07)"). This axis is a tabloid-level measure, so the doubt was real.

    Mathas' own proof of 3.14 settles it, and in this axis's favour. Its case (ii) step is

    m_st = T*_{d(s)} h T_w + Σ_{v ▷ t} r_v m_sv

    — the same-shape terms are indexed by the dominance relation itself, so they cannot be incomparable, and the relation is ▷, strict (Corollary 3.15 states the same: "ν = λ, u = s and v ▷ t"). 3.14's statement uses the loose ⊵; its induction steps on ▷. domGtOn is exactly that strict part, and domGtOn_induction's direction — hypothesis available for strictly-dominating tableaux — is what "by induction on t" needs.

    Why the q = 1 counterexample does not carry, sharper than "different step": there the terms fall out of a coset sum and their tabloids land where they land, so incomparability is possible; here the term set is defined as those v dominating t. Different mechanisms, one of which cannot produce an incomparable term at all. colWordNum remains the right measure for the q = 1 polytabloid straightening and is not needed here.

    Full derivation and the two remaining 3.14 inputs it identifies (3.12, and Corollary 3.8, not previously named on this arc): docs/audits/2026-09-06-tabloid-dominance-vs-the-column-word-measure.md.
