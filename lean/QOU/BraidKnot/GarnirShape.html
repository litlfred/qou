    The Garnir shape, and that it is lex-greater #

    Mathas' Lemma 3.12 (Hecke algebras and Schur algebras of the symmetric group) attaches to a Garnir node (i, j) of λ the composition

    ν = (λ₀, …, λᵢ₋₁,  j,  λᵢ + 1,  λᵢ₊₁ − j − 1,  λᵢ₊₂, …)

    — rows i and i+1 split at column j and rejoined — and asserts that its partition is lex-greater than λ.

    This file proves that, and the whole argument is counting. Two inputs do the work:

    • PartitionOfComposition.colLen_ofComposition — a composition's column lengths are its part counts, so nothing here ever looks at the sorted list;
    • PartitionLex.lexGt_of_colLen — a lex step follows from two column-length facts, with the witness named.

    So the proof reduces to: the three new parts contribute exactly one part above λᵢ that the two old parts did not, and nothing at all above that.

    Why it is lex and not dominance #

    Dominance fails here, measured 86 times in 910 Garnir nodes, the smallest case being λ = (3,3) at (0,1) — see garnir_shape_dominance_probe.py and PartitionLex.dom_fails_lex_holds. Mathas says which order he means in the sentence before the lemma; it is the weaker one, and it is also the total one, which is what makes the straightening recursion well-founded.

    Splitting a list's count at two consecutive positions.

    countP is additive over ++, and drop peels one entry at a time, so a list's count is its count outside a two-entry window plus the two entries' own contributions. Both the original shape and the Garnir composition are read this way, and they share the two outer summands verbatim.

        theorem QOU.BraidKnot.GarnirShape.garnirShape_lexGt {μ : YoungDiagram} {i j : ℕ} (h : (i + 1, j) ∈ μ) :
        garnirShape μ i j >ₗ μ

        Mathas' Lemma 3.12, shape half. The Garnir shape is lex-greater than μ.

        The witness is μ.colLen (μ.rowLen i) — the number of rows longer than row i — which is the c = #{r : λ_r > λᵢ} measured 1435/1435 by the probe, and is not the Garnir row i: at λ = (2,2,2) with node (1,0) the shape moves at row 0, because the merged part overtakes the rows above i that tie with λᵢ.

        Non-vacuity #

        garnirShape_lexGt carries a hypothesis, and a theorem whose hypothesis nothing satisfies proves nothing. It is met by the shape that made the order question concrete in the first place: λ = (3,3) has a cell at (1,1), so (i, j) = (0, 1) is a Garnir node of it.

        That is the same node whose composition (1, 4, 1) sorts to (4,1,1) — the pair PartitionLex.dom_fails_lex_holds uses to show dominance fails. What is checked here is that the hypothesis is satisfiable and the theorem fires, not that garnirShape lam33 0 1 is definitionally nu411: lam33.rowLens does not reduce (decide gets stuck on hasDecEq), so identifying the two shapes would need a separate argument and is not claimed.
