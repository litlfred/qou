    QOU.BraidKnot.HeckeMurphyYoung — Murphy's x_λ over the actual Young subgroup #

    QOU.BraidKnot.HeckeMurphy proves σ_i · x_S = q · x_S for any finite set of permutations closed under right multiplication by s_i, and specialises it to an arbitrary Subgroup. This file connects that to the Young subgroup a tableau actually has, which is what makes it Murphy's x_λ rather than a statement about an abstract subgroup.

    The join, and why it is only bookkeeping #

    The two clusters were built independently and index their symmetric groups differently:

    • the Specht cluster works over Equiv.Perm (Fin (Specht.nOf μ)), where nOf μ = μ.card;
    • the Hecke cluster works over Equiv.Perm (Fin (n + 1)), because IwahoriHecke K q n has n generators and so models S_{n+1}.

    Given μ.card = n + 1 these are the same group, and the corpus already carries the transport (Equiv.permCongrHom (finCongr h), used by SpechtClassification.transportPermHom). So the bridge is a Subgroup.map, not new mathematics — but it is the point at which the q-deformed side can finally name the combinatorial object the q = 1 side has had all along.

    Specht.rowStab t is exactly the Young subgroup S_λ: the stabiliser of the row pattern of t, i.e. the permutations preserving each row of t setwise. It is MulAction.stabilizer, so it is a Subgroup by construction and carries a DecidablePred instance (Specht.instDecidableMemRowStab) — which is what murphySum's Finset.filter needs.

    What this does and does not establish #

    It gives the eigenvector property for the genuine x_λ. It is still not the Murphy basis: that the m_{st} = T_{d(s)}^* x_λ T_{d(t)} are a basis, and cellular axioms (C1)/(C3), remain open. What has changed is that those are now stated against objects that exist — colSuperstandard for the reference tableau t^λ, partitionDom for the dominance order — rather than against combinatorics that would have to be built. Bean qou-phir.

              Toward (C1): the Murphy family has exactly the right size #

              Cellular axiom (C1) asks that {m_{st}}, indexed by pairs of standard tableaux of the same shape, be a basis of H_n(q). A basis claim is two claims — spanning and independence — but for a finite family in a finite-dimensional space, either one plus a cardinality match gives the other. So the cheap half is worth isolating.

              Both ingredients already exist in this corpus, and neither had to be built:

              • QOU.Mathlib.rskBurnsideHolds — ∑_{p ⊢ n} (#SYT p)² = n!, the RSK/Burnside identity, proved for every n from a Robinson–Schensted correspondence built in-repo (the pinned Mathlib has no RSK). That sum counts exactly the pairs (s,t) of standard tableaux of a common shape — the Murphy index set.
              • QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.finrank_eq_factorial — dim_K H_n(q) = (n+1)!, transported to the other presentation on #6158 as basisTwOfSn.

              Putting them together is two lines, and the content is worth stating plainly: the Murphy family is not over- or under-counted. (C1) therefore reduces from "is a basis" to "spans" or "is independent" — whichever is easier — with the other following by dimension count. That is a real reduction of the remaining work, obtained entirely from results the corpus already held.

              The Murphy index as a type — making (C1) a Basis statement #

              murphyIndex_card_eq_finrank above compares a sum to a dimension. To state (C1) as what it actually is — Module.Basis I K (H_n q) — the index must be a type, and its cardinality must be the same n!.

              MurphyIndex n is that type: a partition together with an ordered pair of standard tableaux of its shape, which is exactly the (s,t) that murphyM consumes. Its cardinality is the RSK sum, so (C1) can now be written down as a basis claim over a type of known size.

                The index bridge, and lead #

                murphyIndex_card above matches cardinalities. To get lead we need the bijection, and every piece of it already exists — this is assembly, not construction.

                • Equiv.subtypeSigmaEquiv (Mathlib) turns a subtype of a Σ into a Σ over a subtype;
                • partitionShapeEquiv (SchenstedPartitionShape) re-indexes Young diagrams with n cells by Nat.Partition n, and its _apply lemma is rfl, so the fibres match definitionally;
                • Schensted.rskEquiv (SchenstedRSKEquiv) is the Robinson–Schensted correspondence itself.

                The first two are exactly the composition inside card_pairs_eq_sum's proof, used there only for its effect on cardinalities. Lifting it to the Equiv costs nothing.

                Why this is the answer to lead_inj #

                Triangular's α is forced to be the basis index type — its field is v : ι → (α →₀ R), so α is whatever indexes the coordinate vectors, here Equiv.Perm (Fin (n+1)). lead therefore cannot carry λ alongside; it must land in permutations. Since |MurphyIndex m| = m! = |S_m|, injective ⟺ bijective, and the canonical bijection between permutations and same-shape tableau pairs is RSK.

                So lead := murphyIndexEquivPerm is injective because it is an Equiv — lead_inj is discharged by Equiv.injective, with no combinatorial argument at all.

                This is what refutes the earlier candidate d(s)⁻¹ w_λ d(t): that map could never have been a bijection, because λ ↦ w_λ already collapses distinct partitions (computations/hecke/murphy_lead_injectivity_probe.py).

                      8. The Murphy family, and the route to (C1) that Mathas actually takes #

                      Why Triangular is NOT the route — a measured refutation #

                      The obvious plan was to feed m_{st} to the corpus's linear-independence lever QOU.BraidKnot.SpechtStandardBasis.Triangular against the T_w basis, with the RSK bijection lead of §7 as the leading map. lead_inj is then free (lead_injective). That plan is dead, and not merely for RSK. An exact enumeration of the T_w-supports of every m_{st} — computations/hecke/murphy_supp_rsk_probe.py, integer arithmetic throughout, complete at n = 3, 4, 5 — establishes both halves:

                      • RSK is not even a legal leading map. diag_unit forces lead x ∈ supp (m x), and RSK⁻¹(s,t) lies in that support for only 1/6, 3/24, 11/120 of the indices (11/120 with the other (P,Q) orientation: 3/6, 9/24, 23/120). The smallest witness is hand-checkable: at n = 3, λ = (2,1), s = t = tλ, we have d(s) = d(t) = 1, so m_{st} = x_λ = T_1 + T_{s₁} with support {1, s₁}, while RSK⁻¹(s,t) = s₂, which is not in it.
                      • No leading map works at all. If (≤, lead) satisfied Triangular, the order's maximum M would satisfy: M ∈ supp (m x) → lead x = M, so by injectivity M lies in exactly one support; deleting that index and M leaves the same problem one size down. So a Triangular structure exists iff this peeling never stalls. At n = 3 it stalls immediately — each of the six permutations lies in at least two of the six supports (multiplicities 4, 2, 4, 2, 2, 2), because x_{(3)} = Σ_{w ∈ S₃} T_w alone already has full support. It stalls after 1 of 24 indices at n = 4 and 5 of 120 at n = 5.

                      This is a scope-(b) structural exclusion in the sense of AGENTS.md: the invariant cited is the peeling argument above (the order-maximum must lie in exactly one support), and the n = 3 check is exhaustive rather than sampled. It excludes this lever applied to this pair of bases — it says nothing against the Murphy basis itself, which is a theorem.

                      The route that does work #

                      Mathas takes the other road, and it needs no triangularity ([mathas1999], Thm 3.20):

                      "Lemma 3.9 and Lemma 3.14 show that M spans H. By Theorem 1.11 we know that H is a free R-module of rank n!. However |M| = Σ_λ |Std(λ)|² and it is well-known that this sum is equal to n! (for example, use the Robinson–Schensted correspondence). Thus M is a basis of H."

                      Every ingredient but spanning is already sorry-free in this corpus:

                      Mathas' ingredient here
                      H free of rank n! QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.finrank_eq_factorial
                      Σ_λ (f^λ)² = n! via RSK murphyIndex_card, murphyIndexEquivPerm
                      spanning open — the one remaining input

                      So basisOfSpanning below is the whole of (C1) given spanning, and it is a genuine derivation rather than an assumption dressed up: the hypothesis is Mathas' Lemma 3.14, the conclusion (Module.Basis) is derived from it together with the two counts.

                            9. Step 2 of spanning: the one-column shape already gives every T_w #

                            Mathas' spanning argument (the input basisOfSpanning is missing) is three steps, and the first is already in the corpus. Reading the statements rather than the sentence that cites them:

                            1. {T_w} spans H_n(q) — QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.basisTwOfField.
                            2. Every T_w is a Murphy element of the one-column shape — this section.
                            3. Lemma 3.14 straightens row-standard pairs to standard ones, moving up in dominance. Since (1ⁿ) is the dominance-minimum, everything is reachable from step 2.

                            Step 2 is where the one-column shape earns its place. Its Young subgroup is trivial — every row has length one, so the row stabiliser is {1} — hence x_{(1ⁿ)} = T_1 = 1 and m_{st} = T_{d(s)}^* · 1 · T_{d(t)}. Every filling of a single column is row-standard, so d(t) ranges over the whole symmetric group; taking s to be the reference tableau gives m = T_{d(t)}, i.e. every basis element. So the row-standard Murphy family spans immediately, and spanning reduces to straightening alone.

                            The index set is the catch, and it is not a technicality. Steps 2 and 3 concern the family indexed by row-standard pairs — n! per shape — whereas MurphyIndex and murphyFamily are over standard pairs, (f^λ)² per shape. At the one-column shape the standard family has exactly one element where the row-standard family has n!. So spanning cannot even be stated for murphyFamily directly; a row-standard-indexed family has to exist first, and the corpus has no row-standard tableau type. That gap is the whole of step 3, not an indexing detail. Bean qou-phir.

                            What is proved here is the algebraic core of step 2, which needs no such type: the trivial Young subgroup contributes 1, and the two-sided conjugates of that 1 already span.
