    ∏ Lᵢ = Δ² on the bounded presentation #

    The engine of HeckeFullTwistProduct.lean — the push law, the power identity, and jmProd_eq_delta_pow — ported onto HeckeGeneratorsBelow.

    The bounds, and where they come from #

    delta k = σ₀⋯σ_{k−1} needs k < n; run b k = σ_{b+k−1}⋯σ_b needs b + k < n. Tracing which relation each lemma actually invokes gives:

    lemma relation used bound
    runB_peel_bot none —
    sigma_comm_runB far (b+i) j j + 1 < n
    sigma_comm_deltaB far i j j + 1 < n
    core_braid_pushB far b (m+1), braid m m + 2 < n
    runB_push the three above, at p + 1 = m m + 1 < n
    deltaB_pow_run runB_push m + 1 < n
    jmRawB_eq_run_mul_delta none —
    jmProdB_eq_deltaB_pow deltaB_twist_step k < n

    Two of the eight need no bound at all: they are pure consequences of the run / delta / jmRaw recursions, with no Hecke, braid or far relation anywhere. So the bound bookkeeping is confined to the relation-using half, which is why the port is tractable rather than a rewrite.

    The load-bearing arithmetic is that run_push's induction reaches index p + 1 = m, and the braid relation at m needs m + 2 < n — hence the uniform m + 1 < n on the two big inductions rather than the m < n a naive reading of "delta m is a word in σ₀…σ_{m−1}" would suggest.

    Recovery #

    As in HeckeBoundedJucysMurphy.lean, each ported result is followed by the recovery of its unbounded counterpart through HeckeGenerators.toBelow, so the port strictly generalizes.
