    Symmetric q-integers are bar-symmetric, for every n #

    Bean qou-cds1, tranche S1. The load-bearing half of the S1 finding that the bar-parity condition cannot select a Habiro generator.

    The empirical observation (PR #6282) was that the parity filter admits [2]_q, [3]_q, [4]_q, [5]_q — every symmetric q-integer tested. The reason (computations/qbeta/qbeta_bar_fixed_field_is_q_of_two_probe.py, 19/19) is that Fix(bar) = ℚ(s) with s = q + q⁻¹ = [2]_q, and every [n]_q is the Chebyshev polynomial U_{n-1}(s), hence lies in that fixed field.

    This file proves the consequence directly and for all n, without needing the Chebyshev identity or any field theory: the numerator and denominator of [n]_q are each bar-anti-symmetric, and their quotient is therefore bar-symmetric. The two minus signs cancel.

    What this is for. A gate that admits an entire infinite family cannot select one member of it. This theorem is that statement made permanent for the q-integer family: no candidate generator built from symmetric q-integers can ever be distinguished from another by bar-parity. It is a negative result about the gate, not a claim about which generator is correct.

    Base ring (§7c). Generic {R : Type*} [Field R] — Field only because [n]_q needs q⁻¹. No ordering, no Real.sqrt, no archimedean construct, no numerical q₀. Pre-archimedean throughout.

    The BarInvolution class and IsCPEven are reused from QOU.StandardModel.ThetaQcdFramingCp, which declares them in-library precisely so downstream files need not depend on the content-block sibling (braids-and-knots/bar-involution.lean) being compiled. That sibling carries a richer algebra but sits outside the QOU Lake glob and is imported by nothing.
