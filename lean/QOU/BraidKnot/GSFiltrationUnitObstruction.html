    QOU.BraidKnot.GSFiltrationUnitObstruction — the unit coefficient closes the front #

    Bean qou-09e0, continuing qou-wjbd / wpdb. Six sites read

    Z^(k)(A) = span{ e_λ : gbDepth λ = k }

    off prop:curvature-rank-gb-stratification, which stratifies words. CentreGradingObstruction excluded all three corpus readings of J^• and, via shift_cannot_fill_a_gap, every re-indexing of the surviving Coxeter one. It left the front here:

    Any candidate must either fail to be an algebra filtration, or place a second central element in F_1 — and Lemma 2 shows the Coxeter one cannot.

    This file answers that, in the negative, for a class of filtrations named precisely enough to be checked: no filtration that either places the unit in degree 0, or is monomial in the Gröbner–Shirshov normal-form basis, induces the gbDepth grading on the centre — at any n ≥ 4, under any shift.

    Why the element-level statement is so much more rigid than the dimension one #

    The previous pass compared the two sides as multiplicity functions ℕ → ℕ and had to work to exclude a shift, because a dimension sequence carries little information. But the corpus statement is an equality of spans of named elements, and that pins two things a dimension count cannot see:

    1. 1 = Σ_λ e_λ has every idempotent coordinate nonzero, so 1 ∈ span{e_λ : gbDepth λ ≤ j} forces j ≥ max gbDepth. The unit is not somewhere in the filtration; it is at the very top of it (all_strata_below_of_unit_mem).
    2. τ(e_λ) ≠ 0 for every λ, where τ is the canonical symmetrizing trace (the T_e coefficient). So T_e lies in the support of every central primitive idempotent — and a monomial filtration cannot admit any e_λ without admitting T_e = 1 along with it.

    Put together: the unit is forced to the top of the filtration by (1) and forced to the bottom by (2), and the two meet only when gbDepth is constant.

    The one algebra input, and where it is measured #

    τ(e_λ) ≠ 0 enters as the hypothesis hτ. It is standard — τ(e_λ) = d_λ / c_λ with c_λ the Schur element, nonzero at generic q — but it is load-bearing for a scope-(b) exclusion, so it is measured in docs/audits/scripts/central_idempotent_unit_coefficient.py: exactly, n = 2 … 5, two independent backends (Fraction over ℚ, and 𝔽_p with p = 2⁶¹ − 1) at several specialisations of q, with no floating point anywhere. It is measured as a rank — in the idempotent basis the form (x, y) ↦ τ(xy) restricted to Z is diag(τ(e_λ)), so non-degeneracy is the claim — which sidesteps splitting Z into idempotents. The specialisation direction is the safe one: rank B(q₀) ≤ rank B(q) generically, and the probe reports full rank.

    What is and is not claimed #

    Not claimed: that the identification is false in the abstract. As CentreGradingObstruction records, a filtration inducing the gbDepth grading exists trivially — filter by the Wedderburn blocks, F_k := ⊕_{gbDepth λ ≤ k} R·e_λ. That one satisfies every hypothesis below except hmono (it is not monomial: its pieces are spanned by idempotents, not by normal forms) and except hunit0 (its unit sits in degree n, not 0). So the two hypotheses are exactly what separates the excluded class from the realisable one, and neither is idle.

    Not claimed: that any mass or binding number is wrong. J^• enters the downstream chain as an indexing — which body order an a_D^{(n)} belongs to — not as a magnitude. Nothing here touches the Wedderburn decomposition, the Markov trace, tr_M, y_λ, the idempotents themselves, or gbDepth as a definition.

    Scope (b) — structural exclusion, with the invariant cited: the unit's idempotent coordinates are all nonzero, and τ does not vanish on any central primitive idempotent. The first is hu (immediate from 1 = Σ_λ e_λ), the second is hτ (measured above).

    1. The unit sits at the TOP of any idempotent-graded filtration #

    V j below is the corpus's span{e_λ : gbDepth λ ≤ j}, presented by its coordinate description: a central element lies in it exactly when all its idempotent coordinates in strata above j vanish. π l is the coordinate functional of e l.

    If the unit lies in stratum j, then every stratum is at or below j.

    1 = Σ_λ e_λ has every coordinate equal to 1, so it cannot be missing any idempotent — and V j is precisely the span of those with deg ≤ j. This is the half that pushes the unit to the top of the filtration.

    Note it uses Nontrivial R essentially: over the zero ring 1 = 0 and the statement is false.

It needs no monomiality at all, so it also excludes non-monomial candidates such as the Jucys–Murphy content filtration Z_{≤d} = span of symmetric functions in the L_i of degree ≤ d, whose degree- 0 piece is R·1.

    3. A monomial filtration forces the grading to be constant #

    hmono is the whole of "definable from the Gröbner–Shirshov data": if F k = span{T_w : w ∈ S k} for a set of normal forms S k, then either e ∈ S k — in which case T_e = 1 ∈ F k — or every element of F k has zero T_e-coefficient, i.e. τ vanishes on F k. Stated contrapositively, that is exactly hmono. A filtration whose pieces are not spanned by normal forms — the Wedderburn-block filtration — does not satisfy it, which is why this theorem does not contradict its existence.

    This is the answer to the question bean qou-09e0 was opened on. The front was "find a filtration putting a second central element in F_1"; the obstruction is that a monomial filtration admitting any e_λ must admit T_e = 1 too, because τ(e_λ) ≠ 0 — and then it must admit every other e_μ as well, so it cannot separate the strata at all. Every hypothesis is separately satisfiable — the Wedderburn- block filtration realises all of them but hmono, and the trivial filtration F k = ⊤ realises all of them (including hmono) at n ≤ 3, where deg genuinely is constant. What is impossible is the conjunction with a non-constant grading, which is what gbDepth_ne_on_H4 supplies. Each of hmono, hτ, hu and hbot is used in the proof term.

    5. The hypothesis set is SATISFIABLE — these are implications, not False → _ #

    A refutation is only worth the paper it is on if its hypotheses can be met. The witness below discharges every hypothesis of §3 simultaneously, over ℤ with a one-element index set: the trivial filtration F k = ⊤ on a one-block "centre", where deg is (vacuously) constant.

    Together with the delete-a-binder check — each of hV, hu, huZ, heZ, hπe, hτ, hmono, hid, hbot, hunit0 is used in the proof term, so removing any one of them stops the file compiling — this pins the content of §4 exactly: the hypotheses are jointly satisfiable, each is necessary, and what is impossible is their conjunction with a non-constant grading. That last ingredient is gbDepth_ne_on_H4, and it is the whole of what n ≥ 4 buys.

    It also says something about the mathematics rather than the bookkeeping. The model that satisfies everything is the degenerate one — one stratum — which is the n ≤ 3 situation gbDepth_const_on_H3 describes. The excluded class is realisable exactly where the partition grading has nothing to say.

    6. An independent route: the anti-diagonal "Poincaré duality of strata" #

    prf:quantum-dilogarithm-stratified Step 1 states, of the centre,

    ⟨Z^(k)(A), Z^(ℓ)(A)⟩ = 0   whenever k + ℓ ≠ N,

    and calls the residual Z^(k) × Z^(N-k) → R the Poincaré duality of curvature-rank strata. It cites prf:spectral-sequence-collapse §"Frobenius duality of strata" — which states an annihilator duality (J^ℓ)^⊥ = J^{N-ℓ} for the ideal filtration of the whole algebra A. That is a reasonable thing to say about a Frobenius algebra. What is not reasonable is transporting it to the centre's idempotent strata, and this section is why.

    On Z, the pairing is ⟨a, b⟩ = φ(ab) for the Frobenius integral φ, and distinct central primitive idempotents annihilate each other: e_λ e_μ = 0 for λ ≠ μ. So the pairing matrix in the idempotent basis is diag(φ(e_λ)) — diagonal, not anti-diagonal. A stratum therefore pairs with itself, and the anti-diagonal law can only hold if every occupied depth satisfies 2k = N, i.e. if there is a single stratum.

    So this site's Step 1 collapses to the same conclusion as §2–§3 — the grading must be constant — by a completely independent route: it uses no filtration of words at all, only the multiplication table of the idempotents and the same measured φ(e_λ) ≠ 0. It is worth recording separately because it means the site is not repaired by fixing the word/partition bridge: its own Step 1 is already inconsistent with a non-constant grading.

    Scope (b) — structural exclusion. Invariant cited: distinct central primitive idempotents multiply to zero, and the Frobenius integral does not vanish on any of them. The first is the definition of a Wedderburn decomposition; the second is the measured fact above — and is in any case implied by the same block's own assertion that the pairing restricts to Z non-degenerately.

    φ lands in any type with a zero: the pairing argument needs nothing else of the coefficients, so [Zero R] is the whole requirement.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.antidiagonal_pairing_forces_constant_grading {R : Type u_1} [Zero R] {A : Type u_2} [Ring A] {Λ : Type u_3} (deg : Λ → ℕ) (e : Λ → A) (φ : A → R) (N : ℕ) (hidem : ∀ (l : Λ), e l * e l = e l) (hφ : ∀ (l : Λ), φ (e l) ≠ 0) (hanti : ∀ (l l' : Λ), deg l + deg l' ≠ N → φ (e l * e l') = 0) (l l' : Λ) :
    deg l = deg l'

    An anti-diagonal pairing law on idempotent strata forces the grading to be constant.

    hanti is the block's own displayed hypothesis, restricted to the spanning idempotents (so it is weaker than what the block asserts, which makes the refutation stronger). hidem and hφ are the two structural inputs.

    The argument is three lines: were some l to have 2 · deg l ≠ N, hanti would give φ(e_l · e_l) = 0, i.e. φ(e_l) = 0 by idempotency, contradicting hφ. So every degree is N / 2, hence all equal.

    φ is taken as a bare function, not a linear map: linearity is never used, and the weaker hypothesis makes the theorem stronger.
