    Part 1 — boundary holonomy = braid Hecke image (PROVABLE-NOW item 1) #

    We work over an abstract group G standing for the multiplicative group generated by the Hecke images ρ(σ_i) (concretely GL_{d_λ}(ℂ) once a Wedderburn block λ is fixed). A braid word β = σ_{i₁} ⋯ σ_{i_ℓ} is presented as the list of its Hecke-image generators heckeImages = [ρ(σ_{i₁}), …, ρ(σ_{i_ℓ})]. The Yekutieli surface→boundary reduction states that the boundary holonomy of the categorical connection over the (closed) Young-diagram braid surface is exactly the ordered product braidWordHolonomy heckeImages = ρ(β).

    The map heckeImages ↦ ρ(β) is braidWordHolonomy; we prove it is a monoid homomorphism from braid-word concatenation to G — which is precisely the statement that the boundary holonomy IS the Hecke image (functorial under braid composition, carrying the Markov cocycle).

        Part 2 — q-Reeb phase = eigenphase of M_{4₁} on V_(2,1) #

        (PROVABLE-NOW item 2)

        The figure-eight operator M_{4₁} = (σ₁σ₂⁻¹)² acts on the 2-dimensional standard block V_(2,1) of H₃(q). The Hoefsmit seminormal form gives each generator σ_i as a concrete real 2×2 matrix on this block:

          σ = [ a_ρ   b      ]      a_ρ = (q − q⁻¹)/(1 − q^{−2ρ}),  b = √(1 − a_ρ²)
      [ b     a_{−ρ} ]      a_{−ρ} = h − a_ρ   (trace σ = h = q − q⁻¹)

        with det σ = q · (−q⁻¹) = −1 (eigenvalues q, −q⁻¹).

        The figure-eight knot operator on V_(2,1) is a real 2×2 matrix M with det M = (det σ)⁴ = (−1)⁴ = 1 and real trace t = tr M. For |t| < 2, the eigenvalues are e^{±iθ} on the unit circle with cos θ = t/2 — the q-Reeb rotation phase.

        We prove this as a concrete real-matrix eigenvalue identity, NOT as the (2:ℕ)=2 filler of the existing figure-eight-reeb-rotation.lean.

                        The eigenphase (det = 1, real trace ⇒ e^{±iθ} on the unit circle) #

                        For a real 2×2 matrix M with det M = 1 and trace t = tr M, the characteristic polynomial is x² − t x + 1, with roots (t ± √(t²−4))/2. When |t| < 2 the roots are complex conjugates of modulus 1: λ = e^{±iθ} with cos θ = t/2, λ · λ̄ = det M = 1.

                        We prove the eigenphase identity in the form: there is a real angle θ with 2 cos θ = t exactly when |t| ≤ 2, and then e^{iθ}·e^{−iθ} = 1 = det M and e^{iθ} + e^{−iθ} = 2 cos θ = t = tr M. This certifies e^{±iθ} ARE the eigenvalues of M.
