    Axial distance: the first real prerequisite of the seminormal form #

    The seminormal representation of H_n(q) writes the action of σᵢ on the basis vector v_T with coefficients built from the axial distance between the cells holding i and i+1 — and those coefficients have the axial distance in a denominator. So the representation does not even make sense until one knows the distance is never zero, i.e. that consecutive entries of a standard tableau never sit on the same diagonal.

    This file proves the sharp combinatorial fact that kills the diagonal case.

    The diagonal gap #

    StandardTableau.diag_gap : if (r,c) and (r+1,c+1) are both cells, the entry at the second is at least two more than the entry at the first.

    The proof is short but the "two" is the whole point. A one-step chain would only give <, which is useless — consecutive entries would still be allowed on a diagonal. The gain of two comes from the intermediate cell (r,c+1), which is a cell whenever (r+1,c+1) is (rows are weakly decreasing, so row r is at least as long as row r+1). Its entry is strictly between the two, so the outer two cannot be consecutive.

    Consequence #

    StandardTableau.not_consecutive_diag : entries i and i+1 are never at (r,c) and (r+1,c+1). That is the d = 1 case of "consecutive entries are never on the same diagonal", and it is the case the seminormal denominators actually hit.

    Honest scope #

    This is a prerequisite, not the representation. The general d ≥ 2 diagonal case, the seminormal action of σᵢ itself, and the eigenvalue q^{2·content} for Lᵢ are all still absent. JucysMurphySpectrum is untouched and the general-λ twist stays conditional.

    Refs:

    • [macdonald1995] Symmetric Functions and Hall Polynomials, I.1.
    • [wenzl1988] Hecke algebra representations (seminormal form).

      A diagonal step gains at least two.

      If (r,c) and (r+1,c+1) are both cells then the intermediate cell (r,c+1) exists — row r is at least as long as row r+1 — and its entry is strictly between the other two, by row_incr then col_incr. Hence the outer entries differ by ≥ 2.

      The strength of "two" rather than "one" is what makes this usable: it is exactly what forbids consecutive entries on a diagonal, and hence what makes the seminormal denominators nonzero.
