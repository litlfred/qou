    QOU.BraidKnot.HeckeMurphy — the Murphy element x_J, and its q-eigenvector property #

    Murphy's basis of H_n(q) is m_{st} = T_{d(s)}^* · x_λ · T_{d(t)}, where x_λ = Σ_{w ∈ S_λ} T_w is the sum over the Young subgroup of the partition λ. Everything else in that construction is conjugation by coset representatives; the defining property of x_λ, and the only thing about it that is not bookkeeping, is that it is a q-eigenvector for every generator of that subgroup:

    σ_i · x_λ = q · x_λ      for every `s_i ∈ S_λ`.

    This file proves exactly that, for an arbitrary finite set of permutations closed under right multiplication by s_i — which is weaker than being a Young subgroup, and is all the eigenvector property actually needs.

    The proof, and why it needs no case analysis at the level of the sum #

    The multiplication rule (QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.σ_mul_Tw) has two branches, on whether w ascends or descends at i. Summing over S and splitting into ascents and descents would need a bijection between the two halves.

    That work is avoidable. The two branches are exactly complementary on a pair {w, w s_i}: whichever of the two ascends, the other descends, and the coefficients recombine identically either way — so

    σ_i · (T_w + T_{w s_i}) = q · (T_w + T_{w s_i})

    holds unconditionally (σ_mul_Tw_pair). Restated as f w + f (w s_i) = 0 for f w = σ_i T_w − q T_w, that is precisely the hypothesis of Finset.sum_involution, with w ↦ w s_i the involution. So the sum vanishes with no partition of S, and — the point worth having — no hypothesis on the characteristic. The tempting one-line route (sum the pair identity over all of S to get 2σx = 2qx) does need 2 invertible; this one does not.

    Scope #

    This is the eigenvector property, not the Murphy basis. Still open, and deliberately not claimed here: that the m_{st} are a basis, and the cellular axioms (C1) and (C3) that the basis makes statable.

        The Young-subgroup form — Murphy's x_λ proper.

        A Young subgroup S_λ ≤ S_{n+1} is generated by the simple reflections it contains, so for each such s_i it is in particular closed under right multiplication by s_i, and this corollary applies. Stating it for an arbitrary subgroup rather than for the Young subgroups specifically costs nothing and avoids having to define the composition-to-subgroup map here: what the eigenvector property needs is closure, and a subgroup containing s_i has it.

        x_λ = Σ_{w ∈ S_λ} T_w is then a q-eigenvector for every generator of S_λ — which is Murphy's defining property, and the hypothesis under which m_{st} = T_{d(s)}^* x_λ T_{d(t)} is the right object to build a cellular basis on.

        4. The mirror: right multiplication, for Murphy's T_{d(t)} factor #

        Everything above multiplies on the left by σ_i, and the corpus's structure-constant table (QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.σ_mul_Tw) only goes that way. Note the handedness: left multiplication by σ_i in the algebra is right multiplication by s_i on the permutation, which is why σ_mul_murphySum asks for S closed under w ↦ w * s_i.

        Murphy's m_{st} = T_{d(s)}^* · x_λ · T_{d(t)} needs the other side, and there is no Tw_mul_σ in the corpus. It does not need one to be built from scratch: Tw_mul_Tw already gives the length-additive case in one line once T_{s_i} = σ_i (Tw_swap), and the descending case follows from it by the quadratic relation. The result is the exact mirror of σ_mul_Tw_of_lt / σ_mul_Tw_of_not_lt, with left multiplication on the permutation side.

        These belong upstream in QOU.Hecke.TwMul beside their left-handed twins; they are here because that file sits deep in the Hecke cluster and this is the only consumer so far.
