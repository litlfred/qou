    Level 6 suffices for the n = 5 blocks — the mark-6 block is [5]+1, not [6] #

    Bean bwsc, under parent bws0. That bean sets the WRT obligation at level N ≥ 7, by the reasoning "max mark-6 ⇒ degree 5 in t, so need ≥ 6 distinct fibre points". The premise is wrong, and the corpus already contains the counter-value.

    KeystoneWLambdaContinuation.block_dims_explicit gives the four blocks as

    d((5))     = 1                    d((4,1))   = t³ − 2t
d((3,2))   = t⁴ − 3t² + 1         d((3,1,1)) = t⁴ − 3t² + 2

    The mark-6 block is d((3,1,1)) = [5] + 1, the A₅-split 3 ⊕ 3′ signature — not [6] = t⁵ − 4t³ + 3t. The two agree at t = 2 (both 6, which is what "mark 6" names) and are different polynomials, of degree 4 and 5. mark_six_block_is_not_qi_six exhibits exactly that: equal at t = 2, unequal as polynomials.

    So the maximum degree over the four blocks is 4, not 5; five distinct points suffice; and the level-N fibre supplies N − 1 of them. N = 6 closes the n = 5 case, and the level-7 instance in KeystoneN5ValueMapRigidity is one root more than the mathematics needs. That is a strictly smaller obligation on the still-open half of bwsc — the WRT = modular-data agreement itself, of which KeystoneLRAmplitudeReal (#4365) supplies the Σ(2,3,5) slice.

    The bracket is now tight #

    KeystoneHabiroInfiniteRigidity.two_roots_insufficient brackets bwsc from below with 2. The sharp statement is no_finite_root_set_suffices: without a degree bound no finite set of roots suffices at all, for the same reason and with the same one-line witness V + ∏_{s ∈ S}(X − s). Two was not special.

    With the measured degree bound the bracket closes exactly: four_roots_insufficient (a corollary) fails at four, muon_block_pinned_level6 succeeds at five. Five is the threshold, not a sufficient-but-unsharp count.

    The degree hypothesis is removable, and had never been removed here #

    Bean bwsd was dissolved on 2026-08-01: eq_of_habiro_root_agreement uses the infinite fibre-root family, so agreement at all roots forces equality with no degree bound. That lemma has no consumers anywhere in the corpus, and the concrete n = 5 theorems still carry hbw : bwPoly.natDegree < 6 — the very hypothesis the dissolution removes. muon_block_pinned_all_roots and electron_block_pinned_all_roots are that wiring.

    The two forms are a genuine trade, not a strict improvement, and neither dominates: the level-6 form asks for agreement at five roots of one level; the all-roots form asks for agreement at one root of every level and pays no degree. Which is cheaper depends on what WRT actually supplies, which is bwsc and is open.

    Honest scope (STRICT) #

    Nothing here proves the gauge row bwS0 agrees with the value map at any root. That is bwsc and it stays open; what changes is its price — five roots at level 6 rather than six at level 7, or none at all if the all-levels family is available instead. No fit; nothing wired; L2 unchanged.

      No finite set of roots suffices without a degree bound.

      For any finite S and any value map V, the polynomial V + ∏_{s ∈ S}(X − s) differs from V and agrees with it on all of S. So a finite root family can only ever pin the gauge row together with a degree hypothesis — which is precisely why bwsd's dissolution had to go through the infinite family rather than a bigger finite one.
