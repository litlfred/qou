    The e_inf search is over a finite set #

    EdgeDecoupling says the REV_n excess decomposes into three pieces that stop interacting. This file is about the maximisation that decomposition leaves behind,

    e_inf  =  max over (a, b) of  [ f a + h (a + b) + g b ] ,

    where f and g are the two edge energies as functions of their block sums and h is the run. The maximisation ranges over all pairs of naturals, so on its face it is an infinite search. It is not, and the reason is elementary: f and g are bounded above while h falls without bound, so a pair whose sum is large enough cannot compete with any incumbent.

    Two results, of different kinds, matching the two halves of the argument.

    sub_le_mul_of_antitone_step and sub_le_neg_mul_of_antitone_step are the linearisation. A sequence whose downward steps δ are antitone — e "convex decreasing", in the discrete sense — satisfies

    e a - e (a + D)  ≤  D · δ a          and          e a - e (a - D)  ≤  - (D · δ a) ,

    i.e. one inequality with D carrying its own sign. Both are inductions on D over an Antitone hypothesis: no derivative, no integral, no Real.log. This is what turns each pair term of the block energy into something linear in the displacement, which is the step that makes the whole pair sum telescope.

    join_lt_of_lt_sum and le_of_le_join are the reduction. If f, g ≤ B everywhere and h ℓ < V - 2B past L, then no pair with a + b > L reaches V — so a pair that matches an incumbent V must lie in the finite window a + b ≤ L. Pure order arithmetic; it holds for whatever f, g, h happen to be.

    Why the REV_n arc wanted this #

    rev_e_infinity_search_reduction_probe.py instantiates both. For the linearisation it takes e = ε with ε(m) = -log(1 - q₀^{-2m}) and δ(m) = ε(m) - ε(m+1), checks the inequality on a grid of 2130 (d, D) pairs with both signs of D, and then uses it to bound every block uniformly in its length and its entries — the bound B_rig that makes L exist. For the reduction it takes V to be the incumbent e_inf and reports the resulting window: a + b ≤ 345 from the rigorous constants, ≤ 23 from the measured ones.

    Both signs of D are load-bearing and the probe says so. Keeping only D⁺ — dropping the negative pair terms — makes the bound diverge, because nothing then stops a long alternating profile from collecting δ(1) per step at merely linear cost.

    What none of this does is close the search. It makes the search finite; which finite set is enough, and whether the enumerated maximum is the true one, are separate questions the probe records as open.
    sub_le_mul_of_antitone_step antitone steps ⇒ e a - e (a + D) ≤ D · δ a
    sub_le_neg_mul_of_antitone_step the same inequality below a, with D negative
    join_lt_of_lt_sum a pair past the window cannot reach V
    le_of_le_join so a pair that reaches V lies in the window

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.SearchCoercivity.sub_le_mul_of_antitone_step {e δ : ℕ → ℝ} (hstep : ∀ (m : ℕ), e m - e (m + 1) = δ m) (hδ : Antitone δ) (a D : ℕ) :
    e a - e (a + D) ≤ ↑D * δ a

    Linearisation, upward. If the downward steps δ of e are antitone, then dropping D places costs at most D copies of the first step.

    δ antitone is the discrete form of "e is convex decreasing": later steps are no larger than earlier ones, so the first one dominates the average.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.SearchCoercivity.sub_le_neg_mul_of_antitone_step {e δ : ℕ → ℝ} (hstep : ∀ (m : ℕ), e m - e (m + 1) = δ m) (hδ : Antitone δ) (a D : ℕ) (hDa : D ≤ a) :
    e a - e (a - D) ≤ -(↑D * δ a)

    Linearisation, downward. The same inequality on the other side of a: climbing D places gains at least D copies of the first step, so the signed statement is uniform.

    This is the half an agent drops, and dropping it is what makes the bound in the REV_n arc diverge — the negative pair terms are exactly what stops a long alternating displacement from collecting the largest step over and over at linear cost.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.SearchCoercivity.join_lt_of_lt_sum {f g h : ℕ → ℝ} {B V : ℝ} {L : ℕ} (hf : ∀ (a : ℕ), f a ≤ B) (hg : ∀ (b : ℕ), g b ≤ B) (hh : ∀ (l : ℕ), L < l → h l < V - 2 * B) {a b : ℕ} (hab : L < a + b) :
    f a + h (a + b) + g b < V

    The reduction. With f and g bounded above by B and h below V - 2B past L, a pair whose sum exceeds L cannot reach V.

    Nothing here knows what f, g or h are: it is the observation that a bounded-plus-bounded sum cannot rescue a term that has fallen far enough.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.SearchCoercivity.le_of_le_join {f g h : ℕ → ℝ} {B V : ℝ} {L : ℕ} (hf : ∀ (a : ℕ), f a ≤ B) (hg : ∀ (b : ℕ), g b ≤ B) (hh : ∀ (l : ℕ), L < l → h l < V - 2 * B) {a b : ℕ} (hV : V ≤ f a + h (a + b) + g b) :
    a + b ≤ L

    The finite window. Contrapositive of join_lt_of_lt_sum: a pair that matches the incumbent V lies in a + b ≤ L, so the maximisation is over a finite set.
