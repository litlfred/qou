    3.14's induction measure, both axes combined #

    PartitionLexWellFounded gives the outer axis (lex across shapes), DominanceWellFounded the inner one (dominance within a shape). Mathas' Lemma 3.14 inducts on both at once: case (i) drops the shape in lex order, case (ii) keeps the shape and moves up dominance. So the measure it actually runs on is the lexicographic product, and that is what this file builds and proves well-founded.

    murphyLex n  :  Σ μ : PartOf n, Tabloid μ.1   →   … → Prop

    with ⟨μ, a⟩ > ⟨ν, b⟩ when μ >ₗ ν, or μ = ν and a strictly dominates b.

    Why a sigma, and why that is not incidental #

    The tableau type depends on the shape (Specht.Tabloid μ), so the pair cannot be a product — it is a Sigma. Sigma.Lex's right constructor is stated for two elements of the same fibre, which is exactly what case (ii) needs and means no transport appears anywhere in the argument.

    The well-foundedness route #

    Mathlib's Sigma.Lex carries mono/decidable/swap lemmas but, at the imports this corpus has, no well-foundedness lemma (checked by enumerating the environment, not by grepping source — a Mathlib source grep is worthless here, see scripts/lean-probes/).

    So the proof goes the same way as both axes did: Sigma.Lex of two strict orders is a strict order, the sigma of a Fintype with Fintype fibres is finite, and a strict order on a finite type is well-founded. The transitivity is a four-case analysis and the irreflexivity a two-case one; neither needs the fibres to be related across shapes.

    Scope — this makes 3.14 STATABLE, not proved #

    With this, an induction proving 3.14 can be written down: murphyLex_induction is the recursor it would use. The mathematics of the Garnir step is untouched — that is the content of the lemma and remains the arc's open problem. What is settled is that the induction it needs terminates.

    🛑 RESOLVED — read this, not the doubt it replaced. The doubt was: the corpus retired a tabloid-dominance measure for Garnir straightening at q = 1 on an exact-arithmetic counterexample (μ=(3,2), t=[[1,2,3],[5,4]]), concluding "Garnir produces terms whose tabloids are dominance-incomparable to {t}", hence "no tabloid-level measure orders them" (SpechtStandardBasisCore.lean §"Measure correction (2026-07-07)"). This axis is a tabloid-level measure, so the doubt was real.

    Mathas' own proof of 3.14 settles it, and in this axis's favour. Its case (ii) step is

    m_st = T*_{d(s)} h T_w + Σ_{v ▷ t} r_v m_sv

    — the same-shape terms are indexed by the dominance relation itself, so they cannot be incomparable, and the relation is ▷, strict (Corollary 3.15 states the same: "ν = λ, u = s and v ▷ t"). 3.14's statement uses the loose ⊵; its induction steps on ▷. domGtOn is exactly that strict part, and domGtOn_induction's direction — hypothesis available for strictly-dominating tableaux — is what "by induction on t" needs.

    Why the q = 1 counterexample does not carry, sharper than "different step": there the terms fall out of a coset sum and their tabloids land where they land, so incomparability is possible; here the term set is defined as those v dominating t. Different mechanisms, one of which cannot produce an incomparable term at all. colWordNum remains the right measure for the q = 1 polytabloid straightening and is not needed here.

    Full derivation and the two remaining 3.14 inputs it identifies (3.12, and Corollary 3.8, not previously named on this arc): docs/audits/2026-09-06-tabloid-dominance-vs-the-column-word-measure.md.
