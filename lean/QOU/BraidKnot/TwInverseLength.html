    T_w⁻¹ lives in length ≤ ℓ(w) — the Bruhat-free half of triangularity #

    Reaching Mathas' Lemma 3.9 at the level of m_{st} (rather than of x_μ, which CompositionIdeal.exists_xComp_swap settles) means expanding T_d⁻¹ in the T-basis and controlling where it lands. Measured exactly over S_2, S_3, S_4 in tw_inverse_support_probe.py, the support is the Bruhat interval [e, w⁻¹] — note the inverse, which is the Tw_mul_Tw handedness flip again — with leading coefficient q^{-ℓ(w)}.

    The full statement needs a Bruhat order, and there is none to hand: Mathlib's GroupTheory/Coxeter/ carries no Bruhat at all, and the corpus's QOU/Mathlib/KazhdanLusztigBasis.lean is an abstract BarInvolution/KLBasis axiomatisation with no concrete construction. Building that order is a separate piece of work.

    This file proves the half that needs none of it: the length bound, measured 32/32 by the same probe. It is the "no escape upward" half of triangularity, and unlike the Bruhat half it requires nothing beyond the corpus's own multiplication rule — σ_mul_Tw already says σ_i · T_v lies in the span of T_v and T_{v·sᵢ}, in both of its branches.

    Why the inverse is built rather than taken #

    CompositionIdeal.isUnit_Tw gives T_w a unit, but a unit's inverse is opaque: nothing about ↑u⁻¹ can be induced over. So TwInvOf builds the inverse explicitly as the product of the σᵢ⁻¹ along the reversed word — Tw_mul_TwInv and TwInv_mul_Tw check that it is the inverse — and the span bound is then a list induction on that word.

    Note the ordering: TwOf folds as ω.foldl (fun h i => σ i * h) 1, i.e. σ_{i_k} ⋯ σ_{i_1}, so the inverse is σ_{i_1}⁻¹ ⋯ σ_{i_k}⁻¹ — a foldr, not a foldl. Getting that backwards produces something that is not an inverse at all.
