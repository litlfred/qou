    The hook sum in β-coordinates #

    The β-numbers of a Young diagram at L rows are the first-column hook lengths of the diagram padded to L rows,

    β_i = λ_i + (L − 1 − i),        i < L,

    already in the corpus as QOU.SchurWeyl.betaR. They strictly decrease, and the multiset of hook lengths of row i is {1, …, β_i} with the β_i − β_j (j > i) removed — which turns two cell-indexed statistics into row-indexed ones:
    (III) Σ_{i<L} β_i = n + L(L−1)/2
    (II) Σ_{c∈μ} h(c) = Σ_{i<L} β_i(β_i+1)/2 − Σ_{i<j<L} (β_i − β_j)

    Both are stated here doubled, to stay in ℤ without division: two_mul_sum_betaR and two_mul_sum_hookZ_betaForm.

    (II) is not proved here from the hook multiset. It is derived from the closed form already in HookSumClosedForm — 2Σ_h = p₂(λ) + p₂(λ') — together with the n(λ) register lemmas of that file, which give p₂(λ') = n + 2·Σ_i i·λ_i. The whole β-side then collapses by a termwise polynomial identity in (λ_i, L, i) plus the vanishing of one pure-L sum, sum_shift_quad_eq_zero. That vanishing is the content: −3Σm² + (2L−1)Σm = 0 for m_i = L − 1 − i, which is why (II) holds for every padding L ≥ ℓ(μ) rather than only at L = ℓ(μ). The padding-independence is not a side remark — it is what makes the identity usable against whatever row bound a caller already has, exactly as HookSumClosedForm's N/M hypotheses are.

    What this is for #

    The REV_n / Z_n(q) asymptotics arc (bean qou-hok7) works in these coordinates: the shape free energy's one-body term is Σ_i F(β_i) and its two-body term is −Σ_{i<j} ε(β_i − β_j), and the energy H = Σ_c h(c) enters through exactly (II). The two identities were verified numerically over every partition of n ≤ 16 by computations/deltalambda/rev_beta_coordinate_identity_probe.py; this file is the formalize-first discharge of that numerical check.

    Prior art #

    SYTBetaGapVandermonde and SchurPrincipalBetaForm establish the β-numbers and their products (the Vandermonde denominator of the hook-length formula). No identity about sums over the β-numbers existed anywhere in the corpus before this file — the product side and the sum side are independent uses of the same coordinates.

    ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, (-3 * (↑L - 1 - ↑i) ^ 2 + (2 * ↑L - 1) * (↑L - 1 - ↑i)) = 0

    The pure-L part of the β-form vanishes. With m_i = L − 1 − i, −3 Σ m_i² + (2L−1) Σ m_i = 0 for every L — the reason (II) is independent of the padding.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.HookSum.two_mul_sum_hookZ_betaForm (μ : YoungDiagram) {L M : ℕ} (hL : μ.colLen 0 ≤ L) (hM : μ.rowLen 0 ≤ M) :
    2 * ∑ c ∈ μ.cells, PieceA.hookZ μ c = ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, ↑(SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i) * (↑(SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i) + 1) - 2 * ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, ∑ j ∈ Finset.Ico (i + 1) L, (↑(SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i) - ↑(SchurWeyl.betaR L μ j))

    (II) The hook sum in β-coordinates, doubled to stay in ℤ:

    2 Σ_{c∈μ} h(c) = Σ_{i<L} β_i(β_i + 1) − 2 Σ_{i<j<L} (β_i − β_j),

    for any padding L ≥ ℓ(μ) and any column bound M ≥ λ_1. Equivalently Σ_c h(c) = Σ_i C(β_i + 1, 2) − Σ_{i<j}(β_i − β_j), which is the row-indexed form of the statement that row i's hooks are {1,…,β_i} ∖ {β_i − β_j : j > i}.

    Derived from two_mul_sum_hookZ_eq_sum_sq and the n(λ)-register lemmas, not from the hook multiset.

    (I') The general one-body / two-body decomposition #

    The identity (II) above is the g = id shadow of something much more general, and the general form is what the REV_n arc actually runs on: the shape free energy's one-body term is Σ_i F(β_i) and its two-body term is −Σ_{i<j} ε(β_i − β_j), for F the partial sums of ε.

    That general form is already in the corpus multiplicatively — QOU.SchurWeyl.hookSide_betaR states it for an arbitrary CommMonoid, as the divisor-free hook-length formula with its Vandermonde denominator. Transporting it through Multiplicative N turns the products into sums at no cost. So (I') needs no new combinatorial input here; what it needed was noticing that the product identity and the sum identity are the same statement.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.HookSum.sum_hookF_add_pairF {N : Type u_1} [AddCommMonoid N] (g : ℕ → N) (μ : YoungDiagram) {L : ℕ} (hL : μ.colLen 0 ≤ L) :
    ∑ c ∈ μ.cells, g (QBeta.WenzlWeight.hook μ c) + ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (L - (i + 1)), g (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i - SchurWeyl.betaR L μ (i + 1 + k)) = ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i), g (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i - j)

    (I') For any g : ℕ → N into an additive commutative monoid,

    Σ_{c∈μ} g(h(c)) + Σ_{i<j<L} g(β_i − β_j) = Σ_{i<L} Σ_{m=1}^{β_i} g(m),

    for every padding L ≥ ℓ(μ). The j-index runs as i+1+k, matching the corpus's hookSide_betaR; sum_hookZ_betaForm_of_hookMultiset below reindexes it to Ico (i+1) L.

    Row i's hook lengths are {1, …, β_i} with {β_i − β_j : j > i} removed — that is the whole content, and it is hookSide_betaR's, not this file's.

    The β-side does not see the padding #

    (I') has L on its right-hand side and on one summand of its left, but the other summand — the cell sum — has no L at all. Rearranged over a group that is the whole statement of the two results below: the one-body sum minus the pair sum is the cell sum, hence is the same for every admissible padding.

    This is exactly the quantity the REV_n arc calls the β-side, Σ_i F(β_i) − Σ_{i<j} ε(β_i − β_j) with F the partial sums of ε — take g = ε and note Σ_{j<β_i} g(β_i − j) = Σ_{m=1}^{β_i} ε(m) = F(β_i). Since the hook sum H = Σ_c h(c) is cell-indexed and likewise L-free, the arc's whole objective V is padding-invariant.

    Why this is worth a name. A maximisation of V over shapes at fixed row count ℓ is a real search; a maximisation over ℓ above the shape's own row count is not a search at all — it is re-reading one number. PR #7294 scanned ℓ ∈ [k, k+5] and recorded a caveat that three of its eleven points "sit at the cap, so those three are censored downward". That caveat is withdrawn: the theorem says the scan was over padding, so a value at the cap is the same value as at every larger ℓ, and the only content in the scan is that ℓ must be at least the maximiser's row count (which is why ℓ = k and ℓ = k+1 genuinely lose). Measured in agreement afterwards — rev_l_cap_is_padding_invariance_probe.py, where the residual across ℓ is 1e-49 at mp.dps = 50.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.HookSum.betaSide_eq_hookSum {N : Type u_1} [AddCommGroup N] (g : ℕ → N) (μ : YoungDiagram) {L : ℕ} (hL : μ.colLen 0 ≤ L) :
    ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i), g (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i - j) - ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (L - (i + 1)), g (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i - SchurWeyl.betaR L μ (i + 1 + k)) = ∑ c ∈ μ.cells, g (QBeta.WenzlWeight.hook μ c)

    The β-side is the cell sum. For any g into an additive commutative group,

    Σ_{i<L} Σ_{m=1}^{β_i} g(m) − Σ_{i<j<L} g(β_i − β_j) = Σ_{c∈μ} g(h(c)) .

    The right-hand side carries no L, so the left-hand side is the same for every padding L ≥ ℓ(μ).

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.HookSum.betaSide_padding_invariant {N : Type u_1} [AddCommMonoid N] (g : ℕ → N) (μ : YoungDiagram) {L L' : ℕ} (hL : μ.colLen 0 ≤ L) (hL' : μ.colLen 0 ≤ L') :
    ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i), g (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i - j) + ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L', ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (L' - (i + 1)), g (SchurWeyl.betaR L' μ i - SchurWeyl.betaR L' μ (i + 1 + k)) = ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L', ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (SchurWeyl.betaR L' μ i), g (SchurWeyl.betaR L' μ i - j) + ∑ i ∈ Finset.range L, ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (L - (i + 1)), g (SchurWeyl.betaR L μ i - SchurWeyl.betaR L μ (i + 1 + k))

    Padding invariance, subtraction-free. The same statement over a mere additive commutative monoid: the β-side at L and at L' agree, written without subtraction by adding the other padding's pair sum to each.

    (II) again, from the hook multiset — an independent second proof #

    Everything above (II) went through HookSumClosedForm's 2Σ_h = p₂(λ) + p₂(λ'). The route below instead specialises (I') at g = (· : ℤ). The two proofs share no step: one is a reflection argument over the cells, the other is the hook-length formula's β-multiset. Agreement is therefore a check on both.

    ↑(QBeta.WenzlWeight.hook μ c) = PieceA.hookZ μ c

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.HookSum.two_mul_sum_desc (b : ℕ) :
