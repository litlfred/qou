    QOU.BraidKnot.TwoFiltrationBridge — conjectural class (§3b) #

    §3b class axiomatisation for content block conj:two-filtration-bridge: Two-filtration bridge: iterated-integral depth $\leftrightarrow$ partition-cellular GB-depth via the central idempotents $e_\lambda$.

    This is a conjecture: the class carries the conjecture's actual content as the bridge field (the depth-lift dichotomy); the noncomputable instance's sorry represents the open conjecture itself (per AGENTS.md §3b), NOT a missing proof.

    Mathlib-infra pass (2026-06-25, Tier 1). Both partition-side objects are now real: the partition index is Mathlib YoungDiagram with the genuine conjugate involution YoungDiagram.transpose (self-conjugacy = μ.transpose = μ), and the partition-cellular GB-depth is the concrete definition gbDepth = max hook length over cells (built here from the real rowLen/colLen/cells API) — no longer an abstract dGB class field. Only the QOU-specific iterated-integral depth-lift dII and the chiral-pair predicate remain abstract (the open conjectural content). The faithful conditional form is QOU.AlgebraicSubstrate.TwoFiltrationBridgeGeneral.

        class QOU.BraidKnot.TwoFiltrationBridge :
        Type

        Two-filtration bridge (iterated-integral depth $\leftrightarrow$ partition-cellular GB-depth via $e_\lambda$).

        Conjectural class — see conj:two-filtration-bridge.

        • dII : YoungDiagram → ℕ

          Iterated-integral depth-lift $d_{\mathrm{II}}(\lambda)$ of the central idempotent $e_\lambda$ (the grouplike side).

        • isChiralPair : YoungDiagram → Prop

          Predicate selecting the depth-3 chiral-pair period family.

        • bridge (μ : YoungDiagram) : (μ.transpose = μ → dII μ = 2) ∧ (isChiralPair μ → dII μ = 3) ∧ (μ.transpose ≠ μ → ¬isChiralPair μ → dII μ ≤ gbDepth μ)

          The conjecture (depth-lift dichotomy, from the manuscript): a self-conjugate cell ($\mu^\top = \mu$) lifts to depth $2$ (real / Bloch–Wigner period), a chiral pair to depth $3$ (Tate-twisted period), and every other cell's lift is bounded by its concrete GB-depth gbDepth μ (max hook length).

          What gbDepth actually grades — bean wpdb #

          prf:phi-q-categorical-truncation Step 3 and two sibling blocks use Z^(k)(A) = span{eλ : gbDepth λ = k} as the centre's depth grading, citing prop:curvature-rank-gb-stratification — which stratifies by the curvature rank of words and mentions no partition. That bridge is stated nowhere (audit: docs/audits/2026-08-16-word-depth-vs-partition-depth-bridge-is-unstated.md).

          This section does not close it. It measures the partition side, and what it finds bears directly on the downstream use: the grading is far coarser than its notation suggests, and at the strand count where the framework actually consumes it, it is trivial.

          source
          theorem QOU.BraidKnot.gbDepth_eq_corner (μ : YoungDiagram) (h : μ.cells.Nonempty) :
          gbDepth μ = μ.rowLen 0 + μ.colLen 0 - 1

          The GB-depth is the corner hook, λ₁ + λ'₁ - 1.

          The maximum over all cells is attained at (0,0): rows and columns are antitone, so every cell's arm and leg are dominated by the corner's.

          source
          theorem QOU.BraidKnot.gbDepth_eq_of_border_eq (μ ν : YoungDiagram) (hμ : μ.cells.Nonempty) (hν : ν.cells.Nonempty) (hrow : μ.rowLen 0 = ν.rowLen 0) (hcol : μ.colLen 0 = ν.colLen 0) :
          gbDepth μ = gbDepth ν

          The grading is blind to the interior. Two diagrams sharing a first row and a first column have the same GB-depth however differently they are shaped below and to the right of the corner — an immediate consequence of gbDepth_eq_corner, and the reason the stratification is coarse.

                theorem QOU.BraidKnot.gbDepth_const_on_H3 :

                At n = 3 the partition grading has exactly one stratum.

                All three H₃ blocks have GB-depth 3, so Z(H₃) = Z^(3) and every other stratum is zero.

                The downstream consequence is the point. prop:phi-q-categorical-truncation sets k⋆(T_w) := max {gbDepth λ : λ ∈ supp(T_w)} and states that the truncation depth is "structurally locked to the NF support of T_w". At n = 3 that maximum is 3 for every T_w with nonempty support — it is constant, so it locks onto nothing. Whatever the truncation claim is worth at the single-nucleon level, it is not carried by the support.

                Scope-(a): this says the grading is degenerate here, not that the truncation is false. It becomes non-trivial from n = 4, where (2,2) has depth 3 against 4 for the four hooks.

                    gbDepth is the content spread — the form the algebra side can meet #

                    gbDepth_eq_corner says the depth is λ₁ + λ'₁ - 1. That is a statement about the shape's bounding arms, and nothing in it speaks the language of H_n(R, q). This section rewrites it in a form that does.

                    The content of a cell (i, j) is j - i, its diagonal. Contents are what the Jucys–Murphy elements measure: L_k acts on the λ-isotypic component by the content of the cell added at step k, and the centre of H_n(R, q) is generated by symmetric functions in the L_k. So a statement about contents is a statement the centre can hear; one about hook maxima is not.

                    gbDepth_eq_content_spread below says the two coincide: the GB-depth is the number of distinct diagonals λ meets. That does not close the word-depth ↔ partition-depth bridge (bean wpdb) — it relocates the partition side onto the axis the algebra side is indexed by, which is what a proof would have to match.

                    The content, its extremes, and the corner-positivity lemmas were originally defined locally here — a sixth copy of a one-line definition already present five times in the tree. Worse, the spelling chosen to dodge a shadowing problem (cellContent, so as not to occupy the bare QOU.BraidKnot.content slot that open QOU.BraidKnot would shadow) collided head-on with QOU.BraidKnot.cellContent in the standalone sibling mass-theory/jucys-murphy-central-elements-qou.lean: the same fully-qualified name at an incompatible signature. Shadowing is a readability hazard; a duplicate declaration is a hard error the moment anything imports both. The rename traded down.

                    Both are now gone: this file imports QOU.MathlibExt.YoungDiagramCells and uses its content / sup_content / inf_content / rowLen0_pos / colLen0_pos. That module is the single home for the nine copies (bean qou-cdup); this is the first migration onto it.

                    source
                    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.gbDepth_eq_content_spread (μ : YoungDiagram) (h : μ.cells.Nonempty) :
                    ↑(gbDepth μ) = μ.cells.sup' h MathlibExt.YoungDiagramCells.content - μ.cells.inf' h MathlibExt.YoungDiagramCells.content + 1

                    The GB-depth counts the diagonals λ meets.

                    gbDepth μ = (max content − min content) + 1. Since the contents of a Young diagram fill the whole range between their extremes — the first row supplies 0 … λ₁-1 and the first column -(λ'₁-1) … 0 — this is exactly the number of distinct contents.

                    This is the same number as the corner hook, said in the algebra's coordinates rather than the diagram's.

                    source
                    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.content_image_eq_range (μ : YoungDiagram) (h : μ.cells.Nonempty) :
                    Finset.image MathlibExt.YoungDiagramCells.content μ.cells = Finset.image (fun (j : ℕ) => ↑j - (↑(μ.colLen 0) - 1)) (Finset.range (gbDepth μ))

                    The contents of λ are exactly the integers j - (λ'₁ - 1) for 0 ≤ j < gbDepth λ — an unbroken interval, no gaps.

                    Both inclusions are cheap and neither is vacuous. Forward: every cell's content lies between -(λ'₁-1) and λ₁-1 because its row and column lengths are dominated by the corner's. Backward: a non-negative target is hit by a cell of the first row and a negative one by a cell of the first column, and the bound j < λ₁+λ'₁-1 is exactly what puts the witness inside the diagram.

                    The algebraic restatement, conditional on the seminormal eigenvalue GAP #

                    source
                    class QOU.BraidKnot.SeminormalEigenvalue (K : Type u_1) :
                    Type u_1

                    The seminormal eigenvalue theorem, carried as a §3b-cond hypothesis.

                    The content is: the Jucys–Murphy element L_k acts on the seminormal basis of the λ-block by a scalar depending only on the content of the cell added at step k — q^{2c} in the QOU convention — and that assignment is injective at generic q. This is not proved in the corpus: QJucysMurphy.lean records it as an open GAP (Ref: [ram1997] Thm 4.1, [murphy1992], [hoefsmit1974]).

                    Per §3b-cond the class carries the hypotheses, never the conclusion. The two fields are the eigenvalue assignment and its injectivity; card_eigen_image below derives the count from them rather than asserting it. Discharging this class is option A of bean wpdb and would make the results downstream of it unconditional; it is deliberately left without an instance, which is the honest representation of an open statement.

                    • eigenOfContent : ℤ → K

                      The eigenvalue L_k acts by, as a function of the added cell's content.

                    • eigenOfContent_injective : Function.Injective eigenOfContent

                      At generic q the assignment c ↦ q^{2c} separates contents.

                        Where this stops, and why #

                        The natural next step is to read the content spread back inside the algebra. The Jucys–Murphy element L_k acts on the seminormal basis with eigenvalue q^{2c(k)} (QOU convention, QOU/SchurWeyl/QJucysMurphy.lean), and c ↦ q^{2c} is injective at generic q, so

                        gbDepth λ = #{ distinct JM eigenvalues on the λ-block }

                        would be an intrinsically algebraic restatement — no diagram, no hook — and that is the shape the word side would have to match.

                        It is not provable here, and the obstruction is not this file's. The seminormal eigenvalue statement is itself an open GAP in the corpus: QJucysMurphy.lean records "Seminormal eigenvalue — GAP: L_k acts with eigenvalue q^{2c(k)} on the seminormal basis" (Ref: [ram1997] Thm 4.1, [murphy1992], [hoefsmit1974]), and the block-eigenvalue statement is a further GAP downstream of it.

                        So the bridge bottoms out in an already-known gap rather than in a new one. Closing it needs the seminormal eigenvalue theorem first — after which the identity above should follow from gbDepth_eq_content_spread plus injectivity of c ↦ q^{2c}, and the remaining question becomes the well-definedness of the word filtration (bean wpdb).
