    The Hecke relation's two roots FORCE the twist on the 1-dimensional blocks #

    KeystoneModularTwist.lean builds the modular T-matrix θ_λ := q^{c(λ)} and proves its transpose law, but its header hedges: "for the Hecke MTC the exponent is (up to convention) the content sum". Nothing in the corpus discharged that hedge — the exponent was defined, not derived, so θ was a definition wearing a physical name.

    This file discharges the hedge for the two one-dimensional blocks, from the corpus's own Hecke convention (σᵢ − q)(σᵢ + q⁻¹) = 0 and nothing else.

    The argument #

    A one-dimensional representation sends every σᵢ to a scalar s satisfying the Hecke relation. Over a field that relation has exactly two roots — isHeckeRoot_iff — namely q (trivial) and −q⁻¹ (sign). There is no third choice and no free parameter.

    The full twist Δ² = (σ₁σ₂⋯σ_{n−1})^n is a positive braid word of length n(n−1), so on a one-dimensional rep it acts by s^{n(n−1)}. Then:

    • trivial root: q^{n(n−1)};
    • sign root: (−q⁻¹)^{n(n−1)} = q^{−n(n−1)} — the minus sign cancels because the word length is even (fullTwistLength_even), which is why the sign block gets a genuine inverse power of q rather than a sign.

    Against these, 2·c([n]) = n(n−1) — which the corpus already proves (two_mul_contentSum_single_row) — gives θ_{[n]}² = q^{n(n−1)}, and the transpose law c(λ') = −c(λ) (contentSum_conj) gives the column block its inverse. So the content-sum exponent is not a convention on these blocks: it is the Hecke root, squared.

    Honest scope #

    This is a scope-(a) result, not the general theorem. It covers λ = [n] and λ = (1ⁿ) — the one-dimensional blocks — because those are the ones a character argument can reach. It does not derive θ_λ = q^{c(λ)} for general λ; that needs the Jucys–Murphy elements acting on the seminormal basis with eigenvalue q^{2·content}, which the corpus does not yet carry in Lean. The hedge in KeystoneModularTwist.lean therefore stands for dim V_λ > 1 and is not edited by this file.

    The covered case is nonetheless the one the mass path uses: at n = 3 the column block gives θ_{(1³)} = q^{−3}, the /q³ appearing in F_Bor (beans qou-w1j9, qou-gzs0).

    Refs:

    • [macdonald1995] Symmetric Functions and Hall Polynomials, I.1 (content, conjugate).
    • [bakalovkirillov2001] Lectures on Tensor Categories and Modular Functors (S, T modular data).

          Main result — the Hecke root determines the one-dimensional twist.

          Whichever of the two roots a one-dimensional block takes, its full twist Δ² is θ² at the corresponding partition: the one-row [n] for the trivial root, the one-column [n]' for the sign root. The content-sum exponent is therefore derived on these blocks rather than chosen, which is what the KeystoneModularTwist header's "(up to convention)" left open.

          Scope: one-dimensional blocks only — see the module docstring.
