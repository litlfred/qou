    The mirror side: T_a · x_μ collapses onto the right-coset representative #

    CompositionCosetReduce.xComp_mul_Tw_reduce handles x_μ · T_a, on the left coset e·S_μ with e⁻¹ distinguished. Mathas' m_{st} = T*_{d(s)} · x_μ · T_{d(t)} has a factor on each side, so 3.9 needs the mirror too:

    `T_a · x_μ = q^{ℓ(v)} · (T_d · x_μ)`,   `a = v·d`, `d ∈ D_μ`, `v ∈ S_μ`

    and this time it is the ordinary right coset S_μ·d, with IsDistinguished rather than IsDistinguishedL. The two orientations are not interchangeable — see the handedness note in CompositionCosetReduce — and having both is exactly what lets a two-sided expression be reduced on both sides independently.

    Structure #

    Tw_mul_xComp_of_mem mirrors CompositionEigenPower.xComp_mul_Tw_of_mem and its proof is the same induction, differing only in which of the two eigenvector identities it consumes (σ_mul_xComp here, xComp_mul_σ there). In particular it reuses descent_index_eq verbatim: that lemma is about v alone, not about which side it is multiplied on, so the "a Young-subgroup element can only descend within a block" argument carries over unchanged.
