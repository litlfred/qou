    QOU.BraidKnot.SpechtAbsolutelyIrreducible — the commutant of S^μ is the base field #

    W2 of the Wedderburn epic (qou-bxw4): the division rings 𝔇 i appearing in the abstract Wedderburn split WedderburnSn.sn_wedderburnArtin_alg collapse to the base field R. That collapse is exactly absolute irreducibility of the Specht modules, and its module-theoretic content is the statement proved here:

    every S_n-equivariant k-linear endomorphism of S^μ is a scalar multiple of the identity.

    Mathlib supplies the collapse only over an algebraically closed base (IsSemisimpleRing.exists_algEquiv_pi_matrix_of_isAlgClosed, via Schur + algebraic closure); over a general characteristic-zero field it is a genuine S_n fact, and it is the first of the four items listed as the residual of SnWedderburnPartitionBridge.sn_isotypic_specht_split.

    The proof (James/Sagan, on the corpus' own κ_t machinery) #

    No rationality argument and no character theory is needed — the column antisymmetrizer does all the work, over any field of characteristic zero:

    1. κ_t maps all of M^μ into the line k·e_t (SpechtIrreducible.kapL_mem_line, James §4.6 — already sorry-free in this corpus, it is the core of the submodule theorem).
    2. κ_t e_t = |C_t| · e_t (SpechtCrossKappa.kapL_polytabloid, from κ_t² = |C_t| κ_t).
    3. κ_t is a ℤ-combination of the group action, so an equivariant θ commutes with it. Hence |C_t| · θ(e_t) = θ(κ_t e_t) = κ_t (θ e_t) ∈ k·e_t, and |C_t| ≠ 0 in characteristic zero gives θ(e_t) = c · e_t for a single scalar c.
    4. The polytabloids are a single S_n-orbit (tableau_transitive + polytabloid_rep_equivariant), so equivariance propagates that same c to every e_s; they span S^μ, so θ = c · id.

    Step 3 is the step that avoids extending θ to M^μ — the same manoeuvre SpechtClassification.dom_of_kapCross uses for the cross-shape dominance theorem. This file reuses that pattern rather than re-deriving it.

    Scope #

    • Characteristic zero only (step 3 divides by |C_t|). Nothing here needs simplicity of S^μ, the standard basis, dominance, or Garnir straightening.
    • This is the commutant statement. Turning it into the block-level 𝔇 i ≃ R of sn_isotypic_specht_split also needs the labelling (W1) and the block-size identification (W3); those are separate items of the epic and are not claimed here.

    Absolute irreducibility of S^μ (the W2 content). Over a characteristic-zero field, every S_n-equivariant k-linear endomorphism of the Specht module S^μ is a scalar.

    Equivalently: the commutant End_{k[S_n]}(S^μ) is k itself — no genuine division ring appears, which is precisely the collapse 𝔇 i ≃ k that the abstract Wedderburn split (WedderburnSn.sn_wedderburnArtin_alg) leaves open over a non-algebraically-closed base.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.SpechtAbsolutelyIrreducible.specht_end_comm {k : Type} [Field k] [CharZero k] {μ : YoungDiagram} (θ ψ : ↥(Specht.SpechtSubmodule μ) →ₗ[k] ↥(Specht.SpechtSubmodule μ)) (hθ : ∀ (σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin (Specht.nOf μ))) (x : ↥(Specht.SpechtSubmodule μ)), θ (((SpechtClassification.spechtRep μ) σ) x) = ((SpechtClassification.spechtRep μ) σ) (θ x)) (hψ : ∀ (σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin (Specht.nOf μ))) (x : ↥(Specht.SpechtSubmodule μ)), ψ (((SpechtClassification.spechtRep μ) σ) x) = ((SpechtClassification.spechtRep μ) σ) (ψ x)) :
    θ ∘ₗ ψ = ψ ∘ₗ θ

    The commutant is commutative. An immediate consequence of specht_end_scalar: any two equivariant endomorphisms of S^μ commute, since both are scalars. This is the form in which the Wedderburn block collapse consumes absolute irreducibility — a noncommutative division ring 𝔇 i would supply a noncommuting pair.

    The division-ring collapse, at module level #

    specht_end_scalar is a statement about Specht modules. The Wedderburn blocks are indexed by the simple k[S_n]-modules, and a block's division ring 𝔇 i is the commutant of the corresponding simple. Composing with the classification theorem (SpechtClassification.specht_exhausts_irreducibles — every simple k[S_n]-module is a Specht module, closed by a sibling in the 2026-08 window) upgrades the Specht statement to the general one: no simple k[S_n]-module admits a non-scalar equivariant endomorphism, over any characteristic-zero field. That is item (a) of the residual listed in SnWedderburnPartitionBridge, in module-theoretic form.

    What it is not: the block-level 𝔇 i ≃ R inside a specific ∏ Matrix split still needs the index↔partition labelling (W1) to say which simple sits in block i. That is a separate epic item; nothing here claims it.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.SpechtAbsolutelyIrreducible.simple_end_scalar {k : Type} [Field k] [CharZero k] {n : ℕ} {W : Type} [AddCommGroup W] [Module k W] (ρ : Representation k (Equiv.Perm (Fin n)) W) (hsimple : IsSimpleModule (MonoidAlgebra k (Equiv.Perm (Fin n))) ρ.asModule) (θ : W →ₗ[k] W) (hθ : ∀ (g : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)) (x : W), θ ((ρ g) x) = (ρ g) (θ x)) :
    ∃ (c : k), ∀ (x : W), θ x = c • x

    No simple k[S_n]-module has a non-scalar commutant. Over a characteristic-zero field, every S_n-equivariant k-linear endomorphism of a simple k[S_n]-module is a scalar.

    Proof: transport an equivariant endomorphism along the classification isomorphism to the Specht model, apply specht_end_scalar, and transport back. The intertwiner is a k-linear equivalence, so the scalar is preserved.

    This is the module-theoretic form of the division-ring collapse: the commutant of a simple module is its Wedderburn block's division ring, and here it is the base field itself.
