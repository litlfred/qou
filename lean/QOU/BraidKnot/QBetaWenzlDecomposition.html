        noncomputable def QOU.BraidKnot.QBetaWenzlDecomposition.q_beta_c3_wenzl_coefficient (q : ℝ) :
        ℝ

        The Q_beta N=3 brane-tower truncation structural residual coefficient, -2 - (3/2) * W_4(q).

        Provenance, and a corrected precision claim (2026-09-04, bean qou-floy). This docstring asserted the identification was "exactly" this form, "yielding an error of -0.0005 ppm, significantly below the 0.0094 ppm Kashiwara N=3 ceiling". Both halves need scoping:

        • The -0.0005 ppm figure is not reproducible. A 216-combination sweep over the q0 pin (retired / CODATA-2022 muon / proton), m_e, the Q_AME source, the Markov convention, the hbar_q definition and three coefficient variants puts the closest candidate at +0.0046 ppm — ten times the quoted figure's own magnitude away. The committed witness's -0.0257 ppm IS reproducible, uniquely and to 4.2e-9, at: retired pin A, m_e = 0.51099895000, a hardcoded Q_AME = 0.78233341, convention C, balanced brackets.
        • That recipe names what has since changed. The producer now computes Q_AME from CODATA rather than hardcoding it, and convention D has been canonical since 2026-08-09. Re-measured under D at the canonical proton pin, the residual is +600.97 ppm — roughly 470x worse than the N=2 baseline (1.27 ppm) this claim was compared against, and ~1.2e6 times the quoted error.

        The convention is the cause, not the pin: C to D moves the residual ~2760x, while the pin moves it 0.03 ppm in either convention.

        Scope (a), not (b). This is a scope-limited negative: the expression, at the canonical convention, does not reproduce Q_beta to the claimed precision. It does not exclude the Wenzl weight 1/[4]_q! as an ingredient of a c_3 closure — no proved invariant forbids it — and the F_3 truncation reading is untouched. The definition is kept for that reason; only the precision claim attached to it was wrong.
