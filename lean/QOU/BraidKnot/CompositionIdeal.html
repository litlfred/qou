    Mathas' Lemma 3.9, in ideal form — and it comes out sharper than stated #

    Mathas 3.9: for μ a composition and ν its reordering into a partition, an m_{st} with s,t row-standard μ-tableaux is an R-combination of m_{uv} with u,v row-standard ν-tableaux.

    The proof he gives is a conjugation: pick d with S_ν = d S_μ d⁻¹ and d ∈ D_μ, so that x_ν T_d = T_d x_μ. Both halves of that are already in the corpus and sorry-free: sortPerm/youngOfComp_conj_of_blockMatch supply the d, and CompositionIntertwine.Tw_sortPerm_mul_xComp is the intertwining identity.

    What this file adds is the observation that T_d is a unit whenever q is (each σ_i is, by the quadratic relation, and T_d is a product of them along a reduced word). That makes the conjugation invertible, and the conclusion correspondingly stronger than the statement being formalised:

    a · x_μ · b  =  (a T_d⁻¹) · x_ν · (T_d b)

    — a single ν-term, not a combination, and the same in the other direction. So the two-sided ideals generated by x_μ and x_ν are equal, not merely nested.

    🛑 "Sharper" in one direction and WEAKER in another — read both #

    The single-term conclusion is stronger than Mathas' combination as a statement about ideals, and weaker as a statement about the Murphy basis, because nothing here says the produced a' = a·T_d⁻¹ and b' = T_d·b are again of Murphy form T*_{d(u)} and T_{d(v)}.

    They are generally not, and the reason is exact rather than a suspicion: T_w⁻¹ ≠ T_{w⁻¹}. Already at a simple reflection, s is an involution so T_{s⁻¹} = T_s, while the quadratic relation forces the inverse to spread over two basis elements — inv_Tw_sw_expansion below states that, and it is proved, not asserted. Iterating along a reduced word, the support of T_d⁻¹ is measured — exactly, over all of S_2, S_3, S_4 — in tw_inverse_support_probe.py: it is the Bruhat interval [e, d⁻¹], with leading coefficient q^{-ℓ(d)} at d⁻¹.

    Note the d⁻¹, and note it is the handedness trap for the third time on this arc. The naive statements — support in [e, d], leading coefficient at d — hold on exactly 16 of the 32 elements measured, which are exactly the 16 involutions. Tw_mul_Tw's flip is what turns d into d⁻¹ here, as it did for sortPerm and for IsDistinguishedL.

    The naive 2^ℓ(d) bound is real but slack: it is attained only for ℓ ≤ 3, and the longest element of S_4 (ℓ = 6) spreads over 24 terms, not 64 — the interval size, not the power of two.

    That expansion IS Mathas' R-combination. So the two statements are not in tension: his combination lives at the basis level, and collapses to one term exactly when you stop insisting the coefficients be Murphy-shaped. Getting from here to 3.9 as he states it means expanding T_d⁻¹ and showing the surviving terms are indexed by row-standard ν-tableaux — a triangularity argument, and the real remaining work.

    Scope — what this is not #

    This is 3.9's algebra, at the level of x_μ rather than of m_{st}. Passing to m_{st} means indexing the two sides by row-standard tableaux, which for a composition shape is vocabulary the corpus does not yet carry (TableauPerm.dOf is indexed by a YoungDiagram, i.e. a partition). Recorded so this is not read as the full lemma: what is proved here is that the μ-side and the ν-side generate the same ideal, which is the step 3.9's proof actually performs.

    Note also the hypothesis [Invertible q], absent everywhere upstream in this arc. It is genuinely needed — at q = 0 the quadratic relation gives σ_i² = -σ_i, σ_i is not a unit, and the conjugation argument has nothing to invert.

    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.CompositionIdeal.inv_Tw_sw_expansion (K : Type u_1) [Field K] (q : K) {n : ℕ} [Invertible q] (i : Fin n) :
    Hecke.IwahoriHecke.σInv K q n i = ⅟q • Hecke.TwBasis.Tw K q n (HeckeMurphy.sw i) - (⅟q * (q - 1)) • Hecke.TwBasis.Tw K q n 1

    T_w⁻¹ ≠ T_{w⁻¹} — the exact obstruction to reading the above as Mathas' 3.9.

    At a simple reflection s = sᵢ the group inverse is s itself, so T_{s⁻¹} = T_s. The algebra inverse is not: the quadratic relation spreads it over two basis elements, T_s and T_1. Along a reduced word for d this compounds, and the resulting spread is precisely the R-combination Mathas' statement carries.
