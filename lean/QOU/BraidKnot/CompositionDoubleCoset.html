    Mathas' Lemma 3.9 at the level of spans: the double-coset terms are everything #

    Putting the two coset reductions together:

    `H · x_μ · H = span { T_d · x_μ · T_e : d ∈ D_μ, e⁻¹ ∈ D_μ }`

    Every product with x_μ in the middle, however the two outer factors are chosen, is an R-combination of the double-coset-indexed terms — and, because both reductions produce only powers of q, the coefficients are q-powers times the basis coefficients of the outer factors. That is Mathas' Lemma 3.9 in span form.

    The T_d⁻¹ expansion turns out not to be needed #

    The plan recorded on the previous branch was to expand T_d⁻¹ over the Bruhat interval [e, d⁻¹] (TwInverseBruhat.TwInv_mem_spanBruhatLe) and control the surviving terms. That is not necessary for this statement: expanding the outer factors in the T-basis is already enough, since each T_h · x_μ and x_μ · T_k reduces on its own.

    The interval bound is still the sharp fact and remains worth having — it is what would bound which d occur, rather than merely that some do — but it is a refinement of this result, not an input to it. Recording that because the reverse impression would send the next agent to build machinery it does not need.

    Scope: this is the span statement, not the basis statement #

    What is not proved here is that the T_d · x_μ · T_e are linearly independent, nor that the index set matches pairs of row-standard tableaux under CompositionTableau.existsUnique_rowStandard. The first is a genuine further claim; the second is the tableau-vocabulary step. Mathas' 3.9 asks only for the combination, which is what is proved — but a reader should not take "3.9 is done" to mean the Murphy basis is.
