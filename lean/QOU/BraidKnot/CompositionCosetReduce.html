    The coset reduction: x_μ · T_a collapses onto the distinguished representative #

    This is the step Mathas' Lemma 3.9 performs when it replaces an arbitrary index by a coset representative:

    `x_μ · T_a = q^{ℓ(v)} · (x_μ · T_e)`,   `a = e·v`, `e ∈ D_μ`, `v ∈ S_μ`

    So the family {x_μ · T_a : a ∈ S_n} — which has n! members — spans the same thing as {x_μ · T_e : e ∈ D_μ}, one per coset, with every ratio a power of q and nothing else. Together with CompositionCosetSum.linearIndependent_xComp_mul_Tw (those x_μ T_e are independent) this is the structure of the right module x_μ H.

    The three inputs, and why each is needed #

    • CompositionCosetSum.existsUnique_isDistinguishedL — Prop 3.3, left-coset form: the factorisation a = e·v exists and e is unique.
    • CompositionCosetSum.length_mul_eq_add_of_isDistinguishedL — the lengths add, which is what lets Tw_mul_Tw fire at all.
    • CompositionEigenPower.xComp_mul_Tw_of_mem — x_μ · T_v = q^{ℓ(v)} · x_μ for the whole Young subgroup, not merely its generators.

    Handedness, once more #

    Tw_mul_Tw is flipped (T_v · T_w = T_{w·v}), so the factor that ends up adjacent to x_μ is T_v, the Young-subgroup part — which is exactly the one the eigenvector property can absorb. That is why the reduction is stated on the left coset e·S_μ with e⁻¹ distinguished (IsDistinguishedL), and not on the right coset. With the other orientation the T_v lands on the far side of T_e and nothing collapses.
