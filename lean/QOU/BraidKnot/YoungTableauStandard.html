    source
    structure QOU.BraidKnot.YoungTableauStandard.SkewStandardYoungTableau (outer inner : YoungDiagram) :
    Type

    A skew standard Young tableau of shape outer / inner is a filling of the skew cells outer.cells \ inner.cells by {0, …, |diff|−1}, each used once, strictly increasing along rows and columns among the skew (non-inner) cells.

    • entry : ℕ → ℕ → ℕ

      entry i j is the value in cell (i, j).

    • row_strict {i j₁ j₂ : ℕ} : j₁ < j₂ → (i, j₂) ∈ outer → (i, j₁) ∉ inner → self.entry i j₁ < self.entry i j₂

      Rows are strictly increasing among the skew cells.

    • col_strict {i₁ i₂ j : ℕ} : i₁ < i₂ → (i₂, j) ∈ outer → (i₁, j) ∉ inner → self.entry i₁ j < self.entry i₂ j

      Columns are strictly increasing among the skew cells.

    • zeros {i j : ℕ} : (i, j) ∈ inner ∨ (i, j) ∉ outer → self.entry i j = 0

      entry vanishes off the skew region (on inner, or outside outer).

    • content : Finset.image (fun (c : ℕ × ℕ) => self.entry c.1 c.2) (outer.cells \ inner.cells) = Finset.range (outer.cells \ inner.cells).card

      The entries on the skew cells are exactly {0, …, |diff|−1}.

            Why fDimSkew must guard on containment (machine-proved, no sorry).

            (2,1) ⊄ (3), yet SkewStandardYoungTableau (3) (2,1) is inhabited: the skew region (3).cells \ (2,1).cells is the single cell (0,2), both strictness fields are vacuous there, and the all-zero filling satisfies zeros and content. So the raw Nat.card is 1 where the classical f^{(3)/(2,1)} is 0.

            Unguarded, branching_two_one would be false already at k = 3: the three partitions of 3 contribute f^{(3)}·1 + f^{(2,1)}·1 + f^{(1,1,1)}·1 = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4 (each raw skew count is 1, the shapes (3) and (1,1,1) contributing spuriously), and 3 · 4 = 12 ≠ 6 = 3!. Guarded, only (2,1) survives and the sum is 2, with 3 · 2 = 6 = 3! ✓ — and at k = 4 the surviving shapes (3,1), (2,2), (2,1,1) give 3·1 + 2·1 + 3·1 = 8 with 3 · 8 = 24 = 4! ✓.

            Skew ↪ straight — the injection that discharges skew_le_straight #

            For μ ⊆ λ, a skew SYT of shape λ/μ extends to a straight SYT of λ: fill μ with a fixed standard tableau of μ (values 1…|μ|) and shift every skew value up past it. The extension is injective — subtract the shift on the skew cells to recover the skew tableau — so f^{λ/μ} ≤ f^λ shape by shape, and summing gives skew_le_straight.

            Everything is specialised to μ = (2,1), the only shape the C8 corollary needs; a general version would take a chosen SYT of μ as a parameter. Note the target QOU.Mathlib.StandardYoungTableau is 1-indexed (a Set.BijOn onto Icc 1 |λ|) while SkewStandardYoungTableau is 0-indexed (image = range d), so the shift is +4, not +3.

                    The one remaining RSK-family identity (a hypothesis, not a sorry) #

                    branching_two_one is the deep Standard-Young-Tableau counting fact Mathlib does not prove. It states the real proposition over the canonical dimSYT / partitionShape / skew count.

                    How the gap is carried. This section text once said the fill "is sorry with a -- Ref: to Sagan". It is not: the identity is §3b-conditional on the RSKIdentitiesContext class below, and the theorem's body is the matching class field. Nothing here is axiomatised and nothing is sorry, so #print axioms is clean — the gap is visible in the signature, as an instance argument a caller has to produce.

                    No instance exists. RSKIdentitiesContext has no instance anywhere in this corpus, degenerate or otherwise, so branching_two_one cannot be applied at a concrete k and nothing downstream is silently discharged.

                    sum_dimSYT_eq_involution used to be the class's second field, and is now PROVED (2026-08-30). It has been removed from the class and the theorem is unconditional; QOU.Mass.sum_dimSYT_eq_involution_keystone is the proof.

                    Worth recording why it sat here as long as it did, because the reason was wrong rather than merely out of date. This section said the identity needed the RSK correspondence, which Mathlib lacks. It needs no RSK at all. Young's lattice is a 1-differential poset, and that route proves the identity with finite combinatorics on YoungDiagram — no insertion, no bumping, no permutation↔tableau-pair bijection (QOU.Mathlib.upSum_eq is the step it turns on). "Mathlib lacks X, therefore blocked" is sound only when X is actually required; here the textbook route was mistaken for the only route, and that mistake was recorded in this file, in beans qou-jlam / qou-m3xr, and in three other docstrings as though it were an infrastructure fact.

                    Their sibling skew_le_straight is stated here too but is no longer cited — it is proved above, unconditionally.

                    branching_two_one is probably not RSK-blocked either (2026-08-30) — do not assume the P1–P5 program is the only route. This text listed it as "discharged by the RSK correspondence (open P1–P5 program docs/scope/2026-06-24-identity-i-plancherel-snrep-infra-program.md)", which is the same framing that turned out to be wrong for its former companion. The differential-poset route reaches it too, by induction on k:

                    G(μ, k) := Σ_{|λ|=k} f^λ · f^{λ/μ},   claim  G(μ, k) = (k!/|μ|!) · f^μ

                    which at μ = (2,1) (f^μ = 2, |μ|! = 6) is 3 · Σ = k! verbatim. The step is

                    G(μ, k+1) = Σ_λ f^λ Σ_{ν ⋖ λ, ν ⊇ μ} f^{ν/μ}      (skew branching)
          = Σ_ν f^{ν/μ} Σ_{λ ⋗ ν} f^λ              (transpose, as `pairTerm` does)
          = (k+1) · G(μ, k)                        (`QOU.Mathlib.upSum_eq`)

                    with base G(μ, |μ|) = f^μ. Two of the three inputs are in hand: upSum_eq is proved sorry-free, and the transposition is the Finset.sum_comm manoeuvre already used for it. The one open input is the skew branching rule — remove the largest entry of a skew SYT — the direct analogue of the proved QOU.Mathlib.numStandardYoungTableaux_branching.

                    Checked exactly for k = 3..11 in computations/combinatorics/branching_two_one_route_probe.py. That probe confirms the route's arithmetic, not its open input: it computes f^{λ/μ} by the skew branching recursion, so it assumes that rule rather than verifying it. So this is a credible route with one real obligation left, not a proof — branching_two_one stays open, and no instance of the class exists.
