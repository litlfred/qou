    Dipper–James: dominance on row-standard tableaux is the Bruhat order #

    Mathas states this immediately before using it, and the sentence is worth quoting because it is the reason this file exists at all:

    The partial order ⊵ on S_n is the ubiquitous Bruhat–Chevalley order. Perhaps surprisingly, it agrees with the dominance ordering on row standard λ-tableaux (when restricted to D_λ).

    3.7 Proposition (Dipper–James). Let λ be a composition of n and suppose that s and t are row standard λ-tableaux. Then s ⊵ t if and only if d(s) ⊵ d(t).

    TableauPerm.dOf is a bijection onto Perm (Fin (nOf μ)) (equivPerm), so this says the two orders are the same order transported along d — not two orders that co-operate.

    Why that matters here #

    MurphyInductionMeasure.murphyLex is lex-on-shapes × dominance-on-tabloids. Under 3.7 its inner axis is Bruhat, which is what makes Mathas' Corollary 3.8 — whose proof opens by invoking Theorem 1.1.11, that T_{d(v)} T_w is supported on subexpressions of d(v)w — speak about the same order the induction descends in.

    🛑 THE ORIENTATION IS REVERSING. Do not transcribe 3.7 off the page. #

    The obvious reading of "s ⊵ t iff d(s) ⊵ d(t)" is d(t) ≤ d(s) in standard Bruhat. That is backwards. t^λ is dominance-MAXIMAL while d(t^λ) = 1 is Bruhat-MINIMAL (TableauPerm.dOf_refTab), so d is order-reversing onto standard Bruhat. Mathas' ⊵ on S_n is standard Bruhat with the order reversed, and the correct correspondence is

    s ⊵ t   ↔   d(s) ≤ d(t)      (standard Bruhat, i.e. `CoxeterBruhat.BruhatLe`)

    This was measured before being stated, and the wrong-way version is what caught it: dipper_james_dominance_is_bruhat_probe.py run against d(t) ≤ d(s) fails at 30/46, 511/773, 14201/19426, 565748/708062 — identically in both directions and under both definitions of Bruhat. Equal forward and converse counts is the signature of a flipped relation, not of a counterexample, which is how it was caught rather than reported as a refutation of a published lemma. Run the right way round:

    n ordered pairs s ⊵ t → d(s) ≤ d(t) converse
    3 46 46/46 46/46
    4 773 773/773 773/773
    5 19 426 19 426/19 426 19 426/19 426
    6 708 062 708 062/708 062 708 062/708 062

    Sixth instance of the handedness/orientation trap on this arc, and the first caught before any Lean was written rather than after.

    Which Bruhat, and which d — both settled by measurement #

    Which Bruhat. CoxeterBruhat.BruhatLe is the subword definition; Mathas' is reflection-generated with length increase (w = v t₁⋯t_k with ℓ(v t₁⋯t_j) = ℓ(v) + j). Their equivalence is a theorem, not an unfolding — but the probe compares them permutation by permutation and finds them identical on S₃…S₆ (6/6, 24/24, 120/120, 720/720). So this file states 3.7 against BruhatLe without choosing sides. That is evidence, not a discharge of the subword property as a proof obligation: Corollary 3.8 needs it for arbitrary n.

    Which d. 3.7 is stated for a composition λ. This file is for partition shapes, because a partition is a composition (so this is a genuine special case, not an analogue); because TableauPerm.dOf t = refTab.trans t.symm with refTab = t^λ row-superstandard is Mathas' d(t), the one defined by t = t^λ · d(t); and because murphyLex is indexed by PartOf n, so the partition case is exactly what consumes it. The composition form stays available in CompositionCoset* if 3.9 at basis level turns out to need it.

    Status of what is below #

    refTab_dOf_bruhatLe is sorry-free and is the orientation fact in its own right: the reference tableau sits at the bottom of Bruhat while being the top of dominance.

    dominance_iff_bruhatLe is stated, not proved — the honest state-and-queue marker of AGENTS.md §1, with its -- Ref: citation. It is a published theorem (Dipper–James, via Mathas 3.7), not an open problem, and it is confirmed by exhaustive measurement to n = 6; what is missing is the formalisation.

    🛑 What discharging that sorry ACTUALLY needs — measured, not guessed #

    Mathas' proof is an induction on a transposition (a,b) chosen at the first row where s and t differ, and needs no Hecke algebra. But it does not run against the statement as written here, and that is worth knowing before anyone starts:

    • the inductive step is "if v = t·(a,b) then v ▷ t and d(v) ▷ d(t)" — a Bruhat cover by an arbitrary transposition with length increase. That is the reflection characterisation of Bruhat;
    • this file states 3.7 against CoxeterBruhat.BruhatLe, the subword one.

    So the proof route needs the bridge subword ⟺ reflection. Surveyed:

    what where
    a reflection Bruhat order absent from the corpus
    subword ⟺ reflection absent from the corpus
    length under a simple transposition Hecke.ParabolicPairing.length_swap_dichotomy, length_mul_swap_eq_succ_iff, length_swap_cases — proved, but simple reflections only, while Mathas needs arbitrary (a,b)

    The probe's 720/720 does not shortcut this. Subword and reflection agreeing on S₃…S₆ is evidence the bridge is true; it is not the bridge.

    🟩 CORRECTED WITHIN THE HOUR — #6894 landed and the table above is stale #

    That survey was written against main before #6894 merged (6d0cb3a094), and two of its three rows are now wrong. Verified against main itself, not against the PR body:

    what then now
    a reflection Bruhat order absent IwahoriHecke.BruhatReflection.ReflLe — ReflStep v w := ∃ t, IsReflection t ∧ w = v*t ∧ ℓ v < ℓ w, then ReflTransGen. That is exactly Mathas' step.
    subword ⟺ reflection absent one direction proved: bruhatLe_of_reflLe, and bruhatLe_of_reflLe_An is the hypothesis-free type-A corollary — i.e. it applies to Sₙ with nothing to discharge
    Bruhat antisymmetry / subword property / transitivity / lifting all four deferred in CoxeterBruhat all four proved (#6894, via Matsumoto)

    So the two routes above collapse: route 2 is unnecessary (the reflection order exists), and route 1 is half done.

    What that makes provable, and what it does not #

    Mathas' proof constructs precisely ReflSteps — multiply by a transposition, length goes up. Composing with bruhatLe_of_reflLe_An therefore reaches BruhatLe. So:

    • forward (s ⊵ t → d(s) ≤ d(t)) is unblocked: the Coxeter side is now supplied and what remains is the tableau combinatorics — the induction on a transposition at the first differing row;
    • converse (d(s) ≤ d(t) → s ⊵ t) appeared to need BruhatLe → ReflLe — bean qou-3k66, which #6894's own "left open" section names as "what a consumer citing Mathas' Prop 3.7 needs for an ↔", this file being that consumer.

    Both halves of that assessment turned out to be wrong, in opposite directions, and the sections below are the record: the forward half is not unblocked (its tableau step does not close), and the converse is — by a route through rank matrices that never mentions the reflection order. bruhatLe_to_dominance is proved.

    The iff is nonetheless still split below into its two implications, because only one of them is closed.

    🛑 THE SOURCE'S INDUCTIVE STEP DOES NOT CLOSE — measured, not suspected #

    The paragraph above said the forward half was "unblocked" and that what remained was "purely the tableau combinatorics". The Coxeter side is supplied, and the handedness trap it warned about is closed (BruhatReflection.LeftReflStep / LeftReflLe / bruhatLe_of_leftReflLe_An, this branch, sorry-free). What is not true is that Mathas' argument transcribes.

    Mathas' proof, from the source verbatim:

    First suppose that s ▷ t and choose r minimal such that row r of s and row r of t are different. Let a be the smallest number which is in row r of s but not in row r of t; similarly, let b be the smallest number which is in row r of t but not in row r of s. Then a < b since s ▷ t. Therefore, if v = t(a,b) then v ▷ t and d(v) ▷ d(t). Further, if a is in row t of t and b is in row s of s then t > r and t ≥ s; so, either v = s or s ▷ v. Therefore, by induction, d(s) ⊵ d(v); so d(s) ⊵ d(t) as required.

    dipper_james_forward_step_probe.py measures each clause of that separately. At n = 6, over all 142 314 ordered pairs of row-standard tableaux with s ▷ t strictly:

    the raw swap is row standard 129833/142314 91.2 % — but see below
    v is one LEFT reflection step below t 129833/142314 91.2 % — likewise
    s ⊵ v 112096/142314 78.8 %

    s ⊵ v is the substantive failure. The justification Mathas gives for it is ℓ_t(a) ≥ ℓ_s(b) — a's row in t at or below b's row in s — and that is the condition which produces the overshoot rather than preventing it: swapping sends b to a's row in t, i.e. at or below where s puts it. Smallest instance, verified by hand and by probe:

    λ = (2,1,1)
s = ((1,2),(4),(3))      t = ((1,4),(3),(2))      s ▷ t
r = 0,  a = 2,  b = 4,  a < b
v = t(2,4) = ((1,2),(3),(4))
v ▷ t  ✓   but   s ⊵ v  is FALSE — at i = 1, m = 3 the counts are s : 2, v : 3

    The two 91.2 % rows dissolve — and the lemma that dissolves them is proved #

    When the raw swap leaves a row unsorted, v is still the correct tabloid but is no longer (a,b) · t as a tableau, so the single-step route through bruhatLe_of_leftReflLe_An is unavailable there. That looked like a second obstruction. It is not: d(v) is the distinguished (minimal-length) representative of the coset S_λ · d((a,b)·t), so ℓ is additive across that factorisation and a reduced word for d(v) is a prefix of one for d((a,b)·t) — hence a subword.

    That is CoxeterBruhat.bruhatLe_of_length_add, proved sorry-free alongside this file, and the corpus's own unconditional Bourbaki lifting lemma CoxeterReflectionCocycle.length_minRep_mul_wordProd' supplies its hypothesis. Measured first, over every transposition of every row-standard tableau rather than only the chosen one: 2 134 710/2 134 710 at n = 6.

    So the raw swap need not be row standard, and under the relaxed valid set that drops the requirement, s ⊵ v is the ONE remaining failing clause — Mathas' rule then scores exactly the s ⊵ v count, 112 096/142 314.

    This is a repair, not a refutation — and the difference is measured #

    The last row is why. For every such pair there exists a transposition meeting all of Mathas' conditions at once — raw swap row standard, s ⊵ v ▷ t, d(v) = (a,b) · d(t), length down. So the strategy is sound and only the selection rule is wrong: what a Lean proof needs is a correct rule, not a different induction.

    Nor is this a claim against Mathas. 3.7 itself is measured true, in both directions, 708 062/708 062 on the same range. The source is OCR, and the sentence carrying the failing clause prints its two row indices as s and t — the same glyphs as the two tableaux — so a transcription error in either direction is entirely possible. What is established is that the argument as this corpus can read it does not close, which is a reason not to write Lean against it.

Dominance is a global count comparison, not a per-label one.

    🟩 THE TRANSPOSITION INDUCTION IS THE WRONG ROUTE — and the converse half is unblocked #

    The negative above ("no rule that names a label can be right") points somewhere specific, and the destination is measured in dipper_james_rank_criterion_probe.py. Write rank(u, i, m) = #{x < i : u(x) < m} and let N_i = μ₀ + ⋯ + μ_i be the row boundaries. Then, at n ≤ 6, all four of:
    s ⊵ t ⟺ the rank inequality at the boundaries N_i 708062/708062 ok
    u ≤ w ⟺ the rank inequality at every i (Ehresmann) 708062/708062 ok
    boundary ⟹ full, for d-images 143916/143916 ok
    full ⟹ boundary (specialisation) 143916/143916 ok

    The first is an identity, not a coincidence. d(t) sends a reference label to t's label at the same cell and refTab is row-superstandard, so the row of y in t is the block of d(t)⁻¹(y), whence

    domCount (rowPattern t) i m = #{x < N_i : d(t) x < m} = rank (d t) N_i m.

    Dominance on tabloids IS a rank comparison on permutations, read only at the row boundaries. So 3.7 carries no tableau content at all: both sides compare the rank matrices of the same two permutations, and the entire difference is "at the boundaries" versus "at every index" — reconciled by d being a minimal coset representative, which is increasing within each block and therefore has its rank function determined by interpolation.

    What that changes for each half #

    • Converse — no longer blocked on qou-3k66. d(s) ≤ d(t) ⟹ full rank (the easy direction of Ehresmann: a subword can only raise the rank matrix, an induction on deletions from the subword definition) ⟹ boundary rank by specialisation ⟹ s ⊵ t. No reflection order appears anywhere in that chain, so bruhatLe_to_dominance's recorded blocker is not a blocker on this route. It is now proved on exactly this route: rk_le_of_bruhatLe (easy Ehresmann, Machinery/BruhatRankCriterion), then rankAt_eq_rk to specialise to the row cuts, then domRel_iff_rankAt. (qou-3k66 was open when this was written and a sibling has since closed it — lean(qou-3k66), the two Bruhat orders are the same relation. That does not change this route, which never needed it.)
    • Forward — PROVED. The interpolation bridge (rk_le_of_rankAt_le, on BruhatRank.rk_le_of_endpoints + dOf_increasingOn_block + exists_block) reduced it to the hard direction of Ehresmann's criterion, and that is now proved too: BruhatRank.bruhatLe_of_rk_le, sorry-free, by induction on ℓ(w) at a right descent. So dominance_to_bruhatLe is [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound] — no sorryAx, checked with #print axioms rather than by the absence of a sorry token, which is not the same thing. Both halves of 3.7 are now unconditional.

    What remains free and is not in doubt: swapping two labels is the group action (TableauPerm.dOf_smul, verified), the handedness bridge is landed, the coset-minimality bridge is proved (CoxeterBruhat.bruhatLe_of_length_add), and DominanceWellFounded.domGtOn_wellFounded supplies a measure should any induction still be wanted.

    The orientation, proved #

    dOf_refTab puts the reference tableau at 1, and 1 is below everything in Bruhat. So t^λ — which is the maximum of the dominance order — maps to the minimum of Bruhat. That is the whole content of "the correspondence reverses", and it is why writing 3.7 as d(t) ≤ d(s) produces a statement that fails on half its instances.

    Dominance is a rank comparison — the tableaux come out of the statement #

    The measured reduction in the module docstring, made precise. rowPattern_dOf is the whole of it; everything below is reindexing along the bijection dOf t.

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidKnot.DominanceIsBruhat.rowPattern_dOf {μ : YoungDiagram} (t : Specht.Tableau μ) (x : Fin (Specht.nOf μ)) :
    Specht.rowPattern t ((TableauPerm.dOf t) x) = Specht.rowPattern (TableauPerm.refTab μ) x

    The key identity: d(t) transports t's row pattern back to the reference tableau's.

    dOf t x = t⁻¹(refTab x), so t sends dOf t x to the cell refTab assigns to x, and the row of that cell does not mention t at all. Two lines, and it is the reason 3.7 has no tableau content: the row of a label in t is the row of its d(t)-preimage in the reference tableau.
