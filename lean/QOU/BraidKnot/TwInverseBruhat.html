    T_w⁻¹ lives in the Bruhat interval [e, w⁻¹] #

    TwInverseLength.TwInv_mem_spanLe bounds T_w⁻¹ in length. The sharper fact — measured exactly over S_2, S_3, S_4 in tw_inverse_support_probe.py at 32/32 — is that its support is the Bruhat interval [e, w⁻¹]. This file proves it.

    Note the w⁻¹, because the naive statement is false #

    Against w rather than w⁻¹ the claim holds on 16 of the 32 elements measured, which are exactly the 16 involutions — the elements where the two agree. Tw_mul_Tw's flipped handedness is the cause, the same flip that forced sortPerm's orientation and IsDistinguishedL. Stating this against w would be a false lemma.

    What the induction needs, and what it does not #

    Only the two ascending-extension steps of CoxeterBruhat — u ≤ t ⇒ u ≤ t·sᵢ and u ≤ t ⇒ u·sᵢ ≤ t·sᵢ — together with the corpus's own σ_mul_Tw, which puts σᵢ · T_u in the span of T_u and T_{u·sᵢ} in both branches.

    It needs neither transitivity, nor antisymmetry, nor the subword property, all of which CoxeterBruhat leaves open pending the exchange condition. That is worth stating plainly: this theorem does not silently depend on BruhatLe being a partial order, which is not yet known here.

    The length hypothesis comes free from reducedness: wordProd (i :: ω) = sᵢ · v, so reducedness of i :: ω gives ℓ(sᵢv) = ℓ(v) + 1, and inverting — ℓ is inverse-invariant — gives exactly ℓ(v⁻¹·sᵢ) = ℓ(v⁻¹) + 1, which is the hypothesis both extension lemmas want.
