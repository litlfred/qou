    def QOU.BraidKnot.TableauContent.content (cell : ℕ × ℕ) :
    ℤ

    Content of a cell (i, j): column index minus row index, c(i,j) = j − i. Cells on the same diagonal share a content; it is the seminormal-form coordinate of a box.

    Overlap note (reuse audit): QOU.IwahoriHecke.Hoefsmit.content (i j : ℕ) carries the same j − i formula in two-argument shape. This file keeps the pair-shaped version deliberately — it is a lightweight foundational brick (imports only Mathlib.Tactic + the weight layer), whereas Hoefsmit sits on the heavy Wedderburn/Matrix.Charpoly cone. The correct consolidation direction is to extract this pair primitive here and have Hoefsmit depend on it, not to pull the heavy cone into the combinatorial base; tracked as a follow-up, not done in this PR to avoid coupling the brick to Hoefsmit's dependencies.

      def QOU.BraidKnot.TableauContent.axialDistance (a b : ℕ × ℕ) :

      Axial distance between two cells: the difference of their contents. For the boxes holding i and i+1 in a standard tableau this is the ρ of the seminormal weight a_ρ.

        theorem QOU.BraidKnot.TableauContent.axialDistance_antisymm (a b : ℕ × ℕ) :
        axialDistance a b = -axialDistance b a

        Axial distance is antisymmetric: ρ(a,b) = −ρ(b,a). This is the cell-geometry root of the seminormal ρ ↔ −ρ pairing — swapping the two boxes (the i ↔ i+1 transposition acting on a standard tableau) negates ρ.

        theorem QOU.BraidKnot.TableauContent.reducedWeight_pair {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (q : R) (a b : ℕ × ℕ) (hq : q ≠ 0) (hx : q ^ (2 * axialDistance a b) ≠ 1) :

        Box-pair pairing — the cell-geometry form of g_ρ + g_{−ρ} = 1. For two off-diagonal cells (q^{2·ρ(a,b)} ≠ 1, i.e. a, b not on the same diagonal in the sense that makes the weight degenerate), the reduced seminormal weights at the two orientations of the pair sum to 1. Composing Infra 1 (axialDistance_antisymm) with Infra 2 (reducedWeight_add_neg): each off-diagonal box pair contributes exactly 1 to the reduced-weight sum, which is why χ_λ(σ)/h = c_λ ∈ ℤ.
