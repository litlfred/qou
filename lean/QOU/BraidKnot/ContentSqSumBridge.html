    QOU.BraidKnot.ContentSqSumBridge — the two content-square sums are the same sum #

    The corpus computes Σ_{c ∈ λ} c(c)² twice, over different indices and by different formulas:

    • JucysMurphy.contentSqSum (μ : YoungDiagram) := ∑ c ∈ μ.cells, (cellContent c)^2 — a Finset sum over the diagram's cells, which is the form the Jucys–Murphy / Okounkov–Vershik layer speaks;
    • IdentityI.sumSqContents (l : List ℕ) — nested List sums over the row-length list, which is the form the Identity-I / Plancherel layer speaks.

    contentSqSum_ofRowLens identifies them. Nothing in the corpus did, which is what kept identityB_general (Identity (ii) for general n, stated with contentSqSum) from reaching PlancherelSnData (whose field is stated with sumSqContents, and whose instances therefore stopped at n = 12). PlancherelSnDataGeneral is the consumer: with this lemma the datum is built at every n, and Identity I comes off that cap.

    The proof is the weighted analogue of QOU.Mathlib.card_cells_ofRowLens: the cells are the disjoint union of the rows (cells_biUnion, rows_pairwiseDisjoint) and a row is a singleton times a range (row_eq_prod), so the cell sum reorganises into the nested row sum. As there, the pieces come from SchenstedCellsBridge, whose lemmas are general even though its headline theorem is stated only for a tableauShape.

    Pure combinatorics on ℕ/ℤ; nothing archimedean; no fit.
