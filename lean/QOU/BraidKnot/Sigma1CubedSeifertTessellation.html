    1. Seifert's algorithm on σ₁³ — the derived CW cell count #

    Seifert's algorithm on the standard σ₁³ diagram (2-strand braid, 3 positive crossings) yields 2 Seifert circles (⇒ 2 Seifert disks = 2-cells) joined by 3 twisted bands (one per crossing = 1-cells). These counts are canonical (Seifert's algorithm has no free choice), so the surface's Euler characteristic, genus and first-homology rank are derived, not fitted.

                2. The genus-1 surface relator is a single commutator, and its #

                abelian shadow vanishes

                For a genus-1 surface with one boundary component the fundamental-group relation is ∂ = [a₁, b₁] = a₁ b₁ a₁⁻¹ b₁⁻¹ — a single commutator of the two handle holonomies. The non-abelian Stokes theorem assigns exactly this object (the surface-ordered curvature integral) to the surface.

                Its abelianisation — the exponent-sum in each generator — is (0, 0): the abelian (unordered) holonomy of the surface term is trivial. This is the combinatorial shadow of holonomy_perm / trace-cyclicity tr[A,B]=0: the surface-ordering correction contributes nothing to any abelian / character functional.
