    Prop 3.3, existence: a non-distinguished element is shortenable inside S_μ #

    The uniqueness half is CompositionCoset.isDistinguished_unique. Existence — every right coset of S_μ contains a distinguished element — reduces to the shortening step:

    `d` not distinguished  ⇒  some `v ∈ S_μ` has `ℓ(v · d) < ℓ(d)`

    after which the minimal-length element of a coset is distinguished, and there is one. Measured before formalising, exhaustively over all 5316 right cosets for n = 1..6: existence, "the minimum is distinguished", and this shortening step all hold (coset_minimal_is_distinguished_probe.py).

    The handedness problem, and why it dissolves #

    PermInversions.invCount_mul_swap moves the inversion count by one under an adjacent transposition, but on the right: w * sᵢ. The step above is a left multiplication. They meet through ℓ(x) = ℓ(x⁻¹):

    ℓ(sᵢ · d) = ℓ((sᵢ · d)⁻¹) = ℓ(d⁻¹ · sᵢ)

    so the existing right-handed lemma applies to d⁻¹, and the descent condition it wants — d⁻¹ i < d⁻¹ (i+1) or its reverse — is exactly the condition the block argument below produces. No new inversion machinery is needed.

    Finding an adjacent transposition inside the block #

    d not distinguished gives a < b with d a, d b in one block and d b < d a. That is a transposition of two block elements, not an adjacent one. Blocks are intervals (lt_of_index_lt and index_eq_of_between below), so the whole range [d b, d a] lies in that block, and along it d⁻¹ starts high and ends low — hence it descends across some consecutive pair, which is the sᵢ ∈ S_μ wanted.

    Proposition 3.3, assembled #

    Existence by induction on length: a non-distinguished element is strictly shortened inside S_μ, so descending must halt, and it can only halt at a distinguished one. With CompositionCoset.isDistinguished_unique that is Mathas' statement in full — D_μ meets every right coset of S_μ exactly once.
