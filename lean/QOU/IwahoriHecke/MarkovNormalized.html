    The normalised Markov trace, and where convention D's z comes from #

    QTraceCyclic gave Markov move I (cyclicity) and MarkovPeel gave move II (the peel), both for the unnormalised site-weighted trace tr_w. What is missing for a Markov trace proper is the third axiom, normalisation tr(1) = 1 — qTrace_one is (∑ₖ w k)^{m+1}, not 1.

    This file divides it out, and the peel scalar moves with it. That is the whole content, and it is short; the reason to write it down is what the moved scalar turns out to be.

    Division without a field #

    R is a bare CommRing throughout this arc, so the inverse of the total weight is carried as a separate scalar with a witnessing equation — exactly the idiom q * qi = 1 already uses for q⁻¹. So wi with (∑ₖ w k) * wi = 1, and

    tr̂(x) := wi^{m+1} · tr_w(x).

    markovTr_one, markovTr_mul_comm and markovTr_peel are then the three Markov axioms: normalised, cyclic, and peeling with scalar z · wi.

    🟧 The scalar is convention D's z #

    Feeding in what MarkovPeel forces — the weight must be w = (1, q²) and the unnormalised peel scalar is then z = q³ — the total weight is 1 + q², so the normalised peel scalar is

    ẑ = q³ · (1 + q²)⁻¹ = q³ / (q² + 1),

    which is the repo's convention D Markov parameter verbatim (tools/pyhecke/python/pyhecke/gram.py, z_mp: "z = q³/(q² + 1) — convention D"), and not convention C's √q/(q+1). It is stated here division-free as markovParam_eq_conventionD — ẑ · (q² + 1) = q³ — with markovParam_unique showing that equation pins ẑ whenever 1 + q² is invertible.

    Nothing along the way chose it. The weight was forced by the peel existing at all (peel_forces_physical_weight), the unnormalised scalar was forced by the same two equations, and the normalisation is division by tr_w(1). Convention D is what the R-matrix does.

    🛑 Two things this does not establish #

    It is a match of algebraic form in the §7a balanced parameter, not a cross-file identification. gram.py's z is evaluated at the substrate q₀, and the parameter it is written in is the §7a one (AGENTS.md §7a fixes (σ_i − q)(σ_i + q^{-1}) = 0, which is IsHeckeGenerator q qi here), so the reading is the natural one — but proving that the numerical z of the compute layer is this object needs the two trace conventions identified, which is not done here and is not attempted.

    This is still not a link invariant. A Markov trace gives one only through the braid group: B_n → H_n(q), Markov's theorem, and closure-invariance. None of that is here, and QOU.IwahoriHecke.MarkovTrace.markovTrace remains noncomputable opaque.
