    Embedding a braiding into a larger tensor product #

    QOU.IwahoriHecke.RMatrixSl2 proved the two relations for Jimbo's Ř and assembled the Hecke action of H₃(q) on V^{⊗3}. Everything there is at a fixed, small number of tensor factors. This file supplies what a general n needs: the operation "act on this factor, do nothing to the rest", and the fact that it preserves every relation in sight.

    The two embeddings #

    For index types σ, ρ (the acted-on factor and the spectator factor):

    • tensorId M = M ⊗ 1 on σ × ρ;
    • idTensor N = 1 ⊗ N on σ × ρ.

    Both are algebra maps (tensorIdHom, idTensorHom), which is the whole point: an algebra map transports the §7a quadratic (IsHeckeGenerator.map), the braid relation, and anything else stated as an equation between products. So the results of RMatrixSl2 hold for the embedded operators for free, at any size.

    What this gives, and what it does not #

    Gives, at arbitrary spectator size:

    • isHeckeGenerator_tensorId_rCheck — Ř ⊗ 1 is still a §7a Hecke generator;
    • tensorId_commute_idTensor — operators on disjoint factors commute, which is the far commutation relation σ_i σ_j = σ_j σ_i for |i − j| ≥ 2;
    • yangBaxter_tensorId — the braid relation survives the embedding.

    Site-indexed, on V^{⊗n} #

    The second half does supply sites, in the model V^{⊗n} = ((Fin n → Fin 2) → R) whose monomial basis is Fin n → Fin 2 — the model QOU.MassTheory.PiTensorMonomialBasis identifies with the genuine PiTensorProduct. embedPair i j acts at any two distinct sites, adjacent or not; embedPairHom is its algebra-map packaging; and isHeckeGenerator_braidAt is the general-n statement:

    for every n, and every pair of distinct sites i ≠ j of V^{⊗n}, the embedded R-matrix satisfies Ř_{ij}² = (q − qi) Ř_{ij} + 1.

    braidAt_commute and braidAt_braid are the other two:

    braidings at disjoint pairs of sites commute — σ_i σ_j = σ_j σ_i for |i − j| ≥ 2; and three distinct sites satisfy the braid relation Ř_{ij} Ř_{jk} Ř_{ij} = Ř_{jk} Ř_{ij} Ř_{jk}.

    All three defining families, so heckeActionN assembles them into an actual algebra map

    H_{m+1}(q) → End(V^{⊗(m+1)}), σ_a ↦ Ř at sites a, a+1.

    The engine for both is sum_collapse: a 2^n-term sum whose summand is supported on the states agreeing with a off {i, j} is a 4-term sum over the assignments to those sites. Far commutation needs no second collapse — sum_collapse is applied once to each side and both reduce to the same four-index normal form (embedPair_mul_apply_of_disjoint), which is symmetric in the two pairs because the base ring is commutative.

    -- Ref: [jimbo1986]; [manuscript] bean qou-k4oq

                                What heckeActionN does NOT say #

                                The three relations and the action are proved at every n. Four things are still open, and none of them follows from what is here:

                                1. Nothing about the image. heckeActionN is an algebra map. That it is surjective onto the commutant of the quantum-group action — the Schur–Weyl statement proper, and the reason one wants this map — is not proved here.

                                  🛑 And injectivity is not an open question: at this fibre it is FALSE. An earlier version of this note said injectivity "would need the T_w basis (QOU.IwahoriHecke.Tw, still open on Matsumoto)", which framed it as a theorem awaiting machinery. q-Schur–Weyl H_n(q) → End_{U_q𝔰𝔩_N}(V^{⊗n}) is injective only when N ≥ n, and item 2 fixes N = 2. So for n ≥ 3 the map has a kernel — spanned by the blocks with more than two rows, i.e. ker(H_n ↠ TL_n), of dimension n! − Catalan(n): 1, 10, 78, 588, 4611, 38890 at n = 3 … 8. heckeActionN is therefore already non-injective at m = 2, and so was the H₃(q) action of RMatrixSl2.

                                  Matsumoto would buy the basis of H_n(q), which is worth having; it cannot make a map with a nontrivial kernel injective, so it is not what stands between this file and injectivity. Note also that at N = 2 "surjective onto the commutant" and "not injective" are two faces of one fact, the image being TL_n(q).

                                  None of that is proved here either — it is the standard statement, and the corpus records the same kernel dimensions at wedderburn_jones_markov.py off the sorry-free QOU.QBeta.WenzlWeight.conventionD_weight_eq_zero_iff. Recorded because the earlier wording pointed future work at the wrong obstruction. Raised by a sibling session on PR #6817; the kernel dimensions were re-derived here from the hook-length formula before the correction was made.

                                2. Fibre Fin 2 only. The general gl_k R-matrix Ř = q Σ_i E_ii⊗E_ii + Σ_{i≠j} E_ji⊗E_ij + (q − q⁻¹) Σ_{i<j} E_ii⊗E_jj satisfies the same relations for any linearly ordered fibre; only sl₂ is done, because Fin 2 is the fibre every consumer in this corpus uses.

                                3. No trace. The q-deformed character of QOU.MassTheory.WeightedPermutationCharacter is the trace-weight half of quantum Schur–Weyl; nothing here connects the braiding to it, and the Markov trace on H_n(q) is still opaque in QOU.IwahoriHecke.MarkovTrace.

                                All three are mathematics. The one item on this list that was mere bookkeeping — that the action lived in the coordinate model rather than on PiTensorProduct — is closed by heckeActionTensor.
