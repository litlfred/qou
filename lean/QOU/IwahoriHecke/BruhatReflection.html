    The reflection-generated Bruhat order, the lifting property, and half the bridge #

    There are two standard definitions of the Bruhat order and this corpus uses one of them.

    • Subword (QOU.Machinery.CoxeterBruhat.BruhatLe): some reduced word for w has a subword multiplying to u.
    • Reflection-generated (ReflLe here): w is reached from u by multiplying on the right by reflections, each step increasing length. This is the ⊵ of Mathas and of Dipper–James, so it is the one a consumer citing that literature is actually working with.

    Their equivalence is a theorem, not an unfolding, and treating them as interchangeable is how a corpus ends up with two orders under one name.

    What closes here #

    ReflLe → BruhatLe, in full. It is short because both inputs already exist:

    • CoxeterReflectionCocycle.strong_exchange turns one downward reflection step into one deleted letter of a reduced word — unconditional, every Coxeter system, no reducedness hypothesis on the reflection side;
    • BruhatSubword.bruhatLe_trans chains the steps.

    So any hypothesis of the form "u ⊵ v in Mathas' sense" may now be consumed by everything proved about BruhatLe — the subword property, transitivity, antisymmetry.

    And the LIFTING property (bruhatLe_mul_simple_of_isRightDescent, lifting) — the fourth and last of the four QOU.Machinery.CoxeterBruhat deferred. With antisymmetry (CoxeterBruhatOrder) and the subword property + transitivity (BruhatSubword), all four are now proved.

    Lifting needs no induction once the subword property is available: a subword of a reduced word for w ending in s either uses that final s or does not, and both branches already exhibit what is wanted.

    The converse bridge — open when this file was written, CLOSED since #

    reflStep_of_eraseIdx shows the atom: deleting one letter from a reduced word is exactly a downward reflection step, with the reflection read off Mathlib's right inversion sequence. The temptation is to iterate it and get BruhatLe → ReflLe for free.

    That does not work. A subword deletes several letters at once, and the intermediate words are not reduced, so their products need not have strictly decreasing lengths — the chain of reflection steps a ReflLe requires is not the chain of deletions.

    Two diagnoses of this obstacle have now been written here and BOTH were wrong. They are recorded rather than deleted, because each one is a plausible thing to try next and each one is a dead end.

    First wrong diagnosis: "the repair is the lifting property". Lifting is now proved, in this file, and the converse still does not follow.

    Second wrong diagnosis: "it needs a subword of an arbitrary word for w to give u ≤ w". That statement is not merely unproved — it is FALSE, and not_forall_bruhatLe_of_sublist below proves it false. Take ω = [i, i], whose product is 1, and the subword [i], whose product is sᵢ: the claim would give sᵢ ≤ 1, and bruhatLe_one_iff forces sᵢ = 1, contradicting ℓ(sᵢ) = 1. Any route through it is dead.

    Where the difficulty actually sits, stated as precisely as it is currently understood. Induct on ℓ(w) with ω = ω₁ ++ [s] reduced for w, and split the reduced witness ω' for u:

    • ω' <+ ω₁ — then u ≤ w·s, the induction hypothesis gives u ≤ᵣ w·s, and w·s →ᵣ w is one reflection step. This branch closes.
    • ω' = a ++ [s] — then u·s ≤ w·s and the induction hypothesis gives u·s ≤ᵣ w·s. What is needed is u ≤ᵣ w, i.e. an upward lifting property for ≤ᵣ: if x ≤ᵣ y and s is a right ascent of both x and y, then x·s ≤ᵣ y·s. This branch is open.

    The single-step case of that upward lifting does go through — if y = x·t with ℓ(x) < ℓ(y) and s is an ascent of both, then y·s = (x·s)·(s t s), s t s is a reflection, and ℓ(x·s) = ℓ(x) + 1 < ℓ(y) + 1 = ℓ(y·s). What fails is the chain: nothing forces s to remain an ascent at the intermediate elements of a ReflTransGen chain, and ReflStep is not defined for a descent.

    ✅ That third statement of the obstacle was the right one, and it is now proved — QOU.IwahoriHecke.BruhatConverse.reflLe_mul_simple_of_ascent, and with it reflLe_of_bruhatLe and bruhatLe_iff_reflLe. The two orders are the same relation.

    What unblocked the chain: the intermediate elements of a ReflTransGen from x to y are all shorter than y, so the outer induction hypothesis of the converse applies to them. Carried into the lifting lemma, it closes the descent case — via bruhatLe_of_reflLe and this file's own bruhatLe_mul_simple_of_isRightDescent. So the lifting property is used, just not where the first wrong diagnosis put it: it resolves the descent case inside the chain, not the branch.

    So: ReflLe → BruhatLe ✅ (here), BruhatLe → ReflLe ✅ (BruhatConverse).

    The two wrong diagnoses above are kept anyway. not_forall_bruhatLe_of_sublist remains a true refutation and is worth having on the record; and the history of what did not work is the part a reader cannot reconstruct from the finished proof.

    The LEFT-handed variant, and why it is here #

    ReflStep multiplies on the right (w = v · t). The tableau side of Mathas' Prop. 3.7 produces the left one: swapping two entries of a tableau is the group action, and TableauPerm.dOf_smul gives dOf (swap a b • t) = swap a b * dOf t — transposition on the left. A consumer that assumes the two are interchangeable fails at the point where bruhatLe_of_reflLe_An is applied: late, and looking like a hard obstruction.

    It is a bridge, not a wall. LeftReflStep / LeftReflLe below are the mirrored definitions, and bruhatLe_of_leftReflLe_An is the mirrored conclusion, obtained by transport through ⁻¹ rather than by re-proving anything: a reflection is an involution (IsReflection.inv), ℓ w⁻¹ = ℓ w, and CoxeterBruhat.bruhatLe_inv_iff carries the conclusion back. Nothing in the ≤ᵣ → ≤ proof is duplicated.

    ✅ The open direction is no longer open. This paragraph read "BruhatLe → LeftReflLe transports to BruhatLe → ReflLe, which is qou-3k66 — still open", and that was true when it was written. QOU.IwahoriHecke.BruhatConverse closes qou-3k66, so the transport that paragraph describes now goes through: BruhatConverse.leftReflLe_of_bruhatLe_An and bruhatLe_iff_leftReflLe_An are the left-handed mirrors, obtained exactly as promised here — by ⁻¹, with nothing re-proved. The observation that the transport is faithful in both directions is what makes it free.

        Deleting one letter of a reduced word is a downward reflection step.

        The reflection is the j-th entry of Mathlib's right inversion sequence, and wordProd_mul_getD_rightInvSeq is the identity that names it. This is the atom the converse direction would iterate.

        🛑 Iterating it does not give BruhatLe → ReflLe. The converse is proved in QOU.IwahoriHecke.BruhatConverse, and not by iterating this: it peels a right descent instead and lifts. This lemma is not the route; it is kept because it is the honest atom.
