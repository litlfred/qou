    The negative peel #

    MarkovPeel peeled a positive crossing: tr(ι(x)·σ) = z·tr(x), with z = q³ at the weight the peel itself forces. Markov move II comes in two flavours, and the negative one is not the same statement — σ and σ⁻¹ do not peel to the same scalar. This file computes the other one.

    The computation is a subtraction #

    No new sum manipulation is needed. The skein relation gives σ⁻¹ = σ − (q − qi) on the nose (negCrossing), so the peel of σ⁻¹ is the peel of σ minus (q − qi) times the peel of 1. The only new ingredient is that last piece:

    • trace_extendSite — tr_w(X ⊗ 1) = (∑ₖ w k) · tr_w(X). Adding an inert site multiplies the weighted trace by the total weight, which is the same fact qTrace_one records at X = 1.

    So z⁻ = z − (q − qi)·(∑ₖ w k), and at the forced weight w = (1, q²) that is

    q³ − (q − qi)(1 + q²) = q³ − (q³ − qi) = qi,

    i.e. z⁻ = q⁻¹ (negPeel_scalar_physical) against z⁺ = q³. The asymmetry is real and it is what a framing correction has to absorb.

    Why the two scalars matter #

    z⁺ · z⁻ = q³ · qi = q², which is not 1. So no rescaling by a power of the writhe alone can make both moves invariant — the framing correction needs a strand-count factor as well. That is the subject of QOU.IwahoriHecke.JonesNormalisation, where both constants are pinned by exactly these two numbers.

    An inert extra site multiplies the trace by the total weight #

    theorem QOU.IwahoriHecke.NegativePeel.trace_extendSite {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] {m : ℕ} (w : Fin 2 → R) (X : Matrix (Fin (m + 1) → Fin 2) (Fin (m + 1) → Fin 2) R) :
    (QuantumTrace.siteWeight w * MarkovPeel.extendSiteHom X).trace = (∑ k : Fin 2, w k) * (QuantumTrace.siteWeight w * X).trace

    tr_w(X ⊗ 1) = (∑ₖ w k) · tr_w(X). The new site contributes its whole weight and nothing else, which is qTrace_one's (∑ₖ w k)^{m+1} one factor at a time.

    At the forced weight the negative peel scalar is qi = q⁻¹.

    q³ − (q − qi)(1 + q²) = q³ − (q³ − qi) = qi, using q·qi = 1 once. Against the positive scalar q³, so the two differ by q⁴ — the asymmetry a framing correction must absorb.

    Markov move II⁻, on braids.

    source
    theorem QOU.IwahoriHecke.NegativePeel.peel_scalars_physical {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (q qi : R) (hqi : q * qi = 1) (w : Fin 2 → R) (h0 : w 0 = 1) (h1 : w 1 = q ^ 2) :
    q ^ 3 * (q ^ 3 - (q - qi) * ∑ k : Fin 2, w k) = q ^ 2

    Both scalars at the forced weight. z⁺ = q³ and z⁻ = qi, so z⁺·z⁻ = q² — not 1. That is exactly why a writhe-only framing correction cannot make both moves invariant, and why JonesNormalisation needs a strand-count factor too.
