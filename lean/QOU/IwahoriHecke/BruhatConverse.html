    BruhatLe → ReflLe — the converse bridge closes #

    QOU.IwahoriHecke.BruhatReflection proved ReflLe → BruhatLe and left the converse open, having recorded two wrong diagnoses of what blocked it (the lifting property; then a non-reduced-subword statement that turned out to be outright false). This file closes it, so the two definitions of the Bruhat order — the corpus's subword BruhatLe and the reflection-generated ReflLe of Mathas and Dipper–James — coincide.

    What closes #

    • reflLe_of_bruhatLe — the converse.
    • bruhatLe_iff_reflLe — hence the two orders are the same relation, so a result stated against either may be cited for the other. That is what a consumer of Mathas' Prop 3.7 needs.
    • reflLe_mul_simple_of_ascent — the upward lifting property for ≤ᵣ that BruhatReflection isolated as the missing input, now proved.

    How the blocked branch was unblocked #

    The obstacle BruhatReflection named was real: the induction on ℓ(w) splits a reduced witness ω' for u inside ω = ω₁ ++ [s], and the branch where ω' uses the final s yields u·s ≤ᵣ w·s and needs u ≤ᵣ w, i.e. upward lifting for ≤ᵣ. reflStep_mul_simple handled one step; the chain was open because nothing keeps s an ascent at the intermediate elements.

    The fix is that the intermediate elements are all shorter than w, so the outer induction hypothesis applies to them. Carrying that hypothesis into the lifting lemma — as ConverseBelow — closes the descent case:

    • s an ascent at the intermediate z — recurse along the chain, then reflStep_mul_simple on the last step.
    • s a descent at z — then x ≤ z in the subword order (via bruhatLe_of_reflLe), and bruhatLe_mul_simple_of_isRightDescent — the lifting property proved in BruhatReflection — gives x·s ≤ z. Since ℓ(z) < ℓ(y) < N, the carried hypothesis converts that to x·s ≤ᵣ z, and z →ᵣ y →ᵣ y·s finishes.

    So the lifting property proved earlier is used here — just not where the first wrong diagnosis said it would be. It resolves the descent case of the chain, not the branch itself.

    Scope #

    Nothing here is conditional. DihedralExact and M i j ≠ 0 used to be carried exactly as Matsumoto carried them, since the subword property is used throughout; CoxeterDihedralOrder proves both from the geometric representation, so the two order equivalences hold for every Coxeter system.

      The left-handed mirror — free, and the form the tableau side needs #

      BruhatReflection's left-handed section (LeftReflStep / LeftReflLe) exists because the tableau side of Mathas' Prop. 3.7 produces left multiplications, and it closed LeftReflLe → BruhatLe by transport through ⁻¹. It recorded the other direction as blocked on qou-3k66.

      qou-3k66 is closed above, so the same transport now runs backwards. Nothing here re-proves any part of the converse — it mirrors reflStep/reflLe inversion the other way and composes.
