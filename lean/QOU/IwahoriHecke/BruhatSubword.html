    The subword property, and Bruhat transitivity #

    QOU.Machinery.CoxeterBruhat defines u ≤ w by "some reduced word for w has a subword multiplying to u" and lists four properties it does not prove. CoxeterBruhatOrder closed antisymmetry by a length squeeze. The remaining three all wait on the subword property:

    u ≤ w implies every reduced word for w has a subword multiplying to u.

    That is a statement about all reduced words, so it needs Matsumoto — which QOU.IwahoriHecke.Matsumoto now supplies. This file spends it and closes two more of the four.

    What closes #

    • subword_property — the statement above, and bruhatLe_iff_forall_reduced, which turns the existential definition into a universal one.
    • bruhatLe_trans — hence transitivity, immediately: the witness for v ≤ w can be taken reduced (bruhatLe_reduced_witness), the subword property applies to that word, and List.Sublist is transitive.

    The argument #

    Matsumoto reduces everything to a single braid move, and a braid move only rewrites one alternating block: a subword splits as a ++ b ++ c with b inside the block, and only b has to be replaced. So the whole content is the rank-two lemma exists_subword_altWord_swap: a subword of altWord i j (M i j) has a same-product subword of altWord j i (M j i).

    That in turn is CoxeterRankTwo's structure theorem plus two list facts — the alternating word of length k ≤ n is a prefix of the one of length n in the same spelling, and a tail of it in the other spelling when k < n. The one case those miss is k = n, where the two spellings have the same product outright: that is the braid relation (wordProd_altWord_braid).

    Scope — nothing here is conditional #

    subword_property and bruhatLe_trans used to carry DihedralExact and M i j ≠ 0, solely because they call Matsumoto; Matsumoto no longer has them (CoxeterDihedralOrder proves both from the geometric representation), so neither does this file. Everything below holds for every Coxeter system.

    exists_subword_altWord_swap and the braid-move transport were already unconditional before that: the swap never needs to know the order of σᵢσⱼ, only that a reduced rank-two word is alternating and that the two full-length spellings share a product. That was not the shape I expected when writing it — the hypotheses were stated on the swap first and the compiler reported them unused.

    ✅ The lifting property landed the same day, in QOU.IwahoriHecke.BruhatReflection, off the subword property proved here — so all four of CoxeterBruhat's deferred properties are now closed.

    ✅ And the two Bruhat orders were shown to agree, in QOU.IwahoriHecke.BruhatConverse: BruhatLe ↔ ReflLe and its left-handed mirror BruhatLe ↔ LeftReflLe, which is what a consumer citing Mathas' Prop 3.7 needs. BruhatReflection had supplied ReflLe → BruhatLe; the converse was the open half.

    The rank-two swap #

    source
    theorem QOU.IwahoriHecke.BruhatSubword.exists_subword_altWord_swap {B : Type u_1} {W : Type u_2} [Group W] {M : CoxeterMatrix B} (cs : CoxeterSystem M W) {i j : B} {b : List B} (hb : b.Sublist (BraidWord.altWord i j (M.M i j))) :
    ∃ (b' : List B), b'.Sublist (BraidWord.altWord j i (M.M j i)) ∧ cs.wordProd b' = cs.wordProd b

    The one piece of real content. A subword of altWord i j (M i j) has a same-product subword of altWord j i (M j i).

    The subword's product v lies in the rank-two parabolic and has length at most M i j; by CoxeterRankTwo it has an alternating reduced word, of one spelling or the other. The prefix and tail facts cover every case but ℓ(v) = M i j in the wrong spelling, and there the braid relation says the whole target word already has product v.
