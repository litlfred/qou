    The braid group, and its representation into the Hecke units #

    Everything in this arc so far has been about H(M, q²) and its matrix representation. The object the Jones polynomial is actually a function of is a braid, and there has been no braid group anywhere to speak of.

    Mathlib has none. Measured at the pinned revision (f897ebcf72, a full 6996-file checkout, not a partial one): grep -rl "BraidGroup" Mathlib/ returns nothing, and the only *braid* files in the whole tree are CategoryTheory/Monoidal/Braided, GradedObject/Braiding.lean and Rigid/Braided.lean — braided monoidal categories, which are a different object and supply no B_n. So this is built from the presentation, exactly as the Hecke algebra was built from its own.

    (The revision and file count are stated because a sibling branch had to retract a "Mathlib has no Bruhat order" claim that turned out to be measuring its own incomplete checkout. This one was re-measured against the pin for that reason.)

    What is here #

    • artinRels M — the Artin relations of a Coxeter matrix M, as a subset of FreeGroup B. They are written with the same altProd the Hecke algebra's T_braid and Balanced.sigma_braid use, so the two statements are literally the same alternating word in two different monoids, and map_altProd moves between them with no re-derivation.

    • ArtinGroup M := PresentedGroup (artinRels M), with braidGen its generators. The braid group on m+1 strands is BraidGroupN m := ArtinGroup (coxA m), coxA being the type-A Coxeter matrix already used for the Hecke action.

    • heckeRep — the representation Art(M) → H(M, q²)ˣ, σ_i ↦ σ_i. The universal property of the presentation supplies it once the braid relation is checked in the unit group, which sigmaUnit_braid gets from Balanced.sigma_braid by pushing along Units.coeHom.

      This is the step the corpus's Hecke files kept gesturing at — isUnit_sigma's docstring says invertibility is "what makes H(M, q²) a quotient of the braid group algebra rather than merely of the free algebra", and until now there was no braid group for that sentence to be about.

    • braidTrace — the normalised Markov trace of a braid, and braidTrace_conj, which is Markov move I stated where it belongs: on braids, as invariance under conjugation.

    🛑 Not a link invariant #

    Two things are missing and neither is small. The tower B_n ↪ B_{n+1} and move II on braids are the next file's business. Markov's theorem — that closure-equivalence of braids is generated by exactly these two moves — is a theorem of low-dimensional topology, is not in Mathlib, and is not proved here; without it a Markov-invariant functional is a function on braids, not on links.

    -- Ref: [jones1987] Hecke algebra representations of braid groups and link polynomials -- Ref: [manuscript] bean qou-k4oq

    The Artin relations of a Coxeter matrix M: for each pair i, j, the alternating word i j i ⋯ of length M i j equals j i j ⋯ of length M j i.

    Written with the same altProd as the Hecke algebra's braid relation, so the two are one statement in two monoids.

                Markov move I, on braids. The trace is a conjugation invariant of the braid, so it is already constant on the conjugacy classes that move I identifies.

                This is markovTr_mul_comm transported through the representation; the representation being a group hom is what lets γ⁻¹ appear at all.
