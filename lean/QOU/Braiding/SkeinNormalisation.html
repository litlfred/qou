    Two Skein normalisations are live in this corpus, and they are not rescalings #

    AGENTS.md §7a states the Skein relation as STRICT:

    (H)   L₊ − L₋  =  (q − q⁻¹) · L₀            bridge  h₁ = q − q⁻¹

    Eight live content blocks in five chapters state it differently:

    (J)   q[L₊] − q⁻¹[L₋]  =  (q^{1/2} − q^{-1/2})[L₀]   bridge  s = q^{1/2} − q^{-1/2}

    The natural first reading — "one relation, two normalisations of the bracket" — is wrong, and no_rescaling_of_skein_weights is why: the weight ratio on (L₊ : L₋) is 1 in (H) and q² in (J), and a rescaling [L] ↦ c[L] leaves that ratio fixed. So the only q at which a constant carries (H) to (J) is q² = 1, i.e. the classical point where both bridges vanish. The two conventions are genuinely different relations.

    How the two bridges are related #

    hOne_eq_sSqrt_mul_barEven gives the factorisation in the square-root variable u (q = u², so no Real.sqrt is needed and the file stays over a generic field):

    h₁(u²) = s(u) · (u + u⁻¹)

    and squaring removes the square root entirely:

    hOneSq_eq_twoQ_sq_sub_four        h₁² = [2]_q² − 4
hOneSq_eq_sSq_mul_twoQ_add_two    h₁² = s² · ([2]_q + 2)

    The second is the one that matters downstream, because s² — not s — is what the decay-coupling rate carries (sSq, and sSq_eq_twoQ_sub_two). So converting a rate between the two conventions is multiplication by [2]_q + 2, which is 4.01089686671 at the proton pin.

    Why the split survived undetected #

    barEven_bar_invariant and sJ_bar_antiinvariant: the bridge ratio [2]_q + 2 is bar-even (invariant under q ↦ q⁻¹) while s itself is bar-odd. A bar-parity argument — the corpus's usual separator — therefore cannot distinguish the two conventions, because the factor between them is even.

    What is NOT decided here #

    Whether the rate is restated in (H) to match §7a, or §7a is amended to record (J) as the physics-sector convention, is an author decision: it touches a STRICT rule and eight blocks in five chapters, and nothing proved here forces either reading. This file measures the split; it does not pick a side. Bean qou-8bxv.

    Ground ring: generic field K throughout (AGENTS.md §7c) — every statement here is an algebraic identity, so no archimedean construct appears and there is no ℝ companion. The numeric evaluation at the proton pin lives in computations/skein-normalisation-two-conventions.witness.json. Verified lean-direct, sorry-free.

          The two conventions are not related by a rescaling #

          source
          theorem QOU.Braiding.SkeinNormalisation.no_rescaling_of_skein_weights {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (q c : K) (hq : q ≠ 0) (hplus : c * 1 = q) (hminus : c * -1 = -q⁻¹) :
          q ^ 2 = 1

          No rescaling of the bracket carries (H) to (J). (H) weights the two crossings (L₊, L₋) as (1, −1); (J) weights them (q, −q⁻¹). If a single constant c reproduced both weights, then q = q⁻¹ — so the conventions agree only at q² = 1, the classical point where both bridges vanish.

          This is the structural content of the finding: the two relations are not two spellings of one relation, and no normalisation constant reconciles them.

          The exact relation between the two bridges #

          theorem QOU.Braiding.SkeinNormalisation.hOne_eq_sSqrt_mul_barEven {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (u : K) (hu : u ≠ 0) :

          The (H) bridge factors through the (J) bridge. h₁(u²) = s(u) · (u + u⁻¹): the two Skein bridges differ by the bar-even cofactor u + u⁻¹.
