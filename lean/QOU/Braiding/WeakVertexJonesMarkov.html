    The weak vertex's normalisation is the Jones–Markov relation #

    WeakVertexCouplingNormalisation excluded the family c · (W − W(1)) · bridge by proving the ratio runs, and left the normalisation itself open: what fixes the relation between the weak-vertex form factor W(q) = tr_M((σ₀σ₁)⁵) and the decay-rate coupling s² = (q^{1/2} − q^{-1/2})²?

    This file answers it. The normalisation is not a constant to be found; it is the framing-and-rank factor of the Jones–Markov relation, and everything else follows as polynomial algebra over a generic field.

    The rule #

    For a braid word β ∈ B_n with exponent sum e, the canonical Markov trace of its closure is q^{2e} · V(q^{-2}) / [2]_q^{n-1} — a framing factor q^{2e} (the writhe correction, in the Jones variable t = q^{-2}) and a rank factor [2]_q^{n-1} (the Markov normalisation tr_M 1 = 1 on H_n). Both are read off the braid word. The weak vertex is the closure of (σ₀σ₁)⁵ ∈ B₃, so n = 3 and e = 10, and weakVertexTrace_eq_jones_markov is that instance:

    W · [2]_q² = q¹² + q⁸ − 1 = q²⁰ · J, where J := V_{T(3,5)}(q^{-2}).

    The producer checks the same rule against the corpus's own trace engine on eight torus knots at n = 2, 3, 4, 5, including the mirror pairs T(3,4)/T(4,3) and T(3,5)/T(5,3).

    Why that removes the free constant #

    V_K(1) = 1 for every knot (jonesT35_one here), and V'_K(1) = 0. So J − 1 is the canonically normalised deviation of the weak vertex from its classical value: both the offset and the vanishing order are topological facts rather than choices. W − W(1) was trying to be this object and could not be — W(1) = 1/4 (weakVertexTrace_one) is the Markov rank factor showing through, not a knot invariant.

    The coupling is a product of two q-differences #

    jonesT35_sub_one_eq_qDiff_product: J − 1 = −h₄·h₆/q¹⁰ exactly, with h_k := q^k − q^{-k} (qDiff). The exponents 4 and 6 are the two Jones exponents of T(3,5) (V(t) = t⁴ + t⁶ − t¹⁰) and q^{-10} is the framing. Since h_k(1) = 0, second-order vanishing at q = 1 is a consequence of there being two q-differences, not something a bridge factor arranges — which is the structural fact the excluded family lacked.

    sSq_of_sq: the rate's own coupling is the same shape at half scale, s²(u²) = (u − u⁻¹)², i.e. s² = h_{1/2}². Stating it at q = u² keeps it over a generic field, with no square root and no ordering.

    The ratio, exactly — no limit and no derivative #

    Clearing the framing makes the deviation polynomial, and the cofactors are cyclotomic (geomCofactor):

    • jonesT35_sub_one_mul_pow: q²⁰(J − 1) = −(q⁸ − 1)(q¹² − 1)
    • jones_dev_eq_sSq_mul_geom: q²⁰(J − 1) = −(q · s²) · G₈(q) · G₁₂(q)
    • jones_dev_div_sSq: (J − 1)/s² = −G₈(q)·G₁₂(q)/q¹⁹ = couplingRatio q
    • couplingRatio_one: couplingRatio 1 = −96 = −G₈(1)·G₁₂(1) = −8·12

    So the vanishing order, the leading coefficient and the running all come off one rational function. Nothing here is a limit, a derivative, or a fit, which is why the whole derivation stays over {K : Type*} [Field K].

    Scope #

    This is a positive derivation of the normalisation. It produces no rescaling constant and cannot: couplingRatio is a non-constant rational function, consistent with no_constant_rescaling.

    🛑 It does NOT derive the order of the decay-rate substitution factor, and this docstring claimed it did until 2026-09-05 (bean qou-xql6). The withdrawn claim read 96² = 9216 against substitutionRateFactor. −96 is the classical value of (J − 1)/s², whose numerator vanishes (V_K(1) = 1 for every knot); the substitution factor's does not (W(1) = 1/4), so |W|²/s^k diverges as q → 1 and has no leading constant for −96 to be. Separately the power was wrong: sSq is s², so the merged factor was |W|²/s⁴ where the rate carries s² once — 129.405107404, not 11875.44. See substitutionRateFactor_has_no_classical_leading_value. Every identity below is untouched; only the bridge to the rate is gone.

    Honest limit. s² = h_{1/2}² is a formal half-scale analogue, not itself a Jones deviation — a Jones exponent is an integer, so no knot's V − 1 equals s². These results explain the magnitude gap between the two couplings; they do not remove it, and they do not license substituting either for the other.
