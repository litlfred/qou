        The index k, and what selects it #

        The family has a much simpler form than its definition suggests. Since b_pp/b_nn = q⁴,

        w_k = 1/(q^{2k} + 1) = 1/(1 + (b_pp/b_nn)^{k/2})

        so k/2 is literally the power of the bond ratio used to weight the average (weight_eq_inv_rpow). k = 0 weights by ratio⁰ — flat; k = 1 by its square root; k = 2 by the ratio itself. And the interface has the closed form

        f_pn(k) = [2]_{q^{2-k}} / ([2]_q · [2]_{q^k})

        (interface_closed_form), one expression covering the arithmetic mean at k = 0, the geometric at k = 1 and the harmonic at k = 2. The reflection that makes δ odd about k = 1 is one line from it: interface_reflection gives f_pn(k)·f_pn(2-k) = 1/[2]_q², the squared loop value — so k ↦ 2-k inverts the interface about the loop value, and k = 1 is its only fixed point.

        Which members have an argument. Two, and the enumeration is the point:

        • k = 0 — the undeformed limit. w_0 = 1/2 is what "flat average" means, so it is derived, from an assumption the deformation contradicts. Wrong sign.
        • k = 1 — interface_one_eq_loop_value: the unique member whose interface factor is the loop value 1/[2]_q, and the unique fixed point of the reflection. Two independent characterisations of the same point. Zero.

        Nothing in the SEMF-signed region k > 1 has an argument. k = 2 (the harmonic mean, weight b_nn/(b_pp+b_nn)) gives the right sign and is not adopted for that reason. So the derivable prediction is k = 1: the p-n interface contributes no symmetry energy, and the SEMF term comes from outside this channel. Falsifiable, and the honest close.

        Measured in computations/qbeta/qbeta_averaging_index_k_probe.py: the corpus's actual weight 1/2 + δ/4 sits at k = -0.00659 — negative, so its one bar-breaking ingredient pushes away from k > 1 rather than toward it.

        Bean qou-yf02.

        w q k * (q ^ 2 / (q + 1 / q)) + (1 - w q k) * (q ^ (-2) / (q + 1 / q)) = (q ^ (2 - k) + q ^ (-(2 - k))) / ((q + 1 / q) * (q ^ k + q ^ (-k)))

        theorem QOU.Hecke.BarWeightFamily.interface_reflection {q : ℝ} (hq : 0 < q) (k : ℝ) :
        (q ^ (2 - k) + q ^ (-(2 - k))) / ((q + 1 / q) * (q ^ k + q ^ (-k))) * ((q ^ k + q ^ (-k)) / ((q + 1 / q) * (q ^ (2 - k) + q ^ (-(2 - k))))) = 1 / (q + 1 / q) ^ 2

        The reflection. k ↦ 2 - k inverts the interface about the loop value: the product of the two is 1/[2]_q². This is why δ is odd about k = 1, and why k = 1 — the only fixed point — is where it vanishes.
