    QOU.Hecke.InstanceOnH4A — the HeckeAlgebra class, on the tree's own H₄(q) #

    QOU.Machinery.HeckeH4AlgebraLaws.H4A is the tree's four-strand Hecke algebra: a Ring and Algebra on the 24-dimensional subscript normal-form module, with the presentation proved in QOU.Machinery.HeckeH4StabilizationRung (mulσ₀/₁/₂_hecke, braid_σ₀σ₁, braid_σ₁σ₂, far_commute_σ₀σ₂) and mulNF₄_assoc for associativity. It never had an instance of the class QOU.Hecke.HeckeAlgebra.

    This file supplies it. Together with QOU.Hecke.isHeckeOfFullRank_H4A (rank 24 = 4!) it completes the pair — relations and dimension — at the last level that exercises a new class field: n = 4 is where farCommute first has content. Companion to QOU.Hecke.InstanceOnH3A. Bean qou-ti7p.

    Keeping spans cheap: the word tree, one generator at a time #

    spans needs every normal-form basis vector to be a word in the generators, and the longest is σ₀σ₁σ₀σ₂σ₁σ₀ — six letters. Expanding six nested mulσs per basis vector is what made the analogous attempt on MarkovTraceH4's ASCII basis run to ~8.7 GB (bean qou-awp7).

    It is avoidable, because right multiplication by a generator is a single mulσ: mulNF₄_e_right gives u · b_j = wordAction j u, and the generators sit at basis indices 1, 2, 6, whose word actions are exactly mulσ₀, mulσ₁, mulσ₂. So each basis vector is its parent times one generator, and every step is depth 1 on a literal vector:

    b_j = b_{parent j} * σ_{last letter of word j}

    The 23 non-unit basis vectors form a tree under that relation — verified, not assumed: every parent word is itself in the basis list. That is what b_*_tree below record, and they are generated from wordAction's own word list so they cannot drift from it.

    Generated by the script in this commit; the word list is read out of QOU.Machinery.HeckeH4AlgebraLaws.wordAction.
