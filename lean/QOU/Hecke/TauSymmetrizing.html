    QOU.Hecke.TauSymmetrizing — the canonical trace τ, and its Gram determinant #

    Third brick of bean qou-kwoa (Option C: derive the Schur-element criterion instead of citing it). QOU.MathlibExt.WeightedTraceGram did the half of Geck–Pfeiffer 7.2.6 that is pure linear algebra; this file starts the half that is genuinely about H_n(q).

    Ref: [geckpfeiffer2000] Thm 7.2.6 and §8.1. The citation stays: Geck and Pfeiffer proved this, and formalising a piece of it does not change that. What changes is its role: it is attribution. The structure-constant identity that the two determinant theorems below take as a hypothesis is discharged in QOU.Hecke.TauStructureConstants (tau_Tw_mul_Tw), which also supplies the unconditional tauGram_det and tauGram_det_ne_zero. The hypothesis form is kept here deliberately: the implication is what this file is about, and it is provable without any Hecke input beyond Tw_mul_Tw.

    τ is definable in one line, and that was the surprise #

    The docstring of WeightedTraceGram recorded that Mathlib has no symmetric-algebra theory — true — and inferred that constructing τ would need that theory built first. That inference was wrong. τ is the T_1-coordinate in the T_w basis, and QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.basisTwOfField already supplies the basis, so τ is Module.Basis.coord. Its defining property τ(T_w) = δ_{w,1} is then immediate.

    What is not immediate — and is the whole content — is the structure-constant identity

    τ(T_v · T_w) = δ_{w, v⁻¹} · q^{ℓ(v)},

    which says the Gram matrix of (x, y) ↦ τ(x y) is supported on the graph of inversion. This file states it as an explicit hypothesis and derives the determinant from it; it does not assume it anywhere.

    What is proved #

    • tau, tau_Tw, tau_one — the functional and its defining property.
    • tau_Tw_mul_Tw_of_length_add — the identity's length-additive locus, proved outright from Tw_mul_Tw: there τ(T_v T_w) = δ_{wv, 1}.
    • tauGram, tauGram_apply — the Gram matrix.
    • tauGram_det_of_structure_constants — given the identity, det = sign(inv) · ∏_v q^{ℓ(v)}.
    • tauGram_det_ne_zero_of_structure_constants — hence nonzero for every q ≠ 0.

    What the last one does NOT say #

    det(τ-Gram) ≠ 0 for all q ≠ 0 is not semisimplicity of H_n(q), which is false at roots of unity. It is the statement that H_n(q) is a symmetric Frobenius algebra, which it is for every q ≠ 0. The criterion needs both this and the split decomposition: on a split semisimple A ≅ ∏_λ M_{d_λ}(K), WeightedTraceGram.weightedGram_det_ne_zero_iff says a weighted trace's Gram is nondegenerate iff every weight is nonzero, and τ's weights are 1/s_λ. So the two together give s_λ = 0 for some λ ⟹ H_n(q) is not split semisimple. The converse — the direction SchurElementCriterion is usually wanted for — is a separate argument (Tits deformation) and is not here.

    The induction, and why it is smaller than the textbook statement #

    Discharged in QOU.Hecke.TauStructureConstants. Recorded here because the route matters: Geck-Pfeiffer 8.1.1 is usually run on ℓ(v) + ℓ(w) with a descent common to both factors, which needs the exchange condition — and Mathlib has none (measured: no occurrence of xchange anywhere in Mathlib/GroupTheory/Coxeter/). Inducting on ℓ(v) alone with an arbitrary left descent avoids it entirely, because a left descent of v and a right descent of w at the same i are contradictory once w is pinned to v⁻¹ or v⁻¹ s_i. The (q-1) term therefore never contributes.

      The identity holds on the length-additive locus, without assumption: where the lengths add, T_v · T_w = T_{wv} and τ reads off δ_{wv,1}.

      This is consistent with the conjectured δ_{w,v⁻¹} q^{ℓ(v)} rather than a proof of it: on this locus w = v⁻¹ forces ℓ(v) + ℓ(w) = ℓ(1) = 0, so the q-power is q^0 = 1. The locus therefore never sees the q-powers, which is exactly why the general identity needs the descent induction instead.
