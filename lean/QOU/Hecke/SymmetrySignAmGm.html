    noncomputable def QOU.Hecke.SymmetrySignAmGm.delta (bpp bpn bnn : ℝ) :
    ℝ

    The p–n slope: how the bond sum changes when one like-species pair of bonds is traded for a p–n bond, in the corpus's L = -log b letters. Positive means mixing binds more — the SEMF sign.

      (q ^ 2 / (q + 1 / q) + (q ^ 2)⁻¹ / (q + 1 / q)) / 2 - 1 / (q + 1 / q) = (q - 1 / q) ^ 2 / (2 * (q + 1 / q))

      The AM–GM gap IS the Hecke parameter, squared. (b_pp+b_nn)/2 - 1/[2]_q = h²/(2[2]_q) with h = q - 1/q, because [2]_q² - 4 = h². The size of the wrong-signed term is not incidental to the deformation — it is the deformation.

      Deriving the convex weight w #

      w is not a free parameter. The corpus's three interface factors are f_pp = z (positive crossing, the Markov axiom tr(ασ) = z·tr(α)), f_nn = z - h (negative crossing, via the Hecke skein σ⁻¹ = σ - h), and f_pn = z - h·(1/2 - δ/4) (bring_color_weighted, the default since 2026-06-01). weight_of_convention shows the convex weight is then forced: w = 1/2 + δ/4, identically, for any z, h, δ.

      So "derive w" reduces entirely to deriving the flat 1/2 in σ_avg = (σ + σ⁻¹)/2 — and weight_miss shows that flat half is the whole story: the miss against the crossing splits as (q²-1)/(2(q²+1)) + δ/4, whose two parts stand at 0.0519819 and 0.000344, a ratio of 151. The only derived ingredient in w is 151× too small to matter.

      theorem QOU.Hecke.SymmetrySignAmGm.weight_of_convention (z h d : ℝ) :

      theorem QOU.Hecke.SymmetrySignAmGm.weight_miss {q d : ℝ} (hq : 0 < q) :
      1 / 2 + d / 4 - 1 / (q ^ 2 + 1) = (q ^ 2 - 1) / (2 * (q ^ 2 + 1)) + d / 4

      The miss against the crossing, split. The deformation term (q²-1)/(2(q²+1)) and the flavour term δ/4 are the only two contributions, and at q₀ the first is 151× the second — so the flavour split, the one derived ingredient in w, is irrelevant to the sign.

      Which means can give the SEMF sign: exactly those below the geometric #

      mean_mul_reciprocal_mean is the two-variable identity M_p · M_{-p} = ab, whose immediate consequence is that δ is odd under p ↦ -p. The convention sits at p = 1; its mirror p = -1 is the harmonic mean, which therefore has exactly the opposite δ — same magnitude, SEMF sign. Since M_p is increasing in p and M_0 is the geometric mean, the sign is right exactly when p < 0: strictly BELOW the geometric mean, which is where no average with p ≥ 0 can go.

      Stated here for p = ±1, where it needs no rpow and is the case that matters; the general statement is measured in computations/qbeta/qbeta_symmetry_sign_is_am_gm_locked_probe.py.

      The three means in [2] form #

      With b_pp = q²/[2]_q and b_nn = q⁻²/[2]_q, writing [2]_x = x + 1/x:

      AM = [2]_{q²}/(2[2]_q)      GM = 1/[2]_q      HM = 2/([2]_q·[2]_{q²})

      ⚠ Their convex weights are 1/2, q⁻¹/[2]_q, q⁻²/[2]_{q²}, which LOOK like a ladder q^{-k}/[2]_{q^k}. They are not. At p = 2 that formula predicts 0.55198 against the true 0.55089, and it fails at every non-classical p tested. It is a coincidence of the three classical means and is recorded here so nobody re-derives it as structure.
