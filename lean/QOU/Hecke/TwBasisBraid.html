    QOU.Hecke.TwBasisBraid — the braid relations for the T_w operators #

    Continues bean qou-twwf. QOU.Hecke.TwBasis proved the Hecke quadratic relation for the right-multiplication operators Rs i on W →₀ R, for an arbitrary Coxeter system. What is missing for a representation of H_n(q) is the braid side of the presentation:

    • distant generators commute, Rs i ∘ Rs j = Rs j ∘ Rs i for |i - j| ≥ 2;
    • adjacent generators braid, Rs i ∘ Rs j ∘ Rs i = Rs j ∘ Rs i ∘ Rs j.

    Abstractly both need Coxeter combinatorics Mathlib does not have (the strong exchange property, parabolic subgroups, minimal coset representatives). For type Aₙ they instead reduce to a finite case analysis, because QOU.Hecke.TwBasisPerm.rsFun_perm makes the branch condition a comparison of w at two adjacent points.

    What is proved here #

    • swap_comm_of_ne — transpositions with disjoint supports commute.
    • Rs_comm_single / Rs_comm — the commuting relation, for any two generators whose four indices are pairwise distinct.
    • Rs_comm_of_lt — the same, from the usual hypothesis i + 1 < j.
    • swap_braid — (ab)(bc)(ab) = (bc)(ab)(bc), both sides being (ac).
    • Rs_braid_single / Rs_braid — the braid relation for adjacent generators, Rs i ∘ Rs j ∘ Rs i = Rs j ∘ Rs i ∘ Rs j when j = i + 1.

    Together with QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.Rs_sq this is the whole Hecke presentation holding for the operators Rs, which is what a representation of H_n(q) on W →₀ R requires. Building that algebra map, and reading the linear independence of the T_w off it, is the next step and is not done here.

    How the braid case goes through #

    The three points i, i+1, i+2 carry three distinct values of w, so their order is one of six. In each, rsFun_perm turns every branch into a comparison already in context, and the six group elements of the rank-2 coset normalise under s² = 1, t² = 1 and sts = tst. So simp only with the value lemmas and the six comparison facts runs the whole computation; three of the six orders need module afterwards to match coefficients, and two of the eight rcases branches are order-inconsistent.

    One trap worth recording: with simp only the discharger for a conditional rewrite sees only the lemmas listed. A positive side condition P closes on P = True, but a negative one ¬ P becomes ¬ False and then goes nowhere unless not_false_eq_true is in the list. Omitting it does not fail loudly — simp silently leaves the Rs unapplied, and the error surfaces much later as module reporting 1 = 0.
