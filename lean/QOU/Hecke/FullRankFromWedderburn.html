    QOU.Hecke.FullRankFromWedderburn — the Iwahori rank for the tree's H₃ and H₄ #

    QOU.Hecke.FullRank introduced IsHeckeOfFullRank R n A — free of rank n! — because QOU.Hecke.HeckeAlgebra does not pin the dimension and is satisfied by algebras far too small (InstanceH2.rankOneModel inhabits it at every n). This file discharges that condition for the tree's own H₃(q) and H₄(q): QOU.Machinery.HeckeH3AlgebraInstance.H3A and QOU.Machinery.HeckeH4AlgebraLaws.H4A.

    Where the dimension actually comes from #

    Both are structures wrapping their coordinate vector — H3A R h has the single field v : Fin 6 → R, H4A R h has v : Fin 24 → R — and their Module R instances are componentwise on that field. So each is R-linearly equivalent to its coordinate space and the dimension is 6 and 24 unconditionally: no genericity hypotheses, no Wedderburn theory. That is what h3CoordEquiv / h4CoordEquiv say, and it is the whole proof.

    Bean qou-awp7 planned to reach n = 4 through a 3 × 24 closure table — the reduced-word operators shown independent, then shown to span. Neither half is needed: the carrier is a coordinate space by construction.

    What this does and does not establish #

    IsHeckeOfFullRank fixes the dimension. That is exactly what the degenerate models fail — rankOneModel is one-dimensional at every n — and it is what QOU.Hecke.not_isHeckeOfFullRank_rankOne turns on. It is not, on its own, evidence that H3A/H4A are the Hecke algebras. That comes from the multiplication, and that is where the tree's real work is:

    • HeckeH3AlgebraLaws and HeckeH4StabilizationRung prove the presentation — quadratic, both braid relations, and at n = 4 far commutation;
    • HeckeH4AlgebraLaws.mulNF₄_assoc proves the product associative, via the structured route (mulσₖ (u·v) = u·(v·σₖ) lifted along the right basis);
    • HeckeH3Wedderburn.wedderburn₃ and HeckeH4Wedderburn.wedderburn₄ are sorry-free AlgEquivs onto K × K × M₂(K) and K × K × M₂(K) × M₃(K) × M₃(K).

    The last two corroborate the count independently — 1 + 1 + 4 = 6 and 1 + 1 + 4 + 9 + 9 = 24 — under the Wenzl genericity conditions (q ≠ 0, q² + 1 ≠ 0, q⁴ + q² + 1 ≠ 0, and at rank 4 also q⁴ + 1 ≠ 0, exactly the degeneracy locus of the coordinate map's determinant). A Wedderburn block count that holds only generically agreeing with a coordinate count that holds always is a real consistency check on the basis, so it is recorded below rather than left implicit.

        The Wedderburn block counts agree with the coordinate counts #

        For the record: the generic block decomposition and the unconditional coordinate count give the same number at both ranks. Disagreement would mean one of the two is wrong about the basis.

        source
        theorem QOU.Hecke.wedderburn₃_block_count :
        1 + 1 + 2 * 2 = Nat.factorial 3

        H₃(q)'s Wedderburn blocks sum to its dimension: 1 + 1 + 2² = 3!.

        source
        theorem QOU.Hecke.wedderburn₄_block_count :
        1 + 1 + 2 * 2 + 3 * 3 + 3 * 3 = Nat.factorial 4

        H₄(q)'s Wedderburn blocks sum to its dimension: 1 + 1 + 2² + 3² + 3² = 4!.
