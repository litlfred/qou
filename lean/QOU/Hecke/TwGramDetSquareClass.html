    QOU.Hecke.TwGramDetSquareClass — the T_w Gram determinant's square class #

    Bean qou-kwoa, the determinant's value, specialised to the object the corpus actually uses.

    QOU.MathlibExt.RegTraceGramProduct derives the integer constant ∏_λ d_λ^(d_λ²) for the Gram determinant of a semisimple algebra in a product basis, and QOU.MathlibExt.RegTraceGramBasisChange transports a Gram determinant between bases at the cost of a square. This file instantiates the two at the Hecke algebra, so the left-hand side is literally (twGram K (q ^ 2) n).det.

    The statement, and why it is an ∃ #

    (twGram K (q ^ 2) n).det = (det P)² · ∏_λ ± d_λ^(d_λ²)

    The ∃ P is not vagueness — it is the correct strength. A Gram determinant is an invariant of a bilinear form only up to squares of units, because congruence G ↦ PᵀGP is the transformation law. So no theorem of this shape can pin an integer on the nose, and what is true is that ∏_λ d_λ^(d_λ²) is exactly the square class of det(twGram). The witness is explicit in the proof — the change of basis from T_w to the matrix-unit basis pulled back along the Wedderburn isomorphism — and is not evaluated.

    d_λ is the number of standard Young tableaux of shape λ, i.e. the dimension of the Wedderburn block, so the constant is the one computations/hecke/tw_gram_determinant_probe.py measured at n = 1, 2, 3 as 1, 2⁴ and 2⁴·3¹⁸.

    Relation to twGram_det_ne_zero #

    QOU.Hecke.TwGramDetNonzero reaches det(twGram) ≠ 0 by a route that never computes a determinant at all. This file gets the same non-vanishing as a corollary of the formula, by a completely different argument: the change-of-basis factor is a unit and each block contributes ± d_λ^(d_λ²). The two agreeing is a real cross-check, and neither is derived from the other.

    That corollary carries a hypothesis — that no block dimension dies in K — which is not discharged here. It is true in characteristic zero, since every shape admits at least one standard tableau, but proving 0 < d_λ is separate work and is not done.

    What is NOT claimed #

    det P is not evaluated. This does not say det(twGram) = ∏_λ d_λ^(d_λ²); it says the two differ by a square. Nothing here touches the q-part of the determinant, where the Schur elements live, so this is not a proof of Geck–Pfeiffer 7.2.6.
