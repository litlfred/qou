    QOU.Hecke.TwBasis — the T_w right-multiplication operators #

    Toward the T_w basis of H_n(q) (bean qou-twwf), which is what det(Gram) must be stated against and hence what the WenzlCriterion / SchurElementCriterion discharge ultimately needs.

    Why this route, and not reduced words #

    The obvious construction — define T_w as the product along a reduced word for w — needs Matsumoto's theorem for well-definedness (any two reduced words differ by braid moves). Mathlib does not have it: Coxeter/Basic.lean:78 lists "State and prove Matsumoto's theorem" as an explicit TODO.

    So this file takes the standard Bourbaki route instead, which avoids Matsumoto entirely. Build the free module W →₀ R with basis indexed by W, define right multiplication by each T_{s i} directly on that basis by the length dichotomy, and verify the Hecke relations for those operators. A representation of H_n(q) on the free module is what makes the T_w linearly independent — well-definedness is then a consequence rather than a prerequisite.

    What is proved here (sorry-free, any Coxeter system) #

    • rsFun / Rs i — right multiplication by T_{s i}: T_w ↦ T_{w s_i} when the length goes up, ↦ (q-1)T_w + q T_{w s_i} when it goes down. Stated for an arbitrary cs : CoxeterSystem M W and [CommRing R].
    • length_flip — the branch condition genuinely flips when s i is applied twice, which is what makes the two cases fit together.
    • Rs_Rs_single and Rs_sq — the Hecke quadratic relation Rs i ∘ Rs i = (q-1)·Rs i + q·id, on basis vectors and then as an operator identity.

    What is NOT proved yet #

    The braid relations for Rs, and hence the algebra map H_n(q) → End (W →₀ R) and the independence of the T_w. The braid case needs more Coxeter combinatorics than the quadratic case, which only needed length_mul_simple. Nothing here bears on it.

    This file is general in cs; it becomes a statement about S_{n+1} by feeding it QOU.Hecke.SymmetricCoxeter.permCoxeterSystem — the type-Aₙ system proved in this same PR, which is what put cs.length within reach of Equiv.Perm in the first place.
