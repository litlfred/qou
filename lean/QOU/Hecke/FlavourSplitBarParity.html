          The bar-norm hypothesis: one structure that gives both constraints #

          Sections above establish two separate facts about the prefactor C: it obeys C(q⁻¹) = q²·C(q) (parity), and — measured, not proved here — it vanishes to order 2 at q = 1, which is what makes the baseline vanish to order 3.

          Both follow from a single hypothesis: that C is q⁻¹ times the bar-norm of something. For any f, write

          barNorm f q  :=  -(f q · f q⁻¹)

          Then barNorm f is bar-even for free (barNorm_bar_even), so q⁻¹ · barNorm f satisfies the parity condition for free (barNorm_scaled_parity) — for every f, with no computation. And when f vanishes at q = 1 so does f q⁻¹, so the product vanishes to order 2; that half is arithmetic rather than a theorem here, but it is the same one hypothesis doing both jobs.

          ℏ_q is the instance: q⁻¹ · barNorm hbarOf q = ℏ_q² exactly (hbarSq_eq_scaled_barNorm).

          🛑 The hypothesis itself is NOT derived. Nothing here says the prefactor must be a bar-norm. What is shown is that assuming it collapses two independent constraints into one, and that ℏ_q is then the minimal instance — a well-posedness result about the search, not a closure of it. The /3 is untouched by this too.
