    QOU.Hecke.PermLength — Coxeter length on S_{n+1} IS the inversion count #

    The gate the braid half of the T_w basis was waiting on (bean qou-twwf).

    Why this is the right target #

    QOU.Hecke.TwBasis proves the Hecke quadratic relation for the operators Rs i using only Mathlib's length_mul_simple dichotomy — that lemma says the length changes by one, without saying which way. The braid relation needs to know which way, and the textbook route (dihedral reduction through parabolic cosets) needs machinery Mathlib does not have: its whole GroupTheory/Coxeter/ directory is four files, with no strong exchange property, no parabolic subgroups and no minimal coset representatives.

    For type A the branch is decidable by a value comparison, and that is what this file establishes.

    What is proved (sorry-free) #

    • permCox_simple — the Coxeter system's simple reflections are the adjacent transpositions. This is what lets Mathlib's abstract length talk to the concrete inversion count.
    • exists_descent — a permutation other than the identity has an adjacent descent (via Fin.strictMono_iff_lt_succ and eq_one_of_strictMono).
    • length_le_invCount — by induction on the inversion count, peeling a descent.
    • invCount_le_length — by reverse induction along a reduced word.
    • length_eq_invCount — ℓ w = invCount w.
    • length_mul_swap_eq_succ_iff — the descent characterisation: ℓ(w · sᵢ) = ℓ(w) + 1 ↔ w(i) < w(i+1).

    Mathlib has neither: it carries signAux, the inversion parity as a ℤˣ-valued product, and no ℕ-valued count, and it never compares either to ℓ.

    What this unlocks, and what it does not #

    length_mul_swap_eq_succ_iff makes every branch condition in Rs a comparison of w at two adjacent points, so the braid relation Rs i ∘ Rs j ∘ Rs i = Rs j ∘ Rs i ∘ Rs j becomes a finite case analysis on the relative order of w(i), w(i+1), w(i+2). That case analysis is not done here, nor is the algebra map H_n(q) → End (W →₀ R) or the independence of the T_w that would follow from it.
