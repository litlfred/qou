    A pair-additive bond-counting law: three per-bond energies against three pair counts. Under Option B the energies are e_t = -ln b_t and the counts are read off a bond graph; nothing below depends on either reading.

      2 * bondSum epp epn enn npp npn nnn = epp * dP + enn * dN + (2 * epn - epp - enn) * npn

      The cut identity, ring form. If the proton and neutron colour classes have total degrees d_P and d_N — the degree-sum formula on each class, 2 n_pp + n_pn = d_P, 2 n_nn + n_pn = d_N — then twice the bond sum is the like-species degree sum plus the cut weighted by 2 e_pn - e_pp - e_nn.         A constant cut carries no symmetry term. On a k-regular graph whose p–n cut is a constant c (every segregated layout; the chain has c = 1), the difference of the bond sums at two compositions is linear in the composition difference, with only the like-species energies — for every e_pp, e_pn, e_nn.         theorem QOU.Hecke.BondCutSymmetry.bondSum_wellMixed {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] (epp epn enn k μ Z N : K) :
        bondSum epp epn enn ((k * Z - μ * Z * N) / 2) (μ * Z * N) ((k * N - μ * Z * N) / 2) = k / 2 * (epp * Z + enn * N) + energySlope epp epn enn * μ / 4 * ((Z + N) ^ 2 - (N - Z) ^ 2)

        The well-mixed (mean-field) layout carries exactly one symmetry term. With cut n_pn = μ·Z·N on a k-regular graph, the bond sum is the like-species line plus δμ/4 times (A² - (N-Z)²): the mixing contribution to the volume term and the symmetry term have the same coefficient, opposite signs.

        This is the only place the mean-field model enters. It is a model, not a theorem about the braid: it is what "well-mixed" means.

          npn ≤ d * Z ∧ npn ≤ d * N

          The cut is bounded by the smaller colour class times the degree bound. From the degree-sum identities with non-negative like-species counts and a degree bound d, n_pn ≤ d·Z and n_pn ≤ d·N. So on any bounded-degree graph the layout-dependent part δ·n_pn is at most |δ|·d·min(Z, N) — a chain (d = 2) can move the law by at most 2|δ|·min(Z,N), which is linear in |N-Z| at fixed A, never quadratic.

          E0 * bondSum epp epn enn npp npn nnn ≠ aV * A - aA * (A * A / A)

          No pair-additive bond count with non-negative energies agrees with the SEMF at pure neutron matter — on any graph, with any interface, at any scale E₀ > 0. The left side is ≥ 0 and the right side is < 0. This is the layout-free form of "the bond count cannot supply the symmetry energy": the magnitude the data demands is the magnitude that makes neutron matter unbound, and positive bond energies cannot unbind anything.

          The interface weight: first-order miss, and the flavour tilt's sign #

          theorem QOU.Hecke.BondCutSymmetry.weight_miss_ge_half_hbar {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [LinearOrder K] [IsStrictOrderedRing K] {q : K} (hq : 1 ≤ q) :
          (q - 1) / (2 * q) ≤ (q ^ 2 - 1) / (2 * (q ^ 2 + 1))

          The miss against the crossing is first order in ℏ_q. With ℏ_q = (q-1)/q, the deformation part of SymmetrySignAmGm.weight_miss satisfies (q²-1)/(2(q²+1)) ≥ ℏ_q/2 for every q ≥ 1 — the difference is (q-1)²/(2q(q²+1)) ≥ 0. So a correction of order ℏ_q² cannot close it unless its coefficient is at least 1/(2ℏ_q), which at q₀ is about 5.

          The flavour tilt pushes the weight the wrong way. The one derived sub-leading ingredient in w = 1/2 + δ_f/4 is the p–n flavour split δ_f = Δm_pn/M_N, whose sign is fixed by the neutron being heavier: δ_f > 0. It moves w up, while the crossing 1/(q²+1) sits below 1/2 (SymmetrySignAmGm.crossing_lt_half). So the miss is larger with the derived correction than without it.
