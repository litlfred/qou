    QOU.Hecke.TauSchurElement — the abstract Schur elements of H_n(q), and they never vanish #

    Option 2 of the qou-kwoa fork, completed. The abstract Schur element is s_λ = 1 / c_λ, where c_λ is the weight of τ on the λ-block of a splitting H_n(q) ≅ ∏_λ M_{d_λ}(K). That is their definition, not a formula; the identification with Wenzl's hook-length product schurElement Q X is option 3 and is not here.

    The chain #

    1. τ(T_v T_w) = δ_{w,v⁻¹} q^{ℓ(v)} (TauStructureConstants) ⟹ the Gram determinant is sign(inv) · ∏_v q^{ℓ(v)}, nonzero for every q ≠ 0.
    2. Hence τ is nondegenerate: ∀ y, τ(z y) = 0 forces z = 0 (tau_eq_zero_of_forall_mul — a determinant argument in the T_w basis).
    3. A vanishing block weight would put a whole block in the radical (QOU.MathlibExt.exists_ne_zero_forall_mul_eq_zero_of_traceWeight_eq_zero).
    4. So every block weight is nonzero, and s_λ = 1/c_λ exists and is nonzero — at every rank and every q ≠ 0, for any splitting.

    What this is, and what it is not #

    It is the conclusion side of the Geck–Pfeiffer criterion, derived: the Schur elements of H_n(q) do not vanish where the algebra splits. Read contrapositively it is the statement the arc has been aiming at — a candidate weight system with a zero is realised by no splitting.

    It is not the criterion itself. SchurElementCriterion asserts the converse implication (all s_λ ≠ 0 ⟹ semisimple), which is Tits deformation and is not here; and it is stated with Wenzl's two-parameter schurElement Q X, a different object from s_λ until the identification of option 3 is made. SchurElementCriterion therefore still has no instance, and the heckeWedderburn shortcut is still refused (see WeightedTraceGram).

    Splittings are taken as a hypothesis E rather than produced here, so nothing below depends on which q admit one; that is deliberate — the statement is "for any splitting, the weights are nonzero".

    Ref: [geckpfeiffer2000] §7.2, §8.1 — attribution.

    The abstract Schur element of the block i, relative to a splitting: the reciprocal of τ's weight there.

    This is the definition of the Schur element. Identifying it with Wenzl's hook-length product QOU.MathlibExt.schurElement Q X is a separate theorem (option 3) and is not available here.
