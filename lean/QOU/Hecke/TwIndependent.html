    QOU.Hecke.TwIndependent — the T_w are linearly independent in H_n(q) #

    The independence half of the T_w basis (bean qou-twwf). QOU.Hecke.TwRep gives the algebra map toEnd : H_n(q) →ₐ Module.End K (S →₀ K) and shows the module is cyclic, generated by T_1 — the spanning half. This file supplies the other direction.

    The move that avoids Matsumoto #

    Define T_w as the product of the σ along some reduced word for w (Classical.choose). That is a priori not well-defined: two reduced words for the same w might give different elements, and ruling that out is exactly Matsumoto's theorem, which Mathlib lists as an open TODO (Coxeter/Basic.lean:78). (This corpus has since proved it — QOU.IwahoriHecke.Matsumoto, hypothesis-free for M i j ≤ 3. The route below remains the cheaper one and is kept: it needs no Coxeter machinery at all.)

    But independence does not need well-definedness. applyWord_single shows that along any reduced word the operators simply walk the basis, so whichever word was chosen, T_w · T_1 = T_w in the module. The images of the T_w are therefore the distinct standard basis vectors, and a family whose image under a linear map is independent is itself independent.

    What is proved #

    • applyWord_single — along a word that is reduced relative to w, the operators send T_w to T_{w·π ω}.

    • orbitMap_Tw — T_w ↦ single w 1.

    • linearIndependent_Tw — the T_w are linearly independent, so dim_K H_n(q) ≥ (n+1)!.

    • basisTw — conditional: if orbitMap is injective the T_w are a basis.

    The remaining gap is stated exactly by that last hypothesis. orbitMap is already surjective (QOU.Hecke.TwRep.orbitMap_surjective), so injectivity is equivalent to dim_K H_n(q) ≤ (n+1)! — that the T_w also span. That is the one place Matsumoto is genuinely needed, and it is not proved here: do not read basisTw as a proof that the T_w are a basis.
