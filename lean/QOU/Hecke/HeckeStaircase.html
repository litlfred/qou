    QOU.Hecke.HeckeStaircase — the descending runs σ_n σ_{n-1} ⋯ σ_{n-k+1} #

    Second brick of the type-A spanning argument (bean qou-twwf). The tower decomposition H_{n+1}(q) = Σ_k incl(H_n(q)) · c_k needs three algebraic facts about the runs c_k, and this file proves them.

    Why an ℕ-indexed generator layer #

    The runs are indexed by n - k, and every side condition is an inequality between such indices. Stated over Fin (n+1) those become Fin.mk juggling; stated over ℕ they are omega calls. So gen m is σ_m for m ≤ n and junk (1) above, exactly as QOU.Hecke.SymmetricCoxeter.gen does for the group side, and everything below is ℕ arithmetic.

    What is proved #

    • stair_comm — a generator strictly below the run commutes past the whole run.
    • stair_fold — re-applying the run's last letter folds back: c_{k+1} σ_{n-k} = (q-1)·c_{k+1} + q·c_k. This is where the quadratic relation enters, and it is the only place a coefficient appears.
    • stair_push — a generator above the run's foot passes through with its index dropped by one: c_k σ_i = σ_{i-1} c_k. Braid on one letter, commutation on the rest; the Hecke analogue of SymmetricCoxeter.t_push. Its hypothesis is n + 1 - k < i, not n - k + 1 < i: the two agree for k ≤ n and differ at the full run, where the second is off by one against truncated subtraction.

    Together with c_k σ_{n-k} = c_{k+1} (definitional) these are the four cases of the closure argument. The closure itself, the submodule, and the induction on n are not here.

        The foot of c_k = σ_n ⋯ σ_{n-k+1} is σ_{n+1-k}, and n + 1 - k is the right way to write it: n - k + 1 agrees for k ≤ n but is off by one at the full run k = n+1, where truncated subtraction turns n - (n+1) + 1 into 1 while the actual foot is σ_0. That would silently exclude i = 1 from the last case of the closure argument, where it is needed and where it is true — at n = 1, c₂ σ₁ = σ₁σ₀σ₁ = σ₀σ₁σ₀ = σ₀ c₂.
