    QOU.Hecke.TwRep — H_n(q) acts on the free module K[S_{n+1}] #

    The payoff of bean qou-twwf. QOU.Hecke.TwBasis and QOU.Hecke.TwBasisBraid verify the whole Hecke presentation for the right-multiplication operators Rs i on S_{n+1} →₀ K:

    • Rs_sq — Rs i * Rs i = (q-1)·Rs i + q;
    • Rs_comm_of_lt — distant generators commute;
    • Rs_braid — adjacent generators braid.

    Those are exactly the three constructors of QOU.Hecke.HeckeRel, so the universal property of the RingQuot presentation turns them into an algebra map out of H_n(q).

    What is proved #

    • toEnd — the algebra map H_n(q) →ₐ[K] Module.End K (S_{n+1} →₀ K).
    • toEnd_σ — it sends the generator σ_i to Rs i.
    • orbitMap / orbitMap_surjective — the module is cyclic, generated by T_1: every element of S_{n+1} →₀ K is h · T_1 for some h ∈ H_n(q).

    This is the representation the Bourbaki route to the T_w basis is built on, and it avoids Matsumoto's theorem (which Mathlib lists as an open TODO at Coxeter/Basic.lean:78; this corpus proved it in QOU.IwahoriHecke.Matsumoto, but this route does not need it). Surjectivity is the spanning half of the basis theorem; the independence half — equivalently, that orbitMap is injective, so dim H_n(q) = (n+1)! — is not proved here.

    One performance note, since it cost this session forty minutes. The three relation checks below were first written as simpa [Module.End.mul_eq_comp], which does not fail — it takes over 150 s and had to be killed. Written as explicit rws against Rs_sq / Rs_braid / Rs_comm_of_lt the whole file elaborates in 6 s. Do not "simplify" them back.
