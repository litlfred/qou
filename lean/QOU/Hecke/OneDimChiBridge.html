    1-dim character ↔ per-generator volume bridge #

    For any 1-dimensional representation φ : H_n(q) → R, the character χ^φ on a braid word β factorises as a product of per-generator contributions:

    χ^φ(β) = ∏_i V̂_i^φ

    where V̂_i^φ collects the φ-image of all crossings at generator i.

    This is the universal 1-dim formula verified numerically at A = 1 (proton, neutron) and A = 2 (deuteron) to ~1e-16 precision in [v_hat_to_delta_lambda_bridge.py]. The proof relies only on two facts:

    1. φ is an R-algebra homomorphism: φ(a · b) = φ(a) · φ(b).
    2. R is commutative, so individual scalar factors commute and can be regrouped by generator index.

    The companion proof in [docs/audits/2026-05-09-kite-tail-reconciliation.md] gives the full reasoning.

    For multi-dim irreps the analogue does NOT hold — the trace of a matrix product does not factor as a product of traces. Closure in the multi-dim sector requires the operator-valued A^λ_{μ,ν}(I) interface coefficient from the refined Sub-lemma 2.1 ([docs/audits/2026-05-04-sublemma-2-1-refined.md]).

    abbrev QOU.Hecke.OneDimChar (R A : Type u) [CommRing R] [CommRing A] [Algebra R A] :
    Type u

    A one-dimensional character on H_n(q) is an R-algebra homomorphism with values in R. Equivalently: a ring map φ : A →+* R that is R-linear (preserves the R-action).

    For H_n(q) the 1-dim characters are exactly two — the symmetric character φ_sym(σ_i) = q (corresponding to the trivial irrep of S_n, partition (n)) and the alternating character φ_alt(σ_i) = -q⁻¹ (corresponding to the sign irrep, partition (1^n)). Higher-dim irreps do NOT factor through 1-dim characters in this way.

    The per-generator factorisation below is stated for the commutative (abelianised) algebra A: a 1-dim character lands in the commutative ring R and factors through the abelianisation of H_n(q), so the Finset-indexed (order-free) product over the word factors is well-defined exactly in this regime. This is the algebraic content of "individual scalar factors commute and can be regrouped" — the multi-dim sector (non-commutative A) requires the operator-valued interface coefficient instead.

      The 1-dim per-generator factorisation theorem. Let β = ∏_{k ∈ s} f k be a braid word written as a finite product of crossing operators f k = (cd k).1 • embed (i_idx k) + (cd k).2 • 1. For any 1-dim character φ,

      φ(β) = ∏_{i ∈ s.image i_idx} ∏_{k ∈ s.filter (i_idx · = i)} ((cd k).1 · φ(embed i) + (cd k).2)

      i.e. the per-generator factorisation of the φ-image. This is the universal product formula χ^{1-dim}(β_K) = ∏_i V̂_i^{1-dim} verified numerically at A=1 + A=2 to ~1e-16 in v_hat_to_delta_lambda_bridge.py.

      The proof is three steps:

      1. φ is multiplicative (algebra-hom), so φ(∏_k f k) = ∏_k φ(f k) (AlgHom.toRingHom.map_prod).
      2. Each f k has the linear form c·σ + d·1, so φ(f k) = c · φ(σ) + d (AlgHom.map_add, map_smul, map_one).
      3. Regroup the product by generator index using Finset.prod_fiberwise (Mathlib lemma): for any g : ι → γ with decidable equality on γ, ∏_{a ∈ s} f a = ∏_{b ∈ s.image g} ∏_{a ∈ s.filter (g · = b)} f a.

      The statement here uses the "fiber form" indexed by s.image i_idx so the outer product ranges only over generators that actually appear in the word — matching the content of wedderburn_pair_volumes.atom_per_generator_full_matrices, which iterates only over generators with non-empty crossing chains.
