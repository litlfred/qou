    QOU.Hecke.TwGramDetNonzero — det(twGram) ≠ 0 at every rank #

    Bean qou-kwoa, closing the non-vanishing half of what qou-0y6h was opened for.

    QOU/Hecke/TwGram.lean proves the implication det(twGram) ≠ 0 ⟹ H_n(q) semisimple and records the converse content as the hard, open half:

    "It does not compute the determinant. That is the remaining content of qou-0y6h — Geck–Pfeiffer 7.2.6, det(Gram) = ∏_λ (Schur_λ)^{f^λ} — and it is the hard half."

    The determinant does not have to be computed to know it is nonzero. Semisimplicity of the Hecke algebra is proved independently of any Gram determinant, via the Wedderburn decomposition (QOU.SchurWeyl.heckeWedderburn), and the converse of the Gram criterion — QOU.MathlibExt.regTraceGram_det_ne_zero_of_algEquiv_pi_matrix — turns that into det ≠ 0 directly. The Schur-element product is still needed for the determinant's value; it is not needed for its non-vanishing.

    What is proved #

    • twGram_det_ne_zero — det (twGram K (q ^ 2) n) ≠ 0, for any field of characteristic zero and any q ≠ 0 that is not a root of unity, at every n.
    • twGram_det_ne_zero_rank_one_agrees — an independent cross-check. The only rank whose determinant was ever computed by hand is n = 1 (QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.twGram_det_rank_one : det = (1 + q) ^ 2). Specialised at q ^ 2 that says det = (1 + q ^ 2) ^ 2, which is nonzero exactly when q ^ 4 ≠ 1 — and that is what NotRootOfUnity q supplies. The general theorem and the hand computation therefore agree at the one point where both are available, on the same hypothesis, by two different routes.

    Why the statement is at q ^ 2 #

    Exactly the reason recorded in QOU/SchurWeyl/HeckeCarrierSemisimple.lean: skeinIwahoriEquiv relates the skein algebra at q to the Iwahori algebra — the T_w / twGram carrier — at q ^ 2, and a general field need not contain a square root. Over ℝ, or over any field where the Iwahori parameter is a square, twGram_det_ne_zero_of_sq gives the statement at Q directly.

    Scope #

    This is a magnitude-axis fact and bears nothing on the P≠NP wall, as TwGram.lean also records. What it settles is that the (n+1)! × (n+1)! table of Markov-trace observables tr(L_{T_v T_w}) is nonsingular at every rank — which is the property the Gram criterion was introduced to use.

    source
    theorem QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.twGram_det_ne_zero {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] (q : K) (hq0 : q ≠ 0) (hq2 : SchurWeyl.GenericParameter.NotRootOfUnity q) (n : ℕ) :
    (twGram K (q ^ 2) n).det ≠ 0

    det(twGram) ≠ 0 at every rank, over any characteristic-zero field, for q ≠ 0 not a root of unity.

    Composes two isomorphisms — skeinIwahoriEquiv (Iwahori at q ^ 2 is the skein algebra at q) and heckeWedderburn (the skein algebra is a product of matrix algebras) — and feeds the result to the converse Gram criterion. No determinant is evaluated anywhere in the proof.

    source
    theorem QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.twGram_det_ne_zero_of_sq {K : Type u_1} [Field K] [CharZero K] {Q q : K} (hQ : Q = q ^ 2) (hq0 : q ≠ 0) (hq2 : SchurWeyl.GenericParameter.NotRootOfUnity q) (n : ℕ) :
    (twGram K Q n).det ≠ 0

    The same at an Iwahori parameter Q given as a square. The shape a caller holding Q wants, once it can exhibit a square root — mirroring QOU.SchurWeyl.isSemisimpleRing_iwahoriHecke_of_sq.

    source
    theorem QOU.Hecke.TwBasis.twGram_det_ne_zero_rank_one_agrees {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (q : K) (hq2 : SchurWeyl.GenericParameter.NotRootOfUnity q) :
    (twGram K (q ^ 2) 1).det ≠ 0

    Cross-check at the one rank computed by hand.

    twGram_det_rank_one gives det (twGram K Q 1) = (1 + Q) ^ 2 by direct evaluation of the four Markov-trace entries. At Q = q ^ 2 its non-vanishing is q ^ 4 ≠ 1, which is exactly what NotRootOfUnity q supplies — so this reaches the same conclusion as twGram_det_ne_zero at n = 1 by a completely different route (an explicit 2 × 2 determinant, rather than Wedderburn plus the trace-form radical).

    Kept as a regression: an error in either route would show up as a disagreement here.
