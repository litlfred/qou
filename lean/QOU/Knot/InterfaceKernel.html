    def QOU.Knot.InterfaceKernel.f {R : Type u_1} [CommSemiring R] (A : R) :
    ℕ → R

    The alternating recursion f 0 = 0, f 1 = 1, f (k+2) = A * f (k+1) + f k.

    Over ℤ[q,q⁻¹] with A = q - q⁻¹ its characteristic roots are q and -q⁻¹ -- the two Hecke eigenvalues -- because A^2 + 4 = (q + q⁻¹)^2. That fact is archimedean-flavoured and is deliberately NOT stated here; see discriminant below for the ring-generic version of it.

          theorem QOU.Knot.InterfaceKernel.pow_eq {R : Type u_1} [CommSemiring R] (A T : R) (hT : T ^ 2 = A * T + 1) (k : ℕ) :
          T ^ (k + 1) = f A (k + 1) * T + f A k

          The interface identity. In any commutative semiring, if T satisfies the Hecke relation T^2 = A*T + 1, then every power of T is a linear combination of T and 1 with the recursion's coefficients:

          `T^(k+1) = f (k+1) * T + f k`

          Induction on k in two-step form: the k+2 case rewrites T^(k+2) as T^(k+1) * T, applies the hypothesis twice, and closes by ring against the defining recursion.

          The closed form #

          pow_eq above is Semiring-generic and holds for every k. The closed form needs inverses, so it is stated over a Field with q ≠ 0 -- an §7c-legitimate specialisation (the base-ring table lists Field/GroupWithZero for exactly this), and still not ℝ: no ordering, no Real.sqrt, no archimedean anything.

          Stated multiplied through by q + q⁻¹ so no division appears in the statement and no q + q⁻¹ ≠ 0 hypothesis is needed. Over ℤ[q,q⁻¹] this is the alternating (balanced) q-integer, and the two q-powers appearing are the Hecke eigenvalues q and -q⁻¹.

          theorem QOU.Knot.InterfaceKernel.f_closed_form {K : Type u_2} [Field K] (q : K) (hq : q ≠ 0) (k : ℕ) :
          f (q - q⁻¹) k * (q + q⁻¹) = q ^ k - (-1) ^ k * q⁻¹ ^ k

          The closed form, for every k. With A = q - q⁻¹,

          `f A k * (q + q⁻¹) = q^k - (-1)^k * (q⁻¹)^k`.

          The two terms are the k-th powers of the Hecke eigenvalues q and -q⁻¹, which are exactly the roots of the recursion's characteristic polynomial t^2 - A t - 1 -- A^2 + 4 = (q + q⁻¹)^2 is what makes them come out rational in q rather than involving a genuine square root.
