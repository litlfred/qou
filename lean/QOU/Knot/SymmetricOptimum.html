    QOU.Knot.SymmetricOptimum — why the angle-structure offset vanishes at a symmetric point #

    qbeta_sdp_angle_offset_theta_probe.py measured, for the two corpus anchors, the offset between two canonical points of the generalised angle-structure polytope:

    • x_geom — the complete hyperbolic structure's angles (Rivin's critical point of the volume functional), and
    • x_LP — the Casson–Rivin max-strictness LP optimum.

    It found ‖x_geom − x_LP‖ = 2.4·10⁻¹⁶ and 5.0·10⁻¹⁶ — zero — because 4₁ is two regular ideal tetrahedra (every angle 1/3 in π-units) and the Borromean rings complement is the regular ideal octahedron decomposition (1/4, 1/2). Both anchors sit at the symmetric point.

    That was a numerical observation with a structural explanation offered only in prose. This file proves the explanation, in the generality that makes it reusable: a concave, symmetry-invariant objective on a convex, symmetry-stable feasible set is always maximised at a symmetric point. Two such optimisations therefore cannot be separated by symmetry alone — which is exactly why the offset vanishes, and equally why any offset that does carry information must come from an objective that BREAKS the symmetry (the confinement SDP, whose objective is the braid's Markov-trace data — the probe's named next step).

    Route: orbit averaging. orbitAvg lands in the feasible set by convexity (orbitAvg_mem), is fixed by the group because left translation permutes it (orbitAvg_fixed), and does not decrease the objective by Jensen (le_apply_orbitAvg). Hence exists_symmetric_maximiser, and — when the symmetric point is unique — symmetric_point_optimal.

    Scope. This is the symmetry half of the story and nothing more. It does not prove that the angle-structure polytope's symmetric point is unique for any particular triangulation, nor anything about hyperbolic volume (Mathlib has no ideal-tetrahedron volume theory); those stay numerical. What it does establish is that the vanishing offset is forced whenever the symmetry is present, rather than a coincidence of two knots.

    Archimedean (ℝ): genuinely archimedean — convexity and Jensen over ℝ (§7c).

      x ∈ K → f x ≤ f p

      The symmetric point is optimal when it is unique.

      If p is the only symmetric feasible point, then no feasible point beats it: every x ∈ K averages into a symmetric feasible point, which must be p, and averaging did not decrease f.

      This is the statement behind the vanishing offset. Two different concave invariant objectives on the same symmetric convex feasible set are both maximised at p, so their optima coincide and the offset between them is 0 — no matter what the objectives are. Consequently an informative offset requires an objective that is not invariant.

      x₁ = p ∧ x₂ = p ∧ x₁ = x₂

      The offset vanishes — two invariant objectives share their optimum.

      This is the statement the angle-structure probe needs. Let f₁ and f₂ be any two concave objectives on the same symmetric convex feasible set, each invariant under the symmetry and each with a unique maximiser. Then both maximisers equal the symmetric point p, hence equal each other: the offset between the two optima is zero, whatever the two objectives are.

      Applied to the angle-structure polytope, f₁ is the Casson-Rivin minimum-angle objective and f₂ the volume functional; both are concave and invariant under the triangulation's symmetry, so no offset between x_LP and x_geom can exist — which is what the probe measured as 2.4·10⁻¹⁶ and 5.0·10⁻¹⁶.

      The contrapositive is the useful half going forward: an offset that carries information requires an objective that is not invariant. The confinement SDP's objective is the braid's Markov-trace data, which breaks the symmetry — so that is where a nonzero offset can live.
