        The Gaussian appears. Taylor-expanding Λ about θ_k turns the main-term difference into linear + quadratic, with the cubic remainder paid for by M, any bound on 1/sin² between the two angles.

        u = k' − k is a real number, not a natural: k' may sit on either side of k, and the statement is the same in both directions.

        The quadratic coefficient π·cot θ_k/N is the a of a Gaussian e^{−a u²} once cot θ_k < 0 — which holds throughout (π/2, π), where the saddle lives.

        Absorbing the two non-Gaussian terms #

        abs_log_kashaevProd_ratio_taylor leaves two pieces that are not part of the Gaussian: the variation of log(2N sin θ) and of cot θ across the window. Both are O(u/N) and O(u/N²) respectively, so both vanish on |u| ≲ √N — but they have to be bounded, not waved at, and the bound is a mean-value estimate on each.

        K bounds |cot| and M bounds 1/sin²; they are not independent (cot² ≤ 1/sin², so K ≤ √M always works) but keeping them separate avoids dragging a square root through the arithmetic for no gain.

        The ratio IS a tilted Gaussian. Everything but 2u·log(2 sin θ_k) (the tilt) and (πu²/N)·cot θ_k (the Gaussian) is bounded by terms that vanish on |u| ≲ √N.

        This is the statement the Laplace step needs: g_{k'}/g_k is e^{tilt + quadratic} times 1 + o(1), with the o(1) uniform over the window. Compare KashaevTailBound, where the corresponding statements are one-sided and carry different constants on the two sides — those can prove Θ(√N) and cannot produce c_σ.
