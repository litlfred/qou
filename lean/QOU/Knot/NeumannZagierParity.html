    Neumann–Zagier parity: the deformation potential is even, so there is no u³ rung #

    For a one-cusped hyperbolic manifold under Dehn-surgery deformation with meridian log-holonomy u, Neumann–Zagier prove the longitude log-holonomy v(u) is an ODD function of u; consequently the NZ potential Φ(u) (with v = ∂Φ/∂u up to normalisation) and the complex-volume expansion CVol(u) = Vol(0) − (τ/4)u² + … are EVEN in u.

    -- Ref: [neumann-zagier1985] Volumes of hyperbolic three-manifolds — see folio/schema/references.ts

    Physical consequence (the prune this file certifies): the geometric NZ series contains NO odd-order term — in particular no u³ rung. Any identification of the Q_β bar-odd target r = S·u_ladder³ with a geometric NZ coefficient is therefore structurally excluded (scope-(b), invariant = parity, proved below); the odd object must come from OUTSIDE the classical NZ potential (1-loop torsion / higher-loop / Φ_b phase accumulation), or from the distinction between the ladder variable u_ladder = b·ℏ_q² and the NZ deformation variable u_NZ = log-holonomy (two different u's — the 2026-07-11 #3562 NZ-u³ proposal conflated them and is corrected by this file).

    We carry the potential as a polynomial truncation Φ ∈ R[X] (any finite NZ-series truncation); evenness as a function is Φ.comp (−X) = Φ, which over an infinite integral domain is equivalent to pointwise evenness. The algebra is generic (§7c): [CommRing R], with two-torsion-freeness exactly where the parity cancellation needs it.

    Type u_2

    Neumann–Zagier even-potential data (§3b-cond hypothesis class).

    The single field is NZ85's parity result for the chosen truncation: the potential is even as a function. An instance for a specific manifold is supplied by the NZ theorem (v(u) odd); we do not fabricate one here.

    • potential : Polynomial R

      A finite truncation of the NZ potential series in the deformation variable u.

    • even_potential : potential.comp (-Polynomial.X) = potential

      NZ parity: the potential is even as a function of u.
