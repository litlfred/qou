      h ^ 2 / 2 * ∑ i ∈ Finset.Ico 1 n, phiDeriv ((↑i + 1) * h) ≤ ∑ i ∈ Finset.Ico 1 n, ∫ (u : ℝ) in ↑i * h..(↑i + 1) * h, phi ((↑i + 1) * h) - phi u ∧ ∑ i ∈ Finset.Ico 1 n, ∫ (u : ℝ) in ↑i * h..(↑i + 1) * h, phi ((↑i + 1) * h) - phi u ≤ h ^ 2 / 2 * ∑ i ∈ Finset.Ico 1 n, phiDeriv (↑i * h)

      Step 3 — the Euler–Maclaurin bracket for φ on the interior cells. On the grid x_i = i·h with nh < π, the interior-cell defect sum (i = 1 … n−1, i.e. the cells covering [h, nh]) is bracketed by h²/2 times the two endpoint Riemann sums of φ′:

      (h²/2)·Σ φ′(x_{i+1})  ≤  Σ ∫_{cell_i} (φ(x_{i+1}) − φ(u)) du
                      ≤  (h²/2)·Σ φ′(x_i).

      Both envelopes are (h/2) times a Riemann sum of φ′ over [h, nh], so both carry the first Euler–Maclaurin term (h/2)(φ(nh) − φ(h)) — the ½ the antitone squeeze could not pin. The excluded i = 0 cell needs no derivative: smooth_defect_squeeze already brackets it between h·(φ(h) − log 2) and 0.

      Convergence: from the two envelopes to the Euler–Maclaurin term #

      phi_interior_bracket brackets the interior-cell defect sum S between (h²/2)·R and (h²/2)·L, where

      L = Σ_{i=1}^{n−1} φ′(x_i),      R = Σ_{i=1}^{n−1} φ′(x_{i+1})

      are the left- and right-endpoint Riemann sums of φ′ over [h, nh]. Two telescopes turn that into the sharp statement:

      • the same antitone MVT bracket applied to φ itself gives h·R ≤ φ(nh) − φ(h) ≤ h·L — so φ(nh) − φ(h) is trapped between the very same two sums (phi_riemann_sum_bracket);
      • L − R = φ′(h) − φ′(nh) telescopes exactly (sum_phiDeriv_telescope).

      Combining, S and (h/2)·(φ(nh) − φ(h)) differ by at most (h²/2)·(φ′(h) − φ′(nh)). Since φ′(x) = cot x − 1/x → 0 as x → 0⁺ and φ′(nh) → φ′(rπ) is finite, that error is O(h²), while the identified term is (h/2)·(φ(nh) − φ(h)) — van der Veen's missing ½. No limit is taken here: the statement is a finite, effective inequality valid for every 0 < h and every n ≥ 1 with nh < π.

      | Σ_{i=1}^{n−1} ∫_{cell_i} (φ(x_{i+1}) − φ(u)) du
      −  (h/2)·(φ(nh) − φ(h)) |   ≤   (h²/2)·(φ′(h) − φ′(nh)).
