    The sum-of-squares identity, as pure algebra #

    No trigonometry and no angles: sa, ca, sb, cb are formal stand-ins for sin α, cos α, sin β, cos β, and the third angle's cotangent enters as (sa·sb − ca·cb)/(sa·cb + ca·sb), which is what cot(π − α − β) expands to. Keeping this step trig-free is what makes it a one-line field_simp; ring instead of a fight with sin_add/cos_add under a field_simp.

    source
    theorem QOU.Knot.Rivin.sos_identity (sa ca sb cb u v : ℝ) (hsa : sa ≠ 0) (hsb : sb ≠ 0) (hsg : sa * cb + ca * sb ≠ 0) :
    (ca / sa * u ^ 2 + cb / sb * v ^ 2 + (sa * sb - ca * cb) / (sa * cb + ca * sb) * (-(u + v)) ^ 2) * (sa * sb * (sa * cb + ca * sb)) = (ca * sb * u - sa * cb * v) ^ 2 + (sa * sb) ^ 2 * (u + v) ^ 2

    The identity. Clearing the denominators of

    `cot α·u² + cot β·v² + cot γ·w²`   (with `γ = π − α − β`, `w = −(u+v)`)

    turns it into a sum of two squares. Verified symbolically (sympy, exact) before being stated.

      Rivin concavity for one ideal tetrahedron, assembled #

      Bean qou-mlgp Gap 3, for a single tetrahedron. The two halves above combine: Λ″ = −cot supplies the Hessian, cot_quadratic_pos supplies its sign.

      The statement is concavity along a line in the angle simplex. That is not a weakening — strict concavity on a convex set is strict concavity along every segment — and it is what makes the whole thing one-variable, so Mathlib's strictConcaveOn_of_deriv2_neg' applies directly.

      The mechanism worth naming: the tangent condition u + v + w = 0 is exactly what keeps the three angles summing to π all along the line,

      `(α+tu) + (β+tv) + (γ+tw) = (α+β+γ) + t(u+v+w) = π + 0 = π`,

      so cot_quadratic_pos applies at every point of the line, not just at t = 0.

          RIVIN CONCAVITY FOR ONE IDEAL TETRAHEDRON.

          Λ(α)+Λ(β)+Λ(γ) is strictly concave along every line in the angle simplex — i.e. along every direction (u,v,w) with u+v+w = 0 that is not zero.

          Since strict concavity on a convex set is exactly strict concavity along every segment, this is Rivin's theorem for a single tetrahedron.
