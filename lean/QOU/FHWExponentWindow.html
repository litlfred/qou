    QOU.FHWExponentWindow #

    Two independent selection principles for the kernel exponent agree, and they agree at a single point.

    The question this settles #

    QOU/MobiusMutualChannel.lean §7 proved that Möbius invariance against the arclength density forces the kernel f(d) = f(1)/d² — exponent exactly −2. A fair objection to that result is that it merely re-derives a convention: the inverse square was the modelling choice all along, so a criterion selecting it could be a criterion reverse-engineered to select it.

    The literature answers half of that, and the honest half is smaller than it first looks — see the two 🛑 sections below before citing this file. Bryson–Freedman–He–Wang (Bull. AMS 28 (1993) 99–103, library/arxiv-math-9301212v1/) treat −2 explicitly as a choice among candidates, and pin it only to an interval. In their words:

    "Note that there are competing candidates for the exponent = −2 in the definition of E; for example, the Newtonian potential in ℝ³ has exponent = −1. When the exponent is strictly larger than −3, finite values are obtained for smooth simple loops. Exponents smaller or equal to −2 yield energies which blow up as a simple loop γ begins to acquire a double point, thus creating an infinite energy barrier to a change of topology. Such a barrier would not exist for the Newtonian potential."

    So their two requirements bracket the exponent:

    • finiteness on smooth simple loops needs e > −3;
    • the topological barrier — infinite energy cost to change knot type — needs e ≤ −2.

    Their window is therefore (−3, −2], a continuum. It does not single out −2; the Newtonian −1 is excluded, but −2.5 is not.

    §7's criterion does single it out, and it picks the endpoint. That is the content here: two criteria with nothing to do with each other — one conformal, one topological — intersect in exactly one exponent.

    What is proved, and what is cited #

    PROVED (sorry-free, from §7): mobiusInvariant_rpow_exponent_eq_neg_two. If the power law d ↦ d^e is Möbius invariant against arclength, then e = −2. No window is assumed; this is §7 specialised to power laws and then solved for the exponent.

    PROVED: neg_two_mem_bfhwWindow, so the intersection statement is not vacuous, and bfhw_window_mobius_unique, the intersection itself.

    🛑 What BFHW actually supply, corrected after reading the surrounding page #

    An earlier draft of this file said the two window facts "are analytic results of [bryson-freedman-he-wang1993]". That is wrong on both halves and is corrected here rather than shipped.

    It is not a theorem of theirs. The passage is an unnumbered remark, sitting between the statement of Theorem E and the proof of Theorem A — it carries no proof in that document.

    It is not theirs to prove. The remark defers, verbatim: "We refer to [O1–O3] for detailed discussions." Those are O'Hara — [O1] Energy of a knot (Topology 30, 1991, held at library/1-s20-0040938391900102-main/), [O2] Family of energy functionals of knots (Topology Appl.), [O3] Energy functionals of knots (World Scientific). [O2] and [O3] are where the exponent range lives, and the corpus holds NEITHER — checked by title across every library/*/structure.json. So BFHWWindow records a claim whose proof is in neither this file nor any held document.

    🛑 And the result here is not news to BFHW — they assert it #

    The same remark closes: "Similarity and Möbius invariance are, of course, special to the exponent −2."

    So the statement mobiusInvariant_rpow_exponent_eq_neg_two establishes was regarded as evident in 1993. This file does not discover it. What it does is narrower and still worth having:

    • it proves what they assert without proof, sorry-free, from the invariance hypothesis alone;
    • it proves uniqueness — BFHW say invariance is "special to" −2, which is a statement that −2 has the property; the theorem here is that −2 is the only exponent that does.

    Do not describe this file as showing that the exponent was open, or as settling a question BFHW left open. They left the modelling choice open among candidate knot energies while treating the Möbius characterisation as obvious. Those are different things and the distinction is the honest one.

    NOT CLAIMED: that any of this applies to the mutual channel's magnitude. BFHW's Theorem D (2πc([γ]) + 4 ≤ E(γ)) is about the self energy of a single closed curve; qou-o6ml route 2 owns the pairwise term, and the bound does not transfer to it. What transfers is the exponent question, which is a statement about the kernel and is shared by both channels.

    def QOU.FHWExponentWindow.BFHWWindow (e : ℝ) :
    Prop

    The BFHW admissible window, as cited from Bull. AMS 28 (1993) 99–103.

    e > −3 is their finiteness condition on smooth simple loops; e ≤ −2 is their infinite-energy-barrier condition. This predicate records the two claims so the intersection below can be stated; it does not derive them.

    Equations
    • QOU.FHWExponentWindow.BFHWWindow e = (-3 < e ∧ e ≤ -2)

      This is the load-bearing non-degeneracy check. Without it, "Möbius invariance picks out the endpoint" would be empty — the endpoint might be the only thing there.

      theorem QOU.FHWExponentWindow.mobiusInvariant_rpow_exponent_eq_neg_two {e : ℝ} (h : MobiusMutualChannel.MobiusInvariantKernel fun (d : ℝ) => d ^ e) :
      e = -2

      Möbius invariance forces the exponent to be exactly −2.

      §7 of MobiusMutualChannel gives f d = f 1 / d ^ 2 for any Möbius invariant kernel. Specialising to the power law f d = d ^ e and evaluating at d = 2 gives 2 ^ e = 2 ^ (−2); taking logarithms and cancelling log 2 ≠ 0 yields the exponent.

      Note what is not assumed: no window, no finiteness, no barrier. The conformal symmetry alone determines the exponent.

      theorem QOU.FHWExponentWindow.bfhw_window_mobius_unique {e : ℝ} (_hw : BFHWWindow e) (hm : MobiusMutualChannel.MobiusInvariantKernel fun (d : ℝ) => d ^ e) :
      e = -2

      THE RESULT: the two criteria intersect in exactly one exponent.

      An exponent that is admissible to BFHW and Möbius invariant is −2, and −2 is admissible — so the conformal criterion selects precisely the endpoint of the topological window.

      Two things make this more than a restatement. The window is a genuine interval (bfhwWindow_not_singleton), so the endpoint is not the only candidate on offer. And the two criteria are independent in origin: BFHW's comes from finiteness and an energy barrier to changing knot type, §7's from invariance under inversions. Neither was derived from the other, and the corpus holds the paper that states the first.
