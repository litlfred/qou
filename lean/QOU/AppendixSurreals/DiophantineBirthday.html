    QOU.AppendixSurreals.DiophantineBirthday — conjecture carrier #

    §3b-cond class axiomatisation for content block conj:diophantine-birthday: Algebraic vs transcendental numbers are separated by birthday trajectory.

    Primary conjecture carrier with no instance — the honest open state per AGENTS.md §3b-tax.

    What changed (bean qou-ffwk) #

    The previous field schema was a scalar plus a field asserting that it vanishes, inhabited by ⟨0, rfl⟩. The class chose its own witness, so it asserted nothing and a downstream [DiophantineBirthday] hypothesis was not a meaningful conditional.

    The docstring link was also wrong: it pointed into appendix-surreals/, and the block lives in appendix-surreals-archimedean/. Corrected.

    What is encoded, and what is deliberately left out #

    The block states four parts. Only the first is unhedged, and it is the one that carries the discrimination:

    • Part 1 — algebraic rigidity. "If α is algebraic of degree d, then b_α(n) = O(log n) up to a bounded error." Encoded. The implied constant depends on α (the block ties it to the degree), so it is existentially quantified inside the field rather than being a class parameter.

    • Part 2 — transcendental depth is not encoded, and that is a faithfulness decision rather than an omission. The block says "if α is transcendental (of "generic" type, e.g. Liouville number), b_α(n) grows super-logarithmically". The parenthesis is a hedge: the claim is made for a generic subclass, not for every transcendental. Writing ∀ α, Transcendental ℚ α → … would assert the unhedged converse, which the corpus does not. Inventing an IsGenericTranscendental predicate to carry the hedge would be worse — a stand-in with no definition anywhere.

    • Parts 3 and 4 — the Roth threshold and the continued-fraction correspondence — are statements about why the exponent behaves as it does and about convergent birthdays. They need the continued-fraction expansion, which nothing in this library constructs.

    The discrimination survives anyway. transcendental_of_not_logarithmic derives, from part 1 alone, that a number whose trajectory is not O(log n) is transcendental. That is the block's own title — "algebraic vs transcendental numbers are separated by birthday trajectory" — and it needs only the unhedged half. Encoding part 2 would have added the converse, which is not what the separation requires.

    trajectory is caller-supplied #

    b_α is defined in the block as a minimisation over rational approximations with bounded denominator. Nothing in this library constructs it, and inventing a definition here would assert a construction the corpus does not have. So it enters as a class parameter, exactly as the two archimedean_agreement carriers take their pipelines. When a construction lands it instantiates trajectory and the class statement is unchanged.

    Base ring (§7c) #

    ℝ is required. The conjecture is about Diophantine approximation of real numbers by rationals, and Real.log is the growth scale the block names. This is the archimedean side by subject matter, not by specialisation. Algebraicity is taken over ℚ, matching "algebraic irrational" in the block.

    Where the rest of qou-bday's findings went #

    Bean qou-bday audited this file on 2026-08-10 and recorded three findings. The first — that the class stipulated its own witness and so encoded nothing — is the defect qou-ffwk fixed above; it no longer describes this file, and is kept only as the history note below the class. The other two are about the target rather than the encoding, so they survive the rewrite unchanged:

    • A separation by the birthday of the number is impossible. See QOU/AppendixSurreals/DiophantineBirthdayScope.lean. The SurrealField axiom birthday_ofReal_le_omega puts every real at birthday ≤ ω, and in Conway's surreals every non-dyadic real is born exactly on day ω — so birthday(√2) = ω = birthday(π), and no_birthday_only_separation proves that no predicate on the birthday can distinguish them. This is why the block's word trajectory is load-bearing, and why trajectory above is a parameter rather than something computed from birthday ∘ ofReal.

    • The trajectory target is unobstructed, and one direction is already a theorem. QOU/AppendixSurreals/DiophantineBirthdayTrajectory.lean shows that a trajectory criterion survives the rational-shift invariance its encoding forces (liouvilleWith_rat_shift_invariant, liouvilleWith_separates) where birthday ∘ ofReal did not, and discharges the fast-approximation ⟹ transcendental direction from Mathlib's Liouville.transcendental as liouville_trajectory_direction_settled. So wholly open overstates what is open here.

    What remains open is the surreal content: that the sign-expansion trajectory computes the approximation exponent. Nothing connects gonshor's length-n prefix to the dyadic approximant at denominator 2ⁿ; SignSequence.lean explains why that induction is not available from the abstract axioms.

    class QOU.AppendixSurreals.DiophantineBirthday (trajectory : ℝ → ℕ → ℕ) :
    Prop

    Birthday-trajectory discrimination: algebraic numbers have logarithmic birthday trajectories.

    The unhedged half of conj:diophantine-birthday.

    trajectory α n is the block's b_α(n) — the minimum birthday among best rational approximations to α with denominator at most n. It is the caller's data; this library constructs no such function.

    An instance is the conjecture, for that trajectory. None is provided.

    • algebraic_logarithmic (α : ℝ) : IsAlgebraic ℚ α → ∃ (C : ℝ), 0 < C ∧ ∀ (n : ℕ), 1 ≤ n → ↑(trajectory α n) ≤ C * Real.log ↑n + C

      Part 1 — algebraic rigidity. For algebraic α the trajectory is O(log n). The constant is existential and α-dependent because the block ties it to the degree of α; a single global constant would be a stronger claim than the block makes.

      Transcendental ℚ α

      A super-logarithmic trajectory forces transcendence — the block's title, derived from part 1 alone.

      If α's trajectory outruns every logarithmic bound, α cannot be algebraic. This is the separation the block claims, and it needs only the unhedged half of the conjecture; the hedged converse (part 2) is not required and is not assumed.

      Inhabitation depends on the data — the property the old schema lacked entirely.
