    QOU.BringsSurface.CoveringDegreeRamification — the branched covering S → ℙ¹ #

    cannot be degree 3 with S₃ monodromy at six branch points

    Bean qou-xlw1, epic qou-8lxd. Companion to QOU.Eightfold.ContactDistributionRank, and a correction to a claim made in 2026-09-04-contact-distribution-complex-rank-and-dimension-seven.

    What the corpus says, and what was wrongly concluded from it #

    Three sites give the degree of the branched covering π : S → ℙ¹(ℂ) of Bring's surface, and they disagree:

    site claim
    brings-qou-4tuple.md:15 degree 3, six branch points; and "the S₃ permutation of the three sheets yields the SO(3) holonomy representation"
    brings-is-qou-verification.md:15 degree 4, six branch points, R_tot = 14 (Riemann–Hurwitz)
    Elaboration.lean:151 covering_degree : ℕ := 4

    The audit above concluded "Riemann–Hurwitz (g = 4, R = 14) gives 4, so line 15 of the 4-tuple is the outlier." That is too strong, and this file exists partly to correct it. Riemann–Hurwitz constrains R given d; it does not adjudicate between two different maps. A non-hyperelliptic genus-4 curve has gonality 3 — its canonical model lies on a quadric in ℙ³ and the two rulings give two degree-3 maps to ℙ¹ — so a degree-3 covering genuinely exists and is not refuted by any count.

    What is true is weaker and sharper: R = 14 forces d = 4 (ramification_inj), so the two blocks are consistent only if they describe different maps π. Both write π : S → ℙ¹(ℂ) with six branch points, so the corpus is at best overloading one symbol — the same defect class as the three distinct S³s and the five meanings of bare W.

    The sharp consequence, which is what this file actually proves #

    Take the 4-tuple block's own numbers at face value — degree 3, genus 4, exactly six branch points — and the ramification is completely determined:

    • Riemann–Hurwitz gives R = 2g − 2 + 2d = 12 (ramification, bring_deg_three).
    • A fibre of a degree-3 map contributes at most d − 1 = 2 to R (fibre_ramification_le), so six branch points contribute at most 12.
    • Twelve is exactly the budget, so every branch point is totally ramified — fibre profile (3), monodromy a 3-cycle (six_points_forces_total_ramification).

    And a 3-cycle is even. So the monodromy group is generated by even permutations, hence contains no transposition, hence is not S₃ (generated_by_three_cycles_ne_top, deg_three_six_branch_points_not_S3). It is the cyclic group A₃ ≅ ℤ/3, and the covering is Galois.

    So brings-qou-4tuple.md:15 is internally inconsistent: its stated degree and branch-point count force cyclic ℤ/3 monodromy, which cannot be the "S₃ permutation of the three sheets" the same sentence derives SO(3) holonomy from. That is a genuine finding about the colour leg, and it is not the finding the audit reported.

    What is proved here, and what is not #

    Proved, sorry-free: the Riemann–Hurwitz arithmetic and its consequences — that R determines d, the per-fibre bound, that six branch points at degree 3 force total ramification everywhere, that a 3-cycle in S₃ is even, and that a subgroup generated by 3-cycles is proper.

    Not proved, and asserted nowhere below:

    • that Bring's surface has a degree-3 covering with six branch points. This file takes the block's numbers as hypotheses and derives what they force; it does not assert the configuration is realised.
    • the Riemann–Hurwitz theorem itself. ramification is a definition encoding its statement for a covering of ℙ¹, not a proof of it; Mathlib has no branched-covering API. Everything below is arithmetic and finite group theory downstream of that definition.
    • which of the two degrees the author intends. That is a modelling decision, and both maps exist.
    • anything about SO(3) holonomy. The monodromy result says the group is not S₃; what representation the correct covering carries is untouched.

    Base ring: none — ℕ, ℤ and Equiv.Perm (Fin 3) (§7c: no ℝ).

    def QOU.BringsSurface.CoveringDegreeRamification.ramification (g d : ℕ) :
    ℤ

    Total ramification of a degree-d branched covering of ℙ¹ by a genus-g curve, as forced by Riemann–Hurwitz: 2g − 2 = d·(2·0 − 2) + R, so R = 2g − 2 + 2d.

    This is a definition encoding Riemann–Hurwitz for a ℙ¹ target, not a proof of it — Mathlib has no branched-covering API.

      def QOU.BringsSurface.CoveringDegreeRamification.fibreRamificationBound (d : ℕ) :
      ℕ

      A single fibre of a degree-d map contributes at most d − 1 to the total ramification: the fibre has at most d preimages and Σ(eᵢ − 1) = d − #preimages, maximised when the fibre is a single totally-ramified point.

At genus 4, degree 3 and exactly six branch points, every branch point must be totally ramified — fibre profile (3), monodromy a 3-cycle.
