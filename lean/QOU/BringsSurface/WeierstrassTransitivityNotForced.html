    QOU.BringsSurface.WeierstrassTransitivityNotForced — transitivity alone narrows nothing #

    Bean qou-qhcr. Direct sequel to QOU.BringsSurface.BranchPointSixNotForced, which established that Steps 3–4 of ex:chi-minus-six-derivation pin χ(A) to the non-positive even integers and nothing more, so that Step 5 alone supplies −6 out of a countably infinite set. That file refuted three candidate sources for the 6. This file tests the fourth and, until now, the most promising one.

    The route under test #

    The one hypothesis found in the corpus sweep that narrows χ(A) without naming six is transitivity of the automorphism group on the Weierstrass points:

    Laing & Singerman, Transitivity on Weierstrass points, Ann. Acad. Sci. Fenn. Math. 37 (2012), Theorem 15 — a compact Riemann surface of genus g > 2 with g³ − g Weierstrass points on which Aut acts transitively is Bring's curve (g = 4), or one of two genus-3 possibilities.

    Since χ = 2 − 2g, that reads χ(A) ∈ {−4, −6} — an infinite set cut to two, by a homogeneity hypothesis of the same register as the QOU observability axiom. The question this file was opened to answer is whether a QOU axiom supplies it.

    The finding #

    It does not, and the reason is structural rather than a failure to find the right axiom: the hypothesis that does the narrowing is not the transitivity.

    g³ − g is the total Weierstrass weight at every genus g ≥ 2 (Hurwitz), and each Weierstrass point carries weight ≥ 1. So "g³ − g Weierstrass points" is not a count appearing alongside transitivity — it is the independent hypothesis that every Weierstrass point is simple (weight exactly 1). The cited theorem has two hypotheses, not one, and the held source says so in as many words:

    "Laing and Singerman in [24] searched for all Riemann surfaces of genus g > 2 admitting a transitive action on the Weierstrass points, but under the additional assumption that such points are simple (namely, of weight 1)." — Reyes-Carocca & Speziali, §4 (library/arxiv-2211.08164v2/sections/sec-003-4-simple-weierstrass-points.md)

    Separating the two hypotheses is what settles the route:

    • (H1) transitivity — proved below (weight_constant_of_transitive) to force all Weierstrass weights equal, and nothing further. Its narrowing power on the genus is zero: the Accola–Maclachlan curve realises it at every genus g ≥ 2, and there are infinitely many non-hyperelliptic examples besides. That is the main theorem of the held source, and it is its title.
    • (H2) simplicity — the constant is 1. This is a genericity condition, not a homogeneity one, and inside the very family the classification is about it is anti-correlated with symmetry: the two members of Laing & Singerman's genus-3 family K₃ that fail simplicity are Fermat's quartic and C₃, the two most symmetric members (held source, §4).

    So an axiom supplying homogeneity pushes away from the hypothesis that carries the narrowing. This is recorded as a scope-(b) structural exclusion of the transitivity half (§6) — the invariant is an explicit infinite family in a published, held source — and a scope-(a) negative on the simplicity half.

    What is nevertheless derived here #

    §3 gives a self-contained narrowing that does not use the classification at all: transitivity + simplicity + Hurwitz's |Aut| ≤ 84(g − 1) forces g ≤ 8. This is strictly weaker than the cited g ∈ {3, 4}, and it is ours — it needs neither Laing & Singerman (not held) nor Reyes-Carocca & Speziali. It is the first narrowing of χ(A) in this arc obtained from a finite, cited, classical input rather than read off Bring's curve.

    §5 records a correction to the arc's framing: χ = −2 survives. The classification is stated for g > 2, and genus 2 satisfies (H1) ∧ (H2) unconditionally — every genus-2 Weierstrass point is simple, because the maximum weight g(g−1)/2 is itself 1 there, and the six of them carry a transitive action. Nothing in the chain supplies g > 2: Step 4 gives g ≥ 1 and observability gives χ ≠ 0, i.e. g ≥ 2. So the honest conclusion of the route is χ(A) ∈ {−2, −4, −6}, not {−4, −6}.

    Discipline #

    No sorry, no axiom, no native_decide, no ℝ (§7c: nothing archimedean appears; the only types are ℕ, ℤ and two abstract carriers). Every classical input is a hypothesis of the theorem that consumes it, never an assumed axiom — genus_le_eight takes Hurwitz's bound as an argument, so the file proves an implication and assumes nothing (§"Axiomatization is NOT a Proof").

    Deliberately import-free. Every statement is closed arithmetic on ℕ/ℤ plus one elementary group-action lemma, so decide, rfl and omega from core suffice. The module therefore verifies in a container with no olean tree at all.

    §1. Transitivity forces equal weights — and only that #

    The Weierstrass weight function is an invariant of the surface, so Aut preserves it. If Aut acts transitively on the Weierstrass locus, the weight is therefore constant on it. That is the entire content of (H1); the lemma below is the proof, stated for an arbitrary action on an arbitrary carrier so that nothing about Riemann surfaces is assumed.

    Note what is not concluded: the constant is not shown to be 1. §4 exhibits a datum where it is not.

    source
    theorem QOU.BringsSurface.WeierstrassTransitivityNotForced.weight_constant_of_transitive {P : Type u} {G : Type v} (act : G → P → P) (w : P → Nat) (hinv : ∀ (γ : G) (p : P), w (act γ p) = w p) (htrans : ∀ (p q : P), ∃ (γ : G), act γ p = q) (p q : P) :
    w p = w q

    (H1) ⟹ equal weights. If a group acts transitively on P and w : P → ℕ is invariant under the action, then w is constant.

    Read on a compact Riemann surface of genus ≥ 2: P is the Weierstrass locus, w the Weierstrass weight, the action that of Aut(X). Invariance is automatic (the weight is defined from the gap sequence, which an automorphism carries along), so transitivity alone gives constancy.

    §2. The Weierstrass weight arithmetic #

    totalWeight g = (g−1)·g·(g+1) = g³ − g is the total Weierstrass weight of a compact Riemann surface of genus g ≥ 2 (Hurwitz). Written with the factor g − 1 in front so that no ℕ-subtraction beyond g − 1 ever appears, and so that the Hurwitz bound 84(g − 1) cancels against it in §3.

    Simplicity (H2) is then exactly |W| = totalWeight g: since every weight is ≥ 1 and the weights sum to totalWeight g, the number of Weierstrass points is ≤ totalWeight g, with equality iff every weight is 1.

        §3. The self-contained narrowing: g ≤ 8 #

        This is the one positive result in the file, and it uses no classification.

        Transitivity makes the Weierstrass locus a single Aut-orbit, so |W| divides |Aut(X)| and in particular |W| ≤ |Aut(X)|. Simplicity makes |W| = g³ − g. Hurwitz bounds |Aut(X)| ≤ 84(g − 1). Cancelling the common (g − 1):

        (g − 1)·g·(g + 1) ≤ 84·(g − 1)   ⟹   g(g + 1) ≤ 84   ⟹   g ≤ 8.

        Every classical input is an explicit hypothesis, so the theorem is an implication and assumes nothing.

        source
        theorem QOU.BringsSurface.WeierstrassTransitivityNotForced.genus_le_eight (g npoints autOrder : Nat) (hg : 2 ≤ g) (hsimple : npoints = totalWeight g) (horbit : npoints ≤ autOrder) (hhurwitz : autOrder ≤ 84 * (g - 1)) :
        g ≤ 8

        Transitivity + simplicity + Hurwitz ⟹ g ≤ 8.

        hsimple is (H2) (|W| = g³ − g); horbit is (H1) via orbit–stabiliser (a single orbit has size dividing, hence at most, the group order); hhurwitz is Hurwitz's |Aut(X)| ≤ 84(g − 1), valid for g ≥ 2.

        Derived here rather than cited: it is strictly weaker than Laing & Singerman's g ∈ {3, 4}, and unlike that theorem it depends on no source this corpus does not hold.

        source
        theorem QOU.BringsSurface.WeierstrassTransitivityNotForced.eight_is_sharp_for_this_argument :
        8 * (8 + 1) ≤ 84 ∧ ¬9 * (9 + 1) ≤ 84

        The bound of genus_le_eight is attained by the arithmetic, i.e. the proof is not throwing away a factor: at g = 8 the inequality g(g + 1) ≤ 84 still holds, and at g = 9 it fails. So 8 is the exact cut-off of this argument, not an artefact.

        §4. Transitivity alone: zero narrowing power #

        The Accola–Maclachlan curve of genus g is hyperelliptic with 2g + 2 Weierstrass points, each of the maximal weight g(g−1)/2, and its automorphism group acts transitively on them — at every genus g ≥ 2 (held source, §1, item 1; the infinitude was already noted by Laing & Singerman).

        Weights are carried doubled below (2w = g(g−1)) so that no ℕ-division appears anywhere; simplicity is then the statement 2w = 2.

        The two theorems are: the datum is arithmetically consistent at every genus (so nothing in the Weierstrass arithmetic obstructs transitivity anywhere), and it fails simplicity at every genus ≥ 3 (so it is invisible to the classification while still being transitive). Together: (H1) cuts out no genus whatsoever.

            §5. Genus 2 survives — the route gives {−2, −4, −6}, not {−4, −6} #

            Laing & Singerman's theorem, and its completion by Reyes-Carocca & Speziali (Theorem 2), are both stated for genus > 2. That restriction is a hypothesis, not a conclusion, and genus 2 satisfies (H1) ∧ (H2) outright:

            • the maximum Weierstrass weight is g(g−1)/2 = 1 at g = 2, so every genus-2 Weierstrass point is simple — (H2) holds for every genus-2 surface;
            • there are 2g + 2 = 6 of them, and totalWeight 2 = 6 agrees;
            • the Accola–Maclachlan curve of genus 2 has Aut transitive on them (held source, §1, item 1, which states the family for g ≥ 2).

            Nothing in ex:chi-minus-six-derivation supplies g > 2: Step 4 gives g ≥ 1 and observability gives χ ≠ 0, i.e. g ≥ 2. So χ = −2 is admissible and the route's honest output is a three-element set.

            §6. Scope of the negative, per AGENTS.md §"Before declaring refuted" #

            • Scope (b) — structural exclusion of the transitivity half. Transitivity alone, however it is sourced, cannot narrow χ(A): the Accola–Maclachlan curves realise it at every genus g ≥ 2 (held source, §1 item 1), and the held source's main theorem adds infinitely many non-hyperelliptic examples. The invariant is an explicit infinite family in a published source, and §4 carries its arithmetic. This is not "no axiom was found"; it is "there is nothing here for an axiom to supply".
            • Scope (a) — the simplicity half. No QOU axiom was found that supplies (H2), and none of the surfaces searched carries one. That is an absence of evidence over the surfaces named in bean qou-qhcr, not a proof that no axiom could. What is argued structurally — and it is an argument, not a theorem — is that (H2) is a genericity condition anti-correlated with symmetry inside the classification's own family, so a homogeneity axiom is the wrong shape to supply it.
            • Not excluded. g = 4 may still be right, and the classification is genuine mathematics; §3's g ≤ 8 is a real narrowing. What is excluded is the reading under which transitivity is the hypothesis doing the work.

            §7. What the axioms actually say (recorded, not formalised) #

            The corpus encodes observability twice, and neither encoding reaches a surface automorphism group:

            • QOU.FluidDynamics.QOUFluidUniverse.observability is an opaque Prop field with no content at all.
            • QOU.FluidDynamics.ObservabilityData gives it its only content in the corpus: a single hom_rank : ℕ with observable : hom_rank = 0, i.e. Hom_𝐂(𝟙, Ad(A)) = 0.

            That is a statement about a Hom-space in the monoidal category — about the algebra object A. There is no theorem in the corpus relating it to Aut(M₀) acting on anything, let alone on a Weierstrass locus. Supplying (H1) from it would require building that bridge; and by §6 the bridge would buy nothing, because (H1) narrows nothing. Deliberately left as prose: a non-existence claim about the corpus is not a Lean proposition, and inventing a class to carry it would be the self-assuming projection §3b-cond item 5 forbids.
