    QOU.BringsSurface.BranchPointSixNotForced — the branch-point count 6 is not forced #

    Bean qou-qhcr. Companions: QOU.BringsSurface.EulerCharacteristicSix (the torus-knot route to g = 4), QOU.BringsSurface.CoveringDegreeRamification (the degree/monodromy audit of the same six branch points), QOU.Information.ChiMinusSix (which carries bringGenus = 4 as a class field).

    What this file is about #

    ex:chi-minus-six-derivation (brings-surface/chi-minus-six-chain.md) runs an eight-step chain to χ(A) = −6. Steps 1–4 and 6–8 are axiom consequences. Step 5 — "the Frobenius–Hopf structure forces the algebra to have exactly 6 branch points in its geometric descent (from the S₃ ⊂ S₅ orbits on the 6 Weierstrass points of Bring's surface)" — is the one step that is not, and the block's own opening sentence says so.

    This file does not derive the 6. It measures how far the derived steps get, records what the classical facts actually are, and refutes three candidate sources for the 6 — including the finite-subgroup / ADE route, which was the route this bean was opened to test.

    Everything below is finite arithmetic over ℕ and ℤ, decidable, and stated so that a wrong cone order, a wrong orbit size or a wrong Riemann–Hurwitz constant makes a theorem fail rather than a docstring lie. No sorry, no axiom, no native_decide, no ℝ (§7c: nothing archimedean appears).

    Deliberately import-free. Every statement is closed arithmetic on ℕ/ℤ and finite lists, so decide, rfl and omega from core suffice. Nothing here needs Mathlib, and not importing it keeps the module verifiable in a container that has only a partial olean tree.

    §1. How much of χ(A) = −6 the derived steps actually supply #

    Step 3 is Riemann–Roch, χ = 2 − 2g. Step 4 is obstruction non-triviality, g ≥ 1. Step 6 ("the sign is negative") adds nothing: it is already implied by Step 4. So Steps 1–4 and 6 together constrain χ(A) to the non-positive even integers, and Step 5 alone picks −6 out of that infinite set.

        §2. "Six branch points" over ℙ¹ does not fix the genus #

        The corpus's own six-branch-point datum is a covering of ℙ¹, not of a torus: brings-qou-4tuple.md:15 gives degree 3, brings-is-qou-verification.md:15 gives degree 4 with total ramification R = 14 (see QOU.BringsSurface.CoveringDegreeRamification for the audit of that disagreement).

        For a degree-d branched cover of ℙ¹, χ(S) = 2d − R where R is the total ramification, not the branch-point count. A branch point contributes between 1 and d − 1, so b branch points give b ≤ R ≤ b(d − 1). The branch-point count therefore pins χ only through R, and at the corpus's own (d, b) = (4, 6) it leaves the whole range genus 0 … 6 open.

              §3. Where the real 6 lives, and what Step 5 says instead #

              Step 5 attributes the 6 to "the S₃ ⊂ S₅ orbits on the 6 Weierstrass points of Bring's surface". Both halves of that parenthetical are wrong against the held source.

              Bring's curve is non-hyperelliptic of genus 4, so all its Weierstrass points have weight 1 and there are g³ − g = 60 of them; Aut(B) = S₅ acts transitively on all 60 ([braden-disney-hogg-2022] Prop. 3.3). There is therefore no set of six Weierstrass points for an S₃ to act on.

              What the same source does record, one paragraph later, is 60 = 6 × 10: six Weierstrass points are fixed by each of the ten transpositions in S₅. The carrier of the 6 is an involution, not an S₃, and 6 is a per-involution count, not a total. Under that reading Step 5 becomes a coherent Riemann–Hurwitz statement: the degree-2 quotient by a transposition has six fixed points, so its base has genus 1 and χ(B) = 2·χ(torus) − 6 = −6.

              That reading repairs the citation. It does not make the step a derivation — it still reads the 6 off Bring's curve, which the chain identifies two steps later.

                    §4. The torus-knot route carries no genus information either #

                    QOU.BringsSurface.EulerCharacteristicSix derives g = 4, sorry-free, from two embeddability constraints: T₂,₉ must embed (Seifert rank 4 ≤ g) and T₂,₁₁ must not (rank 5 ≰ g). The arithmetic is correct and the file says outright that "neither step is deep".

                    It is worth saying exactly how shallow, because the form of the constraint is what a reader is likely to trust. The Seifert rank of T₂,ₛ is (s−1)/2, so for every target genus g the pair T₂,₂g₊₁ / T₂,₂g₊₃ brackets it. At g = 4 that pair is (9, 11) — exactly the corpus's choice. The two constraints therefore carry precisely the information "the genus is 4" and nothing more; they are a restatement of the target, not an independent route to it.

                      §5. The ADE route, tested #

                      QOU.SU2EmergenceRankForced.su2_emergence_rank_forced derives, from QOU axiom 5 (the categorical volume constraint Λ²V ≅ 𝟙) plus a dagger condition and det = 1, that the fibre has rank 2 and that G ⊆ SU(2). The finite subgroups of SU(2) are ADE-classified, so a natural question is whether an axiom picks one of them and thereby produces a 6 as a group-theoretic invariant.

                      Three things go wrong, and the third is the informative one.

                      (a) Nothing makes G finite. The conclusion of su2_emergence_rank_forced is ∀ g ∈ G, g ∈ specialUnitaryGroup, and G is a bare Set. It is satisfied by G = SU(2) itself, which is infinite; qou-axioms-physical-reading.md says so in words — axiom 5 is "the condition that makes the reconstructed gauge group SU(2)". The ADE list classifies finite subgroups, so the classification does not apply to the object the axioms deliver. (Not restated as a Lean theorem here: G = SU(2) satisfying g·g† = 1 ∧ det g = 1 is immediate from the definition of specialUnitaryGroup, and formalising "SU(2) is infinite" would add a Mathlib dependency to an otherwise import-free module without adding information.)

                      (b) Nothing picks the ADE type. Of the five families, exactly one is excluded by an axiom — see §5.3 — and four survive.

                      (c) Granting both, the icosahedral case does not produce a 6. The three exceptional orbits of the icosahedral rotation group on ℙ(V) have sizes 30, 20, 12. Six is an exceptional orbit size for the tetrahedral (E₆), octahedral (E₇) and dihedral-6 groups and is not one for the icosahedral (E₈) group. So an orbit-count reading of "branch points on ℙ(V)" points away from the icosahedral case.

                      A Sylow reading does pick E₈ — A₅ has six Sylow-5 subgroups, equivalently |ℙ¹(𝔽₅)| = 6 — but the two readings disagree on the same group in both directions (§5.4). So "6" is not a function of the ADE type; it is a function of the invariant one chooses to read, and nothing in the axioms names that invariant. Choosing the Sylow reading because it returns 6 is selection against the target, which AGENTS.md §"NO FITTING, EXCEPT CAL-1/2/3" forbids.

                                    §5.3 What the observability axiom does prune #

                                    Axiom 3 is Hom_𝐂(𝟙, Ad(A)) = 0. Read literally — the adjoint carries no invariant vector — it excludes exactly the cyclic family: a cyclic rotation group fixes its axis pointwise, and every other finite rotation group fixes no line.

                                    Two cautions. First, qou-axioms-physical-reading.md glosses the axiom as eliminating "gauge transformations that act as the identity on every observable", which is the kernel of Ad, a different condition — under that reading every binary subgroup of SU(2) fails, since {±1} is always in the kernel. The two readings are not interchangeable and this file does not choose between them; it records the literal one, which is what the axiom says. Second, the corpus's own Lean encodes observability as an opaque Prop field (QOU.FluidDynamics.qou_ns_in_qou), so neither reading is currently formalised anywhere.

                                      §5.4 The Sylow reading, and why it does not rescue the route #

                                      A₅ has six Sylow-5 subgroups; equivalently the six points of ℙ¹(𝔽₅), which is where the exceptional embedding A₅ ↪ A₆ comes from. That six does single out the icosahedral case among the exceptional families.

                                      The Sylow counts are not asserted here: Sylow's theorems constrain n_p to divide |G|/p and to be ≡ 1 (mod p), and that arithmetic is decidable. It leaves two candidates in each case, and simplicity of A₅ (resp. the structure of A₄) picks the larger — that last input is cited, not proved.

                                      The point of this subsection is the disagreement: on the icosahedral group the Sylow reading gives 6 and the orbit reading does not; on the tetrahedral group the orbit reading gives 6 and the Sylow reading does not. So 6 is not determined by the ADE type. Both readings are available for every type and nothing in the axioms names one.

                                          §6. The one narrowing that is not a read-off #

                                          Everything above is negative. The one construction found this session that narrows χ(A) without naming the integer 6 is a homogeneity hypothesis plus a classification theorem the corpus already holds.

                                          Laing & Singerman (Ann. Acad. Sci. Fenn. Math. 37 (2012), Thm 15; quoted as Thm 3.4 of [braden-disney-hogg-2022]): a Riemann surface of genus g > 2 with g³ − g Weierstrass points on which Aut acts transitively is Bring's curve (g = 4), Klein's quartic (g = 3), or a genus-3 curve with Aut ≅ S₄.

                                          The hypothesis is "no Weierstrass point is distinguishable from any other, and none is distinguished by weight" — a homogeneity statement, in the same register as the observability axiom, and not a statement about the number 6. It narrows χ(A) from the infinite set of §1 to exactly two values, {−4, −6}.

                                          Separating the two then needs one further input: an automorphism of order 5. By Cauchy's theorem a group has an element of order 5 iff 5 divides its order, and 5 ∣ 120 while 5 ∤ 168 and 5 ∤ 24 — so an order-5 symmetry selects Bring and excludes both genus-3 cases.

                                          This is not a closure and must not be reported as one. It replaces one unsourced input (the integer 6) with two structural ones (transitivity on Weierstrass points; an order-5 automorphism), neither of which is derived from the five QOU axioms here. Its value is that both are group-theoretic statements of a kind an axiom could plausibly supply, whereas "exactly six branch points" is not.
