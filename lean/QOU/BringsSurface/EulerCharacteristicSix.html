    QOU.BringsSurface.EulerCharacteristicSix — the cohomological axioms force χ = −6 #

    χ(A) = −6 is used as a constant across the corpus — MathlibExt.SubstrateRegime ("the A₅ geometry that fixes the genus-4 / χ(A) = -6 obstruction"), AlgebraicSubstrate.collatz_obstruction_link ("Descartes-universe-specific (Bring's surface Σ₄, genus 4)"), FluidDynamics.qou_ns_in_qou (which carries an eulerChar : ℤ field). The derivation of that constant existed only in prose, in prop:cohomological-embedding (iv). This file is the derivation.

    The chain #

    prop:cohomological-embedding (i) says a torus knot T_{r,s} with gcd(r,s) = 1 is representable on the moduli surface Σ_g exactly when its Seifert rank embeds in H¹(Σ_g):

    rank H₁(Seifert(T_{r,s})) = (r−1)(s−1)/2 ≤ g

    Clause (iv) supplies the two cohomological constraints — T_{2,9} must embed and T_{2,11} must not — and asserts they pin g uniquely. They do:

    seifertRank 2 9  = 4  ≤ g      (so 4 ≤ g)
seifertRank 2 11 = 5  ≰ g      (so g < 5)

    giving g = 4 (genus_forced), hence χ(Σ₄) = 2 − 2·4 = −6 (eulerChar_forced). Neither step is deep; the point is that the constant the corpus quotes is now derived from the stated axioms rather than asserted alongside them, so a change to either constraint moves χ visibly.

    The H₃(q) coincidence, stated as exactly that #

    |χ| = 6 and dim_R H₃(q) = 3! = 6 (finrank_H3Carrier). The two numbers are equal, and abs_eulerChar_eq_finrank_H3 records the equality — machine-checked.

    What is not claimed is a reason. Nothing here derives the Hecke dimension from the cohomology, or the reverse; 6 = 2·4 − 2 and 6 = 3! arrive by unrelated routes and are equal. Recording it as a proved numerical identity, with the absence of a mechanism stated, is the honest form: it is a hypothesis worth holding, not a theorem about why. If a mechanism is found, this is the statement it has to reproduce.
