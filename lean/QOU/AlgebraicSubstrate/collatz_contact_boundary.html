        From integer parity to the $[2]_q$-divisor #

        Classical Collatz hinges on a single question: is $n$ even or odd? Parity is residue mod $2$. The number $2$ is special: it is the smallest prime, the only even prime, and the denominator of the halving step $n/2$.

        In the QOU, the integer $2$ lifts to the quantum integer $[2]_q = q + 1$ (combinatorial convention; see q-Collatz conjecture). The $[2]_q$-divisor is the locus where $[2]_q = 0$, i.e. $q = -1$:

        $$ D_{[2]} := \{q : [2]_q = 0\} = \{-1\}. $$

        This is where the $q$-analog of "dividing by $2$" degenerates — where the halving step ceases to exist.

        Kummer obstruction at the divisor. The point $q = -1$ belongs to $\mu_2$ (the 2nd roots of unity). The Kummer obstruction (root-of-unity truncation) at level $n = 2$ asks: does $2$ divide $\chi(A) = -6$? Yes — $6/2 = 3$. The obstruction vanishes. This means the $[2]_q$-divisor is removable: the QOU axioms can resolve the singularity. At $q = -1$, the residue ring is $\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]/(q+1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (setting $q = -1$), and the $[2]_q$-adic valuation specializes to the ordinary $2$-adic valuation.
