            structure QOU.AlgebraicSubstrate.LocalQZetaFunction :
            Type

            Local q-zeta function of Bring's curve.

            Encodes the generating function for point counts over finite extensions of F_q. The characteristic polynomial P(t) has degree 2g = 8, with roots α₁, …, α₈ — the fundamental Frobenius eigenvalues.

            LaTeX: \label{def:local-q-zeta-function} Ref: [manuscript] Chapter 8, Section 4

            • q : ℝ

              The cardinality of the base field, as a real number.

              Corrected 2026-08-28 (bean qou-lsib). This field was q : R_q, i.e. a formal Laurent polynomial over ℤ. That contradicted both its own narrative and every other field of this structure. The .md defines the object as the classical Weil zeta function of Bring's curve over a finite field — it counts |ℬ(𝔽_{q^n})|, its polynomial is the characteristic polynomial of Fr_q, and the Hasse-Weil bound it exists to state is |αᵢ| = √q — while eigenvalues and charPoly were already ℂ-valued. A field size is not a formal parameter, and √q, q^ρ and |α|² ≤ q are not statements about ℤ[q, q⁻¹]: R_q carries no order and no norm, so the two downstream results were not merely unproved but unstatable.

              This is the archimedean side of the wall (§7c): the ordering and rpow the consumers need are exactly the constructs that require ℝ.

            • q_pos : 0 < self.q

              The base field has more than one element, so q > 0.

              Restored 2026-08-28. The pre-migration structure carried this field commented out, annotated -- (archimedean; not meaningful over R_q) -- an author wanting exactly this hypothesis and unable to state it, which is independent evidence for the q : ℝ correction above. Its absence is why hasse_weil_bound referenced a nonexistent Z.q_pos.

            • eigenvalues : Fin 8 → ℂ

              The 8 fundamental Frobenius eigenvalues α₁, …, α₈.

            • eigenvalues_ne_zero (i : Fin 8) : self.eigenvalues i ≠ 0

              No eigenvalue vanishes.

              Frobenius acts invertibly on H¹, with det(Fr_q) = ±q^g ≠ 0, so no root of the characteristic polynomial is zero. It does not follow from charPoly_roots, which permits zero roots, so it is carried as a field.

              Added 2026-08-28. Without it eigenvalue_norm_sq_axiom in hasse-weil-bound.lean is not merely unproved but FALSE: the all-zeros datum satisfies every other field and every hypothesis of that axiom, and its conclusion ‖αᵢ‖² = q then reads 0 = q, contradicting q_pos. Machine-checked before the axiom was replaced by a proof.

            • charPoly : Polynomial ℂ

              The reversed characteristic polynomial P̃(X) = ∏ᵢ (X − αᵢ). Related to the standard form P(t) = ∏ᵢ (1 − αᵢ t) by P(t) = t^{2g} · P̃(1/t), so the roots of P̃ are the eigenvalues αᵢ themselves (not their reciprocals). We use this convention to match Lean's polynomial root factorization.

            • charPoly_degree : self.charPoly.natDegree = 2 * bringsGenus

              P̃ has degree 2g = 8.

            • charPoly_roots : self.charPoly = ∏ i : Fin 8, (Polynomial.X - Polynomial.C (self.eigenvalues i))

              P̃ factors as ∏ᵢ (X − αᵢ).

              Weil numbers: the functional-equation partner is the conjugate #

              Shared by hasse-weil-bound.lean and spectral-half-line.lean. It lives here rather than in either sibling because content-block .lean siblings sit outside the package globs and cannot import one another (§0a) — and both need it.

              source
              theorem QOU.AlgebraicSubstrate.weil_partner_eq_conj (q : ℝ) (α : ℂ) (hα : α ≠ 0) (h : ‖α‖ ^ 2 = q) :
              ↑q / α = (starRingEnd ℂ) α

              The functional-equation partner is the complex conjugate. For a nonzero α with ‖α‖² = q, the partner q/α supplied by the functional equation equals conj α.

              This is what makes |α| = √q a symmetry statement rather than a bare magnitude bound: the eigenvalue multiset is closed under conjugation.

              Non-vacuity of the conjugate-partner theorem #

              weil_partner_eq_conj is only worth stating if it fires. Two witnesses at q = 4: the supersingular real eigenvalue α = 2, where the statement is 4/2 = 2 and the conjugation is invisible; and the genuinely complex α = 2i, where it says 4/(2i) = -2i — the partner and the conjugate coincide only because |α|² = q. The second is the case the arc actually needs, since Frobenius eigenvalues of a curve come in conjugate pairs off the real axis.
