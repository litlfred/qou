    Transporting a pointwise identity across a single orbit #

    A recurring situation: two maps are compared at ONE point of a space carrying a group action, they agree there, and one wants the agreement at every point. When the space is a single orbit and both maps are equivariant, that upgrade is free -- and this file isolates exactly what it costs.

    The content is that commutativity is the whole hypothesis, and it is used exactly once. eq_smul_of_transitive_orbit says: an equivariant φ that agrees at one point with the action of a fixed b agrees with it on the entire orbit of that point. Its proof is mul_comm and nothing else, and exists_not_forall_eq_of_not_central shows the hypothesis cannot be dropped -- for any non-central b in any group there is an equivariant map agreeing with b at a point and nowhere else forced to.

    rel_of_transitive_orbit is the version actually needed when the two maps are only compared UP TO an equivalence (agreement "modulo a gauge", modulo conjugacy, modulo a torus action). It is stated as an Iff, which is the useful shape: it propagates a POSITIVE agreement and a NEGATIVE one equally, so a verdict that varies across an orbit refutes one of its hypotheses rather than saying anything about the maps. Here commutativity is not needed at all -- only that the relation is stable under the action.

    Intended reading #

    X is a zero-dimensional variety whose points form one Galois orbit, G the Galois group, φ a map defined by formulas over the base field (hence equivariant), b a distinguished group element, and r "differ by an element of a gauge group defined over the base field" (hence action-stable). Nothing in this file refers to that situation; it is stated for an arbitrary group action and proved in full generality.

        (∀ (x : X), r (φ x) (ψ x)) ↔ r (φ x₀) (ψ x₀)

        Agreement up to an action-stable relation propagates across an orbit, both ways.

        Stated as an Iff: the relation holds between two equivariant maps at every point of the orbit precisely when it holds at the generating point. So a failure at one point is a failure at all of them, which is what makes a constant verdict down an orbit a theorem rather than a coincidence -- and a verdict that is NOT constant refutes a hypothesis (equivariance of one map, or stability of r) instead of distinguishing the points.

        No commutativity is needed here; r being carried along by the action is enough.

        theorem QOU.Eightfold.GaloisOrbitTransport.exists_not_forall_eq_of_not_central {G : Type u_1} [Group G] {b : G} (hb : ∃ (g : G), g * b ≠ b * g) :

        Sharpness: commutativity in eq_smul_of_transitive_orbit cannot be dropped.

        For any group G acting on itself by left multiplication and any b that fails to commute with something, right multiplication by b is equivariant, agrees with b • · at the identity, and the identity generates -- yet it does not agree with b • · everywhere. So the conclusion genuinely needs G commutative, not merely the orbit hypothesis.
