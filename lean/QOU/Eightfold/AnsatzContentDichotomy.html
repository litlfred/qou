                        The two fixed-size cases, as instances — and they land on the OTHER side #

                        nonetAnsatz_unconstraining above wires NonetMixingUnconstrained. The file's header also names gmo_cannot_discriminate and equal_spacing_cannot_discriminate; this section wires those, and doing so corrects the header's implicit reading of them.

                        The word "discriminate" is used in two different senses and they point opposite ways:

                        • In GellMannOkuboIdentity and DecupletEqualSpacing, "cannot discriminate" means the relation takes the same value for every parameter choice (a,b,c). That is exactly Forces here — the ansatz predicts the relation. It says the relation cannot separate two mechanisms inside the first-order octet class.
                        • Discriminating here means Forces ∧ ¬ Vacuous — the relation is predicted and is not true of every conceivable measurement, so it can refute the class itself.

                        Both hold. GMO and equal spacing are parameter-blind and falsifiable, and the second fact is what the general theorem detects: by not_unconstraining_of_discriminating, neither ansatz is unconstraining. They have content. That is the opposite of the nonet, whose ansatz reaches every observable and therefore predicts nothing at all.

                        So the eightfold chapter carries ansätze on both sides of the dichotomy, and the two failure modes the header describes as "one failure mode" are correctly attributed to the nonet alone. This is consistent with both source files' own prose — each says the testable content is the deviation (~6 % on GMO, ~9.2 % across the decuplet spacings), which presupposes the relation is falsifiable.
