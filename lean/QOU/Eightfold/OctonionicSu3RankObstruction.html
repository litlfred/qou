    The dimension obstruction. A linear map into a space of strictly smaller finite dimension is never injective.

    Applied below with V = su(3) (dimension 8) and W = Im(𝕆) (dimension 7).

    theorem QOU.Eightfold.not_linearIndependent_of_common_line {K : Type u_1} {V : Type u_2} [Field K] [AddCommGroup V] [Module K V] (x : V) (a b : K) :
    ¬LinearIndependent K ![a • x, b • x]

    The rank obstruction. Two vectors that are multiples of a common vector are linearly dependent — with no side condition on the multipliers at all.

    This is the collapse of a rank-2 Cartan onto a single imaginary unit: if I₃ and the hypercharge Y are both sent to multiples of e₃, they cannot be independent, whatever the multipliers are.

    No nonvanishing hypothesis is needed. The obvious relation b·(a • x) − a·(b • x) is nontrivial only when a or b is nonzero, but the degenerate case is not an exception: if both vanish the pair is (0, 0), and a family containing 0 is never independent either. Stating it without the hypothesis is therefore both stronger and closer to the fact being used.

    theorem QOU.Eightfold.no_faithful_generator_identification {K : Type u_1} [DivisionRing K] (f : (Fin 8 → K) →ₗ[K] Fin 7 → K) :
    ¬Function.Injective ⇑f

    The concrete dimension count. su(3) has dimension 8 as a real Lie algebra and Im(𝕆) has dimension 7, so no identification of the eight Gell-Mann generators with the seven imaginary units is faithful.

    Stated over a generic division ring rather than ℝ, per the base-ring convention: nothing archimedean is used, and the count is what does the work. The hypothesis is not decoration — swap the two dimensions and the statement is false, since K⁷ → K⁸ admits plenty of injections.
