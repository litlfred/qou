    When pointwise agreement IS equality: reducedness is the whole hypothesis #

    A standing objection to reading a finite check as an identity of maps runs: two distinct maps can agree on a finite set. That is true in general, and it is the right objection to raise. This file isolates exactly what has to be measured for it to fail, so that a claim of the form "these two maps agree" can be audited against hypotheses rather than against an agent's confidence.

    Two conditions do it, and both are facts about the target, not about the two maps:

    1. the points tested exhaust the space -- there is no unexamined point left for the maps to differ at;
    2. the coordinate ring is REDUCED.

    Condition 2 is the one that is easy to lose. Pointwise agreement says each difference f b - g b lies in every prime, i.e. in the nilradical; that is 0 precisely when the ring is reduced. In a non-reduced ring a nonzero nilpotent lies in every prime and is therefore invisible to every point, so two genuinely different maps agree at all of them.

    eq_of_apply_sub_mem_all_primes is the positive direction. exists_ne_of_not_isReduced is its sharpness: drop reducedness and the conclusion fails, with twoNilpotentZModFour exhibiting a concrete ring where it does -- (2 : ZMod 4) is a nonzero element lying in every prime. The pair mirrors the shape of GaloisOrbitTransport.lean, where a transport theorem is stated next to the theorem saying its hypothesis cannot be dropped.

    What Lean supplies and what it does not. The hypothesis here is "the difference lies in every prime". Bridging a finite list of tested points to that hypothesis is a MEASUREMENT, not a theorem: it needs the points to exhaust the spectrum. Nothing in this file establishes that for any particular variety, and a caller who has checked finitely many points has not discharged it until the exhaustion is measured separately.

    theorem QOU.Eightfold.PointwiseAgreementOnReduced.eq_of_sub_mem_all_primes {A : Type u_1} [CommRing A] [IsReduced A] {x y : A} (h : ∀ (p : Ideal A), p.IsPrime → x - y ∈ p) :
    x = y

    In a reduced ring, an element lying in every prime ideal is zero -- so two elements agreeing modulo every prime are equal. This is the algebraic content of "pointwise agreement is equality".

    theorem QOU.Eightfold.PointwiseAgreementOnReduced.eq_of_apply_sub_mem_all_primes {A : Type u_1} [CommRing A] [IsReduced A] {B : Type u_2} {f g : B → A} (h : ∀ (b : B) (p : Ideal A), p.IsPrime → f b - g b ∈ p) :
    f = g

    Pointwise agreement is equality, for a reduced target. Two maps into a reduced ring whose values agree modulo every prime are equal as maps. This is the shape the objection takes: the conclusion is an identity of maps, not a list of coincidences.

    theorem QOU.Eightfold.PointwiseAgreementOnReduced.exists_ne_of_not_isReduced {A : Type u_1} [CommRing A] (h : ¬IsReduced A) :
    ∃ (x : A) (y : A), x ≠ y ∧ ∀ (p : Ideal A), p.IsPrime → x - y ∈ p

    Sharpness: reducedness cannot be dropped. In any non-reduced ring there are two DISTINCT elements agreeing modulo every prime -- invisible to every point of the spectrum. So a pointwise check, however exhaustive, does not by itself give equality.

    theorem QOU.Eightfold.PointwiseAgreementOnReduced.twoNilpotentZModFour :
    IsNilpotent 2 ∧ 2 ≠ 0

    A concrete non-reduced witness, so the sharpness theorem is not vacuous: (2 : ZMod 4) is nonzero and nilpotent, hence lies in every prime ideal while differing from 0.
