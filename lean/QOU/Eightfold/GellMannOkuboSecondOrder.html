    QOU.Eightfold.GellMannOkuboSecondOrder — what a second-order term can and cannot carry #

    Bean qou-bhfg. Sequel to QOU.Eightfold.GellMannOkuboIdentity, which proves Gell-Mann–Okubo vanishes identically in the first-order octet ansatz and so has zero discriminating power.

    The question this answers #

    If GMO is content-free at first order, the testable content is its deviation (≈ 6 %), and likewise the decuplet equal-spacing deviation (≈ 9.2 %). The framework derives no second-order term, so it currently predicts 0 for both. Before anyone fits a term to those numbers, it is worth knowing which second-order structures can carry them at all — a formalize-first prune (AGENTS.md §"Formalize-first probe discipline").

    The ansatz #

    First order is m = a + b·Y + c·d with d = I(I+1) − Y²/4. At second order the three structures quadratic in the breaking are Y², d² and Y·d, with coefficients e, f, g. Everything is scaled by 16 and written through fourD = 4·I(I+1) − Y² so that no denominator ever appears; the scaling is uniform and cancels from every statement below.

    What is proved #

    declaration content
    gmo_second_order the GMO combination is 16·(4e − 3f)
    gmo_blind_to_Yd GMO cannot see g at all — the Y·d structure is odd in Y and dies in the hypercharge-averaged kaon
    decuplet_second_differences_equal the two decuplet second differences stay exactly equal
    decuplet_second_order each is 8·(4e + 9f + 6g)
    gmo_zero_decuplet_nonzero / decuplet_zero_gmo_nonzero the two constraints are independent — witnesses both ways
    meson_octet_g_absorbed / decuplet_g_absorbed g is not an independent parameter on either multiplet — absorbed exactly by shifting b (and e)
    meson_octet_second_order_surjective any three meson-octet masses are attainable, so second-order GMO is vacuous
    baryon_octet_second_order_surjective any four baryon-octet masses are attainable — the baryon case is vacuous too

    What it means, stated carefully #

    Two consequences, and neither is a derivation of anything:

    1. Y·d is invisible to GMO. Not "small" — exactly zero, by parity in Y. A GMO-deviation fit therefore cannot constrain g even in principle.

    2. The octet has nothing left to predict. GMO constrains 4e − 3f; the decuplet constrains 4e + 9f + 6g; those are not proportional, so the two deviations are independent. My first reading of that was "two equations, three unknowns — find a third constraint on the coefficients." That was wrong, and the Absorption and Surjectivity sections below say why. g is not a free direction on either multiplet: it is absorbed exactly by shifting b (octet) or b and e (decuplet), because in each multiplet the isospin is a function of the hypercharge. So the residual freedom is a reparametrisation, and no categorical argument forcing g = 0 would buy anything — it would be a statement about a coordinate.

      The real obstruction is stronger and sits one level up: at second order the even-in-Y structures already span all functions on the three independent meson-octet masses. Any (m_π, m_η, m_K) whatsoever is attainable, so the GMO combination takes every value and there is no relation left to deviate from. Matching the observed deviation is not a weak prediction — it is not a prediction at all. The same holds for the baryon octet (BaryonOctet section below), which I first got backwards; the decuplet is the only one of the three that keeps a relation. What is needed is not another multiplet from this list but a derivation of e, f, g — see the closing section.

    The equal-spacing result is worth stating precisely, because it is easy to over-read. At second order the decuplet is not equally spaced — each second difference is 8·(4e + 9f + 6g) ≠ 0 in general. What survives is that the two second differences remain equal to each other: the failure is uniform, so the decuplet masses go from linear to exactly quadratic in the ladder position, with no cubic residue. That is a real constraint on any second-order account, and it is testable against the observed spacings independently of their common size.

    Scope. This is a statement about the standard (I, Y) octet/decuplet mass ansatz, consumed as given — the same ansatz GellMannOkuboIdentity uses. It derives no value for e, f, g, predicts neither deviation, and says nothing about whether QOU produces such a term. Coleman–Glashow is deliberately not included: it is an electromagnetic/isospin-breaking relation, not an SU(3)-breaking one on this ansatz, and folding it in here would be the overloaded-symbol error this branch has already had to correct once.

    -- Ref: [okubo1962] (the octet mass formula and its SU(3)-breaking expansion).

                            The Y·d structure is not an independent parameter — on either multiplet #

                            The independence witnesses above show GMO and the decuplet constrain different combinations, and I first read that as "two equations, three unknowns, so a third constraint on the coefficients would close it." That reading is wrong, and the two theorems in this section are why.

                            g is not a free direction on the octet or on the decuplet. It is absorbed exactly — no residue, not merely to leading order — by shifting the lower-order coefficients, because in each multiplet the isospin is pinned by the hypercharge:

                            • Octet. Hypercharge ±1 forces I = 1/2, hence fourD = 2; and the two Y = 0 states kill the g term outright. So Y·fourD = 2Y on every octet state, and g is a disguised b.
                            • Decuplet. I = 1 + Y/2 along the whole ladder, hence fourD = 8 + 6Y identically; so Y·fourD = 8Y + 6Y², and g is a disguised mixture of b and e.

                            So looking for a categorical argument that forces g = 0 would be proving something about a coordinate, not about the physics: g can be set to zero by reparametrisation, on both multiplets, losing nothing.

                            At second order the meson octet is unconstrained — GMO has nothing left #

                            The sharper statement, and the one that prunes the search. It is not that the second-order ansatz predicts the GMO deviation badly, or predicts it only up to a free parameter. It is that the ansatz can produce any three meson-octet masses whatsoever, so there is no relation among them left to deviate from.

                            Three physical masses are available — π, η, and the charge-conjugation- degenerate kaon — and the even-in-Y structures 1, Y², fourD² already span all functions on those three states. The witness below sets b = c = g = 0, so it does not even need the odd sector.

                            Consequence, stated as a scope-(b) exclusion with its invariant named (the invariant is the rank count: three independent structures on three independent masses): no second-order term in (I, Y) on this ansatz can carry predictive content about the meson octet. Fitting one to the observed deviation is therefore not a weak prediction — it is not a prediction at all. This is a statement about this ansatz, not about SU(3) breaking in general.

                            source
                            theorem QOU.Eightfold.GellMannOkuboSecondOrder.meson_octet_second_order_surjective {R : Type u_2} [Field R] [CharZero R] (p h k : R) :
                            ∃ (a : R) (e : R) (f : R), pion a 0 0 e f 0 = p ∧ eta a 0 0 e f 0 = h ∧ kaonPlus a 0 0 e f 0 = k ∧ kaonMinus a 0 0 e f 0 = k

                            Any three meson-octet masses are attainable at second order. Given targets p, h, k for pion, eta and kaon, explicit coefficients reproduce all three — and the kaon doublet stays degenerate, since the witness has b = g = 0.

                            Hence the Gell-Mann–Okubo relation, which is an identity at first order (GellMannOkuboIdentity), is vacuous at second order: its combination can take any value at all, so matching the observed ≈ 6 % deviation constrains nothing.

                            What survives on the decuplet, and what would actually close this #

                            The decuplet is not surjective in the same way, and that asymmetry is the one piece of testable content left. Because fourD = 8 + 6Y along the ladder, every structure in the ansatz — Y, d, Y², d², Y·d alike — restricts to a polynomial in Y alone, of degree at most two. Four states, a three- dimensional space of quadratics: exactly one relation survives, and it is decuplet_second_differences_equal above.

                            That also bounds what refining the structure list can buy: adding cubic structures would raise the degree to three and destroy the last relation rather than create a new one.

                            I originally continued this paragraph with "what is needed is a third multiplet in which isospin is not a function of hypercharge — the baryon octet is such a multiplet." That was wrong; the BaryonOctet section below retracts it and proves the baryon octet surjective as well. The corrected prescription is there, and it is a derivation of the coefficients rather than another multiplet.

                            The baryon octet — and the correction it forces #

                            I claimed, in the section above and in the PR that carried it, that the baryon octet is the multiplet where the relation survives, "which is why baryon GMO is a genuine relation where the meson one has become vacuous." That is wrong, and this section is the retraction.

                            The reasoning I gave was that Λ and Σ share Y = 0 with different I, so d is not a function of Y on the baryon octet. The premise is true. The inference is backwards: a two-dimensional (I, Y) pattern makes the space of available structures larger, and a larger structure space makes surjectivity more likely, not less. Rank counting settles it —

                            multiplet independent masses rank of the second-order structure space relations
                            meson octet 3 (K⁺ = K⁻ by charge conjugation) 3 0
                            baryon octet 4 4 0
                            decuplet 4 3 (I = 1 + Y/2 ⇒ everything is a quadratic in Y) 1

                            So both octets go vacuous at second order and the decuplet's equal-second- differences rule is the only SU(3) mass relation among the three that survives. The general criterion is the last column: a multiplet retains a relation exactly when its number of independent masses exceeds the rank of the structure space on it, and the octets fail that test because their (I, Y) patterns are generic, not because they are degenerate.

                            What this does to "what would close it." Not a third multiplet from this list — that was the wrong prescription and it followed from the wrong premise. Two routes remain, and the second is the framework-native one:

                            1. A multiplet with more independent masses than the six available structures — anything beyond six distinct (I, Y) values retains relations at second order regardless of its pattern. Nothing in this corpus supplies one.
                            2. Deriving e, f, g from the categorical side rather than measuring them. The rank counting says measurement can never make the octet masses predictions, at any order: a structure space that spans everything absorbs any spectrum. Only theory input can. That is the honest reading of what front 4 established.
