    QOU.Eightfold.Sym2GateComplexType — the parity invariant is a SELF-DUAL theorem #

    Companion to QOU.Eightfold.Sym2GateMultiplicity, whose §"Why the count has no cross terms" states the boundary this file proves:

    Over a group with a non-self-dual irrep the cross terms would have to be carried, and the parity conclusion would not follow.

    That is a prose caveat in a file whose whole point is that prose caveats about scope should be theorems. Here it is one.

    Why it matters for the chapter #

    Sym2GateMultiplicity.dimOfMult_ne_three_of_gate proves that no three-dimensional object of the single-fibre SU(2) category passes the Sym² gate. It is easy to over-read that as "the gate excludes dimension 3" — and the eightfold chapter needs a three-dimensional object, so the difference is the whole question.

    It does not. The proof runs through even_dimOfMult_of_gate, and that runs through self-duality: every SU(2) irrep satisfies V^* ≅ V, so the cross terms V_i ⊗ V_j (i ≠ j) contribute no singlet, the count reduces to the two self-pairing terms, and the survivors are forced to be distinct even-dimensional irreps. Drop self-duality and the argument has no purchase: a complex-type irrep (FS = 0, X ≇ X̄) pairs to a singlet only against its conjugate, so a lone X — conjugate absent from the object — passes the gate at any dimension, odd included.

    complex_three_passes_gate below is that object at dimension exactly 3.

    So the SU(3) fundamental is not an exception the gate tolerates; it lives in a sector the parity theorem never covered. This is the same distinction AGENTS.md §"Before declaring 'refuted'" draws between a scope-(a) and a scope-(b) claim, applied to the corpus's own invariant: even_dimOfMult_of_gate is scope-(b) within the self-dual sector and says nothing outside it.

    What is proved #

    • crossSingletCount_eq_zero_iff — the complex-sector gate: the cross terms vanish exactly when no irrep appears together with its conjugate.
    • ComplexTypeOn / complexType_gate_iff_no_conjugate_pair — the hypothesis that makes the count a gate, carried explicitly, and the gate's reading under it: never an irrep together with its conjugate.
    • conjugate_pair_fails_gate — the failure mode of the complex sector, and witness_fails_once_conjugate_present, the same object failing once the conjugate is added.
    • complex_three_passes_gate + complex_three_dim — the witness: an object of odd dimension 3 that passes.
    • parity_invariant_fails_in_complex_sector — the two combined, as the scope statement: Sym2GateMultiplicity.even_dimOfMult_of_gate has no analogue here.

    Scope — what this does NOT do #

    It does not exhibit the SU(3) fundamental, does not formalise SU(3), and does not prove Sym²(𝟑) = 𝟔 is irreducible. Mathlib has no SU(3) representation type and that remains the gap recorded in qou-8fl1 (T2-hard).

    What it settles is the shape of the question, which was the thing at risk of being got wrong: whether the corpus's parity exclusion is evidence against a three-dimensional flavour fundamental. It is not — the exclusion is a theorem about self-dual fibres, and it is silent, not negative, on the complex-type sector. A reader who took dimOfMult_ne_three_of_gate as a general bar on dimension 3 would be drawing a scope-(b) conclusion from a scope-(a) result.

    The model here is the standard cross-term count, cited not derived, exactly as in the companion file: dim Hom(1, V_i ⊗ V_j) = [V_j ≅ V_i^*].

    Base ring (§7c) #

    Pure combinatorics over ℕ. No ring, no ordering, no archimedean content.

      def QOU.Eightfold.Sym2GateComplexType.crossSingletCount (S : Finset ℕ) (m dual : ℕ → ℕ) :
      ℕ

      The cross-term singlet count on the complex sector. V_i ⊗ V_j contains a singlet exactly when V_j ≅ V_i^*, so the V_i isotypic component pairs against the V_{dual i} one to contribute m_i · m_{dual i}.

      Summed over i ∈ S this counts each conjugate pair from both ends, so it is a count of ordered singlet-producing pairings — twice the unordered number. Only its vanishing is used below, and that is unaffected; the factor is recorded rather than quietly divided out.

      Scope, and it is not cosmetic. This is the complete singlet count only when every occurring irrep is of complex type (ComplexTypeOn below): then Sym²(V_i) carries no singlet of its own and the cross terms are all there is. On a self-dual irrep it is simply the wrong quantity — the true contribution is C(m+1, 2) (orthogonal) or C(m, 2) (symplectic), per Sym2GateMultiplicity.sym2SingletCount, and the symplectic case differs from m² exactly where it matters: at m = 1 the binomial vanishes and m² does not. Reading m_i · m_{dual i} as a singlet count at dual i = i would contradict Sym2GateMultiplicity.fundamental_passes_gate, in which the self-dual 2-dimensional fibre is present and passes. Hence the ComplexTypeOn hypothesis is carried explicitly wherever the count is read as a gate.

          On the complex sector the gate has a conjugate-avoidance reading: it passes exactly when the object never contains an irrep together with its conjugate.

          The hypothesis is not used in the proof, and that is the point. The equivalence is pure arithmetic — it holds for any dual whatever, which is why it is discharged by crossSingletCount_eq_zero_iff alone. What ComplexTypeOn buys is not the equivalence but the right to call the left-hand side a singlet count: off the complex sector crossSingletCount is not the singlet count at all (see the scope paragraph on the definition), so the equivalence would be a true statement about the wrong quantity. Carrying the hypothesis forces call sites to declare the sector rather than inherit the reading silently. Named accordingly — the interpretation, not the arithmetic, is what is conditional.
