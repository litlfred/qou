    QOU.Eightfold.FibreFunctorDimensionNotCategorical — a fibre functor changes #

    dimensions, not fusion rules

    Bean qou-yjwg (front 2 of PR #6458), epic qou-8lxd.

    The mathematical situation this formalises #

    The single-fibre category of a quantum observable universe is the tensor category generated by one object V with Λ²V ≅ 𝟙; the corpus identifies it with Rep(SL_q(2)) at generic q (def:quantum-gauge-group, ex:tannaka-krein-su2). In that category the Clebsch–Gordan rule is

    V ⊗ V_j ≅ V_{j+1/2} ⊕ V_{j−1/2} (and V ⊗ V_0 ≅ V_{1/2}),

    so the simple summands of V^{⊗k} are the V_j with 2j ≡ k (mod 2).

    A fibre functor τ' : C → R-Mod is monoidal and exact, so it carries this decomposition to a decomposition of τ'(V)^{⊗k} and the dimensions d_j := dim τ'(V_j) satisfy the same recursion with d_{1/2} = n := dim τ'(V):

    d_0 = 1, d_{1/2} = n, d_{(k+2)/2} = n · d_{(k+1)/2} − d_{k/2}.

    The eightfold way's baryon decomposition 𝟑 ⊗ 𝟑 ⊗ 𝟑 = 𝟏𝟎 ⊕ 𝟖 ⊕ 𝟖 ⊕ 𝟏 contains a one-dimensional summand (the totally antisymmetric channel). This file proves that no fibre functor of the single-fibre category, of any dimension n ≥ 2, produces a one-dimensional summand of V^{⊗3}: by parity the spin-0 object does not occur in V^{⊗3} at all, and every other summand has dimension at least n. Dimension is a property of τ'; the fusion rules are a property of C, and only the latter carries the eightfold way.

    What is and is not formalised #

    • Formalised: the Clebsch–Gordan spin bookkeeping (cgSpins), its parity invariant, the dimension recursion (dimSeq) and its consequences.
    • Cited, not formalised: that the single-fibre category has the Rep(SL_q(2)) fusion rules at the substrate q₀ (generic, not a root of unity), and that a monoidal exact fibre functor preserves the decomposition. These are the hypotheses under which the theorems below say anything about QOU; the theorems themselves are unconditional combinatorics.
    • Cited, not formalised: the existence of fibre functors of every dimension n ≥ 2 on Rep(SL_q(2)) (Bichon 2003, B(E) for E ∈ GL_n with tr(E⁻¹Eᵗ) = −q − q⁻¹). Their existence is what makes the theorem non-vacuous: a 3-dimensional fibre functor does exist, and it still gives V^{⊗3} ≅ 21 ⊕ 3 ⊕ 3, never 10 ⊕ 8 ⊕ 8 ⊕ 1.

    Base ring: everything here is integer combinatorics (§7c: no ℝ).

    def QOU.Eightfold.FibreFunctorDimension.cgSpins :
    ℕ → Multiset ℕ

    Twice-spins 2j of the simple summands of V^{⊗k} in Rep(SL_q(2)), with multiplicity, by the Clebsch–Gordan rule V ⊗ V_j ≅ V_{j+1/2} ⊕ V_{j−1/2} (V ⊗ V_0 ≅ V_{1/2}).

        s ∈ cgSpins 3 → dimSeq n s ≠ 1

        The theorem. Under a fibre functor of any dimension n ≥ 2, no summand of V^{⊗3} is one-dimensional. The eightfold decomposition 𝟏𝟎 ⊕ 𝟖 ⊕ 𝟖 ⊕ 𝟏 therefore cannot be the image of V^{⊗3} under any fibre functor of the single-fibre category: changing τ' changes n, and only n. The obstruction is the fusion rule (the parity invariant above), which is a property of the category.
