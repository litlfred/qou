    An intertwining verified on generators holds everywhere #

    When two representations are compared up to conjugacy, one is tempted to verify the comparison by SAMPLING: evaluate both sides on many group elements and see whether they agree. A sample can only ever corroborate, and enlarging it does not change that -- so the question becomes how large a sample suffices, and one starts looking for a bound on word length.

    That is the wrong instrument, and this file is why. Conjugation by a fixed element is itself a homomorphism, so x ↦ A · ρ₁ x · A⁻¹ and ρ₂ are two homomorphisms; two homomorphisms agreeing on a GENERATING SET are equal. The verification is therefore finite at word length one, and the conclusion is equality on the whole group rather than agreement on a sample. No character theory, no irreducibility, no trace algebra and no word-length bound enters.

    twist_trivial_of_mem is the companion step: a twist by a homomorphism landing in a normal subgroup N is invisible in H ⧸ N. Applied with N the scalars of a matrix group, a scalar-valued twist disappears in the projective quotient, which is what lets an intertwining "up to a scalar character" be an honest isomorphism projectively.

    Both are stated for an arbitrary group; nothing here refers to matrices, fields or representations.
