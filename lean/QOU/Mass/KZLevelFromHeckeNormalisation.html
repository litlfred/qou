    The level is fixed by the eigenvalue ratio — an exact, convention-free statement #

    Bean qou-d0qq. A half-twist's monodromy under the 𝔰𝔩₂ Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov connection on V ⊗ V (V the fundamental representation) is P · exp(iπħ Ω) with P the swap and Ω the split Casimir, whose eigenvalues are 1/4 on Sym² V and −3/4 on Λ² V; so the monodromy has eigenvalues λ₁ = e^{iπħ/4} on Sym² and λ₂ = −e^{−3iπħ/4} on Λ², with ratio λ₁/λ₂ = −e^{iπħ} and product λ₁λ₂ = −e^{−iπħ/2}. The framework's Hecke normalisation (σ − q)(σ + q⁻¹) = 0 has eigenvalues q, −q⁻¹: product −1, ratio −q². A scalar rescaling of the generator changes the product but never the ratio, so the ratio is the invariant that matches the two — and it gives q² = e^{iπħ}.

    That is the whole "which level" question on the ħ-vs-k axis: whichever way a book writes ħ in terms of k (1/(k+2), 2/(k+2), …), the relation e^{iπħ} = q², i.e. ħ = (2/iπ)·ln q, is invariant under rescaling the generator and under that bookkeeping. For the framework's real q₀ > 1 this makes ħ purely imaginary — the precise content of "imaginary level". The corpus's earlier form, "KD matching forces ħ = 2 ln q" (qbeta_fibre_unit_circle_opens_route5_probe.py), is the same relation with iπħ renamed ħ.

    Scope (corrected 2026-09-07 after the qou-99qc review on PR #7088). The ratio −q² is read off the §7a Skein normalisation (H) L₊ − L₋ = (q − q⁻¹)L₀, which AGENTS.md makes STRICT; the relation is conditional on that normalisation, not normalisation-free. The corpus also carries the normalisation (J) q[L₊] − q⁻¹[L₋] = (q^{1/2} − q^{−1/2})[L₀] (bean qou-8bxv, 8 content blocks), which is not a rescaling of (H): its generator eigenvalues are {q^{−1/2}, −q^{−3/2}}, ratio −q, so under (J) the same matching gives e^{iπħ} = q and ħ halves. ratio_invariant covers rescaling and ħ-vs-k bookkeeping; it does not cover a change of Skein normalisation.

    Level bookkeeping, corrected. With ħ = (2/iπ)·ln q₀ (|ħ| = 0.0664 at the proton pin): ħ = 1/(k+2) gives k+2 = iπ/(2 ln q₀) ≈ 15.05 i; ħ = 2/(k+2) gives k+2 = iπ/ln q₀ ≈ 30.1 i (the convention q = e^{iπ/(k+2)}); the bean's other candidate k+2 = 2πi/ln q₀ ≈ 60.2 i is the convention q = e^{2πi/(k+2)}, i.e. ħ = 4/(k+2). An earlier version of this paragraph mapped 60.2 i and 30.1 i onto ħ = 2/(k+2) and 1/(k+2), which is off by a factor of two; the probe kz_pullback_monodromy_exact_probe.py computed the correct values (15.05 i, 30.1 i) all along.

    This file proves the algebraic core over a generic field: if a pair of eigenvalues has product −1 and ratio −q², it is {q, −q⁻¹} up to a global sign, and the ratio is invariant under rescaling. The exponential bookkeeping (e^{iπħ/4}, e^{−3iπħ/4}) lives in the probe computations/probes/kz_pullback_monodromy_exact_probe.py, which evaluates it at the proton pin.

    theorem QOU.Mass.KZLevelFromHeckeNormalisation.ratio_invariant {K : Type u_1} [Field K] (c a b : K) (hc : c ≠ 0) (hb : b ≠ 0) :
    c * a / (c * b) = a / b

    Rescaling both eigenvalues by the same non-zero scalar leaves their ratio unchanged.

    theorem QOU.Mass.KZLevelFromHeckeNormalisation.eigen_pair_of_product_ratio {K : Type u_1} [Field K] {a b q : K} (hq : q ≠ 0) (hb : b ≠ 0) (hprod : a * b = -1) (hratio : a / b = -q ^ 2) :
    a = q ∧ b = -q⁻¹ ∨ a = -q ∧ b = q⁻¹

    The two signed solutions: (a, b) = (q, −q⁻¹) or (−q, q⁻¹).
