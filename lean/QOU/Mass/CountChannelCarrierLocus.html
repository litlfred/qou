    The canonical carriers sit exactly on the b₃ = 0 locus of the count channel #

    QOU.Mass.BrunnianBindingChannel is qou-brng's surviving route: for a Brunnian pair the signed (linking) channel vanishes and the whole mutual energy is the unsigned count channel countChannel n₊ n₋ q = ½(n₊+n₋)(q − q⁻¹), strictly positive at q > 1. That answers the linking-number objection — binding survives where lk = 0.

    It does not answer the cumulant objection, and this file measures the gap.

    The carriers land on the degenerate point #

    Write μ for a carrier's count multiplicity ½(n₊+n₋), so its binding is s·μ·h with h = q − q⁻¹ and s the MeV scale. Then

    b₃ = s·h·(μ_triple − 3·μ_pair)

    and b3_eq_zero_iff says this vanishes iff μ_triple = 3·μ_pair — which is precisely the pair-local condition, so QOU.Mass.CrossingModelPairLocality.b3_eq_zero_of_pairLocal applies.

    The canonical carriers satisfy it exactly. The deuteron is a Hopf clasp, n₊ = 2, n₋ = 0, so μ_pair = 1. The A = 3 carrier is the Borromean L6a4, n₊ = n₋ = 3, so μ_triple = 3. And 3 = 3 · 1. Hence canonical_carriers_b3_eq_zero: the count channel predicts b₃ = 0 on the framework's own carriers, against the measured b₃ = +1.4262225 (QOU.MassTheory.AmeBindingNotPairwise.ame_b3_eq).

    b3_over_b2_eq shows the scale cancels: b₃/b₂ = μ_triple/μ_pair − 3, so this is a zero-parameter statement — no choice of s or q rescues it. BrunnianBindingChannel's sub-question-1 answer ("one coordination unit per bonded pair, C(A,2) for a cluster") is the same degeneracy stated for general A.

    What this does NOT show — a quantisation obstruction that is not there #

    The first thing to try is that μ is a half-integer (n₊+n₋ ∈ ℕ) while the measured ratio μ_triple/μ_pair = 3.6411239316 is not, so no carrier could work. That argument is wrong and is recorded here so it is not re-attempted. It assumes μ_pair = 1; with μ_pair free, small half-integers get arbitrarily close — (μ_pair, μ_triple) = (7, 51/2) gives b₃/b₂ = 0.642857, 0.27 % from measured, and (11/2, 20) gives 0.74 %.

    So the count channel is not obstructed in principle. It has ample freedom, and the failure is specifically the carrier assignment. That makes the burden sharper rather than lighter: two free crossing integers matched to one measured ratio is a fit, so a carrier reaching b₃ earns nothing unless its crossing numbers are derived. Which is exactly qou-brng's open item.

    Scope #

    Scope-(a) on the conjunction {count channel, Hopf pair, Borromean triple}. It excludes no ingredient: the count channel survives, brunnian_binding_survives is untouched, and the Brunnian mechanism remains the only thing that lets binding exist at lk = 0. What is excluded is that these two carriers with this channel reproduce b₃.

    Ground ring: generic {R : Type*} [Field R] (§7c — algebra only; h and s are parameters). Beans qou-brng, qou-w6vh.

            Non-vacuity: the channel is not obstructed. A carrier pair off the degenerate locus does give a nonzero cumulant — here μ_pair = 7, μ_triple = 51/2, which lands b₃/b₂ within 0.27 % of the measured value. So the failure above is the carrier assignment, not the channel. Two free crossing integers against one measured ratio is a fit, which is why this is recorded as a possibility rather than a result.

            The other reading fails too, and in the opposite direction #

            Applying the count channel to the deuteron is already a stretch: the deuteron is a Hopf clasp with lk = ±1, not Brunnian, so its signed channel is live and brunnian_energy_is_count_channel does not apply to it. The honest alternative is to use the full mutual energy pairEnergy n₊ n₋ q = n₊·q − n₋·q⁻¹ for both carriers.

            That reading is worse, and structurally so. With Hopf (2,0) ↦ 2q and Borromean (3,3) ↦ 3q − 3q⁻¹,

            b₃ = (3q − 3q⁻¹) − 3·(2q) = −3(q + q⁻¹) < 0   for every `q > 0`.

            So it is not that the number comes out wrong at q₀ — no substrate modulus whatever makes this reading positive, while the measurement is b₃/b₂ = +0.6411239316.

            Taken together the two readings bracket the measurement from below: count-only gives exactly 0, full-energy gives −2.7174806 at q₀, and the measured value is positive. The failure is therefore not a matter of having picked the wrong channel.
