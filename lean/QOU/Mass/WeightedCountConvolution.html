    The K-weighted count is multiplicative over disjoint blocks #

    Bean qou-8uxw, candidate S1 of docs/audits/2026-09-07-route-c-step3-and-kz-level-plans.md §A.

    §7h of the binding-gap survey writes the framework's convention-D trace of a braid word w on n strands as a weighted count over the colour-1 strand number k,

    C(w) = Σ_{k=0}^{n} q^{n − 2k} · N_k(w),

    with N_k(w) the per-weight-space fixed-point count. For a disjoint juxtaposition w₁ ⊔ w₂ (the tensor product of two words on n₁ and n₂ strands) the counts convolve — N_k(w₁ ⊔ w₂) = Σ_{i+j=k} N_i(w₁) N_j(w₂) — because a colouring of the union is a pair of colourings. The worry S1 answers is whether the disjoint union over k in C could be where a many-body term hides. It cannot: C is a polynomial evaluation, C(w) = q^n · P_w(q⁻²) with P_w(y) = Σ_k N_k(w) y^k, and convolution of coefficients is multiplication of polynomials, so C(w₁ ⊔ w₂) = C(w₁) · C(w₂) — hence log C is additive over blocks by construction. Non-factorisation over bonds is therefore decided by the word's topology alone (the ordering hypothesis of FormalityBridgeF1), never by the k-structure.

    The statement is proved at the level it has: for polynomials P, Q over a field and x ≠ 0, weighted (P * Q) (n₁ + n₂) x = weighted P n₁ x * weighted Q n₂ x. Identifying P_w with the count polynomial of a specific word is the probe's job (route_c_step2_pivotal_vs_count_probe.py, checks 18–26), not this file's.

    Base ring: a generic Field K; no ℝ.
