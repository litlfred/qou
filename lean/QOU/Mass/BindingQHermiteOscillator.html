    QOU.Mass.BindingQHermiteOscillator — the Rogers–Szegő / continuous q-Hermite family #

    The third natural recurrence family around the binding keystone (answering "what q-difference structures come out naturally — not limited to q-Bessel"). Where BindingHyperbolicRecurrence gives the hyperbolic cosh/sinh tower (Layer 1) and BindingQBesselKernel the super-exponential q-Bessel tower (Layer 2), the Rogers–Szegő polynomials H_n(t;q) = Σ_{k=0}^n [n;k]_q t^k (equivalently the continuous q-Hermite, the q-oscillator bound-state basis) sit between them: a q-deformation of the harmonic-oscillator / Hermite basis whose q → 1 limit is the binomial (1+t)^n and, after the standard rescaling, the Hermite functions — whose own classical tower is the cosh/sinh of Layer 1.

    This file records the family as a §3b recurrence class (the standard three-term recurrence is the hypothesis) and proves two structural consequences:

    • q → 1 collapse to the first-order binomial recurrence — the classical (un-q-deformed) limit;
    • even/odd decoupling at t = −1 — the recurrence becomes two-step H_{n+2} = (1−q^{n+1})H_n, so with the boundary value H_1(−1;q) = 0 all odd terms vanish while the even terms are the finite q-product tower H_{2j}(−1;q) = ∏_{i<j}(1−q^{2i+1})·H_0. This is the q-deformed even/odd parity — both directions now closed: the q-oscillator analogue of the cosh (even) / sinh (odd) split of NaStokesKeystone and BindingHyperbolicRecurrence, at the level of the q-Hermite basis.

    Standard reference recurrence (Rogers–Szegő): H_{n+1}(t;q) = (1+t)·H_n(t;q) − (1−q^n)·t·H_{n−1}(t;q).

    Ground ring: a generic field (the family is fibre-level / q-formal, §7c — no archimedean evaluation). The recurrence is the conjectural/definitional input; the consequences are derived.

By the two-step decoupling above, the odd-index subsequence is 0 for all j. This is the q-deformed even/odd parity: the q-oscillator analogue of "sinh is the odd channel" — the odd sector collapses, leaving the even (cosh-like) Rogers–Szegő tower, exactly mirroring the bar-even/bar-odd split of NaStokesKeystone.
