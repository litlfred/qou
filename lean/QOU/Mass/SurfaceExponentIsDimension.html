    QOU.Mass.SurfaceExponentIsDimension — the surface exponent is not free, and the production chain's value is excluded #

    QOU.Mass.CoordinationSurfaceDeficit shows the per-nucleon binding of a bounded-degree bond count splits as

    B/A = ε·k/2 − (ε(k−k')/2)·(A_surf/A)                        (`perNucleon_split`)

    and left one question: what fixes the surface term. This file answers a sharper version of it. The A-dependence of the correction is carried entirely by the surface fraction A_surf/A, and that fraction is where the DIMENSION of the coordination graph enters. For a compact d-dimensional cluster the boundary is (d−1)-dimensional, so

    A_surf ∼ c·A^((d−1)/d)   ⇒   A_surf/A ∼ c·A^(−1/d).

    So under that hypothesis the exponent of the surface correction is 1/d. Whatever it is read as, the exponent is a one-number shape test rather than a ratio, and unlike the surface-to-volume ratio (measured generic at 67.9 % of its plausible window, qou-kqdn) it is not free to be chosen: perNucleonExp_exponent_inj proves distinct exponents give distinct functions of A, so no choice of amplitude can disguise a wrong one. That much is proved here. The reading p = 1/d is NOT established — see §"the sharpest objection" and the data section below, which reject the chain's p = 1 but do not select d = 3.

    The production word is one-dimensional, and this is exact #

    atom_braid_word_3A walks a chain: nucleon j writes generators 3j, 3j+1 and then a single inter-nucleon letter at 3j+2, for j = 0 … A−2. So the coordination graph is the path P_A:

    • the two endpoint nucleons carry one bond each,
    • every interior nucleon carries two,
    • total bonds A − 1.

    That is the boundary deficit of CoordinationSurfaceDeficit at k = 2, k' = 1, and A_surf = 2 — a constant, not a growing surface (chain_bondCount; the surface count is the literal 2 appearing there, independent of A). Hence

    B/A = ε − ε/A                                               (`chain_perNucleon`)

    i.e. exponent p = 1, the d = 1 case. The chain does have a boundary; its boundary is zero-dimensional.

    What is new here, and what the corpus already had #

    The A^(−1) SCALING IS NOT NEW and must not be reported as a discovery. QOU.MassTheory.ChainBindingNoSaturationPeak.chainPerNucleon_eq_hyperbola already proves, by ring, that an isobaric chain's per-nucleon binding is exactly c₁ + c₂/A — the same object as chain_perNucleon at k = 2, k' = 1. What is new is the derivation from the boundary DEGREE: the corpus states the same deficit only in bond-count language ("an open A-chain has one fewer bond than nucleons", 2026-06-15-delta-lambda-offset-open-chain-boundary.md), never as two endpoints of degree 1 against an interior degree 2 — which is what makes it an instance of CoordinationSurfaceDeficit rather than an isolated arithmetic fact.

    QOU.Mass.AffineCountIsNotASurfaceTerm reaches "affine is not a surface term" by an independent scope-(b) route (strict monotonicity of (12A−1)/A^(2/3)); since A − 1 is affine, that theorem covers this graph too. chain_ne_semf below is the complementary statement in the exponent variable: it excludes every p ≠ 1, not one target exponent.

    The sharpest objection to reading p = 1/d as a dimension, met rather than avoided: the corpus's only derived three-dimensional coordination object is the deltahedron contact graph, and by Euler it has E = 3A − 6 — still affine, so it also gives A^(−1). A convex deltahedron puts every nucleon on the boundary, so it is d = 3 geometrically while having no bulk at all. A_surf ∼ c·A^((d−1)/d) needs a bulk; a surface-only graph is exactly the case where the reading fails. So p = 1/d is a statement about compact clusters, and nothing here asserts the substrate emits one.

    Distinguish also the recorded negative in bean qou-7e67, "Is the exponent a DIMENSION question? NO: no single exponent fits". That tests R ∼ A^x for the radius over A ≤ 4, varying d over ambient manifolds. This varies d over the nucleon coordination graph and predicts the deficit's exponent. Different object, different observable; the negative does not transfer, and is cited so a reader does not think it was missed.

    What the data say (companion probe, 19/19) #

    Profiling p against 2548 AME nuclides with Coulomb, asymmetry and pairing carried separately:

    • the chain's p = 1 is rejected — RSS(1) = 147.0 against RSS(p*) = 38.2, and p = 1 lies outside the interval where RSS stays within 1 % of its minimum, under every analysis variant tried;
    • but the data do not cleanly select d = 3 either. The full-sample optimum is p* = 0.364 (d = 2.75); restricted to A ≥ 40 it is p* = 0.582 (d = 1.72), and the two 1 %-RSS intervals are disjoint. No single power-law exponent describes the whole chart.

    So this file's chain_ne_semf is confirmed against data, and the positive reading p = 1/d is not thereby established. A pre-registered expectation that the analysis variants would agree failed; that is recorded in the probe rather than dropped.

    Scope #

    • Proved: the chain identities (exact, k = 2, k' = 1, A_surf = 2), the general d-dimensional surface fraction as a definition, injectivity of the exponent, and that the chain's law differs from any p ≠ 1 law at some A.
    • Assumed: that a compact d-dimensional cluster has A_surf ∼ c·A^((d−1)/d). Standard geometry, stated as a hypothesis exactly as in CoordinationSurfaceDeficit; not proved. It fails for surface-only graphs, see the E = 3A − 6 objection above.
    • NOT claimed: that the substrate emits a d = 3 graph, nor that the measured exponent is a dimension. What is shown is that the exponent is not free (perNucleonExp_exponent_inj), that the production word's graph is d = 1, and that the data exclude it.
    • Not a fit. No coefficient is chosen against data here.
    • Note on shape: a − b·A^(−p) with b, p > 0 is monotone in A, so it cannot produce the iron peak alone — the same scope-(b) point the Faddeev–Niemi verdict records for pure power laws. The exponent measured is the deficit's, with Coulomb carried separately, exactly as QOU.Mass.SaturationPeakStructure sets the problem up.
