    QOU.Mass.StokesSingularCountAffine — Euler's formula bounds the Stokes singular locus below by an affine function of A, so the non-abelian-Stokes surface term renormalises the volume coefficient #

    QOU.Mass.HyperbolicSurfaceLaw bridged Yekutieli's non-abelian Stokes estimate to the bulk criterion: the estimate splits into a good term counted by the 4^k kites of the tessellation (an area count) and a bad term bounded by the number of singular points of the surface map (|bad| ≤ m; bad = ∅ if the map is linear), one dimension down. The 2026-08-30 audit reads the surface coefficient a_S as proportional to that singular count for the spanning surface of the nuclear braid closure, and bean qou-yc1m asks the question nobody had answered: how does that count scale with A?

    This file answers it without choosing a parametrization, because the answer does not depend on one.

    🛑 First, a correction to the reading this file inherited — including from my own #6929 #

    The 2026-08-30 audit, and HyperbolicSurfaceLaw's header repeating it, said the bad set is "counted by the singular points of the map defining the surface". That is the 1-dimensional string case (held text, sec-000-0-introduction.md:2280: "Let m be the number of singular points of the map σ. Then |bad(k)| ≤ m."). In the 2-dimensional case the kite map τ : I² → X is linear by definition (Def 4.1.1, :3278) and has no singular points at all; there, Def 4.4.12 (:3964) makes an index good iff the forms α|Zᵢ and β|Zᵢ are smooth, and Lemma 4.4.13's proof (:3969-3976) covers "the singular locus of α and the singular locus of β" by m line segments Zⱼ and sets a₀ := 2m, a₁ := 2·Σ len(Zⱼ). So:

    • the bad set is the singular locus of the connection, not of the surface parametrization;
    • a₁ is a length, not a count — "a_S ∝ a count" can only refer to a₀;
    • if (α, β) are smooth, a₀ = a₁ = 0 on every surface at every A — the Stokes route then supplies no surface term of any size. A Stokes surface term requires a singular connection, and the corpus has no (α, β) as forms at all (bean qou-20bk: "Step 2's curvature is stipulated, not a form").

    The Euler argument below is correct as mathematics; what it says about the Stokes surface term is therefore conditional on a hypothesis about the connection, stated as hsing in the theorem and named here so it is not mistaken for a derived fact.

    The argument, with its hypothesis in the open #

    Whatever kites are used, they are glued along a cell decomposition of the surface — V vertices, E edges, F faces. Euler's formula gives, for any decomposition with at least one vertex and one face,

    E = V + F − χ  ≥  2 − χ                                   (`edges_ge_two_sub_chi`)

    and the canonical closure's Seifert surface has χ = −9A + 1 — not measured but computed from n = 3A disks and 12A − 1 bands, both of which the corpus derives for all A (qou-hu1f: "11A + (A−1) = 12A − 1; the fit was right and is now derived"), so

    E  ≥  9A + 1                                              (`edges_ge_of_chi_canonical`)

    The 1-skeleton of any cell decomposition of the spanning surface grows at least linearly in A. That bound is sharp (the one-vertex polygon presentation attains it) and it generalises two exact counts the corpus already holds — AffineCountIsNotASurfaceTerm.crossingCount = 12A − 1 and TransverseSurfaceCurvature.seifertBettiOne_extensive, which is 1 − χ affine for all A — from one decomposition to all of them. It is not a new number.

    (H1) The hypothesis: that the singular locus of the connection forms (α, β) runs along the 1-skeleton of some cell decomposition of the surface, so that the bad-set constant a₀ = 2m inherits the Euler bound m ≥ E ≥ 9A + 1. Under (H1), against the Seifert surface's own cells 3A + (12A − 1) = 15A − 1, the boundary/bulk ratio is bounded below by something tending to 3/5, never to 0, and the Stokes singular count is not a surface term (stokes_canonical_not_surfaceTerm): like the hyperbolic ball, it renormalises the volume coefficient and supplies no A^(−1/3).

    Scope — three tiers, kept apart #

    • Proved (scope-(b), the invariant is Euler's formula): the edge lower bound for every cell decomposition; that a boundary count bounded below by an affine function with positive slope, over a bulk count bounded above by an affine function, has a ratio that does not vanish and so fails StokesSurfaceTerm; and the instantiation at the canonical closure's counts.
    • Derived inputs, cited not measured: n = 3A, c = 12A − 1, hence χ = −9A + 1, 2g = 8A with μ = A + 1. One trap in the corpus, recorded here: μ = A + 1 is carried as a hypothesis (hmu : mu = A + 1) in SLq2LabelParity.lean:77 and TorusKnotPages.lean:82, yet total_curvature_is_the_computable_half_probe.py:43 and its witness cite it as "(exact, Lean-proved)" — conditional-proved ≠ premise-established. The probe-level derivation is the_canonical_closure_is_a_chain_of_torus_links_probe.py:40-55; no Lean proves it.
    • (H1), NOT discharged and not currently dischargeable (scope-(a)): the corpus has no connection forms, so nothing can be said about where they are singular. This file gives the criterion and the bound; it cannot supply the premise. Two ways out, both named: a connection smooth on the surface, which gives a₀ = a₁ = 0 and no Stokes surface term at all; or a singular locus that is a proper sub-complex of sublinear total edge count — which Euler forbids for the whole 1-skeleton but not for a subset, so (H1) is genuinely where the content is.
    • The only escape from the Euler bound itself is a spanning surface with χ sublinear in A, i.e. genus growing slower than A; none is proposed anywhere in the corpus, and for the canonical closure g = 4A is derived.
    • Not a fit. No coefficient appears.

    So the two routes the owner asked to be connected — the hyperbolic ball and the surface of kites — end in the same place for the same structural reason, to the extent the Stokes one is a surface term at all: each has a boundary that is a fixed fraction of its bulk, the ball because S/V ≥ 2 in H³ (proved, unconditional), the Stokes surface because Euler pins its 1-skeleton to ≥ 2 − χ edges with χ affine (proved, conditional on (H1)).

    Ground ring: ℤ for the counting, ℝ for the limit.

    Euler's formula lower-bounds the cut locus #

    source
    theorem QOU.Mass.StokesSingularCountAffine.edges_ge_two_sub_chi (V E F χ : ℤ) (hEuler : χ = V - E + F) (hV : 1 ≤ V) (hF : 1 ≤ F) :
    2 - χ ≤ E

    Any cell decomposition with a vertex and a face has at least 2 − χ edges. Pure Euler.

    source
    theorem QOU.Mass.StokesSingularCountAffine.edges_ge_of_chi_canonical (A V E F : ℤ) (hEuler : -9 * A + 1 = V - E + F) (hV : 1 ≤ V) (hF : 1 ≤ F) :
    9 * A + 1 ≤ E

    At the canonical closure's χ = −9A + 1, every decomposition has ≥ 9A + 1 edges. The 1-skeleton of any cell decomposition grows at least linearly in A. (Whether the connection's singular locus contains it is hypothesis (H1) of the header, not a consequence.)

    source
    theorem QOU.Mass.StokesSingularCountAffine.chi_of_genus_boundary (A g μ : ℤ) (hg : 2 * g = 8 * A) (hμ : μ = A + 1) :
    2 - 2 * g - μ = -9 * A + 1

    Euler's formula in the form χ = 2 − 2g − μ for a compact orientable surface of genus g with μ boundary components, together with the corpus's 2g = 8A, μ = A + 1, reproduces χ = −9A + 1 — the three measured invariants are mutually consistent.

    A bounded-below boundary over a bounded-above bulk is not a surface term #

    source
    theorem QOU.Mass.StokesSingularCountAffine.not_stokesSurfaceTerm_of_bounds (cells sing : ℝ → ℝ) (αc βc αs βs : ℝ) (hαc : 0 < αc) (hαs : 0 < αs) (hsing : ∀ᶠ (A : ℝ) in Filter.atTop, αs * A + βs ≤ sing A) (hcells : ∀ᶠ (A : ℝ) in Filter.atTop, 0 < cells A ∧ cells A ≤ αc * A + βc) :
    ¬HyperbolicSurfaceLaw.StokesSurfaceTerm cells sing

    The general criterion. If the singular count is eventually at least an affine function with positive slope, and the cell count is eventually positive and at most an affine function, then the ratio does not tend to 0, so the pair is not a StokesSurfaceTerm.
