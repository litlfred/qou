    What the two together say, in the form the probe uses: for a determinant-one real 2×2 matrix whose trace is strictly below 2, being PSD is impossible. An elliptic SL₂(ℝ) element's powers all have determinant one and trace at most 2, so this is the exclusion.

    The general case #

    trace_ge_two is the d = 2 instance of a bound that holds in every dimension, and the general form is what the γγ exclusion actually needs: the Wedderburn blocks of Hₙ(q) run to dimension 16 at n = 6, so a 2×2 lemma reaches only the smallest of them.

    Bean qou-99qc; companion computations/qbeta/qbeta_gamma_gamma_trace_ge_one_probe.py (8/8), which measures that every γγ vertex has determinant 1 in every block — the braid generators σᵢ and σᵢ₊₁ are conjugate, so their block images share a determinant and det(σᵢσᵢ₊₁⁻¹) = 1 — hence so does every γγ word.

    theorem QOU.Mass.PsdDetOneTrace.card_le_trace_of_det_one {d : ℕ} {A : Matrix (Fin d) (Fin d) ℝ} (h : A.PosSemidef) (hdet : A.det = 1) :
    ↑d ≤ A.trace

    The bound in every dimension. A real PSD matrix of determinant 1 has trace at least its dimension.

    The eigenvalues are non-negative with product 1, so none is zero, and log x ≤ x − 1 summed over them gives 0 = Σ log λᵢ ≤ Σ λᵢ − d. That is AM-GM, taken in the form whose Mathlib lemma is Real.log_le_sub_one_of_pos.

    Equality holds exactly at the identity (each λᵢ = 1); the d = 2 case of that is eq_one_of_trace_eq_two above.
