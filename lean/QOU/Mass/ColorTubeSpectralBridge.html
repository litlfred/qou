    QOU.Mass.ColorTubeSpectralBridge — the tube ↔ spectral coordinate change #

    The color cubic is carried in the corpus in two coordinate systems, and until now the change of variable between them lived only in .md prose:

    • Spectral coordinate X (the canonical one; QOU.Mass.ColorSpectralCubicGeometry, QOU.Mass.ColorCubicPowerSums):

      C_z(X) = X³ + (z+1)X² + (z²+z+1)X + (z³+z²+z+1)

      Its elementary symmetric data e₁ = −(z+1), e₂ = z²+z+1, e₃ = −(z³+z²+z+1) is the q-free spectral datum (the z2bn power-sum result).

    • Tube coordinate c (the shifted one; the color-tube-cubic / color-tube-constraints content blocks):

      T_z(c) = c³ + (4z+1)c² + (6z²+3z+1)c + (4z³+3z²+2z+1)

      with the block-level Vieta relations Σcᵢ = −(4z+1), Σ_{i<j}cᵢcⱼ = 6z²+3z+1, ∏cᵢ = −(4z³+3z²+2z+1).

    The color-tube-cubic block states that T_z is obtained from C_z by the substitution X = c + z — i.e. the tube roots are the spectral roots shifted, cᵢ = wᵢ − z. That claim was prose-only; this file discharges it and the three induced Vieta transports, sorry-free.

    Why it matters. The spectral coordinate is the canonical one (its symmetric functions are the q-free spectral data), while the constraint blocks are written in the shifted tube coordinate. This bridge is what makes transporting a constraint between the two coordinate systems a checked rewrite rather than a hand substitution — the prerequisite for restating the cubic constraints in the spectral wᵢ.

    Scope (honest). This is a coordinate-change certificate: pure polynomial algebra, no new geometry, no claim about the prolongation operator or the branch locus (see ColorSpectralCubicGeometry for the latter). It does not by itself rewrite any constraint block; it licenses such rewrites.

    Base ring (§7c): generic [CommRing R] — every identity here is a polynomial identity closed by ring, with no archimedean construct. The substrate specialisation (z = √q − 1/√q at R := ℝ) happens at the consumer.

        Bring power sums: spectral form ⇒ tube-chart form #

        The same shift governs the Bring's-surface constraints. In the spectral (Lagrange-resolvent) coordinates w₀,…,w₄ the constraints are simply the vanishing of the first three power sums,

        p₁(w) = p₂(w) = p₃(w) = 0,

        which is the definition of Bring's surface — the clean spectral statement. The color-tube-constraints block instead works in the shifted tube chart cₖ = wₖ − z (color-tube-coordinates: c_r = w₂−z, c_g = w₃−z, c_b = w₄−z, σ = w₁−z), where the constraints pick up z-dependence.

        The theorems below transport the vanishing power sums through the shift and show the tube-chart values are forced: pₘ(c) = (−1)ᵐ · 5 · zᵐ for m = 1, 2, 3. This is the exact sense in which the spectral coordinates are the canonical ones — in w the constraints are 0 = 0 = 0, and every z-dependent term in the tube chart is an artifact of the shift.

              The three color-tube-constraints equations, in spectral form #

              The three constraint equations of the color-tube-constraints block are written in the shifted tube chart and are visibly z-dependent. Transported to the spectral resolvents wₖ they become z-free: every z cancels identically.

              Conventions are the block's own (aggregate-color-quantities), with the tube chart (σ, c_r, c_g, c_b) = (w₁−z, w₂−z, w₃−z, w₄−z):

              • p₁(c) = σ + c_r + c_g + c_b (first power sum)
              • h₂(c) = σ·c_b + c_r·c_g — the Hermitian quadric: it pairs conjugate resolvents wₖ ↔ w₅₋ₖ. It is not the full e₂; this is exactly the convention that had to be pinned before the restatement could be written.
              • h₃(c) = σ²c_g + c_b²c_r + c_r²σ + c_g²c_b (Hermitian cubic, skip-one)
              • S = Σₖ wₖ = p₁(c) + 4z, D = Σₖ ζ⁻ᵏwₖ = χ_ζ(c) − z (block identities)

              The spectral invariants the constraints turn into:

              • H₂(w) = w₁w₄ + w₂w₃ (spectral Hermitian quadric — conjugate pairing)
              • H₃(w) = w₁²w₃ + w₂²w₁ + w₄²w₂ + w₃²w₄ (spectral Hermitian cubic)
