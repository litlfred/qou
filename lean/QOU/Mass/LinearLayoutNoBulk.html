    A linear layout has no bulk — the invariant that decides Avenue 2 #

    Bean qou-brng, Avenue 2 (owner, 2026-09-08): "both derived edge sets on main are affine and an affine count can never be a surface term, so ask what edge set has a bulk at all; that is decidable by a proved invariant rather than a fit."

    The framework's nuclear encoding lays the A nucleon blocks on a line of 3A strands (mass_at_3A_proper.atom_braid_word_3A: block k on strands 3k, 3k+1, 3k+2, every inter-nucleon letter at generator 3k+2, i.e. between blocks k and k+1 of the ordering). Whatever the ordering, whatever the multiplicities, the nucleon pairs that ever cross are pairs consecutive in the ordering. So the coordination graph G of any word this constructor can produce is a linear-layout graph: a subgraph of the path on the ordering. This file proves what such a graph can and cannot be, over Fin n with Mathlib's SimpleGraph:

    • degree_le_two — every vertex has at most two neighbours (k−1 and k+1);
    • not_linearLayout_of_degree_ge_three, card_edgeFinset_le — any graph with a vertex of degree ≥ 3, or with more than n edges, is not a linear-layout graph. The corpus's one bulk-carrying edge set — the max-contact sphere packing, degree up to 12 (MaxContactHasBulk) — is therefore not expressible by this encoding, for any A ≥ 4; neither is the complete graph the pyhecke normal-form count corresponds to;
    • chained_linearLayout_adj_succ — a connected linear-layout graph contains every consecutive edge, i.e. it is the whole path (a missing edge {k, k+1} disconnects k from k+1: no chain of adjacencies in a subgraph of the path crosses it);
    • card_edgeFinset_eq — hence its bond count is exactly n − 1: affine, the p = 1 law (CoordinationBulkCriterion.chain_is_affine);
    • surface_eq_two — and its surface count (vertices with fewer than two neighbours) is exactly 2 for every n ≥ 2;
    • linearLayout_family_not_hasBulk — so a family of connected linear-layout graphs, one per A, has a surface count with no bulk in the sense of CoordinationBulkCriterion.HasBulk (A_surf → ∞ and A_surf / A → 0).

    Connectivity is stated as Chained: every two vertices are joined by a chain of adjacencies (Relation.ReflTransGen G.Adj). That is what SimpleGraph.Connected means (a walk is such a chain); it is spelled out here so the file depends on nothing but the adjacency relation.

    What this closes. Within the framework's encoding — every word atom_braid_word_3A can build, for every ordering — no edge set has a bulk: a connected assembly is the path (surface 2, no bulk); a disconnected one is a disjoint union of paths, whose surface count is twice its component count, so a "bulk" there would be fragmentation into unboundedly many chains, which the closure of a single braid word is not. The scope-(a) narrowing of 2026-09-05-coordination-bulk-criterion.md §5(i) — "no affine edge set has a bulk" — becomes scope-(b) over the encoding: no edge set the encoding can express has a bulk, invariant = linear layout. A bulk needs a non-linear layout (a strand interleaving that lets one block touch three or more others), and the corpus derives no such layout; choosing one to reproduce the surface term would be selection against the target.

    Base type: Fin n, Mathlib SimpleGraph; no ℝ in the graph theory. ℝ enters only in the last theorem, through the corpus's real-valued HasBulk.
