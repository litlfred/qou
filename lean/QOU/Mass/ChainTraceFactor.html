    The chain trace factor: tr₂(σ + d) = z + d, and why the canonical L is a sum #

    computations/probes/the_canonical_mass_trace_factorises_over_the_chain_probe.py (bean qou-m3sh) measured, at 200 bits, that the Markov trace of the full 3A-strand canonical atom word is the product over generators of TWO-strand traces of the per-generator Hecke polynomials — because the closure is a linear connected sum (QOU.Mass.TorusKnotPages, prop:canonical-closure-torus-chain) and multiplicative invariants are multiplicative under connected sum. That multiplicativity is a theorem about the Hecke algebra which this file does NOT formalise. What it does formalise is the algebra of the factors, so that the arc's three bond constants rest on an identity rather than on sixty agreeing digits:

    • The affine law. On H₂ under convention D the two blocks are (2) and (1,1) with σ ↦ q and σ ↦ -q⁻¹, weighted by the Wenzl level-2 weights w₊ = [3]_q / ([3]_q + 1) and w₋ = 1 / ([3]_q + 1). The two-strand trace of the letter σ + d·1 is therefore w₊(q + d) + w₋(-q⁻¹ + d), and this equals z + d with z = q³/(q² + 1) the Markov weight (twoStrandTrace_eq). Every interface generator of the canonical word carries exactly one such letter, so its factor is z + d.

    • The mirror factor. σ⁻¹ = σ - h with h = q - q⁻¹, so the nn factor is z - h, and z - h = 1 / (q (q² + 1)) (markovZ_sub_heckeH): the pp and nn factors multiply to 1/[2]_q² (markovZ_mul_mirror), which is L_pp + L_nn = 2 log(q + q⁻¹) (L_pp_add_L_nn).

    • Positivity. For 1 ≤ q all three factors z, z - h, z + d_pn are positive (markovZ_pos, mirror_pos, pn_factor_pos), so the bond constants L = -log(z + d) are defined; and -log of a product of positive factors is the sum of the -logs (neg_log_prod). Given the factorisation, the canonical L is exactly the sum over the A - 1 interfaces of -log(z + d_i): no many-body term exists in the canonical closure. That additive closed form is the one QOU.BraidKnot.BsymLinearChain (bean 6z40) took as its named structural input.

    Not formalised: the factorisation of the H_{3A} trace itself (a theorem about the Hecke algebra, checked to 1e-59 at A ≤ 4), the numerical values at q₀ (the probe, 60 digits), and the connected-sum topology. Ground field ℝ, q a free variable with the hypotheses each statement needs. Bean qou-m3sh.

                          The pn bond can never be the strongest (bean qou-mu1h) #

                          computations/probes/the_pn_bond_is_an_average_and_cannot_be_extremal_probe.py measured at A = 2, where no ordering or arrangement convention is in play, that the three bond constants come out L_pp < L_pn < L_nn — so the model ranks the unbound dineutron above the bound deuteron. That is not a numerical accident. The pn interface letter is by definition the weighted average of the pp letter (σ, offset 0) and the nn letter (σ⁻¹, offset -h), the two-strand trace is affine in the offset (twoStrandTrace_eq), and -log is strictly decreasing, so the ordering is forced for every q > 0 and every δ ∈ (0,2). Since exactly one of the three two-body systems binds, and a binding-ranking constant would have to put that one at an EXTREME, a convex combination cannot do it.

                          δ at the substrate's q₀ is 0.00138, and it would have to reach 2 — at q ≈ 3.458 — before the convexity even breaks, so nothing here is tunable. The physics is NOT formalised: that only pn binds is an empirical input, and this arc's L carries no spin (the real distinction is 3S1 versus 1S0). What is proved is the ordering.
