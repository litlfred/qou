    QOU.Mass.IrreducibleCubicNoQuadraticRoot — a cubic acquires no root among elements #

    of degree at most two

    Statement. Let F be a field, L an F-algebra which is a nontrivial ring, and a : L. If a is a root of some nonzero g : F[X] of degree ≤ 2, then a is not a root of any irreducible p : F[X] of degree 3. Reason: the minimal polynomial of a would have degree 3 (being p up to a unit) and at the same time degree ≤ 2 (being minimal among annihilators, g included).

    The hypothesis "a is annihilated by a nonzero polynomial of degree ≤ 2" is the usable form of "a lies in a quadratic extension of F": in L = F⟮b⟯ with b² ∈ F every element satisfies such a g. Taking it as the hypothesis keeps this file free of the IntermediateField tower machinery while proving exactly the obstruction the application needs.

    Why this is here (bean qou-7fnr, follows qou-1z28 / #6695) #

    #6695 pruned the first route of wall W1 (qou-d8de): a q-connection on the base z-line with coefficients in ℂ(z) cannot have the colour sheets as flat sections. The chain is — the colour cubic is irreducible over K(z) (QOU.Mass.ColorCubicIrreducible), so no sheet is a rational function of z; each sheet is algebraic, hence D-finite; a rational q-difference system would make a sheet q-holonomic; and D-finite together with q-holonomic forces rationality (Bézivin; Ramis; Schäfke–Singer). Contradiction.

    That PR then asserted that the local q-dilation t ↦ μt at a ramification point z = z_c + t² escapes the same exclusion, on the grounds that a power series always satisfies a first-order q-equation with a power-series coefficient. The theorem is about rational coefficients, so that reason does not support the claim — and the claim is false. This file supplies the missing step.

    In the local coordinate the sheets are power series in t, algebraic over K(t) and therefore D-finite there, so the identical argument applies as soon as no sheet is rational in t. Now t² = z − z_c, so every element of K(t) satisfies a polynomial of degree ≤ 2 over K(z); the colour cubic is irreducible over K(z); so by this lemma it has no root in K(t). Every hypothesis of the rationality theorem is then met in the local chart exactly as on the base, and the local rational q-dilation is excluded too.

    The concrete instance for this curve is checked exactly in w1_local_qdilation_branchpoint_probe.py, which also verifies directly that the local cubic has no root in K[t] at any degree — a leading-term bound for degree ≥ 3, and a Gröbner basis [1] over ℚ[c₀,c₁,c₂,z_c] modulo the branch sextic for degree ≤ 2. What this file carries is the general obstruction, which is the part that was missing and is reusable.

    Ground field is a generic [Field F]; no characteristic assumption and nothing archimedean (§7c).

    theorem QOU.Mass.IrreducibleCubicNoQuadraticRoot.no_root_of_irreducible_cubic {F : Type u_1} {L : Type u_2} [Field F] [Ring L] [Algebra F L] [Nontrivial L] {p : Polynomial F} (hp : Irreducible p) (hp3 : p.degree = 3) {a : L} {g : Polynomial F} (hg0 : g ≠ 0) (hg2 : g.degree ≤ 2) (hga : (Polynomial.aeval a) g = 0) :
    (Polynomial.aeval a) p ≠ 0

    An irreducible cubic has no root of degree at most two. If a is annihilated by a nonzero polynomial of degree ≤ 2 over F — which is what membership of a quadratic extension of F supplies — then a is not a root of an irreducible cubic over F.

    This is the step that carries the #6695 exclusion from the base coordinate z to the local coordinate t at a ramification point, where K(t) / K(z) is quadratic.

    Contrapositive, in the shape the prune uses. No element annihilated by a nonzero quadratic is a root of the irreducible cubic — so the sheets of an irreducible cubic curve are not elements of a quadratic extension of the base rational function field.
