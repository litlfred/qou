    Generator blindness, in the existing braid-word model #

    theorem QOU.Mass.AltCharShiftMultiset.altChar_regen {R : Type u_1} [Field R] (q : R) (f : ℕ → ℕ) (w : GammaGammaWordSelection.Word) :
    GammaGammaWordSelection.altChar q (List.map (fun (c : GammaGammaWordSelection.Crossing) => { gen := f c.gen, pos := c.pos }) w) = GammaGammaWordSelection.altChar q w

    Re-assigning generators does not move the alternating character. altChar reads Crossing.pos and never Crossing.gen, so any relabelling f : ℕ → ℕ of the Artin indices leaves it fixed.

    What the γγ ruling leaves free on three strands #

    The exclusion below says no census reading is γγ-produced. The natural next question is what a γγ-derived word could be instead — and on three strands the answer is that there is almost nothing to choose. H₃(q) has two Artin generators, so a vertex σᵢσᵢ₊₁⁻¹ needs i + 1 < 2, i.e. i = 0: the only pair-production vertex available is σ₀σ₁⁻¹, and every γγ word is one of its powers. The γγ-admissible subset of H₃ is a cyclic monoid on one generator, with a single integer of freedom.

                      Sign parity — the character's sign is (−1)^{length}, for EVERY word #

This section proves the mechanism, which is stronger and is what generalises: every letter of the alphabet has negative character, so the sign of a product of them is decided by nothing but how many there are.

                      Where the obstruction LIVES: the crossing budget, block by block #

                      The corpus builds a nucleon as B₃ core · quark block · gluon dressing, with the core (σ₀σ₁⁻¹)³ at 6 letters and the quark block at 3. Six is even and three is odd, so by the parity above the admissibility of the whole word is decided by one thing: whether the gluon dressing has odd length.

The live pyhecke.atom.nucleon_word_letters emits 11 (6 + 3 + 2); build_nucleon_nf emits 9 (6 + 3, its docstring saying "B₃ IS the gluon structure"). Those two differ by 2, so they have the same parity and the 9-vs-11 question cannot bear on this at all.
