    A γγ vertex at a two-nucleon interface must borrow, and a borrowed vertex cannot carry #

    the two-body isospin pattern #

    Bean qou-ales, step 11. Steps 6–10 computed the interface bond's trace response at one placement, V1 = σ₂σ₃⁻¹, and read its sign as "the γγ-selected diproton is unbound". This file isolates the two structural facts that narrow that reading, and both are about arity, not about magnitude or sign.

    The geometry #

    Two nucleons sit on generator sets {0,1} and {3,4} — strands 1–3 and 4–6 of H₆ — so the interface is the single generator 2. A γγ vertex is a product σ_g σ_{g+1}⁻¹: it occupies two adjacent generators. Since {2} is a single generator and an adjacent pair has two distinct elements, no vertex can sit on the interface alone (no_vertex_on_interface_alone). A vertex that touches the interface is therefore {1,2} or {2,3}, and:

    • {1,2} borrows generator 1 from the left nucleon;
    • {2,3} borrows generator 3 from the right nucleon.

    interfaceVertex_borrows_exactly_one_side proves both together: a straddling vertex meets exactly one of the two nucleon supports, never both and never neither. So every placement breaks the left–right symmetry of the pair by construction — there is no symmetric choice to prefer, and the arc's shift 2 (step 6) picks one of the two without deriving it.

    The arity obstruction #

    Measured in two_nucleon_bond_response_arity_probe (exactly over ℚ(q), deviations 0 rather than tolerances), over all four interface vertices × all four ordered channels: every vertex's response is one-body, in the slot it borrows from. V1 and V2 borrow right and satisfy r_pp = r_np, r_nn = r_pn; V3 and V4 borrow left and satisfy r_pp = r_pn, r_nn = r_np. So the obstruction below is not a defect of the canonical placement — it holds at every placement and both orientations.

    Note which comparisons decide this. r_pn ≠ r_np discriminates nothing: if f a b = g b then r_pn = g n and r_np = g p, which should differ. Only the like-pair comparisons test arity, and an earlier version of the probe omitted them off V2–V4 and so could not have decided the question it was built for.

    That measured one-body property is exactly the hypothesis of no_signMixed_of_rightDependent below, and the conclusion is that such a response can never carry the two-body pattern bean qou-q8vl requires:

    the unlike pair binds while both like pairs do not,

    i.e. f p n of opposite sign to both f p p and f n n. If f a b = g b then f p n = f n n identically, so the two cannot differ in sign — the product of a real with itself is non-negative. The same holds for a left-dependent response by the mirror argument (no_signMixed_of_leftDependent), hence for either placement (no_signMixed_of_oneBody).

    This is a stronger objection than a sign objection. A sign objection says the numbers come out the wrong way round and invites looking for a letter that fixes them. This says the functional form cannot express the distinction at all, whatever the letters are, for as long as the response depends on one nucleon.

    Scope — (a), not (b), and what the corpus already said #

    placement_sign_not_determined_by_channel records the second measured fact: on the same channel, V1 gives a trace ratio above one (unbound) and V3 gives one below (bound), so the binding sign is not a function of the channel. Measured: the discriminator is which of the vertex's two letters carries the inverse — inverse on the borrowed letter (V1, V4) unbinds, inverse on the interface letter (V2, V3) binds, 4/4 — and not which side is borrowed, which sets only the magnitude.

    Do not restate that as "σ₂ versus σ₂⁻¹ flips the binding". That is false and contradicts QOU.QBeta.SingleCrossingAlwaysBinds.interface_mem_Ioo (sorry-free), which proves the single-crossing family μ = z + d, d ∈ [−h, 0], lies wholly in (0,1) — both orientations bind. Nor is V2 that substitution: σ₂ → σ₂⁻¹ in σ₂σ₃⁻¹ gives σ₂⁻¹σ₃⁻¹, not a γγ word at all. V2 = V1⁻¹ is the group inverse (reverse and invert), a third map distinct from both the bar involution and the isospin ℤ/2 swap — two of which the corpus has already had to retract a conflation of. The rows are consistent with interface_mem_Ioo only because a γγ word has length 6, which step 8 established.

    The placement being underived is the corpus's own position, not a new finding here. docs/coordination/2026-05-24-low-isotope-mass-anatomy-ledger.md records the pp ↦ σ, nn ↦ σ⁻¹, pn ↦ σ_avg rule as asserted without a prop: block, a Rule-14 escalation candidate, since 2026-05-24; brings-surface/inter-nucleon-channel-color-tube.md carries a live conjectural derivation route for it. Because that route exists, this stays scope-(a) — "this construction, at these placements, against these words" — and excludes no ingredient.

    What is structural is the implication below: a one-body response cannot carry the isospin pattern. The premise (one-body) is measured at four vertices against the canonical words; promoting the whole to scope-(b) would need a proof that tr_M(W·G^k) always factorises over the strand split, which is the open question this file hands forward.

    What is NOT affected #

    DiprotonResponseClosedForm is untouched. Its closed form, its Θ(h⁴) bracket and its α/100 ceiling are correct statements about the word they are about. The narrowing is to the interpretation of r as a pair quantity.

                  Where the one-body property COMES FROM: junction factorisation #

                  source
                  theorem QOU.Mass.InterfaceVertexArity.response_oneBody_of_junctionFactorisation {tL tRb tR0 : Nucleon → ℝ} (hL : ∀ (a : Nucleon), tL a ≠ 0) (f : Nucleon → Nucleon → ℝ) (hf : ∀ (a b : Nucleon), f a b = tL a * tRb b / (tL a * tR0 b)) :
                  ∃ (g : Nucleon → ℝ), ∀ (a b : Nucleon), f a b = g b

                  From junction factorisation to one-body. The reason the response is one-body is not a coincidence about nucleon species. W₁ lies in the parabolic ⟨σ₀,σ₁⟩ (strands 1–3) while W₂ and a right-borrowing bond both lie in ⟨σ₂,σ₃,σ₄⟩ (strands 3–6); the two strand sets overlap in one strand, so the closure is a connected sum and the Markov trace is multiplicative across it. Both traces then carry the same left factor, which cancels:

                  f a b = (tL a · tR^bond b) / (tL a · tR⁰ b) = tR^bond b / tR⁰ b.

                  Stated here abstractly, so it applies at either placement by swapping the roles.

                  The premise is the standard multiplicativity of a link invariant under connected sum, which mass-theory/canonical-trace-factorises-over-chain.md states and relies on — "a closed braid whose word is a product of sub-words on strand sets overlapping in one strand is a connected sum, and the Markov trace … is multiplicative under connected sum" — and which that block's own §Status records as not formalised. So this file derives the one-body property from a cited principle rather than asserting it from a table; the principle itself remains unproved here, which is why nothing below is scope-(b).

                  Do not read this as MarkovTraceMultiplicative. That models the disjoint union, and bean qou-bsfg exists because the two were once conflated. The free two-nucleon trace is a disjoint union (supports {0,1} and {3,4} share no strand); the bonded one is a connected sum (the bond reaches across strand 3). Different properties.

                  source
                  theorem QOU.Mass.InterfaceVertexArity.no_signMixed_of_junctionFactorisation {tL tRb tR0 : Nucleon → ℝ} (hL : ∀ (a : Nucleon), tL a ≠ 0) {f : Nucleon → Nucleon → ℝ} (hf : ∀ (a b : Nucleon), f a b = tL a * tRb b / (tL a * tR0 b)) :
                  ¬SignMixed f

                  The chain, end to end. Junction factorisation ⇒ one-body ⇒ not sign-mixed. So the isospin pattern's failure is a consequence of the connected-sum structure of the two-nucleon closure, not a property of the particular bond word, the particular placement, or the numbers.

                  Measured corroboration (step-11 probe): holding the right nucleon fixed and replacing the left nucleon by six unrelated words on ⟨σ₀,σ₁⟩ — including the empty word, a single letter, an all-positive word and an averaged-heavy one — leaves the response a symbolic identity 0.0139840866412832240 in every case. The response is independent of the entire left word, not merely of its species, which is what this theorem predicts and what a species-only coincidence would not.

                    Why the SELECTION survives the orientation swap and the BINDING does not #

                    source
                    theorem QOU.Mass.InterfaceVertexArity.trace_cayleyInverse {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (M : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) R) :
                    (M.trace • 1 - M).trace = M.trace

                    The Cayley–Hamilton inverse of a 2×2 matrix of determinant one has the SAME trace. For M : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) R, Cayley–Hamilton reads M² − (tr M)·M + (det M)·1 = 0, so when det M = 1 the matrix N = (tr M)·1 − M satisfies M·N = 1. Its trace is 2·tr M − tr M = tr M. Step 4 proves det V = 1 for the Hoefsmit (2,1) vertex image, so this applies to every vertex power.

                    source
                    theorem QOU.Mass.InterfaceVertexArity.mixed_trace_not_inverse_invariant :
                    ∃ (W : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℝ) (M : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℝ) (N : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℝ), M * N = 1 ∧ N.trace = M.trace ∧ (W * M).trace ≠ (W * N).trace

                    But a MIXED trace is not protected, and that is a theorem, not a measurement. There exist W, M, N over ℝ with M·N = 1 and tr N = tr M — so no character can tell M from N — while tr(W·M) ≠ tr(W·N). Exhibited concretely: W = !![1,0;0,0], M = !![2,1;1,1], N = !![1,-1;-1,2], giving traces 2 against 1.

                    That is exactly the H₆ situation. The interface bond's own character cannot see the orientation swap (trace_cayleyInverse), but tr_M(W·G³) is a mixed trace against the nucleon word W, so nothing protects it — and the step-11 probe measures it flipping the binding sign across the swap. The selection and the binding are guarded by different things, and only one of them is guarded.

                    source
                    theorem QOU.Mass.InterfaceVertexArity.cayleyInverse_preserves_charpoly_data {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (M : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) R) (hdet : M.det = 1) :
                    (M.trace • 1 - M).trace = M.trace ∧ (M.trace • 1 - M).det = 1

                    At det = 1 the orientation swap preserves the whole characteristic polynomial of a 2×2 block, not merely its trace — so a PSD condition on such a block cannot see the swap either.

                    Why this is needed, and it is a correction to this file's own earlier scope. The owner's 2026-09-13 ruling on qou-ales (reading (B), recorded on PR #7249 and in #7319) makes the confinement SDP a condition on every Wedderburn projection of the full word, i.e. on Matrix.PosSemidef of each block. Positive-semidefiniteness is a condition on the matrix, not on its character: in general two matrices of equal trace differ in it, so trace_cayleyInverse by itself does not establish that reading-3 feasibility survives the swap, and step 11's prose claim that "reading-3 feasibility is a condition on the character" is too strong as stated under (B).

                    What rescues the conclusion is a dimension accident, and it is exactly the dimension at issue. For Fin 2 the characteristic polynomial is λ² − (tr M)λ + (det M), so it is determined by the pair (tr, det) — both of which the swap preserves at det M = 1. Identical characteristic polynomials force identical spectra, and a Hermitian matrix is PSD precisely when its spectrum is non-negative. The blocks the ruling names are (2,1) at dimension 2 and (1,1,1) at dimension 1, both ≤ 2, so the selection survives (B) after all — by a spectral argument at fixed determinant, not because feasibility is generally a character condition.

                    Scope, stated rather than assumed: this is the (tr, det) half, and that half is the part which is a theorem. Passing from "equal characteristic polynomial" to "equal PosSemidef" uses the standard Hermitian spectral criterion and is not separately formalised here. And nothing above extends to a block of dimension ≥ 3, where (tr, det) no longer determine the characteristic polynomial — for those, no claim is made in either direction.
