    theorem QOU.Mass.MilnorCarriesOrderNotMagnitude.geometric_order_forces_square {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] [LinearOrder R] [IsStrictOrderedRing R] {g b₂ b₃ b₄ : R} (h₂ : b₂ = g ^ 2) (h₃ : b₃ = g ^ 4) (h₄ : b₄ = g ^ 6) :
    b₃ ^ 2 = b₂ * b₄

    The order law forces a square relation. If b₂ = s₂g², b₃ = s₃g⁴, b₄ = s₄g⁶ with every sᵢ = ±1 and the signs aligned so the products agree, then b₃² = b₂·b₄ — the coupling g is eliminated entirely, so the test needs no knowledge of it.

    The same with an overall sign on each term, since μ̄ = ±1: squaring kills s₃, and the right-hand side needs s₂s₄ = 1. Stated for the aligned case, which is the one the measurement refutes.

    theorem QOU.Mass.MilnorCarriesOrderNotMagnitude.not_geometric_order {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] [LinearOrder R] [IsStrictOrderedRing R] {b₂ b₃ b₄ : R} (h : b₃ ^ 2 ≠ b₂ * b₄) :
    ¬∃ (g : R), b₂ = g ^ 2 ∧ b₃ = g ^ 4 ∧ b₄ = g ^ 6

    The falsifiable form. A measured b₃² ≠ b₂·b₄ refutes the single-coupling order law, whatever the coupling is — g never appears in the hypothesis.

    AME2020 gives b₃² = 2.034 against b₂·b₄ = 20.56 (isospin-averaged), so the physical cumulants are not of this shape.
