    QOU.Mass.HyperbolicSurfaceLaw — in H³ the surface fraction tends to a CONSTANT, so the framework's own geometry predicts no A^(−1/3) term at all #

    Every surface-term tranche on this arc so far (#6761, #6780, #6801, #6886) imported the compact Euclidean surface law A_surf ∝ A^(2/3), and the corpus objected each time: the ambient geometry of this framework is hyperbolic. docs/coordination/sm-26-parameter-chip-away.md already measured that using H³ consistently — R ∼ ½ ln V, so ln A where the Euclidean import has A^(1/3) — costs accuracy on a neighbouring quantity, and qou-rav3 asked for "a packing argument inside the framework" rather than a citation. This file is that argument, for the surface fraction, and it is exact.

    The fact #

    A ball of radius R in H³ (curvature −1) has

    V(R) = π (sinh 2R − 2R)          S(R) = 4π sinh² R = 2π (cosh 2R − 1).

    The volume is Bolyai's formula and it is held: library/arxiv-1010.5321v3/ (Horváth, Formulas on hyperbolic volume, §3.1 "Formulas of J. Bolyai", key horvath2010) gives πk³ sinh(2x/k) − 2πk²x, which at curvature k = 1 is hypVol. The area is not stated there; it is the derivative of the volume, and hasDerivAt_hypVol proves V' = S, so the area rests on the held source too. Nothing had to be ingested for this.

    In Euclidean space S/V = 3/R → 0: the boundary is a vanishing fraction of a large ball, and that vanishing is the whole origin of the A^(−1/3) surface term. In hyperbolic space it does not vanish. Exactly,

    S − 2V = 2π (e^(−2R) + 2R − 1)  ≥  0            since  e^(−x) ≥ 1 − x,

    so S/V ≥ 2 for every R > 0 (surfaceFraction_ge_two), with equality only in the limit — the boundary of a large hyperbolic ball is a fixed fraction of its volume, not a vanishing one.

    What that does to the binding law #

    CoordinationSurfaceDeficit puts the whole A-dependence of the correction in A_surf/A, and CoordinationBulkCriterion needs that fraction to vanish for a surface term to exist: HasBulk := A_surf → ∞ ∧ A_surf/A → 0. A hyperbolic-ball cluster — nucleon count extensive in volume, surface count extensive in area, under any radius assignment R(A) > 0 whatever — has A_surf/A ≥ 2 at every A, so it fails HasBulk (hyperbolic_not_hasBulk). Its "surface term" is a constant shift of the volume coefficient,

    B/A ≤ εk/2 − ε(k − k')                                    (`hyperbolic_perNucleon_le`)

    and the residual A-dependence is f(R) − 2 ≤ 4R/(sinh 2R − 2R) (surfaceFraction_sub_two_le), which with V ∼ (π/2) e^(2R), i.e. R ∼ ½ ln A, is of order (ln A)/A — a different functional form from A^(−1/3), and one the residual had never been tested against. The companion probe tests it. The expectation on the record before it ran was that it fits worse than the Euclidean import, because the corpus had measured that consistency costs accuracy.

    That expectation failed. Against the 2548 measured AME nuclides, with Coulomb, asymmetry and pairing carried separately, the hyperbolic (ln A)/A fits better: RSS 31.44 against the Euclidean A^(−1/3)'s 38.53 (ratio 0.816), and better than the best pure power A^(−p) at any p (38.19, #6780). The exact ball S/V at V = v₀A, profiled over v₀, also beats the import (35.18). The win is not generic by the qou-rav3 discipline: none of nine "reasonable" alternative shapes beats the Euclidean import, the hyperbolic one beats all nine, and within its own family (ln A)^m/A^p at m = 1 the optimum is p* = 0.912, within 0.09 of the hyperbolic p = 1. Two cautions the probe records: the exact ball's best v₀ puts every nucleus at R < 2, the near-Euclidean regime, so the data prefer the asymptotic shape rather than a literal extensive-volume ball; and a two-parameter (m, p) search keeps improving with m, so the hyperbolic point is the best predicted shape, not a global optimum — the latter would be a fit, and is not claimed.

    The non-abelian Stokes connection, and what "the surface of kites" is #

    Yekutieli's estimate (held, library/arxiv-1007.1250v4/, Def 4.4.12 / Lemma 4.4.13) bounds the error of the multiplicative integral by a good term counted by the 4^k square kites of the k-th binary tessellation of I² — an area count — plus a bad term bounded by a₀ + a₁·2^k, one dimension down. Corrected in StokesSingularCountAffine (this header first said the bad term is "counted by the singular points of the surface map"; that is the 1-dimensional string case, :2280): in the 2-dimensional case the kite map is linear by definition and the bad set is where the connection forms (α, β) fail to be smooth (Def 4.4.12), with a₀ = 2m for m segments covering their singular locus and a₁ a length (Lemma 4.4.13). Smooth forms give no bad set at all. So, precisely:

    • the surface of kites is the BULK of the Stokes estimate, not its surface term;
    • the surface term is the singular locus, and 2026-08-30-nonabelian-stokes-and-the-surface-term.md reads a_S as proportional to that count for the spanning surface of the nuclear braid closure.

    That is exactly a bulk/boundary split, so HasBulk's question applies to it verbatim (StokesSurfaceTerm): the singular count is a genuine surface term iff it grows without bound and vanishes as a fraction of the kite count. Nobody has computed how the singular count scales with A — the audit never states it and no probe evaluates it for any nucleus. What the corpus does know is that every Seifert invariant of the canonical closure measured so far is affine in A (every_seifert_invariant_is_affine_probe: χ = −9A + 1, 2g = 8A, …, six nuclei), so any fixed-mesh 2-cell count of the spanning surface is affine too; and affine_over_affine_not_surfaceTerm below shows an affine singular count over an affine cell count tends to a nonzero constant ratio — the same "constant shift of the volume coefficient" the hyperbolic ball gives, and not a surface term. So the Stokes route joins the hyperbolic one: if its singular count is affine, it renormalises the volume term and supplies no A^(−1/3). Whether it is affine is the open computation, and it is beaned rather than guessed.

    (Two homonyms to keep apart, both already in the corpus: the "kite-tail" (1 + r²)^(3A−1) is a Hecke cyclotomic factor of tr_M, not a 2-cell — and its exponent 3A − 1 is affine, so #6801 already excludes it as a surface term; and bean qou-20bk with QOU/LiftingDescent/CrossedModuleSurfaceHolonomy.lean (sorry-free) has settled that no 2-dim Yekutieli object carries a non-abelian 3-body term — kites are not where any many-body term lives.)

    Why this was the right test regardless of outcome #

    A form that comes from the framework's own geometry can be iterated whichever way the fit goes: it says what the geometry predicts, and any discrepancy is information. An imported Euclidean packing cannot — it has already absorbed the discrepancy. The four tranches before this one all ended on the imported side of that line; this is the first on the other side, and it happens to fit better. That is to be read as the shape the framework's geometry predicts is a better description of the residual than the shape the SEMF imports — not as a derivation of the surface coefficient, which stays free.

    Scope #

    • Proved: S = 2π(cosh 2R − 1); V > 0 for R > 0; 2V ≤ S for all R; S/V ≥ 2; the Euclidean contrast S/V = 3/R → 0; a generic lemma that a surface fraction bounded below by a positive constant refutes HasBulk; ¬HasBulk for the hyperbolic cluster under any positive radius assignment; the per-nucleon bound; and the decay bound on f − 2. All exact, no fit.
    • NOT proved here: the limit S/V → 2 itself (the bound f − 2 ≤ 4R/(sinh 2R − 2R) is stated; that its right side tends to 0 is elementary and is checked numerically in the probe, not formalised); and the (ln A)/A asymptotic form of the correction, which needs the inverse R(A) and is left to the probe as a measured statement.
    • Assumed: that the cluster is a metric ball in H³ of curvature −1, with nucleon count extensive in volume and surface count extensive in area. This is the hyperbolic analogue of the compact-packing hypothesis the Euclidean tranches made — but it is the framework's own geometry rather than an import, which is the point.
    • NOT derived: that the substrate emits such a cluster (qou-brng's open input, unchanged), nor the radius assignment R(A); every theorem here holds for all R(A) > 0, so nothing about the assignment is used.
    • Not a fit. No coefficient is chosen against data.
