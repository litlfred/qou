          H2 — dilation invariance #

          This is what earns the measure its name. dx dy dz / z³ is not distinguished among densities by anything above; what makes it hyperbolic is that it is invariant under the isometries of H³. Dilation is the substantive one: Lebesgue scales by λ³, the density 1/z³ by 1/λ³, and the two cancel EXACTLY. Nothing about the exponent 3 is chosen for convenience -- it is forced by finrank ℝ (Fin 3 → ℝ) = 3, which is precisely why the Prod carrier could not host this proof.

          Scaling pulls a clean factor out of the density: 1/(λz)³ = (1/λ³)·(1/z³).

          H2 (dilation) — hypVolume is invariant under p ↦ r • p for r > 0.

          The λ³ from Lebesgue and the 1/λ³ from the density cancel. Proved by transporting the integral along Measure.map_addHaar_smul, which is available only because the carrier is Fin 3 → ℝ.

          H2 — horizontal translation invariance #

          The other generator of the boundary's similarity group, and the easier one: a translation (x,y,z) ↦ (x+a, y+b, z) leaves z alone, so the density does not see it at all, and Lebesgue is translation-invariant. Note this is H³'s horizontal translation only -- moving the whole picture VERTICALLY is not an isometry and is not claimed.

          H4 step 1 — the vertical integral #

          The first step of Milnor's volume computation, and the one that makes the whole approach work: integrating dz/z³ up a vertical line CONVERGES, and collapses the three-dimensional hyperbolic volume to a two-dimensional Euclidean integral over the base. See qou-5gai H4-1.

          This is stated and proved here because it is self-contained -- it needs neither the ideal-tetrahedron region (H3) nor Fubini. Assembling it into Vol = ∫∫ dA / (2(R² - d²)) is H4-2, which does.
