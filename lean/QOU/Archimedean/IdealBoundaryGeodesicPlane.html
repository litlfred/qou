    abbrev QOU.Archimedean.BdryPoint :
    Type

    A boundary point of H³. ∂H³ is the plane z = 0 (identified with ℝ × ℝ, i.e. ℂ) together with the single far point ∞ — its one-point compactification. The four ideal vertices of an ideal tetrahedron are four BdryPoints.

      def QOU.Archimedean.sarea (u v w : ℝ × ℝ) :
      ℝ

      Twice the signed area of the Euclidean triangle (u, v, w) — equivalently the 2-D cross product of v - u and w - u. It vanishes exactly when w lies on the line through u and v, and its sign says which side of that line w is on. This single scalar carries both the vertical-plane equation (= 0) and its oriented half-space (sign), so the whole vertical-face story is expressed through it.

        def QOU.Archimedean.verticalPlaneThrough (b c : ℝ × ℝ) :
        Set (Fin 3 → ℝ)

        The vertical geodesic plane through ∞, b, c (b, c finite): the Euclidean vertical plane over the line through b and c in ∂H³. A point of ℝ³ lies on it iff its horizontal projection is on that line, i.e. iff the signed area of (b, c, projection) vanishes — at ANY height, which is what "vertical" means. (This is geodesicPlaneThrough ∞ b c; the geodesic classification above is the sole cited input.)

          def QOU.Archimedean.verticalHalfSpaceThrough (b c i : ℝ × ℝ) :
          Set (Fin 3 → ℝ)

          The oriented vertical half-space cut by verticalPlaneThrough b c, on the side of the far vertical line over the finite reference point i. A point is in it iff its projection lands strictly on the same side of the line bc as i does — detected by the two signed areas having the same (nonzero) sign, i.e. positive product. In an ideal tetrahedron with a face through ∞, b, c, the interior reference i is the opposite (fourth) vertex, so this is the half-space "toward the interior".

            The oriented half-space lies on one side of its plane: on the plane the signed area is 0, so the defining strict product cannot be positive. (A sanity link between the half-space and its bounding geodesic plane, both defined from the same two vertices.)

            The geodesic faces used downstream #

            The load-bearing geodesic faces are verticalPlaneThrough / verticalHalfSpaceThrough (above) and hemisphere / hemisphereExterior (GeodesicFaceH3); idealTetrahedron (IdealTetFromVertices) is their intersection. A general geodesicPlaneThrough a b c three-boundary-point constructor was removed as unused infrastructure (nothing downstream consumed it, and its finite-triple slot was only correct in Milnor normalization). The upcoming hyperbolic-metric backfill (draining the totally-geodesic citation into a proof) rebuilds this layer directly on the metric.
