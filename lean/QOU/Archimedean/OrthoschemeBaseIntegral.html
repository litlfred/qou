    1. Λ is the primitive of −log(2 sin) #

    T1 gave Λ' = −log(2 sin t) pointwise on the OPEN interval (0,π). Getting the integral form across the closed interval is not bookkeeping: Λ' is unbounded at both endpoints, so the fundamental theorem is applied in its hasDeriv_right_of_le form (continuity on [a,b], derivative on (a,b) only), and the integrand's integrability has to be established rather than inherited from continuity.

    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.integral_neg_log_two_sin {a b : ℝ} (h0 : 0 ≤ a) (hab : a ≤ b) (hbπ : b ≤ Real.pi) :
    ∫ (t : ℝ) in a..b, -Real.log (2 * Real.sin t) = Lob b - Lob a

    Λ is the primitive of −log(2 sin):

    ∫_a^b −log(2 sin t) dt = Λ(b) − Λ(a)   for  0 ≤ a ≤ b ≤ π.

    This is the integral form of T1's hasDerivAt_Lob, and the form in which Λ is classically defined. The endpoints are included: Λ' blows up at 0 and π, so the fundamental theorem is used with the derivative required only on the open interval, and the integrability across the singularity is intervalIntegrable_log_two_sin.

    2. The trig factorization #

    The identity that makes Λ appear: the orthoscheme integrand's numerator is a product of two sines, so its log is a sum of two log sin terms.

    source
    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.cos_sq_sub_cos_sq (ψ φ : ℝ) :
    Real.cos φ ^ 2 - Real.cos ψ ^ 2 = Real.sin (ψ + φ) * Real.sin (ψ - φ)

    cos²φ − cos²ψ = sin(ψ+φ)·sin(ψ−φ).

    3. The orthoscheme value #

    Milnor's Appendix reports the value Λ(a)/2 for the orthoscheme; this is that computation, in the reduced one-dimensional form.

    source
    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.log_orthoscheme_eq {ψ φ : ℝ} (hψ : ψ < Real.pi / 2) (hφ0 : 0 ≤ φ) (hφψ : φ < ψ) :
    Real.log ((Real.cos φ ^ 2 - Real.cos ψ ^ 2) / Real.cos φ ^ 2) = Real.log (Real.sin (ψ + φ)) + Real.log (Real.sin (ψ - φ)) - 2 * Real.log (Real.cos φ)

    The three-log decomposition of the orthoscheme integrand, valid pointwise on the OPEN interval 0 ≤ φ < ψ < π/2.

    It fails at φ = ψ — there the left side is log 0 = 0 by Lean's convention while the right side is not — which is exactly why the integral identity below needs an a.e. rewrite and why the integrability statement is about Ioo.

    The three translated integrands, and their integrability. Each is a translate or reflection of log ∘ sin or log ∘ cos, both of which Mathlib integrates on any interval.

    Stated for Ioo rather than Icc/Ioc on purpose: the three-log decomposition holds pointwise only where φ < ψ strictly, and φ = ψ is where the integrand blows up. This is what makes the improper integral in integral_orthoscheme a real number rather than Lean's junk value.

    source
    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.integral_orthoscheme {ψ : ℝ} (hψ0 : 0 < ψ) (hψ : ψ < Real.pi / 2) :
    ∫ (φ : ℝ) in 0..ψ, -(1 / 4) * Real.log ((Real.cos φ ^ 2 - Real.cos ψ ^ 2) / Real.cos φ ^ 2) = Lob ψ / 2

    H4-4 (reduced) — the orthoscheme base integral is ½Λ(ψ):

    ∫₀^ψ −¼ · log( (cos²φ − cos²ψ) / cos²φ ) dφ  =  ½ Λ(ψ)   for 0 < ψ < π/2.

    The integrand is Milnor's angular integrand for the right triangle with angle ψ at the circumcentre, after the elementary radial integration. It is unbounded at φ = ψ (where the base vertex meets the circle and the fibre mass diverges — fibreMass_eq_top), so the identity is a genuine improper-integral statement, and the integrability that makes it meaningful is intervalIntegrable_log_two_sin, not continuity.

    ψ < π/2 is Milnor's acute hypothesis in local form: it is what puts cos ψ > 0 and therefore the foot of the circumcentre perpendicular at positive distance on the correct side of the edge.
