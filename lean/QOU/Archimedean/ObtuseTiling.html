    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.volume_chordLine (φ c : ℝ) :
    MeasureTheory.volume {w : ℝ × ℝ | w.1 * Real.cos φ + w.2 * Real.sin φ = c} = 0

    A chord line is null. The companion of volume_perpLine for an affine line: {w | ⟨w,u_φ⟩ = c} rather than {w | ⟨w,v_φ⟩ = 0}.

    Two observations make it a corollary rather than a new measure-theoretic argument. First, the corresponding line through the origin is already a perpLine, at the rotated angle φ + π/2:

    −⟨w, (sin(φ+π/2), −cos(φ+π/2))⟩ = −(w₁ cos φ + w₂ sin φ),

    so it vanishes exactly where the chord functional does. Second, the level set at height c is that kernel translated by c · u_φ, and volume on ℝ × ℝ is translation invariant. (Mathlib's Measure.addHaar_affineSubspace would also serve, at the cost of packaging the set as an AffineSubspace.)

    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.notMem_sub_of_mem_inscribedTriangle {R t α β γ : ℝ} {w : ℝ × ℝ} (hw : w ∈ inscribedTriangle R t α β γ) :
    w ∉ wedgeAt R (t + γ + Real.pi) (Real.pi - γ)

    A point of the triangle is never in the subtracted wedge — and this direction is STRICT, no null set needed.

    W_sub's chord condition is the strict complement of the triangle's γ-chord (see gamma_chord_of_notMem_sub), so membership in the triangle refutes it outright. This is the cheap half of T ⊆ RHS; the other half is the covering argument, which does need the edge lines.

    The covering — and why the acute sign relation does NOT survive #

    In the acute case (inscribedTriangle_subset_milnorWedges_of_ne) the covering is carried entirely by perp_sign_relation

    sin(2α)·x + sin(2β)·y + sin(2γ)·z = 0,        x = perp_t, y = perp_{t+2γ},
                                              z = perp_{t+2γ+2α}

    whose three coefficients are positive, so the all-positive and all-negative sign patterns are impossible and the six survivors are exactly the six sectors.

    With γ obtuse that argument fails, and not marginally. sin 2γ < 0 (sin_two_mul_neg_of_obtuse), so the relation reads a·x + b·y = c·z with a, b, c > 0, which forbids only (+,+,−) and (−,−,+). Of the six remaining patterns four — (+,+,+), (+,−,+), (+,−,−), (−,−,−) — put the point in neither surviving wedge. So the sign relation alone cannot close the obtuse covering: it is not that the proof needs adapting, it is that the fact it establishes is no longer sufficient.

    What replaces it is a stronger geometric input, and it makes the sign relation unnecessary rather than merely insufficient: the triangle lies inside the cone spanned by the two rays O→V₁ and O→V₂, i.e.

    w ∈ T   ⟹   perp_t(w) < 0   and   perp_{t+2γ}(w) > 0.

    That is γ obtuse saying the circumcentre has left the triangle across the γ-chord V₁V₂. With both of those in hand a single case split on the sign of perp_{t+2γ+2α} finishes: negative puts the point in W_α, positive in W_β.

    Two consequences worth stating, because they are better than the acute case rather than worse:

    • the covering needs one edge functional to be nonzero, not three, so this direction's null set is a single line rather than three;
    • neither bound needs perp_sign_relation, perp_three_angle_relation, or any disjointness — each is a two-term identity against the two chords meeting at the vertex in question.

    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.perp_start_certificate {R t α β γ : ℝ} (hsum : α + β + γ = Real.pi) (w : ℝ × ℝ) :
    (-(w.1 * Real.sin t) + w.2 * Real.cos t) * Real.sin α = Real.cos γ * (R * Real.cos β - (w.1 * Real.cos (t + 2 * γ + 2 * α + β) + w.2 * Real.sin (t + 2 * γ + 2 * α + β))) - Real.cos β * (R * Real.cos γ - (w.1 * Real.cos (t + γ) + w.2 * Real.sin (t + γ)))

    Certificate for the first edge functional, against the two chords that meet at the triangle's first vertex V₁ = R·u_t.

    Writing P = R − ⟨w,u_t⟩ and Q = perp_t(w), the shift identity ⟨w,u_{t+φ}⟩ = ⟨w,u_t⟩ cos φ + Q sin φ turns the two chord slacks into

    F := R cos γ − ⟨w,u_{t+γ}⟩   = P cos γ − Q sin γ      (the γ-chord, V₁V₂)
G := R cos β − ⟨w,u_{t−β}⟩   = P cos β + Q sin β      (the β-chord, V₃V₁)

    and eliminating P gives Q·sin(β+γ) = G cos γ − F cos β, which is the statement below once sin(β+γ) = sin α and t + 2γ + 2α + β = t − β + 2π. Note R cancels: both sides are R-free, and the identity holds for every w and every angle triple summing to π, acute or not.

    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.wedgeAt_sub_subset_alphaBeta_of_ne {R t α β γ : ℝ} (hα0 : 0 < α) (hβ0 : 0 < β) (hγ : Real.pi / 2 < γ) (hsum : α + β + γ = Real.pi) {w : ℝ × ℝ} (hw : w ∈ wedgeAt R (t + γ + Real.pi) (Real.pi - γ)) (hz : -(w.1 * Real.sin (t + (2 * γ + 2 * α))) + w.2 * Real.cos (t + (2 * γ + 2 * α)) ≠ 0) :
    w ∈ wedgeAt R (t + 2 * γ + α) α ∪ wedgeAt R (t + 2 * γ + 2 * α + β) β

    The subtracted wedge sits inside the union it is subtracted from — off the same single edge line.

    Without this the set difference (W_α ∪ W_β) \ W_sub would be arithmetically smaller than Milnor's signed sum W_α + W_β − W_sub: subtracting a set removes only the part that was there. With it, the two agree, and the volume identity can be read off by additivity rather than by a signed-measure argument.

    The proof is the two certificates read BACKWARDS. W_sub is an acute wedge — its half-angle is π − γ ∈ (0, π/2) precisely because γ is obtuse — so wedgeAt_lower / _upper give it the same two cone bounds the triangle has, perp_{t+2γ} > 0 and perp_t < 0. What it has instead of the triangle's γ-chord is the OPPOSITE inequality ⟨w,u_{t+γ}⟩ > R cos γ, and feeding that sign into

    cos γ·(α-chord slack) = cos α·(γ-chord slack) − perp_{t+2γ}·sin β
cos γ·(β-chord slack) = perp_t·sin α + cos β·(γ-chord slack)

    makes both left sides negative, hence — cos γ being negative — both chord slacks positive. So a point of W_sub satisfies the α- and β-chords even though it fails the γ-chord, which is exactly the geometry of a circumcentre that has left the triangle across one edge.

    def QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.obtuseWedges (R t α β γ : ℝ) :
    Set (ℝ × ℝ)

    Milnor's obtuse figure: the two surviving wedges, minus the one the obtuse angle turns subtractive.

    The acute milnorWedges is a union of three; here the γ-wedge is replaced by W_sub = wedgeAt R (t+γ+π) (π−γ), which sits on the far side of the γ-chord and enters with a minus sign. This is a set difference, not a signed sum: the subtracted wedge is contained in the union up to the chord line, so the arithmetic of Milnor's signed subdivision is realised set-theoretically.

      The Λ-sum on an obtuse triangle #

      The set-level tiling above becomes the volume identity by additivity. The one thing worth watching is that the subtraction never happens in ℝ≥0∞ — it is carried out in ℝ at the very end. Rather than computing Vol(W_α) + Vol(W_β) − Vol(W_sub), which would need truncated subtraction and a finiteness side-condition at every step, the argument reads the partition the other way round:

      W_α ∪ W_β  =ᵐ  T ⊔ W_sub          (disjointly)

      so Vol(W_α) + Vol(W_β) = Vol(T) + Vol(W_sub) is a statement about sums, and Vol(T) is recovered by cancelling the finite term Vol(W_sub). That the union is a.e. the disjoint union of the two is exactly what wedgeAt_sub_subset_alphaBeta_of_ne and notMem_sub_of_mem_inscribedTriangle supply: containment one way, strict disjointness the other.

      The RIGHT-ANGLED case — γ = π/2, where there is nothing to subtract #

      The two theorems above and the acute hypVolume_chimney_inscribedTriangle left exactly one slice uncovered, and this file's header used to say it "degenerates W_sub to a half-plane and the ψ < π/2 wedge machinery fails on both sides". Half right. The machinery does fail — but not into a half-plane:

      `wedgeAt R θ (π/2) = ∅`.

      Lean's tan (π/2) = 0 (division by cos (π/2) = 0, the junk value), so the wedge's second condition reads |⟨w,v_θ⟩| < 0 and no point satisfies it. That is not a defect of the junk value — it is geometrically right: W_sub's chord bound is ⟨w,u⟩ < R cos(π−γ) = 0 and its opening is a half-turn, so the half-plane it would sweep is cut away by its own chord.

      So at γ = π/2 there is nothing to subtract, which is the analytic shadow of Λ(π/2) = 0: the circumcentre sits exactly ON the hypotenuse V₁V₂ (the γ-chord is a diameter), the boundary case between inside and outside, and the two surviving wedges tile the triangle outright.

      The one step that does not survive is the γ-chord. Above it is extracted from w ∉ W_sub (gamma_chord_of_notMem_sub), and with W_sub = ∅ that hypothesis is free and carries no information. It is replaced by the SAME two certificates, used a third time: at cos γ = 0 each collapses to a single term,

      perp_{t+2γ}·sin β = cos α·(γ-chord slack)      (so `w ∈ W_α` ⟹ slack > 0)
perp_t·sin α      = −cos β·(γ-chord slack)     (so `w ∈ W_β` ⟹ slack > 0)

      and the chord comes out STRICT rather than ≤. This direction therefore needs no null set at all — unlike the obtuse case, where the ≤ of gamma_chord_of_mem_alpha is what forces volume_chordLine.

      MILNOR'S THEOREM, unconditional — and the gap in "covers the whole simplex" #

      The three cases above are stated with γ as the distinguished angle. A triple whose non-acute angle is α or β satisfies none of them as written, so "every admissible triple is covered" was, strictly, a claim about a relabelling step that had not been taken. This section takes it.

      The relabelling is exact rather than approximate, because the three vertices are literally the same points. With α + β + γ = π,

      Region(α,β,γ) at `t`   has vertices  t, t+2γ, t+2γ+2α
Region(β,γ,α) at `t+2γ`     "        t+2γ, t+2γ+2α, t+2γ+2α+2β = t+2π

      and t + 2π is t on the circle.       The whole thing rests on one observation: verticalHalfSpaceThrough b c i is a product of two signed areas sharing the edge b c, so swapping b and c negates both and changes nothing.
