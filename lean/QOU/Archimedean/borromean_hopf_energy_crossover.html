      Proposition. Let $q > 1$ be the substrate parameter and $\hbar_q = (q-1)/q$. Consider $6$ quarks (two baryon-number units) with flavor content $(\mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{d}, \mathfrak{s})^{\otimes 2}$. These $6$ quarks can be assembled in two topologically distinct configurations:

      • Borromean phase ($\mathcal{P}_B$): two Borromean baryons $\mathcal{B}_3 \cdot \mathcal{B}_3$, each with Borromean volume $V_B = 7.32772$ and inter-baryon Hopf binding.

      • Diquark phase ($\mathcal{P}_D$): three Hopf diquark pairs (Hopf diquark) $D_{[\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{d}]} \cdot D_{[\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{s}]} \cdot D_{[\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}]}$, each bound by $\mathcal{L}_H$.

      Then:

      1. Mass comparison. The Borromean phase has mass

      $$ M_B #

      2 \cdot \frac{V_B}{\hbar_q^2} #

      \frac{2 \times 7.32772}{((q-1)/q)^2} #

      \frac{14.6554 q^2}{(q-1)^2}. $$

      The diquark phase has mass from crossing energy only (Hopf link volume is zero):

      $$ M_D #

      3 \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}(q + q^{-1}) #

      q + q^{-1} \approx 2.011 \text{Skein units}. $$

      Since $\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{L}_H) = 0$, the diquark mass is purely from crossing energy, not from hyperbolic volume. At any $q > 1$: $M_D \ll M_B$.
