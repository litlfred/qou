    Strand assignment #

    For deuterium ℵ(1,1) = B₃(u_r, u_g, d_b) ⋈ e⁻ ⋈ B₃(u_r', d_g', d_b'): strand 0: u_r (proton up quark, red) strand 1: u_g (proton up quark, green) strand 2: d_b (proton down quark, blue) strand 3: e⁻ (electron) strand 4: u_r' (neutron up quark, red) strand 5: d_g' (neutron down quark, green) strand 6: d_b' (neutron down quark, blue)

    Generator σ_i = crossing between strand i and strand i+1: σ₀: u_r — u_g (proton uu link) σ₁: u_g — d_b (proton ud link) σ₂: d_b — e⁻ (proton-electron Hopf) σ₃: e⁻ — u_r' (electron-neutron Hopf) σ₄: u_r' — d_g' (neutron ud link) σ₅: d_g' — d_b' (neutron dd link)

    I₁: σ₀-extension (leading quark link) #

    Operation: prepend σ₀ to any word Physical: u_r links to u_g (proton's same-flavor quark pair) Ratio: +0.3146 (universal across all families) Energy: Hecke cost when σ₀² forms ≈ 0.2333 Degree: 0

    Every NF family has this extension with a consistent ratio. This is the first step of building the proton's uu pair.

              I₄: 𝔫₁ neutron tail (electron-Borromean-linked neutron) #

              Word: σ₃ · σ₄ · σ₃ · σ₅ Physical decomposition: σ₃: e⁻ links to u_r' (electron Hopf to neutron's up quark) σ₄: u_r' links to d_g' (neutron internal ud crossing) σ₃: e⁻ links to u_r' AGAIN (Borromean: electron wraps u_r') σ₅: d_g' links to d_b' (neutron internal dd crossing)

              This is NOT the neutron's Borromean core alone — it's the electron Borromean-linked through the neutron's u quark, with the neutron's dd pair completed.

              𝔫₁ = (e⁻ ⋈B u_r') ⋈ T{2,1}(d_g'–d_b')

              where ⋈_B means Borromean-linked (σ₃ appears twice around σ₄).

              66 of 276 families (78% of coefficient weight) end with 𝔫₁. The remaining 210 families end with incomplete versions: σ₄·σ₃·σ₅ (𝔫₁ missing first electron wrap) σ₃·σ₄·σ₃ (𝔫₁ missing dd link) σ₃·σ₄·σ₅ (𝔫₁ with broken Borromean)

              These incomplete tails are the INPUT SLOTS for the neutron's individual bonds.

                  Higher-degree ideals (for magic shell closures) #

                  At magic numbers, the torus knot operators enter: Shell 1 (magic 2): T_{2,3} (degree 1, genus 1) Shell 2 (magic 8): T_{2,5} (degree 2, genus 2) Shell 3 (magic 20): T_{2,7} (degree 3, genus 3) Shell 4 (magic 28): T_{3,5} (degree 4, genus 4) Shell 5 (magic 50): T_{2,9} (degree 4, genus 4) Shell 6 (magic 82): T_{3,4} (degree 3, genus 3)

                  For beta decay across shell boundaries (e.g. ⁶⁰Co: neutron shell 5 → proton shell 4), the parent shell unbinds via T_{2,9}⁻¹ and the daughter rebinds via T_{3,5}. These create their own families analogous to the 66/210 split, but with torus knot tails instead of 𝔫₁.

                  I₅: Protium completeness (whole-atom quotient) #

                  Protium has 4 NF terms. When the per-term quotient engine substitutes each independently, it produces ℵ₁ⁿ with n = 1..4 counting how many of the 4 terms were matched.

                  ℵ₁⁴ = 4/4 components present (complete protium) ℵ₁³ = 3/4 (one bond missing) ℵ₁² = 2/4 (two bonds missing) ℵ₁¹ = 1/4 (three bonds missing)

                  This is an artifact of per-term substitution. The correct quotient treats protium as ONE object:

                  ℵ₁ = −0.010·𝔥₁ − 0.067·𝔲₀·𝔥₁ + 0.196·YB(𝔲₀·𝔥₁) + 1.286·B₃⋈T_{2,1}(e⁻)

                  All 4 terms together = one protium. Partial matches (ℵ₁³ etc.) are structural defects — one or more bonds absent from the complete atom. These defects are universal (same across all atoms) and should be factored out as separate ideal generators.

                  The ℵ₁ⁿ count distribution in deuterium: ℵ₁⁴: 24 terms, 69% weight — complete protium (THE ATOM) ℵ₁³: 92 terms, 26% weight — protium missing one bond (INPUT) ℵ₁²: 176 terms, 3% weight — protium missing two bonds ℵ₁¹: 144 terms, 0.5% weight — protium missing three bonds

                    I₆: Structural defects (missing bonds) #

                    When an atom's NF contains a PARTIAL version of a known sub-structure (missing one specific bond), that defect is a universal structural operation with a known energy cost.

                    Each defect is identified by WHICH bond is missing:

                    defect(σ₀) = missing u_r–u_g link (proton uu pair incomplete) defect(σ₅) = missing d_g'–d_b' link (neutron dd pair incomplete) defect(B) = Borromean broken (σ₃ appears once, not twice) defect(e⁻) = electron bridge absent (σ₂·σ₃ missing)

                    These defects appear at EVERY fibre level (ℵ₁³, ℵ₁², ℵ₁¹) with consistent coefficient ratios. They are the "input slots" that describe what the atom is receptive to.

                    In the deuterium NF: "atom without u_r link" → coeff −0.340 (defect(σ₀)) "atom with broken Borromean"→ coeff −0.340 (defect(B)) "atom minus dd link" → coeff −0.617 (defect(σ₅)) "bare neutron slot" → coeff −0.483 (defect(𝔫₁))

                              I₇: Electron bridge (σ₂·σ₃·σ₂ Borromean bridge) #

                              The electron (strand 3) acts as a BRIDGE between proton and neutron. It is NOT visible as T_{2,3} — its trefoil structure was Hecke-reduced during construction.

                              Instead, the electron appears as bridge crossings: σ₂: d_b (proton) → e⁻ σ₃: e⁻ → u_r' (neutron)

                              The dominant bridge pattern is σ₂·σ₃·σ₂ (Borromean bridge): d_b wraps around e⁻ twice with u_r' between.

                              In deuterium: 83% of terms involve σ₂ or σ₃ (the electron). Only 17% bypass the electron entirely (direct quark-quark).

                              The electron bridge is a universal structural operation — every atom with Z ≥ 1 has at least one electron mediating nucleon interactions.
