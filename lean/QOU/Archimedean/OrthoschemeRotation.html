        2. Rotation preserves the integrand #

        The base integrand 1/(2(R² − x² − y²)) sees the point only through x² + y², which is exactly what a rotation fixes. This is why rotation invariance of the base integral holds for THIS integrand; it is not a formal consequence of measure-preservation alone.

        3. The wedge at an arbitrary axis #

        Cartesian definition, rotation proved.

        source
        def QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.wedgeAt (R θ ψ : ℝ) :
        Set (ℝ × ℝ)

        The wedge with axis direction θ — the open isoceles triangle with apex at the circumcentre, apex angle 2ψ, and its axis of symmetry pointing in direction θ.

        Cut out by dot products against the rotated frame u = (cos θ, sin θ), v = (−sin θ, cos θ), so that wedgeAt R 0 ψ is wedge R ψ on the nose.
