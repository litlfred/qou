    The signed-area reduction — the one geometric identity everything rests on #

    source
    theorem QOU.Archimedean.sarea_circlePoint_unit (u ψ : ℝ) (w : ℝ × ℝ) :
    sarea (HyperbolicVolume.circlePoint 1 u) (HyperbolicVolume.circlePoint 1 (u + 2 * ψ)) w = 2 * Real.sin ψ * (Real.cos ψ - (w.1 * Real.cos (u + ψ) + w.2 * Real.sin (u + ψ)))

    The signed area of a unit-circle chord against a point is 2 sin ψ (cos ψ − w·nθ).

    For two points of the unit circle at central angles u and u + 2ψ, the chord's perpendicular-bisector direction is u + ψ and it subtends half-angle ψ. Testing any w against the chord (its signed area) is 2 sin ψ times cos ψ − w·(cos(u+ψ), sin(u+ψ)) — i.e. 2 sin ψ times exactly the functional the chord half-plane {w · (cos(u+ψ), sin(u+ψ)) < cos ψ} compares to 0. This is the bridge from the vertex-defined sarea half-spaces (File A1) to the chord half-planes (OrthoschemeTiling): it lets File A3 derive the chords from the faces.

    The hemisphere face, oriented toward ∞ #

    source
    def QOU.Archimedean.hemisphereExterior (R : ℝ) :
    Set (Fin 3 → ℝ)

    The exterior of the hemisphere of radius R — its ∞-side. A point is in it iff it sits strictly above the spherical cap z = √(R² − x² − y²); because the far vertex ∞ is reached by going upward, this is the side of the geodesic plane hemisphere R that contains ∞. It is definitionally the floor condition of the chimney (IdealTetrahedronRegion), which is what makes the File-A3 identification land on the nose.

      The ideal tetrahedron #

      source
      def QOU.Archimedean.idealTetrahedron (P₁ P₂ P₃ : ℝ × ℝ) :
      Set (Fin 3 → ℝ)

      The Milnor-normalized ideal tetrahedron with far vertex ∞ and three finite vertices P₁, P₂, P₃ on the unit circle, as the intersection of its four geodesic half-spaces, each oriented toward the interior.

      Three vertical faces (each through ∞ and two finite vertices, oriented toward the opposite vertex) and one hemisphere face (the unit hemisphere through the three finite vertices, oriented toward ∞). This is an independent object: it is defined from the four ideal vertices via File-A1 geodesic half-spaces, with no reference to chordHalfplane or inscribedTriangle. File A3 proves it equals the chimney.

      The three finite vertices are taken on the unit circle (Milnor normalization), so the hemisphere face is hemisphere 1; see vertices_on_unit_hemisphere. For finite vertices off the unit circle the hemisphere face would be their circumscribed hemisphere — the deferred general case flagged in File A1.
