    def QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicMetric.IsDHIsometry.comp {φ ψ : E → E} (hψ : IsDHIsometry ψ) (hφ : IsDHIsometry φ) :
    IsDHIsometry (ψ ∘ φ)

    Composition of dH-isometries is a dH-isometry. If ψ and φ are each conformal-with-the-same-factor-on-the-height, so is ψ ∘ φ: its Fréchet derivative is the composite Dψ(φ p) ∘ Dφ(p) (chain rule), its conformal factor is the PRODUCT lam_ψ(φ p) · lam_φ(p) (norms scale by the product), and its height scales by the SAME product (hheight composed). So the line element ‖v‖/z — preserved pointwise by each factor — is preserved by the composite. This lets a finite composition of the WALL-1 generators and a boundary inversion be handled as a single IsDHIsometry.

        Coordinate helpers for the boundary-inversion computation #

        The general two-point normalization uses a boundary inversion; verifying that the two interior points land on a common vertical line reduces to two elementary coordinate identities on E = EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3):

        • distSq_coords — dist p q ² = Σⱼ (pⱼ − qⱼ)², the Pythagorean expansion, so a Euclidean-sphere condition ‖p − O‖ = R becomes polynomial in the coordinates;
        • inversion_coord — the j-th coordinate of inversion c R p is (R/dist p c)²·(pⱼ − cⱼ) + cⱼ, extracted from the +ᵥ/•ᵥ definition exactly as HyperbolicIsometryInversion does for the height coordinate.

        The boundary-inversion horizontal-collapse lemma #

        The geometric heart of the general case. Two interior points whose horizontal parts are (possibly different) multiples α of a common unit horizontal direction u and which both lie on a Euclidean sphere centred at O = c·u on the boundary have, after the boundary inversion at the near ideal endpoint ξ = (c−R)·u, coordinates 0 and 1 collapsed to the SAME value K·uⱼ, K = (c−R) + ρ²/(2R) — INDEPENDENT of the point. A later horizontal translation by −K·u then lands both on the z-axis.

        The mechanism is the single algebraic identity ‖p − ξ‖² = 2R·(α − (c−R)) valid for p on the sphere: the inversion factor ρ²/‖p−ξ‖² times the horizontal displacement (α−(c−R))·u telescopes to ρ²/(2R)·u, erasing the point's own α.

        WALL-2, general (distinct-horizontal) case — DERIVED #

        The genuine content of bean qou-0a0z. When x, y have DISTINCT horizontal parts, a three-map composition carries both onto the z-axis:

        • T1 the horizontal translation p ↦ p + (zpt x₂ − x) (as in the vertical case) sends x to (0,0,x₂) and y to w + (0,0,y₂) with w the horizontal difference, d = ‖w‖ > 0;
        • T2 the boundary inversion at the near ideal endpoint ξ = (c−R)·u (u = w/d, c = (d²+y₂²−x₂²)/(2d), R = √(c²+x₂²)), which by invImage_horiz_collapse collapses coords 0,1 of BOTH image points to the same K·uⱼ;
        • T3 the horizontal translation by −K·u, zeroing both horizontal coordinates.

        Every factor is a derived dH-isometry (horizTranslationIsom, boundaryInversionIsom); IsDHIsometry.comp assembles the composite, and positive height is inherited from IsDHIsometry.maps.
