    QOU.Archimedean.lepton_tower_bend_invariant — the screening invariant for any generation bridge #

    Companion to lepton_continuant_binet / lepton_continuant_positivity, serving bean qou-htk4 (the successor to qou-lsp9). Numerical twin: lepton_tower_bend_screening.py.

    The problem this solves #

    qou-lsp9 closed one candidate bridge at a time — first net, then every positive linear readout of H₂(q). Candidate-by-candidate refutation does not scale, and worse, it invites fitting: a candidate f plugged into m_w = C · g^w · f(w)^p always has three free parameters against the two observed lepton ratios, so something can always be made to fit.

    The fix is to work with a quantity the free parameters cannot touch.

    The bend #

    For a tower x sampled at three consecutive generation windings, define

      bend x w = x(w+2)² / (x(w) · x(w+4)).

    bend_of_scaled_geometric_pow is the point of the whole file:

      m_w = C · g^w · f(w)^p     ⟹     bend m w = (bend f w)^p

    C and g cancel exactly. The overall mass scale and the per-generation geometric factor — the two knobs that make every candidate look adjustable — drop out of the bend identically. Only the exponent survives, so a candidate readout is screened by a single number with a single free parameter, not three.

    What it buys immediately #

    bend_of_geometric: a readout that is exactly geometric in the winding has bend = 1. Combined with the transport law, geometric_readout_forces_unit_bend says any such readout forces bend m = 1 for every C, g and p simultaneously — so a tower whose measured bend is not 1 excludes that entire three-parameter class outright, with no fine-tuning argument and no appeal to how large p would have to be.

    The measured lepton bend is m_μ² / (m_e · m_τ) ≈ 12.295, nowhere near 1. That single number is what kills every exponential-type invariant of T(2,n) at once — the Jones polynomial and the Markov trace included, since in the two-dimensional H₂(q) those are linear readouts and hence geometric up to O(q⁻²ⁿ) by linFunctionalK_two_step.

    bend_of_linear covers the other standard family: a readout linear in the winding has bend = (w+2)²/(w(w+4)), which at the lepton windings (3,5,7) is 25/21 ≈ 1.19 — also nowhere near 12.295. Between them these two lemmas cover essentially every classical knot invariant of T(2,n): each is either polynomial in the winding (bend near 1 from below) or exponential in it (bend exactly 1).

    How to use it #

    Screen a candidate bridge by computing one number. A viable parameter-free bridge needs bend f ≈ 12.295 at p = 1, or ≈ 3.51 at p = 2, or ≈ 2.31 at p = 3. Anything with bend near 1 requires a large exponent and is a fit, not a derivation.

      The bend transports through the whole bridge class, and the two fitting knobs cancel.

      If m_w = C · g^w · f(w)^p then bend m = (bend f)^p: the overall scale C and the geometric factor g drop out identically, leaving the exponent as the only surviving freedom.

      This is what makes the bend a screening tool rather than one more quantity to fit. A candidate readout f is tested by comparing bend f against the measured bend m, with two of the three free parameters already eliminated.
