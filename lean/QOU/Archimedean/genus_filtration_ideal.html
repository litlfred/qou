      Manuscript statement (full body):

      The genus of the moduli surface is not assumed --- it is determined by the Iwahori--Hecke algebra. The QOU axioms force $g = 4$ through a completeness condition on the irreducible representations of $H_2(q) \oplus H_3(q)$, and the genus-filtration ideal $\mathfrak{I}_4 = (u^5)$ is the algebraic obstruction that kills $T_{2,p}$ for all primes $p \geq 11$.

      Part 0: the genus is determined by the algebra #

      Proposition (Genus from Hecke completeness). Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a QOU with $G = \mathrm{SU}_q(2)$. The braiding on $V_1^{\otimes n}$ gives the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$. The relevant strand counts are $n = 2$ and $n = 3$:

      • $H_2(q)$ has $2$ irreducible representations (partitions of $2$: $(2)$ trivial, $(1,1)$ sign).
      • $H_3(q)$ has $3$ irreducible representations (partitions of $3$: $(3)$, $(1,1,1)$, $(2,1)$).
      • Total: $5$ irreps.

      At genus $g$, the embeddable torus knots $T_{r,s}$ with $(r-1)(s-1) \leq 2g$ and $\gcd(r,s) = 1$ are the ones whose Jones polynomials can be evaluated on $\Sigma_g$. Completeness (cohomological embedding) requires that these evaluations span all $5$ irrep characters: the evaluation matrix $M_{ij} = \chi_{\lambda_j}(\sigma_i)$ must have rank $5$.

      The strand counts $m \geq 4$ contribute no new knots: at genus $g$, the constraint $(m-1)(s-1) \leq 2g$ with $m \geq 4$ and $m < s$, $\gcd(m,s) = 1$, gives $s \leq 2g/(m-1) + 1$, which for $m = 4$ yields $T_{4,3} = T_{3,4}$ (already counted) and for $m \geq 5$ yields no valid $(m, s)$ pairs with $m < s$. Therefore $H_2(q) \oplus H_3(q)$ is the complete Hecke algebra of the QOU.
