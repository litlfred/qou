    1. Reflection in the x-axis #

    The mirror symmetry that pairs the two halves of a wedge. It is used twice: to identify the lower half of the wedge with a preimage of orthoBase, and to transport the integral across. Both need it to be MEASURE-PRESERVING, which is where volume's neg-invariance on ℝ enters.

      2. The wedge, and its split by the sign of y #

      The definition is Cartesian, as orthoBase's is, and for the same reason: a polar definition would assume the description the computation consumes.

      The isoceles wedge — the OPEN Euclidean triangle with apex at the origin (the circumcentre), apex angle 2ψ, and its two far vertices (R cos ψ, ± R sin ψ) on the circle of radius R.

          3. The two halves carry equal mass #

          The mirror image of orthoBase has the same base integral, because the integrand depends on y only through y² and reflection preserves the measure. Proved, not waved at.

          source

          5. H4-5b — the wedge value #

          source
          theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.wedge_subset_disk {R ψ : ℝ} (hψ0 : 0 < ψ) (hψ : ψ < Real.pi / 2) (w : ℝ × ℝ) :
          w ∈ wedge R ψ → w.1 ^ 2 + w.2 ^ 2 < R ^ 2

          The wedge lies strictly inside the disk.

          |y| < x tan ψ and x < R cos ψ give x² + y² < x²(1 + tan²ψ) = x²/cos²ψ < R². Its two far vertices are ON the circle, but the wedge is open.

          No 0 < R hypothesis: for R ≤ 0 the wedge is empty (0 < x < R cos ψ is unsatisfiable) and the statement holds vacuously.
