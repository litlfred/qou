      Manuscript statement (full body):

      Parity is violated by the weak interaction if and only if $q \neq 1$.

      Origin: Hecke eigenvalue asymmetry #

      The parity violation follows directly from the categorical origin of the crossing energy (crossing energy): the Hecke generator $\sigma = P \circ R$ has eigenvalues $(q, -q^{-1})$ on $S^{2} V \oplus \Lambda^{2} V$, and $\lvert q \rvert \neq \lvert {-q^{-1}} \rvert$ for $q \neq 1$. A positive crossing $L_{+}$ carries the eigenvalue $q$; a negative crossing $L_{-}$ carries $-q^{-1}$. Parity $\mathcal{P}$ exchanges $L_{+} \leftrightarrow L_{-}$ but does not change $q$ --- hence $\mathcal{P}$-invariance forces $q = -q^{-1}$, i.e.\ $q^{2} = -1$, impossible on the substrate ray. The sign-asymmetry of the eigenvalue pair (inherited from $P|_{\Lambda^{2} V} = -\mathrm{id}$) is the algebraic source of P-violation; it is not a physical postulate added to the framework.

      The detailed Skein derivation below rederives the same conclusion from the Skein relation; both are consequences of the same eigenvalue structure.

      Skein derivation #

      The parity operation $\mathcal{P}$ on a link is the mirror: reverse all crossings ($L_+ \leftrightarrow L_-$). In the braid group $B_n$, this is the involution $\sigma_i \mapsto \sigma_i^{-1}$.

      The Skein relation under parity.

      The Skein relation at a single crossing:

      $$ q [L_+] - q^{-1} [L_-] #

      (q^{1/2} - q^{-1/2}) [L_0] $$

      Apply the mirror $\mathcal{P}$: $L_+ \mapsto L_-$, $L_- \mapsto L_+$, $L_0 \mapsto \mathcal{P}(L_0)$. The mirrored Skein relation reads:

      $$ q [L_-] - q^{-1} [L_+] #

      (q^{1/2} - q^{-1/2}) [\mathcal{P}(L_0)] $$

      Rearranging:

      $$ q^{-1} [L_+] - q [L_-] #

      -(q^{1/2} - q^{-1/2}) [\mathcal{P}(L_0)] $$

      Compare with the original:

      $$ q [L_+] - q^{-1} [L_-] #

      (q^{1/2} - q^{-1/2}) [L_0] $$

      These are the same equation if and only if $q = q^{-1}$, i.e., $q = 1$.
