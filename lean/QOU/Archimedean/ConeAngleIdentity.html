    Cone-angle bookkeeping at an interior vertex (Rivin, Lemma 1.2) #

    Bean qou-0vhl. Target #3 of the Rivin math/9907032 combinatorial-optimization analysis: the cone angle at an interior vertex of a Euclidean cone triangulation is bookkept against the exterior dihedral angles at the edges incident to it.

    The finite model #

    At an interior vertex v of a Euclidean cone surface, the triangles of its star are indexed by a finite set corners. Developing the star into the plane, each incident triangle i contributes an interior angle interiorAngle i at v, and the cone angle Θ(v) is the sum of those corner angles:

    Θ(v) = ∑_{i ∈ corners} interiorAngle i.

    Around an interior vertex the incident triangles and the incident edges are in cyclic bijection (edge e_i sits between two consecutive triangles), so both are indexed by the same corners. Each incident edge carries an exterior dihedral angle; under the flat development this is π minus the interior contribution at that edge:

    exteriorDihedral i = π − interiorAngle i.

    These two are the geometric inputs, carried as explicit hypotheses in the VertexStar structure (cone_eq, flat_rel). Everything below is derived from them by Finset algebra — no hidden geometry.

    The identity #

    Summing exteriorDihedral i = π − interiorAngle i over the n = corners.card incident corners and substituting Θ = ∑ interiorAngle:

    ∑ exteriorDihedral i = n·π − Θ, equivalently Θ + ∑ exteriorDihedral i = n·π.

    This is Rivin's Lemma 1.2 as an affine bookkeeping identity: the cone angle and the total exterior dihedral around v are complementary in n·π, where n is the incidence count. The genuine geometric content (Θ = Σ exterior dihedral) appears in its calibrated / curvature form; the general identity is this affine one, and it is the derivable statement.

    Why this is not vacuous #

    The conclusion Θ + ∑ exteriorDihedral = n·π is false if either the π-offset in flat_rel is wrong (replace π by any other constant c and the right side would be n·c, contradicting a concrete instance) or the incidence count n = corners.card is wrong (drop or add a corner and the multiple of π changes). Both hypotheses are load-bearing; the statement holds by Finset arithmetic, not by rfl or unfolding. A concrete falsifier is given in sum_exteriorDihedral_ne_of_bad_offset at the end of the file: a two-corner star whose flat_rel uses the offset π/2 instead of π violates the identity, so the identity genuinely depends on the offset.

    ℝ is used because angles are real (§7c: archimedean base ring is justified only because the objects — angles — are genuinely real). The pure combinatorial skeleton is factored out as sum_pi_sub_eq over a generic additive group, so the ring choice is confined to the geometric wrapper.

    theorem QOU.Archimedean.sum_pi_sub_eq {ι : Type u_1} {R : Type u_2} [AddCommGroup R] (s : Finset ι) (c : R) (f g : ι → R) (hg : ∀ i ∈ s, g i = c - f i) :
    ∑ i ∈ s, g i = s.card • c - ∑ i ∈ s, f i

    Pure Finset skeleton. Over any additive commutative group, if g i = c - f i on a finite index set s, then ∑ g = s.card • c - ∑ f.

    This is the offset-and-count bookkeeping with no geometry: c plays the role of π, f the interior angles, g the exterior dihedrals. It is non-trivial — it fails if the offset c is replaced inconsistently — and is the engine behind the geometric identity below.

    structure QOU.Archimedean.VertexStar (ι : Type u_1) :
    Type u_1

    The star of an interior vertex of a Euclidean cone triangulation.

    corners indexes the incident triangles (equivalently, the incident edges, in cyclic bijection). interiorAngle i is the corner angle of triangle i at the vertex; exteriorDihedral i is the exterior dihedral at the corresponding edge; coneAngle is the total angle Θ(v) around the vertex.

    The two geometric facts are carried as fields:

    • cone_eq — the cone angle is the sum of the corner angles;
    • flat_rel — the exterior dihedral is π minus the interior contribution.
    • corners : Finset ι

      Incident triangles / edges of the vertex star.

    • interiorAngle : ι → ℝ

      Corner (interior) angle at the vertex contributed by each incident triangle.

    • exteriorDihedral : ι → ℝ

      Exterior dihedral angle at each incident edge.

    • coneAngle : ℝ

      Total cone angle Θ(v) around the vertex.

    • cone_eq : self.coneAngle = ∑ i ∈ self.corners, self.interiorAngle i

      Cone angle = sum of the corner angles.

    • flat_rel (i : ι) : i ∈ self.corners → self.exteriorDihedral i = Real.pi - self.interiorAngle i

      Flat relation: exterior dihedral = π − interior contribution.

      Falsifier: the identity genuinely needs the π-offset #

      The following witnesses that the affine identity depends on the offset being π. We build a two-corner star with corner angles π/2 each (so cone angle π), but with exteriorDihedral defined by the wrong offset π/2 − interiorAngle instead of π − interiorAngle. That star does not satisfy flat_rel — which is exactly the point — but if one nonetheless summed its (wrong) exterior dihedrals and its cone angle, the complementary identity = 2·π would be false; the sum is 0, not 2π. Hence the offset in flat_rel is load-bearing, and coneAngle_add_sum_exteriorDihedral is not a tautology.

      source
      theorem QOU.Archimedean.offset_is_load_bearing :
      have corners := Finset.univ; have interiorAngle := fun (x : Fin 2) => Real.pi / 2; have exteriorBad := fun (x : Fin 2) => Real.pi / 2 - Real.pi / 2; have coneAngle := ∑ i ∈ corners, interiorAngle i; coneAngle + ∑ i ∈ corners, exteriorBad i ≠ corners.card • Real.pi

      A concrete instance showing the π-offset is load-bearing: with a π/2 offset the complementary sum is π, not 2·π, so the identity is falsifiable.
