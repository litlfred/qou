    Where Milnor's sign enters the corpus's own algebra #

    QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.perp_sign_relation (OrthoschemeTiling.lean) states the single linear relation among the three edge functionals, with coefficients sin 2α, sin 2β, sin 2γ. It is proved from α + β + γ = π alone — it carries no acuteness hypothesis — so it holds verbatim in the obtuse case. What acuteness buys is only the positivity of those three coefficients, and its docstring says so: "all POSITIVE for acute angles. That positivity is the whole covering argument."

    The lemmas below locate the failure exactly: when γ is obtuse the relation is unchanged and exactly one coefficient turns negative. So the obtuse case does not need a new relation, only a covering argument run against a different sign pattern — and that lone minus is Milnor's.

    The missing chord helper: a wedge fits under the chord of any SHRUNK #

    half-angle, in the correspondingly rotated direction

    wedgeAt_lt_chord_next / _prev place a wedge under the chords of its two NEIGHBOURS, at directions θ ± ψ ± ψ'. The obtuse decomposition needs something they cannot give: the chord of a SUB-angle of the wedge's own, at direction θ − δ. Solving θ − ψ − ψ' = θ − δ forces ψ' = δ − ψ ≤ 0, and both existing lemmas require 0 < ψ' — which is why this step has no existing helper.

    The statement: for w in the wedge of half-angle ψ about θ, and any 0 ≤ δ ≤ ψ,

    ⟨w, u_{θ−δ}⟩  <  R cos (ψ − δ).

    Sharp at δ = 0 (the wedge's own chord) and at δ = ψ, where the bound R is attained in the limit at the wedge's far vertex on that side.

    The certificate is the same shape as the two existing chord lemmas, and it is simpler than theirs: writing S = ⟨w,u_θ⟩ and P = ⟨w,v_{θ−ψ}⟩,

    cos ψ · (R cos(ψ−δ) − ⟨w,u_{θ−δ}⟩) = cos(ψ−δ) · (R cos ψ − S) + P · sin δ,

    which is a pure ring identity once the angle-differences are expanded — no Pythagorean rewrite is needed, unlike wedgeAt_lt_chord_next/_prev. Both summands on the right are non-negative (the first strictly), the first from the wedge's own chord bound and the second from wedgeAt_lower.

    Coapical cone containment, via a general three-angle perp relation #

    The RHS ⊆ T direction needs the γ-chord, which cannot be proved directly (γ obtuse puts R cos γ < 0, outside every chord helper's range) and must be extracted from w ∉ W_sub instead. That in turn needs W_α's cone to sit inside W_sub's, i.e. perp_t(w) < 0 for w ∈ W_α — while wedgeAt_upper supplies only perp_{t+2γ+2α}(w) < 0, an angle 2β away.

    perp_sign_relation is the special case of the identity below at the triangle's own three angles. Stated generally, it closes the gap: any THREE perp functionals on the plane satisfy one linear relation, and here two of the three coefficients are positive with known signs on their functionals.

    source
    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.perp_three_angle_relation (φ p q : ℝ) (w : ℝ × ℝ) :
    Real.sin q * (-(w.1 * Real.sin (φ - p)) + w.2 * Real.cos (φ - p)) - Real.sin (p + q) * (-(w.1 * Real.sin φ) + w.2 * Real.cos φ) + Real.sin p * (-(w.1 * Real.sin (φ + q)) + w.2 * Real.cos (φ + q)) = 0

    The three-angle perp relation. Three linear functionals on a two-dimensional space satisfy one relation; written at directions φ − p, φ, φ + q its coefficients are sin q, −sin(p+q), sin p. perp_sign_relation is this identity at the triangle's own angles.

    theorem QOU.Archimedean.HyperbolicVolume.wedgeAt_alpha_perp_upper_of_obtuse {R t α β γ : ℝ} (hα0 : 0 < α) (hβ0 : 0 < β) (hγ : Real.pi / 2 < γ) (hsum : α + β + γ = Real.pi) (w : ℝ × ℝ) :
    w ∈ wedgeAt R (t + 2 * γ + α) α → -(w.1 * Real.sin (t + 2 * Real.pi)) + w.2 * Real.cos (t + 2 * Real.pi) < 0

    W_α lies inside W_sub's cone — the upper-edge half, which is the part wedgeAt_upper cannot reach.

    For w ∈ wedgeAt R (t+2γ+α) α the wedge's own edges give perp_{t+2γ} > 0 (lower) and perp_{t+2γ+2α} < 0 (upper). W_sub's upper edge sits at t + 2π, a further 2β on. The three-angle relation at φ = t+2γ+2α, p = 2α, q = 2β reads

    sin(2α)·perp_{t+2π} = sin(2α+2β)·perp_{t+2γ+2α} − sin(2β)·perp_{t+2γ},

    whose right side is (positive)·(negative) − (positive)·(positive) < 0. All three sines are positive because α, β and α+β are acute — which is exactly what γ obtuse buys.

    The γ-chord, extracted from w ∉ W_sub #

    The triangle's third chord cannot be proved for a point of W_α or W_β directly: γ obtuse puts R cos γ < 0, outside every chord helper's range, and in fact W_α ⊄ chordHalfplane R (t+γ) γ — points of W_α near the circumcentre have ⟨w,u_{t+γ}⟩ ≈ 0, which is not < R cos γ < 0. Those points are exactly the ones the subtraction removes.

    The key observation is that W_sub's chord condition is the strict complement of the triangle's γ-chord. Since θ_s = t+γ+π and ψ_s = π−γ, that condition reads −⟨w,u_{t+γ}⟩ < −R cos γ, i.e. ⟨w,u_{t+γ}⟩ > R cos γ. So a point already inside W_sub's CONE fails to be in W_sub exactly when the γ-chord holds — with ≤ rather than <, the equality case being the chord line itself, which is null.
