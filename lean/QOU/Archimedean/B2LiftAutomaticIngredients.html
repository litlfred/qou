              theorem QOU.Archimedean.B2LiftAutomaticIngredients.weil_galois_compose (v : HodgeWeilRealStructure.H1) :
              HodgeWeilRealStructure.cWeil (HodgeWeilRealStructure.FGal v) = HodgeWeilRealStructure.conjV v ∧ HodgeWeilRealStructure.FGal (HodgeWeilRealStructure.cWeil v) = HodgeWeilRealStructure.conjV v

              c ∘ F = F ∘ c = conjV — the Weil real structure and the Galois real structure differ exactly by complex conjugation (the standard c = conj ∘ F relation for a real variety). Both compositions strip the SWAP and leave pure coordinate conjugation. This is what makes the antilinear c, not the linear F, the Hodge conjugation: they are conjugate-related, not equal.

              theorem QOU.Archimedean.B2LiftAutomaticIngredients.c_fixed_iff_real_form (v : HodgeWeilRealStructure.H1) :
              HodgeWeilRealStructure.cWeil v = v ↔ v.snd = HodgeWeilRealStructure.conjPiece v.fst

              Fix(c) = {(w, w̄)} — the complete real-form characterization. A vector is c-fixed iff its H^{0,1} part is the conjugate of its H^{1,0} part. The existing c_fixes_real_form proved only ⊇ (every (w, w̄) is fixed); this is the iff, so Fix(c) is exactly the Weil real form H¹(ℝ), dim_ℝ = 8.

              source
              theorem QOU.Archimedean.B2LiftAutomaticIngredients.FGal_fixed_iff (v : HodgeWeilRealStructure.H1) :
              HodgeWeilRealStructure.FGal v = v ↔ v.snd = v.fst

              Fix(F) = {(a, a)} — the Galois fixed locus is the diagonal, NOT the real form {(w, w̄)}. A vector is F-fixed iff its two Hodge parts are equal. This distinguishes the two real structures at the level of fixed loci: c-fixed means H^{0,1} = conj(H^{1,0}) (c_fixed_iff_real_form); F-fixed means H^{0,1} = H^{1,0}. Different subspaces — the antilinear/linear distinction has fixed-locus content.
