      The substrate parameter $q$ is the variable of the nuclear Skein polynomial. The transfer matrix product computes this polynomial in factored form without expansion.

      Proposition. Let $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ be a nucleus with $A = Z + N$ nucleons. The Markov trace (factorization theorem)

      $$ P_{\mathcal{N}}(q) := \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Markov}}!\bigl( \mathcal{N}(Z, N) \bigr) #

      \prod_{i=0}^{A-2} \hat{V}_i(q) $$

      is a polynomial in $q^{1/2}$ of degree $\leq A^2 - 1$. Each factor

      $$ \hat{V}_i(q) = z(q) \cdot a_i(q) + b_i(q) $$

      is a rational function in $q^{1/2}$, where $a_i(q), b_i(q) \in \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]$ are the transfer matrix coefficients at generator $\sigma_i$ and $z(q) = 1/(q^{1/2} + q^{-1/2})$ is the Markov parameter. The product $P_{\mathcal{N}}(q)$ is the Skein polynomial of the nuclear link in factored form. It is computed by exactly $\binom{A}{2}$ transfer matrix multiplications — one per nucleon pair.
