    4. Milnor's configuration #

    The three wedges his circumcentre subdivision produces from a triangle inscribed in the circle of radius R with inscribed angles (α, β, γ).

    The axes and half-angles are read off the inscribed-angle theorem: the side opposite the vertex with inscribed angle α subtends the CENTRAL angle 2α, so the wedge on that side has half-angle α and its axis points at that side's midpoint. Starting the first vertex at t, the three wedges occupy the consecutive angular ranges

    [t, t+2γ],  [t+2γ, t+2γ+2α],  [t+2γ+2α, t+2γ+2α+2β = t+2π],

    which is why α + β + γ = π is what closes the circle.

      The hypotheses are exactly his: the three angles are positive, sum to π, and are acute. Acuteness is used only through disjoint_wedgeAt_adjacent / disjoint_wedgeAt_wrap — a wedge fits strictly inside a half-plane bounded by its own edge exactly when 2ψ < π.

      5. Scope — and what closed the remaining step #

      hypVolume_chimney_milnorWedges is Milnor's value on Milnor's pieces. What it is not, on its own, is a statement about a triangle. The gap was precise:

      milnorWedges R t α β γ  =ᵐ  (the closed triangle inscribed in the circle
                             of radius R with inscribed angles α, β, γ
                             and first vertex at angle t)

      and it is now proved — milnorWedges_ae_eq_inscribedTriangle in QOU.Archimedean.OrthoschemeTiling, which also restates the Λ-sum on the triangle (hypVolume_chimney_inscribedTriangle). Both inclusions were genuine work, and neither needed the machinery one might expect:

      • ⊆ — each wedge inside the triangle. NOT a convexity argument: the single identity cos ψ · F = sin(ψ+ψ') · G₂ + cos ψ' · G₃ puts a wedge inside a neighbour's chord half-plane as a two-term positive combination (wedgeAt_lt_chord_next / _prev).
      • ⊇ — the three wedges cover. NOT a covering computation: the sign relation sin 2α·x + sin 2β·y + sin 2γ·z = 0 (perp_sign_relation) has positive coefficients, so the edge functionals are never all-positive or all-negative, and the six surviving sign patterns each land in one sector.

      The exceptional set taken there is the three edge LINES rather than the three spine segments — larger than necessary, but null for a cheaper reason (each is the kernel of a nonzero linear functional) — hence =ᵐ.

      The pre-Lean set-level evidence that made it worth starting is in computations/milnor_subdivision_obtuse_scope_probe.py; the identities the proof actually rests on are certified in computations/milnor_tiling_certificate_probe.py (9/9 at 50 dps).

      What that step did not settle is the triangle's definition: inscribedTriangle is defined as an intersection of three chord half-planes, anchored to the intended triangle by inscribedTriangle_vertex_incidence and not by a convexHull identification.

      Beyond that, and unchanged: this says nothing about ideal tetrahedra. That a chimney over the inscribed triangle IS the ideal tetrahedron of the corresponding dihedral angles is Milnor's geometric identification, and Mathlib has no ideal tetrahedra.

      On the obtuse case. Acuteness is used here only to separate the wedges. It is NOT evidence that the identity fails for obtuse triangles — it does not: Λ(α)+Λ(β)+Λ(γ) reproduces the base integral over obtuse and right inscribed triangles to ~1e-17 (computations/milnor_subdivision_obtuse_scope_probe.py, 15/15). What fails for obtuse triangles is this SUBDIVISION: the circumcentre leaves the triangle and one wedge must be subtracted rather than added. Scope-(a) — a statement about a proof route, not about the theorem.
