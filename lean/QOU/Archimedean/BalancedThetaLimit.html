    1. The E-shifted recentring: E cancels identically #

    The balanced Taylor coefficients of D_bal(z)/z have the normalised shape

    d_j = d₀ · (−1)^j · E^{−j} · w^{j²} · g_j ,      w = q̃⁻¹,

    where g_j is the balanced normaliser (g_j = 1/(w;w)_j recovers the SW core). The recentring substitution is z = E w^{1−2n} u: the E in the substitution is exactly the E^{−j} in the coefficient, so the two cancel for every j, and what is left is the SW recentring j² + (1−2n)j = (n−n²) + k(k+1), k = j − n. This is the precise sense in which the E-shift is absorbed by the recentring rather than deforming it.

    Ei^j · w^{j²} · (Ei⁻¹ · w^{1−2n})^j = w^{n−n²} · w^{(j−n)(j−n+1)} .

    The E-dependence cancels identically — for every j, not asymptotically.

    2. The theta limit is the same theta: Jacobi-triple-product indices #

    source
    theorem QOU.Archimedean.BalancedThetaLimit.theta_index_base_change (k : ℤ) :
    k * (k + 1) = k * (k - 1) + 2 * k

    The recentred summand carries the exponent k(k+1), which is a Jacobi-triple-product index at base w²: k(k+1) = k(k−1) + 2k. So the limit Σ_{k∈ℤ} (−1)^k w^{k(k+1)} u^k is the theta function at base p = w² with argument w² u, whose zero set is u ∈ w^{2ℤ} — the balanced tropical lattice. This is the same theta function as in the SW core; only the prefactor (g_∞ in place of 1/(w;w)_∞) differs.

    source
    theorem QOU.Archimedean.BalancedThetaLimit.defect_index_base_change (j k : ℤ) :
    k * (k + 1) + j * k = k * (k - 1) + (j + 2) * k

    The j-th defect term of the SW argument carries the exponent k(k+1) + jk, again a triple-product index at base w², now with argument w^{j+2} u: k(k+1) + j·k = k(k−1) + (j+2)·k. Under hypothesis (H) of the audit the balanced defect has literally this expansion, with the SW coefficients (−1)^{j+1} w^{j(j+1)/2}/(w;w)_j replaced by the Taylor coefficients of the balanced normaliser.

        5. What the SW normaliser has and the balanced one is not known to have #

        The SW defect expansion runs over all k ∈ ℤ, not merely k ≥ −n, because the SW normaliser g_m = 1/(w;w)_m is g_∞ · Ψ_SW(w^m) with Ψ_SW(x) = (w x; w)_∞ an ENTIRE function that vanishes at every negative index x = (w^m)⁻¹, m ≥ 1. That is exactly the convention g_m = 0 for m < 0, realised analytically — and it is what lets the SW argument treat the truncated series as a full bilateral theta series.

        The balanced normaliser's analytic continuation Ψ_bal is measured to exist (hypothesis (H) of the audit) but is not known to be entire, and the numerically-extracted Taylor coefficients decay slower than the SW ones. This is the precise residual: the finite-order statement below is the SW half of a contrast, not a balanced result.
