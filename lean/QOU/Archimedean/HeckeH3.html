    Concrete Representations of H₃(q) #

    Explicit matrices for the three irreducible representations of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra H₃(q):

    1. Symmetric (trivial), dim 1: σ_i ↦ q
    2. Standard, dim 2: explicit 2×2 matrices (Young seminormal form)
    3. Alternating (sign), dim 1: σ_i ↦ −q⁻¹

    The standard representation #

    The 2D standard representation (partition (2,1)) is the key ingredient for computing the axial coupling g_A. The matrices are in the Young seminormal form:

    σ₀ = ⎡ q   1  ⎤     σ₁ = ⎡ −q⁻¹   0 ⎤
     ⎣ 0  −q⁻¹ ⎦          ⎣  1      q ⎦

    These satisfy:

    • Hecke relation: σ² = (q − q⁻¹)σ + I
    • Braid relation: σ₀σ₁σ₀ = σ₁σ₀σ₁

    References #

    • [jones1987] V. Jones, Hecke algebra representations of braid groups and link polynomials.
    • [wenzl1988] H. Wenzl, Hecke algebras of type A_n and subfactors.

            §1a Laurent-T arithmetic closers #

            The per-entry goals below live in R_t = ℤ[t, t⁻¹] = AddMonoidAlgebra ℤ ℤ, where t · t⁻¹ = 1 is T 1 * T (-1) = T 0 — an AddMonoidAlgebra fact, not a ring fact (ring treats T 1 and T (-1) as independent atoms and cannot use the relation). The closers below therefore unfold all scalars to T-monomials, distribute, fold every product with ← LaurentPolynomial.T_add (so T m * T n becomes T (m + n) with a literal exponent), and let simp only (Int.reduceAdd/Int.reduceNeg) evaluate the exponent arithmetic; the surviving goal is ℤ-linear in distinct T k atoms, which ring1 closes.

            §3a T(3, n) center: (σ₀ σ₁)^3 = I in (2,1) — q-independent #

            source
            theorem QOU.Archimedean.stdRep3_M_cubed_eq_one :
            (stdRep3_gen0 * stdRep3_gen1) ^ 3 = 1

            Structural identity (PR derivation 2026-05-24): in the standard (2,1) representation of H₃(q), the cube of σ₀σ₁ is the identity matrix, exactly, for all q.

            Direct consequence: the matrix M = σ₀σ₁ has minimal polynomial X² + X + 1, whose roots are the primitive cube roots of unity e^{±2πi/3} = −1/2 ± i√3/2. These eigenvalues are q-independent.

            Corollary for T(3, n) torus knots. The braid word for the T(3, n) torus knot family is (σ₀σ₁)^n in B₃. The (2,1) character is therefore χ^{(2,1)}((σ₀σ₁)^n) = 2 cos(2πn/3) ∈ {−1, −1, +2} cyclically with n mod 3 — q-independent.

            This means the Fibonacci/Chebyshev-of-second-kind h-polynomial pattern proved for T(2, 2m) in H₂(q) (PR #1165) does NOT extend to T(3, n) via the (2,1) channel of H₃(q): the (2,1) contribution is a pure 3-cycle character with no h-dependence.

            The non-trivial q-dependence of J_{T(3, n)}(q) lives entirely in the trivial (q^{2n}) and sign (q^{-2n}) Wedderburn channels.

            Computational provenance: sympy-verified at q symbolic in [computations/probes/t3n_binomial_pattern_probe.py].

            §3b Borromean (2,1) character — named definition + structural properties #

            This section names χ^{(2,1)}(β_Bor) for the standard 6-crossing Borromean braid β_Bor = (σ₀ σ₁⁻¹)^3 ∈ B₃ and proves the basic algebraic structure (it's the trace of a specific matrix product).

            The EXPLICIT POLYNOMIAL FORM in q is χ^{(2,1)}(β_Bor) = −q⁶ + 3q⁴ − 3q² + 4 − 3/q² + 3/q⁴ − 1/q⁶ which expands in h = q − q⁻¹ to χ^{(2,1)}(β_Bor)(h) = 2 − 3h⁴ − h⁶ (exact, finite-degree 6 polynomial)

            Sympy-verified in computations/crystal_yekutieli_probes_abcd.py (PR #1233 probe B). The Lean theorem stating this polynomial equality would require the q-form to be written in the t-parameter algebra (HA_t / q_from_t machinery), which is non-trivial; this section just names the character as a noncomputable def and proves basic structural facts that don't require the polynomial RHS.

            Wedderburn context: combined with χ^{(3)}(β_Bor) = 1 (trivial rep) and χ^{(1,1,1)}(β_Bor) = 1 (sign rep), this gives the per-irrep decomposition of the Borromean Markov trace at H_3(q).

                        §6 Symmetric representation traces #

                        source
                        theorem QOU.Archimedean.halfBorromean_trivial_eval (n : ℕ) (i j : Fin (n - 1)) :
                        (trivRep n).halfBorromeanMat i j = 1

                        Every half-Borromean evaluates to the identity in the trivial representation.

                        In the trivial rep, σ_i ↦ q · I, σ_i⁻¹ ↦ q⁻¹ · I. So h_{ij} = σ_i⁻¹ σ_j σ_i σ_j⁻¹ ↦ q⁻¹ · q · q · q⁻¹ · I = I.

                        This holds because every half-Borromean is a commutator, and any commutator is trivial in a 1-dimensional (abelian) representation.

                        source
                        theorem QOU.Archimedean.halfBorromeanConj_trivial_eval (n : ℕ) (i j : Fin (n - 1)) :
                        (trivRep n).halfBorromeanConjMat i j = 1

                        Every conjugated half-Borromean also evaluates to the identity in the trivial representation (same reasoning: commutator in abelian rep).
