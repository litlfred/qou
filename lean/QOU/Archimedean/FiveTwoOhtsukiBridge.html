    QOU.Archimedean.FiveTwoOhtsukiBridge — shrinking the 5₂ volume-conjecture axiom #

    The corpus records the volume conjecture for 5₂ as literature-cited (Ohtsuki, On the asymptotic expansion of the Kashaev invariant of the 5₂ knot; library/ki52, library/104171-qt-83). Cited is not derived: in Lean it is still an axiom, and it used to be a blanket one — "VC holds for 5₂".

    This file shrinks that axiom. It separates the statement into four parts and shows that only one of them is Ohtsuki's hard analysis:

    part content status
    (A) ⟨5₂⟩_N = e^{Nζ}·N^{3/2}·ω·(1 + O(1/N)) axiom — Ohtsuki Thm 1.1, the 52-page part
    (B) 2π·Re ζ = 3·D(y₀) PROVED, sorry-free — no geometry
    (C) (A) ⟹ (2π/N)·log‖⟨5₂⟩_N‖ → 2π·Re ζ PROVED, sorry-free
    (D) Vol(5₂) = 3·D(y₊) already axiomatised as FiveTwoTriangulation

    The point of the split is (B). It looks like it should need hyperbolic geometry, and it does not: it is an identity among dilogarithms at the root of a cubic. Nothing in (B) or (C) needs the method of steepest descent — that is required only to prove (A) itself, which we do not attempt.

    This file has no sorry. volume_conjecture_five_two_wired derives the volume conjecture for 5₂ from (A), (D) and the statement that Li₂ is a dilogarithm, and nothing else — (B) and the identification of ζ are theorems here, not hypotheses. Its two class arguments are the honest residue: OhtsukiAsymptotic is Ohtsuki's published analysis, FiveTwoTriangulation is Thurston/SnapPea, and neither has an instance. They are not instance-free for the same reason — see the ⚠ on FiveTwoTriangulation's own declaration, which is satisfiable at a derived vol, where OhtsukiAsymptotic is not satisfiable at all. exists_isOhtsukiShape below shows the shape hypothesis both of them carry is at least met.

    The dilogarithm hypothesis is stated at the five points the proof applies it to (IsKummerAtOhtsukiArgs), not on a half plane. Two are in the upper half plane, where QOU.Knot.KummerRadial.kummer already proves Kummer's identity; IsKummerAtOhtsukiArgs.of_upper_half_plane reaches the other three from those, given only that Li₂ commutes with conjugation off the real axis. So the file no longer depends on continuing kummer across the branch cut.

    ⚠ (D) is stated at the conjugate-inverse shape, and that is not cosmetic. FiveTwoTriangulation lives at y₊, the upper-half-plane root of the saddle cubic y³ − 2y² + 3y − 1; Ohtsuki's y₀ is a root of the reciprocal cubic y³ − 3y² + 2y − 1. They are different numbers — y₀ = 0.3376… + 0.5623…i against y₊ = 0.7849… + 1.3071…i — related by y₊ = conj (1/y₀) (checked to 40 digits, difference exactly 0). Writing the instance at y₀ would be wrong. conj_inv_is_five_two_shape proves the shape supplied is admissible, and BlochWigner_lob_conj_inv proves the two D values agree, so the reconciliation is derived rather than asserted.

    Ohtsuki's Theorem 1.1, verbatim #

    From library/ki52 §1 (the published version is library/104171-qt-83, Quantum Topology; note the section text renders π as a glyph the extractor drops, so 2π√−1 reads as 2 √−1 there — the π is present in the PDF and is required for the constants to come out right, see the numerics below).

    Let y₀ be the unique solution with positive imaginary part of (y−1)³ = y, y₀ = 0.3376410213… + √−1·0.5622795120…. Put x₀ = 1 − 1/y₀,

    ζ = (1/2π√−1)·( Li₂(x₀y₀) + Li₂(1/(x₀y₀)) + Li₂(y₀) − Li₂(1/y₀) − Li₂(1/x₀) − π²/6 )
  = 0.4501096100… + √−1·0.4813049796…
ω = e^{√−1 π/4}·( (y₀−1)/(2y₀+1) )^{1/2}
  = 0.0901905774… − √−1·0.6499757866…

    Theorem 1.1. The asymptotic expansion of the Kashaev invariant ⟨5₂⟩_N of the 5₂ knot is given by the following form, ⟨5₂⟩_N = e^{Nζ}·N^{3/2}·ω·(1 + Σ_{i=1}^{d} κ_i·(2√−1/N)^i + O(1/N^{d+1})), for any d, where κ_i is some constant given by a polynomial in y₀ with rational coefficients; in particular κ₁ is given by κ₁ = ((37/4)y₀² − (31/6)y₀ + 35/8)/(2y₀+1)³ + 1 = (1650y₀² − 3498y₀ + 2197)/12696 + 1 = 1.0537470859… − √−1·0.1055728779…

    What this file assumes, and why it is less than the theorem #

    OhtsukiAsymptotic below assumes only the d = 0 truncation — the ratio tends to 1. That is strictly weaker than Theorem 1.1, which is the right direction for an assumed input: the volume conjecture needs only the leading exponential, so assuming the full Poincaré expansion would be assuming more than the conclusion requires. The κ_i play no part in the derivation; κ₁ appears below only as evidence that the transcription is faithful.

    The prefactor is explicit — so ω ≠ 0 is a theorem, not a hypothesis #

    This is the concrete gain over the previous version of this file. Ohtsuki does not leave ω as an unknown constant; he computes it. Squaring his formula kills the branch ambiguity:

    ω² = e^{√−1 π/2}·(y₀−1)/(2y₀+1) = √−1·(y₀−1)/(2y₀+1)

    a rational function of y₀ times i — no square root, no dilogarithm, no analysis. So the class states ω² = ohtsukiOmegaSq y rather than carrying a bare ω ≠ 0 field, and non-vanishing is then derived (omega_ne_zero) from the cubic alone. One hypothesis became a proof.

    Stating the square rather than ω itself is also faithful rather than merely convenient: the printed ω is the negative of the principal branch of e^{iπ/4}((y₀−1)/(2y₀+1))^{1/2} (checked numerically), so ω is only pinned up to sign by Ohtsuki's own formula. That is the same ± that appears in Ohtsuki–Takata's ω²(D) = ±τ(K)/(2√−1).

    Numerical verification of the transcription #

    Every constant above was recomputed at 120 decimal digits from the polished root of the cubic (residual 1.9e-121) before this file was written:

    • y₀, ζ, ω, κ₁ reproduce Ohtsuki's printed digits;
    • 2π·Re ζ = Vol(5₂) and 2π·Im ζ = 3.024128376509301659719951…, the Chern–Simons half, against computations/substrate/knot_volumes.py;
    • three independent routes to Vol(5₂) — the five-term Li₂ sum, Bloch–Wigner via Im Li₂, and the pure Lobachevsky sum that BlochWigner_lob actually uses — agree to 121 digits;
    • κ₁'s two printed forms agree, and agree with the printed value: this is a real check on the transcription, since dropping the trailing + 1 from either form would have moved the answer by a whole unit;
    • 2√−1·ω² = −2(y₀−1)/(2y₀+1) = 0.2344867660… − √−1·0.8286683659….

    A numerical check is not a proof. It was, however, the thing that caught the error: the hinv hypothesis (B) used to carry was refuted by exactly this kind of check before any Lean was written — see the ⚠ block on ohtsuki_sum_im_eq_three_blochWigner. Numbers are not evidence for a theorem; they are excellent evidence against a wrong one.

    Both (B) and (C) are now proved.

    The tier boundary lives in the type #

    OhtsukiAsymptotic deliberately carries no instance (AGENTS.md §3b-tax). Any theorem that needs the 5₂ volume conjecture must take [OhtsukiAsymptotic …], so a reader sees the assumed input in the signature instead of having to find the knot in a status table. That is the whole point of the refactor.

    A trap this makes it easier to fall into, so it is recorded here. Ohtsuki– Takata (Geom. Topol. 19 (2015) 853–952 — note both authors) prove ω²(D) = ±τ(K)/(2√−1) for any two-bridge knot, and Garoufalidis–Yoon (arXiv:2411.03801) prove 1-loop = adjoint torsion for all hyperbolic two-bridge knots. Both are unconditional, and both are about ω, not about ζ. Neither says the asymptotic expansion exists. The bridge from the Hessian invariant to the actual asymptotic prefactor is Ohtsuki–Takata Prop. 4.9, gated on their Assumption 4.8 (Poisson summation + saddle point), verified in the literature only up to 7 crossings. So for 5₂ both links are theorems, while for 8₁₁ = b(27,10) — two-bridge, hyperbolic, hence covered by their Theorem 1.1 — the volume conjecture stays open. Citing those papers as closing 8₁₁ would be an overstatement.

    The shape, and the three non-vanishing facts the cubic already gives #

    y₀ is pinned by (y−1)³ = y together with Im y₀ > 0. Everything this file needs about it beyond that comes from the cubic alone — no appeal to the imaginary part, and no root-selection machinery. That matters: it means the algebraic half of the argument holds at every root, and the proofs are arithmetic rather than complex analysis.

      1. the intermediate value theorem gives the real root t ∈ [2.3, 2.4], since p(2.3) = −0.103 < 0 < 0.344 = p(2.4);
      2. dividing out (y − t) leaves y² + (t−3)y + (t²−3t+2), whose discriminant is −(3t² − 6t − 1) — and 3t² − 6t − 1 > 0 for t ≥ 2.3, which is exactly why the bracket is taken there rather than at the obvious [2,3]: at t = 2 the quantity is −1, so the coarse bracket does not decide the sign;
      3. its two roots are then written down, and the one with +√d has Im > 0.

      The division in step 2 is legitimate only modulo the cubic — the cofactor's constant term is 1/t, and t² − 3t + 2 = 1/t is p(t) = 0 rearranged, so the whole identity is one linear_combination against ht.

            Everything is a power of w = 1 − y #

            Ohtsuki's five arguments x₀y₀, 1/(x₀y₀), y₀, 1/y₀, 1/x₀ look like five unrelated points. They are not. Put w = 1 − y. The cubic (y−1)³ = y says exactly w³ = w − 1, and then

            x₀y₀ = −w        y₀ = −w³        x₀ = 1/w²        1 − 1/x₀ = 1/w

            so every argument is ±w^k for k ∈ {−2, −1, 1, 3}. That is what makes the correction terms collapse: each log‖·‖ is an integer multiple of log‖w‖ and each arg is an integer multiple of arg w, and the integers cancel.

            These are all pure field identities modulo the cubic — linear_combination certificates, no analysis.

            Re(1 − y₀) > 0 — the one place a root is analysed, not the cubic #

            Everything above holds at all three roots. The arg bookkeeping does not: it needs to know which quadrant w = 1 − y sits in, and that is a fact about the complex roots specifically.

            The elimination is elementary. Writing w = s + it and separating w³ − w + 1 = 0 into real and imaginary parts gives, when t ≠ 0,

            t² = 3s² − 1          (imaginary part, after dividing by t)
8s³ − 2s − 1 = 0      (real part, after substituting)

            and the second has no root with s ≤ 0, because for r = −s ≥ 0

            8r³ − 2r + 1 = 2r(2r−1)² + 8(r − 1/4)² + 1/2 > 0.

            That decomposition is the whole proof, and it is what nlinarith is fed.

                What the hypotheses are, and why they are not the theorem in disguise #

                Mathlib has no polylogarithm, so Li₂ is a parameter. It is constrained by exactly two facts, both of which the corpus proves for its own dilogarithm QOU.MathlibExt.cLiTwoExt:

                • hbw is Kummer's identity, D(z) = Im Li₂(z) + arg(1−z)·log‖z‖ — the statement that the Λ-built BlochWigner_lob and the Li₂-built Bloch–Wigner function are the same function — asserted at the five points this proof applies it to and nowhere else (IsKummerAtOhtsukiArgs, above). QOU.Knot.KummerRadial.kummer proves it sorry-free for Im z > 0, which covers two of the five; the other three are 1/y₀, 1/x₀ and 1/(x₀y₀), in the lower half plane, and IsKummerAtOhtsukiArgs.of_upper_half_plane reaches them from the upper ones given only that Li₂ commutes with conjugation off the real axis. So no continuation across the cut is needed.
                • hone is Im Li₂(1) = 0, i.e. Li₂(1) = π²/6 is real. It is stated separately because hbw is deliberately not assumed at z = 1, where arg(1 − z) = arg 0 and the identity holds only through Lean's arg 0 = 0 convention.

                Neither hypothesis mentions 5₂, the cubic, or a volume.

                ⚠ The hypothesis this replaces was FALSE #

                Until this proof was written the statement also carried

                hinv : ∀ z ≠ 0, Im Li₂(z) + Im Li₂(1/z) = −2·arg(1−z)·log‖z‖

                advertised as "the inversion formula". It is not the inversion formula and it is not true. Euler's inversion Li₂(z) + Li₂(1/z) = −π²/6 − ½log(−z)² has imaginary part −log‖z‖·arg(−z), and arg(−z) ≠ 2·arg(1−z). Measured against mpmath at 40 digits, the discharged form is off by −2.97e-1 at y₀, −9.17e-2 at x₀ and −7.25e-3 at x₀y₀ — not a branch ambiguity, a wrong identity. Worse, it is inconsistent with hbw, which already determines Im Li₂ completely; so the old statement was a sorry guarding a hypothesis set no dilogarithm satisfies. It is gone, and nothing replaced it: the correct inversion is derivable here from hbw plus D(1/z) = −D(z), so the theorem now needs one identity where it claimed to need two.

                The check that failure teaches: are the surviving hypotheses satisfiable? A theorem whose hypotheses no object satisfies is vacuous, and that is exactly what the old one was. hbw and hone are independent — hbw is asserted only at the five non-unit arguments, hone only at z = 1 — and the principal-branch Li₂ satisfies both: Im Li₂(1) = Im(π²/6) = 0, and hbw was checked at 40 digits at fourteen random points in both half planes (worst deviation 7.8e-41) as well as at all five arguments this proof actually uses. Any new hypothesis added here should get the same treatment before it is trusted — as the conjugation hypothesis of of_upper_half_plane was, where the check caught that the unrestricted form is unsatisfiable.

                The proof, in one line per term #

                Apply hbw at each of the five non-unit arguments, then use BlochWigner_lob_inv (D(1/z) = −D(z)) and blochWigner_xOf_eq (D(x₀) = D(y₀)). The D terms give

                D(−w) − D(−w) + D(y) + D(y) + D(x₀) = 3·D(y)

                — note D(x₀y₀) cancels against itself between the first two terms, so the D(x₀y₀) = D(y₀)/2 that an earlier version of this docstring listed as required is not needed. (It is true — measured to 50 digits — but it is not on the path.)

                The correction terms are all of the form (integer · arg w) · (integer · log‖w‖) because of the w-power identities above, and the integers cancel:

                arg:   1 − 1/(x₀y₀) contributes +1 ,  1 − y contributes −3 ,
       1 − 1/y = x₀ contributes +6 ,  1 − 1/x₀     contributes −2      →  0

                The arg(2 − y₀) terms — the only ones that are not multiples of arg w — cancel in pairs between Li₂(x₀y₀) and Li₂(1/(x₀y₀)) before any of that.
