    QOU.FluidDynamics.pair_viscous_core_race #

    The viscous core-growth race — the PDE-anchored leg of the model-to-PDE uniformity question (bean qou-ohaz), for the anti-parallel pair-collision channel.

    The two-regime model (pair_phase1_riccati.lean, rem:townsend-biot-savart-strain, rem:hyperbolic-overlap-saturation) engages its regularizing overlap cutoff when the core scale reaches the separation. Its core-growth law (V₁' = αV₁, strain-driven) is the model's least PDE-grounded ingredient. This file proves that the cutoff engagement does not depend on it:

    viscous diffusion alone grows a vortex core as a²(t) = a₀² + 4νt — the Lamb–Oseen law, an exact solution of the Navier–Stokes equations for a single diffusing vortex — while the phase-1 approach shrinks the separation as d²(t) = d₀² − (Γ/π)t. Both are linear in t, so the race is elementary: for every ν > 0 the lines cross at

    t_ov = (d₀² − a₀²)/(Γ/π + 4ν),

    which is strictly positive and strictly before the inviscid collision time t* = πd₀²/Γ, with strictly positive separation at crossing. Overlap engages before collision even with zero strain-driven core growth. Combined with the Γ(t)-robustness of the strain-driven race (overlap_at_small_separation), the pair channel now has two independent in-model regularization channels, one of them anchored to an exact NS solution.

    Scope (honest). The Lamb–Oseen law is exact for a single axisymmetric vortex; applying it per-core during the approach is a localization hypothesis (the same one Biot–Savart approach itself uses). What this file does NOT address: 3D effects absent from the two-regime model entirely (Crow instability, axial core flow) — those remain the open content of bean qou-ohaz. Statements are over a generic linearly ordered field (§7c): the race is pure order/field algebra; no Real.exp is needed because only the core-variance law, not the full Lamb–Oseen profile, enters.

          The viscous race summary: for every positive viscosity the overlap condition engages at a strictly positive time, strictly before the inviscid collision time, at strictly positive separation — with no strain-driven core growth assumed. The two-regime model's regularizing cutoff is therefore robust to removing its least-PDE-grounded ingredient: the diffusive (Lamb–Oseen, exact-NS) channel alone forces engagement.
