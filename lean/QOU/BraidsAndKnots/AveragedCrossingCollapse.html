    Two averaged crossings on one generator collapse to a scalar #

    qou-cxon Q2 measured that the convention-D cyclotomic exponent is 3A - 1 minus two per neutron, and that the per-neutron cancellation fires iff some generator carries two or more averaged crossings (12/12 configurations, d_frame_neutron_cyclotomic_rule_probe.py). That producer offered a Hecke-collapse reading and flagged it explicitly as an interpretation, not a proof.

    It is a proof, and this file is it.

    An averaged crossing is σ + d with 2d = -h, h = q - q⁻¹. Squaring it inside the Hecke relation σ² = h·σ + 1 gives

    (σ + d)² = (h + 2d)·σ + (1 + d²)

    so at 2d = -h the σ coefficient is exactly zero and the product is a scalar. Scaled by 4 to stay division-free, that scalar is exactly the cyclotomic square the measurement saw cancel:

    q²·(4 + h²) = (q² + 1)²

    Why this explains the rule rather than restating it #

    • Only the averaged coefficient collapses. sq_sigma_coeff_vanishes is matched by sigmaCoeff_full_ne_zero and sigmaCoeff_identity_ne_zero: at d = -h the residual σ coefficient is -h, at d = 0 it is h. Both are nonzero whenever h ≠ 0. The collapse is a property of the colour-averaging value, not of squaring.
    • Co-location is forced. The collapse is a statement about a product of two factors on the same generator. Two averaged crossings on different generators are never multiplied by this identity — which is why the measured rule is max(gen0_avg, gen1_avg) ≥ 2 and not a total count.
    • Saturation follows. A third averaged crossing multiplies the scalar by (σ + d) again, reintroducing σ (cube_eq_scalar_mul), so no further (q²+1) appears. That is the measured saturation: three on one generator still cancels 2, not 3.

    Base ring #

    A generic CommRing (§7c), and division-free throughout. An earlier draft of this file stated the same facts over a Field using h/2 and h²/4; that is unsound at characteristic 2, where 2 = 0 and h/2 is not the averaged coefficient at all. Carrying the hypothesis as 2 * d = -h instead of dividing removes the characteristic assumption rather than patching it, and the scaled cyclotomic identity q²(4 + h²) = (q²+1)² needs no inverse of q either. Nothing archimedean, no ordering, no ℝ.

    The bare cyclotomic identity behind the collapse scalar.

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.scaledCollapseScalar_eq_cyclotomicSq {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (q h : R) (hqh : q * h = q ^ 2 - 1) :
    q ^ 2 * (4 + h ^ 2) = (q ^ 2 + 1) ^ 2

    The scalar is the cyclotomic square. With h = q - q⁻¹ — carried division-free as q * h = q² - 1 — the collapse scalar scaled by 4q² is exactly (q² + 1)², the factor whose cancellation the Q2 measurement observed, once per pair of co-located averaged crossings.

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.cube_eq_scalar_mul {R : Type u_1} [CommRing R] (σ h d : R) (hHecke : σ ^ 2 = h * σ + 1) (hd : 2 * d = -h) :
    (σ + d) ^ 3 = (1 + d ^ 2) * (σ + d)

    Saturation. A third averaged crossing on the same generator reintroduces σ: the cube is the collapse scalar times (σ + d), so no second (q²+1)² is produced. This is why the measured cancellation is 2 even when a generator carries three averaged crossings.
