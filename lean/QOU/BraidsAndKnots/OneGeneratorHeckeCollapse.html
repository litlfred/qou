    Any product of linear factors in ONE Hecke generator is linear in that generator #

    per_generator_V_hat_3A accumulates, at a single generator position i, a product of factors c_k · σ_i + d_k — one per step of the braid word that touches that position. Built naively this is a dense matrix product per step, and qou-9ohz (23b9c4f5ba) measured it as the dominant cost of the A ≥ 3 V̂: the braid word's coefficients mix int and mpf, which promoted the seminormal matrices to dtype=object and ran every M @ M as a Python loop — ~6e7 mpf mul+add per word-step sweep over λ ⊢ 9.

    That commit replaced the matrix product with a scalar recursion, on the stated ground that "every factor at generator i is a polynomial in the SAME σ_i, so they commute, and σ obeys σ² = (q − q⁻¹)σ + 1". It verified the relation numerically against the built matrices (6.661e-16, against 1.992e-01 for the (q−1)σ + q alternative) and shipped.

    This file is the proof of the invariant that optimisation assumes. A measured 6.661e-16 says the matrices satisfy the relation at one q in one arithmetic; it does not say the collapse holds. prod_eq_linear says it, for any list of factors, over any commutative ring.

    Why CommRing is the honest setting, and what it scopes out #

    H_n(q) is not commutative. The subalgebra generated by a single σ_i is, and that is precisely why this collapse works — so stating it over a CommRing is not a convenience, it is the content. It also makes the limitation exact: nothing here licenses collapsing a product that spans two generators, because σ_i σ_j ≠ σ_j σ_i for |i − j| = 1. Co-location is forced, the same scoping AveragedCrossingCollapse states for its special case.

    Relation to AveragedCrossingCollapse #

    That file proves the σ-coefficient vanishes for the specific averaged factor σ + d at 2d = -h. This one is the general statement: the product is always linear, and collapseStep says what its coefficients are. The averaged case is then the question of when α happens to be zero, which is a property of that coefficient rather than of collapsing.

    What this does NOT establish #

    It does not say the V̂ so computed is the right V̂ — canonical_tower's SCOPE section flags the per-generator definition as provisional, and that is a separate question this file does not touch. It says only that computing the product by scalar recursion agrees with computing it by matrix multiplication.

      R × R

      One step of the accumulator: multiplying α·σ + β by c·σ + d again yields something linear in σ, with these coefficients.

      (α σ + β)(c σ + d) = α c σ² + (α d + β c) σ + β d
                   = α c (h σ + 1) + (α d + β c) σ + β d
                   = (α (c h + d) + β c) σ + (α c + β d)

      This is the whole optimisation: two ring multiplications and an add per step, independent of the irrep dimension, where the naive route is a dim × dim matrix product.

            The Markov trace of the collapsed element, and the distance to the Φ_q pole #

            mass_endomorphism.additive_wedderburn_volumes runs exactly collapseStep (a, b = a*c*HA + a*d + b*c, a*c + b*d) and then takes the Markov trace of the result, Vs = z_mark * a + b, using tr(σ) = z and tr(1) = 1. So the whole V̂ is an affine function of the Markov parameter z, with slope the σ-coefficient the collapse produced.

            That matters because Φ_q(V̂) = ∏_k (1 - q^{-k} V̂)^{-1} has a pole at V̂ = q, and the 2026-08-15 owner ruling moved z from convention C (√q/(q+1) ≈ 0.4993) to convention D (q³/(q²+1) ≈ 0.6127) — a 22.7 % increase. Bean qou-dchz records that several Φ_q towers which converged under C emit +inf under D.

            vHat_margin is why a convention change can do that: the margin to the pole, measured in z, is the margin measured in V̂ divided by α. A large σ-coefficient means a small change in the convention is a large change in the distance to the singularity. It is the conditioning statement, exactly.

              Why the proton's identity coefficient equals the neutron's V̂ #

              Measured (tower_vhat_markov_parameter_probe, 60 dps, agreeing to ~50 digits): at generator 1 of the canonical atom words,

              β(proton)  =  1.088367533397188127779206958658256570732…  =  V̂(neutron).

              That is not a coincidence and it is not numerology. The two words differ in exactly one factor. Reading them off atom_braid_word_3A, both are built from σ + d with d ∈ {−h, −h/2}, and at generator 1

              proton   = (σ − h)³ · (σ − h/2)
neutron  = (σ − h)³ · (σ − h),

              so the question is what changes when the last factor's d moves from −h/2 to −h. d = −h/2 is precisely AveragedCrossingCollapse's averaged crossing, the value 2d = −h at which the σ-coefficient vanishes.

              identity_coeff_avg_eq_trace_full answers it in general: appending the averaged factor and reading off the identity coefficient is the same operation as appending the full factor and taking the Markov trace. Both compute α − β·h/2. The measured equality is that identity at x = (σ − h)³, and it holds for every x, over any field — nothing about this particular word, this q, or this atom enters.

              The inter-nucleon generator contributes exactly the colour weighting #

              atom_braid_word_3A's docstring states its crossing template: {0, −h, −h/2, −h(½ − δ/4)} with δ = flavor_split_leading(q). The first three are the ones the intra-nucleon chains use. The fourth appears once in the whole deuteron word — at the single inter-nucleon generator — and is the colour-weighted p–n interface (pn_convention = "bring_color_weighted", made canonical by the owner ruling of 2026-08-15, R3). Its colour-blind sibling is strict_averaged, i.e. δ = 0, which is the averaged crossing −h/2.

              That generator carries a single factor, so its collapse is the factor itself and its V̂ is computable in closed form. inter_nucleon_vHat does it: the answer is h·δ/4, and in particular it is zero when δ = 0.

              So under the colour-blind convention the inter-nucleon generator contributes nothing to the tower at all, and everything it does contribute is the colour weighting. Measured at 50 dps: V̂ = 7.190162809336869…e-5, which is the 7.19e-5 by which that generator's d sits away from −h/2.

              The Markov parameter h/2 averages the two Hecke eigenvalues — and what that #

              costs the tower

              Measured (tower_vhat_chain_length_pole_margin_probe): across every atom from A = 1 to A = 16, per_generator_V_hat_3A's per-generator V̂ takes only seven distinct values, and max |V̂| saturates — it is 1.0883675333971881278 (98.05 % of q) for every atom containing a neutron, already at A = 1, and never grows with nucleon count. (A proton-only system sits strictly further out, at 1.0551103188317245612: the neutron's dd pair contributes a further σ⁻¹ where the proton's uu pair contributes a σ.) Reading the seven off, they are collapses of

              σ⁻⁴,  σ⁻³·avg,  σ⁴·avg·σ²,  σ³·avg²·σ²,  σ⁻¹,  σ,  and the colour-weighted avg

              where σ⁻¹ = σ − h (the skein relation: σ(σ − h) = σ² − hσ = 1, immediate from IsHeckeGen) and avg = σ − h/2 = (σ + σ⁻¹)/2 is the averaged crossing. The maximum is attained at the pure chain σ⁻⁴.

              vHat_half_h_eq_avg_evals is why that chain has a closed form, and is the conceptual content of markov_param_eq_half_h in the other file: at z = h/2 with h = q − q⁻¹, the Markov trace is exactly the average of the two Hecke eigenvalue evaluations σ ↦ q and σ ↦ −q⁻¹. (That is also why transpose-pairing forced h/2 there: transposition swaps the two eigenvalues, so pairing averages them.)

              Since prod_eq_linear says each evaluation is multiplicative, the chain's value follows immediately, and vHat_invChain records it:

              V̂ₙ  =  ((q⁻¹)ⁿ + (−q)ⁿ) / 2.

              The consequence is sharp and is not comfortable. |(−q)ⁿ| grows like qⁿ, so |V̂ₙ| → ∞ and only finitely many chain lengths keep Φ_q finite at all. Measured at the substrate q:

              n = 4   |V̂|/q = 0.98053   ← the length the word actually uses
n = 5   |V̂|/q = 0.49163
n = 6   |V̂|/q = 1.08331   ← PAST THE POLE

              So n = 4 is the last even chain length for which the tower is defined, and one further same-sign crossing pair diverges it. What that does not say is that the 4 is tuned: it is 3 + 1, three from the B₃ closure and one from the quark pair, and both are fixed by the construction upstream of any of this. What it does say is that the 1.95 % margin is the whole of the headroom, and that it is a fact about the chain length rather than robustness.
