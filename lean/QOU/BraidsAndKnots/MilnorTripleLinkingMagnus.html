    QOU.BraidsAndKnots.MilnorTripleLinkingMagnus — a computable μ̄₁₂₃, nonzero on Borromean #

    Bean qou-bhfg (front 4-B). Companion to QOU.BraidsAndKnots.BorromeanPairwiseLinking and to docs/audits/2026-08-25-4b-pairwise-linking-prune.md.

    Why this file exists #

    The pairwise prune next door shows every pairwise linking number vanishes on the proton braid, so an E_EM ansatz built from pairwise data cannot separate it from the unlink. The unique first-order candidate left is the Milnor triple linking number μ̄₁₂₃.

    The corpus did not have one. It has electroweak-triplet-charge-spin-from-involution.lean's evenTwistTripleLinking (_k : ℕ) : ℤ := 0 — correct, but defined only on the two-component carrier T(2,2k), where a triple linking number is 0 by definition — and KnotTheory/Vassiliev/MilnorVassilievBridge.lean, which is honest conjecture classes. Neither computes anything for three components. This file supplies the computation in Lean.

    Correction (same day). This paragraph first said the file supplies "the corpus's first nonzero triple linking value". It does not, and that is withdrawn. computations/binding_mn_milnor_linking.py already computes μ̄₁₂₃ = +1 on the Borromean link by this same Magnus method, and validates it against a Hopf and a 3-chain control. This file is a Lean port of committed Python, not a discovery. It was written without grepping computations/ — AGENTS.md corpus-grep path 3, whose stated purpose is exactly that. See docs/audits/2026-08-25-4b-corrections-duplication-and-two-fronts.md.

    What is computed #

    Milnor's μ̄₁₂₃ is the coefficient of X₁X₂ in the Magnus expansion of the longitude of the third component, read in the free group on the meridians x₁, x₂. Degree 2 suffices, so the whole thing is a finite computation:

    x₁ ↦ 1 + X₁       x₁⁻¹ ↦ 1 − X₁ + X₁²
x₂ ↦ 1 + X₂       x₂⁻¹ ↦ 1 − X₂ + X₂²

    Deg2 carries the seven coefficients of 1, X₁, X₂, X₁X₁, X₁X₂, X₂X₁, X₂X₂, and Deg2.mul is the truncated product. Everything below is decide.

    The coherence check worth noticing #

    The degree-1 coefficients of a Magnus expansion are the linking numbers. So magnus_commutator's c1 = c2 = 0 is the Magnus-side shadow of BorromeanPairwiseLinking.borromean_crossSum_zero, computed by a completely different route — a braid-word crossing simulation there, a free-group series here. That they agree is a real consistency check on both files, not a restatement.

    It also matters mathematically: μ̄₁₂₃ is only well-defined (indeterminacy-free) when the pairwise linking numbers vanish. The vanishing degree-1 part is precisely that hypothesis, verified rather than assumed.

    Scope — two topological inputs are CONSUMED, not proved #

    1. Milnor's identification of μ̄₁₂₃ with the X₁X₂ coefficient of the longitude. That is Milnor's theorem, cited, not re-derived here.
    2. That the Borromean longitude is the commutator [x₁, x₂]. This is the standard presentation and is consumed exactly as the braid word (σ₀σ₁⁻¹)³ is consumed next door.

    What this file adds is the computation those inputs feed, which the corpus lacked. It does not derive E_EM, and it does not supply the map from a triple linking number to an energy — that gap is untouched and is the second of the two objects 4-B still needs.

                          The exchange antisymmetry, and what it forbids #

                          μ̄₁₂₃ reverses sign under an odd permutation of the three components. For 4-B that is a constraint on the map to an energy, not just a curiosity: the two like-charged strands of the proton are components 1 and 2 (the two up quarks, with the down as component 3), and a Coulomb self-energy between two identical charges must be symmetric under exchanging them.

                          So an E_EM that is odd in μ̄₁₂₃ is forced to vanish. The map has to be even — |μ̄₁₂₃|, μ̄₁₂₃², and so on.

                            What the prune leaves open — the positive half #

Route (2) of bean qou-o6ml (the Möbius / knot-energy class) needs the complementary statement: that an even functional is not merely un-excluded but compatible by construction, and can be nonzero. Without it, "the map must be even" is only half an instruction.

                            source
                            theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.MilnorTripleLinkingMagnus.exchange_symmetry_forces_agreement_on_pm_one (F : ℤ → ℤ) (hsym : F (mubar123 borromeanLongitude) = F (mubar123 swappedLongitude)) :
                            F 1 = F (-1)

                            The sharper reading, and the one that constrains route (2). Exchange symmetry alone — with no parity assumption at all — forces any admissible functional to take the same value on the Borromean longitude and on its component-swapped partner.

                            Since those two are separated by μ̄₁₂₃ = 1 against μ̄₁₂₃ = -1 (mubar_separates_borromean_from_unlink gives the analogous separation from the unlink), the consequence is that the sign of μ̄₁₂₃ is invisible to any exchange-symmetric E_EM: on this pair such a functional sees at most |μ̄₁₂₃|.

                            That is strictly stronger than "odd functionals vanish", which is the special case F (-m) = - F m. For route (2) it says the triple-linking channel can supply a magnitude to the EM weight but never a chirality, so any construction whose EM content is carried by the sign of μ̄₁₂₃ is excluded before it is built.

                            The third prune: the carrier's own μ̄ is ZERO, which closes the even branch #

                            The two prunes above are about the shape of E_EM as a function of μ̄₁₂₃: odd is forbidden (odd_symmetric_functional_vanishes), and the sign is invisible (exchange_symmetry_forces_agreement_on_pm_one). What survived was the even branch — |μ̄₁₂₃|, μ̄₁₂₃² — kept alive by even_symmetric_functional_can_be_nonzero, whose witness F m = m² is nonzero precisely because the Borromean sits at μ̄₁₂₃ = ±1.

                            The derived carrier does not sit at ±1. It sits at 0. computations/probes/milnor_mubar_vanishes_on_every_triple_probe.py computes μ̄ on every triple of the canonical atom word's closure and gets zero — for ²H, ³H, ³He, ⁴He, ⁶Li, ⁸Be, ¹⁰B, in both crossing-resolution branches, across all three indeterminacy classes. Its positive controls run the same code path: the Borromean is asserted nonzero and the Hopf link at μ = 2, lk = 1, so the zeros are a result and not a broken implementation.

                            The mathematical content below is that hypothesis, not the proofs. The proofs are a rewrite and a funext; they are short because the work was the computation. What the statements buy is that the prune is written down in a form a reader can check against the probe, and — the part that matters — that it closes the escape an author would actually reach for.

                            The escape it closes. Faced with "μ̄ vanishes on my triple", the natural move is use a different triple, or use a symmetric function of all of them. The second theorem covers that: a functional of the whole family of triple invariants is equally constant, because the family is pointwise zero. So the even branch is closed on the derived carrier, not merely inconvenienced.

                            Scope. This is scope (a) on the derived carrier as computed, inherited verbatim from the probe. It says nothing about Milnor invariants of length > 3 — which is what this route's own title ("higher-order Milnor") names, and which the probe explicitly does not touch — and nothing about a future carrier. The Borromean control in that same probe is a standing counterexample to any reading of this as "μ̄ vanishes on every link".

                            Not claimed: that a length-4 or higher Milnor invariant vanishes, or that this route is dead. Its length-3 rung is.

                            The fourth prune: a link invariant of a NON-LINK — the ladder is unaskable #

                            The prune above closes the length-3 rung on the derived carrier, and leaves the obvious question: what about length 4? Do not answer it by computing length 4. Walking up the Milnor ladder until a rung is nonzero is selection of a discrete parameter against the target — the anti-fit directive names exactly that. This section derives, instead, that the ladder is asking for something the object does not have.

                            The atom element is not a braid, and the corpus established this before this section — cite it, do not re-derive it. braids-and-knots/markov-disambiguation.md (PR #5765, 2026-08-25) states the category fact directly:

                            tr_M is defined on ALL of H_n(q) … no braid, no closure, and no topology are needed for the number to exist. What the closure supplies is the INTERPRETATION. … for W in H_n(q) that is NOT in the image of ρ : B_n → H_n(q), tr_M(W) is a well-defined number WITH NO LINK BEHIND IT.

                            and computations/binding_is_not_a_link_invariant_probe.py (bean qou-brng) measures which letters leave the image. Its result is sharper than "the averaged ones are the d ≠ 0 ones", which is the reading to avoid: under this module's convention σ⁻¹ = σ - h, a d = -h letter is a braid generator. Per bond type —

                            pp   d = 0                    σ_g        IS a braid generator
nn   d = -h                   σ_g - h    IS one too (= σ_g⁻¹)
pn   d = -h(1/2 - δ/4)                   is NOT — the only bond letter that isn't

                            Milnor invariants are invariants of links — closures of braids. A superposition is not a link, so it has none; to get one you must resolve the averaged letters, and each resolution is a different link.

                            One tension, flagged rather than resolved. That probe concludes the word "leaves the braid image exactly at the p-n bond", while atom_braid_word_3A's own docstring describes the per-nucleon block as eleven letters "two of them averaged (σ - (h/2)·1)" — and -h/2 is in the outside set. Those are statements about different letters (inter-nucleon bonds vs intra-nucleon core), and whether a Z ≥ 1, N = 0 word would be inside the image turns on it.
