      def QOU.BraidsAndKnots.wenzl_qmn_synergy [ctx : WenzlQmnSynergyContext] :
      10000 ≤ WenzlQmnSynergyContext.wenzl_speedup ∧ 100 ≤ WenzlQmnSynergyContext.qmn_speedup ∧ (WenzlQmnSynergyContext.combined_speedup = WenzlQmnSynergyContext.wenzl_speedup * WenzlQmnSynergyContext.qmn_speedup ∧ 1000000 ≤ WenzlQmnSynergyContext.combined_speedup) ∧ WenzlQmnSynergyContext.trM_Tw = WenzlQmnSynergyContext.z ^ WenzlQmnSynergyContext.lengthW ∧ WenzlQmnSynergyContext.wenzlSum = WenzlQmnSynergyContext.z ^ WenzlQmnSynergyContext.lengthW

      Proposition statement (from manuscript):

      The Wenzl-bridge (y-lambda-closed-form) gives $y_\lambda(q)$ as a closed q-rational expression in $\sqrt q$ in $O(p(n) \cdot \max\text{hooks})$ time, while q-Murnaghan-Nakayama ([Halverson-Ram 1995, Wenzl q-MN as in q_mn_canonical.py]) gives $\chi_\lambda(T_w; q)$ for canonical permutation braids $T_w$ in $O(n^2)$ time.*

      Combining them gives the Markov trace

      $$ \mathrm{tr}_M(T_w) \;=\; \sum_{\lambda \,\vdash\, n} y_\lambda(q) \cdot \chi_\lambda(T_w; q) $$

      on canonical permutation braids in $O(p(n) \cdot (\max\text{hooks} + n^2))$ time — far faster than the Hoefsmit-seminormal path ($O(p(n) \cdot \dim_\lambda^2 \cdot \ell(w))$).
