    Binomial-square sums and the hook-shape bracket collapse #

    The classical moment sums of the squared binomial weight C(m,k)², in division-free form, together with the algebraic collapse they produce in the (TAIL) first-instance computation of the covariance-kernel epic.

    Why these sums #

    The carrier lemma (YoungDiagramKernelPincer.hookShape_of_hookLen_eq_sum + its converse) confines the top hook statistic N_n to the corner cells of the n hook shapes (a, 1^c), and the hook-shape data (sytDim_hookShape: f^{(a,1^c)} = C(n−1, a−1); contentSq_hookShape) turns the covariance ν_n = Cov_Planch(N_n, ω₃) into a single classical sum over k = a − 1:

    n! · ν_n = Σ_{k ≤ m} C(m,k)² · [Q(k) + Q(m−k) − C(n,2)], m = n − 1,

    with Q(x) = Σ_{t ≤ x} t². Expanding the bracket, the cubic terms cancel identically between the arm and the leg contributions:

    6·[Q(k) + Q(m−k) − C(n,2)] = 2(m+1)·(3k² − 3mk + m(m−1)),

    so only the zeroth, first and second C(m,k)²-moments are needed — a simplification of the five-step derivation recorded in docs/audits/2026-07-26-covariance-kernel-A2-tail-first-instance.md, whose step 4 lists the cubic sum as well. (two_mul_sum_cube_mul_choose_sq below records the cubic sum anyway: it completes the moment family and makes the cancellation claim checkable.)

    The moments #

    Each is stated division-free (odd moments carry an explicit factor 2), over ℕ:

    moment value
    Σ_{k≤m} C(m,k)² C(2m, m) (Vandermonde + symmetry)
    2·Σ_{k≤m} k·C(m,k)² m·C(2m, m) (reflection k ↦ m − k)
    Σ_{k≤m+1} k²·C(m+1,k)² (m+1)²·C(2m, m) (absorption k·C(m+1,k) = (m+1)·C(m,k−1))
    2·Σ_{k≤m+1} k³·C(m+1,k)² (m+1)²(m+2)·C(2m, m) (absorption + reflection)

    and the payoff bracket_sum_collapse is the step-5 algebra: the bracket sum collapses to (m−1)(m−2)·C(2m−2, m−1), whose factor (m−2) = (n−3) is the exact source of the n = 3 zero in the closed form ν_n = n(n−2)(n−3)·C(2n−4, n−2)/(3·n!).

    The zeroth moment: Vandermonde convolution at the centre #

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.BinomialSquareSums.sum_choose_sq (m : ℕ) :
    ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (m + 1), m.choose k ^ 2 = (2 * m).choose m

    Vandermonde square sum: Σ_{k ≤ m} C(m,k)² = C(2m, m). The central Vandermonde convolution C(m+m, m) = Σ_{i+j=m} C(m,i)·C(m,j) with the symmetry C(m, m−k) = C(m, k).

    The first moment: reflection #

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.BinomialSquareSums.two_mul_sum_mul_choose_sq (m : ℕ) :
    2 * ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (m + 1), k * m.choose k ^ 2 = m * (2 * m).choose m

    First moment (division-free): 2·Σ_{k ≤ m} k·C(m,k)² = m·C(2m, m). Reflecting k ↦ m − k leaves the weight invariant, so summing the two orderings replaces k by k + (m − k) = m.

    The absorption identity and the second moment #

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.BinomialSquareSums.succ_mul_choose_succ (m k : ℕ) :
    (k + 1) * (m + 1).choose (k + 1) = (m + 1) * m.choose k

    Absorption: (k+1)·C(m+1, k+1) = (m+1)·C(m, k) — the standard index-shift that turns a k-weighted binomial into an unweighted one at one lower order.

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.BinomialSquareSums.sum_sq_mul_choose_sq (m : ℕ) :
    ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (m + 2), k ^ 2 * (m + 1).choose k ^ 2 = (m + 1) ^ 2 * (2 * m).choose m

    Second moment: Σ_{k ≤ m+1} k²·C(m+1, k)² = (m+1)²·C(2m, m). The k = 0 term vanishes; absorption turns each remaining term into (m+1)²·C(m, k−1)², and the zeroth moment finishes.

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.BinomialSquareSums.two_mul_sum_cube_mul_choose_sq (m : ℕ) :
    2 * ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (m + 2), k ^ 3 * (m + 1).choose k ^ 2 = (m + 1) ^ 2 * (m + 2) * (2 * m).choose m

    Third moment (division-free): 2·Σ_{k ≤ m+1} k³·C(m+1, k)² = (m+1)²(m+2)·C(2m, m). Absorption reduces the cube to (k+1)·[(m+1)·C(m,k)]², then the first and zeroth moments at order m finish. Recorded for completeness of the moment family: the cubic terms cancel in the hook-shape bracket (see bracket_sum_collapse), so this moment is not consumed there.

    The central-binomial step and the collapse #

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.BinomialSquareSums.succ_mul_central_succ (m : ℕ) :
    (m + 1) * (2 * (m + 1)).choose (m + 1) = 2 * (2 * m + 1) * (2 * m).choose m

    Central-binomial step: (m+1)·C(2m+2, m+1) = 2(2m+1)·C(2m, m) — the ratio of successive central binomial coefficients, in division-free form (Nat.succ_mul_centralBinom_succ).

    source
    theorem QOU.BraidsAndKnots.BinomialSquareSums.bracket_sum_collapse (r : ℕ) :
    ∑ k ∈ Finset.range (r + 2), ↑((r + 1).choose k) ^ 2 * (3 * ↑k ^ 2 - 3 * (↑r + 1) * ↑k + (↑r + 1) * ↑r) = ↑r * (↑r - 1) * ↑((2 * r).choose r)

    The step-5 collapse. With m = r + 1, the hook-shape bracket sum collapses:

    Σ_{k ≤ m} C(m,k)²·(3k² − 3mk + m(m−1)) = (m−1)(m−2)·C(2m−2, m−1).

    Stated over ℤ at m = r + 1 (so the subtractions are genuine): the left side is the bracket sum, the right side r(r−1)·C(2r, r). The factor (r − 1) = (m − 2) = (n − 3) is the exact source of the n = 3 zero of ν_n.

    Proof: substitute the three moments (sum_sq_mul_choose_sq, two_mul_sum_mul_choose_sq, sum_choose_sq), eliminate C(2r+2, r+1) via succ_mul_central_succ, and collapse the polynomial coefficient 6(r+1)² − 2(2r+1)(r+3) = 2r(r−1).
