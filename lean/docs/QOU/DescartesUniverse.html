    Chapter 6 — The Descartes Universe #

    Formalization of the Descartes condition and the Descartes universe from Chapter 6 of the QOU manuscript.

    LaTeX source: chapters/descartes-universe.tex

    Structure #

    1. Descartes condition — χ(E) = −6
    2. Genus theorem — one-complex-dimensional descent has genus 4
    3. Exceptional divisor — structure at singular fibre points

    §1 The Descartes Condition #

    source
    structure QOU.DescartesUniverse.DescartesCondition :
    Type (max (max (u_1 + 1) (u_2 + 1)) (u_3 + 1))

    Descartes condition: a ℂ quantum observable universe satisfies the Descartes condition if the Euler characteristic of the bulk bundle is χ(E) = −6. A QOU satisfying this is a Descartes universe.

    LaTeX: \label{def:descartes-condition}

    • cqou : CQuantumObservableUniverse

      The ℂ quantum observable universe.

    • eulerChar : ℤ

      The Euler characteristic of the bulk bundle.

    • descartes : self.eulerChar = -6

      The Descartes condition: χ(E) = −6.

        §2 Implications on Examples #

        On a Calabi–Yau manifold, the Descartes condition χ(E) = −6 constrains the Hodge numbers of the underlying Calabi–Yau. LaTeX: \label{rem:descartes-calabi-yau} Ref: [atiyah1988] https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02698547

        On an ALE space with finite subgroup Γ ⊂ SU(2), the Descartes condition selects specific Γ via arithmetic constraints on the McKay quiver. LaTeX: \label{rem:descartes-ale}

        On affine space M = ℂⁿ, the Descartes condition forces non-trivial topology on the principal bundle despite contractibility of the base. LaTeX: \label{rem:descartes-affine}

        §3 Descent to One Complex Dimension #

        source
        theorem QOU.DescartesUniverse.genus_of_maximal_descent (dc : DescartesCondition) (g : ℕ) (hchi : dc.eulerChar = 2 - 2 * ↑g) :
        g = 4

        Proposition (Genus of the maximal descent): if a Descartes universe descends to a compact one-complex-dimensional variety M₀, then M₀ is a Riemann surface of genus g = 4.

        Proof: χ = 2 − 2g = −6 gives g = 4.

        LaTeX: \label{prop:genus-maximal-descent}

        §4 Leptons, Chirality, and the Reeb Decomposition #

        Electrons and muons correspond to fermions (Definition \label{def:fermion}) with specific Reeb decompositions: the vertical component encodes the lepton generation. LaTeX: \label{rem:descartes-leptons}

        Chirality corresponds to the orientation of the Reeb vector field ξ on the CR manifold: left-handed particles have θ(ξ) = +1, right-handed have θ(ξ) = −1. LaTeX: \label{rem:descartes-chirality}

            §6 The Descartes Theorem #

            source
            theorem QOU.DescartesUniverse.descartes_universality (dc : DescartesCondition) :
            dc.eulerChar = -6

            Theorem (Descartes): every Descartes universe with one-complex-dimensional descent is isomorphic (as a QOU) to Bring's surface.

            This is the universality statement: the Descartes condition χ(E) = −6 uniquely determines the geometry.

            LaTeX: \label{thm:descartes}

            Type

            Terminality data: packages the Tannaka–Krein rigidity argument. A fibre-preserving monoidal functor between Tannakian categories over the same fibre functor is unique up to natural isomorphism.

            • iso_count : ℕ

              The number of natural isomorphisms between any two fibre-preserving monoidal functors with the same fibre functor. Tannaka–Krein rigidity says this is exactly 1 (up to iso).

            • rigid : self.iso_count = 1

              Rigidity: the isomorphism count equals 1.

              §7 Light Cone and Bring's Curve #

              The light cone of an affine chart on the Descartes universe is determined by the contact structure: null geodesics are integral curves of the horizontal distribution H = ker θ. LaTeX: \label{rem:light-cone-affine}

              Bring's curve is the one-dimensional descent of the Descartes universe: a genus-4 Riemann surface with automorphism group S₅. LaTeX: \label{rem:brings-curve}
