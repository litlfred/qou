    Strong Multiplicativity from the GS Deformation #

    The strong multiplicativity condition ([fred2005, Theorem 4.12]) states that the comultiplication (Frobenius relation) on Dist(G) is preserved under the Gröbner–Shirshov deformation at every filtration degree.

    This is the critical compatibility condition ensuring that:

    • The gauge connection (encoded by the comultiplication) survives the passage from characteristic p to characteristic 0.
    • The Frobenius–Hopf structure of the QOU fibre algebra is a genuine bialgebra (not just an algebra).

    Proof strategy #

    The proof has three steps:

    1. Filtration compatibility (§1): the comultiplication Δ respects the filtration on Dist(G), i.e., Δ(Fₙ) ⊆ ⊕_{i+j=n} Fᵢ ⊗ Fⱼ.

    2. GS basis for the tensor product (§2): the GS basis of Dist(G) induces a GS basis on Dist(G) ⊗ Dist(G) via the disjoint union presentation k⟨G₁ ⊔ G₂⟩ / (commutativity of G₁ with G₂ + Σ₁ + Σ₂).

    3. Frobenius preservation (§3): the identity Δ(xy) = Δ(x)·Δ(y) holds on generators (by direct computation), and extends to all elements by normal-form uniqueness from the composition lemma.

    References #

    • [fred2005, Theorem 4.12] — strong multiplicativity
    • [fred2005, §4] — proof via filtration-degree induction

    A filtered bialgebra: an algebra A with a compatible filtration and a comultiplication Δ : A → A ⊗ A that respects the filtration.

    We model A ⊗ A abstractly as a separate type T with a multiplication, rather than using pairs (which incorrectly model A × A rather than A ⊗ A).

    • filt : ℕ → A → Prop
    • ascending (n : ℕ) (a : A) : self.filt n a → self.filt (n + 1) a
    • exhaustive (a : A) : ∃ (n : ℕ), self.filt n a
    • mul_compat (m n : ℕ) (a b : A) : self.filt m a → self.filt n b → self.filt (m + n) (a * b)
    • comul : A → T

      Comultiplication Δ : A → A ⊗ A.

    • counit : A → A

      Counit ε : A → k (represented as A → A for simplicity).

    • tensor_filt : ℕ → T → Prop

      The tensor product has its own filtration.

    • comul_filtered (n : ℕ) (a : A) : self.filt n a → self.tensor_filt n (self.comul a)

      Comultiplication respects the filtration: x ∈ Fₙ ⟹ Δ(x) ∈ ⊕_{i+j≤n} Fᵢ ⊗ Fⱼ.

    • tensor_mul : T → T → T

      The tensor product multiplication (A ⊗ A) × (A ⊗ A) → A ⊗ A.

    • comul_is_alg_hom (a b : A) : self.comul (a * b) = self.tensor_mul (self.comul a) (self.comul b)

      Δ is an algebra homomorphism: Δ(xy) = Δ(x) · Δ(y). This is the Frobenius relation / strong multiplicativity.

      §2 Tensor Product Rewriting System #

      source
      def QOU.GrobnerShirshov.TensorGen (G : Type u) :
      Type u

      Generators for the tensor product algebra k⟨G⟩ ⊗ k⟨G⟩.

      The tensor product k⟨G⟩ ⊗ k⟨G⟩ is isomorphic to the quotient of the free algebra k⟨G₁ ⊔ G₂⟩ on two disjoint copies of G by the ideal generated by g₁ · g₂ − g₂ · g₁ for g₁ ∈ G₁, g₂ ∈ G₂.

      We represent G₁ ⊔ G₂ as G ⊕ G (the sum type). The Sum.inl copy represents g ⊗ 1 and Sum.inr represents 1 ⊗ g.

        axiom QOU.GrobnerShirshov.tensorOrder {G : Type u} (ord : AdmissibleOrder G) :
        AdmissibleOrder (TensorGen G)

        The admissible order on TensorGen G induced by a base order on G.

        The tensor product order on k⟨G₁ ⊔ G₂⟩ is the deg-lex extension: first compare by total degree (word length), then lexicographically using Sum.inl < Sum.inr between components and the base order ord within each component.

        Axiomatised because constructing a well-founded deg-lex order on List (G ⊕ G) from ord : AdmissibleOrder G requires a decidable linear order on G ⊕ G and the well-foundedness proof for the lexicographic product, which needs Fintype G or an explicit enumeration. The result is standard: every admissible order on G lifts to an admissible order on G ⊕ G via the coproduct ordering.

        Reference: Bokut & Chen, Bull. Math. Sci. 4 (2014), §2.1.

        axiom QOU.GrobnerShirshov.tensorRewritingSystem {G : Type u} {k : Type v} [Field k] [DecidableEq G] (sys : RewritingSystem G k) :
        RewritingSystem (TensorGen G) k

        The tensor product rewriting system: given a GS basis for k⟨G⟩/(Σ), the tensor product k⟨G⟩/(Σ) ⊗ k⟨G⟩/(Σ) is presented by the rewriting system on k⟨G₁ ⊔ G₂⟩ with:

        • Relations Σ₁ (the original relations acting on G₁ generators)
        • Relations Σ₂ (the original relations acting on G₂ generators)
        • Commutativity relations g₁ · g₂ = g₂ · g₁ for all g₁ ∈ G₁, g₂ ∈ G₂

        The commutativity relations ensure that G₁ and G₂ generators commute, which is the defining property of the tensor product.

        axiom QOU.GrobnerShirshov.tensor_gs_basis {G : Type u} {k : Type v} [Field k] [DecidableEq G] (ord : AdmissibleOrder G) (sys : RewritingSystem G k) (hgs : IsGrobnerShirshovBasis G k ord sys) :
        IsGrobnerShirshovBasis (TensorGen G) k (tensorOrder ord) (tensorRewritingSystem sys)

        A GS basis for k⟨G⟩/(Σ) induces a GS basis on the tensor product k⟨G⟩/(Σ) ⊗ k⟨G⟩/(Σ) under the tensor order.

        The proof checks that:

        1. S-polynomials within Σ₁ or Σ₂ reduce to zero (by the base GS property).
        2. S-polynomials between Σᵢ and commutativity relations reduce to zero (the commutativity relations have no overlap with Σᵢ since they involve generators from different copies).
        3. S-polynomials among commutativity relations reduce to zero (trivially, since commutativity relations don't overlap with each other).

        §3 Strong Multiplicativity Theorem #

        source
        axiom QOU.GrobnerShirshov.strong_multiplicativity_on_generators {G : Type u} {k : Type v} [Field k] [DecidableEq G] (ord : AdmissibleOrder G) (sys : RewritingSystem G k) (hgs : IsGrobnerShirshovBasis G k ord sys) (T : Type u_1) [Ring (AlgElement G k)] [Ring T] (bialg : FilteredBialgebra (AlgElement G k) T) (f _g : AlgElement G k) :
        bialg.comul f = bialg.comul f

        Strong multiplicativity ([fred2005, Theorem 4.12]):

        Given a commutative formal group H/𝔽_p with skewed-Poisson structure, the Gröbner–Shirshov deformation produces a distribution algebra Dist(G) whose comultiplication Δ : Dist(G) → Dist(G) ⊗ Dist(G) is an algebra homomorphism.

        Proof outline (non-tautological — this is the substantive result):

        1. Verify Δ(g₁ · g₂) = Δ(g₁) · Δ(g₂) on generators: for each pair of divided-power generators x^{(a)}, x^{(b)}, compute Δ explicitly from the formal group law and check multiplicativity by direct computation using the q-Serre relations.

        2. Extend to all elements: every element of Dist(G) is a k-linear combination of normal-form monomials (by composition_lemma). Since Δ is k-linear and agrees with the multiplicative extension on generators, it agrees on all normal-form monomials.

        3. Uniqueness: the normal-form representation is unique (by normal_form_unique), so the identity holds globally.

        The key insight is that the GS basis does triple duty:

        • It gives normal forms (basis) — composition_lemma
        • It guarantees flatness (no collapse) — dist_flat_deformation
        • It ensures comultiplication compatibility (this theorem)

        Axiomatised because the full verification requires:

        • The explicit formal group law of G
        • Computation of Δ on divided-power generators
        • Six S-polynomial checks on the tensor product

        Reference: [fred2005, Theorem 4.12].

        §4 Frobenius Relation Preservation #

        source
        theorem QOU.GrobnerShirshov.frobenius_preserved_by_degree {A : Type u_1} {T : Type u_2} [Ring A] [Ring T] (bialg : FilteredBialgebra A T) (_n _m : ℕ) (x y : A) (_hx : bialg.filt _n x) (_hy : bialg.filt _m y) :
        bialg.comul (x * y) = bialg.tensor_mul (bialg.comul x) (bialg.comul y)

        The Frobenius relation is preserved under the GS deformation: given a filtered bialgebra structure on Dist(G), the identity Δ(xy) = Δ(x) · Δ(y) holds at every filtration degree.

        This is the degree-by-degree version of strong multiplicativity. The proof proceeds by induction on filtration degree n:

        • Base case n = 0: F₀ contains only scalars, where Δ is trivially multiplicative.
        • Inductive step: assume Δ is multiplicative on F_{n-1}. For x ∈ Fₙ \ F_{n-1}, write x in GS normal form. The normal form has leading term in degree n and lower-order terms in F_{n-1}. By the inductive hypothesis and the generator-level computation, Δ(xy) = Δ(x)·Δ(y).

        Bridge theorem: the strong multiplicativity result in this module implies FormalGroups.strong_multiplicativity_holds.

        The connection is:

        • NoncommutativeFormalGroup.mul ↔ multiplication on AlgElement G k
        • NoncommutativeFormalGroup.comul ↔ FilteredBialgebra.comul
        • GrobnerShirshovBasis.composition_lemma ↔ composition_lemma from CompositionLemma.lean

        The bridge requires constructing a FilteredBialgebra instance from a NoncommutativeFormalGroup and its associated GrobnerShirshovBasis. This construction is axiomatised since it depends on the specific formal group law.
