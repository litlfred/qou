    UGB.GrobnerShirshov.Quotient — the quotient algebra the framework talks about #

    Every statement of the main theorem is about A = k⟨G⟩ / I. The development had no quotient. AlgElement says so in its own docstring — "a representation, not a quotient" — and CompositionLemma.composition_lemma carries the claim

    a GS basis (all S-polynomials reduce to zero) implies that normal-form monomials form a k-basis of the quotient k⟨G⟩ / (Σ)

    in a comment, above a theorem whose type says only reduce (reduce f) = reduce f. The basis claim was not a proposition anywhere. This file makes it one.

    Two independent threads reached the same gap: bean qou-auq3 (the Hecke route to H_k for k > 4 needs normal forms to be an algebra) and bean qou-k6ks (Leitner–Pawloski's A is the quotient, so "no quasi-zeros" cannot bite without one).

    Why it could not be built directly #

    AlgElement is a raw structure carrying a coeff function and a support Finset that is only a superset of the true support. So structural equality is strictly finer than mathematical equality, and the arithmetic already in Elem.lean is not a Module on the nose:

    • smul c f keeps f.support even when c = 0, so (0 : k) • f has support f.support, not ∅ — zero_smul fails as a structure equation;
    • add unions supports, so associativity holds only up to Finset.union_assoc plus funext.

    Elem.lean anticipated this and defined CoeffEq f g := ∀ m, f.coeff m = g.coeff m as "the equality that means anything mathematically". Quotienting by it first is what makes everything below routine — and once FreeAlg is a Module, the ideal is a Submodule and the quotient algebra is Mathlib's, for free.

    What is proved here, and what is stated #

    Proved: CoeffEq is an equivalence; FreeAlg with its AddCommGroup and Module k structure; the two-sided ideal of a rewriting system as a Submodule; the quotient QuotAlg.

    Stated, not proved: NormalFormBasis — the property that the normal-form monomials form a k-basis of QuotAlg. It is a definition here, so that composition_lemma's comment becomes something a theorem can conclude and a consumer can require. Proving it is the diamond lemma, and it additionally needs the framework's standing axioms. Those were five when this file was written; they are now two — reduction_well_founded and local_confluence. confluence and normal_form_unique were proved from them (Newman's lemma), and normal_form_exists turned out to be false and was replaced by a theorem carrying the unit-leading-coefficient hypothesis it had dropped (beans qou-ch3j, qou-lzoo). Nothing here discharges the remaining two.

                      §6 The Composition–Diamond lemma, named and cited #

                      Bokut, Chen & Li, Gröbner–Shirshov bases for categories, arXiv:1101.1563, Theorem 4.4, proves for a small category C(X) that the following are equivalent:

                      • (i) S is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis in kC(X);
                      • (ii) f ∈ Id(S) ⇒ f̄ = a·s̄·b for some s ∈ S and words a, b;
                      • (iii) Irr(S) = {u | u ≠ a·s̄·b} is a linear basis of kC(X)/Id(S).

                      and closes with the Remark that decides its relevance here:

                      If the category in Theorem 4.4 has only one object, then Theorem 4.4 is exact Composition-Diamond lemma for free associative algebras.

                      The one-object case is precisely this development's setting. So Theorem 4.4 is the statement UGB's two remaining standing axioms (reduction_well_founded, local_confluence) collectively stand in for — five when this was written, before Newman's lemma discharged confluence and normal_form_unique and the false normal_form_exists was replaced by a theorem. Its clause (iii) is NormalFormBasis above — the paper's Irr(S) is {u | u ≠ a·s̄·b}, which is NormalFormMonomial's "no rule's leading monomial is an infix", the same condition.

                      Naming it gives the framework a single citable target in place of five assumptions, and makes the Hecke consequence below statable.
