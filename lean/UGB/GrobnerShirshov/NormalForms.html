    Normal Forms and PBW-Type Bases #

    This module specialises the abstract Gröbner–Shirshov framework of Basic.lean and CompositionLemma.lean to the two concrete algebras appearing in the QOU manuscript:

    1. U_q(su(2)) — the quantum group with q-Serre relations. The GS basis yields the Lusztig divided-power (PBW) basis {E_+^a · K^b · E_-^c : a,b,c ≥ 0}.

    2. Dist(G) — the distribution algebra of a non-commutative formal group over ℤ_p. The GS basis yields the flat deformation of Dist(H) from [fred2005, §3].

    Key results #

    • qSerre_is_gs_basis: the q-Serre relations form a GS basis under the deg-lex order on {E_+, K, K⁻¹, E_-}.
    • pbw_monomials_are_normal_forms: PBW monomials are exactly the normal-form monomials under the q-Serre rewriting system.
    • dist_algebra_gs_basis: the relations of Dist(G) form a GS basis.

    References #

    • [fred2005, §3, Proposition 3.8]
    • Lusztig, Introduction to Quantum Groups, Birkhäuser 1993, Ch. 3.
    • Bokut & Malcolmson, Gröbner–Shirshov bases for quantum enveloping algebras, Israel J. Math. 96 (1996), 97–113.

        SU2Gen → ℕ

        Numeric rank of each generator for the lexicographic order: E_- < E_+ < K_inv < K (ascending).

        Corrected twice, both times because the rules constrain it and nothing was checking.

        • qou-ix9e: originally K = 2 < E_+ = 3, which made rule 3's leading monomial [E_+, K] — its own right-hand side — so the system rewrote in the direction opposite to the one its docstring claimed.
        • qou-18s7: the five-rule system turned out not to be a Gröbner–Shirshov basis at all (see §3), and completing it adds the two K⁻¹ commutation rules, which impose two further constraints that the previous ranking violated.

        The five orientation constraints, one per non-inverse rule:

        rule needs
        3 K·E_+ → q²·E_+·K rank K > rank E_+
        4 K·E_- → q⁻²·E_-·K rank K > rank E_-
        5 E_+·E_- → E_-·E_+ + … rank E_+ > rank E_-
        6 K⁻¹·E_+ → q⁻²·E_+·K⁻¹ rank K_inv > rank E_+
        7 K⁻¹·E_- → q²·E_-·K⁻¹ rank K_inv > rank E_-

        Rules 1 and 2 constrain nothing: their leading monomials beat [] on length. This ranking satisfies all five, and qSerre_lm_1 … qSerre_lm_7 prove every leading monomial outright, so the orientation is checked rather than asserted.

                §2 The q-Serre Relations #

                source
                structure UGB.GrobnerShirshov.QSerreRelations (k : Type v) [Field k] :
                Type v

                The q-Serre relations defining U_q(su(2)):

                1. K · K⁻¹ = 1 and K⁻¹ · K = 1
                2. K · E_+ = q² · E_+ · K
                3. K · E_- = q⁻² · E_- · K
                4. E_+ · E_- − E_- · E_+ = (K − K⁻¹) / (q − q⁻¹)

                Each relation is oriented so that the left-hand side has the larger leading monomial under deg-lex. This yields four reduction rules.

                • q : k

                  The deformation parameter q ≠ 0, ±1.

                • q_ne_zero : self.q ≠ 0
                • q_ne_one : self.q ≠ 1
                • q_ne_neg_one : self.q ≠ -1

                  §3 GS Basis for U_q(su(2)) #

                  §3.1 The rewriting system, built rather than axiomatised #

                  The five oriented relations, with their leading monomials under deg-lex:

                  1. K · K⁻¹ → 1 (lm = [K, K_inv])
                  2. K⁻¹ · K → 1 (lm = [K_inv, K])
                  3. K · E_+ → q² · E_+ · K (lm = [K, E_plus])
                  4. K · E_- → q⁻² · E_- · K (lm = [K, E_minus])
                  5. E_+ · E_- → E_- · E_+ + (K − K⁻¹)/(q − q⁻¹) (lm = [E_plus, E_minus])

                  qSerreRewritingSystem was an axiom. It is now built, because an opaque system cannot be asked whether its rules are monic — and ugb_pbw_corollary needs exactly that in order to discharge the unit-leading-coefficient hypothesis of normal_form_exists rather than assume it (beans qou-lzoo, qou-ix9e).

                  Building it also made the leading monomials checkable, and the check failed against the old generator ranking: see the correction note on SU2Gen.rank. qSerre_lm_1 … qSerre_lm_5 now prove all five outright.

                                        axiom UGB.GrobnerShirshov.qSerre_is_gs_basis (k : Type v) [Field k] (qs : QSerreRelations k) :
                                        IsGrobnerShirshovBasis SU2Gen k degLexOrder (qSerreRewritingSystem k qs)

                                        Theorem (Bokut–Malcolmson 1996): The q-Serre relations form a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for U_q(su(2)) under the deg-lex order.

                                        The overlap analysis this docstring used to carry was wrong, and the five-rule system it described was not a Gröbner–Shirshov basis (bean qou-18s7). It listed six "S-polynomial computations", of which three — (1,3), (1,4), (3,4) — are not ambiguities at all: those pairs share a first letter, and an overlap requires a proper suffix of one leading word to equal a proper prefix of the other. It also recorded (2,3) as "no overlap", when [K⁻¹,K] followed by [K,E_+] overlaps at K and is in fact one of the two ambiguities that fail.

                                        The real analysis, computed exactly over ℚ(q) by computations/qserre_gs_basis_check.py:

                                        • the five-rule system has exactly 5 overlap ambiguities, and two of them do not resolve — both decisively, with each pair of reducts genuinely irreducible:

                                          ambiguity word one way the other
                                          R2/R3 K⁻¹ K E_+ E_+ q²·K⁻¹ E_+ K
                                          R2/R4 K⁻¹ K E_- E_- q⁻²·K⁻¹ E_- K

                                          Both are instances of the same gap: nothing rewrites K⁻¹ past E_±, so K⁻¹ E_± K is stuck.

                                        • adding the two missing rules (6 and 7, the K⁻¹ commutation rules) gives 8 ambiguities, and all 8 resolve.

                                        So this axiom is now stated of the completed seven-rule system, where the critical-pair check passes. That check is a symbolic computation, not a Lean proof: the axiom records a verified-but-not-formalised claim, which is a materially better position than the unverified one it was in, and materially worse than a theorem. Formalising the eight resolutions is the way to remove it.

                                        History worth keeping: this is the third claim in this file to be examined by making it checkable, and the second to be found false — see pbw_monomials_are_not_all_normal_forms for the other.

                                        Reference: Bokut & Malcolmson, Israel J. Math. 96 (1996).

                                        §4 PBW Basis #

                                        source
                                        structure UGB.GrobnerShirshov.PBWMonomial :
                                        Type

                                        A PBW monomial is a word of the form E_+^a · K^b · E_-^c. These are exactly the normal-form monomials under the deg-lex order with the q-Serre rewriting system: no leading monomial of any relation appears as a subword.

                                        • a : ℕ

                                              ⚠ The PBW normal-form claim, as it was stated, is FALSE.

                                              This used to be an axiom:

                                              every PBWMonomial is in normal form under the q-Serre system

                                              with PBWMonomial.toMonomial m = E_+^a ++ K^{b₊} ++ K⁻¹^{b₋} ++ E_-^c. Once qSerreRewritingSystem became a definition (§3.1) the claim became checkable, and it fails. ⟨a,b₊,b₋,c⟩ = ⟨0,1,1,0⟩ is the word [K, K⁻¹], which is exactly rule 1's leading monomial, so it is reducible.

                                              The axiom's own justification is where it went wrong. It argued that the PBW word "contains no forbidden subword … because generators appear in canonical order with each block homogeneous" — but the K block is immediately followed by the K⁻¹ block, which produces [K, K⁻¹] whenever both are non-empty. Homogeneity within blocks says nothing about the joins between them, and there are three bad joins, not one:

                                              join when is the lm of
                                              [K, K⁻¹] b₊ ≥ 1 and b₋ ≥ 1 rule 1
                                              [K, E_-] b₊ ≥ 1 and c ≥ 1 rule 4
                                              [E_+, E_-] a ≥ 1, b₊ = b₋ = 0, c ≥ 1 rule 5

                                              So the letter order E_+ … K … E_- is not the normal-form order for this system at all. Reading the rules' orientations off §3.1, reduction moves E_- left past E_+ (rule 5) and moves K right past both (rules 3, 4), so the normal words are E_-^c E_+^a K^b — with K and K⁻¹ never mixed.

                                              Nothing downstream is left resting on the false claim: ugb_pbw_corollary now takes normality as an explicit hypothesis rather than importing it from here. Establishing it for the corrected shape is open, and is the genuine remaining content of Bokut–Malcolmson Thm 3.2.

                                              Reference: Bokut & Malcolmson, Israel J. Math. 96 (1996), Theorem 3.2.
