    UGB.Introduction.main_theorem_statement #

    Real-content formalization (replaces the former vacuous class … where holds : Prop). The freeness theorem is stated as a concrete claim about the reduction system RewritingSystem G k: for a Gröbner–Shirshov basis, every algebra element has a unique normal form (the canonical representative of its class in A/I), and distinct elements of the remainder module Õ(G) — already in normal form — are their own representatives. This is the module-isomorphism content A/I ≅ Õ(G) at the level of the canonical-form map: it cannot be instantiated with True.

    Content block thm:ugb-main-theorem-statement: Freeness of quotient by unital Gröbner–Shirshov basis.

    Manuscript statement #

    Let $R$ be a commutative algebra with unity, $A$ an $R$-algebra free as an $R$-module without quasi-zeros, $(B,<)$ an admissible system, and $I \trianglelefteq A$ a two-sided ideal with a unital Gröbner–Shirshov basis $G$. Let $\widetilde{O}(G)$ be the free $R$-module spanned by the monomials that are not leading monomials of members of $G$. Then (1) $A/I \cong \widetilde{O}(G)$ as free $R$-modules, and (2) $A = I \oplus \widetilde{O}(G)$.

    What is weakened relative to the manuscript #

    The manuscript asserts a full $R$-module isomorphism and direct-sum decomposition. We state its algorithmic core: the canonical-form map $A \to \widetilde{O}(G)$ is well-defined (existence) and class-injective (uniqueness of normal forms). Concretely this is the conjunction of ReducesToNF (every element reaches a normal form, i.e. an element of Õ(G)) and normal_form_unique (the normal form is unique), which is exactly the statement that each A/I-class has a unique Õ(G)-representative. The R-linearity of the resulting bijection and the internal-direct-sum statement are not formalized here (they require the full module structure on AlgElement, which is a raw data type — see AlgOps.lean §6). Both halves are now proved: existence is normal_form_exists, from reduction_well_founded plus the hypothesis hlc that every rule has a unit leading coefficient (bean qou-lzoo — the axiom of that name it used to invoke was false, refuted in NormalFormExistsRefutation); uniqueness is normal_form_unique.

    theorem UGB.Introduction.main_theorem_statement {G : Type u} {k : Type v} [Ring k] [DecidableEq G] (ord : GrobnerShirshov.AdmissibleOrder G) (sys : GrobnerShirshov.RewritingSystem G k) (hgs : GrobnerShirshov.IsGrobnerShirshovBasis G k ord sys) (hnqz : GrobnerShirshov.NoQuasiZerosFree G k) (hlc : ∀ r ∈ sys.rules, IsUnit (r.lc ord)) (f : GrobnerShirshov.AlgElement G k) :
    (∃ (nf : GrobnerShirshov.AlgElement G k), GrobnerShirshov.ReducesToNF ord sys f nf) ∧ ∀ (nf₁ nf₂ : GrobnerShirshov.AlgElement G k), GrobnerShirshov.ReducesToNF ord sys f nf₁ → GrobnerShirshov.ReducesToNF ord sys f nf₂ → nf₁ = nf₂

    Freeness theorem — canonical-form core.

    For a Gröbner–Shirshov basis sys (admissible order ord), every algebra element f admits a normal form, and any two normal forms of f coincide. The normal forms are precisely the elements of the remainder module Õ(G) (those whose support avoids every leading monomial of sys), so this is the statement that the canonical-form map A → Õ(G) descends to a well-defined injection A/I ↪ Õ(G) — the substantive content the former holds : Prop lacked.

    The conclusion is a concrete conjunction over AlgElement (an existential ReducesToNF plus a ∀-uniqueness clause), not a bare Prop field.
