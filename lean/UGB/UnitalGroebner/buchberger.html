    Content block thm:buchberger: Buchberger's theorem over commutative rings.

    Manuscript statement #

    Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity, $A$ an $R$-algebra free as an $R$-module, $(B,<)$ an admissible system, $I \leq A$ an ideal generated by a unital set $G=\{g_\gamma\mid\gamma\in\Gamma\}$. Then $G$ is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for $I$ iff all S-polynomials for $G$ have zero remainder under the division algorithm.

    What is weakened relative to the manuscript #

    The full biconditional (with the "zero-remainder of all S-polynomials" side) is recorded separately, on the concrete S-polynomial structures, in BuchbergerPBW.buchberger_theorem. Here we formalize the principal content of "G is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis": the resulting reduction is confluent. Operationally this is what makes the division algorithm well-defined — it is the property that distinguishes a GS basis from an arbitrary generating set. The statement is a concrete existence of a common reduct (MultiStepReduction), proved from the confluence result of CompositionLemma.lean (itself the Newman's-lemma corollary of the resolved S-polynomials). No sorry.

    theorem UGB.UnitalGroebner.buchberger {G : Type u} {k : Type v} [Ring k] [DecidableEq G] (ord : GrobnerShirshov.AdmissibleOrder G) (sys : GrobnerShirshov.RewritingSystem G k) (hgs : GrobnerShirshov.IsGrobnerShirshovBasis G k ord sys) (hnqz : GrobnerShirshov.NoQuasiZerosFree G k) (f g₁ g₂ : GrobnerShirshov.AlgElement G k) (h₁ : GrobnerShirshov.MultiStepReduction ord sys f g₁) (h₂ : GrobnerShirshov.MultiStepReduction ord sys f g₂) :
    ∃ (h : GrobnerShirshov.AlgElement G k), GrobnerShirshov.MultiStepReduction ord sys g₁ h ∧ GrobnerShirshov.MultiStepReduction ord sys g₂ h

    Buchberger's theorem — confluence consequence.

    A Gröbner–Shirshov basis sys is confluent: whenever f →* g₁ and f →* g₂, there is a common reduct h with g₁ →* h and g₂ →* h. This is the operational meaning of "G is a GS basis for I" — the division algorithm produces well-defined remainders.

    The conclusion is a concrete existential over AlgElement (a common MultiStepReduction target), not a bare Prop. Proved from confluence. The manuscript's standing hypothesis that the algebra has no quasi-zeros (Leitner–Pawloski; monomial-basis.md) is carried explicitly as hnqz: it is what the S-polynomial half of the composition lemma needs, and it is discharged for the free algebra on any non-empty generating set by noQuasiZerosFree_of_generator.
