Abstract

This paper presents a mathematical framework for the Quantum Observable Universe (QOU). We introduce the notion of quantum torsion in this setting, study its lifting to higher categorical structures, and examine the resulting interpretational and experimental consequences. Speculative extensions are discussed in the final chapter.

Quantum Observable Universe

Definition 1.1 (\(\mathbb{C}\) Quantum Universe). A \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum universe is a triple \((M, G, E)\) where:

  1. \(M\) is a CR manifold;

  2. \(G = \mathrm{SU}(2)\);

  3. \(E \to M\) is a principal \(G\)-bundle (called the bulk) with fibres \(\mathbb{C}\) and \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\) holonomy.

Definition 1.2 (\(\mathbb{C}\) Quantum Observable Universe). A \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe is a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum universe \((M, G, E)\) (Definition 1.1) that satisfies the following compactly supported cohomology conditions: \[H^0_c(M, \mathrm{Ad}(E)) = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad H^0_c(M, E) = 0.\]

Remark 1.3 (Jet-bundle formulation). The conditions in Definition 1.2 admit a natural reformulation in terms of the first jet bundle \(J^1 E\). The vanishing \(H^0_c(M, E) = 0\) ensures that no non-trivial compactly supported section of \(E\) exists, while \(H^0_c(M, \mathrm{Ad}(E)) = 0\) eliminates compactly supported infinitesimal gauge symmetries. Together, these conditions mean that the space of sections of \(J^1 E\) with compact support injects into the space of connections, preventing any local degeneracy of the gauge field.

Lemma 1.4 (Uniqueness of the connection). Let \((M, G, E)\) be a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe. Then the vanishing conditions \[H^0_c(M, \mathrm{Ad}(E)) = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad H^0_c(M, E) = 0\] imply that the connection \(A\) on \(E\) is unique up to gauge equivalence.

Example 1.6 (Calabi–Yau manifolds). Let \(M\) be a Calabi–Yau manifold. The Ricci-flat Kähler metric on \(M\) endows it with a natural CR structure, and the trivial \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\)-bundle \(E = M \times \mathrm{SU}(2)\) yields a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe when the compactly supported cohomology conditions of Definition 1.2 are satisfied.

Example 1.7 (ALE spaces). Let \(M\) be an asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE) space. ALE spaces arise as minimal resolutions of quotient singularities \(\mathbb{C}^2 / \Gamma\) for finite subgroups \(\Gamma \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)\). Equipped with a hyper-Kähler metric and the corresponding principal \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\)-bundle, \(M\) provides a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe satisfying self-dual curvature (Proposition 1.5).

Example 1.8 (Descartes universe). Let \(M = \mathbb{C}^2\). We call the resulting \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe the Descartes universe.

Definition 1.9 (Substrate parameter). Let \((M, G, E)\) be a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe with connection \(A\). Write \(F_A = dA + A \wedge A\) for the curvature and let \(F_A^+\) denote its self-dual part. Let \(L \in \Gamma(T^*M \otimes \overline{T^*M})\) be the Levi form, a Hermitian form on the tangent bundle of \(M\). The substrate parameter is the function \(q \colon M \to \mathbb{R}\) defined by \[q(p) \;=\; \exp\!\bigl(-\lVert L \wedge F_A^+ \rVert(p)\bigr), \qquad p \in M.\]

Definition 1.10 (Quantum Gauge Group). Let \((M, G, E)\) be a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe with substrate parameter \(q\) (Definition 1.9). The quantum gauge group is the quantum group \(\mathrm{SU}_q(2)\) obtained by deforming the structure group \(G = \mathrm{SU}(2)\) at the parameter \(q\).

Remark 1.11 (\(J^1E\) as the space of local states). The jet bundle \(J^1 E\) serves as the space of local states of the \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe. The classical gauge dynamics, encoded by the instanton connection \(A\) (Lemma 1.4), are weighted by the substrate parameter \(q\) (Definition 1.9) to define the quantum field algebra \(\mathrm{SU}_q(2)\) (Definition 1.10).

Remark 1.12 (General Relativity as the “Substrate Geometry”). In this model the metric is effectively replaced by the Levi form \(L\) and its coupling to the gauge curvature \(F_A\). Gravity is expressed through the Variational Bicomplex as the back-reaction of the gauge fields on the CR manifold \(M\).

The Einstein–Hilbert action is recovered by examining the variation of the action \(S_q\) with respect to the Levi form \(L\): \[\frac{\delta S_q}{\delta L} \;\implies\; G_{\mu\nu} \;\sim\; T_{\mu\nu}^{(q)},\] where \(T_{\mu\nu}^{(q)}\) is a quantum-weighted stress-energy tensor. Because \(q(p) = \exp\!\bigl(-\lVert L \wedge F^+\rVert\bigr)\), the geometry of the brane \(M\) is forced to curve to accommodate the tension of the \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) instantons.

Remark 1.13 (The Schrödinger Equation as “Jet Co-action”). The Schrödinger equation describes the evolution of a state in the fibre \(\mathbb{C}\). In the jet-bundle framework this is expressed as the condition of parallel transport along the connection \(A\).

For a section \(\psi \in \Gamma(E)\), the Schrödinger dynamics are found in the kernel of the \(q\)-deformed covariant derivative on \(J^1 E\): \[i\,\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}(p)\, D_A \psi \;=\; \hat{H}_q\, \psi,\] where \(\hat{H}_q\) is the \(q\)-deformed Hamiltonian operator and:

  • \(\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}(p) = 1 - q(p)\): the Planck constant is a local field;

  • \(D_A = d + A\): the connection \(A\) (the instanton) acts as the potential.

As \(q(p) \to 1\) (classicality), the uncertainty vanishes and the wave-like nature of the section freezes into a classical trajectory.

Remark 1.14 (Main Commutative Diagram). The relationship between these two seemingly disparate equations is captured by the main commutative diagram of the \(\mathbb{C}\)-quantum universe, where \(\mathbf{E}\) denotes the Euler–Lagrange operator. The link is the substrate parameter \(q(p)\): \[\begin{CD} J^1E @>{\text{Variational Derivative } \mathbf{E}}>> \text{General Relativity (Geometry)} \\ @V{\text{Jet Co-action}}VV @VV{\text{Coupling via } q(p)}V \\ \Gamma(E) @>{\text{Parallel Transport}}>> \text{Schr\"odinger Eq.\ (Quantum States)} \end{CD}\]
