Abstract

This paper presents a mathematical framework for the Quantum Observable Universe (QOU). We introduce the notion of quantum torsion in this setting, study its lifting to higher categorical structures, and examine the resulting interpretational and experimental consequences. Speculative extensions are discussed in the final chapter.

Quantum Observable Universe

Definition 1.1 (\(\mathbb{C}\) Quantum Universe). A \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum universe is a triple \((M, G, E)\) where:

  1. \(M\) is a CR manifold;

  2. \(G = \mathrm{SU}(2)\);

  3. \(E \to M\) is a principal \(G\)-bundle (called the bulk) with fibres \(\mathbb{C}\) and \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\) holonomy.

Definition 1.2 (\(\mathbb{C}\) Quantum Observable Universe). A \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe is a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum universe \((M, G, E)\) (Definition 1.1) that satisfies the following compactly supported cohomology conditions: \[H^0_c(M, \mathrm{Ad}(E)) = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad H^0_c(M, E) = 0.\]

Remark 1.3 (Jet-bundle formulation). The conditions in Definition 1.2 admit a natural reformulation in terms of the first jet bundle \(J^1 E\). The vanishing \(H^0_c(M, E) = 0\) ensures that no non-trivial compactly supported section of \(E\) exists, while \(H^0_c(M, \mathrm{Ad}(E)) = 0\) eliminates compactly supported infinitesimal gauge symmetries. Together, these conditions mean that the space of sections of \(J^1 E\) with compact support injects into the space of connections, preventing any local degeneracy of the gauge field.

Lemma 1.4 (Uniqueness of the connection). Let \((M, G, E)\) be a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe. Then the vanishing conditions \[H^0_c(M, \mathrm{Ad}(E)) = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad H^0_c(M, E) = 0\] imply that the connection \(A\) on \(E\) is unique up to gauge equivalence.

Example 1.6 (Calabi–Yau manifolds). Let \(M\) be a Calabi–Yau manifold. The Ricci-flat Kähler metric on \(M\) endows it with a natural CR structure, and the trivial \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\)-bundle \(E = M \times \mathrm{SU}(2)\) yields a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe when the compactly supported cohomology conditions of Definition 1.2 are satisfied.

Example 1.7 (ALE spaces). Let \(M\) be an asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE) space. ALE spaces arise as minimal resolutions of quotient singularities \(\mathbb{C}^2 / \Gamma\) for finite subgroups \(\Gamma \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)\). Equipped with a hyper-Kähler metric and the corresponding principal \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\)-bundle, \(M\) provides a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe satisfying self-dual curvature (Proposition 1.5).

Example 1.8 (Descartes universe). Let \(M = \mathbb{C}^2\) and assume \(\chi(E) = -6\). We call the resulting \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe the Descartes universe.

Remark 1.9 (Descartes universe and the CMB). In the Descartes universe (Example 1.8), the Euler characteristic \(\chi(E) = -6\) constrains the topology of the bulk bundle \(E\). The \(q\)-harmonic forms on this bundle (Definition 1.15) determine the spectrum of stable excitations. In the regime \(q(p) \to 1\) (near-classicality), the zero-modes of \(\Delta_q\) reproduce the thermal fluctuation spectrum of the Cosmic Microwave Background: the Hodge numbers of \(E\) govern the multiplicity of angular modes, and the negative Euler characteristic forces a non-trivial distribution of these modes across the CR manifold \(M = \mathbb{C}^2\).

Remark 1.10 (Electrons, muons and the Reeb decomposition). The Reeb decomposition (Definition 1.16) applied to the Descartes universe yields two classes of fermionic excitations (Definition 1.21). The degree of the vertical component \(\alpha_V\) distinguishes them: forms with \(\deg\alpha_V = 1\) correspond to electrons, while forms with \(\deg\alpha_V = 2\) correspond to muons. More generally, each additional unit of vertical degree is identified with a successive lepton generation. The constraint \(\chi(E) = -6\) bounds the number of such generations, since the Hodge decomposition of \(E\) admits only finitely many independent vertical harmonic forms.

Remark 1.11 (Chirality as Reeb orientation). The Reeb vector field \(\xi\) on the CR manifold \(M\) carries a natural orientation: the sign of the contact form \(\theta(\xi) = 1\) singles out a preferred direction along the vertical distribution. Left-handed and right-handed chiralities correspond to the two orientations of \(\xi\) relative to the complex structure of \(M\). For fermions (Definition 1.21), the vertical component \(\alpha_V\) transforms with a definite sign under reversal of \(\xi\), so chirality is encoded in the \(\pm\)-eigenspaces of the Reeb involution \(\xi \mapsto -\xi\). Bosons (Definition 1.22), being purely horizontal, are chirality-blind — consistent with the observed parity invariance of the electromagnetic and strong interactions.

Definition 1.12 (Substrate parameter). Let \((M, G, E)\) be a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe with connection \(A\). Write \(F_A = dA + A \wedge A\) for the curvature and let \(F_A^+\) denote its self-dual part. Let \(L \in \Gamma(T^*M \otimes \overline{T^*M})\) be the Levi form, a Hermitian form on the tangent bundle of \(M\). The substrate parameter is the function \(q \colon M \to \mathbb{R}\) defined by \[q(p) \;=\; \exp\!\bigl(-\lVert L \wedge F_A^+ \rVert(p)\bigr), \qquad p \in M.\]

Definition 1.13 (Quantum Gauge Group). Let \((M, G, E)\) be a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe with substrate parameter \(q\) (Definition 1.12). The quantum gauge group is the quantum group \(\mathrm{SU}_q(2)\) obtained by deforming the structure group \(G = \mathrm{SU}(2)\) at the parameter \(q\).

Remark 1.14 (\(J^1E\) as the space of local states). The jet bundle \(J^1 E\) serves as the space of local states of the \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe. The classical gauge dynamics, encoded by the instanton connection \(A\) (Lemma 1.4), are weighted by the substrate parameter \(q\) (Definition 1.12) to define the quantum field algebra \(\mathrm{SU}_q(2)\) (Definition 1.13).

Definition 1.15 (\(q\)-Harmonic form (Particle)). Let \((M, G, E)\) be a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe with substrate parameter \(q\) (Definition 1.12). Let \(d_q\) denote the \(q\)-deformed exterior derivative on the CR manifold \(M\). A particle is a section \(\alpha \in \Gamma(M,\, \Lambda^k T^*M \otimes J^1 E)\) that is \(q\)-harmonic, i.e. lies in the kernel of the \(q\)-Laplacian: \[\Delta_q \alpha \;=\; (d_q\,\delta_q + \delta_q\,d_q)\,\alpha \;=\; 0,\] where \(\delta_q\) is the formal adjoint of \(d_q\). The condition \(\Delta_q\alpha = 0\) ensures that the form sits in a ground state of the \(q\)-viscosity.

Definition 1.16 (Reeb decomposition). Let \(M\) be a CR manifold equipped with a contact form \(\theta\) and let \(\xi\) denote the associated Reeb vector field, characterised by \(\iota_\xi\,d\theta = 0\) and \(\theta(\xi) = 1\). Every differential form \(\alpha\) on \(M\) decomposes as \[\alpha \;=\; \alpha_H + \alpha_V, \qquad\text{where}\qquad \iota_\xi\,\alpha_V \neq 0,\] with \(\alpha_H\) the horizontal component (annihilated by \(\iota_\xi\)) and \(\alpha_V\) the vertical component (having a non-trivial contraction with \(\xi\)).

Definition 1.17 (\(q\)-Deformed Reeb vector field). Let \((M, G, E)\) be a \(\mathbb{C}\) quantum observable universe with substrate parameter \(q\) (Definition 1.12) and Reeb vector field \(\xi\) (Definition 1.16). The \(q\)-deformed Reeb vector field is the vector field \(\xi_q\) on \(M\) defined by its action on smooth functions \(f \in C^\infty(M)\): \[\xi_q(f) \;=\; q\,\xi(f) + (1 - q)\,[\xi, X_f]_q,\] where \(X_f\) is the horizontal lift of \(\mathrm{grad}\,f\) to \(H\) and \([\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]_q\) denotes the \(q\)-deformed Lie bracket on \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). When \(q = 1\), one recovers the classical Reeb field \(\xi_1 = \xi\).

Definition 1.18 (\(q\)-Deformed Reeb flow). Let \(\xi_q\) be the \(q\)-deformed Reeb vector field (Definition 1.17). The \(q\)-deformed Reeb flow is the one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms \(\phi_t^q \colon M \to M\), \(t \in \mathbb{R}\), generated by \(\xi_q\): \[\frac{d}{dt}\,\phi_t^q \;=\; \xi_q \circ \phi_t^q, \qquad \phi_0^q = \mathrm{id}_M.\]

Definition 1.19 (\(q\)-Torsion leaf). Let \(H = \ker\theta \subset TM\) be the horizontal distribution on the CR manifold \(M\). A \(q\)-torsion leaf is a maximal integral submanifold \(L \hookrightarrow M\) of \(H\) that is invariant under the \(q\)-deformed Reeb flow (Definition 1.18): \[T_p L \;\subset\; H_p \quad\text{for all } p \in L, \qquad\text{and}\qquad \phi_t^q(L) = L \quad\text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}.\]

Remark 1.20 (\(q\)-Torsion leaves as energy minima). Let \(A_q\) denote the \(q\)-deformed connection on \(E\) and define the \(q\)-curvature \[\Omega_q \;=\; dA_q + \tfrac{1}{2}\,[A_q,\, A_q]_q.\] A \(q\)-torsion leaf \(L\) (Definition 1.19) is a local minimum of \(\lVert\Omega_q\rVert\) restricted to the horizontal distribution: the invariance of \(L\) under \(\phi_t^q\) forces the first variation of \(\lVert\Omega_q|_L\rVert\) to vanish, so \(L\) sits in a \(q\)-curvature well. Physically, a \(q\)-torsion leaf represents a region of the CR manifold where the gauge curvature is minimised and the \(q\)-deformed dynamics are in equilibrium.

Definition 1.21 (Fermion). A fermion is a \(q\)-harmonic form (Definition 1.15) that has a non-trivial vertical component in the Reeb decomposition (Definition 1.16): \[\Delta_q\,\alpha = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \iota_\xi\,\alpha_V \neq 0.\]

Definition 1.22 (Boson). A boson is a \(q\)-harmonic form (Definition 1.15) that is purely horizontal in the Reeb decomposition (Definition 1.16): \[\Delta_q\,\alpha = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \alpha = \alpha_H \quad(\text{equivalently, } \iota_\xi\,\alpha = 0 \text{ by Definition~\ref{def:reeb-decomposition}}).\]

Remark 1.23 (Physical interpretation of the Reeb decomposition). The Reeb vector field \(\xi\) singles out the direction of internal spin on the CR manifold. Forms that “see” this direction (fermions) carry half-integer spin; forms confined to the horizontal distribution (bosons) do not couple to the Reeb flow and carry integer spin. The decomposition \(\alpha = \alpha_H + \alpha_V\) is therefore the geometric origin of the spin–statistics distinction in this framework.

Remark 1.24 (Physical interpretation of \(q\)-harmonicity). The \(q\)-Laplacian \(\Delta_q\) encodes the \(q\)-deformed viscosity of the CR geometry. A \(q\)-harmonic form is a zero-mode of this operator and thus represents a stable excitation — a particle — of the quantum observable universe. The deformation parameter \(q\) interpolates between the classical regime (\(q \to 1\), where \(\Delta_q\) reduces to the Hodge Laplacian) and the deep-quantum regime (\(q \to 0\)), so the notion of “particle” is itself \(q\)-dependent.

Remark 1.25 (General Relativity as the “Substrate Geometry”). In this model the metric is effectively replaced by the Levi form \(L\) and its coupling to the gauge curvature \(F_A\). Gravity is expressed through the Variational Bicomplex as the back-reaction of the gauge fields on the CR manifold \(M\).

The Einstein–Hilbert action is recovered by examining the variation of the action \(S_q\) with respect to the Levi form \(L\): \[\frac{\delta S_q}{\delta L} \;\implies\; G_{\mu\nu} \;\sim\; T_{\mu\nu}^{(q)},\] where \(T_{\mu\nu}^{(q)}\) is a quantum-weighted stress-energy tensor. Because \(q(p) = \exp\!\bigl(-\lVert L \wedge F^+\rVert\bigr)\), the geometry of the brane \(M\) is forced to curve to accommodate the tension of the \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) instantons.

Remark 1.26 (The Schrödinger Equation as “Jet Co-action”). The Schrödinger equation describes the evolution of a state in the fibre \(\mathbb{C}\). In the jet-bundle framework this is expressed as the condition of parallel transport along the connection \(A\).

For a section \(\psi \in \Gamma(E)\), the Schrödinger dynamics are found in the kernel of the \(q\)-deformed covariant derivative on \(J^1 E\): \[i\,\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}(p)\, D_A \psi \;=\; \hat{H}_q\, \psi,\] where \(\hat{H}_q\) is the \(q\)-deformed Hamiltonian operator and:

  • \(\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}(p) = 1 - q(p)\): the Planck constant is a local field;

  • \(D_A = d + A\): the connection \(A\) (the instanton) acts as the potential.

As \(q(p) \to 1\) (classicality), the uncertainty vanishes and the wave-like nature of the section freezes into a classical trajectory.

Remark 1.27 (Main Commutative Diagram). The relationship between these two seemingly disparate equations is captured by the main commutative diagram of the \(\mathbb{C}\)-quantum universe, where \(\mathbf{E}\) denotes the Euler–Lagrange operator. The link is the substrate parameter \(q(p)\): \[\begin{CD} J^1E @>{\text{Variational Derivative } \mathbf{E}}>> \text{General Relativity (Geometry)} \\ @V{\text{Jet Co-action}}VV @VV{\text{Coupling via } q(p)}V \\ \Gamma(E) @>{\text{Parallel Transport}}>> \text{Schr\"odinger Eq.\ (Quantum States)} \end{CD}\]
