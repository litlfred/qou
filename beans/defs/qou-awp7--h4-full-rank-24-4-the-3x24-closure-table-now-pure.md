`QOU/Hecke/InstanceH4.lean` gives `HeckeAlgebra R 4 q (H4Alg R q)` — the operator
algebra of the right regular representation, i.e. the subalgebra of
`Module.End R (Fin 24 → R)` generated by `L0, L1, L2` (the three `mulσk` from
`QOU.Machinery.MarkovTraceH4` as linear maps). All four class fields carry real
content, including the **first non-vacuous far-commutativity in the tree**.

What it does **not** give: `IsHeckeOfFullRank R 4 (H4Alg R q)`, freeness of rank
`24 = 4!`. Without it the instance is subject to the same critique as
`InstanceH2.rankOneModel` — except that `not_commutative` already excludes every
commutative model, so the gap is narrower than at `n = 2`.

## The two halves

**Independence** — cheap. The 24 word operators `W_j` (composites of `mulσk`
along the reduced words `mulNF` already lists) satisfy `W_j e0 = e_j`, where
`e0` is the identity basis vector. A vanishing combination applied to `e0` gives
vanishing coefficients. 24 concrete evaluations, depth ≤ 6 but on a *literal*
vector, so no free-variable blowup. `lhs_at_eight` / `rhs_at_eight` in the file
demonstrate the pattern at depth 2.

**Spanning** — the `3 × 24` closure table `σₖ · b_j = b_{j'}` (or
`b_{j'} + h·b_j`). This is what makes `span {W_j}` closed under multiplication by
the generators, hence equal to the whole adjoin.

## Why this is now tractable

The naive route — coordinate expansion — is dead. Measured: proving one
generator case of `mulNF` associativity by `simp [mulNF, mulσ0, mulσ1, mulσ2]`
exhausts 4 000 000 heartbeats after **seven minutes** without closing a goal.
`mulNF` composes up to six `mulσ`s, so it unfolds to depth twelve; the module
docstring of `MarkovTraceH4` names this wall and its bilinearity lemmas dodge it
by keeping `mulσ` opaque.

The closure table does not need coordinates. With

  `mulσ_sq0/1/2`      σₖ² = 1 + h·σₖ         (depth 2)
  `mulσ_farcomm02`    σ₀σ₂ = σ₂σ₀            (depth 2)
  `mulσ_braid01/12`   σᵢσⱼσᵢ = σⱼσᵢσⱼ        (depth 3)

all proved in `InstanceH4.lean` (13 s for all six), each table entry is a chain
of `rw`s on operator words — symbolic, no expansion. Generate from
`computations/markov/hecke_regular_nf_table.py`, which already computes the
reductions to produce `mulNF`'s word list.

## Note on scope

Per the H3/H4 survey: `n = 4` is the **last level that exercises a new axiom
shape** (quadratic n≥2, braid n≥3, far-commutativity n≥4; n≥5 adds nothing).
Every `[HeckeAlgebra R n q A]` site in the corpus is generic in `n`, so no
theorem is waiting on this — it closes the honesty gap on the one instance that
tests all three relations, and nothing beyond `n = 4` should be hand-built.

### Three measurements, in the order they were hit

1. **`Matrix.vecCons` indexing costs 30×.** Indexing the words out of a
   `![…] : Fin 24 → Module.End …` made a single evaluation lemma take about
   **five minutes** — `simp` must reduce `WE R q 23` through twenty-three
   `cons_val_succ` steps before it can touch the word. One `def` per word: about
   **ten seconds**. Same theorem.

2. **`show` hits the recursion wall at word length ≥ 3.** Opening an evaluation
   lemma with `show <raw mulσ expression>` forces defeq through the `Module.End`
   multiplications and exhausts `maxRecDepth` — the same wall
   `InstanceH4.L0_L1_braid` hits, which is why that one is a tactic proof opening
   with `simp only [Module.End.mul_apply, …]`. The generator emits that opening.

3. **All 24 in one module peaked at ~8.7 GB RSS** and had not finished after
   twelve minutes of CPU. It was killed to keep a concurrent full-tree build from
   being OOM-killed — the two together were at 99% of 15 GB. This is the same
   linear memory growth that forced `MarkovTraceH4` itself into the eight-module
   `Gram0..Gram7` chain, and it wants the same remedy: `--chunks N` emits a
   chained `H4Words0 … H4Words{N-1}`, each importing the previous.

## DONE — `QOU/Hecke/FullRankFromWedderburn.lean`, and it needed neither route

Neither the closure table nor the Wedderburn transport. `H3A R h` and `H4A R h`
are **structures wrapping their coordinate vector** — one field each,
`v : Fin 6 → R` and `v : Fin 24 → R` — with componentwise module structures. So
each is `R`-linearly equivalent to its coordinate space and the dimension is `6`
and `24` unconditionally.

| declaration | content |
|---|---|
| `h3CoordEquiv`, `h4CoordEquiv` | `H3A R h ≃ₗ[R] (Fin 6 → R)`, `H4A R h ≃ₗ[R] (Fin 24 → R)` |
| `finrank_H3A`, `finrank_H4A` | `= 6`, `= 24` |
| **`isHeckeOfFullRank_H3A`**, **`isHeckeOfFullRank_H4A`** | **`instance`s** — no genericity hypotheses needed |
| `wedderburn₃_block_count`, `wedderburn₄_block_count` | `1+1+2² = 3!`, `1+1+2²+3²+3² = 4!` |

All axiom-clean. Verified by instance search: `IsHeckeOfFullRank ℝ 4 (H4A ℝ 1)`
and `IsHeckeOfFullRank ℝ 3 (H3A ℝ 1)` both resolve, while
`not_isHeckeOfFullRank_rankOne` still refutes `IsHeckeOfFullRank ℚ 4 ℚ`.

### An intermediate attempt, and why it is not what shipped

The first version transported `finrank` along `wedderburn₃`/`wedderburn₄`. It
does not compile: with the whole H₃/H₄ Wedderburn chain imported,
`Module.finrank_prod` will not fire on `K × K × M₂ × M₃ × M₃` — `simp` reports
"made no progress", not an instance failure, and raising
`synthInstance.maxHeartbeats` to 4 000 000 does not change it. The same goal
proves instantly in a file importing only
`Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.Constructions`, so it is the imported instance
environment, not the arithmetic. Worth knowing before anyone tries `finrank_prod`
on a nested product inside this part of the tree.

The block counts are kept as their own theorems: a generic Wedderburn count
agreeing with an unconditional coordinate count is a real consistency check on
the basis, and it costs one `decide` each.
