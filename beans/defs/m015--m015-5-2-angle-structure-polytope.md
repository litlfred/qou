The corpus has angle-structure polytopes for **m004** (figure-eight,
`QOU/Knot/M004AngleStructure.lean`) and **L6a4** (`L6a4AngleStructure.lean`, generated by
`computations/probes/gen_l6a4_angle_structure_lean.py`). There is **no m015** — the SnapPy
census name for the 5₂ complement. That is the missing anchor on the geometric route to
`Vol(5₂)`.

**Deliverable.** The 5₂ gluing equations in π-units as an affine set, via the corpus's own
`gluing` / `ga_equalities` helpers (the same path `borromean_angle_structure_lp_probe.py`
and `gen_l6a4_angle_structure_lean.py` already use), plus the coordinate-permutation
symmetry group found by exhaustive search — as was done for m004, where the search over all
720 permutations is what established the order-4 group was *complete*, not a lucky subgroup.

**Expect the m004 surprise to recur, and check for it explicitly.** For m004 the bean's
assumption that the symmetric point is unique was **FALSE** — the fixed locus inside the
affine set is a *line*, `(t, 1−2t, t, 1−2t, t, t)`, not a point (`fixed_iff_line`, proved as
an iff). Symmetry alone did not pin the optimum; the min-angle objective finished the job on
the line. Do **not** assume m015 behaves better. 5₂ has 3 tetrahedra to m004's 2 and less
symmetry (5₂ is chiral, 4₁ is amphichiral), so the fixed locus is likely to be *larger*, and
the min-angle argument that rescued m004 may not close it.

**Gated on `rvnc`** for the volume statement: without Rivin concavity this gives the
angle-structure polytope and a max-min-angle optimum, but not `Vol(5₂) = 3·D(y₁)`. The
polytope is still worth having on its own — it is the object every other route needs.

**What this does NOT give.** The volume conjecture (the Kashaev asymptotic). That is bean
`thmb`. `Vol(5₂) = 3·D(y₁)` and `lim (2π/N)log⟨5₂⟩_N = Vol(5₂)` are different statements and
must not be conflated in any claim.

**Control.** Run the same pipeline on the trefoil `3₁`. It is not hyperbolic, so its gluing
system must admit **no** positively-oriented solution — the angle-structure polytope should
come out empty. That is the check that the pipeline is detecting geometry rather than
solving a linear system and calling the answer a volume.

**2026-08-10 — the method extends to 23 manifolds; and a novelty claim withdrawn.**

Two questions asked of the previous entry, answered.

### 1. ⚠ NONE OF THIS IS A NEW RESULT. The previous entry read as though it might be.

Checked against the literature. The Gröbner derivation of the shape field from the
gluing ideal is standard, not new:

- Gluing equations and their variety: **Thurston (1978)**, **Neumann–Zagier (1985)**.
- Computing a triangulation's shape field is exactly what **`snap`** does
  (Coulson–Goodman–Hodgson–Neumann, *Computing arithmetic invariants of 3-manifolds*,
  Experiment. Math. 9 (2000)), and what SnapPy exposes as
  `tetrahedra_field_gens().find_field()` — which needs Sage, which is the only reason
  this probe re-implements identification instead of calling it.
- By **Neumann–Reid**, the shape field of a cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold *is* its
  invariant trace field — so every field in the table above is a **published
  invariant**, not a discovery. That includes 6₁'s quartic and 7₂'s quintic.
- Exact solution of gluing/Ptolemy varieties by Gröbner bases is standard:
  **Garoufalidis–Goerner–Zickert**, *Gluing equations for PGL(n,ℂ)-representations of
  3-manifolds*, and SnapPy's `ptolemy` module, which drives Magma/Sage Gröbner engines.

Sharpest: Garoufalidis–Goerner–Zickert state outright that Gröbner computations are
*"usually impractical for the gluing equations even when the cusp equations are
added"* — which is **precisely the n=4 ceiling gate G7 measures**. G7 rediscovers a
documented fact; it does not establish one. Corrected in the probe docstring.

What is actually new is only local infrastructure: this material is now in-corpus,
gated, witnessed, calibrated against the two shape fields already proved in Lean, and
carrying the negative control. Worth having; **not** a mathematical contribution.

**2026-08-10 (later) — ceiling raised: PARI polmod arithmetic. The bottleneck MOVED.**

Acting on the previous entry's own recommendation ("replace sympy `cancel`/`rem` with
real number-field arithmetic"). Gate G8 rewritten, new gate G9.

**The change.** `verify_pari` multiplies PARI **polmods** — reduced mod `P` at every
step — instead of building a rational function and cancelling at the end. Same
mathematics, no approximation, but no intermediate ever exceeds `deg(P)`. sympy's
`cancel`/`together` let the numerator swell to enormous degree before any reduction
happens; that swell *was* the ceiling.

| manifold | tets | deg | sympy | PARI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7₅ | 7 | 8 | 24.4s | 1.6s |
| 7₆ | 8 | 9 | 14.9s | 1.4s |
| 8₂ | 6 | 8 | **98.4s** | 0.6s (verify 0.00s) |
| 10₁₀₀ | 14 | 14 | **never finished** | **7.2s** (verify 0.12s) |

Whole probe: 48s → **18s**, while going from 19 manifolds to **27** and adding the two
that previously could not be done at all. Nothing is excluded for runtime any more.

**Reach now: 23 hyperbolic manifolds all EXACT, 4 torus knots correctly rejected**, up
to **14 tetrahedra** and shape field **degree 14**.

**⚠ The bottleneck moved, and that is the real result.** Verification is now
effectively free (0.00s for most of the set, 0.12s at degree 14). Essentially all
remaining time is **identification** — `algdep`/LLL — whose precision requirement grows
with degree × height. So the honest statement is no longer "degree is the bottleneck via
expression swell"; it is:

> exact verification is free; finding the field is the whole cost.

**Where it still stops, stated as a limit of this identifier and not of the manifolds.**
11a1 (17 tets), 11a2 (18), 12a1 (20): searched degree 2..40 at 150/600 digits (67s each)
and again degree 2..60 at 800/2500 digits without converging in budget. That is **not**
a claim that these shape fields are large — only that this identifier did not find them.
Raising this further means using certified LLL in `snap`/Sage (`find_field`), which is
the tool the literature already points at; it is not a matter of more precision here.

**A trap I nearly shipped.** After the rewrite, G8 reported 10₁₀₀ and t12000 as "NOT
IDENTIFIED" — not because identification failed, but because the probe's `MAXDEG = 12`
was **below their degrees (14 and 13)**. A search bound silently masquerading as a
negative result. Raised to 20, with precision scaled to match, and the reason is now a
comment at the constant. G1's calibration is what makes raising it safe: the identifier
returns the *smallest* degree passing the gates, so 4₁ stays degree 2 and 5₂ degree 3.

**New gate G9 — the two exact verifiers must agree.** `verify_pari` (polmod) and
`gluing_residuals` (sympy over ℚ[x]/(P)) are independent implementations of the same
check. A fast path that silently returned "exact" for everything would sail through G8
unnoticed; G9 is what rules that out. Both agree on all 7 manifolds small enough to run
both. **Do not delete the slow verifier** — it is the only check on the fast one.

### Mass/binding — YES, already correct, and honestly so

`computations/binding_link_volumes.py` handles the degenerate case explicitly:
`"2H": {"link": "Hopf (L2a1)", "vol": mpf("0"), "hyperbolic": False}` — the deuteron's
binding link is the **Hopf link, which is Seifert fibred**, exactly the non-hyperbolic
case. The zero volume triggers a documented substitution to `Vol(𝓑₃) = 8G`
(`v_eff = v if v > 0.01 else VOL_B3`), printed as `→ 8G (Hopf=0)` so it is visible in
output, and the docstring records the whole thing as an **honest negative**: the
endomorphism matches the deuteron at +0.2% *via that substitution* but gives −60% for
A ≥ 3, so the A-dependence cannot live in a single binding-link volume.

⚠ One fragility worth a later fix, not a bug today: the substitution tests
`v > 0.01` — the **volume** — when the semantic flag `"hyperbolic": False` is right there
in the same dict. It is safe in practice because Cao–Meyerhoff put the minimum cusped
orientable hyperbolic volume at 2.0298832…, so no genuinely hyperbolic link can fall
under 0.01. But it is testing the wrong thing, and would silently mis-fire if the
threshold or the registry ever changed. Prefer `if not rec["hyperbolic"]`.

### Lean geometry — NO, it was too strong. Fixed in `QOU/Knot/FlatTetrahedra.lean`.

`IdealTetrahedron` carries `im_pos : 0 < shape.im` as a **structure field**, so the whole
gluing layer built on it excluded flat shapes by construction. Concrete cost: **Route 75's
own G5 finding was not statable in Lean.** New module, sorry-free, standard axioms:

- `BlochWigner_lob_ofReal (x : ℝ) : BlochWigner_lob ↑x = 0` — **no hypotheses.**
  Generalises the corpus's `BlochWigner_lob_real_eq_zero`, which assumed `0 < x < 1` and
  therefore could not see the trefoil's shapes `−1` and `2` at all. Includes the two
  junk-value cases (`x = 0`, `x = 1`), where Lean's `1/0 = 0` still lands on `arg = 0`.
- `Shape` — `IdealTetrahedron` minus `im_pos`: any `z ∈ ℂ ∖ {0,1}`. `param_ne_zero` and
  `param_prod` (`z z′ z″ = −1`) now proved from `z ≠ 0, 1` **alone**. Note
  `IdealTriangulation.param_ne_zero` proves the same from `param_im_pos` — positivity was
  never what made the parameters units.
- `ShapedTriangulation.SatisfiesEdgeEquations` — the edge equations over possibly-flat
  shapes. `IdealTetrahedron.toShape` keeps every existing result available.
- Trefoil worked: `trefoil_edge_zero` (exponents `[1,2,2, 2,1,2]`), `trefoil_edge_one`
  (`z₀·z₁′ = (−1)(−1) = 1`), `trefoil_flat`, `trefoil_volume_zero`, and
  `edge_equations_do_not_detect_hyperbolicity` stating all three together.

**The split is the mathematical content, not packaging.** The **edge** equations descend
to flat shapes; the **angle** equations do not (they need `angle_sum`, hence `im_pos`).
That is precisely why positivity is the hyperbolicity detector and the equations are not.

**Follow-on:** fold `Shape` back into `QOU/MathlibExt/IdealTriangulation.lean` so there is
