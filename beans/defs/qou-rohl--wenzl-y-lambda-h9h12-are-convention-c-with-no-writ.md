## What was found

Surfaced while extending the `qou-eea0` harness resolver (PR #4928). Both
witnesses are convention C by the structural test — nonzero `y_λ` on a
partition with three or more rows — and **neither has a producer anywhere in
the live tree**. Not recorded under `scriptFile`, `script` or `producer`; no
slug match; nothing in `computations/`, `tools/` or `scripts/` writes them.

They are not dead data. Six live scripts *read* them:

| consumer | witness |
|---|---|
| `probes/frobenius_gram_delta_lambda_probe.py` | h9 (T, ³He), h12 (⁴He) |
| `habiro/habiro_h9_spectral_decomposition.py` | h9 |
| `habiro/habiro_h9_correction_series.py` | h9 |
| `nucleus_real_sdp.py` | h12 |
| `deltalambda/he4_binding_k_fingerprint_stage1_probe.py` | h12 |
| `deltalambda/he4_binding_k_averaged_interface_probe.py` | h12 |
| `deltalambda/deltalambda_extensivity_gap_synthesis_probe.py` | h9, h12 |

## Why it matters more than an orphan count suggests

This is the propagation hazard `qou-eea0` named, in its worst form. A
convention-C witness that *can* be regenerated is a scheduling problem. One
that **cannot** is a permanent upstream: every consumer above is a
convention-D script reading convention-C weights, and its output looks clean
because nothing in the witness says which convention produced it. That is
exactly how the co-Casimir producer came to print `live channels = 11 of 11`
at `n = 6`, where 7 must be dead.

The nuclear layer is the whole consumer set here — tritium and ³He at H₉,
⁴He at H₁₂ — so the affected quantities are the binding energies, not a
side probe.

## Options, none yet chosen

1. **Find the retired writer in history.** `git log --diff-filter=D` over
   `computations/**` for a script whose name matches; if it was deleted
   rather than deprecated, it can be recovered and re-run under D.
2. **Re-derive from a live producer.** `y_λ` at H₉/H₁₂ is computable from
   `schur_principal_mpmath.y_lambda_at_q0_mpmath(n, q)`; the question is
   whether the Wenzl-bridge normalisation these witnesses carry is
   reproducible or is itself the retired step.
3. **Retire the witnesses and repoint the six consumers** at a live source.
   Largest change, and the only one that removes the class rather than
   refreshing an instance.

## Close condition

Either the two witnesses are regenerated by a live producer under convention
D, or all six consumers are repointed and the witnesses retired with a note
saying which convention they recorded. Refreshing them by hand is not a
close — nothing hand-edited (`qou-eea0` hard constraint).

## CLOSED (PR #4928)

Resolved by option 2 of the three recorded above — re-derived from a live
producer — rather than by retiring the witnesses or repointing the six
consumers.

`computations/regenerate_y_lambda_orphans_conventionD.py` recomputes every
`y_λ` from the convention-D closed form and writes it back into each file's own
schema. Both witnesses now carry a `scriptFile` for the first time, so they are
no longer producerless, and both test clean: 0 fingerprints, 0 structural hits.

| | |
|---|---|
| `wenzl-y-lambda-h9` | 30 weights, n=9 |
| `wenzl-y-lambda-h12` | 77 weights, n=12 |

The six consumers are untouched — `nucleus_real_sdp.py`,
`probes/frobenius_gram_delta_lambda_probe.py`, the two `habiro/habiro_h9_*`,
and the two `deltalambda/he4_binding_k_*` probes all read the same field names
as before. That was the point of rewriting in place rather than emitting a
canonical shape: migrating the convention and breaking every reader in one
commit would have been the worse trade.

Verified before writing: `Σ_λ dim_λ · y_λ = 1` on the rewritten values for
every complete table, and every `ℓ(λ) ≥ 3` weight exactly zero.
