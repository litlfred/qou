One sees at once that, if $f \in {\mathbb A}_{q}^{\Lambda}$, its asymptotic expansion, written $J(f)$, belongs to the space of $q$-Gevrey series of level $\nu$, whence the following linear map: $$\label{not68} J: \quad {\mathbb A}_{q}^{\Lambda} \rightarrow {{\mathbf C}[[z]]}_{q;1/\nu}, \quad f \mapsto J(f).$$ (For the notation ${{{\mathbf C}[[z]]}_{q;s}}$, see paragraph [\[subsection:qGevreylevels\]](#subsection:qGevreylevels){reference-type="ref" reference="subsection:qGevreylevels"} of the general notations, in the introduction.) In section [\[section:BorelRitt\]](#section:BorelRitt){reference-type="ref" reference="section:BorelRitt"}, we shall see that this map is onto, providing a \"meromorphic $q$-Gevrey version\" of classical Borel-Ritt theorem.
