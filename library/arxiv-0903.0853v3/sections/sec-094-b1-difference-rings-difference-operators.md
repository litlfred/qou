APPENDIX A. CLASSIFICATION OF ISOGRADED FILTERED DIFFERENCE MODULES
More precisely, we shall assume the following situation. Let C be a commu-
tative ring (we may think of the the ﬁeld C of complex numbers, or else some
arbitrary ﬁeld of “constants”). Assume that K is a commutative C-algebra
and σ a C-algebra automorphism, that is, a C-linear ring automorphism. We
shall always assume that the automorphism σ is of inﬁnite order, i.e. the iter-
ates σk, k ∈Z, are all diﬀerent. (In the case of q-diﬀerences, this means that
q is not a root of unity.) Then the ring of constants:
Kσ := {x ∈K | σx = x}
is actually a sub C-algebra of K. The ring of σ-diﬀerence operators is deﬁned
as the ¨Ore-Laurent ring:
DK,σ := K < T, T −1 >
with T an invertible indeterminate, subject to the twisted commutation rela-
tions:
∀k ∈Z , ∀λ ∈K , T k.λ = σk(λ)T k.

The ring DK,σ is actually is a C-algebra with center Kσ. As a consequence,
the category ModDK,σ of left DK,σ-modules is a C-linear abelian category.
Mapping the element P akT k ∈DK,σ to the C-linear endomorphism
P akσk of K, one deﬁnes a morphism of C-algebras from DK,σ to LC(K).
The image of this morphism is the sub-C-algebra K < σ, σ−1 > generated by
K (that is, the operators of left multiplication by elements of K), σ and σ−1.
Beware however that neither K < σ, σ−1 > nor DK,σ is a K-algebra, since K
is not central in either case.
Since we have assumed that σ is of inﬁnite order, one deduces from Artin-
Dedekind’s lemma on independence of characters that, when K is a ﬁeld (more
generally, when it is an integral ring), the morphism is injective, so that we
can identify:
DK,σ = K < σ, σ−1 > .
For this reason, we shall rather sometimes write K < σ, σ−1 > for DK,σ when
no confusion thus arises, even if K is not an integral ring.
