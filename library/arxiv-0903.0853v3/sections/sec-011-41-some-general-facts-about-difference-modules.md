[]{#subsection:generalitiesdiffmods label="subsection:generalitiesdiffmods"}

Here, we also refer to the classical litterature about difference fields, like [@Cohn] and [@Franke] (see also [@Ore]). A detailed proof of many elementary algebraic facts can be found in [@LDVS]. We shall also use specific results proved in appendix [\[chapter:ISOGRAD\]](#chapter:ISOGRAD){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:ISOGRAD"}.\
We call *difference field*[^1] a pair $(K,\sigma)$, where $K$ is a (commutative) field and $\sigma$ a field automorphism of $K$. We write indifferently $\sigma(x)$ or $\sigma x$ the action of $\sigma$ on $x \in K$. One can then form the Ore ring of difference operators: $$\label{not9}
{\mathcal{D}}_{K,\sigma} := K \langle T,T^{-1} \rangle$$ characterized by the twisted commutation relation: $$\forall k \in {\mathbf Z}~,~ x \in K ~,~ T^{k} x = \sigma^{k}(x) T^{k}.$$ We shall rather write somewhat improperly ${\mathcal{D}}_{K,\sigma} := K \langle \sigma,\sigma^{-1} \rangle$ and, for short, ${\mathcal{D}}:= {\mathcal{D}}_{K,\sigma}$ in this section. The center of ${\mathcal{D}}$ is the "field of constants": $$\label{not10}
K^{\sigma} := \{x \in K ~ | ~\sigma(x) = x\}.$$ The ring ${\mathcal{D}}$ is left euclidean and any ideal is generated by a unique entire unitary polynomial $P = \sigma^{n} + a_{1} \sigma^{n-1} + \cdots + a_{n}$.\
Any (left) ${\mathcal{D}}$-module $M \in {Mod_{{\mathcal{D}}}}$ []{#not11 label="not11"} can be seen as a $K$-vector space $E$ and the left multiplication $x \mapsto \sigma.x$ as a *semi-linear* (or $\sigma$-linear) automorphism $\Phi: E \rightarrow E$, which means that $\Phi(\lambda x) = \sigma(\lambda) \Phi(x)$; and any pair $(E,\Phi)$ []{#not12 label="not12"} of a $K$-vector space $E$ and a semi-linear automorphism $\Phi$ of $E$ defines a ${\mathcal{D}}$-module; we just write $M = (E,\Phi)$. Morphisms from $(E,\Phi)$ to $(E',\Phi')$ in ${Mod_{{\mathcal{D}}}}$ are linear maps $u \in \mathcal{L}_{K}(E,E')$ such that $\Phi' \circ u = u \circ \Phi$.\
The ${\mathcal{D}}$-module $M$ has finite length if, and only if, $E$ is a finite dimensional $K$-vector space. A finite length ${\mathcal{D}}$-module is called a *difference module* over $(K,\sigma)$, or over $K$ for short. The full subcategory of ${Mod_{{\mathcal{D}}}}$ whose objects are of difference modules is written $DiffMod(K,\sigma)$. []{#not13 label="not13"} The categories ${Mod_{{\mathcal{D}}}}$ and $DiffMod(K,\sigma)$ are abelian and $K^{\sigma}$-linear.\
By choosing a basis of $E$, we can identify any difference module []{#not14 label="not14"} with some $(K^{n},\Phi_{A})$, where $A \in {\text{GL}}_{n}(K)$ and $\Phi_{A}(X) := A^{-1} \sigma X$ (with the natural operation of $\sigma$ on $K^{n})$; the reason for using $A^{-1}$ will become clear soon. If $B \in {\text{GL}}_{p}(K)$, then morphisms from $(K^{n},\Phi_{A})$ to $(K^{p},\Phi_{B})$ can be identified with matrices $F \in {\text{Mat}}_{p,n}(K)$ such that $(\sigma F) A = B F$ (and composition amounts to the product of matrices).

[^1]: Much of what follows will be extended to the case of difference *rings* in appendix [\[chapter:ISOGRAD\]](#chapter:ISOGRAD){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:ISOGRAD"}, where the basic linear constructions will be described in great detail; the reader is encouraged to refer to the appendix only when necessary.
