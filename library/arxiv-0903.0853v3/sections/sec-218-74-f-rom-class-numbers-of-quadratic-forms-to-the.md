[]{#section:QuadForms label="section:QuadForms"}

This topic is related to a paper of Mordell [@Mordell] and to recent work [@CZMORD] by the third author. We follow their notations, except for the use of the letter $q$, and also for the dependency on the modular parameter $\omega$, which we do not always make explicit.\
We shall have here use for the classical theta functions, defined for $x \in {\mathbf C}$ and $\Im(\omega) > 0$: []{#not111 label="not111"} $$\begin{aligned}
\theta_{0,1}(x) = \theta_{0,1}(x,\omega) & :=
\sum_{n \in {\mathbf Z}} (-1)^{n} e^{{\text{i}}\pi (n^{2} \omega + 2 n x)}, \\
\theta_{1,1}(x) = \theta_{1,1}(x,\omega) & :=
\dfrac{1}{{\text{i}}} \sum_{m~odd} (-1)^{(m-1)/2} e^{{\text{i}}\pi (m^{2} \omega/4 + m x)}, \\
& =
e^{{\text{i}}\pi (\omega/4+x-1/2)} \sum_{n \in {\mathbf Z}} (-1)^{n} e^{{\text{i}}\pi (n(n+1) \omega + 2 n x)}.
\end{aligned}$$ We shall set $q := e^{- 2 {\text{i}}\pi \omega}$ (so that indeed $\left |q \right |> 1$) and $z := e^{2 {\text{i}}\pi x}$. The above theta functions are related to ${\theta_{q}}$ through the formulas: $$\begin{aligned}
\theta_{0,1}(x,\omega) & = {\theta_{q}}(- \sqrt{q} z), \\
\theta_{1,1}(x,\omega) & = \dfrac{\sqrt{z}}{{\text{i}}q^{1/8}} {\theta_{q}}(-z).
\end{aligned}$$ (Thus, the latter is multivalued as a function of $z$.)
