This will be our only example with more than two slopes. Consider the square $\hat Y := \text{\tencyr\cyracc Ch}^{2}$ of the Tshakaloff series: $$\hat Y(z) = \left(\sum_{n\ge 0}q^{n(n-1)/2} z^n\right)^2.$$ As we shall see in section [\[section:qEulersquareanalytic\]](#section:qEulersquareanalytic){reference-type="ref" reference="section:qEulersquareanalytic"}, the series $\hat Y = \text{\tencyr\cyracc Ch}^{2}$ does not support the same process of analytic summation as $\text{\tencyr\cyracc Ch}$ itself. This comes from the fact that the Newton polygon of $\hat Y$ has three slopes, as we shall see, while that of $\text{\tencyr\cyracc Ch}$ has two slopes. First, however, we want to give some recipes to tackle such examples.
