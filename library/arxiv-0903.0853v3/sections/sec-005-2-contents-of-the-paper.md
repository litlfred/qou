[]{#section:contents label="section:contents"}

We shall classify analytically isoformal $q$-difference equations. That is, we consider analytic $q$-difference modules together with an isomorphism of their formalisation with a given formal $q$-difference module, and we consider equivalences preserving this additional structure. This is in adequation to the corresponding problem for differential equations, see for instance [@BV].\
The isoformal analytic classes form an affine space, we shall give *three descriptions* of this space respectively in chapters [\[chapter:isoformalclasses\]](#chapter:isoformalclasses){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:isoformalclasses"}, [\[chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya\]](#chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya"} and [\[chapter:geometryofF(M)\]](#chapter:geometryofF(M)){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:geometryofF(M)"} (this third case is a particular case of the second and is based upon chapter [\[chapter:asymptotictheory\]](#chapter:asymptotictheory){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:asymptotictheory"}) using different constructions of the analytic invariants. A *direct and explicit* comparison between the second and the third description is straightforward but such a comparison between the first and the second or the third construction is quite subtle; in the present paper it will be clear for the "abelian case\", of two integral slopes; for the general case we refer the reader to [@RS1; @RS2] and also to work in progress [@JSmoduli].\
Chapter [\[chapter:generalnonsense\]](#chapter:generalnonsense){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:generalnonsense"} deals with the general setting of the problem (section [\[section:catqdiffmods\]](#section:catqdiffmods){reference-type="ref" reference="section:catqdiffmods"}) and introduces two fundamental tools: the Newton polygon and the slope filtration (section [\[section:Newtonetfiltration\]](#section:Newtonetfiltration){reference-type="ref" reference="section:Newtonetfiltration"}). From this, the problem of analytic isoformal classification, that we state in section [\[section:analyticisoformalclassification\]](#section:analyticisoformalclassification){reference-type="ref" reference="section:analyticisoformalclassification"}, admits a purely algebraic formulation: the isograded classification of filtered difference modules. Simple examples are tackled in the same section to give an idea of the landscape. This is also the occasion to introduce the $q$-Borel transformation. The corresponding algebraic theory is developped in a more general setting in the appendix [\[chapter:ISOGRAD\]](#chapter:ISOGRAD){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:ISOGRAD"}.\
The first attack at the classification problem for $q$-difference equations comes in chapter [\[chapter:isoformalclasses\]](#chapter:isoformalclasses){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:isoformalclasses"}. Section [\[section:isoformalclasses\]](#section:isoformalclasses){reference-type="ref" reference="section:isoformalclasses"} specializes the results of appendix [\[chapter:ISOGRAD\]](#chapter:ISOGRAD){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:ISOGRAD"} to $q$-differences, and section [\[section:indextheorems\]](#section:indextheorems){reference-type="ref" reference="section:indextheorems"} provides the proof of some related index computations. One finds that the space of classes is an affine scheme (theorem [\[theo:schemadesmodules\]](#theo:schemadesmodules){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:schemadesmodules"}) and computes its dimension. This is a rather abstract result. In order to provide explicit descriptions (normal forms, coordinates, invariants \...) from section [\[section:explicitdescription\]](#section:explicitdescription){reference-type="ref" reference="section:explicitdescription"} up to the end of the paper, we assume that *the slopes of the Newton polygon are integers*. This allows for the more precise theorem [\[theo:kintegralslopesspace\]](#theo:kintegralslopesspace){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:kintegralslopesspace"} and the existence of Birkhoff-Guenther normal forms originating in [@Birkhoff3]. This also makes easier the explicit computations of the following chapters. Then some precisions about $q$-Gevrey classification are given in section [\[section:qGevreyinterpolation\]](#section:qGevreyinterpolation){reference-type="ref" reference="section:qGevreyinterpolation"}.\
Analytic isoformal classification by normal forms is a special feature of the $q$-difference case, such a thing does not exist for differential equations. To tackle this case Birkhoff introduced functional $1$-cochains using Poincaré asymptotics [@Birkhoff0; @Birkhoff1]. Later, in the seventies, Malgrange interpreted Birkhoff cochains using sheaves on a circle $S^1$ (the real blow up of the origin of the complex plane). Here, we modify these constructions in order to deal with the $q$-difference case, introducing a new asymptotic theory and replacing the circle $S^1$ by the elliptic curve ${\mathbf{E}_{q}}= {\mathbf C}^{*}/q^{{\mathbf Z}}$.\
In this spirit, chapter [\[chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya\]](#chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya"} tackles the extension to $q$-difference equations of the so called Birkhoff-Malgrange-Sibuya theorems. In section [\[section:asymptotics\]](#section:asymptotics){reference-type="ref" reference="section:asymptotics"} is outlined an asymptotic theory adapted to $q$-difference equations but weaker than that of section [\[section:asymptoticsreldivisor\]](#section:asymptoticsreldivisor){reference-type="ref" reference="section:asymptoticsreldivisor"} of the next chapter: the difference is the same as between classical Gevrey versus Poincaré asymptotics. The counterpart of the Poincaré version of Borel-Ritt is theorem [\[theo:BorelRittfaible\]](#theo:BorelRittfaible){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:BorelRittfaible"}; also, comparison with Whitney conditions is described in lemma [\[prop:asymptotiqueetWhitney\]](#prop:asymptotiqueetWhitney){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:asymptotiqueetWhitney"}. Indeed, the geometric methods of section [\[section:fundamentalisomorphism\]](#section:fundamentalisomorphism){reference-type="ref" reference="section:fundamentalisomorphism"} rest on the integrability theorem of Newlander-Niremberg. They allow the proof of the first main theorem, the $q$-analogue of the abstract Birkhoff-Malgrange-Sibuya theorem (theorem [\[theo:firstqMalgrangeSibuya\]](#theo:firstqMalgrangeSibuya){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:firstqMalgrangeSibuya"}). Then, in section [\[section:theisoformalclassificationJP\]](#section:theisoformalclassificationJP){reference-type="ref" reference="section:theisoformalclassificationJP"}, it is applied to the classification problem for $q$-difference equations and one obtains the $q$-analogue of the concrete Birkhoff-Malgrange-Sibuya theorem (theorem [\[theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya\]](#theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya"}): there is a natural bijection from the space ${\mathcal{F}}(M_{0})$ of analytic isoformal classes in the formal class of $M_{0}$ with the first cohomology set $H^{1}({\mathbf{E}_{q}},\Lambda_{I}(M_{0}))$ of the "Stokes sheaf". The latter is the sheaf of automorphisms of $M_{0}$ infinitely tangent to identity, a sheaf of unipotent groups over the elliptic curve ${\mathbf{E}_{q}}= {\mathbf C}^{*}/q^{{\mathbf Z}}$. The proof of theorem [\[theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya\]](#theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya"} appeals to the fundamental theorem of existence of asymptotic solutions, previously proved in section [\[section:existasymptsols\]](#section:existasymptsols){reference-type="ref" reference="section:existasymptsols"}.\
Chapter [\[chapter:asymptotictheory\]](#chapter:asymptotictheory){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:asymptotictheory"} aims at developping a summation process for $q$-Gevrey divergent series. After some preparatory material in section [\[section:preparatorymaterial\]](#section:preparatorymaterial){reference-type="ref" reference="section:preparatorymaterial"}, an asymptotic theory "with estimates\" well suited for $q$-difference equations is expounded in section [\[section:asymptoticsreldivisor\]](#section:asymptoticsreldivisor){reference-type="ref" reference="section:asymptoticsreldivisor"}. Here, the sectors of the classical theory are replaced by preimages in ${\mathbf C}^{*}$ of the Zariski open sets of the elliptic curve ${\mathbf C}^{*}/q^{{\mathbf Z}}$, that is, complements of finite unions of discrete $q$-spirals in ${\mathbf C}^{*}$; and the growth conditions at the boundary of the sectors are replaced by polarity conditions along the discrete spirals. The $q$-Gevrey analogue of the classical theorems are stated and proved in sections [\[section:BorelRitt\]](#section:BorelRitt){reference-type="ref" reference="section:BorelRitt"} and [\[section:relativedivisorsandmultisummable\]](#section:relativedivisorsandmultisummable){reference-type="ref" reference="section:relativedivisorsandmultisummable"}: the counterpart of the Gevrey version of Borel-Ritt is theorem [\[theo:BorelRitt\]](#theo:BorelRitt){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:BorelRitt"} and multisummability conditions appear in theorems [\[theo:mniveaux\]](#theo:mniveaux){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:mniveaux"} and [\[theo:masymptotique\]](#theo:masymptotique){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:masymptotique"}. The theory is then applied to $q$-difference equations and to their classification in section [\[section:analyticclassificationCZ\]](#section:analyticclassificationCZ){reference-type="ref" reference="section:analyticclassificationCZ"}, where is proved the summability of solutions (theorem [\[theo:sommabilite\]](#theo:sommabilite){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:sommabilite"}), the existence of asymptotic solutions coming as a consequence (theorem [\[theo:solutionsasymptotiques\]](#theo:solutionsasymptotiques){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:solutionsasymptotiques"}) and the description of Stokes phenomenon as an application (theorem [\[theo:Stokes\]](#theo:Stokes){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:Stokes"}).\
Chapter [\[chapter:geometryofF(M)\]](#chapter:geometryofF(M)){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:geometryofF(M)"} deals with some complementary information on the geometry of the space ${\mathcal{F}}(M_{0})$ of analytic isoformal classes, through its identification with the cohomology set $H^{1}({\mathbf{E}_{q}},\Lambda_{I}(M_{0}))$ obtained in chapter [\[chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya\]](#chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya"}. Theorem [\[theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya\]](#theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:secondqMalgrangeSibuya"} of chapter [\[chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya\]](#chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:qMalgrangeSibuya"} implicitly attaches cocycles to analytic isoformal classes and theorem [\[theo:Stokes\]](#theo:Stokes){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:Stokes"} of chapter [\[chapter:asymptotictheory\]](#chapter:asymptotictheory){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:asymptotictheory"} shows how to obtain them by a summation process. In section [\[section:privilegedcocycles\]](#section:privilegedcocycles){reference-type="ref" reference="section:privilegedcocycles"}, we give yet another construction of "privileged cocycles" (from [@JSStokes]) and study their properties. In section [\[section:devissageqGevrey\]](#section:devissageqGevrey){reference-type="ref" reference="section:devissageqGevrey"}, we show how the devissage of the sheaf $\Lambda_{I}(M_{0})$ by holomorphic vector bundles over ${\mathbf{E}_{q}}$ allows to identify $H^{1}({\mathbf{E}_{q}},\Lambda_{I}(M_{0}))$ with an affine space, and relate it to the corresponding result of theorem [\[theo:kintegralslopesspace\]](#theo:kintegralslopesspace){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:kintegralslopesspace"}. In section [\[section:vectorbundles\]](#section:vectorbundles){reference-type="ref" reference="section:vectorbundles"}, we recall how holomorphic vector bundles over ${\mathbf{E}_{q}}$ appear naturally in the theory of $q$-difference equations and we apply them to an interpretation of the formula for the dimension of ${\mathcal{F}}(M_{0})$.\
Chapter [\[chapter:examplesStokes\]](#chapter:examplesStokes){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:examplesStokes"} provides some elementary examples motivated by their relation to $q$-special functions, either linked to modular functions or to confluent basic hypergeometric series.\
The appendix is devoted to the study of the following purely algebraic problem: to classify (in a given abelian category) all finitely filtered objects with fixed associated graded object, up to equivalence compatible with the graduation. Since the space under study looks like some generalized extension module, it relies on homological algebra and index computations. The problem is solved here under restrictive assumptions, but which are sufficient to apply them to our specific situation. Note that, to ease the reading of chapter [\[chapter:generalnonsense\]](#chapter:generalnonsense){reference-type="ref" reference="chapter:generalnonsense"}, there are some redundancies, so that the reader does not have to swallow the whole appendix before reading that chapter !
