A hyperbolic ideal tetrahedron T is the convex hull in H3 of four distinct points on ∂H3. The four points on ∂H3 are called ideal vertices of T, and are not part of the tetrahedron. The tetrahedron T is called degenerate if it lies in a single plane, and non-degenerate otherwise. Recall that an isometry of H3 is completely determined by its action on three points on ∂H3.
