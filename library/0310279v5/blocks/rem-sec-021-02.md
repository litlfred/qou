By our basic construction, the only way that CA will fail to have clasp arcs is if X′ −, the pushoff of X−onto a Seifert surface which was constructed during the proof of Lemma 2.1.1, is geometrically unlinked from X−. The only way that can happen is if X is the unlink. So, if X is not the unlink, then clasp arcs occur. Short clasp arcs arise in the basic construction in situations where we could have arranged for X+ to have the correct linking with X−locally by a braid isotopy.
