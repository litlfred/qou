A division algebra is an algebra over a ﬁeld where division is always possible, with the excep-
tion of division by zero. Nature admits only four normed division algebras over the reals, the real
numbers R, the complex numbers C, the quaternions Q and the octonions O. Starting from the real
numbers and generalizing to the complex numbers, one has to give up the ordered property of the
reals. Generalizing in turn to the quaterions one furthermore gives up the commutatativity of the
reals and complex numbers. The quaternions are spanned by 1,I,J,K with 1 being the identity and

Every straight line in the Fano plane of the octonions (together with the identity) generates
a copy of the quaternions, for example e4,e1,e6. The circle e1,e3,e5 also gives a copy of the
quaternions, making for a total of seven copies of the quaternions in the octonions.
It was very recently (2015) shown by Furey that one can represent much of SM physics in
terms of the NDAs acting on themselves [ 19]. Although we will not go into the details here, the
main results are that the complex quaternions, C⊗ H admit all the representations of the Lorentz
group that one ﬁnds in the SM including the scalar, vector, spinor, and ﬁeld strength tensor repre-
sentations, whereas the complex octonionsC⊗O provide a representations of the unbroken internal
symmetry SU(3)×U(1) of a single generation of SM fermions. That is, within the Dixon algebra,
deﬁned to be the product R⊗ C⊗ H⊗ O, one recovers both the spacetime and internal symmetries
of SM matter.
