[]{#6.1 label="6.1"}

Here we shall discuss the predictions for vector particle decays. As was already mentioned in Section [\[2\]](#2){reference-type="ref" reference="2"}, in the ENJL model we are considering, all the couplings can be written in terms of three parameters, namely $g_A$, $M_Q$ and $\Lambda_\chi$. The coupling $g_A$ was defined in Eq. ([\[ga\]](#ga){reference-type="ref" reference="ga"}) and in this model takes the following value [@bbr]

$$\label{gad}
g_A\,=\,1\,-\,\frac{\displaystyle f_\pi^2}{\displaystyle f_V^2 \, M_V^2}\, .$$

We shall choose for $g_A$, $M_Q$ and $\Lambda_\chi$ the values that are obtained from fitting the ${\cal O} (p^2)$ and ${\cal O} (p^4)$ couplings of the chiral Lagrangian [@bbr], *i.e.*

$$\label{param} \matrix{
g_A \,=\,0.65\,, \hspace*{0.5cm} x\equiv \frac{\displaystyle M_Q^2}
{\displaystyle\Lambda_\chi^2} \,=\,0.06 \hspace*{0.5cm}
{\rm and} \hspace*{0.5cm} M_Q\,=\,260 \,\,{\rm MeV} \,.}$$

These values correspond to $f_V \,=\, 0.17$, $g_V \,=\, 0.083$ and $f_A \,= \,0.080$.

For the vector mixing $\omega - \phi$ we use the ideal angle, *i.e.* $\tan \varphi_V \,=\, 1/\sqrt 2$ and for the pseudoscalar mixing we use $\tan \varphi_P \,=\, -\,
1/2 \sqrt 2$. In both cases we assume nonet symmetry. The diagonalized $\omega$, $\phi$, $\eta$ and $\eta'$ states in terms of the SU(3) octet and singlet states in Eqs. ([\[eta8\]](#eta8){reference-type="ref" reference="eta8"}), ([\[eta1\]](#eta1){reference-type="ref" reference="eta1"}), ([\[w8\]](#w8){reference-type="ref" reference="w8"}) and ([\[w1\]](#w1){reference-type="ref" reference="w1"}) are

$$\label{diag} \matrix{
\eta' \,=\, \cos \varphi_P \,\, \eta_1 \,+\, \sin \varphi_P
\,\, \eta_8 \,, \hfill \cr
\eta \,=\, -\, \sin \varphi_P \,\, \eta_1 \,+\, \cos \varphi_P
\,\, \eta_8 \,, \hfill \cr\cr
\omega \,=\, \cos \varphi_V \,\, \omega_1 \,+\, \sin \varphi_V
\,\, \omega_8 \,, \hfill \cr
\phi \,=\, -\, \sin \varphi_V \,\, \omega_1 \,+\, \cos \varphi_V
\,\, \omega_8 \,. \hfill \cr\cr }$$

First we shall study the decays $V \to P \gamma$ ($P \to V \gamma$), for which the Feynman diagrams are shown in Figure [\[fig1\]](#fig1){reference-type="ref" reference="fig1"}. The amplitude for the $V \to P \gamma$ decay is given by the expression

$$\label{vpga} \matrix{
A(V \to P (p) \gamma (k)) \,=\,
C_{VP\gamma} \,4 \, |e| \, \sqrt 2 \,
\frac{\displaystyle h_V}{\displaystyle f_\pi} \, \epsilon_{\mu \nu \alpha
\beta} \, \varepsilon^{\mu}_{(\gamma)} \, k^\nu \,
\varepsilon^{*\alpha}_{(V)} \, p^\beta \hfill \cr\cr
\hphantom{A(V \to P (p) \gamma (k)) \,=\,
C_{VP\gamma} }\,\times \, \left[ 1 \,+ \, \sqrt 2
\, \frac{\displaystyle f_V \sigma_V}
{\displaystyle h_V} \, \frac{\displaystyle k^2}{\displaystyle M_{V'}^2 \,-\,
k^2 \,-\, i \, M_{V'} \Gamma_{V'}} \right] \,.
\hfill \cr \cr}$$

Here, $C_{VP\gamma}$ is an SU(3)-light-flavour symmetry factor that relates the different amplitudes. The different values for this factor and the intermediate resonance $V'$ appearing in Eq. ([\[vpga\]](#vpga){reference-type="ref" reference="vpga"}) for each process are given in Table [1](#table1b){reference-type="ref" reference="table1b"}. We denote the polarization pseudovector corresponding to the particle $V$ by $\varepsilon^\mu_
{(V)}$. The decay rate corresponding to the amplitude in Eq. ([\[vpga\]](#vpga){reference-type="ref" reference="vpga"}) is

$$\label{vpgr} \matrix{
\Gamma(V \to P \gamma) \,=\, |C_{VP\gamma}|^2 \,
\frac{\displaystyle 4 \, \alpha \, h_V^2}{\displaystyle 3} \,
\frac{\displaystyle M_V^3}{\displaystyle f_\pi^2} \,
\lambda^{3/2} \left(1, \frac{\displaystyle m_P^2}{\displaystyle
M_V^2}, \frac{\displaystyle k^2}{\displaystyle M_V^2} \right) \hfill \cr\cr
\hphantom{\Gamma(V \to P \gamma) \,=\, |C_{VP\gamma}|^2 \,
\frac{\displaystyle 4 \alpha h_V^2}{\displaystyle 3}} \times\,
 \left| 1 \,+ \, \frac{\displaystyle 2 \, k^2}{\displaystyle M_{V'}^2
\,-\, k^2 \,-\, i \, M_{V'} \Gamma_{V'}} \right|^2 \, ,
\hfill \cr\cr }$$

with $\alpha \,= \, \frac{\displaystyle e^2}{\displaystyle 4 \pi}$ and

$$\lambda(x,y,z) \,=\, x^2 \,+\, y^2 \,+\, z^2 \,-\,2\,
x y \,-\,2\, x z \,-\,2\, y z \,.$$

The decay rates for the $\omega$ and $\rho^0$ vectors can have contributions from a possible $\omega_8-\rho^0$ mixing. In addition, the amplitude for $\rho \to \pi \gamma$ has a sizable contribution from the absortive part of pion and kaon chiral loops (see Figure [\[fig2\]](#fig2){reference-type="ref" reference="fig2"}). (For the other processes this contribution is negligible.) We have taken into account this last contribution which has the following expression

$$\label{abs}
\Gamma(\rho \to \pi \gamma){\displaystyle|}_{\rm Abs}
 \,=\,
\frac{\displaystyle\alpha \,g_V^2\,M_\rho^{11}}{\displaystyle(768 \sqrt 6)^2
\, \pi^6 \, f_\pi^{10}} \, \left(1\,-\, \frac{\displaystyle
m_\pi^2}{\displaystyle M_\rho^2} \right)^3 \, \left(1\,-\, \frac{\displaystyle
4 m_\pi^2}{\displaystyle M_\rho^2}\right)^3 \,.$$

The predictions listed in the column Prediction 1 in Table [2](#table1){reference-type="ref" reference="table1"} correspond to the values of $g_A$, $M_Q$ and $x$ in Eq. ([\[param\]](#param){reference-type="ref" reference="param"}). The worst results in this column, taking into account the experimental errors, are for the decays $\phi \to \eta \gamma$ and $K^{* +} \to K^+ \gamma$ which are between 4 and 6 standard deviations ($\sigma$) from the experimental value. These disintegrations, however, can be explained with the inclusion of explicit chiral symmetry breaking terms proportional to the strange-quark mass as shown in the non-relativistic quark model in Ref. [@chiv]. The predictions for the other processes are less than 2 $\sigma$ from the central experimental values. The processes $\rho^0
\to \pi^0 \gamma$ and $\omega \to \pi^0 \gamma$ are likely to be influenced by a possible $\omega_8-\rho^0$ mixing. All the predictions for the transitions in Table [2](#table1){reference-type="ref" reference="table1"} are made in the chiral limit and depend on two couplings, $h_V$ and $g_V$; in fact the constant $g_V$ only affects to $\rho \to \pi \gamma$ decays and its contribution is not dominant. We refrain from doing a fit of the experimental results since all the amplitudes are related by SU(3)-symmetry factors (up to the small part proportional to $g_V$ in $\rho \to \pi \gamma$ decays) and higher order chiral corrections for these processes are very different for each of them.

Instead, the most reliable prediction is for the $\rho^+ \to \pi^+ \gamma$ decay because is not affected by neutral particle mixings and the explicit chiral symmetry breaking is small. All the other transitions are either affected by mixings or by sizable explicit chiral symmetry breaking effects. The agreement between the result for the $\rho^+ \to \pi^+ \gamma$ decay in the column Prediction 1 and experiment can be improved by using either a larger value for $g_A$ or a smaller value for $f_V$ (*i.e.* a larger value of $x$) or both. Given the poor knowledge of the parameter $g_A$ and the rather good value for $f_V$ we are using ($f_V\,=$ 0.17 compared to the experimental value $f_V {\displaystyle|}_{\rm exp} \simeq 0.20$), we shall fix the value of $f_V$ to $f_V\,=$ 0.17 and fix from the decay $\rho^+ \to \pi^+ \gamma$ the value of $g_A$. We find then $g_A \,=\, 0.76$. So that, this value of $g_A$ leads to $h_V\,=\,0.033$. We shall use this value to see how the predictions in Table [2](#table1){reference-type="ref" reference="table1"} vary with $g_A$. The corresponding results with $g_A\,=\,0.76$ are in the column Prediction 2. We find that, in general, the results are improved, except for the decay $\phi \to \eta \gamma$ which is now 10 $\sigma$ from the central experimental value. However, as was already stated, this decay rate is very sensitive to explicit chiral symmetry breaking. The prediction for the decay $\omega
\to \pi^0 \gamma$ improves but is still 3.5 $\sigma$ from the experimental value which probably means that the $\omega_8-\rho^0$ mixing is likely to be important.

Next, we shall study the predictions for the $V \to \pi \pi \pi$ decays for which the results can be found in Table [3](#table2){reference-type="ref" reference="table2"}. The Feynman diagrams for this process are shown in Figure [\[fig3\]](#fig3){reference-type="ref" reference="fig3"}. The amplitude for $\omega \to \pi^+ \pi^- \pi^0$ has the form,

$$\label{w3p} \matrix{

\frac{\displaystyle p_{ij}^2}
{\displaystyle M_\rho^2 \,-\, p_{ij}^2 \, -\,i \, M_\rho \Gamma_\rho}
\right]
\hfill \cr\cr }$$

with $p_{ij}\,=\,p_i\,+\,p_j$. The amplitudes for $\rho \to \pi \pi \pi$ decays vanish at lowest order. The decay rate for $\omega \to \pi^+ \pi^-
\pi^0$ is

with

{\displaystyle 2}\,. \hfill \cr\cr}$$

For the transitions with two identical particles in the final state there is an additional factor 1/2 in the decay rate in Eq. ([\[vppg\]](#vppg){reference-type="ref" reference="vppg"}). The amplitudes for the $\omega \to \rho^0
\gamma$, $\phi \to \omega \gamma$, $\phi \to
\rho^0 \gamma$ and $\phi \to \omega \pi^0$ decays are zero at lowest order.

Up to now we have considered the $\omega-\phi$ mixing angle to be ideal. The $\phi \to \pi^0
\gamma$, $\phi \to \rho^+ \pi^-$, $\phi \to \pi^+ \pi^- \pi^0$ and $\phi \to P^0 P^0 \gamma$ decays are proportional to the deviation of the actual $\omega-\phi$ mixing angle from the ideal one. The effect of a small deviation on the other $\phi$ vector decays studied before is not sizable. We define the deviation of the $\omega - \phi$ mixing angle from the ideal one as the difference

$$\epsilon \equiv \varphi_V \,-\, \varphi \,,$$

where $\varphi$ is the physical $\omega - \phi$ mixing angle and $\varphi_V$ is the ideal mixing angle. The amplitude for $\phi \to \rho^+ \pi^-$ we obtain is

$$\label{firhop} \matrix{
A(\phi \to \rho^+(p_1) \pi^-(p_2)) \,=\, \epsilon \, \,

## The decay $\pi^0 (\eta) \to \gamma \ell^+ \ell^-$ {#6.2}

Here we shall study the process $\pi^0 (\eta) \to \gamma \gamma^*$ (and the related one $e^+ e^- \to \omega, \rho^0 \to \pi^0
\gamma$) in the limit of complete vector dominance, *i.e.* assuming that the ${\cal O} (p^6)$ couplings are saturated by the contribution coming from integrating out the spin-1 particles as is the case for the ${\cal O}
(p^4)$ chiral Lagrangian couplings [@egpr]. The amplitudes for these processes acquire a dependence on the invariant mass of the off-shell photon from chiral ${\cal O} (p^6)$ terms. Thus, one can define a slope parameter as follows,

$$\label{slope} \matrix{
\rho \equiv \left(
\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle{\rm A} ( P \to \gamma \gamma^*)} \,
\, \frac{\displaystyle{\rm d \hphantom{\, s^*}}}
{\displaystyle{\rm d} \, s^*} \,\,{\rm A}
(P \to \gamma \gamma^*) \right)_{s^*=0}
\hfill \cr\cr }$$

which is independent of the pseudoscalar meson $P$. The slope $\rho$ has been measured in the $\pi^0
\to \gamma e^+ e^-$ transition [@pdg] with the following result

$$\rho \,=\, (1.8 \pm 0.14) \,\, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,.$$

It has been also measured in the $\eta \to \gamma e^+ e^-$ transition [@pdg] and the result is

$$\rho \,=\, (1.4 \pm 0.2) \, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,.$$

The weighted average from both results is

$$\label{sloex}
\rho \,=\, (1.68 \pm 0.2) \, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,.$$

Theoretically, the slope $\rho$ has two kinds of contributions. One from chiral loops (see Figure [\[fig5\]](#fig5){reference-type="ref" reference="fig5"}) and the other from the exchange of vector mesons (see Figure [\[fig6\]](#fig6){reference-type="ref" reference="fig6"}). The result we obtain in the ENJL model is the following

$$\label{slopeth}
\rho \,=\, \frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\Lambda_l^2} \,+\,
\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle M_V^2} \, \left(
1 \,-\, \frac{\displaystyle 2}{\displaystyle 3} \,(1 \,-\, g_A) \right)\,,$$

where $\Lambda_l^2 \,=\, 3.57$ GeV$^2$ is the contribution from the chiral loops which has been estimated in Ref. [@bij]. The dependence in Eq. ([\[slopeth\]](#slopeth){reference-type="ref" reference="slopeth"}) on the coupling $g_A$ comes from the combination of vector couplings $f_V h_V$. If we take for the parameters in Eq. ([\[slopeth\]](#slopeth){reference-type="ref" reference="slopeth"}) the ENJL predictions obtained with the choice in Eq. ([\[param\]](#param){reference-type="ref" reference="param"}), *i.e.* $M_V \,=\, 0.8$ GeV and $g_A \,=\, 0.65$ [@bbr], then we get

$$\rho \,=\, 1.48 \, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,,$$

to be compared with the experimental result in Eq. ([\[sloex\]](#sloex){reference-type="ref" reference="sloex"}). If instead, we take $g_A\,=\, 0.76$, which is the other value we have been using in the previous Section, we find

$$\rho \,=\, 1.59 \, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,.$$

Both predictions are less than 1 $\sigma$ from the experimental result in Eq. ([\[sloex\]](#sloex){reference-type="ref" reference="sloex"}).

Next, we want to study the related process $e^+ e^- \to \omega\,, \rho^0 \to
\pi^0 \gamma$. The function $C_l(\mu,s)$ in Eq. ([\[cross\]](#cross){reference-type="ref" reference="cross"}) comes from chiral loops [@bgp] and has the following expression

If instead, we use the value for $g_A\,=\,0.76$ we find

The last result is better (3 $\sigma$ from the experimental result).

## $K_L \to \pi^0 \gamma^* \gamma^* \to \pi^0
e^+ e^-$

The decay $K_L \to \pi^0 e^+ e^-$ has received a great deal of attention as a possible candidate for observing CP-violation effects (for a review see Ref. [@pr]). The $K_L$ state consists mostly of a CP-odd state $K_2$ with a small mixing of the CP-even state $K_1$,

$$\label{kl} \matrix{
K_L \simeq K_2\,+\, \epsilon\,K_1
\hfill\cr\cr}$$

where $|\epsilon| \simeq 2.26 \times 10^{-3}$ is the standard CP-violation parameter in $K \to \pi \pi$ decays. This decay is interesting because "direct" CP-violation in the $K_2$-decay amplitude and "indirect" ($\epsilon$ effect) CP-violation branching ratios are estimated to be of the same order [@epr3]-[@fr]. (Of the order of $10^{-11}$ [@ddg; @fr].)

In addition to these CP-violating decay modes there is a two-photon exchange contribution to the transition $K_L \to \pi^0 e^+ e^-$ which is CP-conserving. In order to interpret future experimental measurements of this decay it is crucial to elucidate if this decay mode may compete or not with the CP-violating one-photon exchange contributions. The two-photon exchange decay mode was studied in Ref. [@epr3] at ${\cal O} (p^4)$ in the context of chiral perturbation theory and was shown to be suppressed at this order [@epr3; @dono]. At ${\cal O} (p^6)$ there is a vector meson exchange diagram (shown in Figure [\[fig8\]](#fig8){reference-type="ref" reference="fig8"}) for the CP-conserving two-photon exchange contribution which a priori could be potentially large [@seh] and may compete with the one-photon CP-violating decay modes. We can make a prediction for the absortive ${\cal O} (p^6)$ vector meson dominance (VMD) contribution to the process $K_L \to
\pi^0 \gamma^* \gamma^* \to \pi^0 e^+ e^-$ in the model we are considering. To this VMD prediction one must add further direct weak ${\cal O} (p^6)$ contributions, as was pointed out in Ref. [@epr5]. In this Reference, the whole ${\cal O} (p^6)$ contribution to $K_L \to \pi^0 \gamma^*
\gamma^* \to \pi^0 e^+ e^-$ was estimated by using the so-called "weak deformation" model. Disregarding the ${\cal O} (p^4)$ helicity suppressed contributions, these authors find

$$\label{brk} \matrix{
{\rm BR} \, \left(K_2 \to \pi^0 e^+ e^-\right)
{\displaystyle|}_{\rm Abs} \simeq 4.4 \, a_V^2 \, 10^{-12}
\hfill\cr\cr}$$

with

$$\left|a_V \right| \equiv \frac{\displaystyle 512\, \pi^2\, h_V^2\, m_{K^0}^2}
{\displaystyle 9 \, M_V^2}$$

where $h_V$ is the vector coupling introduced in the Lagrangian in Eq. ([\[47\]](#47){reference-type="ref" reference="47"}) and the intermediate resonance $V$ denotes the $\rho^0$ and $\omega$ vector particles.

With our determination of this coupling and using the set of input parameters in Eq. ([\[param\]](#param){reference-type="ref" reference="param"}), we obtain $h_V\,=$ 0.030 which leads to

$$\label{hv1} \matrix{
\left|a_V\right|\,=\, 0.21 \, .\hfill\cr\cr}$$

If we use the higher value for $g_A\,=\,0.76$, which as we have seen is favoured by the overall data on spin-1 particle decays, then we obtain $h_V\,=$ 0.033 which leads to

$$\label{hv2} \matrix{
 \left|a_V\right|\,=\, 0.25 \, .\hfill\cr\cr}$$

These values for $\left|a_V\right|$ are in good agreement with the different phenomenological estimates [@fr; @epr5] made for the branching ratio in Eq. ([\[brk\]](#brk){reference-type="ref" reference="brk"}).

Recently, a measurement of the branching ratio for the CP-conserving decay $K_L \to \pi^0 \gamma \gamma$ has been reported by the NA31 experiment at CERN [@na31]. This measurement gives the following bounds at 90 $\%$ C.L.,

$$\matrix{ - \,0.32 \,<\,a_V\,< 0.19 \, .
\hfill\cr\cr }$$
