Here we shall study the process $\pi^0 (\eta) \to \gamma \gamma^*$ (and the related one $e^+ e^- \to \omega, \rho^0 \to \pi^0
\gamma$) in the limit of complete vector dominance, *i.e.* assuming that the ${\cal O} (p^6)$ couplings are saturated by the contribution coming from integrating out the spin-1 particles as is the case for the ${\cal O}
(p^4)$ chiral Lagrangian couplings [@egpr]. The amplitudes for these processes acquire a dependence on the invariant mass of the off-shell photon from chiral ${\cal O} (p^6)$ terms. Thus, one can define a slope parameter as follows,

$$\label{slope} \matrix{
\rho \equiv \left(
\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle{\rm A} ( P \to \gamma \gamma^*)} \,
\, \frac{\displaystyle{\rm d \hphantom{\, s^*}}}
{\displaystyle{\rm d} \, s^*} \,\,{\rm A}
(P \to \gamma \gamma^*) \right)_{s^*=0}
\hfill \cr\cr }$$

which is independent of the pseudoscalar meson $P$. The slope $\rho$ has been measured in the $\pi^0
\to \gamma e^+ e^-$ transition [@pdg] with the following result

$$\rho \,=\, (1.8 \pm 0.14) \,\, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,.$$

It has been also measured in the $\eta \to \gamma e^+ e^-$ transition [@pdg] and the result is

$$\rho \,=\, (1.4 \pm 0.2) \, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,.$$

The weighted average from both results is

$$\label{sloex}
\rho \,=\, (1.68 \pm 0.2) \, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,.$$

Theoretically, the slope $\rho$ has two kinds of contributions. One from chiral loops (see Figure [\[fig5\]](#fig5){reference-type="ref" reference="fig5"}) and the other from the exchange of vector mesons (see Figure [\[fig6\]](#fig6){reference-type="ref" reference="fig6"}). The result we obtain in the ENJL model is the following

$$\label{slopeth}
\rho \,=\, \frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\Lambda_l^2} \,+\,
\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle M_V^2} \, \left(
1 \,-\, \frac{\displaystyle 2}{\displaystyle 3} \,(1 \,-\, g_A) \right)\,,$$

where $\Lambda_l^2 \,=\, 3.57$ GeV$^2$ is the contribution from the chiral loops which has been estimated in Ref. [@bij]. The dependence in Eq. ([\[slopeth\]](#slopeth){reference-type="ref" reference="slopeth"}) on the coupling $g_A$ comes from the combination of vector couplings $f_V h_V$. If we take for the parameters in Eq. ([\[slopeth\]](#slopeth){reference-type="ref" reference="slopeth"}) the ENJL predictions obtained with the choice in Eq. ([\[param\]](#param){reference-type="ref" reference="param"}), *i.e.* $M_V \,=\, 0.8$ GeV and $g_A \,=\, 0.65$ [@bbr], then we get

$$\rho \,=\, 1.48 \, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,,$$

to be compared with the experimental result in Eq. ([\[sloex\]](#sloex){reference-type="ref" reference="sloex"}). If instead, we take $g_A\,=\, 0.76$, which is the other value we have been using in the previous Section, we find

$$\rho \,=\, 1.59 \, {\rm GeV}^{-2}\,.$$

Both predictions are less than 1 $\sigma$ from the experimental result in Eq. ([\[sloex\]](#sloex){reference-type="ref" reference="sloex"}).

Next, we want to study the related process $e^+ e^- \to \omega\,, \rho^0 \to
\pi^0 \gamma$. The Feynman diagrams for this transition can be obtained from diagrams in Figure [\[fig5\]](#fig5){reference-type="ref" reference="fig5"} and Figure [\[fig6\]](#fig6){reference-type="ref" reference="fig6"} by inserting an $e^+ e^-$ pair in the off-shell photon leg. The cross section for this process is given by

$$\label{cross} \matrix{
\sigma_{e^+e^-\to\omega, \rho^0 \to
\pi^0\gamma} \, (s)\,=\, \frac{\displaystyle\alpha^3}
{\displaystyle 96 \, \pi^2 f_\pi^2} \, \left| \, {\displaystyle\sum_
{V=\omega, \rho^0}} \,\left( C_l(\mu=M_V,s) \,+\,
1 \vphantom{\frac{\displaystyle s}{\displaystyle M_V^2}}
\right. \right. \hfill\cr\cr \left. \left.
\hphantom{\sigma_{e^+e^-\to\omega, \rho^0 \to
\pi^0\gamma} \, (s)\,=\,}\,+\,
\left(1\,-\, \frac{\displaystyle 2}{\displaystyle 3}
\left(1-g_A\right)\right) \,
\frac{\displaystyle s}{\displaystyle M_V^2 \,-\, s \,-\,i\,
\sqrt s \, \Gamma_V} \right) \, \right|^2
\hfill \cr\cr}$$

where we have used the full expression for the vector propagator as suggested in Ref. [@bgp]. (In this Reference this cross section was given in the HGS model [@ban; @fuji].) The function $C_l(\mu,s)$ in Eq. ([\[cross\]](#cross){reference-type="ref" reference="cross"}) comes from chiral loops [@bgp] and has the following expression

$$\label{cl} \matrix{
C_l (\mu,s) \,=\, \frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 48 \pi^2
\, f_\pi^2}\, \left[ s \, \ln \frac{\displaystyle\mu^2}{\displaystyle
m_\pi \, m_K} \, +\, \frac{\displaystyle 5}{\displaystyle 3}\, s \,+\,
2\, F(m_\pi^2,s)\,+\,2\,F(m_K^2,s) \right] \hfill
\cr\cr{\rm with} \hfill \cr\cr
F(m^2,s)\,=\, m^2\, \left(1\,-\,\frac{\displaystyle x}{\displaystyle 4}
\right)\, \left(1\,-\,\frac{\displaystyle 4}{\displaystyle x}\right)^{1/2}
\, \ln \frac{\displaystyle\sqrt x \,+\, \sqrt{x-4}}{
\displaystyle\sqrt x \,-\, \sqrt{x-4}} \,-\, 2\, m^2
\hfill\cr\cr {\rm for} \hspace*{0.2cm}
x\equiv\frac{\displaystyle s}{\displaystyle m^2}\,>\,4 \hspace*{0.5cm}
{\rm and} \hfill\cr\cr
F(m^2,s)\,=\, m^2\, \left(1\,-\,\frac{\displaystyle x}{\displaystyle 4}
\right)\, \left(\frac{\displaystyle 4}{\displaystyle x}\,-\,1\right)^{1/2}
\, \arctan \sqrt{\frac{\displaystyle x}{\displaystyle x\,-\,4}}\,-\, 2\, m^2
\hfill\cr\cr {\rm for} \hspace*{0.2cm} x\,\leq\,4\,.
\hfill \cr\cr}$$

The result we get for this cross section at the $\omega$-mass peak using the set of parameters in Eq. ([\[param\]](#param){reference-type="ref" reference="param"}) is

$$\label{nb1} \sigma ( e^+ e^- \to \omega, \rho \to
\pi^0 \gamma) {\displaystyle|}_{s=M_\omega^2}\,=\,
94 \, {\rm nb} \, ,$$

to be compared with the experimental result [@dol],

$$\sigma ( e^+ e^- \to \omega, \rho \to
\pi^0 \gamma) {\displaystyle|}_{s=M_\omega^2}\,=\,
(152 \pm 13) \, {\rm nb}\,.$$

If instead, we use the value for $g_A\,=\,0.76$ we find

$$\sigma ( e^+ e^- \to \omega, \rho \to
\pi^0 \gamma) {\displaystyle|}_{s=M_\omega^2}\,=\,
113 \, {\rm nb}\,.$$

The last result is better (3 $\sigma$ from the experimental result).

## $a_1$-decays

In this Section we shall study the decays corresponding to the axial-vector particle $a_1$. These decays have been studied in Ref. [@ivan] at the quark-loop level within a different ENJL model. The results we find for these decays are presented in Table [1](#table3){reference-type="ref" reference="table3"} and the Feynman diagrams in Figure [\[fig7\]](#fig7){reference-type="ref" reference="fig7"}. This particle decays mainly into $\rho \pi$ and the full width reported in Ref. [@pdg] ranges between 350 and 450 MeV. The amplitudes that we obtain for the transitions $a_1^+ \to \rho^+ \pi^0$ and $a_1^+ \to \rho^0 \pi^+$ are

$$\label{a1rho} \matrix{
A(a_1^+\to\rho^+(p_1)\pi^0(p_2))\,
=\,-\,A(a_1^+\to\rho^0(p_1)\pi^+(p_2))
\hfill\cr\cr \hphantom{A(a_1^+(P)\to
)}\,=\,i\,\frac{\displaystyle 2}{\displaystyle f_\pi}\,
\left[ A^{(2)}\,\left(\left(p_1 + p_2\right)^\mu
 \varepsilon^{*\nu}_{(a_1)}\,-
\,\left(p_1 + p_2\right)^\nu
\varepsilon^{*\mu}_{(a_1)}\right)\, \varepsilon^
{(\rho)}_\mu \,\, p_{2,\nu} \, \right. \hfill\cr\cr \left.
\hphantom{A(a_1^+(P)\to)\,=\,\frac{\displaystyle 2}
{\displaystyle f_\pi^2}} +\,
A^{(3)}\, \left( p_1^\mu \, \varepsilon^\nu_{(\rho)}\,-\,
p_1^\nu \, \varepsilon^\mu_{(\rho)}\right)\, \varepsilon^
{*(a_1)}_\mu \,\, p_{2,\nu} \right]
\hfill\cr\cr}$$

with the following corresponding decay rates

$$\label{a1rate} \matrix{
\Gamma(a_1^+\to\rho\pi)\,=\, \frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 48 \,
\pi M_{a_1}}\, \lambda^{1/2}\left(1,
\frac{\displaystyle m_\pi^2}{\displaystyle M_{a_1}^2}, \frac{\displaystyle
M_\rho^2}{\displaystyle M_{a_1}^2}\right)\, {\displaystyle\sum_{\rm pol}}
\left| A\right|^2 \,. \hfill\cr\cr}$$

We have also studied the direct decays into three pions of the $a_1$ axial-vector. The amplitude we get for the transition $a_1^+ \to \pi^+ \pi^0 \pi^0$ is the following

$$\label{a13pia} \matrix{
A(a_1^+\to\pi^+(p_1)\pi^0(p_2)\pi^0(p_3))\,=\,
-\,i\, \frac{\displaystyle 2 \sqrt 2}{\displaystyle f_\pi^3}
\, \varepsilon^{*\mu}_{(a_1)}\,
\left[ \left(\gamma^{(1)}+\gamma^{(3)}\right)\,
\right.\hfill\cr\cr \left.
\hspace*{0.2cm} \times\,
\left((p_1 \cdot p_2)\, p_{3,\mu} \,+\, (p_1
 \cdot  p_3)\, p_{2,\mu} \right)\,+\,
\left(2\,\gamma^{(2)}\,-\,\gamma^{(1)}\,
+\,2\,\gamma^{(4)}\right)\,
(p_2  \cdot  p_3)\, p_{1,\mu} \right] \, ;
\hfill\cr\cr}$$

and for $a_1^+ \to \pi^- \pi^+ \pi^+$,

$$\label{a13pib} \matrix{
A(a_1^+\to\pi^-(p_1)\pi^+(p_2)\pi^+(p_3))\,=\,
-\,i\, \frac{\displaystyle 2 \sqrt 2}{\displaystyle f_\pi^3}
\, \varepsilon^{*\mu}_{(a_1)}\,
\left[ 2\, \left(\gamma^{(1)}+\gamma^{(3)}\right)\,
(p_2  \cdot  p_3)\, p_{1,\mu} \right. \hfill\cr\cr \left.
\hspace*{0.2cm} \,+\,
\left(2\, \gamma^{(2)}\,+\,\gamma^{(3)}\,
+\,2\, \gamma^{(4)}\right)\, \left(
(p_1  \cdot  p_2)\, p_{3,\mu}
\,+\, (p_1  \cdot  p_3)\, p_{2,\mu} \right)\right]\,.
\hfill\cr\cr}$$

The experimental information we have is just on $a_1 \to \left(\pi \pi\right)_S \pi$ decays, *i.e.* where two of the pions are in an S-wave. For this process there is an educated guess in Ref. [@pdg] (the error is of the same order) for the experimental upper bound which is around 3 MeV. If we assume that the reported partial width for the decay $a_1 \to \left(\pi \pi
\right)_S \pi$ is only due to direct production and not to a possible intermediate scalar particle, *i.e.* $a_1 \to a_0 (f_0) \pi \to \left(\pi
\pi \right)_S \pi$ that would have been eventually detected, then we obtain the following amplitudes. For the transition $a_1^+ \to \left(\pi^+ \pi^0 \right)_S \pi^0$,

$$\label{a13pias} \matrix{
A(a_1^+\to\left(\pi^+(p_1)\pi^0(p_2)\right)_S
\pi^0(p_3))\,=\,\hfill\cr\cr
\hphantom{A(a_1^+\to\left(\pi^+(p_1)\pi^0(p_2)
\right)_S \,=\,}
-\,i\, \frac{\displaystyle 2 \sqrt 2}{\displaystyle f_\pi^3}
\, \left(\gamma^{(1)}\,+\,\gamma^{(3)}\right)\,
\, \left(\varepsilon^*_{(a_1)}  \cdot  p_3
\right) \, (p_1  \cdot  p_2) \, ; \hfill\cr\cr}$$

and for the $a_1^+ \to
\left(\pi^- \pi^+\right)_S \pi^+$,

$$\label{a13pibs} \matrix{
A(a_1^+\to\left(\pi^-(p_1)\pi^+(p_2)\right)_S
\pi^+(p_3))\,=\,\hfill\cr\cr
\hphantom{A(a_1^+\to \left(\pi^+(p_1)\right)}
-\,i\, \frac{\displaystyle 2 \sqrt 2}{\displaystyle f_\pi^3}
\, \left(2\,\gamma^{(2)}\,+\,\gamma^{(3)}\,
+\,2\,\gamma^{(4)}\right)
\, \left(\varepsilon^*_{(a_1)}  \cdot
p_3 \right) \, (p_1  \cdot  p_2) \, .\hfill\cr\cr}$$

The decay rate for $a_1^+ \to \pi^- \pi^+ \pi^+$ (for $a_1^+ \to \pi^+ \pi^0 \pi^0$ and $a_1^+ \to \left(\pi \pi\right)_S \pi$ are analogous) can be written as follows

$$\label{a13prate}
\Gamma(a_1^+ \to \pi^- \pi^+ \pi^+) \, = \,
\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 768 \, \pi^3 M_{a_1}} \, {\displaystyle\int}
\, {\rm d} E_+ \, {\displaystyle\int} \, {\rm d} E_- \,
{\displaystyle\sum_{\rm pol}} \, \left|A \right|^2$$

with $E_\pm\,=\, E_{\pi^+}\pm
E_{\pi^-}$ and the limits given by

$$\label{limitsc1} \matrix{
(E_+)_{\rm max} \, = \, M_{a_1} \, - \, m_\pi \,,
\hspace*{1.5cm}
(E_+)_{\rm min} \,=\, \frac{\displaystyle M_{a_1}}{\displaystyle 2} \,+\,
\frac{\displaystyle 3 m_\pi^2}{\displaystyle 2 M_{a_1}} \,,\hfill \cr\cr
(E_-)_{{\rm max}}
\,=\, \frac{\displaystyle m_\pi^2 + M_{a_1}^2 - 2 x - 2
 \left(M_{\pi^+\pi^-}^2\right)_{{\rm min}}}
{\displaystyle 2 M_{a_1}}\,, \hfill \cr\cr
(E_-)_{{\rm min}}
\,=\, \frac{\displaystyle m_\pi^2 + M_{a_1}^2 - 2 x - 2
 \left(M_{\pi^+\pi^-}^2\right)_{{\rm max}}}
{\displaystyle 2 M_{a_1}}\,, \hfill \cr\cr }$$

with

$$\label{limitsc2} \matrix{
\left(M_{\pi^+\pi^-}^2\right)_{{\rm min}({\rm max})}  \,= \,
\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 8 (m_\pi^2 + x)} \, \left[
\left(M_{a_1}^2 - m_\pi^2\right)^2 \right. \hfill \cr\cr
\hspace*{0.1cm} \left. \,-\,
\left( \lambda^{1/2}(2(m_\pi^2+x), m_\pi^2, m_\pi^2)
+(-) \, \lambda^{1/2}(M_{a_1}^2, m_\pi^2, 2 (m_\pi^2+x))
\right)\right] \,, \hfill \cr\cr
x\equiv p_1 \cdot p_2
\,=\, M_{a_1} E_+ - \frac{\displaystyle M_{a_1}^2 + m_\pi^2}
{\displaystyle 2}\,. \hfill \cr\cr}$$

Another interesting decay is $a_1^+ \to \rho^+ \gamma$. The amplitude we obtain is

$$\label{a1rhog} \matrix{
 A(a_1^+ \to \rho^+(p) \gamma(k)) \,=\, i\,|e|\,\frac{\displaystyle 4}
{\displaystyle 3} \,H\, \epsilon_{\mu \nu \alpha \beta}\,
\varepsilon^\mu_{(\rho)}\, \varepsilon^{* \nu}_{(a_1)}\,
k^\alpha \, \varepsilon^\beta_{(\gamma)}
\hfill\cr\cr }$$

and the corresponding decay rate

$$\label{a1rhpgr} \matrix{
 \Gamma(a_1^+\to \rho^+ \gamma) \,=\,\alpha \,
 \frac{\displaystyle 2}{\displaystyle 27}\,
 H^2 \, M_{a_1}\, \left(1\,-\, \frac{\displaystyle
M_\rho^2}{\displaystyle M_{a_1}^2}\right)^3\,
\left(1\,+\, \frac{\displaystyle M_\rho^2}{\displaystyle M_{a_1}^2}
\right) \, . \hfill\cr\cr}$$

Finally, we shall report on the decay $a_1^+ \to \pi^+ \gamma$. The amplitude we get for this process is

$$\label{a1pg} \matrix{
A(a_1^+\to \pi^+(p)\gamma(k))\,
=\, -\,i\, |e|\, \frac{\displaystyle f_A\,-\, 2
\, \sqrt 2 \, \alpha_A}{\displaystyle 2\, f_\pi}
\hfill\cr\cr \hphantom{A(a_1^+\to \pi^+(p))} \times \,
\, (\varepsilon_{(a_1)}^{*\mu} \, (p+k)^\nu\,-\,
\varepsilon_{(a_1)}^{*\nu} \, (p+k)^\mu)\,
(\varepsilon^{(\gamma)}_\mu k_\nu\,-\,
\varepsilon^{(\gamma)}_\nu k_\mu)
\hfill\cr\cr}$$

and the corresponding decay rate

$$\label{a1pgra} \matrix{
\Gamma(a_1^+\to\pi^+\gamma)\,=\,
\alpha \, \frac{\displaystyle M_{a_1}^3}{\displaystyle 24 \, f_\pi^2}
\, \left(f_A\,-\,2\sqrt 2 \, \alpha_A
\right)^2 \,\left(1\,-\, \frac{\displaystyle m_\pi^2}{\displaystyle
M_{a_1}^2}\right)^3 \, .\hfill\cr\cr}$$

The result we obtain for the full width of the $a_1$ axial-vector from the results in the column Prediction 1 is $\Gamma_{\rm full}\,=$ 261 MeV, to be compared with the experimental value quoted at the beginning of this Section (350 MeV $<$ $\left(\Gamma_{\rm full}
\right)_{\rm exp}$ $<$ 450 MeV).

In the column Prediction 2 we present the results obtained using the value $g_A\,=\, 0.76$. Given the poor experimental information we have on $a_1$-decays, both sets of predictions in Table [1](#table3){reference-type="ref" reference="table3"} are quite good. However, the full width for the $a_1$ axial-vector particle we obtain from the results in the column Prediction 2, $\Gamma_{\rm full}\,=$ 359 MeV, is better when compared with the experimental result quoted above.
