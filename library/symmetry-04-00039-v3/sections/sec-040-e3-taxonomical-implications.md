As discussed in the foregoing, the four basic FMS give rise to a “super(anti)symmetric” kind of
duplicate taxonomy: there is a half that involves the fermion triplet A, B and C and their conjugates
and combinations and which largely replicates the taxonomony of the Standard Model; and there is
another half involving D, C and B, which has no such connection but which exists by reasons of
symmetry. We recall, that with the replacement of A, B and C by D, C and B, respectively, the two

halves are manifestly isomorphic.
