The $q$-Weyl algebra $W_q$ is an associative algebra over $\mathbb{C}[q, q^{-1}]$ generated by invertible generators $x,y$ satisfying $$xy = q^2 yx.$$ The following is well known:

::: proposition
There is a unique algebra homomorphism $\phi : \mathcal{U}_q(\sla) \to W_q[z,z^{-1}]$ acting on generators as $$\phi(K)=x, \ \ \phi(E)=qy(z-x), \ \ \phi(F)=y^{-1}(1-z^{-1}x^{-1})$$
:::

It is easy to compute the image of the Casimir: $$\phi(\Omega) = qz + (qz)^{-1}.$$ From now on we write $\mathcal{W}_q = W_q[z, z^{-1}]$.

::: proposition
[]{#thm:R-action-on-weyl label="thm:R-action-on-weyl"} There exists a unique algebra automorphism $\mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}$ of the division algebra $\mathop{\mathrm{Div}}{\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2}}$ of $\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2}$ which acts on the generators as $$\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(z_1)&=z_1 \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(z_2)&=z_2 \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(x_1)&=x_1g, \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(x_2)&=g^{-1}x_2, \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(y_1^{-1})&=y_2^{-1}+(y_1^{-1}-z_2^{-1}y_2^{-1})x_2^{-1}, \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(y_2)&=\frac{z_1}{z_2}y_1+(y_2-{z_2}^{-1}y_1)x_1, \\
    \intertext{
    where
    \(
    x_1 = x \otimes 1, y_2 = 1 \otimes y, z_1 = z \otimes 1, \text{ etc.\ and} 
    \)
  }
    g&=1-x_1^{-1}y_1(z_1-x_1)y_2^{-1}(x_2-z_2^{-1}).
  
\end{aligned}$$ The following diagram commutes: $$\label{eq:automorphism-pullback}
    \begin{tikzcd}
     \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{U}_q^{\otimes 2}) \arrow[r, "\mathcal{R}"] \arrow[d, "\phi \otimes \phi"] & \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{U}_q^{\otimes 2}) \arrow[d, "\phi \otimes \phi"] \\
      \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2}) \arrow[r, "\mathcal{R}^W"] & \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2})
    \end{tikzcd}$$ The inverse of $\mathcal{R}^W$ acts on generators as $$\begin{aligned}
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(x_1)&=x_1\widetilde{g}^{-1}, \\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(x_2)&=\widetilde{g}x_2, \\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(y_1^{-1})&=\frac{z_1}{z_2}y_2^{-1} + (y_1^{-1}-{z_1}y_2^{-1})x_2,\\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(y_2)&=y_1 + (y_2 - {z_1}y_1)x_1^{-1},
  
\end{aligned}$$ where $$\widetilde{g}= 1 - y_1 (z_1 - x_1) y_2^{-1} (1 - z_2^{-1} x_2^{-1}).\qedhere$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The rules for $x_1$ and $x_2$ follow directly from those for $K_1$ and $K_2$ and it is natural to choose $\mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}$ to preserve the $z_i$ as $\mathcal{R}$ preserves the Casimirs. We give $y_1$ as an example, and $y_2$ can be computed similarly. Since $\mathcal{R}(E_1) = E_1 K_2$, we have $\mathcal{R}^W(q y_1(z_1 - x_1)) = q y_1(z_1-x_1) x_2$, or $$\mathcal{R}^W(y_1)
    \left[
      z_1 - x_1 + y_1(z_1 - x_1) y_2^{-1} (x_2 - z_2^{-1})
    \right]
    =
    y_1(z_1 - x_1)x_2.$$ We can multiply by $y_1^{-1}(z_1 - x_1)^{-1}$ on the right to get $$\mathcal{R}^W(y_1) \left[ y_1^{-1} + y_2^{-1} (x_2 - z_2^{-1}) \right] = x_2$$ from which it is easy to derive $$\mathcal{R}^W(y_1^{-1}) = y_2^{-1}+(y_1^{-1}-z_2^{-1}y_2^{-1})x_2^{-1}.\qedhere$$ ◻
:::
