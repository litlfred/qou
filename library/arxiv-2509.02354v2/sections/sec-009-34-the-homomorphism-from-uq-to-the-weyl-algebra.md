We now consider the presentation of $\mathcal{U}_q$ in terms of $\mathcal{W}_q$ at $q = \xi$. The map $\phi$ takes the center of $\mathcal{U}_\xi$ to the center of $\mathcal{W}_\xi$:

::: lemma
The center of $\mathcal{W}_{\xi}$ is generated by $z$, $x^N$, and $y^N$ and $\phi$ takes the center of $\mathcal{U}_\xi$ to the center of $\mathcal{W}_{\xi}$. Explicitly, $$\begin{aligned}
      \phi(K^N)
      &=
      x^N
      \\
      \phi(E^N)
      &=
      y^N(x^N - z^N)
      \\
      \phi(F^N)
      &=
      y^{-N}(1 - z^{-N} x^{-N})
    \end{aligned}$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The claim about the center of $\mathcal{W}$ is clear. $K^N$ is obvious and $F^N$ follows from the same reasoning as $E^N$. For $E^N$, notice that $$\begin{aligned}
    \phi(E^N)
    &=
    \left( \xi y (z - x) \right)^{N}
    \\
    &=
    (\xi y)^2 (z - \xi^2 x) (z - x) \left( y (z - x ) \right)^{N-2}
    \\
    &\dots
    =
    (\xi y)^N \prod_{k=0}^{N-1} (z - \xi^{2k} x)
  
\end{aligned}$$ All the terms in the product except $z^N$ and $x^N$ vanish. The coefficient of $z^N$ is clearly $1$, while the coefficient of $x^N$ is $(-1)^{N}$ times $\xi$ raised to the power $\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (2 k) =  N(N-1)$ so that $$\phi(E^N)
    = -y^N( z^N + (-1)^{N} \xi^{N(N-1)} x^N)
    = -y^N( z^N + (-1)^{N} (-1)^{N-1} x^N)
    = y^N( x^N - z^N). \qedhere$$ ◻
:::

As before the automorphism $\mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}$ of $\mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2})$ induces a map on the root-of-unity specialization. It is easy to compute the action on the generators:

::: proposition
[]{#prop:RW-action-on-center label="prop:RW-action-on-center"} The action of $\mathcal{R}^W$ on the center of $\mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{W}_\xi^{\otimes 2})$ is given by $$\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{R}^W(z_1)&=z_1, \\
    \mathcal{R}^W(z_2)&=z_2, \\
    \mathcal{R}^W(x_1^N)&=x_1^N G , \\
    \mathcal{R}^W(x_2^N)&=x_2^N G^{-1}, \\
    \mathcal{R}^W(y_1^{-N})&= y_2^{-N}+\left(y_1^{-N} - \frac{y_2^{-N}}{z_2^{N}}\right)x_2^{-N}, \\
    \mathcal{R}^W(y_2^N)&=\frac{z_1^N}{z_2^{N}}y_1^{N}+ \left(y_2^N - \frac{y_1^N}{z_2^N}\right)x_1^N,
  
\end{aligned}$$ where $$G = 1 + x_1^{-N} \frac{y_1^N}{y_2^N}(x_1^N - z_1^N)(x_2^N - z_2^{-N}).$$ The inverse action is $$\begin{aligned}
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(x_1^N)&= x_1^N \widetilde G^{-1}, \\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(x_2^N)&= x_2^N \widetilde G, \\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(y_1^{-N})&= \frac{z_1^N}{z_2^N}y_2^{-N}+(y_1^{-N} - z_1^N y_2^{-N})x_2^{N} ,  \\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(y_2^{N})&=y_1^{N}+(y_2^N - z_1^N y_1^N)x_1^{-N},
  
\end{aligned}$$ where $$\widetilde{G} = 1 + x_2^{-N} \frac{y_1^N}{y_2^N} (x_1^N - z_1^N)(x_2^N - z_2^{-N}).
    \qedhere$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* These follow from the $q$-binomial theorem and the action on the generators in [\[thm:R-action-on-weyl\]](#thm:R-action-on-weyl){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:R-action-on-weyl"}. ◻
:::

When acting on the algebra itself (not the division algebra) the map $\mathcal{R}^{W}$ is not always well-defined: one needs to ensure that each invertible generator is sent to a nonzero scalar. Before we dealt with this by inverting a certain element. Here it is more convenient to work directly with characters.

::: definition
The central subalgebra $\mathcal{Z}_0^\mathcal{W}\mathrel{:=}\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm N}, y^{\pm N}, z^{\pm N}] \subseteq \mathcal{W}_\xi$ maps to $\mathcal{Z}_0$ under $\phi$. We write $\Wchar$ for the set of characters $\chi : \mathcal{Z}_0^\mathcal{W}\to \mathbb{C}$. Such a character is determined by the numbers $$a = \chi(x^N), b = \chi(y^N), m = \chi(z^N).$$ and it corresponds to the $\mathcal{Z}_0$-character with $$\begin{gathered}
    \chi(K^{N})  = a
    \\
    \chi(E^{N})  = b(a - m)
    \\
    \chi(F^{N}) = (ab)^{-1}(a - m^{-1}).
  
\end{gathered}$$

The full center of $\mathcal{W}_\xi$ is $\mathcal{Z}^\mathcal{W}= \mathcal{Z}_0^\mathcal{W}[z]$. Characters of this algebra are elements of $\Wchar$ plus a choice of $N$th root of $m = \chi(z^N)$. We denote the space of such characters $\hat \chi$ by $\Wcharhat$. There is an obvious $N$-fold covering map $\Wcharhat \to \Wchar$.
:::

::: definition
[]{#def:braiding label="def:braiding"} Let $\chi_1, \chi_2 \in \Wchar$ and write $\chi_i(x^N) = a_i, \chi_i(y^N) = b_i, \chi_i(z^N) = m_i$. We say the pair $(\chi_1, \chi_2)$ is if the complex numbers defined by $$\begin{gathered}
    \label{eq:a-transf-positive}
    \begin{aligned}
      a_{1'}
      &=
      a_1 A^{-1}
      \\
      a_{2'}
      &=
      a_2 A
      \\
      A &= 1 - \frac{m_1 b_1}{b_2} \left(1 - \frac{a_1}{m_1}\right)\left(1 - \frac{1}{m_2 a_2}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    \\
    \label{eq:b-transf-positive}
    \begin{aligned}
      b_{1'}
      &=
      \frac{m_2 b_2}{m_1}
      \left(
        1 - m_2 a_2 \left( 1 - \frac{b_2}{m_1 b_1} \right)
      \right)^{-1}
      \\
      b_{2'}
      &=
      b_1
      \left(
        1 - \frac{m_1}{a_1}\left( 1 - \frac{b_2}{m_1 b_1} \right)
      \right)
    \end{aligned}
    \\
    \label{eq:m-transf-positive}
    \begin{aligned}
      m_{1'}
      &= m_1
      &
      m_{2'}
      &= m_2
    \end{aligned}
  
\end{gathered}$$ are not $0$ or $\infty$. In this case we write $(\chi_{2'} , \chi_{1'}) = B(\chi_1, \chi_2)$ where $\chi_{1'}(x^N) = a_{1'}$ and so on. There is an obvious extension to elements of $\Wcharhat$ by setting $\hat \chi_1(z) = \hat \chi_{1'}(z)$ and $\hat \chi_2(z) = \hat \chi_{2'}(z)$ and admissibility only depends on the image in $\Wchar$.
:::

As before $B$ is a partially defined map on pairs of characters. The inverse map $B^{-1}(\chi_1, \chi_2) = (\chi_{2'}, \chi_{1'})$ is given by $$\begin{gathered}
  \label{eq:a-transf-negative}
  \begin{aligned}
    a_{1'}
      &=
      a_1 \tilde A^{-1}
      \\
      a_{2'}
      &=
      a_2 \tilde A
      \\
      \tilde A
      &=
      1 - \frac{b_2}{m_1 b_1}\left(1 - m_1 a_1 \right)\left(1 - \frac{m_2}{a_2}\right).
  \end{aligned}
  \\
  \label{eq:b-transf-negative}
  \begin{aligned}
    b_{1'}
      &=
      \frac{m_2 b_2}{m_1}
      \left(
        1 - \frac{a_2}{m_2} \left( 1 - \frac{m_1 b_1}{b_2} \right)
      \right)
      \\
      b_{2'}
      &=
      b_1
      \left(
        1 - \frac{1}{m_1 a_1} \left( 1 - \frac{m_1 b_1}{b_2} \right)
      \right)^{-1}
  \end{aligned}
  \\
  \label{eq:m-transf-negative}
  \begin{aligned}
    m_{1'}
      &= m_1
      &
      m_{2'}
      &= m_2
  \end{aligned}
\end{gathered}$$

::: proposition
[]{#thm:braiding formulas label="thm:braiding formulas"} Whenever $B(\chi_1, \chi_2) = (\chi_{2'}, \chi_{1'})$ we have $$(\hat \chi_1 \otimes \hat \chi_2)(x) = (\hat \chi_{1'} \otimes \hat \chi_{2'})\mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(x)$$ for every $x \in \mathcal{Z}^\mathcal{W}\otimes \mathcal{Z}^\mathcal{W}$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* This follows from the action of $\mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}$ on the center computed in [\[prop:RW-action-on-center\]](#prop:RW-action-on-center){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:RW-action-on-center"}. It is easiest to use $$(\hat \chi_1 \otimes \hat \chi_2)\left((\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(x)\right) = (\hat \chi_{1'} \otimes \hat \chi_{2'})(x)$$ to compute $B$. ◻
:::
