Here we recall basic facts about quantum $\sla$, its structure at a root of unity and its braiding properties.

## The braiding for quantum $\sla$ {#sec:qgrp-conventions}

Recall that $\mathcal{U}_q(\sla)$ is the $\mathbb{C}[q, q^{-1}]$-algebra generated by $K^{\pm 1}, E, F$ with defining relations $$\begin{align*}
  KE &= q^2 EK \\
  KF &= q^{-2} FK \\
  [E,F] &= (q - q^{-1}) (K - K^{-1}).
\end{align*}$$ It is a Hopf algebra, with the coproduct $$\Delta(K)=K\otimes K, \ \ \Delta(E)=E\otimes K+1\otimes E, \ \ \Delta(F)=F\otimes 1+ K^{-1}\otimes F$$ and antipode $$S(K) = K^{-1}, \, \, S(E) = - EK^{-1}, \, \, S(F) = -KF.$$ The center of $\mathcal{U}_q(\sla)$ is freely generated by the Casimir element $$\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Casimir}
  \Omega = EF + q^{-1} K + q K^{-1}.
\end{equation}$$

## The braiding for $\mathcal{U}_q(\sla)$ {#sec:outer-R-matrix}

The algebra $\mathcal{U}_q(\sla)$ is not quasitriangular. Instead it is *braided* [@Reshetikhin1995] which means there is an outer automorphism $$\mathcal{R}:  \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{U}_q(\sla)^{\otimes 2}) \to \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{U}_q(\sla)^{\otimes 2})$$ of the division ring of $\mathcal{U}_q(\sla)^{\otimes 2}$ which intertwines the coproduct and opposite coproduct $$\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal{R}(\Delta(x)) =  \Delta^{op}(x)
\end{equation*}$$ and satisfies hexagon identities $$\begin{align*}
  
  (\Delta \otimes 1) \mathcal{R}(x \otimes y) &= \mathcal{R}_{13} \mathcal{R}_{23} (\Delta(x) \otimes y) \\
  
  (1 \otimes \Delta) \mathcal{R}(x \otimes y) &= \mathcal{R}_{13} \mathcal{R}_{12} (x \otimes \Delta(y))
\end{align*}$$ that imply the Yang-Baxter equation. Here $\mathcal{R}_{ij}$ means that $\mathcal{R}$ acts on tensor factors $i$ and $j$. That is, for each $x, y \in \mathcal{U}_q$ one can write $$\mathcal{R}(x \otimes y) = \sum_k f_k \otimes g_k$$ for some $f_k, g_k \in \mathcal{U}_q$ and we define $$\mathcal{R}_{13}(x \otimes z \otimes y) = \sum_k f_k \otimes z \otimes g_k$$ and so on. The automorphism $\mathcal{R}$ is derived from the conjugation action of the universal $R$-matrix in the $h$-adic quantum group, $q = e^h$ [@Kashaev2004].

$\mathcal{R}$ acts on generators as $$\begin{align*}
  \mathcal{R}(\Delta(x)) &= \Delta^{\operatorname{op}}(x), \ x \in \mathcal{U}_\xi \\
  \mathcal{R}(K_1) &= K_1 (1  - q^{-1} K_1^{-1} E_1 F_2 K_2 ) \\
  \mathcal{R}(E_1) &= E_1 K_2 \\
  \mathcal{R}(F_2) &= K_1^{-1} F_2
\end{align*}$$ where we abbreviate $K_1 = K \otimes 1, E_2 = 1 \otimes E$, etc.

::: proposition
$\mathcal{R}$ acts on the remaining generators of $\mathcal{U}_q^{\otimes 2}$ by: $$\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{R}(K_2) &= (1 - q^{-1} K_1^{-1} E_1 F_2 K_2)^{-1} K_2 \\
    \mathcal{R}(E_2) &= E_1 + K_1 E_2 - E_1 K_2^2 (1 - q K_1^{-1} E_1 F_2 K_2)^{-1} \\
    \mathcal{R}(F_1) &= F_1 K_2^{-1} + F_2 - K_1^{-2} F_2(1 - q K_1^{-1} E_1 F_2 K_2)^{-1}
\end{align*}$$ and preserves Casimirs: $\mathcal{R}(\Omega_1) = \Omega_1, \mathcal{R}(\Omega_2) = \Omega_2.$
:::

These follow from computations like $$\mathcal{R}(1 \otimes K) = \mathcal{R}(K \otimes 1)^{-1} \mathcal{R}(K \otimes K) = \mathcal{R}(K \otimes 1)^{-1} K \otimes K$$ and $$\mathcal{R}(1 \otimes E) = \mathcal{R}(E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E - E \otimes K) = E \otimes 1 + K \otimes E - \mathcal{R}(E \otimes K).$$

## Representation into the Weyl algebra

The $q$-Weyl algebra $W_q$ is an associative algebra over $\mathbb{C}[q, q^{-1}]$ generated by invertible generators $x,y$ satisfying $$xy = q^2 yx.$$ The following is well known:

::: proposition
There is a unique algebra homomorphism $\phi : \mathcal{U}_q(\sla) \to W_q[z,z^{-1}]$ acting on generators as $$\phi(K)=x, \ \ \phi(E)=qy(z-x), \ \ \phi(F)=y^{-1}(1-z^{-1}x^{-1})$$
:::

It is easy to compute the image of the Casimir: $$\phi(\Omega) = qz + (qz)^{-1}.$$ From now on we write $\mathcal{W}_q = W_q[z, z^{-1}]$.

::: proposition
[]{#thm:R-action-on-weyl label="thm:R-action-on-weyl"} There exists a unique algebra automorphism $\mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}$ of the division algebra $\mathop{\mathrm{Div}}{\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2}}$ of $\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2}$ which acts on the generators as $$\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(z_1)&=z_1 \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(z_2)&=z_2 \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(x_1)&=x_1g, \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(x_2)&=g^{-1}x_2, \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(y_1^{-1})&=y_2^{-1}+(y_1^{-1}-z_2^{-1}y_2^{-1})x_2^{-1}, \\
    \mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}(y_2)&=\frac{z_1}{z_2}y_1+(y_2-{z_2}^{-1}y_1)x_1, \\
    \intertext{
    where
    \(
    x_1 = x \otimes 1, y_2 = 1 \otimes y, z_1 = z \otimes 1, \text{ etc.\ and} 
    \)
  }
    g&=1-x_1^{-1}y_1(z_1-x_1)y_2^{-1}(x_2-z_2^{-1}).
\end{align*}$$ The following diagram commutes: $$\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:automorphism-pullback}
    \begin{tikzcd}
     \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{U}_q^{\otimes 2}) \arrow[r, "\mathcal{R}"] \arrow[d, "\phi \otimes \phi"] & \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{U}_q^{\otimes 2}) \arrow[d, "\phi \otimes \phi"] \\
      \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2}) \arrow[r, "\mathcal{R}^W"] & \mathop{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathcal{W}_q^{\otimes 2})
    \end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}$$ The inverse of $\mathcal{R}^W$ acts on generators as $$\begin{align*}
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(x_1)&=x_1\widetilde{g}^{-1}, \\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(x_2)&=\widetilde{g}x_2, \\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(y_1^{-1})&=\frac{z_1}{z_2}y_2^{-1} + (y_1^{-1}-{z_1}y_2^{-1})x_2,\\
    (\mathcal{R}^W)^{-1}(y_2)&=y_1 + (y_2 - {z_1}y_1)x_1^{-1},
\end{align*}$$ where $$\widetilde{g}= 1 - y_1 (z_1 - x_1) y_2^{-1} (1 - z_2^{-1} x_2^{-1}).\qedhere$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The rules for $x_1$ and $x_2$ follow directly from those for $K_1$ and $K_2$ and it is natural to choose $\mathcal{R}^\mathcal{W}$ to preserve the $z_i$ as $\mathcal{R}$ preserves the Casimirs. We give $y_1$ as an example, and $y_2$ can be computed similarly. Since $\mathcal{R}(E_1) = E_1 K_2$, we have $\mathcal{R}^W(q y_1(z_1 - x_1)) = q y_1(z_1-x_1) x_2$, or $$\mathcal{R}^W(y_1)
    \left[
      z_1 - x_1 + y_1(z_1 - x_1) y_2^{-1} (x_2 - z_2^{-1})
    \right]
    =
    y_1(z_1 - x_1)x_2.$$ We can multiply by $y_1^{-1}(z_1 - x_1)^{-1}$ on the right to get $$\mathcal{R}^W(y_1) \left[ y_1^{-1} + y_2^{-1} (x_2 - z_2^{-1}) \right] = x_2$$ from which it is easy to derive $$\mathcal{R}^W(y_1^{-1}) = y_2^{-1}+(y_1^{-1}-z_2^{-1}y_2^{-1})x_2^{-1}.\qedhere$$ ◻
:::
