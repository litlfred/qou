[]{#sec9 label="sec9"} As advertised at the outset, the goal of this paper has been to show how SSLT developed and was generalized in response to questions arising in the analysis of the Ising model. Although the case $|||\beta|||=1$ in [@DIK1] does not arise in the Ising model (the same is true for the Hankel and Toeplitz + Hankel determinants [\[eq108\]](#eq108){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq108"} and [\[eq115\]](#eq115){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq115"} respectively), this case does arise in many other problems in statistical physics. For example, the probability $P_E(n)$ of a string of length $n$ of ferromagnetically aligned spins in the antiferromagnetic ground state in the $XY$ spin chain, is given, for a certain range of parameters, by a Toeplitz determinant with $2$ $\beta$-singularities such that $|||\beta|||=1$. Thus [\[eq100\]](#eq100){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq100"} verifies the result on $P_E(n)$, $n\to\infty$, presented in [@FrAb], which the authors based on the Basor-Tracy conjecture. In a similar way, the results in [@DIK1] can be used to justify the asymptotic results for correlations arising in the theory of the impenetrable Bose gas that were obtained in the work of Ovchinnikov [@Ov] on the basis of the Basor-Tracy conjecture. In another direction in [@GGM], the authors use [\[eq100\]](#eq100){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq100"} to evaluate the long-time behavior of a variety of non-equilibrium 1D many-body problems. In particular, in the Fermi edge singularity and tunneling spectroscopy problems considered in [@GGM], the authors show that the various contributions to [\[eq100\]](#eq100){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq100"} have a very transparent physical meaning: they correspond to contributions to the Green's functions for these problems from multiple Fermi edges.

Theorem [\[BT\]](#BT){reference-type="ref" reference="BT"} can also be used to analyze the asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues $\lambda^{(n)}_1 \le \lambda^{(n)}_2 \le \dots \le \lambda^{(n)}_n$, $n\to\infty$, of a Toeplitz matrix $T_n(\varphi)$ with a real symbol $\varphi$. At the beginning of our story (see Theorem [\[theorem2\]](#theorem2){reference-type="ref" reference="theorem2"}) we interpreted Szegő's early results as saying that the $\lambda^{(n)}_j$'s were equidistributed as $n\to\infty$. But what about the behavior of individual eigenvalues, $\lambda^{(n)}_k$, $n\to\infty$? Early work on this question considered the so-called extreme eigenvalues $\lambda^{(n)}_k$ and $\lambda^{(n)}_{n-k}$ with $k$ fixed as $n\to\infty$ (see [@GreSz], [@BGrM] and the references therein). The important case for $\lambda^{(n)}_k$ with $n\to\infty$ and $k/n\to x \in (0,1)$ was only considered for the first time recently in [@BGrM]. Recall that by general principles (see e.g. [@GreSz]), for bounded, real-valued functions $f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)$, the eigenvalues $\{\lambda^{(n)}_k\}$ lie in the interval $[L, M]$, $L=\inf \, f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)$, $M=\sup
\, f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)$, and as $n\to\infty$ the $\lambda^{(n)}_k$'s fill out the interval in the sense that if $\lambda\in [L, M]$, then there exist $k_n(\lambda)$ such that $\lambda^{(n)}_{k_n(\lambda)} \to \lambda$. In [@BGrM] the authors consider function $f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)$ that are unimodal, real-valued trigonometric polynomials, i.e., for some $0<\theta_0 < 2\pi$, $\frac{d}{d\theta} \: f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)>0$ for $0< \theta<\theta_0$ and $\frac{d}{d\theta} \:f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right) <0$ for $\theta_0 < \theta< 2\pi$, with the additional property that $\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2} \:f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right) \neq 0$ at $\theta=0$ and $\theta=\theta_0$. For any $\lambda\in (L,M)$, there exist unique $0 < \theta_1(\lambda) < \theta_0 < \theta_2(\lambda) < 2\pi$ such that $f\!\left(e^{i\theta_j(\lambda)}\right) - \lambda=0, \;
j=1,2$. The function $$\psi(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2}\left[\theta_1(\lambda) - \theta_2(\lambda)\right] + \pi$$ extends to a homeomorphism from $[L,M]$ onto $[0,\pi]$. In [@BGrM] the authors prove, in particular, that for any $x\in (0,1)$ $$\label{eq118}
\lambda^{(n)}_k = \lambda_x + o(1)$$ as $n\to\infty$ and $k/n \to x$, where $\psi (\lambda_x) = \pi x$, and $$\label{eq119}
\lambda^{(n)}_{k+1} - \lambda^{(n)}_k = \frac{\pi}{\psi'(\lambda_x)} \:\frac{1}{n+1} + o\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)$$ if $\left| k/n - x\right|= O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)$ as $n\to\infty$.

The estimate [\[eq119\]](#eq119){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq119"} brings precision to the equidistribution result in Theorem [\[theorem2\]](#theorem2){reference-type="ref" reference="theorem2"}. In [@DIK2] the authors extend the result in [@BGrM] to $C^\infty$ unimodal, real-valued functions on $S^1$, and they also consider piecewise--constant symbols of the form $$\label{eq120}
\begin{aligned}
f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right) &= 1, \qquad & \theta&\in \left[0, \widehat{\theta}_1 \right) \cup \left[\widehat{\theta}_2, 2\pi\right) \\
&= e^{2\pi\gamma}, \qquad &\theta&\in \left[ \widehat{\theta}_1, \widehat{\theta}_2\right)
\end{aligned}$$ where $0\le \widehat{\theta}_1 < \widehat{\theta}_2 < 2\pi$ and $\gamma >0$ are given. For symbols such as [\[eq120\]](#eq120){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq120"}, the eigenvalues $\lambda^{(n)}_k$ cannot be spaced $1/n$ apart as in [\[eq119\]](#eq119){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq119"}. This is clear, for example, from [\[eq4\]](#eq4){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq4"} in Theorem [\[theorem2\]](#theorem2){reference-type="ref" reference="theorem2"}, if we let $F(\lambda)$ be any non-negative continuous function with support in $\left(1, e^{2\pi\gamma}\right)$. And indeed, the general result in [@Bas2] asserts that in the case [\[eq120\]](#eq120){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq120"}, for any small $\epsilon>0$, the interval $\left(1+\epsilon, e^{2\pi\gamma}-\epsilon\right)$ contains order $\log n$ eigenvalues $\lambda^{(n)}_k$, spaced at distance $1/\log n$ apart, as $n\to\infty$: The bulk of the eigenvalues accumulate at $1$ and at $e^{2\pi\gamma}$. On the basis of numerical computations, the authors in [@LeSh] (see also [@LeSoSo]) conjectured that in the case $$\widehat{\theta}_2 - \widehat{\theta}_1 = 2\pi \, p/q, \qquad p,\, q\in \mathbb{Z}, \qquad 0 < p < q$$ there is a "near periodicity" in the spectrum of $T_n(f)$. In [@DIK2] the authors prove this conjecture in the following sense: for $n$ sufficiently large, if $\lambda^{(n)}_k$ in an eigenvalue of $T_n(f)$ in $\left(1+\epsilon,\, e^{2\pi\,\gamma}-\epsilon\right)$, then there is an eigenvalue $\lambda^{(n+q)}_j$ of $T_{n+q}$ such that $\left|\lambda^{(n)}_k - \lambda^{(n+q)}_j \right| \le c (n\log n)^{-1}$. Of course $1/(n \, \log  n) << 1/\log n$, the spacing of the $\lambda^{(n)}_k$'s, $k=1, \dots, n$.

The proof of the above results in [@DIK2] rests on the simple fact that $\det \left(T_n(f) - \lambda\right)=
\det \left(T_n (f-\lambda)\right)$, so that the eigenvalue equation for $T_n(f)$ is equivalent to the vanishing of the Toeplitz determinant $D_n(f-\lambda)$. Now if $f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)$ is a smooth, unimodal function on $S^1$, then $f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)-\lambda$ is a FH symbol with two singularities at $z_1=e^{i\theta_1 (\lambda)},
\; z_2=e^{i\theta_2(\lambda)}$ and $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 =\frac{1}{2}, \; \beta_1=-\beta_2 =\frac{1}{2}$ (see [\[eqBG\]](#eqBG){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqBG"}) $$\label{eq121}
f(z) -\lambda=e^{V(z)} \left|z-z_1\right| \left|z-z_2\right| g_{z_1,\, \frac{1}{2}} (z) \:g_{z_2,\, -\frac{1}{2}}(z)
\left(\frac{z_1}{z_2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}$$ where $V\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)$ is smooth on $S^1$. This is a case of a FH symbol with $|||\beta|||=1$. Therefore we need to use Theorem [\[BT\]](#BT){reference-type="ref" reference="BT"} to obtain the asymptotics for $D_n(f-\lambda)$. The set $M_{\beta}$ here contains 2 elements, and the condition for an eigenvalue is the vanishing of the sum in [\[eq100\]](#eq100){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq100"}: $$D_n(f-\lambda)=R_n(f(\beta_1,\beta_2)-\lambda)(1+o(1))+R_n(f(\beta_1-1,\beta_2+1)-\lambda)(1+o(1))=0$$ The asymptotic results on the eigenvalues follow from this formula.

The Toeplitz matrix $T_n(f)$ with the symbol $f\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)$ in [\[eq120\]](#eq120){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq120"} is handled similarly. The function $f(z)-\lambda$ in this case has 2 $\beta$-type singularities whose positions on the circle are fixed, but the imaginary parts of $\beta$'s depend on $\lambda$: $\beta_1=-\beta_2=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma(\lambda)$. Note that we again have $|||\beta|||=1$, and the condition for the eigenvalues is thus given by Theorem [\[BT\]](#BT){reference-type="ref" reference="BT"}.

Finally we describe an example where the results in [@DIK1] are needed for a Toeplitz+Hankel determinant of type [\[eq115\]](#eq115){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq115"}. The following problem arises in the framework of the random matrix approach to the theory of the Riemann zeta-function and other $L$-functions (see [@Keat] for a recent survey). Define $$\label{eq123}
\phi(e^{i\theta}) = \left|2 \sin \frac{\theta}{2}\right|^{2k} \, e^{V(e^{i\theta})}, \qquad k\in \mathbb{N}$$ where $$\begin{aligned}
V\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right) &= 2k \left[\int^e_1 u(y)
\left\{\sum^\infty_{j=-\infty} \mathrm{Ci}\, \left(  \left|\theta+2\pi j\right|
(\log y) (\log X)\right)\right\}dy- \log \left|2\sin \frac{\theta}{2}\right| \right], \\
&\quad \mathrm{Ci}\,(z) =- \int^\infty_z \frac{\cos t}{t} \; dt
\end{aligned}$$ and $u(y)$ is a smooth, non-negative function supported on $\left[e^{1-X^{-1}}, e\right]$ and of total mass one. Consider the following average over the orthogonal group $SO(2n)$, $$\mathbb{E}_{SO(2n)} \left(\prod^n_{j=1} \phi \left(e^{i\theta_i}\right)\right).$$ Here $e^{\pm i\theta_1}, \dots e^{\pm i\theta_n}$ are the eigenvalues of a random matrix in $SO(2n)$: note that $\phi$ is even, $\phi\!\left(e^{i\theta}\right)=\phi\!\left(e^{-i\theta}\right)$. This expectation was introduced by Bui and Keating in [@BuKe] (following [@GHK]) as the random matrix counterpart of a key term contributing to the mean values of certain Dirichlet $L$-functions in the Katz-Sarnak orthogonal family. The issue is the large $n$ and large $X$ behavior of this average. The question can be resolved with the help of [\[eq117\]](#eq117){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq117"}. One observes that $$\mathbb{E}_{SO(2n)} \left(\prod^n_{j=1} \phi \left(e^{i\theta_j}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\,\det \left( \phi_{j-k}+
\phi_{j+k}\right)^{n-1}_{j,\,k=0}$$ and that the symbol [\[eq123\]](#eq123){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq123"} is of FH type with a single $\alpha$-singularity at $z_0=1$ and $\alpha_0=k$. Directly from (the precise version of) the asymptotic formula [\[eq117\]](#eq117){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq117"} (see Theorem 1.25 in [@DIK1]), one obtains, for $X$ large, as $n\to\infty$ $$\label{eq124}
\mathbb{E}_{SO(2n)} \left(\prod^n_{j=1} \phi\left(e^{i\theta_j}\right)\right) \sim G(1+k)
\left( \frac{\Gamma (1+2k)}{G(1+2k) \;\Gamma(1+k)} \right)^{\!\frac{1}{2}} \left(\frac{2n}{e^{\gamma_E} \,\log X}
\right)^{\!\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}$$ where $\gamma_E$ is Euler's constant. Formula [\[eq124\]](#eq124){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq124"} is precisely the asymptotic form conjectured by Bui and Keating in [@BuKe].
