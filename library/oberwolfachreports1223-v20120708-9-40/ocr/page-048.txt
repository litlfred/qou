The Seiberg-Witten invariants are not well defined [1]: A generic SW moduli
space contains a circle of reductions and finitely many regular irreducible points.
What can try to define an invariant by counting only the irreducible points (by

ducible and then disappear, so the algebraic cardinality of the irreducible part of
the moduli space will jump. One has a countable chamber structure in the param-
eter space, and the SW “invariant” obtained by counting algebraically irreducible
points can take infinitely many values [1].
