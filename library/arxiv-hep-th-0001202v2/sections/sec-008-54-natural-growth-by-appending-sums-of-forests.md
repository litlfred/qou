Let $F$ be a forest. We define $N_F(X)$ to be the sum of forests obtained by appending $F$ to every node of $X$, in turn. To append $F=\prod_j t_j$ to a particular node, one connects the roots of all the $t_j$ to that node. We note that $N_F$ obeys a Leibniz rule, with $N_F(e)=0$ and $N_F(l_1)=B_+(F)$. We have already encountered two examples, namely the grading operator $Y:=N_{e}$, which merely counts nodes, and the simplest natural growth operator $N:=N_{l_1}$, which appends a single node. Finally, with $Z=F_1+F_2$, we make $N_Z:=N_{F_1}+N_{F_2}$ linear in its subscript, as well as its argument.

The commutation relations ([\[np\]](#np){reference-type="ref" reference="np"},[\[nm\]](#nm){reference-type="ref" reference="nm"}) then generalize to $$\begin{aligned}
\left[N_Z,\,B_+\right]&=&B_+\circ L_Z\label{npx}\\
\left[B_-,\,N_Z\right]&=&L_Z\circ B_-\label{nmx}
\end{aligned}$$ with $L_Z(X):=Z X$. Thus $[N_Z,C]=0$ and $N_Z$ preserves the kernel of $C$ for all $Z\in{\cal H}_R$.

The great virtue of this construct is that it gives $$U_1\circ N_{Z}(X)=Z\otimes Y(X)
\label{H2}$$ when both $Z$ and $X$ are 1-primitive.

**Proof**: We use the shorthand notation $\Delta(X)=X\otimes e +e\otimes X+X^\prime\otimes X^{\prime\prime}$ for any Hopf algebra element $X$, with the final term denoting a sum over tensor products containing no scalars. Let $Z$ be any 1-primitive. Then $$U_1\circ N_Z(X)=
N_Z(X^\prime)\otimes X^{\prime\prime}
+X^\prime\otimes N_Z(X^{\prime\prime})
+(L_Z\otimes Y)\circ\Delta(X)
\label{UNZ}$$ consists of terms in which $X^\prime$ or $X^{\prime\prime}$ grow naturally, with a final contribution where $Z$ is itself completely cut from any node to which it was connected by $N_Z$, with the grading operator $Y$ acting on the right, to count the number of cuts. The case with $Z=l_1$ was proven in [@CK], by an analysis of admissible cuts. Here, where $Z$ is 1-primitive, we obtain a result of the same form, since the internal cuts of $Z$ cancel when $U_1(Z)=0$. (A more general formula, for arbitrary $Z$, can be given but is not required here.) When $X$ is 1-primitive, with $X^\prime=X^{\prime\prime}=0$, we obtain ([\[H2\]](#H2){reference-type="ref" reference="H2"}) from $L_Z\otimes Y$ acting on the second term of $\Delta(X)=X\otimes e+e\otimes X$. $\Box$

The result ([\[H2\]](#H2){reference-type="ref" reference="H2"}) immediately proves that $H_2(x)=[H_1(x)]^2$, since it shows that each pairing $N_{X_1}(X_2)$ of 1-primitives gives an element of ${\cal H}_2$ that is inequivalent to any other pairing. Hence ([\[weak\]](#weak){reference-type="ref" reference="weak"}) is saturated at $k=2$. Now we define the iteration $$V_{k+1}(X_1,\ldots,X_k,X_{k+1}):=N_{V_k(X_1,\ldots,X_k)}(X_{k+1})
\label{Vk}$$ for $k>0$, with $V_1:={\rm id}$. Then, for example, $V_2(X_1,X_2):=N_{X_1}(X_2)$ and $$V_3(X_1,X_2,X_3):=N_{N_{X_1}(X_2)}(X_3)
\not=N_{X_1}\left(N_{X_2}(X_3)\right)\,.
\label{V3}$$ We remark that a Hochschild boundary can be defined for maps $V_{k+1}:{\cal H}_R^{\otimes(k+1)}\to{\cal H}_R$. For this, it is sufficient to define terms of the form $V_k(X_1,\ldots,X_j X_{j+1},\ldots,X_{k+1})$, where one argument is a product. Natural growth by forests supplies this. Consequences will be described in future work. For the present, we are content with the following result.

**Theorem**: The dimensions $H_{n,k}$ of the bigrading of the Hopf algebra of undecorated rooted trees, by weight $n$ and degree of primitivity $k$, are generated by ([\[ans\]](#ans){reference-type="ref" reference="ans"}).

**Proof**: Let $X_1,X_2,\ldots,X_k$ be 1-primitives, which need not be distinct. Then $$U_{k-1}\circ V_k(X_1,X_2,\ldots,X_k)=
X_1\otimes Y(X_2)\otimes\ldots\otimes Y(X_k)
\label{Ukm}$$ by coassociativity and iteration of the argument that led to ([\[H2\]](#H2){reference-type="ref" reference="H2"}). Thus $H_k(x)=[H_1(x)]^k$ saturates ([\[weak\]](#weak){reference-type="ref" reference="weak"}). Then $H(x,y)=1/(1-H_1(x)y)$ gives $R(x)=x/(1-H_1(x))$, at $y=1$. Solving for $H_1(x)=1-x/R(x)$, we obtain ([\[ans\]](#ans){reference-type="ref" reference="ans"}). $\Box$
