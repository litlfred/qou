We will now ﬁnd that left- and right-handed Weyl spinors, Majorana spinors, and Dirac
spinors are all generalized ideals under the same complex invariant-action,
b′ =ab P +a∗bP∗. (3.5)
For concreteness, we deﬁne P to be the projector 1
2 (1 +iϵz), although it is clear that
a continuum of other possibilities exist. P and its complex conjugate, P∗, exhibit the
properties
PP =P, P ∗P∗ =P∗, PP ∗ =P∗P = 0, P +P∗ = 1. (3.6)
Before we begin, though, it will be useful to carry out a change of basis from{1, iϵx, iϵy, iϵz}
to a new, suggestively named basis,{ϵ↑↑, ϵ↓↑, ϵ↑↓, ϵ↓↓}. This new basis will be linked to the
operator, iϵz∼σz, in that these basis elements will be deﬁned so as to have the properties:
iϵzϵ↑↑ = +ϵ↑↑ ϵ↑↑iϵz = +ϵ↑↑
iϵzϵ↓↑ =−ϵ↓↑ ϵ↓↑iϵz = +ϵ↓↑
iϵzϵ↑↓ = +ϵ↑↓ ϵ↑↓iϵz =−ϵ↑↓
iϵzϵ↓↓ =−ϵ↓↓ ϵ↓↓iϵz =−ϵ↓↓.
(3.7)
In terms of the old basis, we deﬁne these new basis vectors to be
ϵ↑↑≡ 1
2 (1 +iϵz) ϵ↓↑≡ 1
2 (ϵy +iϵx)
ϵ↑↓≡ 1
2 (−ϵy +iϵx) ϵ↓↓≡ 1
2 (1−iϵz).
(3.8)
For convenience, we also include here the old basis in terms of the new one,
1 =ϵ↑↑ +ϵ↓↓ ϵx =−i (ϵ↓↑ +ϵ↑↓)
ϵy =ϵ↓↑−ϵ↑↓ ϵz =−i (ϵ↑↑−ϵ↓↓).
(3.9)
