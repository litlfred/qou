\subsubsection{Non-commutative probability theory} The originality of this section is that, although the problem is formulated in a classical probability space, it is natural to work in the setting of non-commutative probability theory. \begin{definition} A {\em non-commutative probability space} is a complex unital $\star$-algebra $\mathscr{A}$ with some linear functional $\varphi:\mathscr{A} \to \C$ such that $\varphi(1)=1$ and, for any $a \in \mathscr{A}$, $\varphi(a^\star a) \ge 0$. \end{definition} This generalizes the notion of probability space: elements of $\mathscr{A}$ should be thought as random variables, while $\varphi$ should be thought as the expectation.
