A complex such as I in Theorem 1.12 is called a residual complex. It actually depends functorially on R: I = ER, where E is the Cousin functor. Noncommutative variants of the Cousin functor are studied in [Ye2] and [YZ2]. In particular one can show that the complex I can be made into a complex of bimodules, where on the right it is the minimal injective resolution of R in ModB◦. It follows that R is an Auslander dualizing complex, as deﬁned in Section 2. In [YZ2] we show that a more complicated version of Theorem 1.13 holds w henA =B is a PI algebra.
