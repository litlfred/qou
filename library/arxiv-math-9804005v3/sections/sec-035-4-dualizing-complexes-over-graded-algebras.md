In this section we consider connected $\mathbb{Z}$-graded $k$-algebras, namely algebras $A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_{n}$ with $A_{0} \cong k$ and $A_{n}$ a finitely generated $k$-module.

For such an algebra $A$ let $\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A$ be the category of $\mathbb{Z}$-graded left modules, with degree $0$ homomorphisms. For $M, N \in \operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A$ we write $M(n)$ for the shifted module with $M(n)_{i} = M_{n + i}$, and $$\operatorname{Hom}_{A}^{\mathrm{gr}}(M, N) := \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A}^{\mathrm{gr}}(M, N(n)) .$$ There is a forgetful functor $\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A \rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod}} A$. Observe that $\operatorname{Hom}_{A}^{\mathrm{gr}}(M, N) \subset \operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M, N)$ with equality when $M$ is finitely generated.

$\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A$ is an abelian category with direct and inverse limits, enough injectives and enough projectives. Let $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A)$ be the derived category. The derived functors $\mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{gr}}_{A}(M, N)$ and $M {\otimes}^{\mathrm{L}}_{A} N$ are calculated just as in the ungraded case, see Section 1, but using graded-projectives or graded-injectives.

We say $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} k$ is *locally finite* if each $M_{n}$ is a finitely generated $k$-module. Let $M^{*} := \operatorname{Hom}_{k}^{\mathrm{gr}}(M, k)$. Denote by $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}_{\mathrm{lf}}( \operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A)$ the subcategory of complexes with locally finite cohomologies. Matlis duality says that $M \cong M^{**}$ for $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{D}}_{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A)$.

We denote by $\mathfrak{m}$ the augmentation ideal $\bigoplus_{n > 0} A_{n}$ of $A$, and we write $\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M$ for the $\mathfrak{m}$-torsion submodule of a graded $A$-module $M$. There is a derived functor $\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}$, which is calculated by graded-injectives (see [@Ye1]). The cohomology modules are $\mathrm{H}^{i} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M =
\lim\limits_{n \rightarrow} \operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(A / A_{\geq n}, M)$. We write $\mathfrak{m}^{\circ}$ for the augmentation ideal of $A^{\circ}$.

The definitions and results of the previous sections can all be translated to the graded category by adding the adjective "graded" where needed, like "graded dualizing complex," "graded Auslander property" etc. The proofs of the graded variants of these results are identical to the ungraded ones, so there is no need to repeat them. In the rest of the paper we shall refer to such a result by writing something like "according to the graded variant of Theorem \...\".

::: rem
[]{#a6.1 label="a6.1"} Let $G$ be any finitely generated abelian group. Fix an isomorphism $G \cong \mathbb{Z}^{r} \times T$, where $T$ is a finite group, and a basis $g_{1}, \ldots, g_{r}$ of $\mathbb{Z}^{r}$. Let $G_{+}$ be the semigroup generated by $0$ and the elements $g_{i} + t$, $1 \leq i \leq r$, $t \in T$. For $g, g' \in G$ we write $g \geq g'$ if $g - g' \in G_{+}$, and this defines a partial order on $G$. A $G$-graded $k$-algebra $A$ is called *connected* if $A = \bigoplus_{g \in G_{+}} A_{g}$, $A_{0} = k$ and each $A_{g}$ is finitely generated as a module over $k$. The augmentation ideal of $A$ is $\mathfrak{m} := \bigoplus_{g > 0} A_{g}$.

Note that the group homomorphism $\phi : G \rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$ sending $g_{i} \mapsto 1$ makes $A$ into a connected $\mathbb{Z}$-graded algebra, with $A_{n} = \bigoplus_{\phi(g) = n} A_{g}$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

It is not hard to see that all results in this paper which are stated for connected $\mathbb{Z}$-graded algebras are also valid for connected $G$-graded $k$-algebras, for any $G$ as above.
:::

Throughout this section $A$ and $B$ are connected graded noetherian $k$-algebras.

::: dfn
[]{#a6.3 label="a6.3"} A *balanced dualizing complex* over $A$ is a graded dualizing complex $R$ such that $$\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} R \cong
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}^{\circ}} R \cong A^{*}$$ in $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$.
:::

A balanced dualizing complex $R$ is unique up to isomorphism in $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$, and its endomorphisms are just elements of $k$. Thus if we choose an isomorphism $\phi : \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} R \stackrel{\simeq}{\rightarrow}A^{*}$ in $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$, the pair $(R, \phi)$ is unique up to a unique isomorphism.

It had been known for some time (by [@Ye1]) that balanced dualizing complexes exist for Artin-Schelter Gorenstein algebras, twisted homogeneous coordinate algebras and algebras finite over their centers. Recently additional existence results became available, due to the work of Van den Bergh. First recall the following definition taken from [@AZ].

::: dfn
[]{#a6.4 label="a6.4"} The condition $\chi$ holds for a noetherian connected graded $k$-algebra $A$ if for every $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}}_{\mathrm{f}} A$ and integer $i$, $\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(k, M)$ is a finitely generated $k$-module.
:::

In view of [@AZ] Proposition 3.1(3), this definition is equivalent to [@AZ] Definition 3.2; and by [@AZ] Proposition 3.11(2) it is equivalent to [@AZ] Definition 3.7. The next lemma provides further characterization of the condition $\chi$. Recall that a graded module $M$ is said to be *right bounded* if $M_{n} = 0$ for $n \gg 0$.

::: lem
[]{#newlem4 label="newlem4"} Let $A$ be a noetherian connected graded $k$-algebra and $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}}_{\mathrm{f}} A$. Then the following are equivalent:

1.  $\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(k, M)$ is a finitely generated $k$-module for all $i$.

2.  $\mathrm{H}^{i} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M$ is right bounded for all $i$.

3.  $\mathrm{H}^{i} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M$ is an artinian $A$-module for all $i$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* (i) $\Leftrightarrow$ (ii) is by [@AZ] Corollary 3.6(3). (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) is immediate, since the socle of $\mathrm{H}^{i} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M$ is a finitely generated $k$-module, hence bounded. Finally assume (i), and let $I$ be a minimal graded-injective resolution of $M$. From [@Ye1] Lemma 4.3 it follows that $\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} I^{i} \cong A^{*} \otimes
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(k, M)$, which is artinian. Hence $\mathrm{H}^{i} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M$ is artinian. ◻
:::

In an earlier paper we proved the next theorem.

::: thm
[]{#ourthm label="ourthm"} Let $A$ be a noetherian connected graded $k$-algebra. If $A$ has a balanced dualizing complex then the condition $\chi$ holds for $A$ and $A^{\circ}$, and the functors $\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}^{\circ}}$ have finite cohomological dimensions.
:::

The converse, which is quite harder, was proved by Van den Bergh.

::: thm
[]{#a6.5 label="a6.5"} Let $A$ be a noetherian connected graded $k$-algebra. Assume the condition $\chi$ holds for $A$ and $A^{\circ}$, and the functors $\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}^{\circ}}$ have finite cohomological dimensions. Then $$R_{A} := (\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} A)^{*}$$ is a balanced dualizing complex.
:::

Let us summarize some other known results related to the balanced dualizing complexes.

::: thm
[]{#a6.6 label="a6.6"} Let $R$ be a balanced dualizing complex over a noetherian connected graded $k$-algebra $A$. Then for any graded $k$-algebra $B$ and any $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} (A \otimes B^{\circ}))$ there is a functorial isomorphism $$\mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{gr}}_{A}(M, R) \cong
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M)^{*} .$$
:::

This is proved by combining [@VdB] Theorems 5.1 and 6.3. The theorem was first proved in [@Ye1], but only for $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{f}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A)$.

::: prop
[]{#a6.25 label="a6.25"} A balanced dualizing complex $R$ is rigid in the graded sense.
:::

::: rem
[]{#a6.2 label="a6.2"} According to an exercise in [@VdB] (whose only proof we know is quite involved), if $I$ is a graded-injective $A$-module, then $I$ has injective dimension $\leq 1$ in $\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod}} A$. An immediate consequence of this fact is that a graded dualizing complex $R$ over $A$ is also an ungraded dualizing complex. The special case we need, namely that a balanced dualizing complex $R$ is rigid in the ungraded sense, is proved by other means in Corollary [\[a8.6\]](#a8.6){reference-type="ref" reference="a8.6"}.
:::

Here is another result from [@VdB]. Let us write $\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\mathrm{e}}}$ for the augmentation ideal of $A^{\mathrm{e}}$, so $\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\mathrm{e}}} = \mathfrak{m} \otimes A^{\circ} +
A \otimes \mathfrak{m}^{\circ}$.

::: thm
[]{#a6.7 label="a6.7"} Assume $A$ has a balanced dualizing complex $R$. Let $M \in \mathsf{D}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$ have finitely generated cohomology modules on both sides. Then there is a functorial isomorphism $$\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M \cong
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\mathrm{e}}}} M \cong
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}^{\circ}} M .$$
:::

We obtain the following interesting result:

::: cor
[]{#a6.20 label="a6.20"} Let $R$ be a balanced dualizing complex over $A$. Then there is a functorial isomorphism $$\mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M, R) \cong
\mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}_{A^{\circ}}(M, R)$$ for $M \in \mathsf{D}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$ with finitely generated cohomology modules on both sides.
:::

We shall write $\operatorname{Cdim}_{A}$ instead of $\operatorname{Cdim}_{R; A}$ when $R$ is the balanced dualizing complex, and when we are working in $\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A$. Since a balanced dualizing complex is rigid in the ungraded sense (by Corollary [\[a8.6\]](#a8.6){reference-type="ref" reference="a8.6"}), this is consistent with Definition [\[a4.11\]](#a4.11){reference-type="ref" reference="a4.11"}.

::: dfn
[]{#a6.21 label="a6.21"} If $A$ has a graded Auslander balanced dualizing complex $R$ we say $A$ is *graded Auslander*. Furthermore if $\operatorname{dim}$ is an exact dimension function on graded modules, and if $R$ is graded $\operatorname{dim}$-Macaulay, then we say $A$ is *graded Auslander $\operatorname{dim}$-Macaulay*.
:::

According to [@Ye1] Theorem 3.9, any two graded dualizing complexes $R_{1}, R_{2}$ satisfy $R_{2} \cong R_{1} \otimes_{A} A^{\sigma}(m)[n]$, for an automorphism $\sigma$ and an integers $n, m$. It follows that $R_{1}$ is graded Auslander iff $R_{2}$ is so. In particular, if $A$ is graded Gorenstein, then $A$ is graded Auslander-Gorenstein in the usual sense iff it is graded Auslander in the sense of Definition [\[a6.21\]](#a6.21){reference-type="ref" reference="a6.21"}.

Taking cohomologies in the previous corollary we get:

::: cor
[]{#a6.9 label="a6.9"} Suppose $A$ is graded Auslander. Then $\operatorname{Cdim}$ is symmetric on graded modules. That is to say, if $M$ is a graded $A$-bimodule, finitely generated on both sides, then $\operatorname{Cdim}_{A} M = \operatorname{Cdim}_{A^{\circ}} M.$
:::

If $A$ is graded Auslander we have a bound on Krull dimension of graded modules:

::: thm
[]{#a6.11 label="a6.11"} Suppose $A$ is graded Auslander. Then $$\operatorname{Kdim} M \leq \operatorname{Cdim} M=
\operatorname{sup} \{ q \mid \mathrm{H}^{q} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M \neq 0 \}
< \infty$$ for all finitely generated graded $A$-modules $M$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* By Theorem [\[a6.6\]](#a6.6){reference-type="ref" reference="a6.6"}, if $M \neq 0$, we have $\operatorname{Cdim} M =
\operatorname{sup} \{ q \mid \mathrm{H}^{q} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}} M
\neq 0 \}$. Next for a finitely generated graded $A$-module $M$ the Krull dimension is the same when computed in $\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A$ and in $\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod}} A$. And by the graded variant of Corollary [\[a2.13\]](#a2.13){reference-type="ref" reference="a2.13"} we get $\operatorname{Kdim} M \leq \operatorname{Cdim}_{R} M$, since clearly $d_{0} = 0$. ◻
:::

::: lem
[]{#a6.13 label="a6.13"} Let $A \rightarrow B$ be a finite homomorphism of noetherian connected graded $k$-algebras, with augmentation ideals $\mathfrak{m}_{A}, \mathfrak{m}_{B}$. Assume $A$ satisfies condition $\chi$. Then there is a functorial isomorphism $$\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}} M \cong
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} M$$ for $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} B)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* For any homomorphism $A \rightarrow B$ of graded algebras there is a functorial morphism $\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}} M \rightarrow
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} M$ in $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A)$. By [@AZ] Lemma 8.2, the extra assumptions guarantee that $\mathrm{H}^{p} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}} M \rightarrow
\mathrm{H}^{p} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} M$ is bijective for all $p$. ◻
:::

The following theorem is a generalization of [@Jo1] Theorem 3.3.

::: thm
[]{#a6.14 label="a6.14"} Let $A \rightarrow B$ be a finite homomorphism of graded $k$-algebras and let $R_{A}$ be a balanced dualizing complex over $A$. Then:

1.  $B$ has a balanced dualizing complex $R_{B}$.

2.  There is a morphism $\operatorname{Tr}_{B / A} : R_{B} \rightarrow R_{A}$ in $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$, which satisfies conditions [(i)]{.upright} and [(ii)]{.upright} of Theorem [[\[a4.2\]](#a4.2){reference-type="ref" reference="a4.2"}]{.upright}.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* From [@VdB] Theorem 6.3 we know that $A$ and $A^{\circ}$ satisfy $\chi$, and $\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}^{\circ}_{A}} =\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\circ}}}$ have finite cohomological dimensions. So by Lemma [\[a6.13\]](#a6.13){reference-type="ref" reference="a6.13"} the same is true for $B$. Thus $B$ has a balanced dualizing complex $R_{B} \cong (\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}} B)^{*}$.

The morphism $A \rightarrow B$ in $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$ induces a morphism $(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} B)^{*} \rightarrow
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} A)^{*}$, also in $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$. But $(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} B)^{*} \cong
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}} B)^{*}$ and we get $\operatorname{Tr}_{B / A} : R_{B} \rightarrow R_{A}$. The isomorphism of functors $\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} \cong
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{\circ}}$ of [@VdB] Corollary 4.8 shows that $\operatorname{Tr}_{B / A}$ is the same when calculated on the right, i.e., using $\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{\circ}}$.

Condition (i) of Theorem [\[a4.2\]](#a4.2){reference-type="ref" reference="a4.2"} is a consequence of local duality. To verify condition (ii) we again view $A \rightarrow B$ as a morphism in $\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} A^{\mathrm{e}})$. By [@VdB] Theorems 4.7 and 5.1 we get a commutative diagram $$\begin{CD}
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}} B)^{*} @> \cong >>
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B^{\mathrm{e}}}} B)^{*} @> \cong >>
\mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}_{B^{\mathrm{e}}}(B,
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B^{\mathrm{e}}}} B^{\mathrm{e}})^{*}) \\
@VVV @VVV @VVV \\
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} A)^{*} @> \cong >>
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\mathrm{e}}}} A)^{*} @> \cong >>
\mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}_{A^{\mathrm{e}}}(A,
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\mathrm{e}}}} A^{\mathrm{e}})^{*})
\end{CD}$$ Finally by [@VdB] Theorem 7.1 $$(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\mathrm{e}}}} A^{\mathrm{e}})^{*}
\cong (\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} A)^{*} \otimes
(\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{\circ}} A)^{*} ,$$ and of course the same for $B$. ◻
:::

Applying the graded variant of Proposition [\[a4.9\]](#a4.9){reference-type="ref" reference="a4.9"} we obtain the following corollary.

::: cor
[]{#a6.15 label="a6.15"} Let $A$ and $B$ be as in Theorem [[\[a6.14\]](#a6.14){reference-type="ref" reference="a6.14"}]{.upright}.

1.  There is a functorial isomorphism $$\mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{gr}}_{B}(M, R_{B}) \cong
    \mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{gr}}_{A}(M, R_{A})$$ for all $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} B)$.

2.  If $A$ is graded Auslander then so is $B$.

3.  $\operatorname{Cdim}_{A} M = \operatorname{Cdim}_{B} M$ for $M \in \operatorname{\mathsf{GrMod}} B$.

4.  If $A$ is graded Auslander $\operatorname{GKdim}$-Macaulay then so is $B$.

5.  Suppose $A \rightarrow B$ is surjective. If $A$ is graded Auslander $\operatorname{Kdim}$-Macaulay, then so is $B$.
:::

The next three propositions show that the graded Auslander property can be transferred from one algebra to a related algebra.

::: prop
[]{#a6.16 label="a6.16"} Suppose $A$ has a balanced dualizing complex. Let $\operatorname{dim}$ stand for either $\operatorname{Kdim}$ or $\operatorname{GKdim}$.

1.  Let $\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}$ be graded ideals. If the quotient algebras $A / \mathfrak{a}$ and $A / \mathfrak{b}$ are graded Auslander [(]{.upright}resp. and graded $\operatorname{dim}$-Macaulay[)]{.upright}, then so is $A / \mathfrak{a} \mathfrak{b}$.

2.  Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a nilpotent graded ideal of $A$. If $A / \mathfrak{a}$ is graded Auslander [(]{.upright}resp. and graded $\operatorname{dim}$-Macaulay[)]{.upright} then so is $A$.

3.  If for every minimal graded prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ the quotient algebra $A / \mathfrak{p}$ is graded Auslander [(]{.upright}resp. and graded $\operatorname{dim}$-Macaulay[)]{.upright}, then so is $A$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* 1. As usual we write $D := \mathrm{R} \operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{gr}}_{A}(- , R)$ where $R$ is the balanced dualizing complex. We may assume $\mathfrak{a} \mathfrak{b} = 0$. Given a finitely generated graded module $M$ consider the exact sequence $0 \rightarrow\mathfrak{b} M \rightarrow M \rightarrow M / \mathfrak{b} M \rightarrow 0$, and note that $\mathfrak{b} M$ is an $A / \mathfrak{a}$-module. For any $i$ there is an exact sequence $\mathrm{H}^{i} D (M / \mathfrak{b} M) \rightarrow
\mathrm{H}^{i} D M \rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{i} D (\mathfrak{b} M)$. Since $A / \mathfrak{a}$ and $A / \mathfrak{b}$ have the graded Auslander property, the subquotients of $\mathrm{H}^{i} D (M / \mathfrak{b} M)$ and $\mathrm{H}^{i} D (\mathfrak{b} M)$ have $\operatorname{Cdim}$ no more than $i$. Observe that here we are using Corollary [\[a6.15\]](#a6.15){reference-type="ref" reference="a6.15"}. Hence by the long exact sequence of duality, submodules of $\mathrm{H}^{i} D M$ have $\operatorname{Cdim}$ no more than $i$. The assertion about the Macaulay property is clear.\
2. Use part 1 and induction.\
3. Let $\mathfrak{p}_{1}, \ldots, \mathfrak{p}_{m}$ be the minimal prime ideals of $A$. Then $(\prod_{i} \mathfrak{p}_{i})^{n} = 0$ for some $n$, and we can use parts 1 and 2. ◻
:::

::: prop
[]{#a6.17 label="a6.17"} Let $A \rightarrow B$ be a finite homomorphism of connected graded algebras, and assume $B \cong A \oplus L$ as graded $A$-bimodules. If $B$ has a balanced dualizing complex then so does $A$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let $M$ be a finitely generated graded $A$-module. Then by Lemma [\[a6.13\]](#a6.13){reference-type="ref" reference="a6.13"} we get $$\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}} (B \otimes_{A} M) \cong
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} (B \otimes_{A} M) \cong
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} M \oplus
\mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} (L \otimes_{A} M) .$$ By Theorem [\[ourthm\]](#ourthm){reference-type="ref" reference="ourthm"} and Lemma [\[newlem4\]](#newlem4){reference-type="ref" reference="newlem4"} applied to $B$, we see that the graded $B$-module $\mathrm{H}^{i} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}} (B \otimes_{A} M)$ is right bounded, and vanishes for large $i$. Hence the same is true for the $A$-module $\mathrm{H}^{i} \mathrm{R} \Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{A}} M$. Now apply Theorem [\[a6.5\]](#a6.5){reference-type="ref" reference="a6.5"}. ◻
:::

We do not know if the under the assumptions of the proposition above the Auslander property can be transferred from $B$ to $A$. However as shown to us by Van den Bergh this is true in a special case:

::: prop
[]{#a6.18 label="a6.18"} Let $G$ be a finite group of order prime to $\operatorname{char} k$, acting on $B$ by graded $k$-algebra automorphisms, and let $A := B^{G}$ be the fixed ring. If $B$ is graded Auslander then so is $A$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Given a finitely generated graded $A$-module $M$ and a graded $A^{\circ}$-submodule $N \subset \operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{A}(M, R_{A})$ we want to prove that $\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A^{\circ}}(N, R_{A}) = 0$ for all $p < q$. Let $L := \{ b \in B \mid \sum_{g \in G} g(b) = 0 \}$, so $B = A \oplus L$. We have isomorphisms of graded $A^{\circ}$-modules $$\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{A}(M, R_{A}) \oplus
\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{A}(L \otimes_{A} M, R_{A}) \cong \\
\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{A}(B \otimes_{A} M, R_{A}) \cong
\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{B}(B \otimes_{A} M, R_{B})
\end{gathered}$$ that respect the $G$-action. Note that $G$ acts trivially on $\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{A}(M, R_{A})$. Consider the graded $B^{\circ}$-module $N \cdot B \subset \operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{B}(B \otimes_{A} M, R_{B})$. Clearly $N \cdot B = N + N \cdot L$. But if $$n = \sum_{i} n_{i} l_{i} \in N \cap (N \cdot L)$$ with $n_{i} \in N$ and $l_{i} \in L$, then $$n = |G|^{-1} \sum_{g \in G} g \left(\sum_{i} n_{i} l_{i} \right)
= \sum_{i} n_{i} \cdot |G|^{-1} \sum_{g \in G} g(l_{i}) = 0 .$$ We conclude that $N \cdot B = N \oplus N \cdot L$ as graded $A^{\circ}$-modules. Therefore $$\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A^{\circ}}(N, R_{A}) \oplus
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A^{\circ}}(N \cdot L, R_{A}) \cong \\
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A^{\circ}}(N \cdot B, R_{A}) \cong
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{B^{\circ}}(N \cdot B, R_{B}) = 0 .
\end{gathered}$$ ◻
:::
