TQFT in the Semiclassical Limit
In Chern-Simons theory, the coupling constant k (also called the level of Chern-Simons theory) appears as
a factor multiplying the Lagrangian, e.g. (79). Hence, it has a role similar to the inverse Planck constant.
In particular, the limit k →∞localizes the path integral on classical configurations, computing the
semiclassical wavefunction (83). It is instructive to learn what happens with the topological model in
this limit.

Braid generators (33) take the form of permutation generators. In other words, no knots can be formed

This has an important consequence for the type of states, for which there exists a topological presenta-
tion. For states like the one of equation (69), what really matters in the classical limit is the information
about which point is connected to which, while the mutual relations of different connections are unim-
portant. Many states that are different for finite k become indistinguishable in the classical limit – a sort
of a classical decoherence of the quantum state. The states split into a set of infinite equivalence classes
generated by relation (100). The situation can be compared with neuroscience, where one builds maps
of a brain, indicating which neuron is connected to which. Such maps are called connectomes. Here we
can call connectomes similar topological equivalence classes of quantum states.
