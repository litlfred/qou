Quantum Entanglement
In the previous section we described an approach in which some quantum states can be understood
as topological configurations (spaces).
In this section we will show how this approach can be useful
to understand and interpret fundamental concepts of quantum mechanics.
The main subject of the
discussion will be quantum entanglement.
The idea that entanglement can be thought as of topological tangling has been in the market for
a while. Intuitive, but not rigorous, diagrammatic depiction of systems entangled in different ways is
common in quantum information literature, cf. [33]. Proposal of Aravind [88], comparing features of the
topology of the Borromean rings (2) with the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state is often cited
in this relation. (Observe that removing any of the rings in the Borromean link makes the other two
disconnected, while measuring any of the qubits of the GHZ state in the computational basis, makes the
other two separable.) Some early mathematical approach to the subject, which can be heuristically called
“entanglement=topology”, is reviewed in [35]. More recently, a specific program to study different types
of entanglement using Chern-Simons theory states has been carried out in [59, 69–73, 75, 76, 79, 81–86]
developing upon the ideas explored in [89].
