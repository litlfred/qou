Thus, plugging equations ([\[eq:beta\]](#eq:beta){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:beta"}), ([\[eq:mu\]](#eq:mu){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:mu"}), ([\[eq:tau\]](#eq:tau){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:tau"}) into equation ([\[eq:gamma\]](#eq:gamma){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:gamma"}) gives $$\begin{aligned} (-1)^{{\bf p}} \gamma_{K_a} &=& \frac{ (s-\varepsilon_{{\bf q}}) (s-\varepsilon_{{\bf q}}+1)}{2} +\mu - \tau \notag \\ &=& \frac{s^2 + s - 2s \varepsilon_{{\bf q}}}{2} + s - \#\{i : p_i = 1 \} - \#\{i : q_i = 1 \} + \varepsilon_{{\bf q}}+ \delta_{s={\bf q}=2} - \delta_{{\bf q}= 3} \notag \\ &=& \frac{s^2 + 3s}{2} - (s-1)\varepsilon_{{\bf q}}+ \delta_{s={\bf q}=2} - \#\{i : p_i = 1 \} - \#\{i : q_i = 1 \} - \delta_{{\bf q}= 3} \notag \\ &=& \frac{s^2 + 3s}{2} - \#\{i : p_i = 1 \} - \#\{i : q_i = 1 \} - \delta_{{\bf q}= 3}. \label{eq:gamma-exact} \end{aligned}$$ The last equality holds because both $(s-1)\varepsilon_{{\bf q}}$ and $\delta_{s={\bf q}=2}$ must be $0$ for $s \neq 2$, while on the other hand $\delta_{s={\bf q}=2} = \varepsilon_{{\bf q}}$ for $s = 2$.
