[]{#sect:combconst label="sect:combconst"}

Following [@ramcalib], the *calibration graph* of $t\in\mathrm{Hom}(L,\mathbb{C}^{\times})$ is the graph $\Gamma(t)$ with $$\begin{aligned}
&\textrm{vertex set $\{wt\mid w\in W_0\}$, and}\\
&\textrm{edges $\{wt,s_iwt\}$ if and only if $\alpha_i\notin N(wt)$.}
\end{aligned}$$ For each $J\subseteq N(t)$ define $$F_J(t)=\{w\in W_0\mid R(w^{-1})\cap N(t)=J\textrm{ and }R(w^{-1})\cap D(t)=\emptyset\}.$$ By the argument in [@ramcalib Theorem 2.14], if $W_t=W_{(t)}$ then the connected components of $\Gamma(t)$ are precisely the sets $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:concal}
\{wt\mid w\in F_J(t)\}\qquad\textrm{such that $J\subseteq N(t)$ and $F_J(t)\neq \emptyset$}.
\end{aligned}$$

::: remark
[]{#rem:calib label="rem:calib"} We note that if $W_t=W_{(t)}$ then the geometric argument in [@ramcalib Theorem 2.14] also shows that if $w,v\in F_J(t)$, and if $wv^{-1}=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}$ is a reduced expression, then each element $$w,\,\,s_{i_1}w,\,\,s_{i_2}s_{i_1}w,\,\,\ldots,\,\,s_{i_{\ell}}\cdots s_{i_2}s_{i_1}w=v\quad\textrm{is in $F_J(t)$}.$$ (Because the "smaller regions" in the proof of [@ramcalib Theorem 2.14] which correspond to the connected components of the calibration graph are convex in the sense that they are intersections of half spaces, and hence by [@ronan Proposition 2.8] all minimal length paths between $w$ and $v$ are contained in this region). Thus $F_J(t)$ is 'geodesically closed' in the (dual of the) underlying Coxeter complex.
:::

::: prop
[]{#prop:calib label="prop:calib"} If $M$ is a finite dimensional $\mathscr{H}_L$-module then $$\mathrm{dim}(M_t^{\mathrm{gen}})=\mathrm{dim}(M_{t'}^{\mathrm{gen}})$$ whenever $t$ and $t'$ are in the same connected component of the calibration graph $\Gamma(t)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* If $\alpha_i\notin N(t)$ then Lemma [\[lem:bijection\]](#lem:bijection){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:bijection"} gives $\mathrm{dim}(M_t^{\mathrm{gen}})=\mathrm{dim}(M_{s_it}^{\mathrm{gen}})$. Hence the result. ◻
:::

Let $R_{ij}$ be the rank $2$ subsystem of $R$ generated by the simple roots $\alpha_i$ and $\alpha_j$. That is, $R_{ij}$ is the intersection of $R$ with the $\mathbb{Z}$-span of $\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}$. We say that a weight $t\in\mathrm{Hom}(L,\mathbb{C}^{\times})$ is *$(i,j)$-regular* if $(wt)^{\alpha_i^{\vee}}\neq 1$ and $(wt)^{\alpha_j^{\vee}}\neq 1$ for all $w\in W_{ij}=\langle s_i,s_j\rangle$, and $(i,j)$-*calibratable* if one of the following conditions holds:

1.  The weight $t$ is $(i,j)$-regular.

2.  $R_{ij}$ is of type $C_2$ (assume $\alpha_i$ short and $\alpha_j$ long) with

    1.  $q_i=q_j$ and $(t^{\alpha_i^{\vee}},t^{\alpha_j^{\vee}})=(q_i,q_i^{-1})$ or $(q_i^{-1},q_i)$, or

    2.  $q_i=q_j^2$ and $(t^{\alpha_i^{\vee}},t^{\alpha_j^{\vee}})=(q_j^{-2},q_j)$ or $(q_j^2,q_j)$.

3.  $R_{ij}$ is of type $G_2$ (assume $\alpha_i$ short and $\alpha_j$ long) with

    1.  $q_i=q_j$ and $(t^{\alpha_i^{\vee}},t^{\alpha_2^{\vee}})=(q_i^{-1},q_i),(q_i,q_i^{-1}),(q_i^2,q_i^{-1}),(q_i^{-2},q_i)$, or

    2.  $q_i=q_j^3$ and $(t^{\alpha_i^{\vee}},t^{\alpha_j^{\vee}})=(q_j^3,q_j^{-1}),(q_j^{-3},q_j),(q_j^{-3},q_j^2),(q_j^{3},q_j^{-2})$.

4.  $R_{ij}$ is of type $BC_2$ (assume $\alpha_i$ middle-length and $\alpha_j$ short) with

    1.  $q_i=q_0q_j$ and $(t^{\alpha_i^{\vee}},t^{\alpha_j^{\vee}/2})=(q_0q_j,q_0^{-1/2}q_j^{-1/2}),(q_0^{-1}q_j^{-1},q_0^{1/2}q_j^{1/2})$, or

    2.  $q_i=q_0q_j^{-1}$ or $q_0^{-1}q_j$ and $(t^{\alpha_i^{\vee}},t^{\alpha_j^{\vee}/2})=(q_0^{-1}q_j,-q_0^{1/2}q_j^{-1/2}),(q_0q_j^{-1},-q_0^{-1/2}q_j^{1/2})$, or

    3.  $q_i=q_0^{1/2}q_j^{1/2}$ and $(t^{\alpha_i^{\vee}},t^{\alpha_j^{\vee}/2})=(q_i^{-1},q_i),(q_i,q_i^{-1})$, or

    4.  $q_i=q_0^{-1/2}q_j^{1/2}$ or $q_0^{1/2}q_j^{-1/2}$ and $(t^{\alpha_i^{\vee}},t^{\alpha_j^{\vee}/2})=(q_i^{-1},-q_i),(q_i,-q_i^{-1})$.

Conditions (1), (2)(a) and (3)(a) are equivalent to Ram's definition of calibratable in the $1$-parameter case. Note that if $R_{ij}$ is of type $BC_2$ then the underlying root system $R$ is necessarily non-reduced, and hence is of type $BC_n$, and so $1\leq i\leq n-1$ and $j=n$ (since $\alpha_j$ is assumed to be short).

In the following theorem we construct a class of irreducible calibrated $\mathscr{H}_L$-modules.

::: thm
[]{#thm:repmain label="thm:repmain"} Let $t\in\mathrm{Hom}(L,\mathbb{C}^{\times})$, and let $J\subseteq N(t)$. Suppose that $F_J(t)\neq\emptyset$ and that each $wt$ with $w\in F_J(t)$ is $(i,j)$-calibratable for each pair $(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$ of simple roots of $R$. Let $M_J(t)$ be the vector space over $\mathbb{C}$ with basis $\{e_{wt}\mid w\in F_J(t)\}$, and define linear operators $\tilde{T}_i$ ($i=1,\ldots,n$), $\tilde{x}^{\lambda}$ ($\lambda\in L$), on $M_J(t)$ by linearly extending the formulae $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:diagonal}\tilde{x}^{\lambda}e_{wt}&=(wt)^{\lambda}e_{wt}&&\lambda\in L\\
\label{eq:Taction}\tilde{T}_ie_{wt}&=q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_{i}'(wt)e_{wt}+q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_{i}(wt)e_{s_iwt}&&1\leq i\leq n,
\end{aligned}$$ with the convention that $e_{vt}=0$ if $v\notin F_J(t)$. Then the map $\mathscr{H}_L\to \mathrm{End}(M_J(t))$ with $T_i\mapsto \tilde{T}_i$ and $x^{\lambda}\mapsto\tilde{x}^{\lambda}$ defines an irreducible calibrated representation of $\mathscr{H}_L$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* (a) We check that the operators $\tilde{T}_i$ and $\tilde{x}^{\lambda}$ satisfy the Bernstein relation. We have $$\begin{aligned}
 (\tilde{T}_i\tilde{x}^{\lambda}-\tilde{x}^{s_i\lambda}\tilde{T}_i) e_{wt}&=((wt)^{\lambda}-\tilde{x}^{s_i\lambda})\tilde{T}_i e_{wt}=((wt)^{\lambda}-x^{s_i\lambda})\big(q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i'(wt)e_{wt}+q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i(wt)e_{s_iwt}\big)\\
 &=((wt)^{\lambda}-(wt)^{s_i\lambda})q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i'(wt)e_{wt}=q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i'(\tilde{x})(\tilde{x}^{\lambda}-\tilde{x}^{s_i\lambda})e_{wt}.
 
\end{aligned}$$

\(b\) We now check that the operators $\tilde{T}_i$ satisfy the quadratic relation $\tilde{T}_i^2=1+(q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}-q_i^{-\frac{1}{2}})\tilde{T}_i$. $$\begin{aligned}
\tilde{T}_i^2 e_{wt}&=\tilde{T}_i \big(q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i'(wt)e_{wt}+q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i(wt)e_{s_iwt}\big)\\
&=q_i\left(c_i'(wt)^2+c_i(wt)c_i(s_iwt)\right)e_{wt}+q_ic_i(wt)\left(c_i'(wt)+c_i'(s_iwt)\right)e_{s_iwt}\\
&=\big(1+(q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}-q_i^{-\frac{1}{2}})q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i'(wt)\big)e_{wt}+(q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}-q_i^{-\frac{1}{2}})q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i(wt)e_{s_iwt}=(1+(q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}-q_i^{-\frac{1}{2}})\tilde{T}_i) e_{wt}.
\end{aligned}$$

\(c\) We verify the braid relation $\cdots \tilde{T}_i\tilde{T}_j\tilde{T}_i=\cdots \tilde{T}_j\tilde{T}_i\tilde{T}_j$ ($m_{ij}$ factors). Fix $w\in F_J(t)$. Suppose first that $wt$ is $(i,j)$-regular. Let $v\in W_{ij}$. If $e_{vwt}\neq 0$ then ([\[eq:Taction\]](#eq:Taction){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:Taction"}) gives $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:ind}
\big(\tilde{T}_i-q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i'(vwt)\big) e_{vwt}=q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i(vwt)e_{s_ivwt},
\end{aligned}$$ and by Remark [\[rem:calib\]](#rem:calib){reference-type="ref" reference="rem:calib"} this formula is also true when $e_{vwt}=0$ and $\ell(s_iv)>\ell(v)$.

Consider the product (well defined by $(i,j)$-regularity) $$\begin{aligned}
A_{ij}(wt)&=\cdots(\tilde{T}_i-q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i'(s_js_iwt))(\tilde{T}_j-q_j^{\frac{1}{2}}c_j'(s_iwt))(\tilde{T}_i-q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}c_i'(wt))&&\textrm{($m_{ij}$ factors)}.
\end{aligned}$$ Let $v_0$ be the longest element of $W_{ij}$. Repeatedly using ([\[eq:ind\]](#eq:ind){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:ind"}) and $c_{\alpha}(vwt)=c_{v^{-1}\alpha}(wt)$ gives $$\begin{aligned}
A_{ij}(wt) e_{wt}&=q_{v_0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[c_{\alpha_i}(wt)c_{s_i\alpha_j}(wt)c_{s_is_j\alpha_i}(wt)c_{s_is_js_i\alpha_j}(wt)\cdots \big]e_{v_0wt}.
\end{aligned}$$ By ([\[eq:inversionset\]](#eq:inversionset){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:inversionset"}) we have $\{\alpha_i,s_i\alpha_j,s_is_j\alpha_i,\ldots\}=\{\alpha_j,s_j\alpha_i,s_js_i\alpha_j,\ldots\}$ and so $A_{ji}(wt) e_{wt}=A_{ij}(wt) e_{wt}.$ Each $v\in W_{ij}\backslash\{v_0\}$ has a unique expression as a product of simple generators, and so for $v<v_0$ we may unambiguously define operators $\tilde{T}_v=\tilde{T}_{i_1}\tilde{T}_{i_2}\cdots\tilde{T}_{i_{\ell}}$ where $v=s_{i_1}s_{i_2}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}$ is the unique reduced expression for $v\in W_{ij}$. Expanding $A_{ij}(wt)$ and $A_{ji}(wt)$ and using the already verified quadratic relation for $\tilde{T}_i$ and $\tilde{T}_j$ we see that there are rational functions $p_v(wt)$ and $q_v(wt)$ in $wt$ such that $$\begin{aligned}
A_{ij}(wt)e_{wt}&=\cdots \tilde{T}_i\tilde{T}_j\tilde{T}_i e_{wt}+\sum_{v<v_0}p_v(wt)\tilde{T}_v e_{wt}\\
A_{ji}(wt)e_{wt}&=\cdots \tilde{T}_j\tilde{T}_i\tilde{T}_j e_{wt}+\sum_{v<v_0}q_v(wt)\tilde{T}_v e_{wt},
\end{aligned}$$ One now shows that $p_v(wt)=q_v(wt)$ for all $v<v_0$. This is achieved exactly as in [@ramcalib Proposition 2.7] by using the action of the $\tau$-operators on principal series representations, and we omit the details. Thus the braid relation, in the $(i,j)$-regular case, holds.

We now verify the braid relation in the case where $wt$ is $(i,j)$-calibratable but not $(i,j)$-regular. Consider the $R_{ij}=C_2$ case with $q_i=q_j$ and $(wt)^{\alpha_i^{\vee}}=q_i$ and $(wt)^{\alpha_j^{\vee}}=q_i^{-1}$. By ([\[eq:concal\]](#eq:concal){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:concal"}) we have $F_J(t)=\{wt\}$, and so the braid relation is trivially satisfied (as $M_J(t)$ is $1$-dimensional). All other $C_2$ cases are similar. In the $G_2$ case with $q_i=q_j^3$ and $(wt)^{\alpha_i^{\vee}}=q_j^3$ and $(wt)^{\alpha_j^{\vee}}=q_j^{-2}$, by ([\[eq:concal\]](#eq:concal){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:concal"}) we compute $F_J(t)=\{w,s_jw\}$, and a direct calculation gives $$\begin{aligned}
\tilde{T}_ie_{wt}&=q_i^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{wt}&\tilde{T}_je_{wt}&=\frac{1}{q_j+1}\left(-q_j^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{wt}+q_j^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{s_jwt}\right)\\
\tilde{T}_ie_{s_jwt}&=-q_i^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{s_jwt}&\tilde{T}_je_{s_jwt}&=q_j^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1-q_j^{-3}}{1-q_j^{-2}}e_{wt}+\frac{1}{1+q_j^{-1}}e_{s_jwt}\right)
\end{aligned}$$ The braid relation follows by direct calculation. The remaining $G_2$ cases are similar (or trivial). Finally, in all $BC_2$ cases we have $F_J(t)=\{wt\}$ and so the braid relation is trivially satisfied.

To conclude the proof we show that the module $M_J(t)$ is irreducible and calibrated. By the construction the generalised weight spaces of $M_J(t)$ are $M_J(t)_{wt}$, with $w\in F_J(t)$, and each generalised weight space has dimension $1$. So $M_J(t)$ is calibrated. Furthermore, it follows that if $M$ is a proper submodule of $M_J(t)$ then there is $w,w'\in F_J(t)$ with $wt\neq w't$ such that $M_{wt}\neq 0$ and $M_{w't}=0$, contradicting Proposition [\[prop:calib\]](#prop:calib){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:calib"}. Thus $M_J(t)$ is irreducible. ◻
:::

We note the following subtle point: In Theorem [\[thm:repmain\]](#thm:repmain){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:repmain"} the basis of $M_J(t)$ is indexed by the set $\{wt\mid w\in F_J(t)\}$, while in the construction [@ram5 Theorem 3.5] the basis is indexed by $F_J(t)$. The reason for this refinement is that we work with general lattices $Q\subseteq L\subseteq P$, while in [@ramcalib; @ram5] the lattice $L=P$ is specified. See Examples [\[ex:1\]](#ex:1){reference-type="ref" reference="ex:1"} and [\[ex:2\]](#ex:2){reference-type="ref" reference="ex:2"} below.

::: remark
Recently [@davis] Ram's construction has been applied to study the representation theory of $1$-parameter rank $2$ affine Hecke algebras with $q$ a root of the Poincaré polynomial, and analogously the above construction could be applied to the study of such representations in the multi-parameter case.
:::
