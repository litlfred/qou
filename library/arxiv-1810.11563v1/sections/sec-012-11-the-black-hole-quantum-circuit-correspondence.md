Complexity is mostly studied in computer science departments. The language is that of
bits, qubits, and logical gates, not Hamiltonians and quantum ﬁelds. At the present time
there is no completely consistent framework in which a black hole can be represented as
a ﬁnite collection of qubits, the kind of system for which complexity is a reasonably well
formulated concept. I will not try to ﬁll the gap in this lecture. What we will do is
to to exhibit a pattern of similarities between black hole phenomena and corresponding
phenomena that occur in k-local all-to-all quantum circuits. The correspondences include:
• Black hole of entropy S —— Quantum circuit with K∼S qubits.
• k-local gates —— k-local Hamiltonians
• Clock time τ —— Rindler time τ
It is possible that all quantum systems can be described as systems of qubits, but often the
description would involve many more qubits than are necessary to represent the phenomena
at hand. A good rule of thumb is that in order to describe a system with entropy S it
is suﬃcient to use a number of qubits of order S. In that case the qubit system should
be studied at temperatures high enough that every degree of freedom carries of order one
unit of entropy.
To keep the discussion simple and concrete I will generally set k = 2 but things are
easily generalized to arbitrary k as long as k <<K.
