Part V Special Topics
This part oﬀers a sampling of more advanced topics, ranging over cell theory and Hecke algebra repre-
sentaions (Chapter 22) and their connections to knot theory (Chapter 21), categorical diagonalization
(Chapter 23), diagrammatics for individual Soergel bimodules (Chapter 24), Koszul duality for category
O, reinterpreted using Soergel bimodules (Chapters 25 and 26), and the situation in positive characteristic
(Chapter 27).
Chapter 21 Connections to Link Invariants
This chapter relates the quantum group of gln and the Hecke algebra of the symmetric group Sm through
a quantum version of Schur-Weyl duality, showing how both can be used to systematically generate link
invariants. In the case of the quantum group, braided tensor category concepts lead to the construction of
the Reshetikhin-Turaev quantum invariants, which include the Jones polynomial as a special case. Hecke
algebras are employed together with Markov traces to obtain the celebrated HOMFLYPT polynomial in
two variables, which can be specialized to give the quantum invariants as speical cases. The chapter ﬁnally
describes how the HOMFLYPT polynomial is categoriﬁed using Rouquier complexes and Khovanov’s
triply-graded link homology.
Chapter 22 Cells and Representations of the Heck Algebra in Type A
This chapter describes the notion of cells in a Krull-Schmidt monoidal category. Applied to the category of
Soergel bimodules in characteristic 0, Soergel’s conjecture implies that this produces the Kazhdan-Lusztig
cells in the Hecke algebra. The rest of the chapter focuses on type A, recalling the Robinson-Schensted
correspondence and the description of the two-sided cells, which leads to a more explicit description of
the k-row quotient of the Hecke algebra in quantum Schur-Weyl duality. The chapter ﬁnally discusses the
relation between cells and representations of the Hecke algebra in type A.
Chapter 23 Categorical Diagonalization
In classical linear algebra, given a diagonalizable on a vector space, Lagrange interpolation produces an
idempotent decomposition of the identity corresponding to the projections to eigenspaces. This chap-
ter explains a categorical analogue of this procedure, due to B. Elias and M. Hogancamp [“Categori-
cal diagonalization”, Preprint, arXiv:1707.04349; “Categorical diagonalization of full twists”, Preprint,
arXiv:1801.00191]. The main application is to the full twist Rouquier complex acting on the homotopy
category of Soergel bimodules.

Germany, in March 2015. Zürich: European Mathematical Society (EMS). 171–186 (2017; Zbl 1369.19003);
B. Elias and G. Williamson, “Localized calculus for the Hecke category”, Preprint, arXiv:2011.05432],
ithe montroducing diagrammatic calculus which eﬃciently encodes the morphisms between singular Bott-
Samelson imodules as planar diagrams. It is ﬁnally shown that, just as the Frobenius structure R′ ⊂R
controls the diagrammatics for induction and restriction bimodules, the Frobenius extension structures
for the extension RJ ⊂RI (when I ⊂J) control the diagrammatics for all of SBSBim.
Chapter 25 Koszul Duality I
In 1979, I. N. Bernstein et al. [Funct. Anal. Appl. 12, 212–214 (1979; Zbl 0424.14002)] described a
duality between certain parts of the derived categories of the symmetric and exterior algebra. This type
of duality, called Koszul duality, has since been witnessed in various contexts. After introducing the
necessary homological algebra, this chapter formulates the phenomenon as a diﬀerential graded version
of Morita theory, presenting Koszul duality for a polynomial ring in one variable. Some evidence of such
a duality in the principal block of category O is then presented.
Chapter 26 Koszul Duality II
This chapter formulates a Koszul duality for the principal block of the BGG category O in the language
of Soergel bimodules, giving some indication of the proof.
