Part III Historical Context: Category O and the Kazhdan-Lusztig Conjectures
This part places Soergel bimodules in context, describing the connections between them and the other
incarnations of the Hecke category.
Chapter 13 Category O and the Kazhdan-Lusztig Conjectures
This chapter gives a historical introduction to the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjectures [D. Kazhdan and G.
Lusztig, Invent. Math. 53, 165–184 (1979; Zbl 0499.20035)], beginning with Verma’s thesis [D. N. Verma,
Bull. Am. Math. Soc. 74, 160–166 (1968; Zbl 0157.07604)] and introducing the problems in category
O these conjectures are meant to solve. After meandering through connections with the ﬂag variety, the
chapter considers Soergel’s proof [W. Soergel, J. Am. Math. Soc. 3, No. 2, 421–445 (1990; Zbl 0747.17008)]
of these conjectures, where SoergelSoergel bimodules played their ﬁrst major role.
Chapter 14 Lightning Introduction to Category O
This chapter reviews the structure of the category O [I. N. Bernstein et al., Funct. Anal. Appl. 12,
212–214 (1979; Zbl 0424.14002)], starting with some basic results about complex semisimple Lie algebras
and then deﬁning the category of modules in question. Some fundamental structural results about this
category, such as the decomposition of objects into ﬁnite-dimensional weight spaces, the existence of
Verma modules and the parametrization of simple modules, are established. A notion of duality in O is
deﬁned, and the BGG reciprocity theorem is stated. The chapter introduces central characters and blocks
of category O, ending with a careful examination of the example g = sl3 (C).
Chapter 15 Soergel’s V Functor and the Kazhdan-Lusztig Conjecture
This chapter recalls basic facts about category O, introducing the translation functors between its blocks.
The chapter then explains Soergel’s approach to the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture via his V functor relating
the principal block O0 to Soergel (bi)modules.
Chapter 16 Lightning Introduction to Perverse Sheaves
This chapter gives an introduction to perverse sheaves. By focusing on the table of stalks of a perverse
sheaf, one can study these objects concretely. The chapter uses the theory of perverse sheaves to explain
why the two diﬀerent descriptions of indecomposable Soergel bimodules should agree, at least for Weyl
groups.
Part IV The Hodge Theory of Soergel Bimodules
This part concerns the Hodge theory of Soergel bimodules, as developed in [B. Elias and G. Williamson,
Ann. Math. (2) 180, No. 3, 1089–1136 (2014; Zbl 1326.20005)].
Chapter 17 Hodge Theory and Lefschetz Linear Algebra
The cohomology rings of smooth complex projective algebraic varieties abide by a package of such proper-
ties as Poincaré duality, weak Lefschetz, hard Lefschetz and the Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations, which
impose conditions on the structure of the cohomology ring, giving rise to interesting linear operators and
bilinear forms on the ring. This chapter discusses these properties genuinely in terms of linear algebra,
so that some of the techniques and intuition from geometry can be brought to bear on more general
problems.
Chapter 18 The Hodge Theory of Soergel Bimodules
This chapter is an introduction to Elias and Williamson’s Hodge-theoretic proof of Soergel’s conjecture
for arbitrary Coxeter systems [B. Elias and G. Williamson, Ann. Math. (2) 180, No. 3, 1089–1136 (2014;
Zbl 1326.20005)], covering its §§1–5 while leaving §6 to Chapters 19–20.
Chapter 19 Rouquier Complexes and Homological Algebra
In the previous chapter, an outline of the proof of Hodge theory for Soergel bimodules was given, where
a key issue was the absence of the weak Lefschetz theorem. In the proof of Elias and Williamson, a
substitute for this theorem is provided by the homological algebra of Rouquier complexes categorifying
the braid group. This chapter brieﬂy describes the underlying idea, reviewing some necessary bits of
homological algebra and introducing Rouquier complexes formally.
Chapter 20 Proof of the Hard Lefschetz Theorem
This chapter completes the induction outlined in Chapter 18, thereby showing that the characters of the
indecomposable Soergel bimodules are given by the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.
