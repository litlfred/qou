This chapter gives a ﬁrst introduction to the theory of Coxeter groups.
Chapter 2 Reﬂection Groups and Coxeter Groups
This chapter explains why reﬂection groups are Coxeter groups, motivating the general deﬁnition of
Coxeter groups.
Chapter 3 The Hecke Algebra and Kazhdan-Lusztig Polynomials
The Hecke algebra associated to a Coxeter system (W, S) is a Z[v, v−1]-algebra that recovers the group
algebra Z [W] under the specialization v 7→1. It has two important bases, namely, the standard basis
and a certain self-dual basis called the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis, with the change of basis between the two
given by the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials. This chapter brieﬂy reviews the deﬁnition and construction
of the Hecke algebra and its Kazhdan-Lusztig basis.
Chapter 4 Soergel Bimodules
Soergel bimodules provide an algebraic categoriﬁcation of the Hecke algebra. This chapter provides a ﬁrst
introduction to these bimodules, considering the symmetric algebra on the geometric representation of the
Coxeter group and stating the Chevalley theorem concerning its invariant subrings. Demazure operators
are then discussed. The chapter ﬁnally introduce Bott-Samelson bimodules and Soergel bimodules, stating
Soergel’s categoriﬁcation theorem.
Chapter 5 The “Classical” Theory of Soergel Bimodules
This chapter deﬁnes standard bimodules and standard ﬁltrations of Soergel bimodules, exploring the
technique of localization.

Part II Diagrammatic Hecke Category
This part covers the diagrammatic approach to Soergel bimodules, giving the diagrammatic proof of the
fact that Soergel bimodules categorify the Hecke algebra.
Chapter 7 How to Draw Monoidal Categories
This chapter takes a short break from Soergel bimodules to learn about diagrammatics, illustrating the
method of string diagrams for drawing morphisms in 2-categories and contrasting it with the way that
they are typically drawn. With these diagrams in hand, the Tempeley-Lieb category is deﬁned.
Chapter 8 Frobenius Extensions and the One-Color Calculus
This chapter uses the planar diagrammatics of the previous chapter to describe a Frobenius algebra object
in a monoidal category. It is then explained how the graded Frobenius extension R′ ⊂R gives a Frobenius
algebra object structure to the generating Bott-Samelson bimodule Bs.
Chapter 9 The Dihedral Cathedral
This chapter introduces the diagrammatic category HBS for the inﬁnite and ﬁnite dihedral groups.
Chapter 10 Generators and Relations for Bott-Samelson Bimodules and the Double Leaves Basis
This chapter describes the category HBS for an arbitrary Coxeter system, discussing the new details
and giving a diﬀerent set of motivations. It is also brieﬂy discussed what happens for other realizations,
where the diagrammatic category may behave better than the algebraic category BSBim of Bott-Samelson
bimodules. Using morphisms called light leaves, Libedinsky
constructed a right R-basis for the Hom-
spaces of BSBim, called the double leaves basis, which is described diagrammatically.
Chapter 11 The Soergel Categoriﬁcation Theorem
From the diagrammatic Bott-Samelson category HBS deﬁned in Chapter 10 one can obtain the diagram-
matic Hecke category H by a formal analogue of the process that turned BSBim into SBim, the Karoubi
envelope. This chapter includes a comprehensive discussion of Karoubi envelopes and the Krull-Schmidt
property.
