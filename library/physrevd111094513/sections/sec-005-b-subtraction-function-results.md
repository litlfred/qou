We perform our calculation of the proton subtraction
function on three gauge ensembles generated by the
QCDSF Collaboration [41] using a nonperturbative,
OðaÞ-improved clover fermion action with unimproved
current operators [42]. All ensembles have 2 þ 1 quark
flavors at the SU(3) flavor symmetric point, and have
an unphysical pion mass—approximately 3 times the
physical pion mass. In expectation of the presence of
large discretization artifacts, we utilize three ensembles

fine
values
of
the

For each gauge ensemble, we determine the subtraction
function for a set of Q2 values given in Table II, which are
restricted by our Feynman-Hellmann procedure to q4 ¼ 0.
For ease in isolating the subtraction function, we have also
introduced the kinematic constraint q3 ¼ 0. The overall
range of kinematics is 1 ≲Q2 ≲16 GeV2, which pushes
below the threshold of the perturbative region, and up to the
region where we expect the only significant contributions to
be from the OPE, Eq. (7). To determine the subtraction
function we extract the energy shifts for each 3-momentum
q of the external current, following the methodology given
in Sec. III for two values of the external coupling
λ ∈½0.0125; 0.0250 as has been done in the same way
in
previous
Feynman-Hellmann
calculations
of
the
Compton amplitude [29,30]. A single plateau fit is per-
formed to each ratio given in Eq. (14) with the plateau
regions chosen following a covariance-matrix based χ2
analysis. This allows us to perform simple quadratic fits in λ
to determine the subtraction functions following Eqs. (11)
and (15).

Although the lattice spacing varies by more than
20% across the three sets of gauge configurations
(a ¼ 0.074; 0.068; 0.058 fm), the trend of our values
becoming smaller as the lattice spacing becomes finer is
rather subtle, and difficult to discern. Such a result naturally
suggests the presence of discretization artifacts which
converge somewhat slowly.
