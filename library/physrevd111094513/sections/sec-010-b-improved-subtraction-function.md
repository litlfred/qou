Finally, we determine the improved subtraction function
by removing the OðaÞ discretization artifacts calculated in
Sec. IV using Eq. (18). Combining the direct lattice
subtraction function, Fig. 3, with the leading discretization
artifacts, Fig. 6, we present our final results for the up (uu)
and down (dd) contributions in Fig. 7, and for the proton in
Fig. 8. Included in both plots (dotted curve) is the
continuum OPE calculated from Eq. (7) using gS and
hxi from Table I. The results presented in Fig. 8 are listed in
Table III of Appendix B.
We immediately observe an improvement in the Fig. 7
results as compared to Fig. 3, with the asymptotic Q−2
behavior restored for large momentum. Putting the results
together to determine the subtraction function of the proton,
Fig. 8, we observe a similar phenomenon. It appears as
though S1ðQ2Þ follows rather simple Q−2 behavior down to
Q2 ≈1 GeV2. Given the large size of the leading discre-
tization artifacts in Fig. 6, removing these artifacts from
Fig. 3 is sufficient to shift the points upwards into the
positive domain. This upwards shift brings the results into
remarkable
agreement
with
the
continuum
OPE
in
Ref. [21]. Furthermore, the three sets of results at different
lattice spacings are very consistent with each other.
Over the intermediate Q2 values in Fig. 8, the proton
subtraction function is rather well behaved, and a simple
Q−2 curve appears sufficient to capture the behavior of the
function.
As a consequence of the different subtraction point used
in Ref. [24], we do not provide a direct comparison with the
two results here, but we intend to pursue such a comparison
in future work.
