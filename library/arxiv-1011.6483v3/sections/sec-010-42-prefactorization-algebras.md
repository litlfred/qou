[]{#S:Factorization label="S:Factorization"}

We now explain a relationship between factorization algebras (as defined by Costello and Gwilliam [@CG; @Co]) and Higher Hochschild chains.

Let $A$ be a CDGA and $X$ be a topological space. We denote $Op(X)$ the set of open subsets of $X$. For every open subset $V$ of $X$ and a family of disjoint open subsets $U_1,\dots, U_n \subset V$, there is a canonical morphism of CDGAs $$\mu_{U_1,\dots, U_n,V}: CH_{U_1}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \otimes \cdots \otimes CH_{U_n}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \to \big(CH_{V}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)\big)^{\otimes n} \to CH_{V}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$$ induced by functoriality by the inclusions $U_i \hookrightarrow V$ and the multiplication in $CH_{V}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$.

These maps are the structure maps of a prefactorization algebra on $X$ in the sense of [@CG; @Co]. Note that for a (possibly $(\infty,1)$-) symmetric monoidal category $(\mathcal{C},\otimes)$, we denote by $\mathop{PFac}_{X}(\mathcal{C})$ the ($(\infty,1)$-) category of **prefactorization algebras on $X$ taking values in $\mathcal{C}$** (see [@CG]). In particular, $\mathop{PFac}_{X}(CDGA)$ is the category of *commutative* prefactorization algebras on $X$ as defined in [@CG]. We will actually be only interested in the case where $\mathcal{C}$ is an ($(\infty,1)$-)category of algebras over an ($\infty$)-Hopf operad. We have:

::: lemma
[]{#P:prefactorization label="P:prefactorization"} The rule $U\mapsto CH_U^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ together with the maps $\mu_{U_1,\dots, U_n,V}$ define a natural structure of a *prefactorization algebra* on $X$. Further

1.  The above rule $A\mapsto \Big( \big(CH_{(U)}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)\big)_{ U\in Op(X)}; \, \big(\mu_{U_1,\dots,U_n,V}\big)\Big)$ defines a functor $\mathcal{CH}_X: CDGA\to \mathop{PFac}_{X}(CDGA)$.

2.  $\mathcal{CH}_X$ lifts as an $(\infty,1)$-functor $\mathcal{CH}_X:CDGA_\infty\to \mathop{PFac}_{X}(CDGA_\infty)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* First we note that $CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_\emptyset (A)\cong k$ and that the maps $\mu_{U_1,\dots, U_n,V}$ are CDGAs morphisms. Further, if $V_1,\dots, V_l$ is a collection of pairwise disjoint open subsets of $V\in Op(X)$ and $U_1,\dots, U_n$ is another family of pairwise disjoint open subsets of $V$ such that each $U_i$ is contained in some $V_j$, we can form the diagram $$\xymatrix{ \bigotimes_{j=1}^l \left(\bigotimes_{U_i \subset V_j} CH_{U_i}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)\right) \ar[rr]^{\quad \qquad \mu_{U_1,\dots, U_n, V}} \ar[d]_{ \bigotimes_{j=1}^l\Big(\mu_{(U_i\subset V_j), V_j}\Big)} && CH_{V}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)\\
 \bigotimes_{j=1}^l \big( CH_{V_j}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)\big) \ar[urr]_{\quad \mu_{V_1,\dots, V_j,V}} && }$$ which is commutative by functoriality of Hochschild chains. This proves that the rule $U\mapsto CH_U^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ is a prefactorization algebra with value in the category $CDGA$. The naturality follows from the naturality of Hochschild chains in the algebra variable and the lift to the $(\infty,1)$-framework follows from (the proof of) Proposition [\[P:Topderived\]](#P:Topderived){reference-type="ref" reference="P:Topderived"} and Theorem [\[T:derivedfunctor\]](#T:derivedfunctor){reference-type="ref" reference="T:derivedfunctor"}. ◻
:::

In particular, the prefactorization algebra $U\mapsto CH_{U}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$ is a *commutative* prefactorization algebra.

Following the terminology of [@CG; @Co], we said that an open cover $\mathcal{U}$ of $U\in Op(X)$ is **factorizing** if, for all finite collections $x_1,\dots, x_n$ of distincts points in $U$, there are *pairwise disjoint* open subsets $U_1,\dots, U_k$ in $\mathcal{U}$ such that $\{x_1,\dots, x_n\} \subset \bigcup_{i=1}^k U_i$. To define a factorization algebra, we need to introduce the Čech complex of a prefactorization algebra $\mathcal{F}$. Let $\mathcal{U}$ be a cover and denote $P\mathcal{U}$ the set of finite pairwise disjoint open subsets $\{U_1,\dots,U_n \, ,\, U_i\in \mathcal{U}\}$. Now the **Čech complex $\check{C}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{F})$** is the chain (bi-)complex $$\check{C}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{F})= \bigoplus_{P\mathcal{U}} \mathcal{F}(U_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal{F}(U_n)  \leftarrow \bigoplus_{P\mathcal{U} \times P\mathcal{U}} \mathcal{F}({U_1}\cap {V_1}) \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal{F}({U_n}\cap {V_m})  \leftarrow \cdots$$ where the horizontal arrows are induced by the alternate sum of the natural inclusions as for the usual Čech complex of a cosheaf (see [@CG]). Let us introduce a convenient notation for the Čech complex: given $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k \in P\mathcal{U}$, we denote $$\mathcal{F}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k) = \bigotimes_{U_{i_1}\in \alpha_1,\dots, U_{i_k}\in \alpha_k} \mathcal{F}(U_{i_1}\cap \cdots \cap U_{i_k}),.$$ The prefactorization algebra structure yields, for all $j=1,\dots, k$, natural maps $\mathcal{F}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k)\to \mathcal{F}(\alpha_1,\dots,\widehat{\alpha_j},\cdots,\alpha_k)$. The Čech complex of $\mathcal{F}$ can be simply written as $$\label{eq:Cechcomplex}\check{C}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_{k>0} \bigoplus_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k \in P\mathcal{U}} \mathcal{F}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k) [k-1].$$ The prefactorization algebra structure also induce a canonical map $\check{C}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{F})\to \mathcal{F}(U)$.

A prefactorization algebra $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ (with value in $CDGA$ or $k\textit{-}Mod$) is said to be a **factorization algebra** if, for all open subset $U\in Op(X)$ and every factorizing cover $\mathcal{U}$ of $U$, the canonical map $$\check{C}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{F})\to \mathcal{F}(U)$$ is a quasi-isomorphism (see [@Co; @CG]). When $\mathcal{F}$ is a commutative factorization algebra, the sequence $\bigoplus_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k \in P\mathcal{U}} \mathcal{F}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k) [k-1]$ is naturally a simplicial CDGA and thus the Čech complex $\check{C}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{F})$ has a natural structure of CDGA.

Note that $X$ itself, is always a factorizing cover. A Hausdorff space usually admits many different factorizing covers. This is in particular true for manifolds. Indeed, choosing a Riemannian metric on a manifold $X$ yields a nice factorizing cover given by the set of geodesically convex neighborhoods of every point in $X$.

It is shown in [@CG] that, if $\mathcal{U}$ is a *basis* for the topology of a space $X$ which is also a *factorizing cover*, and $\mathcal{F}$ is a *$\mathcal{U}$-factorization algebra*, then one obtains a factorization algebra $i_*^{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{F})$ on $X$ defined by $$\label{eq:DFacAlginducedbyaBasis} i_*^{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{F})(V):=\check{C}(\mathcal{U}_V,\mathcal{F})$$ where $\mathcal{U}_V$ is the cover of $V$ consisting of open subsets in $\mathcal{U}$ which are also subsets of $V$. We recall that a **$\mathcal{U}$-factorization algebra** is like a factorization algebra, except that $\mathcal{F}(U)$ is only defined for $U\in \mathcal{U}$ and further that we only require a quasi-isomorphism $\check{C}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{F}) \stackrel{\sim}\to \mathcal{F}(U)$ for factorizing covers $\mathcal{V}$ of $U$ consisting of open sets in $\mathcal{U}$.

A **factorizing good cover** is a good cover which is also a factorizing cover. For instance, any CW-complex has a factorizing good cover. Admitting a basis of factorizing good cover is a sufficient condition to prove that the Hochschild prefactorization algebra $\mathcal{CH}_X$ is a factorization algebra:

::: theorem
[]{#T:CH=FH label="T:CH=FH"} Let $X$ be a topological space with a factorizing good cover and $A$ be a CDGA. Assume further that there is a basis of open sets in $X$ which is also a factorizing good cover. The prefactorization algebra $\mathcal{CH}_X: U\mapsto CH_U^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ given by Lemma [\[P:prefactorization\]](#P:prefactorization){reference-type="ref" reference="P:prefactorization"} is a *factorization algebra* on $X$.

In particular, for any factorizing cover $\mathcal{U}$ of $X$, there is a canonical equivalence of CDGAs $$CH_{X}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \cong  \check{C}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{CH}_X).$$
:::

For instance the theorem applies to all manifolds (that we always assume to be paracompact) and more generally to CW-complexes.

::: proof
*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{U}$ be a factorizing good cover. We first prove that the rule $U\mapsto CH_{U}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$ is a *$\mathcal{U}$-factorization algebra*. Since, we already know that $U\mapsto CH_{U}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$ is a prefactorization algebra (Lemma [\[P:prefactorization\]](#P:prefactorization){reference-type="ref" reference="P:prefactorization"}), we only need to prove that, for any $U\in \mathcal{U}$ and any factorizing cover $\mathcal{V}$ of $U$ consisting of open sets in $\mathcal{U}$, the canonical map $\check{C}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{CH}_X) \stackrel{\sim}\to CH_{U}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Since $U$ is contractible, $CH^U_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)\cong A$ by Theorem [\[T:derivedfunctor\]](#T:derivedfunctor){reference-type="ref" reference="T:derivedfunctor"}. Let us denote $P\mathcal{V}$ the set of finite pairwise disjoint open subsets $\{(U_1,\dots,U_n \, ,\, U_i\in \mathcal{V}\}$. Now the Čech complex $\check{C}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{CH}_X)$ is the chain (bi-)complex $$\bigoplus_{P\mathcal{V}} CH_{U_1}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \otimes \cdots \otimes CH_{U_n}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)  \leftarrow \bigoplus_{P\mathcal{V} \times P\mathcal{V}} CH_{{U_1}\cap {V_1}}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \otimes \cdots \otimes CH_{{U_n}\cap {V_m}}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)  \leftarrow \cdots$$ where the horizontal arrows are induced by the alternate sum of the natural inclusions (see [@CG]). Since $\mathcal{U}$ is a good cover, Theorem [\[T:derivedfunctor\]](#T:derivedfunctor){reference-type="ref" reference="T:derivedfunctor"} and the prefactorization algebra structure of $\mathcal{CH}_X$ gives a natural equivalence of chain complexes $$\label{eq:diagFH=HH} {\small
\xymatrix@R=2pc{ \mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{P\mathcal{V}} CH_{U_1}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \otimes \cdots \otimes CH_{U_n}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)  \ar[d]_{\simeq} & \bigoplus\limits_{P\mathcal{V} \times P\mathcal{V}} \Big(\bigotimes CH_{{U_i}\cap {V_j}}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \Big) \ar[l] \ar[d]_{\simeq} & \ar[l]\cdots \\
\bigoplus\limits_{P\mathcal{V}} A\otimes \cdots \otimes A & \bigoplus\limits_{P\mathcal{V} \times P\mathcal{V}} A\otimes \cdots \otimes A \ar[l]& \cdots \ar[l] }}$$ We can form a simplicial set $N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{V})$ given by the nerve of the cover $\mathcal{V}$. Since $\mathcal{V}$ is factorizing, the canonical map $$\label{eq:diagFH=HH2}\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{\scriptsize \begin{array}{l} K_\bullet\stackrel{disj}\hookrightarrow N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{V}) \\
K_\bullet\mbox{ finite}\end{array}} \!\!\!\!CH_{K_\bullet}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \;\; \; \longrightarrow \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{\scriptsize \begin{array}{l} K_\bullet\to N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{V})\\
K_\bullet\mbox{ finite}\end{array}} \!\!CH_{K_\bullet}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)
\cong CH_{N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{V})}(A)$$ (where the the left colimit is over maps whose images are required to be disjoint open subsets) is an equivalence. The bottom line of diagram [\[eq:diagFH=HH\]](#eq:diagFH=HH){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:diagFH=HH"} now identifies with the left colimit of the map [\[eq:diagFH=HH2\]](#eq:diagFH=HH2){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:diagFH=HH2"}, hence with Hochschild chain complex of nerve $N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{V})$. Since $\mathcal{U}$ is a good cover, the intersections $(U_1 \coprod \cdots \coprod U_n) \cap (V_1\coprod \cdots \coprod V_m)$ are contractible. Thus by the Nerve Theorem (or Leray acyclic cover), the geometric realization of $N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{V})$ is quasi-isomorphic to the reunion $\bigcup_{\mathcal{V}} U_i = U$. Since $U$ is assumed to be contractible, we get from above and Proposition [\[P:homologyinvariance\]](#P:homologyinvariance){reference-type="ref" reference="P:homologyinvariance"} a natural equivalence (of prefactorization algebras) $\check{C}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{CH}_X)(U) \stackrel{\simeq}\to  CH_{U}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \,(\cong A)$. Thus the $\mathcal{U}$-prefactorization algebra $U\mapsto CH_{U}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$ is a $\mathcal{U}$-factorization algebra. We denote $CH_{\mathcal{U}}$ this $\mathcal{U}$-factorization algebra.

To conclude, we are left to prove that the induced factorization algebra $i_*^{\mathcal{U}}(CH_{\mathcal{U}})$ on $X$ is equivalent to $\mathcal{CH}_X$ as a prefactorization algebra. Let $V$ be an open subset of $X$. By [@CG], there is a natural equivalence $i_*^{\mathcal{U}}(CH_{\mathcal{U}})(V)\cong \check{C}(\mathcal{U}_V,CH_{\mathcal{U}})$ (where $\mathcal{U}_V$ is the cover of $V$ consisting of open subsets in $\mathcal{U}$ which are also subsets of $V$). Since the cover $\mathcal{U}_V$ is a good cover, as in the case where $V$ was in $\mathcal{U}$ above, there is a natural equivalence $${\small
\xymatrix@R=2pc{ \mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{P\mathcal{U}_V} CH_{U_1}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A) \otimes \cdots \otimes CH_{U_n}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)  \ar[d]_{\simeq} & \bigoplus\limits_{P\mathcal{U}_V \times P\mathcal{U}_V} \Big(\bigotimes CH_{{U_i}\cap {V_j}}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)\Big)   \ar[l] \ar[d]_{\simeq} & \ar[l]\cdots \\
\bigoplus\limits_{P\mathcal{U}_V} A\otimes \cdots \otimes A & \bigoplus\limits_{P\mathcal{U}_V \times P\mathcal{U}_V} A\otimes \cdots \otimes A \ar[l]& \cdots \ar[l] }}$$ where, as for diagram [\[eq:diagFH=HH\]](#eq:diagFH=HH){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:diagFH=HH"} above, the bottom line is equivalent to the Hochschild chain complex $CH_{N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{U}_V)}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ of the simplicial set $N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{U}_V)$ given by the nerve of $\mathcal{U}_V$. Since $\mathcal{U}$ is a good cover, we can again use the Nerve Theorem to see that $CH_{N_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(\mathcal{U}_V)}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)\cong CH_{\bigcup_{\mathcal{U}_V}U_i}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)\cong CH_{V}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ and thus to get the natural equivalence (of prefactorization algebras) $\check{C}(\mathcal{V},i_*^{\mathcal{U}}(CH_{\mathcal{U}}))(U) \stackrel{\simeq}\to CH_{V}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)\cong \mathcal{CH}_X(V)$. ◻
:::

If $\mathcal{F}$ is a factorization algebra on $X$ (with value in $k\textit{-}Mod$ or $k\textit{-}Mod_\infty$), and $f:X\to Y$ is a continuous map, one can define the **pushforward $f_*(\mathcal{F})$** by the formula $f_*(\mathcal{F})(V) =\mathcal{F}(f^{-1}(V))$ which actually is a factorization algebra on $Y$, see [@CG]. Costello and Gwilliam [@CG Section 3.a] have defined the **factorization homology** $HF(\mathcal{F})$ of $\mathcal{F}$ as the pushforward $p_*(\mathcal{F})$ where $p:X\to pt$ is the unique map. In other words, we have natural equivalences $$HF(\mathcal{F})\;\cong\; p_*(\mathcal{F})\;\cong\; \mathcal{F}(X)\;\cong\; \check{C}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{F})$$ in $k\textit{-}Mod_\infty$ (for any factorizing cover $\mathcal{U}$ of $X$). Note that, despite its name, $HF(\mathcal{F})$ is a cochain complex (up to equivalences) and, in particular a *derived* object (which may be thought as the ''derived global sections'' of $\mathcal{F}$). If $\mathcal{F}$ has value in $CDGA$, then $HF(\mathcal{F})$ is an object in $CDGA_\infty$ too. Theorem [\[T:CH=FH\]](#T:CH=FH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:CH=FH"} and Lemma [\[P:prefactorization\]](#P:prefactorization){reference-type="ref" reference="P:prefactorization"} yields

::: corollary
[]{#C:HFoCH=CH label="C:HFoCH=CH"} Let $X$ be a CW-complex. The Hochschild prefactorization functor $\mathcal{CH}_X$ is actually a functor $\mathcal{CH}_X: CDGA_\infty\to \mathop{Fac}_X(CDGA_\infty)$. Further, the factorization homology of $\mathcal{CH}_X(A)$ is equivalent to $CH^X_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ (as an object of $CDGA_\infty$); in other words the following diagram commutes: $$\xymatrix{CDGA_\infty\ar[rr]^{CH_{X}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(-)} \ar[d]_{\mathcal{CH}_X} & &CDGA_\infty\\ 
\mathop{Fac}_X(CDGA_\infty) \ar[rru]_{HF(-)} && }$$
:::

For manifolds, we will give below another geometric interpretation of the functor $\mathcal{CH}_X$ in terms of embeddings of manifolds in euclidean spaces (see Example [\[E:cstFac\]](#E:cstFac){reference-type="ref" reference="E:cstFac"}, Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} and Remark [\[R:cstFac\]](#R:cstFac){reference-type="ref" reference="R:cstFac"}).

A factorization algebra $\mathcal{F}$ on a manifold is said to be **locally constant**, if, the natural map $\mathcal{F}(U) \to \mathcal{F}(V)$ is a quasi-isomorphism when $U\subset V$ are homeomorphic to a ball (see [@CG; @Co]). Furthermore, we call $\mathcal{F}$ a **commutative constant factorization algebra** (on $X$), if there is a CDGA $A$, and natural quasi-isomorphisms $\mathcal{F}(U) \to A$ for any open $U\subset X$ homeomorphic to a ball. Here, natural means, that for any pairwise disjoint open subsets homeomorphic to a ball $U_1,\dots, U_n \in V$ of a contractible open subset $V\in X$ also homeomorphic to a ball, the following diagram is commutative in $k\textit{-}Mod_\infty$ $$\label{eq:cstFacAlg}\xymatrix{ \mathcal{F}({U_1})  \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal{F}({U_n}) \ar[d] \ar[rr]^{\qquad \quad \mu_{U_1,\dots, U_n,V}}  & & \mathcal{F}({V})  \ar[d]\\ A^{^{\otimes n}} \ar[rr]_{m^{(n)}} & & A}$$ where $m^{(n)}$ is the $(n-1)$-times iterated multiplication of $A$.

::: example
[]{#E:cstFac label="E:cstFac"} A class of examples of locally constant factorization algebras occurs as follows. By results of Lurie [@L-VI] (also see Proposition [\[P:Fac=En\]](#P:Fac=En){reference-type="ref" reference="P:Fac=En"} below), the data of a locally constant factorization algebra on $\mathbb R^n$ is the same as the data of an $E_n$-algebra. Thus any CDGA yields a locally constant factorization algebra (denoted $\mathcal{A}$ for the moment) on $\mathbb R^n$ (for any $n\geq 1$) and also, by restriction, on any open subset of $\mathbb R^n$.

Now, let $X$ be any manifold, and let $i:X\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^n$ be an embedding of $X$ in $\mathbb R^n$. Let $NX$ be an open tubular neighborhood of $X$ in $\mathbb R^n$. We write $p:NX\to X$ for the bundle map. Any factorization algebra $\mathcal{F}$ on $\mathbb R^n$ restricts to a factorization algebra $\mathcal{F}_{|NX}$ on $NX$ and the pushforward $p_*(\mathcal{F}_{|NX})$ is a factorization algebra on $X$. Thus, a CDGA $A$ yields a locally constant factorization algebra $p_*(\mathcal{A}_{|NX})$ on $X$ for any manifold $X$. Since $p_*(\mathcal{F}_{|NX})(U)\cong \mathcal{F}(p^{-1}(U))$, it is easy to check that $p_*(\mathcal{A}_{|NX})$ is indeed locally constant. Since by construction there is a natural quasi-isomorphism $\mathcal{A}(B) \stackrel{\sim}\to A=\mathcal{A}(\mathbb R^n)$ for any open ball $B\subset \mathbb R^n$, the induced locally constant factorization algebra $p_*(\mathcal{A}_{|NX})$ satisfies that there exists a natural equivalence $\mathcal{A}_{|NX}(B)\stackrel{\sim} \to A$ for any open set $B\in Op(X)$ homeomorphic to a ball. In other words, we can think to the factorization algebra $p_*(\mathcal{A}_{|NX})$ as being *constant* (and commutative).

Note that the previous analysis can be extended to any space $X$ which embeds as the base of locally trivial fibration $U\to X$ where $U$ is an open in some $\mathbb R^n$ and the fibers are homeomorphic to a ball.
:::

::: example
Let $G$ be a discrete group acting properly discontinuously on a manifold $X$. According to [@CG], any $G$-equivariant factorization algebra $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ yields a factorization algebra $\mathcal{F}^G$ on $X/G$. Further, it is easy to check that, if $\mathcal{F}$ is locally constant then so is $\mathcal{F}^G$ too. In particular any CDGA $A$ yields a locally constant factorization algebra on $\mathbb R^n/G$ for any discrete group acting properly discontinuously on $\mathbb R^n$.
:::

The next corollary describes what *constant* commutative factorization algebra are; namely they all are equivalent to derived Hochschild chains for some CDGA.

::: corollary
[]{#C:HFact=CH label="C:HFact=CH"} Let $X$ be a manifold and $\mathcal{F}$ a commutative factorization algebra such that there exists a CDGA $A$ and a natural equivalence $\mathcal{F}(B)\stackrel{\sim} \to A$ for any open set $B\in Op(X)$ homeomorphic to a ball. Then $\mathcal{F}$ is equivalent to the Hochschild chain factorization algebra $\mathcal{CH}_X(A)$ (given by Lemma [\[P:prefactorization\]](#P:prefactorization){reference-type="ref" reference="P:prefactorization"}).
:::

In particular, there is a natural equivalence $HF(\mathcal{F}) \cong CH_{X}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$.

::: proof
*Proof.* Choosing a Riemannian metric on $X$, the set $\mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X)$ of geodesic open balls is *factorizing*. Further, $\mathcal{F}$ being constant, we have, for any $\alpha=\{B_1,\dots, B_k\}\in \mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X)$, natural equivalences $$\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal{F}(\alpha)=\mathcal{F}(B_1)\otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal{F}(B_k) &\cong & \bigotimes_{i=1}^k A 

\cong  CH_{B_1}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A) \otimes \cdots \otimes CH_{B_k}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A) 
\cong \mathcal{CH}_X(\alpha)
\end{aligned}$$ Similarly, there are natural equivalences $$\mathcal{F}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_j)\; \cong \; \mathcal{CH}_X(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_j)$$ for any $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_j\in P\mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X)$. Thus, by Theorem [\[T:CH=FH\]](#T:CH=FH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:CH=FH"}, we have $$\begin{aligned}
 HF(\mathcal{F})&\cong & \check{C}(\mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X),\mathcal{F}) \\ 
&\cong & \bigoplus_{k>0} \bigoplus_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k \in P\mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X)} \mathcal{F}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k) [k-1]\\
 &\cong &\bigoplus_{k>0} \bigoplus_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k \in P\mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X)} \mathcal{CH}_X(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k) [k-1] \; \cong \; CH_{X}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A).
\end{aligned}$$ It follows that we have an equivalence $\check{C}(\mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X),\mathcal{F}) \cong\check{C}(\mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X),\mathcal{CH}_X)$. The same analysis can be made for any open subset $U\in Op(X)$ instead of $X$ and the naturality of Hochschild chains ensures that the equivalence $\mathcal{F}(U)\cong CH_U^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ is natural in $U$. ◻
:::

::: remark
Note that the above Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} can be extended to manifolds with corners, where, by a locally constant factorization algebra on a manifold with corners, we mean a factorization algebra which is locally constant if, whenever restricted to the strata (which are manifolds), it is locally constant see [@CG; @AFT]. One can extend the definition of constant factorization algebra in the same way.

Let us also sketch how Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} can be used in the general case of *locally constant commutative factorization algebras*. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a locally constant factorization algebra on a manifold $M$ and assume that there is a codimension $1$ submanifold (possibly with corners) $N$ of $M$ with a trivialization $N\times I$ of its neighborhood such that $M$ is decomposable as $M=X\cup_{N\times I}Y$ where $X,Y$ are submanifolds (with corners) of $M$ glued along $N \times I$. The inclusion $i:N\times I\to X$ induces a map of factorization algebras $i_\ast(\mathcal{A}_{|N\times I}) \to \mathcal{A}_{|X}$, which gives a structure of $\mathcal{A}_{|N\times I}$-module to $\mathcal{A}_{|X}$ since $\mathcal{A}$ is commutative.

::: lemma
[]{#L:HFact=CHloc label="L:HFact=CHloc"} If $\mathcal{A}_{|Y}$ is constant (say $\mathcal{A}_{|Y}(B)\cong A$ for a CDGA $A$ and any ball $B\in Op(Y)$), $\mathcal{A}$ is equivalent to $\mathcal{A}_{|X} \mathop{\otimes}\limits^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)} \mathcal{CH}_{Y}(A)$ in $\mathop{Fac}_M(CDGA_\infty)$ (where the factorization algebras are pushforward to $M$ along the natural inclusions). In particular, $$HF(\mathcal{A})\; \cong\; HF(\mathcal{A}_{|X})\mathop{\otimes}^{\mathbb{L}}_{CH_{N\times I}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)} CH_{Y}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A).$$
:::

Using a handle decomposition of $M$, one can use the lemma above to compute the homology of a locally constant commutative factorization algebra $\mathcal{A}$ in terms of (iterated) derived tensor products of derived Hochschild functors (see Section [\[S:locality\]](#S:locality){reference-type="ref" reference="S:locality"} for a related construction).

::: proof
*Proof.* It essentially follows from Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} and Lemma [\[L:Enmodule\]](#L:Enmodule){reference-type="ref" reference="L:Enmodule"}.

Indeed, since $\mathcal{A}_{|Y}$ is constant of type $A$, it is isomorphic to $\mathcal{CH}_{Y}(A)$ and similarly, $\mathcal{A}_{|N\times I}\cong \mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)$ (by Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"}). Further, there is a quasi-isomorphism of prefactorization algebras: $$\label{eq:HFact=CHloc}\mathcal{A}_{|X} \mathop{\otimes}\limits^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)} \mathcal{CH}_{Y}(A) \; \cong \; \mathcal{A}_{|X} \otimes \bigoplus_{\ell\geq 0} \mathcal{A}_{|N\times I}[\ell] \otimes \mathcal{A}_{|Y}$$ where the middle term is the bar construction on $\mathcal{A}_{|N\times I}$. Applying the same construction to the Čech complex of a factorizing cover yields that $\mathcal{A}_{|X} \mathop{\otimes}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)} \mathcal{CH}_{Y}(A)$ is indeed a factorization algebra.

By the identity eq[\[eq:HFact=CHloc\]](#eq:HFact=CHloc){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:HFact=CHloc"} above, for any open $U$ in $X$ or open $V$ in $Y$, we have, $$\mathcal{A}_{|X} \mathop{\otimes}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)} \mathcal{CH}_{Y}(A)\,\Big(U\Big) \;\cong \;\mathcal{A}_{|X}\big(U\big), \quad \mathcal{A}_{|Y} \mathop{\otimes}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)} \mathcal{CH}_{Y}(A)\,\Big(V\Big) \;\cong \;\mathcal{A}_{|Y}\big(V\big)$$ Similarly, using that the two-sided bar construction of $CH_{W}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ is naturally isomorphic to $CH_{W}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ for any open $W$ in $N\times I$, we get that the restriction (to $N\times I$) $\big(\mathcal{A}_{|X} \mathop{\otimes}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)} \mathcal{CH}_{Y}(A)\big)_{|N\times I}$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)$. Thus the factorization algebra $\mathcal{A}_{|X} \mathop{\otimes}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{CH}_{N\times I}(A)} \mathcal{CH}_{Y}(A)$ is equivalent to the one obtained by gluing together $\mathcal{A}_{X}$ and $\mathcal{A}_{Y}$ along their intersection $\mathcal{A}_{N\times I}$, that is $\mathcal{A}$. ◻
:::

::: remark
[]{#R:cstFac label="R:cstFac"} Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} implies that the factorization algebras $p_*(\mathcal{A}_{|NX})$ are independent (up to equivalences in $\mathop{Fac}_X(k\textit{-}Mod_\infty)$) of the choices of the embedding and of the tubular neighborhood made in Example [\[E:cstFac\]](#E:cstFac){reference-type="ref" reference="E:cstFac"}. Indeed, if $i_1:X\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^{n_1}$ and $i_2:X\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^{n_2}$ are two embeddings of a manifold $X$ in an euclidean space, and given two choices $\mathbb R^{n_1}\supset N_1 X \stackrel{p_1}\to X$,$\mathbb R^{n_2}\supset N_2 X \stackrel{p_2}\to X$ of tubular neighborhoods, Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} implies that there are natural equivalences $${p_1}_*(\mathcal{A}_{|N_1 X})(U)\stackrel{\simeq} \longrightarrow CH^U_{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)  \stackrel{\simeq}\longleftarrow {p_2}_*(\mathcal{A}_{|N_2 X})(U)$$ for open subsets $U\subset X$.
:::

::: example
[]{#E:Surface label="E:Surface"} Let $M$ be a manifold and $A=C^{\infty}(M)$ its algebra of functions. Also let $\Sigma^g$ be a compact Riemann surface of genus $g$ and $\mathcal{F}$ the factorization algebra (see Theorem [\[T:CH=FH\]](#T:CH=FH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:CH=FH"}) on $\Sigma^g$ defined by the rule $U\mapsto C_{U}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(C^{\infty}(M))$ (here, in the definition of Hochschild chains, the tensor product over $k$ is the *completed* tensor product so that $C^\infty(M)\otimes C^\infty(M)\cong C^\infty(M\times M)$). Let $\Omega^n(M)$ denote the de Rham $n$-forms on $M$, viewed as complex concentrated in degree $0$.

An analogue of Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg theorem for Hochschild chains over surfaces [@GTZ Theorem 4.3.3] implies that the factorization homology of $\mathcal{F}$ on $\Sigma^g$ is given by $$HF(\mathcal{F}) \cong S_{C^\infty(M)}\Big(\Omega^1(M)[2]\Big)\mathop{\otimes}_{C^\infty(M)} S_{C^\infty(M)}\Big(\Omega^1(M)\Big) \mathop{\otimes}_{C^\infty(M)} \!\bigotimes_{C^\infty(M)}^{i=1\dots 2g}\! \Omega^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(M)[\bullet]$$ where $V[n]$ is the graded space $(V[n])^i=V^{i+n}$ (*i.e.* with cohomological degree shifted down by $n$) and $S_{C^\infty(M)}(W)$ is the symmetric graded algebra of a graded $C^\infty(M)$-module $W$. In terms of graded-geometry the above isomorphism is equivalent to saying that $HF(\mathcal{F})$ is (equivalent to) the algebra of functions of the graded manifold $$HF(\mathcal{F}) \; \cong \;T[2](M)\oplus  \bigoplus_{i=1}^{2g} T[1]M.$$
:::

We now study a homotopical strengthening of the locally constant condition. We said that a factorization algebra on $X$ is **strongly locally constant** if the natural map $\mathcal{F}(U) \to \mathcal{F}(V)$ is a quasi-isomorphism when $U\subset V$ are contractibles. Let $A$ be a CDGA; we say that a factorization algebra $\mathcal{F}$ is **strongly constant of type $A$**, if there exists a natural quasi-isomorphism $\mathcal{F}(U) \to A$ for any contractible $U$. Here, natural means, that for any pairwise disjoint contractible open subsets $U_1,\dots, U_n \in V$ of a contractible open subset $V\in X$, the following diagram (similar to diagram [\[eq:cstFacAlg\]](#eq:cstFacAlg){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:cstFacAlg"}) is commutative in $k\textit{-}Mod_\infty$ $$\xymatrix{ \mathcal{F}({U_1})  \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal{F}({U_n}) \ar[d] \ar[rr]^{\qquad \quad \mu_{U_1,\dots, U_n,V}}  & & \mathcal{F}({V})  \ar[d]\\ A^{^{\otimes n}} \ar[rr]_{m^{(n)}} & & A}$$

::: example
The factorization algebras given by Theorem [\[T:CH=FH\]](#T:CH=FH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:CH=FH"} are strongly constant of type $A$.
:::

::: example
[]{#E:stcstFac label="E:stcstFac"} Let $X$ be a manifold, $A$ a CDGA and $p_*(\mathcal{A}_{|NX})$ be the factorization algebra on $X$ constructed in Example [\[E:cstFac\]](#E:cstFac){reference-type="ref" reference="E:cstFac"}. Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} and the homotopy invariance of Hochschild chains implies that $p_*(\mathcal{A}_{|NX})$ is a *strongly constant* factorization algebra.

Now let $X$ be a topological space that embeds as a retract $i:X \stackrel{\hookrightarrow}{\leftarrow} UX:r$, where $UX$ is an open subset of some $\mathbb R^n$ (where $n$ can be infinite). Then, similarly to the manifold case, the CDGA $A$ yields a factorization algebra $\mathcal{A}$ on $\mathbb R^n$ and a factorization algebra $r_*(\mathcal{A}_{|UX})$ on $X$. The above paragraph implies that $\mathcal{A}$ is strongly constant. Further if the fibers of $r$ are contractible, then $r_*(\mathcal{A}_{|UX})$ is *strongly constant of type $A$*.
:::

We have the following analogue of Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} for strongly constant factorization algebras on a topological space that admits a factorizing good cover (for instance those given by Example [\[E:stcstFac\]](#E:stcstFac){reference-type="ref" reference="E:stcstFac"} when $X$ is a CW-complex)

::: corollary
[]{#C:CH=FH label="C:CH=FH"} Let $X$ be a topological space with a factorizing good cover and $A$ be a CDGA. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a factorization algebra on $X$.

1.  Assume $\mathcal{F}$ is strongly constant of type $A$, then one has a natural equivalence $HF(\mathcal{F})\cong CH_X^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ in $k\textit{-}Mod_\infty$.

2.  Assume that there is a basis $\mathcal{B}$ of open sets which is a factorizing good cover and that $\mathcal{F}$ is a factorization algebra which satisfies the strongly constant condition (of type $A$) with respect to opens in $\mathcal{B}$, *i.e.*, there exists a natural quasi-isomorphism $\mathcal{F}(U) \to A$ for $U\in \mathcal{B}$ (which is automatically contractible). Then, there is a natural equivalence of factorization algebras $\mathcal{F} \cong \mathcal{CH}_X(A)$ between $\mathcal{F}$ and the Hochschild prefactorization algebra given by Lemma [\[P:prefactorization\]](#P:prefactorization){reference-type="ref" reference="P:prefactorization"} (in particular, $\mathcal{F}$ is strongly constant of type $A$).
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The first assertion is proved as in Corollary [\[C:HFact=CH\]](#C:HFact=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFact=CH"} using a factorizing good cover $\mathcal{U}$ for $X$ instead of $\mathcal{B}\mathit{all}^g(X)$ (and using a proof similar to the proof of Theorem [\[T:CH=FH\]](#T:CH=FH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:CH=FH"} to get that $\bigoplus_{k>0} \bigoplus_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k \in P\mathcal{U}} \mathcal{CH}_X(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k) [k-1] \; \cong \; CH_{X}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$).

Since any factorization algebra is uniquely defined by its restriction to a factorizing basis, the second assertion follows easily from the first one applied to all $V\in \mathcal{B}$. ◻
:::

As a further corollary to Theorem [\[T:CH=FH\]](#T:CH=FH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:CH=FH"}, we can extend the Hochschild construction as a pullback and pushforward of factorization algebras in a particular setting. The pushforward construction of factorization algebras was described above Corollary [\[C:HFoCH=CH\]](#C:HFoCH=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFoCH=CH"}. Following [@CG], there is also a **pullback** construction for factorization algebras given for an *open immersion* $f:N\to M$. For a factorization algebra $\mathcal F$ on $M$, let $f^*\mathcal F$ be the factorization algebra on $N$ given by $f^*\mathcal F(U)=\mathcal F(f(U))$ for all open subsets $U\subset N$ such that $f|_U:U\to f(U)$ is a homeomorphism, extended to a full factorization algebra.

Now, assume that $X, Y, Z$ are topological spaces, and that there is an open immersion of $X\times Y\hookrightarrow Z$ of $X\times Y$ into $Z$. For a factorization algebra $\mathcal F$ on $Z$, we define the Hochschild factorization algebra with respect to $X$ to be the factorization algebra ${\bf CH}_X(\mathcal F)$ of $\mathcal F$ on $Y$ given by $${\bf CH}_X(\mathcal F) := (pr_Y)_*\circ f^*(\mathcal F), \text{ where } \quad
 Y \stackrel {pr_Y} \longleftarrow X\times Y \stackrel f \longrightarrow  Z.$$ Here $pr_Y:X\times Y\to Y$ denotes the projection to $Y$. This construction induces a functor, called ${\bf CH}_X: \mathop{Fac}_Z(CDGA_\infty) \to \mathop{Fac}_Y(CDGA_\infty)$, $\mathcal F\mapsto (pr_Y)_*\circ f^*(\mathcal F)$ which satisfies the following naturality condition.

::: corollary
[]{#L:CHYoCHX=CHXoCHZ label="L:CHYoCHX=CHXoCHZ"} Assume that $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ all admit a basis of open sets which is a factorizing good cover. Under the functors $\mathcal{CH}_Y: CDGA_\infty\to \mathop{Fac}_Y(CDGA_\infty)$ and $\mathcal{CH}_Z: CDGA_\infty\to \mathop{Fac}_Z(CDGA_\infty)$ from Corollary [\[C:HFoCH=CH\]](#C:HFoCH=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:HFoCH=CH"}, the functor ${\bf CH}_X$ represents the functor $CH_X^\bullet$ on $CDGA_\infty$, *i.e.*, the following diagram commutes: $$\xymatrix{CDGA_\infty\ar[rr]^{CH_{X}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}} \ar[d]_{\mathcal{CH}_Z} & &CDGA_\infty\ar[d]^{\mathcal{CH}_Y} \\ 
\mathop{Fac}_Z(CDGA_\infty) \ar[rr]^{{\bf CH}_X} && \mathop{Fac}_Y(CDGA_\infty) }$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let $A\in CDGA_\infty$ and $\mathcal{W}$ be a basis and factorizing good cover by open subsets $U\times V\subset X\times Y$ such that $U,V$ are contractible and $f|_{U\times V}:U\times V\to f(U\times V)$ is a homeomorphism. In particular, for $U\times V\in \mathcal{W}$, we have natural equivalences $$f^*( \mathcal{CH}_Z (A)) (U\times V) \cong  \mathcal{CH}_Z (A)(f(U\times V)) \cong CH^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}_{f(U\times V)}(A)\cong A$$ since $f(U\times V)$ is contractible. Hence Corollary [\[C:CH=FH\]](#C:CH=FH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:CH=FH"} implies that $f^*( \mathcal{CH}_Z (A))$ is strongly constant of type $A$ and further that, for any contractible open $U\subset Y$, $${\bf CH}_X ( \mathcal{CH}_Z (A)) (U) 
\;\cong \; f^*( \mathcal{CH}_Z (A)) (X\times U)\; \cong \;CH_{X\times U}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)\;\cong \; CH_X^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A).$$ Thus $\mathcal{CH}_X ( \mathcal{CH}_Z (A))$ is a strongly constant factorization algebra on $Y$ of type $CH^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}_{X}(A)$, hence, by Corollary [\[C:CH=FH\]](#C:CH=FH){reference-type="ref" reference="C:CH=FH"}.(2), is naturally equivalent to $\mathcal {CH}_Y(CH^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}_X(A))$. ◻
:::

As an application of the above Corollary [\[L:CHYoCHX=CHXoCHZ\]](#L:CHYoCHX=CHXoCHZ){reference-type="ref" reference="L:CHYoCHX=CHXoCHZ"}, we look at the particular case where $X\times D^{n-k}\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^n$ is an open immersion. In this case, ${\bf CH}_X$ maps factorization algebras in $\mathbb R^n$ to factorization algebras in $D^{n-k}$, and thus via the inclusion $D^{n-k}\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^{n-k}$ also to factorization algebras in $\mathbb R^{n-k}$, $${\bf CH}_X: \mathop{Fac}\, _{\mathbb R^{n}}(CDGA_\infty)\to \mathop{Fac}\, _{\mathbb R^{n-k}}(CDGA_\infty).$$
