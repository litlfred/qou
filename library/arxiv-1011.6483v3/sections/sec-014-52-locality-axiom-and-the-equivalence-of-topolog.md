[]{#S:locality label="S:locality"}

In view of Proposition [\[P:TCHpushout\]](#P:TCHpushout){reference-type="ref" reference="P:TCHpushout"} and Theorem [\[T:derivedfunctor\]](#T:derivedfunctor){reference-type="ref" reference="T:derivedfunctor"}, Morse theory (or any triangulation) suggests the following result, which is the main result of this section.

::: theorem
[]{#T:TCH=CH label="T:TCH=CH"} Let $M$ be a manifold of dimension $m$ endowed with a framing of $M \times D^k$ and $A$ be a differential graded commutative algebra viewed as an $E_{m+k}$-algebra. Then, the topological chiral homology of $M$ with coefficients in $A$, denoted by $\int_M A$ is equivalent to $CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_M(A)$ viewed as an $E_k$-algebra (in other words to $j_k^*(CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_M(A))$).
:::

In particular, topological chiral homology $\int_M A$ with coefficient in a CDGA $A$ is always equivalent to a CDGA and is defined for *any* manifold $M$.

This theorem is similar to [@L-VI Theorem 5.3.3.8] (with the difference that we assume $M$ to be smooth and keep track of the $E_k$-algebra structure). In this section, we wish to prove it by using a straightforward geometrical approach based on the gluing property. Indeed, the key idea to prove Theorem [\[T:TCH=CH\]](#T:TCH=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:TCH=CH"} is to use handle decomposition which is very appropriate to deal with manifolds and the definition of topological chiral homology. However, note that, with respect to Hochschild chains, a representation of $M$ as a CW complex is already nice enough.

Before proving Theorem [\[T:TCH=CH\]](#T:TCH=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:TCH=CH"}, we recall a few facts on the handle decompositions. Let $M$ be a smooth manifold of dimension $m$. A *handle decomposition* of $M$ is a sequence $\emptyset \subset M_0 \subset \cdots \subset M_m =M$, where each $M_j$ is obtained by attaching $j$-handles to $M_{j-1}$, see [@Mil]. That is gluing a copy of $H^j= D^j \times D^{m-j}$ using the attaching map $S^{j-1} \times D^{m-j} \to \partial M_{j-1}$ which is assumed to be an embedding. In particular all handles of same dimension are attached using diffeomorphisms.

We can achieve such a handle decomposition for $M$ using Morse theory as follows. Let $f: M \to \mathbb R$ be a Morse function with critical points $p_1, \cdots, p_k$ numbered in a way that $f(p_1) < \cdots < f(p_k)$. Choose $a_0, \cdots, a_k$ such that $a_0 < f(p_1) < a_1 < \cdots < a_{k-1} < f(p_k) < a_k$. Now it is sufficient to note that $f^{-1}([a_{i-1}, a_{i}])$ is diffeomorphic to attaching a $j$-handle to $f(a_{i-1}) \times [0, 1]$, where $j$ is the index of the critical point $p_i$, *i.e.* the number of the negative eigenvalues of the Hessian of $f$ at that critical point. For example a torus with the height function is first given by attaching a $D^0 \times D^2$ to the empty set, then attaching a $D^1 \times D^1$ (think of it as a thin ribbon) to the boundary of the previous $D^2$. Then attaching another ribbon to the boundary of the previous ribbon, and the finally attaching a $D^2 \times D^0$ to what has been obtained along the boundary.

::: lemma
[]{#L:TCHhandling label="L:TCHhandling"} Let $M$ be an $n$-framed manifold and $N$ be an $n$-framed manifold obtained from $M$ by attaching a countable sequence of handles $(H_i)_{i\in \mathbb N}$. For any $n\in \mathbb N$, we write $X_{k}$ for the result of attaching the first $k$-handles to $M$. For any $E_n$-algebra $A$, there is a natural equivalence $$\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{k\in \mathbb N} \int_{X_k} A \stackrel{\simeq} \longrightarrow \int_N A.$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We may assume $\dim(M)=n$. The maps $X_k\to X_{k+1}$ yield a diagram $$\label{eq:seqbfD} N({\text{Disj}}(M)) \to N({\text{Disj}}(X_1)) \to \cdots \to N({\text{Disj}}(X_k)) \to \cdots \to N({\text{Disj}}(N))$$ of faithfull maps, hence canonical maps $\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{k\in \mathbb N} N({\text{Disj}}(X_k)) \to N({\text{Disj}}(N))$ and $$\label{eq:TCHunion}  \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{k\in \mathbb N} \int_{X_k} A\cong \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{k\in \mathbb N} \big(\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{} \psi_{X_k}\big) \to \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{} \psi_N\cong \int_N A$$ (using the notation introduced in Definition [\[Def:TCHasColimit\]](#Def:TCHasColimit){reference-type="ref" reference="Def:TCHasColimit"}). Note that $\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{k\in \mathbb N} \big(\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{} \psi_{X_k}\big)$ can be identified with the colimit $\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits\tilde{\psi}_N$ given informally by the diagram $$\tilde{\psi}_N (V_1^k\cup \cdots\cup V_j^k)=\int_{V_1^k}A \otimes \cdots \otimes \int_{V_j^k} A$$ where the $V_i^k$ are disjoint open subsets of $X_k$ homeorphic to a ball (here $k$ is not fixed).

Since $A$ is a fixed $E_n$-algebra, it is in particular an $\mathbb{E}(X)$-algebra (in the sense of [@L-VI Section 5.2.4]) for any $n$-framed manifold $X$. In particular, by [@L-VI Theorem 5.2.4.9] (also see Proposition [@L-VI Proposition 5.3.2.13]), if $U\subset V$ are two open subsets of $X$ which are homeomorphic to a ball, then the induced map $\int_U A \to \int_V A$ is an equivalence.

Now, let $V$ be an open subset of $N$ which is homeomorphic to a ball. Since $N=\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{k\in \mathbb N} X_k$, there exists a $k$ such that $V\cap X_k$ is a non-empty open subset of $X_k$, and thus contains an open ball $V^k$ in $X_k$ which is lying in $V$ too. In particular the natural map $\int_{V^k} A\to \int_{V} A$ is an equivalence. It follows that, given a finite set $V_1,\dots,V_n$ of open homeomorphic to a ball in $N$, we can find an integer $k$ big enough and open sets $V^k_1\subset V_1$, \...., $V^k_n\subset V_n$ in $X_k$ which are homeomorphic to a ball, yielding a natural equivalence $$\tilde{\psi}_N (V_1^k,\dots, V_n^k)=\int_{V_1^k}A \otimes \cdots \otimes \int_{V_n^k} A \cong \int_{V_1}A \otimes \cdots \otimes \int_{V_n} A=\psi_N (V_1,\dots, V_n).$$ This proves the cofinality of the functor $N(\widetilde{\mathop{\rm Disj}}(N))\hookrightarrow N(\mathop{\rm Disj}(N))$ induced by the natural inclusion. Here we have denoted $\widetilde{\mathop{\rm Disj}}(N)$ the partially ordered set of open subsets of $N$ which are homeomorphic to $F\times R^n$ for a finite set $F$ and included in some $X_k$ (where $k$ is not fixed). Passing to colimits, we get that the map $\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{k\in \mathbb N} \big(\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{} \psi_{X_k}\big)\cong \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits\tilde{\psi}_N \to \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits\psi_N$ is an equivalence and thus the canonical map [\[eq:TCHunion\]](#eq:TCHunion){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:TCHunion"} is an equivalence as well. ◻
:::

::: proof
*Proof of Theorem [\[T:TCH=CH\]](#T:TCH=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:TCH=CH"}.* Let us sketch the key idea of the proof first: by the value on a point axiom, both topological chiral homology and higher Hochschild chains agree on a point and further on any disk $D^k$ (up to neglect of structure). Using handle decompositions, one can chop manifolds on disks which are glued along their boundaries. Since both topological chiral homology and higher Hochschild chains satisfy a similar gluing axiom (and also behave the same way under disjoint unions), one then can lift the natural equivalence for disks to any manifold using handle decompositions. We now make the above scheme precise.

Assume $M$ is compact. Let us choose a generic Morse function on $M$ and the associated handle decomposition $\emptyset \subset M_0 \subset \cdots \subset M_m =M$ of $M$. Then $\emptyset \subset M_0\times  D^k \subset \cdots \subset M_m\times D^k =M \times D^k$ is a handle decomposition of $M� \times D^k$. That is $(M\times D^k)_j=M_j\times D^k$ where we replace each $j$-handle $H^j=D^j\times D^{m-j}$ attached to $M_{j-1}$ by the $j$-handle $D^j\times D^{m+k-j}\cong D^j\times \big(D^{m-j}\times D^{k}\big)$ attached to $M_{j-1} \times D^{k}= (M\times D^k)_{j-1}$. The $(m+k)$-framing of $M$ induces an $(m+k)$-framing of each $M_j\times D^k =(M\times D^k)_j$.

By homotopy invariance of Hochschild chains, one has an equivalence of CDGAs $CH_{M\times D^d}(A)\cong CH_{M}(A)$ (for any integer $d$). Further, from diagram [\[E:EnCom\]](#E:EnCom){reference-type="eqref" reference="E:EnCom"} we deduce that, for any CDGA $B$, one has $j_{k}^*(B)\cong \iota_{d}^*(j_{k+d}^*(B))$ where $\iota_d: E_k\hookrightarrow E_{k+d}$ is the natural map. Since, for any $E_{m+k}$-algebra $B$, one has $\int_{D^m}B \cong B$ viewed as an $E_k$-algebra, the result of Theorem [\[T:TCH=CH\]](#T:TCH=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:TCH=CH"} holds for all disks.

We now prove by induction that it holds for all ($(m+d)$-framed) spheres $S^m$ and $E_{m+d}$-algebra $A$. For $S^0=pt\coprod pt$, it follows from Theorem [\[T:derivedfunctor\]](#T:derivedfunctor){reference-type="ref" reference="T:derivedfunctor"} and [@L-VI Theorem 5.3.3.1] that $\int_{S^0}A \cong A\otimes A \cong CH_{S^0}(A)$ (as $E_{d}$-algebras). Now, assume the result for $S^{m-1}$ and $m\geq 1$. We have a decomposition of the $m$-sphere $S^m$ as $S^m\cong D^m \cup_{S^{m-1}} D^m$ as in the assumption of Proposition [\[P:TCHpushout\]](#P:TCHpushout){reference-type="ref" reference="P:TCHpushout"}. Since this decomposition is also an homotopy pushout, it follows from the induction hypothesis, Proposition [\[P:TCHpushout\]](#P:TCHpushout){reference-type="ref" reference="P:TCHpushout"} and Theorem [\[T:derivedfunctor\]](#T:derivedfunctor){reference-type="ref" reference="T:derivedfunctor"}.(3) that there are natural equivalences $$\int_{S^m}A \;\cong \; A\!\mathop{\otimes}\limits^{\mathbb{L}}_{\int_{S^{n-1}}\! A}\! A \;\cong\; CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{D^m}(A)\!\mathop{\otimes}\limits^{\mathbb{L}}_{CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{S^{m-1}}(A)} \! CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{D^m}(A) \;\cong \; CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{S^m}(A)$$ of $E_d$-algebras which finishes the induction step.

Clearly, $M_0$ is a disjoint union $M_0=\coprod_{I_0} D^m$ of finitely many $m$-dimensional balls (here $I_0$ is the set indexing the various disks in $M_0$). Using again that, for any $E_{m+k}$-algebra $B$, one has $\int_{D^m}B \cong B$ viewed as an $E_k$-algebra, we get a natural equivalence of $E_k$-algebras $\int_{M_0} A \cong \bigotimes_{I_0} j_k^*(A)\cong j_k^*(\bigotimes_{I_0} A)$ since $\int_{M\coprod N} A\cong \int_M A \otimes \int_N A$ by [@L-VI Theorem 5.3.3.1], the set $I_0$ is finite and $j_k$ is monoidal (that is commutes with the diagonals of the $E_k$-operads). Further $CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{D^m}(A) \cong CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{pt}(A)\cong A$ and, by Theorem [\[T:derivedfunctor\]](#T:derivedfunctor){reference-type="ref" reference="T:derivedfunctor"}.(1) and (2), there is a natural equivalence $CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_0}(A)\cong \bigotimes_{I_0} A$ of CDGAs. Hence the theorem is proved for $M_0$.

By assumption $M_1$ is obtained by attaching finitely many $1$-handles $H^1_1,\dots, H^1_{i_1}$ (the sequence may be empty) to $M_0$. Choosing appropriate tubular neighborhoods for the image of $\partial{D^1}\times D^{m-1}$ in $M_0$ and gluing it with $\big(\partial{D^1}\times D^{m-1}\big)\times [0,\varepsilon]\cong\big(\partial{D^1}\times [0,\varepsilon]\times D^{m-1}\big)\subset H^1_1$, we can assume that the result $M_0\cup_{\partial D^1 \times D^{m-1}} H_1^1$ of attaching $H^1_1$ to $M_0$ satisfies the assumption of Proposition [\[P:TCHpushout\]](#P:TCHpushout){reference-type="ref" reference="P:TCHpushout"}. Then, by Proposition [\[P:TCHpushout\]](#P:TCHpushout){reference-type="ref" reference="P:TCHpushout"} we have a natural equivalence of $E_k$-algebras: $$\label{eq:attachhandle}
\int_{M_0} A\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\int_{\partial D^1 \times D^{m-1}} A}^{\mathbb{L}} \int_{H^1_{1}} A\cong \int_{M_0\cup_{\partial D^1 \times D^{m-1}} H_1^1} A.$$ By Theorem [\[T:derivedfunctor\]](#T:derivedfunctor){reference-type="ref" reference="T:derivedfunctor"}.(3), we also have a natural equivalence of CDGAs (and thus of the underlying $E_{k}$-algebras) $$\label{eq:attachhandleCH}
CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_0} (A)\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{\partial D^1 \times D^{m-1}}(A)}^{\mathbb{L}} CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{H^1_{1}} (A)\cong CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_0\cup_{\partial D^1 \times D^{m-1}} H_1^1} (A).$$ We have already seen that we have natural equivalences $CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_0}(A) \cong \int_{M_0}(A)$ of $E_k$-algebras and similarly for $\partial D^1 \times D^{m-1}$ and $D^1\times D^{m-1}$ in place of $M_0$ (since those manifolds are disks). Combining these equivalences with those given by the identities [\[eq:attachhandle\]](#eq:attachhandle){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:attachhandle"} and [\[eq:attachhandleCH\]](#eq:attachhandleCH){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:attachhandleCH"}, we get a natural equivalence $$\int_{M_0\cup_{\partial D^1 \times D^{m-1}} H_1^1} A \cong CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_0\cup_{\partial D^1 \times D^{m-1}} H_1^1} (A).$$ Attaching more $1$-handles, we inductively get a natural equivalence $\int_{M_1} A \cong CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_1}(A)$ of $E_k$-algebras.

We proceed the same for attaching $j\geq 2$-handles (by induction on $j$). The proof is identical to the $1$-handles case once we notice that there are also a natural equivalence $\int_{\partial{ D^j}\times D^{m-j}} A \cong CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{\partial{ D^j}\times D^{m-j}}(A)$. The later follows from the natural equivalences relating topological chiral homology and higher Hochschild chains of spheres (with value in $A$) proved above. Indeed, $\partial{ D^j}\times D^{m-j} \cong S^{j-1}\times D^{m-j}$ and there is a natural equivalence $\int_{S^{j-1}\times D^{m-j}} A\cong i_{m-j}^*\big( \int_{S^{j-1}} A \big)$ where $i_{m-j}: E_{k+1}\hookrightarrow E_{m+k-j+1}$ is the canonical map (neglecting part of the structure). Similarly, there are natural equivalences $CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{S^{j-1}}(A) \cong CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{S^{j-1} \times D^{m-j}}(A)$ of CDGAs. It follows, since $j_{k+m -j+1}^*\big(CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{S^{j-1}} (A)\big) \cong \int_{S^{j-1}} A$, that we get a natural equivalence $$j_{k+1}^*(CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{S^{j-1}\times D^{m-j}} \big( (A)\big)\cong i_{m-j}^*\, j_{k+m-j+1}^*\big( CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{S^{j-1}\times D^{m-j}}(A)\big) \cong \int_{S^{j-1}\times D^{m-j}} A$$ which finishes the proof in the compact case.

If $M$ is non-compact, we still have a handle decomposition, but we may have to attach countable many handles to go from $M_i$ to $M_{i+1}$. In particular, we can find an increasing sequence of relatively compact open subsets $M_i=X_0\subset X_1\subset\cdots X_n\subset \cdots \subset \bigcup_{n\geq 0} X_n =M_{i+1}$ (for instance by choosing $\overline{X_n}$ to be the result of attaching the first $n$ $i$-handles to $M_i$). We wish to prove the result by induction on $i$. Note first that, by definition of the Hochschild chain functor and Lemma [\[L:TCHcoproduct\]](#L:TCHcoproduct){reference-type="ref" reference="L:TCHcoproduct"}, there is an equivalence (for the underlying $E_k$-algebras structures) $$CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_0}(A)\cong \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{\small \begin{array}{l}F_0 \subset I_0\\ 
F_0 \mbox{ finite} \end{array}
} \left(\bigotimes_{f\in F_0} CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{D^m}(A)\right) \cong \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{\small \begin{array}{l}F_0 \subset I_0\\ 
F_0 \mbox{ finite} \end{array}} \left(\bigotimes_{f\in F_0} \int_{D^m}(A)\right) \cong \int_{M_0} A$$ which proves the result for $M_0$. Now, assume we have an natural equivalence $CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_i}(A)\cong \int_{M_i} A$. Writing $M_i=X_0\subset X_1\subset\cdots X_n\subset \cdots \subset \bigcup_{n\geq 0} X_n =M_{i+1}$, by the above argument for the finite handles case, we have natural equivalences $CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{X_n}(A) \cong \int_{X_n} A$ for all $n$ and thus a commutative diagram $$\xymatrix{\mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{n\in \mathbb N} CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{X_n}(A) \ar[r]^{\simeq} \ar[d] & \mathop{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longrightarrow}}\limits_{n\in \mathbb N} \int_{X_n} A \ar[d] \\ CH^{\scriptstyle\bullet}_{M_{i+1}}(A) \ar[r] &  \int_{M_{i+1}} A}.$$ By Lemmas [\[L:TCHhandling\]](#L:TCHhandling){reference-type="ref" reference="L:TCHhandling"} and Lemma [\[L:CHhandling\]](#L:CHhandling){reference-type="ref" reference="L:CHhandling"}, the vertical arrows are equivalences,hence the lower map is too, which finishes the induction. ◻
:::

::: remark
A geometric intuition behind Theorem [\[T:TCH=CH\]](#T:TCH=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:TCH=CH"} can be seen as follows. Let $M$ be a dimension $m$ manifold. Since a CDGA $A$ is an $E_{n}$-algebra for any $n$, the topological chiral homology $\int_M A$ is defined for any $n$-framing of $M$, and is an $E_{n-m}$-algebra. Further, if $M$ is $n$-framed (hence we have chosen a trivialization of $M\times D^{n-m}$), then $M$ is also naturally $(n+k)$-framed for any integer $k$. Since $A$ is a CDGA, it is an $E_{n+k}$-algebra as well and thus we could have used the trivialization of $M\times D^{n-m} \times D^k\cong M\times D^{n+k-m}$ as well to define $\int_M A$ as an $E_{n-m+k}$-algebra.

It is well known that the transversality theorem implies that two embeddings $\phi_1: M \to S^n$ and $\phi_2: M\to S^n$ of $M$ are isotopic if $n$ is large enough. In particular, for large $n$ the framing that comes from the embedding into $S^n$ is unique. This unique invariant of $M$ is called the stable normal bundle. Note that this implies that any two abstract framings of $M\times D^k$ and $M \times D^l$ are stably equivalent since, for example, for $M\times D^k$ we can make $M$ sit inside $\mathbb R^n$ and then the normal bundle of $M$ in $\mathbb R^n$ is the complement of the framing of the tangent bundle of $M\times D^k$ in $\mathbb{R}^n \times D^k$.
:::

Building upon the last remark, we see that the topological chiral homology of an $(m+k)$-framed manifold $M$ with value in a CDGA should be equivalent (up to neglect of structure) to the topological chiral homology of $M$ equipped with the stable normal framing so that we get

::: corollary
Topological chiral homology with values in CDGAs is independent of the framing. In other words, if $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$ are diffeomorphic manifolds equipped respectively with an $(n+k_1)$-framing and $(n+k_2)$-framing, then there is a canonical equivalence $\int_{M_1} A \cong \int_{M_2} A$ of $E_{\min(k_1,k_2)}$-algebras.
:::

In particular, $\int_M A$ is naturally defined for every manifold $M$ when $A$ is a CDGA.

::: proof
*Proof.* By Theorem [\[T:TCH=CH\]](#T:TCH=CH){reference-type="ref" reference="T:TCH=CH"}, there are natural equivalences $\int_{M_1} A \cong CH_{M_1}^{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}(A)$ of $E_{k_1}$-algebras and $\int_{M_2} A \cong CH_{M_2}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ of $E_{k_2}$-algebras. Since $M_1$ and $M_2$ are diffeomorphic, we have an equivalence $CH_{M_1}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)\cong CH_{M_2}^{\scriptstyle\bullet}(A)$ as CDGAs. ◻
:::
