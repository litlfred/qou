In particular if we look for solutions of the Klein-Gordon equation of the form $$\phi\equiv r^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}e^{-i\omega t}Y_{\ell}(\Omega_{d-2})\Psi_{\omega \ell}(r),$$ it is not hard to see that $\Psi_{\omega \ell}$ must obey a Schrodinger-type equation $$-\frac{d}{dr_*^2}\Psi_{\omega \ell}+V_{eff}(r)\Psi_{\omega\ell}=\omega^2 \Psi_{\omega \ell}.$$ Here $r_*$ is a "tortoise" type coordinate obeying $$r_*'(r)=f^{-1},$$ and the effective potential is $$\begin{aligned} \nonumber V_{eff}(r)=R^{-2}\bigg(1+\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^2&-\frac{\alpha R^2}{r^{d-1}}\bigg)\bigg[\left(m^2+\frac{d(d-2)}{4R^2}\right)r^2\\ &+\left(\ell(\ell+d-3)+\frac{(d-2)(d-4)}{4}\right)+\frac{(d-2)^2}{4}\cdot \frac{\alpha}{r^{d-3}}\bigg].\label{AdSPot} \end{aligned}$$ The details of this potential do not matter, but we see that it vanishes linearly at the only real positive root of $f(r)$, that is at the horizon, and that it grows quadratically with $r$ at large $r$.
