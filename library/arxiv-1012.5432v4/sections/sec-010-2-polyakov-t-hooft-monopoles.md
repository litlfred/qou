In broken gauge theories, it is not Dirac monopoles but rather Polyakov-'t Hooft monopoles that play a key role. These are of a very different nature than Dirac monopoles - they are non-singular solutions of the field equations that carry finite energy, and necessitate no new degrees of freedom, i.e. all their parameters can be expressed in terms of the ordinary scalar and vector meson parameters.

Recall that $\pi_i(M), i \geq 2$ is always abelian.
