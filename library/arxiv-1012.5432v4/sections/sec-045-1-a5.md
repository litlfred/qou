A5
Ogburn and Preskill showed that A5 = PSL(2, F4) is suﬃcient for universal quantum
computation under the assumption that a supply of magnetic ancillas is available (but not
necessarily electric ancillas). [20] This suﬃciency condition was subsequently generalized to
any nonsolvable ﬁnite group. [21] Note that geometrically A5 is the subgroup of rotations
of the icosahedral group (the symmetries of a regular icosahedron - one of the ﬁve platonic
solids), which in turn is a subgroup of SO(3). Out of the class of nonsolvable ﬁnite groups,
A5 has the best chance of being engineered in the lab since it has the smallest order (60
elements). It is unlikely to ﬁnd a symmetry group with such high order in nature in a useful
form.
