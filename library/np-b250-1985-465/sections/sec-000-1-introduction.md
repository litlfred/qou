In refs. [26] (quoted as II) we have shown how to systematically determine the 
low-energy structure of the Green functions in QCD. The method is based on a 
simultaneous expansion in powers of the external momenta and of the quark masses. 
We have applied the method to an expansion in powers of mu and md at fixed ms, 
m ..... (chiral SU(2) × SU(2)) and have shown that the observed low-energy structure 
of zr,r scattering offers a precision test of the theory. This confirms that mu and md 
are indeed small parameters: if one expands physical quantities like F~, M,,, form 
factors or scattering amplitudes in powers of mu and md and only retains the first 
two terms in this expansion one obtains a very accurate representation of the quantity 
in question. 
In the present paper we extend this framework to expansions in powers of ms.

Recently, Skyrme's proposal [49] to identify the proton with a topologically stable 
soliton solution of the effective lagrangian was revived by Witten [57] (the recent 
literature on the subject may be traced from refs. [7, 21,9]). As is well known, the 
properties of the soliton are sensitive to higher-order terms in this effective 
lagrangian, in particular to the terms of order pa which we are investigating in the 
present paper. In the chiral limit, the coupling constants which occur at order p4 
may be fixed with the experimental information on the D-wave ~-Tr scattering lengths. 
Donoghue, Golowich and Holstein [21] have shown, e.g., that in this approximation 
(disregarding higher-order contributions and loop corrections) the energy of the 
soliton turns out to be remarkably close to the mass of the proton. 
It is conceivable that a description of the proton in which the degrees of freedom 
of the three valence quarks do not play an important role, and where the energy of 
the configuration is stored in a coherent field of pions, indeed offers a semi- 
quantitative understanding of its basic properties. If so, the properties of the chiral 
lagrangians would be of interest even beyond their certified domain of relevance, 
viz. the low-energy expansion. In the present paper we however restrict ourselves 
to this domain.
