In the classical case, when g = 1, it was shown that the intertwiner carries an irreducible u(n) representation [28].
We can wonder whether a similar result also holds here. The answer is positive. A cumbersome proof can probably
be obtained by looking at the Casimirs of U/,(u(n)). We do not want to follow this route. Instead we would like to
recall the seminal results by Jimbo, Rosso and Lustzig which essentially state that all the finite dimensional
representations of the deformation U,(g) of the enveloping algebra U/(g), where g is any complex simple Lie algebra,
are completely reducible. The irreducible representations can be classified in terms of highest weights and in particular
they are deformations of the irreducible representations of U(g), when q is not root of unity. We can extend this
result to the semi-simple case and to U,(u(n)) in particular (see Section 2.5 of for example). Now we know
that when gq = 1, the intertwiner is an irreducible representation of u(n), hence by deforming the enveloping algebra,
the representation of U,(u(m)) carried by the U,(su(2)) intertwiner must stay an irreducible representation. As a
consequence, the U4,(su(2)) intertwiner must carry an irreducible representation of U,(u(n)), just as in the classical
case.
