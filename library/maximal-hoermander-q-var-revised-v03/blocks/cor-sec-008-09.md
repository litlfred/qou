Let 0 < a < 2, d ∈N and s > 2a. Let A = (−∆)a/2. Then exp(itA)f(x) → f(x) as t →0 for a.e. x ∈Rd if f ∈Hs(Rd). Proof : Note that we can apply Proposition 6.6. Indeed, A generates a positive contraction semigroup on L2(Rd) (as it is subordinated to the heat semigroup) and is self-adjoint. Our proposition applies for f ∈D(Aδ) = D((−∆)aδ/2) = Haδ(Rd) with δ > 2 so with aδ > 2a. In another direction, in [LW, Theorem 1.4], a.e. convergence of exp(itA)f(x) is proved with A = P(D), where P : Rd →R is a real valued function, and P(D) is the selfadjoint Fourier multiplier with symbol P(ξ).
