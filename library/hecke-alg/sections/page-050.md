NOTES ON CHAPTER 4 AB

. In a different guise, the semistandard basis of the Schur algebra (i.e. the case
q = 1), first appeared as J. A. Green’s [28] codeterminant basis; shortly after-
wards, Woodcock [62] gave a more direct proof. Richard Green [29] generalized
Woodcocks argument to give a g-analogue of the codeterminant basis; it turns
out that this basis is the semistandard basis of Theorem 4.14. The construction
of the semistandard basis given here is based [17] and is very different from
Green’s; that the semistandard basis and the Murphy basis are related was first
observed by Du and Scott [21].

. In [18] Donkin parameterizes the irreducible modules of the 0-Schur algebra
over a field and also gives an explicit character formula for the simple modules.
As one might expect, the 0-Schur algebra is not quasi-hereditary; surprisingly,
however, it is a quasi-Frobenius algebra (at least when d > n).

. It is shown in [10, 61.2] that whenever A is a quasi—Frobenius algebra, a € A
and J is an ideal of A then Homa(J,aA) = J MaA. By Proposition 1.14, # is
quasi—Frobenius so this gives another proof of Corollary 4.9. (See also [17, §5].)

. Parshall and Wang [58] first showed that the g-Schur algebra is quasi—hereditary.
In particular, this implies that the g-Schur algebras have finite global dimension;
this dimension has been explicitly calculated by Donkin [18] when d > n.

. The double centralizer property (4.20) is normally phrased in terms of actions
of Y(d,n) and # upon “q-tensor space”; see [5,16].
