Notes on Chapter 2

1. All of the results in this Chapter are due to Graham and Lehrer and can be
found in [25,26].

2. The introduction of cellular algebras was largely motivated by the Kazhdan—
Lusztig [46] bases of the Iwahori—Hecke algebras of type A. Many more exam-
ples of cellular algebras can be found in [25,26]. For example, (generalized)
Temperley-Lieb algebras, Jones’ annular algebra, Ariki-Koike algebras, and the
cyclotomic g-Schur algebras of [17] are all cellular.

3. A ring theoretic definition of cellular algebras has been Kénig and Xi [50]; in
particular, their approach is independent of a choice of basis. They have also
investigated cellular algebras under Morita equivalence [48] and shown that the
poset A is not, in any sense, an invariant of a cellular algebra [49].

4. If A is also a symmetric algebra then Graham [25, 4.12] has shown that the dual
basis of a cellular basis of A is again cellular, providing that the trace form on A
commutes with the «automorphism.

5. Du and Rui [20] have proved a converse to Corollary 2.19; they showed that any
quasi-hereditary algebra possessing a certain type of involution is cellular.
