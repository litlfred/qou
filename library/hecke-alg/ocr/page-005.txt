In Chapter 3 these notes begin in earnest. The combinatorics of tableaux are
introduced and Murphy’s basis theorem is proved in the first two sections. Using
Chapter 2 this yields the simple #-modules. The third section uses the Jucys—
Murphy elements to construct the g-analogue of Young’s seminormal form for the
Specht modules of .# in the semisimple case. The fourth section classifies the
simple #—modules.

Chapter 4 shows how the Murphy basis of # can be “lifted” to give a cel-
lular basis of the g—-Schur algebra. This involves the introduction of semistandard
tableaux which correspond to orbits of the standard tableaux which index Murphy’s
basis. The simple modules for the g—Schur algebras are constructed and classified
using the results of Chapter 2. For free, we obtain the fact that the g—Schur algebras
are quasi—hereditary.

Chapter 5 is devoted to proving the Jantzen—Schaper theorem and classifying
the blocks of these algebras. Here we follow the paper of James and the author [39],
using improvements from [40]. This is by far the most technical part of these notes.
The key idea is to first construct an orthogonal basis of the Weyl modules of (d,n)
ina “modular system”; it is here that generalizations of the Jucys-Murphy elements
play their most crucial role.

The most obvious omission from these notes is any mention of the beautiful
result of Dipper and James [15] which says that the representation theory of the
general linear groups in non-defining characteristic is completely determined by
the g-Schur algebras. It would also have been good to include the Kleshchev—
Brundan branching rules [4] and the work of Ariki [2], Grojnowski [30] and Lascoux,
Leclerc and Thibon [51] connecting quantum groups and decomposition matrices
of Iwahori—Hecke algebras over field of characteristic zero. These matters would
have more than doubled the size of these notes so instead we refer the interested
reader to the literature and, in time, to Brundan’s forthcoming book [5].
