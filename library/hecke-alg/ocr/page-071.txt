3. The Weyl modules W* of the q-Schur algebra can also viewed as Weyl modules
for the quantum general linear groups (see, for example, [12, Theorem 3.3.2]).
Using a geometric argument Andersen, Polo and Wen [1] proved a sum formula
for the Weyl modules of quantum groups; Theorem 5.31 can be deduced from
their result using the argument of Schaper’s thesis [60].

4. Jantzen [43] proved his sum formula first for the Weyl modules of the general
linear groups and later [44] extended the sum formula to Weyl modules of other
types. In Jantzen’s work the sum formula is expressed in terms of the dot
action of an affine Weyl group upon the weight lattice. Starting with Jantzen’s
result, and guided by [42], Schaper [60] deduced the combinatorial statement. of
Corollary 5.32 in the case of the symmetric groups.

5. Necessary and sufficient conditions for two Specht modules of # to belong to
the same block were proved in [14] and [39] respectively.

6. In the representation theory of the symmetric group, Corollary 5.37 is known as
the Nakayama conjecture (even though it has been proved for a long time). The
elegant idea of using the double centralizer property to classify the blocks of #
is due to Konig.

7. Theorem 5.36 classifies the blocks for the g—Schur algebras when d > n; when
d<n the blocks of .(d,n) have been classified by Cox [9].

8. Proposition 5.38 first appeared in [38]. In this paper James also gives a series
of “rules” which partially determine the decomposition matrices of the q—Schur
algebras together with tables of decomposition numbers when n < 10.
