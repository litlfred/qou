Introduction

Twahori-Hecke algebras first appeared in the work of Iwahori [34] and now
have applications in subjects ranging from the ordinary and modular representation
theory of Coxeter groups and the groups of Lie type, to knot theory, quantum
groups and statistical mechanics (sce [52] and the references there). Schur algebras
were introduced by Schur [61] who used them to classify the ordinary irreducible
polynomial representations of GE,(C); their “quantum analogues,” the g-Schur
algebras, also determine the representation theory of the finite general linear groups
in non-defining characteristic [15].

These notes aim to give a clear and concise treatment of the modular repre-
sentation theory of the Iwahori-Hecke algebras of the symmetric groups and of the
q-Schur algebras. Both of these theories were pioneered by Dipper and James in a
series of landmark papers [13-16]. Taking full advantage of recent advances, these
notes recast this theory with a primary goal of classifying and constructing the sim-
ple modules and the blocks of these algebras. Apart from some basic representation
theory, these notes are entirely self-contained.

One of the central themes of these notes is to view the Iwahori—Hecke algebras
and q-Schur algebras as cellular algebras, a class of algebras introduced by Graham
and Lehrer [26]. This amounts to constructing a basis which is extremely well
adapted to the representation theory of the algebra. For example, given a cellular
basis one can immediately construct all of the simple modules of the algebra.

The cellular basis of the Iwahori-Hecke algebra which we concentrate upon
is the so-called Murphy basis [55]; this is a very natural basis indexed by pairs
of standard tableaur. Apart from being cellular, the Murphy basis has another
marvelous property in that it is very easy to “lift” this basis to give a cellular basis
for the g-Schur algebra, which is indexed by pairs of semistandard tableaux. In this
way, one obtains a very clean and very elegant construction of the simple modules
of both the Iwahori-Hecke algebras and the q—Schur algebras.

In order to classify the blocks of the our algebras we prove an analogue of
Jantzen’s sum formula for the Weyl modules of the g-Schur algebras. Key to our
proof of this result are generalizations of the Jucys—Murphy elements from the
representation theory of the symmetric groups. Using these elements we are able
to construct orthogonal bases for the Weyl modules (when the g-Schur algebras are
semisimple), which then allow us to compute the Gram determinant of the cellular
basis of the Weyl modules. Essentially some highest weight theory then allows us
to deduce the sum formula and so classify both the blocks of the g-Schur algebras
and Iwahori—Hecke algebras.
