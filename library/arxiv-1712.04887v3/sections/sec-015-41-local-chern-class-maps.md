[]{#sub.localchern label="sub.localchern"}

Let $\mathfrak{q}$ be a prime of norm $q \equiv -1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$ in $\mathcal{O}_F$. We work with these primes for several (related) reasons. The first is that the groups $K_3(\mathbf F_q)$ we consider below have order $q^2 - 1$, and so to see interesting classes in $K_3(F)/n K_3(F)$ we require that $q^2 -1$ be divisible by $n$, and this necessitates choosing $q \equiv \pm 1 \bmod n$. On the other hand, if $q \equiv 1 \bmod n$, then $\mathbf F_q$ contains the $n$th roots of unity, which we generally need to avoid in our construction. The reason to avoid $n$th roots of unity manifests itself quite concretely in this setting: the Bloch group $B(\mathbf F_q)$ itself turns out to have order (more or less) $q+1$ rather than $q^2 - 1$, so it won't see any interesting $n$-torsion classes unless $q \equiv -1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$. The residue field of $\mathcal{O}_F$ at $\mathfrak{q}$ is $\mathbf F_q$, and the residue field of $\mathcal{O}_{F(\zeta)}$ at a prime $\mathfrak{Q}$ above $\mathfrak{q}$ is $\mathbf F_{q^2} = \mathbf F_{q}(\zeta)$. Following Lemma [\[lemma:avoidS\]](#lemma:avoidS){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:avoidS"}, suppose that $S$ does not contain any primes dividing $q$.

::: lemma
There exists a commutative diagram of Chern class maps as follows: $$\begin{diagram}
K_3(F)/nK_3(F) & 
\rTo^{c_\zeta} & 
(\mathcal{O}[1/S]^{\times}_{F(\zeta)}/\mathcal{O}[1/S]^{\times n}_{F(\zeta)})^{\chi^{-1}} \\
\dTo & & \dTo \\
K_3(\mathbf F_q)/nK_3(\mathbf F_q) & \rEquals^{c_{\zeta,\mathfrak{q}}} & 
\mathbf F^{\times}_{q^2}/\mathbf F^{\times n}_{q^2}.
\end{diagram}$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* By the Chinese Reminder Theorem, we may reduce to the case when $n= p^{m}$. There is an isomorphism $K_3(F) \otimes \mathbf Z_p \simeq K_3(\mathcal{O}_F) \otimes \mathbf Z_p$ (see Theorem [\[theorem:surjective2\]](#theorem:surjective2){reference-type="ref" reference="theorem:surjective2"}). Let $\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}}$ be the completion of $\mathcal{O}_{F}$ at $\mathfrak{q}$. We have a more general diagram as follows: $$\begin{diagram}
K_3(\mathcal{O}_F)/nK_3(\mathcal{O}_F) & 
\rTo^{c_\zeta} & H^1_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_F[1/p],\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z(2)) & \rTo & 
(\mathcal{O}[1/S]^{\times}_{F(\zeta)}/
\mathcal{O}[1/S]^{\times n}_{F(\zeta)})^{\chi^{-1}} \\
\dTo & & \dTo & &  \dTo \\
K_3(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}};\mathbf Z_p)/nK_3(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}};\mathbf Z_p)  & \rEquals^{c_{\zeta,\mathfrak{q}}} 
&  H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}},\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z(2)) & \rEquals & 
\mathbf F^{\times}_{q^2}/\mathbf F^{\times n}_{q^2} \\
\uEquals & & \uEquals & & \uEquals \\
K_3(\mathbf F_q)/nK_3(\mathbf F_q) & \rEquals^{c_{\zeta,\mathfrak{q}}} &  H^1(\mathbf F_q,\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z(2)) 
& \rEquals & \mathbf F^{\times}_{q^2}/\mathbf F^{\times n}_{q^2}.
\end{diagram}$$ The image of $H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_F[1/p])$ in the cohomology of $\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}}$ for $\mathfrak{q}$ prime to $p$ lands in the subgroup $H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}$ of unramified classes. This subgroup is precisely the image of $H^1(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{q},\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z(2))$ under inflation. The first horizontal map on the right hand side was constructed previously, and the other two horizontal maps on the right hand side are constructed in the same way. The reason that the $\chi^{-1}$-invariants do not appear on the factors $\mathbf F^{\times}_{q^2}/\mathbf F^{\times n}_{q^2}$ is that the Galois group already acts by $\chi^{-1}$. More precisely, on the one hand, $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf F_{q^2}/\mathbf F_{q})$ is generated by Frobenius which acts as multiplication by $q \equiv -1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$, and on the other hand, $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf F_{q^2}/\mathbf F_q) = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf F_q(\zeta_p)/\mathbf F_q)$, and the character $\chi^{-1}$ is precisely the non-trivial character of this group. The identification of the two lower horizontal lines is a reflection of Gabber rigidity, which implies that $K_3(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}};\mathbf Z_p) \simeq K_3(\mathbf F_{\mathfrak{q}}) \otimes \mathbf Z_p$. ◻
:::

::: proposition
[]{#prop:cheb label="prop:cheb"} Let $\widetilde{F}$ denote the Galois closure of $F$, and suppose that $\zeta\notin \widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$. (Equivalently, suppose that $n$ is prime to $\widetilde w_F$ of Equation [\[wtwiddle\]](#wtwiddle){reference-type="ref" reference="wtwiddle"}.)

1.  []{#parta.cheb label="parta.cheb"} There is a map: $$\begin{diagram}
    K_3(F)/nK_3(F) & \rTo^{\bigoplus c_{\zeta,\mathfrak{q}}} & 
    \bigoplus 
    \mathbf F^{\times}_{q^2}/\mathbf F^{\times n}_{q^2}
    \end{diagram}$$ where the sum ranges over all primes $\mathfrak{q}$ of prime norm $q \equiv -1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$ which split completely in $F$, or alternatively runs over all but finitely many primes $q \equiv -1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$ which split completely in $F$.

2.  []{#partb.cheb label="partb.cheb"} The image of this map is isomorphic to the image of the global map $c_\zeta$, which is injective if $(n,w_2(F)) = 1$.

3.  []{#partc.cheb label="partc.cheb"} For $\xi \in K_3(F)$, the set $$\left\{ \mathfrak{q}\subset \mathcal{O}_F[1/S] \ \left| \ c_{\zeta,\mathfrak{q}}(\xi) = 0 \right. \right\}$$ (for any finite $S$) determines the image of $\xi$ up to a scalar.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* It suffices to consider the case when $n= p^{m}$. Let $\xi \in K_3(F)$, and let the class of $c_{\zeta}(\xi)$ be represented by an $S$-unit $\epsilon$. Because of the Galois action, this gives rise via Kummer theory to a $\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z$-extension $H$ of $F(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$, and such that the reduction mod $\mathfrak{q}$ of $\epsilon$ determines the element $\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}} \in \mathrm{Gal}(H/F(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1}))$. (Explicitly, we have $H(\zeta)=F(\zeta,\epsilon^{1/n})$.) Hence our assumptions imply that any prime $q$ which splits completely in $F(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$ (which forces $q \equiv \pm 1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$) and is additionally congruent to $-1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;p$ must split in $H$. Let $\widetilde{H}$ denote the Galois closure of $H$ over $\mathbf Q$, and $\widetilde{F}$ the Galois closure of $F$ over $\mathbf Q$. Note that the Galois closure of $F(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$ is $\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$. A prime $q$ splits completely in $H$ if and only if it splits completely in $\widetilde{H}$, and splits completely in $F(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$ if and only if it splits completely in $\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$. We have a diagram of fields as follows: $$\begin{diagram}
\widetilde{H}& \rLine & \widetilde{H}(\zeta) \\
\dLine & & \dLine \\
\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1}) & \rLine & \widetilde{F}(\zeta) \end{diagram}$$ By assumption, we have $\zeta\notin \widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$. Since $H/F(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$ is cyclic of degree $n$, it follows that $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{H}/\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1}))$ is an abelian $p$-group. On the other hand, $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{F}(\zeta)/\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})) = \mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z$. so $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{H}(\zeta)/\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1}))$ is the direct sum of $\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z$ with a $p$-group, Let $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{H}(\zeta)/\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})) \subset \mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{H}/\mathbf Q)$ denote an element of order $2p$. By the Chebotarev density theorem, there exist infinitely many primes $q \in \mathbf Q$ with Frobenius element in $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{H}/\mathbf Q)$ corresponding to $\sigma$. By construction, the prime $q$ splits completely in $\widetilde{F}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})$ because the corresponding Frobenius element is trivial in $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})/\mathbf Q)$. On the other hand, since $\sigma$ has order divisible by $2$ and by $p$, it is non-trivial in both $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{F}(\zeta)/\widetilde{F}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})) = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf Q(\zeta)/\mathbf Q(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1}))$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{H}/\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1}))$. The first condition implies that $q \equiv -1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$, and the second condition implies that $q$ does not split completely in $H$, a contradiction. The injectivity (under the stated hypothesis) follows from Lemma [\[lemma:sahinj\]](#lemma:sahinj){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:sahinj"}. ◻
:::

::: remark
The condition that $\zeta\notin \widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$ is automatic if $p$ is unramified in $F$, because then the ramification degree of $\mathbf Q(\zeta)$ is $p-1$ whereas the ramification degree of $\widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$ is $(p-1)/2$ for $p$ odd. If $\zeta\in \widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$, then there are no primes $q$ which split completely in $F$ and have norm $-1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$. In particular, when $\zeta\in \widetilde{F}(\zeta+ \zeta^{-1})$, we have $B(\mathbf F_q)\otimes\mathbf F_p=0$ for every prime $q$ which splits completely in $F$.
:::
