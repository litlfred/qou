## Preliminaries on the Bloch group

In this section, we compare our definition to that of Suslin [@Suslintwo §1], and give an alternate presentation of $B(F)$ as a subquotient of $\mathbf Z[F^{\times} \setminus 1]$.

::: definition
[]{#def.BFsuslin label="def.BFsuslin"} Suslin's *Bloch group* of a field $F$ is the quotient $$\widetilde{B}(F)=\widetilde{A}(F)/\widetilde{C}(F)\,,$$ where $\widetilde{Z}(F) = \mathbf Z[F^{\times} \setminus 1]$, $\widetilde{A}(F)$ is the kernel of the map $$d: \widetilde{Z}(F)\;\longrightarrow\;\mbox{\small$\bigwedge^2$}F^{\times}, \qquad [X] \mapsto (X) \wedge (1-X)$$ and $\widetilde{C}(F)\subseteq \widetilde{A}(F)$ the group generated by the five term relation ([\[5term\]](#5term){reference-type="ref" reference="5term"}) for $X$ and $Y$ ranging over $F^{\times} \setminus 1$ with $X \ne Y$.
:::

Following ([\[BFn\]](#BFn){reference-type="ref" reference="BFn"}), we define $\widetilde{B}(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z):=\widetilde{A}(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)/(n \widetilde{Z}(F) + \widetilde{C}(F))$.

::: lemma
[]{#suslincomparison label="suslincomparison"} There is a surjection $\widetilde{B}(F) \rightarrow B(F)$ whose kernel is the $2$-torsion group generated by the elements $\langle X \rangle = [X] + [X^{-1}]$ for $X \in F^{\times} \setminus 1$. There is surjection $\widetilde{B}(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z) \rightarrow B(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)$ whose kernel is generated by the elements $\langle X \rangle$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The argument is the same in both cases so we only consider the first map. Since $\widetilde{C}(F)$ includes into $C(F)$, there is a natural map $\widetilde{B}(F) \rightarrow B(F)$. By [@Suslintwo Lemma 1.3], the element $c:=[X]+[1-X]$ in $\widetilde{B}(F)$ does not depend on $X$. But the image of $c$ in $B(F)$ is given by $[X]+[1-X]=[0]$, so $[0]$ lies in the image. Moreover, $[1]=0$ and $[\infty]=-[0]$ in $B(F)$ so the map is surjective. The image of $\langle X \rangle$ in $B(F)$ is $[X]+[X^{-1}] =0$. There is an isomorphism $$\widetilde{B}(F) = \widetilde{B}(F) \oplus [0]\mathbf Z\oplus [1]\mathbf Z\oplus [\infty]\mathbf Z/([0]=c, [1]=0, [\infty]=-[0]),$$ and the map $\widetilde{B}(F) \rightarrow B(F)$ factors through this isomorphism in the obvious way. It therefore suffices to show that any specialization of the $5$-term identity to a "forbidden" pair of parameters $X$ and $Y$ can be written as a sum of elements of the form $\langle X \rangle$, $[1]$, and $c - [0]$. Up to symmetries, this reduces to the case where one takes $Y$ to be either $0$, $1$, $\infty$, or $X$. The resulting specializations of ([\[5term\]](#5term){reference-type="ref" reference="5term"}) are as follows: $$\begin{aligned}
0= \ & [X]-[0]+[0]-[0]+[1-X] =  c - [0], \\ 
0 = \ & [X]-[1]+[1/X]-[\infty]+[\infty] =   \langle X \rangle - [1], \\
0 = \ & [X] - [\infty] + [\infty] - [1-1/X] + [0] =  [X] + [1/X] - [1/X]-[1-1/X]+[0] \\
= \ &  \langle X \rangle - c + [0],  \\
0 = \ & [X] - [X] +[1] - [1] + [1] =  [1],
\end{aligned}$$ and thus the kernel consists precisely of the subgroup generated by $\langle X \rangle$. Finally, the fact that $2\langle X \rangle  = 0$ in $\widetilde{B}(F)$ follows by [@Suslintwo Lemma 1.2]. ◻
:::

## The map $P_\zeta$

Let $n$ be a positive integer $n$ and $\zeta=\zeta_n\in F_n$ be a primitive $n$th root of unity, which we usually consider as fixed and omit from the notations. Let $F$ be a field of characteristic prime to $n$ and $F_n= F(\zeta_n)$.

Let $\mu = \langle \zeta\rangle$ denote the $\mathrm{Gal}(F_{n}/F)$-module of $n$th roots of unity. Note that $\mu$ naturally has an action of $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ for any Galois extension $E/F$ containing $F_{n}$.

The *universal Kummer extension* is by definition the extension $H/F_n$ obtained by adjoining $n$th roots of every element in $F$. Let $\Phi = \mathrm{Gal}(H/F_n)$. We have [@Lang:algebra Chpt.VI]:

::: lemma
[]{#lemma:easy label="lemma:easy"} The extension $H/F$ is Galois. There is a natural isomorphism $$\phi: F^{\times}/F^{\times n} 
\;\simeq\; \mathrm{Hom}(\Phi,\mu) \;\simeq\; H^1(\Phi,\mu)$$ given by $X \,\mapsto\, (\sigma \in \Phi \mapsto \sigma x/x)$, where $x\in H^{\times}$ is any element that satisfies $x^n= X$, and where $\mathrm{Hom}$ denotes continuous homomorphisms with respect to the usual topology on Galois groups.
:::

By Hilbert's Theorem $90$, these groups are all isomorphic to $H^1(F,\mu)$. The Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(H/F)$ respects these isomorphisms. More explicitly, any $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(H/F)$ acts trivially on $F^{\times}$ and acts on both $\Phi$ and $\mu$ via the cyclotomic character.

Consider the function $$\label{defRz2}

P_\zeta(X) := {\frac{D_\zeta(x)}{D_{\zeta}(1)}} \;\,\in\;H^\times/H^{\times n}
  \qquad(X\in F^\times\smallsetminus\{0,1\},\; x^n=X)\,,$$ where $D_\zeta(x)$ is the cyclic quantum dilogarithm defined in [\[defDz\]](#defDz){reference-type="eqref" reference="defDz"}. (We previously defined $P_{\zeta}(X)$, in Equation [\[defDz\]](#defDz){reference-type="eqref" reference="defDz"} of the introduction, as an element of $H^{\times}$, but only its image modulo $n$th powers was ever used, and it is more canonical to define it in the manner above.) We extend the definition of $P_{\zeta}$ to $X = 1$ and $X = 0$ by the same formula after insisting when $X = 1$ on the choice $x = 1$. In particular, $P_{\zeta}(1) = 1$ and $P_{\zeta}(0) = D_{\zeta}(1)^{-1}$.

::: lemma
[]{#lem.Rz label="lem.Rz"} The functions $P_\zeta, D_{\zeta}:F^\times\to H^\times/H^{\times n}$ have the following properties.

1.  []{#parta.Rz label="parta.Rz"} $P_\zeta(X)$ is independent of the choice of $n$th root $x$ of $X$.

2.  []{#partb.Rz label="partb.Rz"} If $n$ is odd, then $D_\zeta(1)= \zeta^{n/3} \;\text{\rm mod}\;H^{\times n}$, where we interpret this to mean $1 \;\text{\rm mod}\;H^{\times}$ if $(3,n) = 1$. If $n$ is even, then $D_{\zeta}(1)^2$ is a root of unity $\;\text{\rm mod}\;H^{\times n}$.

3.  []{#partc.Rz label="partc.Rz"} $(P_\zeta(X)P_\zeta(1/X))^2=1$ for any $X\in F_n^\times$.

4.  []{#partd.Rz label="partd.Rz"} $P_\zeta(X)\in\;H^\times/H^{\times n}$ is invariant under the action of $\Phi=\mathrm{Gal}(H/F_n)$.

5.  []{#parte.Rz label="parte.Rz"} $\sigma(P_\zeta(X)) = P_\zeta(X)^{\chi^{-1}(\sigma)}$ for all $\sigma\in G$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We begin by establishing an equality for $D_{\zeta}(\zeta^m x)/D_{\zeta}(x)$ which implies part [\[parta.Rz\]](#parta.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="parta.Rz"} and will be useful in the sequel. For $0 \le m \le n-1$, we have an equality $$\frac{D_{\zeta}(\zeta^m x)}{D_{\zeta}(x)} =
\prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{(1 - \zeta^{k+m} x)^k}{(1 - \zeta^k x)^k} \,.$$ Since $\displaystyle{ \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} (1 - \zeta^{k+m} x)^m} = (1-X)^m$, we may also write $$(1 - X)^m \frac{D_{\zeta}(\zeta^m x)}{D_{\zeta}(x)} =
  \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{(1 - \zeta^{k+m} x)^{k+m}}{(1 - \zeta^k x)^k}
  = \prod_{k=0}^{m-1} (1 - \zeta^k x)^n \,,$$ and hence $$\label{referee}
   \frac{D_{\zeta}(\zeta^m x)}{D_{\zeta}(x)}  = \prod_{k=0}^{m-1} \frac{(1 - \zeta^k x)^n}{1 - X} 
   \in H^{\times n},$$ which proves part [\[parta.Rz\]](#parta.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="parta.Rz"}. We note also that this equality holds for *any* $m$, since adding multiples of $n$ to $m$ certainly doesn't change the LHS of equation ([\[referee\]](#referee){reference-type="ref" reference="referee"}) and does not change the RHS since $\prod_{k=0}^{n-1} (1 - \zeta^k x)^n/(1-X) = (1-X)^n/(1-X)^n = 1$. For the remainder of the argument, note that, because $P_\zeta(X)$ is defined only up to $n$th powers, we can use the alternate equality

$$\label{16*}
P_\zeta(X) \;=\;  {\frac{1}{D_{\zeta}(1)}} \prod_{k\;\text{\rm mod}\;n}
\frac
{(1\,-\,\zeta^kx)^k}
{(1\,-\,\zeta^k)^k}
\quad\;\text{\rm mod}\;{H^\times}^n\qquad(x^n=X),$$ for $X \ne 1$, where the product is over $k \not\equiv 0 \bmod n$.

Reversing the order of the product, we deduce the equality (for $n$ odd): $$\label{D1}
D_{\zeta}(1)^2 =
 \prod_{k=1}^{n-1} (1 - \zeta^k)^k (1 - \zeta^{-k})^{n-k}
= \prod_{k=1}^{n-1} (1 - \zeta^k)^n (- \zeta^k)^k = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2} \zeta^{(n-1)n(2n-1)/6} n^n.$$ We note that if $(n,6) = 1$ then $D_{\zeta}(1)^2 =  (-1)^{n(n-1)/2} n^n$ is a perfect $n$th power, and thus, since $n$ is odd, $D_{\zeta}(1)$ is itself a perfect $n$th power. If $3|n$, then $3n | n^2$, and combining the same argument with the formula ([\[D1\]](#D1){reference-type="ref" reference="D1"}) above shows that $D_{\zeta}(1)^2 \equiv  \zeta^{n/6} 
\equiv \zeta^{2n/3} \;\text{\rm mod}\;H^{\times n}$, and thus $D_{\zeta}(1) \equiv \zeta^{n/3} \;\text{\rm mod}\;H^{\times}$. If $n$ is even, then from ([\[D1\]](#D1){reference-type="ref" reference="D1"}) we see that $D_{\zeta}(1)^2$ is transparently a root of unity in $H^{\times}$. This establishes part [\[partb.Rz\]](#partb.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="partb.Rz"}.

Replacing $k$ by $-k$ in the definition of $P_\zeta(1/X)$, gives (working modulo $H^{\times n}$) $${P_{\zeta}(X)P_{\zeta}(1/X)\;=\;\prod_{k = 1}^{n-1}
\frac{ 
(1-\zeta^kx)^k(1-\zeta^{-k}x^{-1})^{-k}}
{(1-\zeta^k)^k(1-\zeta^{-k})^{-k}}
\;=\; \prod_{k= 1}^{n-1} \frac{(-\zeta^kx)^k}{ (-\zeta^k )^k  }  \;=\; x^{n(n-1)/2},}$$ proving part [\[partc.Rz\]](#partc.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="partc.Rz"}.

For part [\[partd.Rz\]](#partd.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="partd.Rz"}, we note that the effect of an element $\sigma\in\Phi$ on $D_\zeta(x)$ is to replace $x$ by $\zeta^i x$ for some $i$, so the result follows from part [\[parta.Rz\]](#parta.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="parta.Rz"}. For part [\[parte.Rz\]](#parte.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="parte.Rz"}, we first observe that the statement makes sense because $\Phi = \mathrm{Gal}(H/F_n)$ is a normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Gal}(H/F)$ and hence acts trivially on $P_\zeta(X)\in H^{\times}/H^{\times n}$ by virtue of [\[partd.Rz\]](#partd.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="partd.Rz"}, so that the quotient $G = \mathrm{Gal}(F_n/F)$ acts on $P_\zeta(X)$. For the proof, we choose a lift of $\sigma \in G$ to $\mathrm{Gal}(H/F)$ that fixes $x$. Then $$\sigma P_\zeta(X) = \prod_k
\frac{(1-\sigma(\zeta)^kx)^k }{(1-\sigma(\zeta)^k )^k }
= 
\prod_k 
 \frac
 {(1-\zeta^{k\chi(\sigma)}x)^k}
 {(1-\zeta^{k\chi(\sigma)})^k}
= \prod_k 
 \frac
 {(1-\zeta^k x)^{k\chi(\sigma)^{-1}}}
 {(1-\zeta^k)^{k\chi(\sigma)^{-1}}}  
=P_\zeta(X)^{\chi(\sigma)^{-1}}\,,$$ where all products are over $k \not\equiv 0 \;\text{\rm mod}\;n$ and all calculations are modulo $H^{\times n}$. ◻
:::

We extend the map $P_\zeta$ to the free abelian group $Z(F)=\mathbf Z[\mathbf P^1(F)]$ by linearity as in [\[defRz\]](#defRz){reference-type="eqref" reference="defRz"}, with $P_{\zeta}(\infty) = P_{\zeta}(0)^{-1} = D_{\zeta}(1)$.

## The map $R_\zeta$ {#sub.Rz}

Let $w_F = w_1(F)$ denote the number of roots of unity in $F$. The assumption in Theorem [\[thm.R\]](#thm.R){reference-type="ref" reference="thm.R"} that $F$ contains no non-trivial $n$th roots of unity is the assumption that $(n,w_F) = 1$. The next proposition associates an element $R_\zeta(\xi) \in (F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n})^{\chi^{-1}}$ to every element of $A(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)$ as long as $(n,w_F)=1$.

More generally,

when $(n,w_F) > 1$, we may define an element $R_{\zeta}(\xi)^{w_F}
\in (F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n})^{\chi^{-1}}$ which coincides with the $w_F$th power of $R_{\zeta}(\xi)$ whenever $(n,w_F)=1$.

Recall the group $A(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)$ from subsection [\[sub.intro.bloch\]](#sub.intro.bloch){reference-type="ref" reference="sub.intro.bloch"}.

::: proposition
[]{#prop.Pdef label="prop.Pdef"} Let $\xi \in A(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)$.

1.  []{#newparta label="newparta"} The image of $P_\zeta(\xi)^{w_F}$ lifts to $F_n^\times/F_n^{\times n}$.

2.  []{#newpartb label="newpartb"} The image of $P_{\zeta}(\xi)^{w_F}$ admits a unique lift to $F_n^\times/F_n^{\times n}$ on which $G$ acts by $\chi^{-1}$, that we denote by $R_{\zeta}(\xi)^{w_F}$. If $n$ is prime to $w_F$, then $P_{\zeta}(\xi)$ itself admits a unique lift $R_\zeta(\xi) \in (F^{\times}_{n}/F^{\times n}_{n})^{\chi^{-1}}$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* For part [\[newparta\]](#newparta){reference-type="ref" reference="newparta"}, by Hilbert $90$ and inflation-restriction, there is a commutative diagram: $$\begin{diagram}
H^1(\Phi,\mu) & \rTo & H^1(F_n,\mu) & \rTo & H^1(H,\mu)^{\Phi} 
& \rTo^{\delta} & H^2(\Phi,\mu) \\
 & & \dEquals & & \dEquals & & \\
  & & F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n} 
& \rTo & \left(H^{\times}/H^{\times n}\right)^{\Phi} & & 
\end{diagram}$$ That is, there is an obstruction to descending from $\left(H^{\times}/H^{\times n}\right)^{\Phi}$ to $F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}$ which lands in $H^2(\Phi,\mu)$.

We now claim that there is a commutative diagram as follows: $$\begin{diagram}
Z(F) & \rTo^{P_\zeta\qquad} &  
(H^{\times}/H^{\times n})^\Phi \\ \dTo^d  &  & \dTo_\delta \\
\mbox{\small$\bigwedge^2$}(F^{\times}/F^{\times n}) & \rInto^{\quad \cup} & H^2(\Phi,\mu) \;,
\end{diagram}$$ where the left vertical map is the one defined in [\[defd\]](#defd){reference-type="eqref" reference="defd"} and the bottom horizontal map is the map induced by the cup product from the isomorphism $F^{\times}/F^{\times n} \rightarrow H^1(\Phi,\mu)$ of Lemma [\[lemma:easy\]](#lemma:easy){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:easy"}. Note that the cup product is more naturally a map $\mbox{\small$\bigwedge^2$}H^1(\Phi,\mu)\rightarrow H^2(\Phi,\mu^{\otimes 2})$, but can be interpreted as in the theorem by using the trivialization $\mu \simeq \mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\simeq\mu^{\otimes 2}$ defined by the choice of the root of unity $\zeta$.

We now show that the above diagram commutes. By linearity, it suffices to prove this for elements $\xi$ of the form $[X]$. Write $X=x^n$ and $1-X=y^n$. For $Z \in F^{\times}/F^{\times n}$ and $z^n = Z$, let (following Lemma [\[lemma:easy\]](#lemma:easy){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:easy"}), we may write $$\sigma(z) = \zeta^{\phi(z,\sigma)} z.$$ where $\phi(z,\sigma)$ is defined to satisfy $\zeta^{\phi(z,\sigma)} = \phi(z)(\sigma) \in \mu$. By definition, we have $P_{\zeta}([X]) = {D_\zeta(x)/D_{\zeta}(1)}$ modulo $n$th powers. Since $D_{\zeta}(1) = \zeta^{n/3}$ already lifts to $F^{\times}_{n}/F^{\times n}_{n}$, the obstruction to lifting $P_{\zeta}([X])$ is the same as the obstruction to lifting $D_{\zeta}(x)$. Lifting $D_{\zeta}(x)$ amounts to finding an element $u \in H^{\times}$ such that $D_\zeta(x)/u^{n}  \in F_n^{\times}$. In light of equation ([\[referee\]](#referee){reference-type="ref" reference="referee"}), such a $u$ would necessarily satisfy $$\label{defineh}
\left(\frac{\sigma u}{u}\right)^n= \frac{\sigma D_\zeta(x)}{D_\zeta(x)}
\;=\; \frac{D_\zeta(\zeta^{\phi(x,\sigma)} x)}{D_\zeta(x)} \;=\;
\biggl(\,\prod_{k=0}^{\phi(x,\sigma) - 1} \frac{1- \zeta^k x}{y}\,\biggr)^n.$$ The expression inside the $n$th power is determined exactly modulo $\mu = \langle \zeta\rangle$. Hence we may define a cocycle $$h = h_{X}: \Phi \;\to\; H^\times/\mu\,, \qquad h(\sigma)\;:=\;
\prod_{k=0}^{\phi(x,\sigma)-1}\frac{1- \zeta^k x}{y} \;.$$ To verify that $h$ is a cocycle, it suffices to show that $h(\sigma \tau) = h(\sigma) \sigma h(\tau) \in H^{\times}/\mu$. This identity holds for $h^n$ thought of as valued in $H^{\times}$ by equation ([\[defineh\]](#defineh){reference-type="ref" reference="defineh"}), since in that formula it is manifestly given by the coboundary $\sigma \mapsto \sigma 
D_\zeta(x)/D_\zeta(x)$. But this implies immediately that $h$ itself satisfies this equation modulo $\mu$. Thus $h$ gives an element of $H^1(\Phi,H^\times/\mu)$, which by consideration of the exact sequence $$H^1(\Phi,H^{\times}) \longrightarrow H^1(\Phi,H^{\times}/\mu) 
\longrightarrow H^2(\Phi,\mu)$$ maps to $H^2(\Phi,\mu)$. This is actually an injection, because the first term vanishes by Hilbert 90. This is the image of $\delta$; explicitly, the class $\delta(h) \in H^2(\Phi,\mu)$ (or its inverse, depending on one's convention for the boundary map) is given by $$\begin{aligned}
\delta(h)(\sigma,\tau) =  & 
\ \frac{h(\sigma \tau)}{h(\sigma) \sigma h(\tau)} \\
= & 
\ \frac{1}{h(\sigma) \sigma h(\tau)} 
\prod_{k=0}^{\phi(x,\sigma) + \phi(x,\tau) - 1} \frac{1 - \zeta^k x}{y} \\
= & 
\ \frac{1}{h(\sigma)  \sigma h(\tau)} 
\prod_{k=0}^{\phi(x,\sigma)  - 1} \frac{1 - \zeta^k x}{y}
\prod_{k=0}^{\phi(x,\tau)  - 1} \frac{1 - \zeta^k \zeta^{\phi(x,\sigma)} x}{y}
\\
 = & \ \frac{1}{h(\sigma) \sigma h(\tau)} 
\prod_{k=0}^{\phi(x,\sigma)  - 1} \frac{1 - \zeta^k x}{y}
\prod_{k=0}^{\phi(x,\tau)  - 1} 
\frac{1 - \zeta^k \zeta^{\phi(x,\sigma)} x}{\zeta^{\phi(y,\sigma)} y} \cdot
\zeta^{\phi(y,\sigma)} 
\\
 = & \ \zeta^{\phi(x,\tau) \phi(y,\sigma)}
\end{aligned}$$

On the other hand, the class in $H^1(\Phi,\mu)$ associated to $X = x^n$ is the map $\tau \mapsto \zeta^{\phi(x,\tau)}$, and the class associated to $1-X = y^n$ is the map $\sigma \mapsto \zeta^{\phi(y,\sigma)}$, and the exterior product of these two classes in $H^2(\Phi,\zeta)$ is precisely $\delta(h)$. The fact that the cup product gives an injection is an easy fact about the cohomology of abelian groups of exponent $n$. This concludes the proof of part [\[newparta\]](#newparta){reference-type="ref" reference="newparta"}.

For part [\[newpartb\]](#newpartb){reference-type="ref" reference="newpartb"}, suppose that $\xi \in A(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)$. By the argument above, there certainly exists an element in $F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}$ which maps to $P_\zeta(\xi)$. Let $M$ denote the image of $F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}$ in $(H^{\times}/H^{\times n})^{\Phi}$, and let $S = F^{\times}/F^{\times n}$. We have a short exact sequence as follows: $$0 \longrightarrow S \longrightarrow F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}
\longrightarrow M \longrightarrow 0.$$ Taking $\chi^{-1}$-invariants is the same as tensoring with $\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z(1)$ and taking invariants. Hence there is an exact sequence $$\left(F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}\right)^{\chi^{-1}} \longrightarrow
M^{\chi^{-1}} \longrightarrow H^1(G,S(1)).$$ In particular, the obstruction to lifting to a $\chi^{-1}$-invariant element lies in $H^1(G,S(1))$, and it suffices to prove that this group is annihilated by $w_F$. By construction, the module $S$ is trivial as a $G$-module, and hence the action of $G$ on $S(1)$ is via the character $\chi$. Sah's Lemma ([@Lang Lem.8.8.1])

implies that the self-map of $H^1(G,S(1))$ induced by $g-1$ for any $g \in Z(G) = G$ is the zero map. On the other hand, since $\chi: G \rightarrow (\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^{\times}$ is the cyclotomic character, the greatest common divisor of $\chi(g) - 1$ for $g \in G$ is $w_F  \mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z$. In particular, the group is annihilated by $w_F$. The result follows. ◻
:::

::: remark
[]{#rem.effectiveR label="rem.effectiveR"}

Suppose $(w_F,n)=1$, and let $P \in H^\times$ be a representative of $P_\zeta(\xi) \in H^\times/H^{\times n}$. Then the construction of the element $R_\zeta(\xi)$ whose existence is asserted by Proposition [\[prop.Pdef\]](#prop.Pdef){reference-type="ref" reference="prop.Pdef"} reduces to the problem of finding $S \in H^\times$ such that

1.  $P/S^n\in F_n^\times$, and

2.  the image of $P/S^n$ in $F_n^\times/F_n^{\times n}$ lies in the $\chi^{-1}$-eigenspace,

since then $R_\zeta(\xi) = P/S^n\in (F_n^\times/F_n^{\times n})^{\chi^{-1}}$. In practice, $S$ will be constructed via a Hilbert 90 argument as an additive Galois average, and the difficulty is ensuring that $S \neq 0$. See Section [\[sec.nahm\]](#sec.nahm){reference-type="ref" reference="sec.nahm"}, where this is done for a particular $P$ constructed as a radial limit of a Nahm sum.
:::
