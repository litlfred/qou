[]{#sub.intro.bloch label="sub.intro.bloch"}

We first recall the definition of the classical Bloch group, as introduced in [@Bloch]. Let $Z(F)$ denote the free abelian group on $\mathbf P^1(F)=F\cup\{\infty\}$, i.e. the group of formal finite combinations $\xi=\sum_i n_i [X_i]$ with $n_i\in\mathbf Z$ and $X_i\in\mathbf P^1(F)$.

::: definition
[]{#def.BF label="def.BF"} The *Bloch group* of a field $F$ is the quotient $$\label{defBF}
B(F)=A(F)/C(F)\,,$$ where $A(F)$ is the kernel of the map

$$\label{defd}
d:\,Z(F)\;\longrightarrow\;\mbox{\small$\bigwedge^2$}F^{\times}\,, \qquad [X]\;\mapsto\; (X) \wedge (1-X)$$ (and $[0],\,[1],\,[\infty]\mapsto0$) and $C(F)\subseteq A(F)$ the group generated by the *five-term relation* $$\label{5term}
\xi_{X,Y} \;=\; [X] \,-\,[Y] \,+\,  \biggl[ \frac{Y}{X}\biggr] 
\,-\,\biggl[\frac{1-X^{-1}}{1-Y^{-1}}\biggr] \,+\, \biggl[\frac{1-X}{1-Y} \biggr]$$ with $X$ and $Y$ ranging over $\mathbf P^1(F)$ (but forbidding arguments $\frac00$ or $\frac\infty\infty$ on the right-hand side).

We remark that there are a number of different definitions of the Bloch group in the literature which usually agree up to $6$-torsion. With our convention, the relations $[1]=3\,[0]=[X]+[1/X]=[X]+[1-X]-[0]=0$ hold in $B(F)$ for all $X$. Our Bloch group is a quotient of Suslin's Bloch group $\widetilde{B}(F)$ by a subgroup of exponent $2$ (see Lemma [\[suslincomparison\]](#suslincomparison){reference-type="ref" reference="suslincomparison"}).
:::

In this paper, we will study an invariant of the Bloch group whose values are units in $F_n$ modulo $n$th powers of units, where $n$ is a natural number and $F_n$ the field obtained by adjoining to $F$ a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta=\zeta_n$. The extension $F_n/F$ is Galois with Galois group $G=\mathrm{Gal}(F_n/F)$, and $G$ admits a canonical map $$\label{eq.chi}
\chi:\,G \;\longrightarrow\;(\mathbf Z/n \mathbf Z)^{\times}$$ determined by $\sigma\zeta= \zeta^{\chi(\sigma)}$. The powers $\chi^j$ ($j\in\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z$) of this character define eigenspaces $\bigl(F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}\bigr)^{\chi^j}$ in the obvious way as the set of $x\in F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}$ such that $\sigma(x)=x^{\chi^j(\sigma)}$ for all $\sigma\in G$, and similarly for $(\mathcal{O}_n^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_n^{\times n})^{\chi^j}$ or $(\mathcal{O}_{S,n}^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{S,n}^{\times n})^{\chi^j}$, where $\mathcal{O}_n$ (resp. $\mathcal{O}_{S,n}$) is the ring of integers (resp. $S$-integers) of $F_n$. Our main result is the following theorem.

::: theorem
[]{#thm.R label="thm.R"} Suppose that $F$ does not contain any non-trivial $n$th root of unity. Then there is a map $$\label{eq.Rmap}  
R_\zeta:\, B(F)/nB(F)\;\longrightarrow\;
\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{S,n}^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{S,n}^{\times n}\bigr)^{\chi^{-1}}
\;\subset\;\bigl(F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}\bigr)^{\chi^{-1}}$$ for some finite set $S$ of primes depending only on $F$. If $n$ is prime to a certain integer $M_F$ depending on $F$, then the map $R_\zeta$ is injective and its image is contained in $\bigl(\mathcal{O}_n^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_n^{\times n}\bigr)^{\chi^{-1}}$, and equal to this if $n$ is prime.
:::

The map $R_{\zeta}$ satisfies various natural compatibilities as one varies either $n$ or the field $F$; see Lemmas [\[lemma:compatibility\]](#lemma:compatibility){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:compatibility"} and [\[lemma:compatibility2\]](#lemma:compatibility2){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:compatibility2"}.

::: remark
Note that the field $F_n$ and the character $\chi$ of [\[eq.chi\]](#eq.chi){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq.chi"} depend only on $n$ and not on the primitive $n$th root of unity $\zeta$. The map $R_\zeta$ from $B(F)$ to $F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}$ does depend on $\zeta$, but in a very simple way, described by either of the formulas $$\label{eq.2actions}
\sigma\bigl(R_\zeta(\xi)\bigr)\,=\,R_{\sigma(\zeta)}(\xi)\;\quad(\sigma\in G),\qquad
R_\zeta(\xi) \,=\,R_{\zeta^k}(\xi)^k \quad\;(k\in(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times)\,,$$ where the simultaneous validity of these two formulas explains why the image of each map $R_\zeta$ lies in the $\chi^{-1}$ eigenspace of $F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}$.
:::

::: remark
[]{#quotableremark label="quotableremark"} The optimal definition of $M_F$ is somewhat complicated to state. However, one may take it to be $6\,\Delta_F\,|K_2(\mathcal{O}_F)|$. When $n$ is not divisible by $9$, one may take $M_F$ to be $2 \,\Delta_F\,|K_2(\mathcal{O}_F)|$. (Both assertions are proved in §[\[sub.thm.c\]](#sub.thm.c){reference-type="ref" reference="sub.thm.c"}.)
:::

The detailed construction of the map $R_\zeta$ will be given in Section 2. A rough description is as follows. Let $\xi=\sum n_i[X_i]$ be an element of $Z(F)$ whose image in $\wedge^2(F^\times/F^{\times n})$ under the map induced by $d$ vanishes. We define an algebraic number $P_\zeta(\xi)$ by the formula $$\label{defRz}
 P_\zeta(\xi) \;=\; \prod_i {\frac{D_\zeta(x_i)^{n_i}}{D_{\zeta}(1)^{n_i}}} \,,$$ where $x_i$ is some $n$th root of $X_i$ and $D_\zeta(x)$ is the *cyclic quantum dilogarithm function* $$\label{defDz}
 D_\zeta(x) \;=\; \prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1\,-\,\zeta^k x)^k\,.$$ The number $P_\zeta(\xi)$ belongs to the Kummer extension $H_\xi$ of $F$ defined by adjoining all of the $x_i$ to $F_n$ and is well-defined modulo $H_\xi^n$. We show that for $n$ prime to some $M_F$ it has the form $ab^{n}$ with $b$ in $H_\xi^\times$ and $a\in F_n^\times$ (or even $a\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_n$ under a sufficiently strong coprimality assumption about $n$). Then $R_\zeta(\xi)$ is defined as the image of $a$ modulo $n$th powers.
