[]{#sub.5term label="sub.5term"}

In this section, we use a result of Kashaev, Mangazeev and Stroganov to show that the map $R_\zeta$ satisfies the $5$-term relation, and consequently descends to a map of the group $B(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)$.

::: theorem
[]{#thm.5term label="thm.5term"} Let $F$ be a field and $F_n=F(\zeta)$, where $\zeta$ is a root of unity of order $n$ prime to $w_F$ and to the characteristic prime of $F$. Then the map $R_\zeta$ vanishes on the subgroup $C(F) \subset A(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z) \subset Z(F)$ generated by the $5$-term relation, and therefore induces a map $$B(F) \longrightarrow  B(F)/nB(F) \longrightarrow B(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)
\stackrel{R_\zeta}{\longrightarrow} 
\bigl(F_n^{\times}/F_n^{\times n}\bigr)^{\chi^{-1}} \,.$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Denote by $H$ the universal Kummer extension as before. Then it suffices to show that the appropriate product of the functions $D_\zeta$ is a perfect $n$th power in $H$.

It suffices to consider the case when $X, Y \ne 0,1,\infty$ and $X \ne Y$, since, when $n$ is odd, the groups $B(F)/nB(F)$ and $B(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)$ coincide with Suslin's Bloch group $\widetilde{B}(F)/n\widetilde{B}(F)$ and the group $\widetilde{B}(F;\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)$ respectively by Lemma [\[suslincomparison\]](#suslincomparison){reference-type="ref" reference="suslincomparison"}. Let $X,\,Y,\,Z\in F^\times$ be related by $Z=(1-X)/(1-Y)$, and choose $n$th roots $x,\,y,\,z$ of $X,\,Y,\,Z$. Using the standard notation $(x;q)_k=(1-x)(1-qx)\cdots(1-q^{k-1}x)$ ($q$-Pochhammer symbol) and following the notation of [@KMS] (except that they use $w(x|k)$ for $(x\zeta;\zeta)_k^{-1}$), we set $$f(x,\,y\,|\,z) = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{(\zeta y;\zeta)_k}{(\zeta x;\zeta)_k} 
= \sum_{k \;\text{\rm mod}\;n} \frac{(\zeta y;\zeta)_k}{(\zeta x;\zeta)_k}\, 
z^k\;\in H,$$ where the second equality follows from the relation between $x$, $y$, and $z$. By Equation (C.7) of [@KMS], we have $$(\zeta y)^{n(1-n)/2} \, f(x,\,y\,|\,z)^n
\;=\; \frac{D_\zeta(1) D_\zeta(y \zeta/x) D_\zeta(x/y z)}
{D_\zeta(1/x) D_\zeta(y \zeta) D_\zeta(\zeta/z)}.$$

Considering this modulo  $n$th powers, and using Lemma [\[lem.Rz\]](#lem.Rz){reference-type="ref" reference="lem.Rz"}, we find

$$1 \;=\; P_\zeta(X)\,P_\zeta(Y)^{-1}\,P_\zeta(Y/X)\,P_\zeta(YZ/X)^{-1}\,P_\zeta(Z)\,.$$ This is precisely the 5-term relation for the map $P_\zeta$, and the uniqueness clause in Proposition [\[prop.Pdef\]](#prop.Pdef){reference-type="ref" reference="prop.Pdef"} implies the same 5-term relation for the map $R_\zeta$. ◻
:::
