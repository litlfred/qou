sedenions
The CD process is an iterative construction that generates at
each stage an algebra (with involution) of dimension twice
that of the previous. Each algebra is constructed as a direct
sum of the previous algebra, so that C = R⊕Ri where i is the
complex structure introduced in the process. Similarly, H =
C⊕CJ, where i, J and i J are identiﬁed with the quaternion
imaginary bases I, J, K, and similarly O = H ⊕Hi4.
This process does not terminate with O but continues,
generating a series of 2n-dimensional (non division) alge-
bras. A generic element of the CD algebra An, can then
be written as a + bu, where a, b ∈An−1, and u is the
new imaginary unit introduced by the CD process applied
to An−1. The ﬁfth CD algebra A4 (A0 = R), generated
from O, is the 16-dimensional algebra of sedenions S. This
algebra is non-commutative, non-associative, and not even
alternative (x(xy)̸ = (xx)y and y(xx)̸ = (yx)x in general).
Other properties, like ﬂexibility ((xy)x = x(yx)) and power-
associativity (xn associative), still hold (and hold for all CD
algebras).
An orthonormal basis for S comprises 15 mutually anti-
commuting imaginary units s1, . . . , s15 together with the unit
1 = s0. The imaginary units s1, .., s7 correspond to the orig-
inal octonion units i1, . . . , i7. A general sedenion may then
be written as
w = w0s0 + w1s1 + · · · + w15s15,
w0, . . . , w15 ∈R.
with the sedenion conjugate w deﬁned as w = w0s0−w1s1−
· · · −w15s15, and the sedenion norm |w| deﬁned by |w|2 =
ww = ww. Whenever |w|2̸ = 0, the inverse of w is given
by w−1 = w/|w|2.
The product of two sedenions w, v can be determined
using the multiplication table of the orthonormal basis units
of S provided in Appendix A, source [40,42], together with
linearity.
Because S is not a division algebra, it contains zero divi-
sors. These are elements of the form
(sa + sb)(sc + sd) = 0,
sa, sb, sc, sd ∈S.
There are 84 such zero divisors, and the subspace of zero
divisors of unit norm is homeomorphic to G2 [43].
