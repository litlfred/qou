## Hopf algebra $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ {#qsl2}

Let $v$ be a formal parameter and consider the $\mathbb{Q}(v)$-algebra $U_v(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ generated by $E, F, K^{\pm1}$ with relations $$K K^{-1} = K^{-1}K =1 , \quad \quad K E = v^2 E K , \quad \quad K F = v^{-2} F K ,$$ $$EF - F E  = \frac{K - K^{-1}}{v - v^{-1}} .$$ This is a Hopf algebra with the coproduct, counit and the antipode given by $$\Delta E = E \otimes 1 + K \otimes E , \quad \quad \Delta F = F \otimes K^{-1} + 1 \otimes F, \quad \quad \Delta K^{\pm 1}  = K^{\pm 1} \otimes K^{\pm 1} ,$$ $$\varepsilon(E) = \varepsilon(F) = 0, \quad \quad \varepsilon(K^{\pm 1}) = 1  .$$ $$S(E) = -K^{-1} E, \quad \quad S(F) = -FK, \quad \quad S(K) = K^{-1} .$$

Set $\mathcal A= \mathbb{Z}[v, v^{-1}]$ and denote by $U_\mathcal A(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ the $\mathcal A$-subalgebra of $U_v(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ with $1$ generated by $$E^{(n)} = \frac{E^n}{[n]!},   \quad F^{(n)} = \frac{F^n}{[n]!}$$ for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$. For any $t \geq 0$, $c \in \mathbb{Z}$ let $$\left[  \begin{array}{c}  K;\; c \\ t  \end{array}  \right] = \prod_{s=1}^t \frac{K v^{c-s+1} - K^{-1} v^{-c+s-1}}{v^s - v^{-s}}  \in U_\mathcal A(\mathfrak{sl}_2) .$$

We have in $U_\mathcal A(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ [@Lus89]: $$E^{(n)} E^{(m)} = \left[  \begin{array}{c}  n+m \\ m  \end{array}  \right] E^{(n+m)} , \quad \quad F^{(n)} F^{(m)} = \left[  \begin{array}{c}  n+m \\ m  \end{array}  \right] F^{(n+m)} .$$ $$E^{(p)} F^{(r)} = \sum_{0 \leq t \leq p,r} F^{(r-t)} \left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; \; 2t-p-r \\ t  \end{array}  \right] E^{(p-t)} ,  \quad  p, r \geq 0.$$

Let $q = e^{\frac{\pi im}{l}}$ be a primitive $2l$-th root of unity, where we assume $l$ to be odd and $m$ and $l$ to be mutually prime. We have $q^l =-1$, $q^{2l} =1$. Let $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2) = U_\mathcal A(\mathfrak{sl}_2) \otimes_{\mathcal A} \mathbb{Q}[q,q^{-1}]$, where $\mathcal A$ acts by $v \rightarrow q$. This is the big quantum group with divided powers specialized at $q$.

::: proposition
[]{#E^lp label="E^lp"}

1.  Let $p \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$. Then we have in $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$: $$E^{(lp)} = (-1)^{p(p-1)/2} \; \frac{(E^{(l)})^p}{p!}  .$$

2.  Let $m = m_1 l + m_0$, where $0 \leq m_0 \leq l-1$, and $m_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, then $$E^{(lm_1 + m_0)} = (-1)^{m_1(m_1-1)/2+ m_1m_0} \frac{E^{\,m_0}}{[m_0]!} \; \frac{(E^{(l)})^{m_1}}{m_1!}  .$$ A similar relation holds for $F^{(m_1 l + m_0)}$.

3.  The algebra $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is generated over $\mathbb{Q}(q)$ by $E, E^{(l)}, F, F^{(l)}, K^{\pm 1}$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Part (a) follows from Corollary [\[q-bin2\]](#q-bin2){reference-type="ref" reference="q-bin2"}, and (b) from Proposition [\[q-bin1\]](#q-bin1){reference-type="ref" reference="q-bin1"}, then (c) is a direct consequence of (b). ◻
:::

::: lemma
In $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ we have $K^{2l}=1$ and $K^l$ is central.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* By definition we have in $U_\mathcal A(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ $$\prod_{s=1}^l (K v^{-s+1} - K^{-1} v^{s-1}) = \prod_{s=1}^l (v^s - v^{-s}) \cdot \left[  \begin{array}{c}  K; \; 0 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] .$$ Specializing $v \to q$ we obtain zero in the right-hand side, since $q^l - q^{-l} = -1 -(-1) =0$, and therefore we have in $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ $$\prod_{s=1}^l (K q^{-s+1} - K^{-1} q^{s-1}) = K^{-l}  q^{-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}} \prod_{s=1}^l (K^2 - q^{2s-2}) =  K^{-l}  q^{-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}} (K^{2l} -q^{l(l-1)} ) = 0 .$$ As we have $q^{l(l-1)} = (-1)^{l-1} =1$ since $l$ is odd. Therefore $K^{2l} =1$ in $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$.

We have $K^l E^{(p)} = q^{2lp} E^{(p)} K^l = E^{(p)} K^l$ and $K^l F^{(p)} = q^{-2lp} F^{(p)} K^l = F^{(p)} K^l$ for any $p \geq 0$, therefore $K^l$ is central in $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. ◻
:::

## Weyl, dual Weyl and tilting modules {#modules}

There is a unique structure of $U_v(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ module on $U_v(\mathfrak{sl}_2)^-$, where $F$ acts by left multiplication, $E\cdot 1 =0$, $K \cdot 1 = v^m 1$ for some $m \in \mathbb{Z}$. (The assignment $K \cdot 1 =  v^m 1$ leads to the definition of modules of type ${\bf 1}$. If we chose $K \cdot 1 = -v^m 1$, we would obtain modules of type ${\bf -1}$, that exhibit similar properties. In what follows we will consider only modules of type ${\bf 1}$). This module is denoted by $Y(m)$ and it has a unique simple quotient $W(m)$. Then we can consider the $U_{\mathcal A}$-modules of $Y_{\mathcal A}(m)$ and $W_{\mathcal A}(m)$, and finally specialize them to $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ by a change of base. We will use the same notations $Y(m)$ and $W(m)$ for the obtained $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-modules. Let $x$ denote the image of $1$ in $W(m)$, then $E \cdot x = E^{(l)} \cdot x =0$, and $K x = q^m x$, $\left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; 0 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] = \left[  \begin{array}{c}  m \\ l  \end{array}  \right] x$. By Corollary [\[q-bin3\]](#q-bin3){reference-type="ref" reference="q-bin3"} the action of $K$ and $\left[  \begin{array}{c}  K;0 \\ l  \end{array}  \right]$ determine $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ uniquely, therefore we can say that $x$ has weight $m \in \mathbb{Z}$.

If $m \geq 0$, then $W(m)$ is finite dimensional of dimension $m+1$ and with character given by the Weyl formula (its weight structure comes from that of the simple module over $U_v(\mathfrak{sl}_2)^-$).

Now we will present the explicit action of the generators of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ in the modules $W(m)$, $m \geq 0$. We can consider the following basis in $W(m)$: $$W(m)  = \{ x, F^{(1)}x,  F^{(2)}x,  \ldots  F^{(m)} x \},$$ where $E x = 0, E^{(l)} x =0, Kv = q^m x$. Using the commutation relations, we derive the following action of the generators $E, E^{(l)}, F, F^{(l)}, K$ on the basis: $$F \cdot F^{(t)} x= [ t+1] F^{(t+1)} x ; \quad \quad  E \cdot F^{(t)} x=  [m-t+1] F^{(t-1)} x$$ $$K \cdot  F^{(t)} x =  q^{m-2t} F^{(t)} x, \quad \quad \left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; 0 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] F^{(t)} x =  \left[  \begin{array}{c}  m-2t \\ l  \end{array}  \right] F^{(t)} x$$ $$F^{(l)} \cdot F^{(t)} x = \left[  \begin{array}{c}  l+t \\ l  \end{array}  \right] F^{(l+t)} x,$$ $$E^{(l)} F^{(t)} x =  \left[  \begin{array}{c}  m+l-t \\ l  \end{array}  \right] F^{(t-l)} x .$$ Here we assume that $F^{(<0)}x = 0, F^{(>m)}x =0$. The module $W(m)$ has a unique simple quotient $L(m)$. Therefore, for any $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ there exists a simple module $L(m)$ of type ${\bf 1}$ with a primitive vector of weight $m$.

We can also define the dual Weyl module by setting $W^*(m) = {\rm Hom}(W(m), \mathbb{Q}(q))$ for any $m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$. In particular, we have that if $Kv = q^k v$ for $v \in W(m)$, then $Kv^* = q^{-k} v$ for $v^* \in W^*(m)$.

The properties of modules $L(m), W(m), W^*(m)$ are investigated in detail in [@Lus89], [@AT] ,[@APW1], section 4. Here is a summary of their properties:

::: theorem
[@AT], section 2. []{#Weyl label="Weyl"} For each $m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ there exists a Weyl module $W(m)$, dual Weyl module $W^*(m)$ over $U_q({\mathfrak{s}l}_2)$ defined above that have the following properties:

1.  $W(m) \simeq W^*(m) \simeq L(m)$ if and only if either $0 \leq m \leq l-2$, or $m = lm_1 -1$.

2.  If $m = lm_1 + m_0$ with $0 \leq m_0 \leq l-2$ and $m_1 \geq 0$, then we have the short exact sequences of $U_q({\mathfrak{s}l}_2)$-modules: $$0 \longrightarrow L(lm_1 - m_0 -2) \longrightarrow W(m)  \longrightarrow L(m) \longrightarrow 0 ,$$ $$0 \longrightarrow L(m) \longrightarrow W^*(m) \longrightarrow L(m_1 l -m_0 -2) \longrightarrow 0 .$$
:::

::: definition
A tilting module $T$ (of type ${\bf 1}$) over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is a finite dimensional module that has a filtration by Weyl modules and a filtration by dual Weyl modules. $$0 \subset  M_1 \subset M_2 \subset ... \subset M_n = T , \quad \quad M_i / M_{i-1} \cong W_{k(i)}  ,$$ $$0 \subset Q_1 \subset Q_2 \subset  ... \subset Q_n = T , \quad \quad Q_i / Q_{i-1} \cong W_{m(i)}^* ,$$ for some $k(i)$, $m(i) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$.
:::

It follows from the definition that a dual of a tilting module is also tilting.

::: theorem
[@AT], Section 2. []{#tilt_prop1 label="tilt_prop1"}

1.  For each $k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ there exists a unique indecomposable tilting module $T(k)$ of type ${\bf 1}$ such that $T(k) : W(k)=  1$ and $T(k) : W(k') =0$ for $k' >k$. These modules form a complete list of inequivalent indecomposable tilting modules of type ${\bf 1}$ over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$.

2.  A tensor product, a direct sum and a direct summand of tilting modules is a tilting module.
:::

Thus the tilting modules over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ form an additive category, closed under finite direct sums, finite tensor products and taking the dual.

::: theorem
[@AT], []{#isomLW label="isomLW"} Proposition 2.20. []{#tilt_prop2 label="tilt_prop2"}

1.  We have $T(k) \cong W(k) \cong W(k)^* \cong L(k)$ if and only if either $0 \leq k \leq l-2$, or $k = lk_1 -1$ for some $k_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}$.

2.  For $k = l k_1 + k_0$, $k_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}$, $0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2$ we have the short exact sequence of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-modules $$0 \longrightarrow  W(lk_1+k_0 ) \longrightarrow T(lk_1 + k_0) \longrightarrow W(l k_1 -2 - k_0) \longrightarrow 0 .$$ Since $T_k^* \simeq T_k$, we also have $$0 \longrightarrow  W^*(lk_1- 2 -k_0 ) \longrightarrow T(lk_1 + k_0) \longrightarrow W^*(l k_1 + k_0) \longrightarrow 0 .$$
:::

::: corollary
[]{#tilt_char label="tilt_char"} The character of an indecomposable tilting module over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is $${\rm{ch}}_{T(k)} = {\rm{ch}}_{W(k)} , \quad \quad {\rm if} \quad  0 \leq k \leq l-2 \quad {\rm or} \quad k = lk_1-1, \;\; k_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0},$$ $${\rm{ch}}_{T(lk_1 + k_0)} = {\rm{ch}}_{W(lk_1 + k_0)} + {\rm{ch}}_{W(lk_1 - 2 - k_0)} , \quad \quad  k_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}, \;\; 0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2.$$
:::

## Tensor product theorem for simple modules of type $1$ over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ {#tpthm}

Let $u_q = u_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ be the subalgebra of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ generated by $E,F, K^{\pm 1}$.

::: proposition
[]{#Lm0 label="Lm0"} For $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $0 \leq m \leq l-1$, consider the simple $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ module $L(m)$, and let $x \in L(m)$ be a nonzero vector such that $K x = q^m x$ and $Ex =0$. Then

1.  $L(m) = u_q \cdot x$.

2.  The restriction of $L(m)$ to $u_q$ is a simple $u_q$ module.

3.  $E^{(l)} , F^{(l)}$ act as $0$ in $L(m)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* This follows from the fact that $W(m) \simeq L(m)$ for $0 \leq m \leq l-1$ by Theorem [\[Weyl\]](#Weyl){reference-type="ref" reference="Weyl"} and the explicit definition of $W(m)$. From the same definition we have that $E^{(l)}$ and $F^{(l)}$ act by $0$ on any vector in $L(m)$. ◻
:::

Let $\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ be the subalgebra of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ generated by $E^{(l)}$, $F^{(l)}$.

::: proposition
[]{#L(lm) label="L(lm)"} Let $m = lm_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, and consider the simple $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ module $L(m)$, and $x \in L(m)$ be such that $\left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; 0 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] x = \left[  \begin{array}{c}  l m_1 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] x$ and $E^{(l)}x =0$. Then

1.  $E, F, K - (-1)^{m_1}$ act as $0$ in $L(m)$.

2.  $L(m) = \mathcal{U} \cdot x$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Define inductively the subspaces in $L(m)$ by setting $V_0 = \mathbb{C}\cdot x$, $V_k = F^{(l)} V_{k-1} + E^{(l)} V_{k-1}$. We will prove by induction that $EV_k = FV_k=0$.

By Theorem [\[Weyl\]](#Weyl){reference-type="ref" reference="Weyl"} we have $$0 \longrightarrow L(lm_1 -2) \longrightarrow W(l m_1)  \longrightarrow L(lm_1) \longrightarrow 0 .$$ Taking into account the definition of $W(l m_1)$, we have that $E \tilde{x} =0$, $F \tilde{x} \in L(lm_1 -2)$, where $\tilde{x}$ is a lift of $x \in L(lm_1)$ to $W(lm_1)$. Therefore, we have $Ex = Fx =0$, and $E V_0 = F V_0 = 0$.

Now consider $V_k = F^{(l)} V_{k-1} + E^{(l)} V_{k-1}$. In $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ we have ([@Lus89], 4.1): $$F E^{(l)}  = E^{(l)} F - \frac{-Kq + K^{-1} q^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}} E^{(l-1)} .$$ $$E F^{(l)}  = F^{(l)}E + F^{(l-1)} \frac{-Kq + K^{-1} q^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}} .$$ Let $D$ denote $E$ or $F$. Then $$D V_k \subset E^{(l)} D V_{k-1} + F^{(l)} D V_{k-1} + U_q E^{l-1} V_{k-1} + U_q F^{l-1} V_{k-1} .$$ By the induction hypothesis, we have $DV_{k-1} =0, E^{l-1}V_{k-1} = 0, F^{l-1} V_{k-1} =0$, and this completes the induction step. We have $K y = q^{lm_1 \pm 2lk} y = (-1)^{m_1} y$ for any $y \in V_k$. Hence $E, F, K- (-1)^{m_1}$ act as zero on $V =\sum_{k} V_k$.

By construction $E^{(l)} V \subset V$, $F^{(l)} V \subset V$, hence $V$ is a $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ submodule of $L(m)$, and since $L(m)$ is simple, we have $V = L(m)$. ◻
:::

Let $m = m_1 l + m_0$ such that $0 \leq m_0 \leq l-1$ and $m_1  \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$. Using the Hopf algebra structure of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, we can consider the tensor product of simple $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ modules $L(m_0) \otimes L(lm_1)$. By the Proposition [\[L(lm)\]](#L(lm)){reference-type="ref" reference="L(lm)"} above, we have $$E^{(l)} (x_0 \otimes x_1) = E^{(l)} x_0 \otimes x_1  + K^l x_0 \otimes E^{(l)} x_1 ,$$ $$F^{(l)} (x_0 \otimes x_1) = F^{(l)} x_0 \otimes K^{-l} x_1  + x_0 \otimes F^{(l)} x_1 .$$

::: proposition
[]{#tensor_pr label="tensor_pr"} The following $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ modules are isomorphic: $$L(lm_1 + m_0)  \cong L(m_0)  \otimes L(lm_1) .$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let $z_0 \in L(m_0)$ be such that $E z_0 =0$, $K z_0 = q^{m_0} z_0$, and $z_1 \in L(lm_1)$ such that $E^{(l)} z_1 =0$ and $\left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; 0 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] z_1 = \left[  \begin{array}{c}  lm_1 \\ l  \end{array}  \right]$. These vectors are unique up to a scalar, as they are obtained as the image of $x \in W(k)$ under the projection $W(k) \to L(k)$ for $k=m_0$ and $k = lm_1$ respectively. Then $$E(z_0 \otimes z_1) = E (z_0) \otimes z_1 = 0 , \quad  E^{(l)} (z_0 \otimes z_1) = 
K^l z_0 \otimes E^{(l)}  z_1 =0 ,$$ so that $(z_0 \otimes z_1)$ is a primitive vector in $L(m_0) \otimes L(lm_1)$. Then applying $E^{(l)} F^{(l)} - F^{(l)} E^{(l)} = \sum\limits_{0 \leq t \leq l} F^{(l-t)} \left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; 2t-2l \\ t  \end{array}  \right] E^{(l-t)}$ ([@Lus89], 4.1), to the primitive vector $(z_0 \otimes z_1)$, we obtain $$\left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; 0  \\ l  \end{array}  \right] (z_0 \otimes z_1) = K^l (z_0) \otimes (E^{(l)} F^{(l)} - F^{(l)} E^{(l)}) z_1 =$$ $$= (-1)^{m_0} z_0 \otimes \left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; 0 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] (z_1) = (-1)^{m_0} \left[  \begin{array}{c}  lm_1 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] (z_0 \otimes z_1) = \left[  \begin{array}{c}  lm_1 + m_0 \\ l  \end{array}  \right] (z_0 \otimes z_1) .$$ In the last equality we used the identity [\[q-bin3\]](#q-bin3){reference-type="ref" reference="q-bin3"} We also have $K(z_0 \otimes z_1) =  q^{lm_1+m_0} (z_0 \otimes z_1)$. This implies that the vector $(z_0 \otimes z_1)$ has the same properties as a primitive vector of the simple module $L(lm_1 + m_0)$.

Let us prove that $L(m_0) \otimes L(lm_1)$ is generated by $(z_0 \otimes z_1)$. Let $x_0 \otimes x_1 \in L(m_0) \otimes L(m_1)$, Since $z_0$ and $z_1$ generate $L(m_0)$ and $L(lm_1)$ over $u_q$ and $\mathcal {U}$ respectively, there exist elements $f \in u_q$ and $\mathcal{F} \in \mathcal{U}$ such that $x_0 = f z_0$ and $x_1 = \mathcal{F} z_1$. Then we have using Propositions [\[Lm0\]](#Lm0){reference-type="ref" reference="Lm0"}, [\[L(lm)\]](#L(lm)){reference-type="ref" reference="L(lm)"} $$f \mathcal{F} (z_0 \otimes z_1) = f(\pm z_0 \otimes \mathcal{F} z_1) = \pm f(z_0 \otimes x_1) = \pm f(z_0) \otimes x_1 = \pm x_0 \otimes x_1 .$$ So $L(m_0) \otimes L(lm_1)$ is generated by $z_0 \otimes z_1$ of weight $lm_1+ m_0$.

Suppose that there exists a nontrivial proper submodule $M \subset L(m_0) \otimes L(lm_1)$, and let $w \in M$ be primitive. Let $\{v_j\}_{j=1}^r$ be a basis over $\mathbb{C}$ of $L(lm_1)$. Then we can write uniquely $w = \sum_{j=1}^r w_j \otimes v_j$, where $w_j \in L(m_0)$. Since the vector $w$ is primitive in $M$, we have $Ew = \sum_{j=1}^r E w_j \otimes v_j =0$, hence $Ew_j =0$ for all $j$, therefore $w_j$ are multiples of $z_0 \in L(m_0)$, and $w = z_0 \otimes v$ for some $v \in L(lm_1)$. We also have $E^{(l)} w = z_0 \otimes E^{(l)} v =0$, and therefore $v$ is a multiple of $z_1 \subset L(lm_1)$. Therefore, any primitive vector in $M$ is a multiple of $z_0 \otimes z_1$, and this implies that $L(m_0) \otimes L(lm_1)$ is simple, and that the primitive vector in it has the weight $lm_1 + m_0$. Any such module is a simple quotient of $W(lm_1 + m_0)$ and therefore it is isomorphic to $L(lm_1+ m_0)$. ◻
:::

::: theorem
[]{#tilt_prod label="tilt_prod"} For any $0 \leq k_0 \leq l-1$ and $k_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ we have the tensor product identity for the indecomposable tilting modules over $U_q({\mathfrak{s}l}_2)$: $$T(k_1 l + 2(l-1) - k_0) \cong L(lk_1)  \otimes T(2(l-1) -k_0) .$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Using the filtrations of the tilting and Weyl modules in Theorems [\[Weyl\]](#Weyl){reference-type="ref" reference="Weyl"} [\[tilt_prop2\]](#tilt_prop2){reference-type="ref" reference="tilt_prop2"}, we conclude that $L(lk_1 +k_0)$ is the socle of the tilting module $T(lk_1 + 2(l-1) -k_0)$, and $L(lk_1) \otimes L(k_0)$ is the socle of the module $L(lk_1) \otimes T(2(l-1) - k_0)$. By the tensor product theorem [\[tensor_pr\]](#tensor_pr){reference-type="ref" reference="tensor_pr"}, these two simple modules are isomorphic. By a standard argument following [@APW1], Section 9, [@APW2], 4.6, the tilting modules in both sides are injective; in particular, the module $T(l k_1+ 2(l-1) -k_0)$ is an injective hull of the simple module $L(lk_1 + k_0)$, and therefore it has to be a direct summand of the module $L(lk_1) \otimes T(2(l-1) - k_0)$. Comparing the dimensions gives the isomorphism (we use that the dimensions of the Weyl modules in the filtration of tilting modules are given by the Weyl dimension formula). ◻
:::

# Decomposition of tensor powers of $T(1)$ over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ {#tp}

We are interested in the tensor powers of the module $L(1)$ over the quantum group $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, given explicitly by the following action of the generators: $$F v_1  = v_{-1} \quad \quad F v_{-1} =0 , \quad \quad Ev_1= 0, \quad \quad Ev_{-1} =   v_1 .$$ $$Kv_1 =  q v_1, \quad \quad  Kv_{-1} = q^{-1} v_{-1} .$$ $$E^{(l)} v_i =0, \quad \quad F^{(l)} v_i =0, \quad \quad i=1, -1 .$$ Since $L(1) \simeq T(1)$ is a tilting module, its tensor powers decompose into a direct sum of indecomposable tilting modules over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$.

Let $V(n)$ be the simple module of highest weight $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ over the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}(2, \mathbb{C})$. Define the coefficients $F_k^{(N)}$ as multiplicities in the following decomposition: $$\label{sl2} 
 V(1)^{\otimes N} = \bigoplus_{\substack{0 \leq k \leq N\\ k \equiv N\,{\rm mod}\,{2}}}  \; V(k)^{\oplus F_k^{(N)}}  \; .$$ i.e. $F_k^{(N)}=\dim\mathrm{Hom}(V(1)^{\otimes N}, V(k))$. It is known that $F_k^{(N)}$ are $$\label{tkN} 
F_k^{(N)} = {N\choose{\frac{N+k}{2}}} \frac{2k+2}{N+k+2}= {N\choose{\frac{N-k}{2}}}-{N\choose{\frac{N-k-2}{2}}} .$$

The following theorem provides the mutliplicities of $T(m)$ in $(L(1))^{\otimes N}$ over the algebra $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$.

::: theorem
see also [@And17], Corollary 3.5. []{#comb_coef label="comb_coef"} Using the classical coefficients $F_k^{(N)}$ defined in ([\[tkN\]](#tkN){reference-type="ref" reference="tkN"}), we have $$T(1)^{\otimes N} =  \bigoplus_{\substack{0 \leq k \leq N\\ k \equiv N\,{\rm mod}\,{2}}}  \; T(k)^{\oplus M_{k}^{(l)}(N)}  \; ,$$ where

1.  If $k = k_1 l -1 \leq N$ for $k_1 \geq 1$, then $M_{k_1 l -1}^{(l)}(N) = F_{k_1l-1}^{(N)}$,

2.  If $k = k_1 l + k_0 \leq N$ with $k_1 \geq 0$ and $0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2$, then $$\begin{aligned}
    \label{p-numbers}
    M_{k_1 l +k_0}^{(l)}(N) =   
     \sum\limits_{m=0}^{\lfloor\frac{N-k}{2l}\rfloor} F_{(k_1+2m)l + k_0}^{(N)}-  \sum\limits_{n=0}^{\lfloor\frac{N-(k_1+ 2)l +k_0+2}{2l}\rfloor} F_{(k_1+2n +2)l -k_0-2}^{(N)} .  \nonumber
    \end{aligned}$$ Here $\lfloor a\rfloor$ denotes the integer part (floor) of $a$. If the upper limit for $n$ is negative, the sum is void.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* By Theorem [\[tilt_prop1\]](#tilt_prop1){reference-type="ref" reference="tilt_prop1"}, the tensor power of $T(1)  = L(1)$ is a tilting module whose decomposition into the indecomposable tilting modules is determined by its character. Denote by $M_{k}^{(l)}(N)$ the multiplicity of the tilting module $T(k)$ in the decomposition of the tensor power $T(1)^{\otimes N}$ over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$.

Note that the formula [\[sl2\]](#sl2){reference-type="ref" reference="sl2"} provides the coefficients $F_k^{(N)}$ of the decomposition of the character of $T(1)^{\otimes N}$ in terms of the characters of the Weyl modules over $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. Therefore our task reduces to rewriting this decomposition in the basis of the tilting characters.

The decomposition of the basis of tilting characters in terms of the Weyl characters is given by Corollary [\[tilt_char\]](#tilt_char){reference-type="ref" reference="tilt_char"}. Consider the vector space $Q_N$ spanned by the characters $\{ {\rm ch}_{W(k)} \}_{0 \leq k \leq N}$. Then the subspace $Q_{N, l-1} \subset Q_N$ spanned by $\{{\rm ch}_{W(k_1l-1)}\}_{0 < k_1l-1 \leq N}$ coincides with the subspace spanned by $\{{\rm ch}_{T(k_1l-1)}\}_{0 < k_1l-1 \leq N}$, and we have ${\rm ch}_{W(k_1l -1)} = {\rm ch}_{T(k_1l -1)}$, so that the change of basis matrix on $Q_{N, l-1}$ is trivial. This proves (a).

The subspace $Q_{\rm tilt}$ of $Q_N= Q_{\rm tilt} \oplus Q_{N, l-1}$ spanned by the characters $\{{\rm ch}_{W(k_1l + k_0)} \}_{\substack{0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2,\\ k_1l + k_0 \leq N}}$ splits into a direct sum of subspaces $$Q_{\rm tilt} =  \bigoplus_{0\leq k_0 \leq l-2} \; Q_{N, k_0}$$ spanned by the Weyl characters of dominant highest weights in the orbit of $k_0$ with respect to the reflections about the weights of the form $\{ jl-1\}_{ j \in \mathbb{Z}}$. This follows from the formula for the tilting character ${\rm ch}_{T(lk_1 + k_0)}$, $0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2$ in Corollary [\[tilt_char\]](#tilt_char){reference-type="ref" reference="tilt_char"} and is a manifestation of the *linkage principle* (cf. [@AT], 2.4). Let us fix $k_0$ such that $0  \leq k_0 \leq l-2$. Then the weights of the Weyl (or tilting) characters spanning $Q_{N, k_0}$ are the following: $$\begin{array}{ll} 
 \{ \lambda_{k_1} =   k_1 l + k_0 \}_{0 \leq k_1l + k_0 \leq N},   &    k_1   \; {\rm even}  \\
 \{ \lambda_{k_1} =  k_1 l + l-j_0 -2\}_{ 0 \leq  k_1 l + l - k_0 -2 \leq N}    &  k_1  \; {\rm odd} 
   \end{array}$$ Let $k_{\rm max}$ denote the maximal value of $k_1$ satisfying the above conditions. Then the Weyl characters that span $Q_{N, k_0}$ are enumerated by all integers $k_1$ such that $0 \leq k_1 \leq k_{\rm max}$, namely they are $\{{\rm ch}_{W(\lambda_{k_1})} \}_{0 \leq k_1 \leq k_{\rm max}}$. The same space is also spanned by the characters $\{ {\rm ch}_{T(\lambda_{k_1})} \}_{0 \leq k_1 \leq k_{\rm max}}$, and we can rewrite Corollary [\[tilt_char\]](#tilt_char){reference-type="ref" reference="tilt_char"} as follows: $$\begin{array}{l} 
 {\rm ch}_{T(\lambda_0)} = {\rm ch}_{W(\lambda_0)} , \\ 
  {\rm ch}_{T(\lambda_{k_1})} = {\rm ch}_{W(\lambda_{k_1})} + {\rm ch}_{W(\lambda_{k_1-1})}   , \;\;\;\;  1 \leq k_1 \leq k_{\rm max}  . 
  \end{array}$$ This defines the change of basis matrix between the tilting and Weyl characters and its inverse: $$\begin{array}{lll} 
  A = \left( \begin{array}{ccccc} 
              1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 
                 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 
               \ldots  &   \ldots  &  \ldots & \ldots   & \ldots    \\
                 &    &    &  1  & 1  \\
                  &    &   &     &  1 \\    
                  \end{array} \right)  ,    &  \;\;\;\;\;   &
     A^{-1} = \left( \begin{array}{ccccc} 
              1 & -1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 
                 & 1 & -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 
          \ldots  &   \ldots  &  \ldots & \ldots   & \ldots    \\
                 &    &    &  1  & -1  \\
                  &    &   &     &  1 \\    
                  \end{array} \right)     
                  \end{array}$$ Applying the matrix $A^{-1}$ to the column of classical coefficients $\{ t_{k, N}\}$, we obtain the formula given in part (b) of the theorem. ◻
:::

# Restriction from $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ to $u_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ {#restUu}

Let $U_q^0 \subset U_q({\mathfrak{s}l}_2)$ denote the subalgebra of $U_q({\mathfrak{s}l}_2)$ generated by $K^{\pm 1}$, $\left[  \begin{array}{c}  K ; c \\ t  \end{array}  \right]$ for $t \geq 0, c \in \mathbb{Z}$. Let $u_q^{\pm} \subset u_q$ denote the subalgebras of $u_q$ generated by $F$ and $E$ respectively. Using Theorem [\[tilt_prod\]](#tilt_prod){reference-type="ref" reference="tilt_prod"}, we can obtain the following result for the restriction of the tilting modules over the algebra $u_q^- U_q^0 u_q^+$:

::: corollary
[]{#restr label="restr"} Restricting Theorem [\[comb_coef\]](#comb_coef){reference-type="ref" reference="comb_coef"} over the algebra $u_q^- U_q^0 u_q^+$, we obtain after a change of variables $$T(k_1 l + (l-1) + k_0) \big\vert_{u_q^- U_q^0 u_q^+} \cong \bigoplus_{\substack{-k_1 \leq j \leq k_1\\  j\equiv k_1 \,{\rm mod}\, 2}} \mathbb{C}[lj]  \otimes  T((l-1) +k_0) .$$ Here $k_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, $0 \leq k_0 \leq l-1$, and $\mathbb{C}[lj]$ is the one-dimensional module of weight $lj$.
:::

Note that $$K \mathbb{C}[lj] = q^{lj} \mathbb{C}[lj] = \left\{  \begin{array}{cl} 
                                          \mathbb{C}[lj] ,   &    j \; {\rm even}  \\
                                          -\mathbb{C}[lj],  & j \; {\rm odd }   
                                          \end{array}    \right.    
                                          \quad \quad  \quad   E \mathbb{C}[lj] = F \mathbb{C}[lj] = 0.$$

Here is the list of indecomposable modules that can occur in the tensor powers of $L(1) \cong  T(1)$ over $u_q^- U_q^0 u_q^+$: $$\{ T(k_0)  \}_{0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2 } , \quad   \{  \mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l-1) \}_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} , \quad  
\{ \mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l + k_0) \}_{j \in \mathbb{Z}, \; 0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2} .$$ Note that $T(k_0) \cong L(k_0)$, $0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2$ and $T(l-1)\cong L(l-1)$ are simple modules.

By Corollary [\[restr\]](#restr){reference-type="ref" reference="restr"} we have the following decomposition over $u_q^- U_q^0 u_q^+$: $$T(1)^{\otimes N} \cong \bigoplus_{0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2, \; k_0 \leq N} T(k_0)^{\oplus M_{k_0}^{(l)}(N)} \oplus \bigoplus_{j \in \mathbb{Z}, \; l|j| + l-1 \leq N}  (\mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l-1))^{\oplus m^{(l)}_{l-1,j}(N)}  \oplus$$ $$\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;  \bigoplus_{j \in \mathbb{Z}, \; 
0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2, \; l|j| + l + k_0 \leq N} (\mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l + k_0))^{\oplus m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,j}(N)} .$$ Here we denoted by $m^{(l)}_{l-1,j}(N)$ and $m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,j}(N)$ the multiplicities of $\mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l-1)$ and $\mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l + k_0)$ respectively.

::: theorem
[]{#uUu label="uUu"} Let $j \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, and $0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2$. The multiplicities $m^{(l)}_{l-1, \pm j}( N)$ and $m^{(l)}_{l+k_0, \pm j}( N)$ are given by the formulae $$m^{(l)}_{l-1,\pm j}(N) =  \sum_{s \geq \lfloor \frac{j}{2} \rfloor}  M^{(l)}_{l-1+ j({\rm mod}2)l + 2sl}(N) ;$$ $$m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,\pm j}(N)  = \sum_{s \geq \lfloor \frac{j}{2} \rfloor} M^{(l)}_{l+k_0+ j({\rm mod}2)l + 2sl}(N) .$$ The upper limit for $s$ can be calculated as $\lfloor \frac{N-(l-1+ j({\rm mod}2)l)}{2l}\rfloor$ or $\lfloor \frac{N-(l+k_0+ j({\rm mod}2)l)}{2l}\rfloor$, but in fact this is unnecessary because for higher $s$ the coefficients $M^{(l)}_\bullet(N)$ under the sum are zero.
:::

The proof of this is a direct consequence of Theorem [\[comb_coef\]](#comb_coef){reference-type="ref" reference="comb_coef"} and Corollary [\[restr\]](#restr){reference-type="ref" reference="restr"}.

Let $T(l + k_0)$, $0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2$ and $T(l-1)$ be the restrictions of the tilting modules with given highest weights to the small quantum group $u_q$. Following [@APW2], we conclude that these are indecomposable injective modules. Over the small quantum group $u_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, define the modules $T_{\pm}(m)$, $m \geq l-1$ as follows: $$\mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l+ k_0) \cong T_+(l+ k_0), \quad   \mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l-1) \cong T_+(l-1)  \quad j  \; {\rm even}  ;$$ $$\mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l+ k_0) \cong T_-(l+ k_0), \quad   \mathbb{C}[lj] \otimes T(l-1) \cong T_-(l-1)  \quad j  \; {\rm odd}  ;$$ Notes that we have natural isomorphisms $T_-(k)\simeq T_+(k)\otimes 1_{-}$ where $1_-$ is a 1-dimensional representation $K\mapsto -1, E,F\mapsto 0$.

Then over the small quantum group we obtain the following decomposition.

::: theorem
[]{#small_mult label="small_mult"} $$T(1)^{\otimes N} \cong \bigoplus_{0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2} T(k_0)^{\oplus M^{(l)}_{k_0}(N)} \oplus  T_+(l-1)^{\oplus m^{(l)}_{l-1,+}(N)}   \oplus  T_-(l-1)^{\oplus m^{(l)}_{l-1,-}(N)}  \oplus$$ $$\oplus  \bigoplus_{0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2}T_+(l + k_0)^{\oplus m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,+}(N)}    
 \oplus  \bigoplus_{0 \leq k_0 \leq l-2}T_-(l + k_0)^{\oplus m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,-}(N)} ,$$ where $$m^{(l)}_{l-1,+}(N) = \sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}, \,{\rm even}} m^{(l)}_{l-1,j}(N) ,  \quad \quad  m^{(l)}_{l-1,-}(N) = \sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}, \,{\rm odd}} m^{(l)}_{l-1,j}(N) ,$$ $$m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,+}(N)  = \sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}, \,{\rm even} } m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,j}(N)  \quad \quad  m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,-}(N) = \sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}, \,{\rm odd} } m^{(l)}_{l+k_0,j}(N) .$$
:::

This theorem follows directly from Theorem [\[uUu\]](#uUu){reference-type="ref" reference="uUu"} and the definition of the modules $T_\pm (l + k_0)$, $T_\pm(l-1)$.
