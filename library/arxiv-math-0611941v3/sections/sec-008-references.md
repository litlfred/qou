where a, b are non-negative integers. Then we have two cellular structures on H: the one
given by Theorem 3.1, and the one given by Dipper–James–Murphy [4] (or, alternatively,
by Graham–Lehrer [16, §5]). In both cases, Λ is the set of all pairs of partitions (λ, µ) such
that |λ| + |µ| = n. The partial order on Λ that we use here is deﬁned in terms of the a-
function; it heavily depends on the weight function L, i.e., on the integers a and b. The partial
order used by Dipper–James–Murphy (or Graham–Lehrer) is deﬁned in purely combinatorial
terms: it does not depend at all on the weight function L. Small examples show that the
two partial orders indeed are diﬀerent. Hence, in general, the cellular structure that we
construct will be essentially diﬀerent from the one constructed by Dipper–James–Murphy
(or Graham–Lehrer).
These diﬀerences can be seen explicitly in the descriptions of the
sets Λ◦⊆Λ parametrizing the irreducible representations of non-semisimple specialisations
of H, as discussed in [13].
By [13, Theorem 2.8], the two parametrizations coincide if
b > (n −1)a > 0. Thus, one may expect that the two cellular structures are equivalent
under this condition; see Iancu–Pallikaros [18] for a further discussion of this question.
