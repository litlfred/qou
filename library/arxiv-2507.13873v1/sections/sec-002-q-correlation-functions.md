To construct a $q$-deformed version of holography, we analogously imagine the correlators of the bulk gravitational theory to be invariant under the SL$_q(2)$ global subgroup of the dual $q$-deformed conformal boundary theory. To set up the $q$-deformed SL$_q(2)$ conformal subgroup, we claim that the natural generalization of the quasi-primary transformation law [\[eq:quasiprimarytransf\]](#eq:quasiprimarytransf){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:quasiprimarytransf"} acts as a *corepresentation* in a tensor product structure $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:corep}
    \mathcal{O}(z) \mapsto  (1\otimes N^{\Delta/2}) \left( z\otimes z_{12} + 1\otimes z_{22} \right)^{-2\Delta} \;\mathcal{O}\left( \frac{z\otimes z_{11} + 1\otimes z_{21}}{ z\otimes z_{12} +1\otimes z_{22}}\right) (1\otimes N^{\Delta/2}).
\end{aligned}$$ The coordinates $z_{ij}$ are suitably defined coordinates on the *non-commutative* GL$_q(2)$ manifold for which the determinant $N$ projects down onto the definition of SL$_q(2)$. The meaning of this (co)transformation law is deferred to appendix [\[app:cft\]](#app:cft){reference-type="ref" reference="app:cft"} and is not important for the rest of the main text. Important for us are the analogues of the "infinitesimal" generators [\[eq:infgenerators\]](#eq:infgenerators){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:infgenerators"}, which can be derived from the above cotransformation rule (see again appendix [\[app:cft\]](#app:cft){reference-type="ref" reference="app:cft"}, where we now redefine $K\rightarrow q^{2L_0}$, $E^+\rightarrow L_{+1}$, $E^-\rightarrow L_{-1}$). They act in terms of scaling operators $R_qf(z)\equiv f(qz)$ on the holomorphic coordinates, and are given by $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:qprincipalgenerators2}
     L_0=\Delta +z\partial_z, \qquad L_{-1}=\frac{q^{-1/2+\Delta}}{z}\frac{R_{q^2}-1}{q-q^{-1}}, \qquad L_{+1}=-q^{-1/2+\Delta} z\frac{q^{-4\Delta} R_{q^{-2}}-1}{q-q^{-1}},
\end{aligned}$$ which form a realization of the $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ algebra, defined by the commutation relations $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:qalgebra}
    [L_0,L_{\pm1}]=\pm L_{\pm1}, \qquad [L_{+1},L_{-1}]=-\frac{q^{2L_0}-q^{-2L_0}}{q-q^{-1}}.
\end{aligned}$$ Note that in terms of $q^{2L_0}$, the first relations are also equivalent to $q^{2L_0}L_{\pm1}=q^{\pm2}L_{\pm1}q^{2L_0}$. In the limit $q\rightarrow1$, both the infinitesimal generators and the algebra relations reduce to their classical $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ counterparts.

In a $q$-deformed setup, we again demand the correlation functions to be invariant under the global SL$_q(2)$ conformal group, whose infinitesimal action is generated by the generators [\[eq:qprincipalgenerators2\]](#eq:qprincipalgenerators2){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:qprincipalgenerators2"}. To distribute the action of the generators on the primary fields in the correlation function as in [\[eq:distribute\]](#eq:distribute){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:distribute"}, we need a more general coproduct which is compatible with the above commutation relations [\[eq:qalgebra\]](#eq:qalgebra){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:qalgebra"}.[^1] A consistent set of coproducts is given by $$\begin{aligned}
    \Delta(q^{2L_0}) = q^{2L_0} \otimes q^{2L_0}, &\quad \Delta(L_{+1}) =1 \otimes L_{+1} + L_{+1} \otimes q^{-2L_0}, \quad \Delta(L_{-1}) =  q^{2L_0} \otimes L_{-1} + L_{-1} \otimes 1.
\end{aligned}$$ Focusing specifically on two-point correlation functions, leads to the three Ward identities $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:qward}
    m\circ\braket{(\Delta(q^{2L_0})-1)[\mathcal{O}(z_1)\otimes\mathcal{O}(z_2)]}\equiv 0, \quad m\circ\braket{\Delta(L_{\pm1})[\mathcal{O}(z_1)\otimes\mathcal{O}(z_2)]}\equiv 0.
\end{aligned}$$ The explicit recursion relations are then respectively $$\begin{aligned}
    &0=\braket{\mathcal{O}(z_1)\mathcal{O}(z_2)}-q^{4\Delta}\braket{\mathcal{O}(q^2z_1)\mathcal{O}(q^{2}z_2)},\\
    &0=z_1\left[q^{-6\Delta}\braket{\mathcal{O}(q^{-2}z_1)\mathcal{O}(q^{-2}z_2)}-q^{-2\Delta}\braket{\mathcal{O}(z_1)\mathcal{O}(q^{-2}z_2)}\right] \nonumber \\&\qquad \qquad+z_2\left[q^{-4\Delta}\braket{\mathcal{O}(z_1)\mathcal{O}(q^{-2}z_2)}-\braket{\mathcal{O}(z_1)\mathcal{O}(z_2)}\right], \\&0=\frac{1}{z_1}\left[\braket{\mathcal{O}(q^2z_1)\mathcal{O}(z_2)}-\braket{\mathcal{O}(z_1)\mathcal{O}(z_2)}\right] \nonumber \\&\qquad \qquad+\frac{q^{2\Delta}}{z_2}\left[\braket{\mathcal{O}(q^2z_1)\mathcal{O}(q^{2}z_2)}-\braket{\mathcal{O}(q^2z_1)\mathcal{O}(z_2)}\right].
\end{aligned}$$ The effect of a non-trivial coproduct breaks the cyclic permutation symmetry of the correlator, since the distributive action of the generators acts differently on the two coordinates. When both operators share the same conformal weight $\Delta$, a consistent solution to all three equations is given by a ratio of double sine functions $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:exactcorrelation}
    \boxed{\braket{\mathcal{O}(z_1)\mathcal{O}(z_2)}=\frac{1}{(z_1z_2)^\Delta}\frac{S_b\left(-b\Delta+\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2\pi ib}\right)}{S_b\left(b\Delta+\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2\pi ib}\right)}},
\end{aligned}$$ when $q=e^{i\pi b^2}$ with $b\in \mathbb{R}$. Indeed, we see that the first recursion relation is trivially satisfied. Using the recursion relation $S_b(x+b)=2\sin(\pi bx) S_b(x)$, the second equation is equivalent to $$\begin{aligned}
&z_1\left[q^{-2\Delta}\sin\pi b\left(b\Delta+\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2\pi ib}\right)-\sin\pi b\left(-b\Delta+\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2\pi ib}\right)\right]\\&+z_2\left[q^{-2\Delta}\sin\pi b\left(-b\Delta+\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2\pi ib}\right)-\sin\pi b\left(b\Delta+\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2\pi ib}\right)\right] \overset{?}{=} 0.
\end{aligned}$$ Using $q=e^{\pi ib^2}$ this is indeed true. One analogously solves the third equation.

Using the reflection property $S_b(Q-x)=1/S_b(x)$ and the recursive properties of the double sine functions $S_b(x+b)=\sin(\pi bx)S_b(x)$, $S_b(x+1/b)=\sin\frac{\pi x}{b}S_b(x)$, we can rewrite [\[eq:exactcorrelation\]](#eq:exactcorrelation){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:exactcorrelation"} as: $$\begin{aligned}
    \sim \frac{1}{(z_1z_2)^\Delta}\sin\left(b^2\pi\Delta-i\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2 }\right)\sin\left(-\pi \Delta +i\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2 b^2}\right)S_b\left(-b\Delta\pm i\frac{\ln(z_1/z_2)}{2\pi b}\right),
\end{aligned}$$ which is a useful form for taking the $b\to 0$ limit. Indeed, upon using the asymptotics $\lim_{b\rightarrow0}S_b(bx\pm i\alpha/\pi b)\sim \sinh(\alpha)^{2x-1}e^{-\alpha/b^2}$, the correlation function limits to the classical conformally invariant two-point function $$\begin{aligned}
    \braket{\mathcal{O}(z_1)\mathcal{O}(z_2)}\sim \frac{1}{(z_1-z_2)^{2\Delta}}.
\end{aligned}$$

To obtain a finite temperature solution, we set the boundary coordinates $z_1=e^{i\varphi},\;z_2=e^{i\theta}$. Under the classical conformal transformation, the $q$-independent prefactors vanish, leading to $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:generalsolution}
    \braket{\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\mathcal{O}(\theta)}=\frac{S_b\left(-b\Delta+\frac{\varphi-\theta}{2\pi b}\right)}{S_b\left(b\Delta+\frac{\varphi-\theta}{2\pi b}\right)}=\frac{S_b\left(-b\Delta+\frac{\tau}{\beta b}\right)}{S_b\left(b\Delta+\frac{\tau}{\beta b}\right)},
\end{aligned}$$ where the difference in the angular coordinates is related to the temperature $\beta$ as $\varphi-\theta=\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\tau$. Note that the above expressions are not symmetric under $\tau\rightarrow \beta-\tau$. We can make a substitution to "fake" coordinates $$\begin{aligned}
    \varphi\rightarrow\varphi+\frac{\pi b^2}{2}, \qquad \theta\rightarrow\theta-\frac{\pi b^2}{2},
\end{aligned}$$ to obtain the correlator on a "fake" quantum disk $$\begin{aligned}
\label{res}
\braket{\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\mathcal{O}(\theta)}_{\text{fake}}= \frac{S_b\left(-b\Delta+\frac{b}{2}+\frac{\tau}{\beta b}\right)}{S_b\left(b\Delta+\frac{b}{2}+\frac{\tau}{\beta b}\right)}.
\end{aligned}$$ Using the recursive property $S_b(Q-x)=1/S_b(x)$, it is readily seen that the expression [\[res\]](#res){reference-type="eqref" reference="res"} now *does* exhibit the KMS symmetry $\tau\rightarrow \beta-\tau$. This is the expression which will be compared below to the probe approximation of the sinh dilaton gravity two-point function [\[eq:final\]](#eq:final){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:final"}, which inherently preserves the KMS symmetry of the exact answer.

We can also rewrite the exact expression [\[eq:generalsolution\]](#eq:generalsolution){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:generalsolution"} suggestively by assuming $\Delta\in \mathbb{N}$. Again using the recursion property $S_b(x+b)=2\sin(\pi bx)S_b(x)$ successively, we arrive at an integer product of shifted sine functions $$\begin{aligned}
\label{2.46}
    \braket{\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\mathcal{O}(\theta)}=\frac{1}{\prod_{n=-\Delta}^{\Delta-1}2\sin\left(\pi b^2n+\frac{\pi\tau}{\beta }\right)}.
\end{aligned}$$ For integer $\Delta$ this finite product can, up to a $\tau$-independent prefactor, also be expressed as $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:qpoch}
    \braket{\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\mathcal{O}(\theta)}\simeq \frac{1}{\left(q^{-2\Delta}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{\beta}\tau};q^2\right)_\Delta\left(q^{-2\Delta+2}e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{\beta}\tau};q^2\right)_\Delta},
\end{aligned}$$ where the integer $q$-Pochhammer symbol is defined as $(a,q)_\Delta=\prod_{n=0}^{\Delta-1}(1-aq^n)$. The solution to the Ward identities is then unique depending on the range of $q$. For $q=e^{i\pi b^2}$ on the unit circle, [\[eq:generalsolution\]](#eq:generalsolution){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:generalsolution"} is the natural analytic continuation to non-integer $\Delta$. For $0<q<1$, one can analytically continue the $q$-Pochhammer symbols in [\[eq:qpoch\]](#eq:qpoch){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:qpoch"} to a convergent infinite product as $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:analyplch}
     \braket{\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\mathcal{O}(\theta)}=\frac{\left(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{\beta}\tau};q^2\right)_\infty}{\left(q^{-2\Delta}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{\beta}\tau};q^2\right)_\infty}\frac{\left(q^2e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{\beta}\tau};q^2\right)_\infty}{\left(q^{-2\Delta+2}e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{\beta}\tau};q^2\right)_\infty}.
\end{aligned}$$ This solution solves the same $q$-Ward identities [\[eq:qward\]](#eq:qward){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:qward"} in this range of $0<q<1$ after going back to real-time coordinates, and matches the old results found by [@Bernard:1989jq; @Matsuzaki:1991vu], and more recently revisited in [@Almheiri:2024ayc]. This change in structure, depending on whether $q$ is on the unit circle or not, is of a similar spirit as the motivation behind Faddeev's quantum dilogarithm [@Faddeev:1999fe].

In order to find a bulk description of the solution [\[res\]](#res){reference-type="eqref" reference="res"} of the $q$-Ward identities, we consider its spectral decomposition. To this end, we make use of a modified version of Ramanujan's summation formula [\[master\]](#master){reference-type="eqref" reference="master"}. We report it here: $$\begin{aligned}
\label{ram}
\int_\mathbb{R} \mathrm{d}\tau e^{-2\pi \beta \tau} e^{\pi (Q-\alpha)\tau} \frac{S_b(\alpha/2 \pm i \tau)}{S_b(\alpha)}= \frac{S_b(\beta)}{S_b(\alpha+\beta)}.
\end{aligned}$$ We can use [\[ram\]](#ram){reference-type="eqref" reference="ram"} to rewrite our result [\[res\]](#res){reference-type="eqref" reference="res"} in Fourier space as ($\beta_M=b \Delta$): $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:ramanexplicit}
    \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega\; e^{\frac{\omega \beta}{2b^2}-\frac{\tau\omega}{b^2}}\frac{S_b\left(\beta_M\pm i \frac{\omega\beta}{2\pi b}\right)}{S_b(2\beta_M)}= \frac{S_b(-\beta_M+\frac{b}{2}+\frac{\tau}{b\beta})}{S_b(\beta_M+\frac{b}{2}+\frac{\tau}{b\beta})}.
\end{aligned}$$ The LHS of [\[eq:ramanexplicit\]](#eq:ramanexplicit){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:ramanexplicit"} contains dynamical information about the dual bulk gravitational system as follows. After Fourier transforming the boundary two-point function, one can interpret the poles at $\Im \omega < 0$ in the frequency domain as the quasi-normal modes of the bulk system. This evaluates here to just the poles of the double sine function as a double array: $$\label{qnm}
\omega_{n,m} = -i \frac{2\pi b}{\beta}\left(\beta_M+nb+\frac{m}{b}\right), \quad n,m \geq 0.$$ These quasi-normal mode frequencies contribute to the retarded Green's function, and their appearance directly points to a dissipative and thermalizing bulk system, pointing at a black hole horizon. In the AdS$_2$ limit where $b\to 0$, the second set of poles at nonzero $m$ shifts to infinity and is effectively lost. The surviving set of poles just become the known AdS$_2$ black hole quasi-normal mode frequencies (see e.g. [@Keeler:2014hba]).

[^1]: When we say a "compatible" coproduct, we mean that at the level of the commutation relations, we have $$\begin{aligned}
    \Delta(q^{2L_0}L_{\pm1})=q^{\pm2}\Delta(L_{\pm1}q^{2L_0}),\qquad [\Delta(L_{+1}),\Delta(L_{-1})]=\Delta\left(-\frac{q^{2L_0}-q^{-2L_0}}{q-q^{-1}}\right),
    \end{aligned}$$ where by definition of the coproduct, we have $\Delta(AB)=\Delta(A)\Delta(B)$.
