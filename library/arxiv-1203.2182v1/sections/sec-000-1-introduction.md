[]{#sec-intro label="sec-intro"}

The story of the "volume conjecture" started with the crucial observation [@Kashaev] that the so-called Kashaev invariant of a knot $K$ defined at the $n$-th root of unity $q = e^{2 \pi i / n}$ in the classical limit has a nice asymptotic behavior determined by the hyperbolic volume $\text{Vol} (M)$ of the knot complement $M = S^3 \setminus K$. Shortly after, it was realized [@MurMur] that the Kashaev invariant is equal to the $n$-colored Jones polynomial of a knot $K$ evaluated at $q = e^{2 \pi i / n}$, so that the volume conjecture could be stated simply as $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2 \pi \log |J_n (K; q = e^{2 \pi i / n})|}{n} \; = \; \text{Vol} (M) \,.
\label{VCbasic}$$

The physical interpretation of the volume conjecture was proposed in [@Apol]. Besides explaining the original observation [\[VCbasic\]](#VCbasic){reference-type="eqref" reference="VCbasic"} it immediately led to a number of generalizations, in which the right-hand side is replaced by a function of various parameters (see [@DGreview] for a review). Below we state two such generalizations -- associated, respectively, with the parameters $\hbar$ and $u$ -- that in the rest of the paper will be "refined" or, morally speaking, "categorified."
