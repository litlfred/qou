We now repeat the above calculation for the conifold, whose ordinary mirror curve in general framing takes the form [@abmodel]: $$A^{\text{ref}} (x,y;q_1=1) = 1 - y + x(-y)^f + Q x (-y)^{f+1} \; = \; 0 \,.    \label{mirrorConif}$$ To find the refined and quantum version of this curve, we again consider the brane located along the preferred direction of the conifold. More precisely, we wish to consider the brane amplitude normalized by the closed string partition function. Such an amplitude can be written as $$Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}} \; = \; \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k = \sum_{R=(k)}  s_R(x) f_R(q_1,q_2)^{-f} \frac{b_R}{b_{\bullet}},   \label{Zopen-coni}$$ where $$\begin{aligned}
b_R & = & \sum_{P} (-Q)^{|P|} C_{P\bullet R}(q_1^{-1},q_2^{-1})  C_{P^t \bullet \bullet}(q_2^{-1},q_1^{-1})  \nonumber\\
& = & \Big( \frac{q_1}{q_2} \Big)^{||R||^2/2}  P_{R^t}(q_1^{\rho};q_2^{-1},q_1^{-1})
\prod_{i,j=1}^{\infty} (1 - Q q_1^{-i+1/2} q_2^{-j+1/2+R_i})
\end{aligned}$$ and $Q$ denotes the Kähler parameter of the conifold. The normalization factor in this case is nothing but $$\begin{aligned}
Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{closed}} = b_{\bullet} & = & \sum_{P} (-Q)^{|P|} C_{P\bullet \bullet}(q_1^{-1},q_2^{-1})  C_{P^t \bullet \bullet}(q_2^{-1},q_1^{-1})  \nonumber  \\
& = & \prod_{i,j=1}^{\infty} (1 - Q q_1^{-i+1/2} q_2^{-j+1/2}),
\end{aligned}$$ and it follows that for representation $R=(k)$, *cf.* ([\[C00R\]](#C00R){reference-type="ref" reference="C00R"}): $$\frac{b_{(k)}}{b_{\bullet}} = C_{\bullet \bullet (k)}(q_1^{-1},q_2^{-1}) \prod_{j=1}^k \big(1 - Q q_1^{-1/2} q_2^{-j+k+1/2} \big)
= \Big(- \frac{q_1}{\sqrt{q_2}} \Big)^k \prod_{i=1}^k \frac{1 - Q q_1^{-1/2} q_2^{i-1/2}}{1 - q_1 q_2^{i-1}}.$$ Collecting the above ingredients, we find the following structure of the open BPS state partition function $$Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}} \; = \; \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k
= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (-1)^{(f+1)k} q_2^{\frac{f}{2}k(k-1)} \Big(x \frac{q_1}{\sqrt{q_2}}\Big)^k \prod_{i=1}^k \frac{1 - Q q_1^{-1/2} q_2^{i-1/2}}{1 - q_1 q_2^{i-1}} \,.   \label{Zopen-BPS-coni}$$ We also find that the consecutive terms $a_k$ are related as $$(1 - q_1 q_2^k) a_{k+1} \; = \; (-1)^{f+1} q^{f k} \frac{q_1}{\sqrt{q_2}} x (1 - Q q_1^{-1/2} q_2^{k+1/2} ) a_k \,.$$ Summing both sides over $k$ gives rise to the inhomogeneous equation $$\Big(1 - \frac{q_1}{q_2} \widehat{y} + \frac{q_1}{\sqrt{q_2}} \widehat{x}(-\widehat{y})^f  + Q q_1^{1/2} \widehat{x}(-\widehat{y})^{f+1} \Big) Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}} \; = \; 1 - \frac{q_1}{q_2} \,.   \label{AquantumConiinhom}$$ This is the refined and quantum version of the conifold mirror curve ([\[mirrorConif\]](#mirrorConif){reference-type="ref" reference="mirrorConif"}). We can also bring this equation to the homogeneous form at the expense of increasing its degree in $\widehat{y}$, by multiplying both sides with $(1-\widehat{y})$. Commuting all $\widehat{y}$ operators to the right gives $$\Big(1 - \Big(1 + \frac{q_1}{q_2}\Big) \widehat{y} + \frac{q_1}{q_2}\widehat{y}^2 +
\frac{q_1}{\sqrt{q_2}} \widehat{x}(-\widehat{y})^f + \Big(Q\sqrt{q_1} +  q_1\sqrt{q_2} \Big) \widehat{x} (-\widehat{y})^{f+1}
+ Q\sqrt{q_1} q_2 \widehat{x} (-\widehat{y})^{f+2}
  \Big) Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}} = 0.$$

## Refined mirror curves, $S_0(u,t)$ and quantizability

Once we found the refined quantum curves, we can easily determine the classical refined mirror curves. Namely, much as in the knot theory examples, we can find them in two ways. First, they arise as $q_2\to 1$ limit of the quantum curves. Secondly, they can be determined by the saddle point analysis. As we will see momentarily, both methods consistently give the same result. The limit $q_2\to 1$ of the ${\mathbb C}^3$ quantum curve ([\[AquantumC3inhom\]](#AquantumC3inhom){reference-type="ref" reference="AquantumC3inhom"}) leads to the following refined mirror curve $$A^{\text{ref}} (x,y;q_1) \; = \; 1 - q_1 y + q_1 x (-y)^f \,.  \label{Aq1-C3}$$ Similarly, the refined mirror curve for the conifold can be obtained form ([\[AquantumConiinhom\]](#AquantumConiinhom){reference-type="ref" reference="AquantumConiinhom"}) and takes the form $$A^{\text{ref}} (x,y;q_1) \; = \; 1 - q_1 y + q_1 x (-y)^f + Q \sqrt{q_1} \, x (-y)^{f+1} \,.    \label{Aq1-coni}$$ By looking at the form of this refined $A$-polynomial, a careful reader will recognize a close relation with the example of the unknot discussed in section [\[sec:knots\]](#sec:knots){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:knots"}. Indeed, a simple change of variables $$\begin{array}{c@{\quad}c@{\quad}c}
\underline{\text{unknot}} & & \underline{\text{conifold}} \\[.1cm]
x & \mapsto & (-1)^{f+1}t^{-2}xy^f  \\[.1cm]
y & \mapsto & (-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}y
\end{array}
\label{unknotconi}$$ relates refined $A$-polynomials [\[Atunknot2\]](#Atunknot2){reference-type="eqref" reference="Atunknot2"} and [\[Aq1-coni\]](#Aq1-coni){reference-type="eqref" reference="Aq1-coni"}, provided that we identify $q_1 = -t$ as in [\[qqqt\]](#qqqt){reference-type="eqref" reference="qqqt"} that the Kähler parameter of the conifold is tuned to a special value: $$Q \; = \; it^{-\frac{3}{2}} \,.$$ Note, the transformation [\[unknotconi\]](#unknotconi){reference-type="eqref" reference="unknotconi"} preserves the holomorphic symplectic form $\Omega = \frac{i}{\hbar} \frac{dx}{x} \wedge \frac{dy}{y}$ on ${\mathbb C}^* \times {\mathbb C}^*$ relevant to quantization.

To confirm that the same refined curves [\[Aq1-C3\]](#Aq1-C3){reference-type="eqref" reference="Aq1-C3"} and [\[Aq1-coni\]](#Aq1-coni){reference-type="eqref" reference="Aq1-coni"} arise from the saddle point analysis we follow the strategy of section [\[sec:saddle_point\]](#sec:saddle_point){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:saddle_point"}, where we discussed analogous curves coming from knot theory examples. Specifically, much like in ([\[Pn-saddle\]](#Pn-saddle){reference-type="ref" reference="Pn-saddle"}), we wish to approximate the BPS partition functions $Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}}$ in ([\[Zopen-BPS-C3\]](#Zopen-BPS-C3){reference-type="ref" reference="Zopen-BPS-C3"}) and ([\[Zopen-BPS-coni\]](#Zopen-BPS-coni){reference-type="ref" reference="Zopen-BPS-coni"}) by the integral $$Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}} \; \sim \;
\int dz\; e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(V(z,x;q_1)+{\cal
                         O}(\hbar)\right)} \,.     \label{Zopen-BPS-saddle}$$ As the computation is analogous for ${\mathbb C}^3$ and the conifold, we present it just in the latter case; the result for ${\mathbb C}^3$ is easily obtained by setting $Q=0$. Therefore, using the conifold amplitude in the form ([\[Zopen-BPS-coni\]](#Zopen-BPS-coni){reference-type="ref" reference="Zopen-BPS-coni"}) and the expansion of the quantum dilogarithm given in ([\[q-dlog_exp\]](#q-dlog_exp){reference-type="ref" reference="q-dlog_exp"}), we find $$\begin{aligned}
V(z,x;q_1) & = & (\log z)\big(\pi i (f+1) + \log x q_1   \big) + \frac{f}{2} (\log z)^2 \nonumber \\
& & + \, \textrm{Li}_2(q_1 z) - \textrm{Li}_2(Q q_1^{-1/2} z) - \textrm{Li}_2(q_1) + \textrm{Li}_2(Q q_1^{-1/2}) \,. \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ The saddle point equation $\partial_z V |_{z=z_0}=0$, written in the exponential form $1=\exp (z \partial_z V |_{z=z_0})$, leads to the condition $$1 \; = \; -x q_1 (-z_0)^f  \frac{1 - Q z_0 q_1^{-1/2}}{1 - z_0 q_1} \,.    \label{Coni-saddle1}$$ On the other hand, from the relation between $y$ and $S_0\equiv V$ we get $$y = e^{S_0'} = e^{x\partial_x V} = z_0 \,.     \label{Coni-saddle2}$$ Eliminating $z_0$ from the above two equations we find $$A^{\text{ref}} (x,y;q_1) = 1 - q_1 y + q_1 x (-y)^f + Q \sqrt{q_1} \, x (-y)^{f+1}.$$ This result indeed reproduces the refined $A$-polynomial for the conifold ([\[Aq1-coni\]](#Aq1-coni){reference-type="ref" reference="Aq1-coni"}), and setting $Q=0$ gives the refined curve for the ${\mathbb C}^3$ geometry obtained in ([\[Aq1-C3\]](#Aq1-C3){reference-type="ref" reference="Aq1-C3"}).

We also note that the two equations ([\[Coni-saddle1\]](#Coni-saddle1){reference-type="ref" reference="Coni-saddle1"}) and ([\[Coni-saddle2\]](#Coni-saddle2){reference-type="ref" reference="Coni-saddle2"}) can be rewritten in the form $$\left\{\begin{array}{l} x=x(z_0) = -(-z_0)^{-f} \frac{q_1^{-1}-z_0}{1 - Q z_0 q_1^{-1/2}} \,, \\
y=y(z_0) = z_0  \,. \end{array} \right.
\label{Conif-param}$$ This is a parametric form of the refined mirror curve, and for $q_1=1$ it agrees with the parametrization considered in [@abmodel], where such a representation was used to find the (unrefined) quantum curve. Nonetheless, the framework of [@abmodel] cannot be applied to the present case since it works only if we start from the unrefined classical curve, and upon quantization introduce a single quantum parameter $q=q_1=q_2$. On the other hand, for the purposes of the present paper, we would like to keep the *classical* parameter $q_1$ fixed in ([\[Conif-param\]](#Conif-param){reference-type="ref" reference="Conif-param"}), and perform quantization with respect to another (*quantum*) parameter $q_2$; this is a different quantization problem compared to the one considered in [@abmodel].

We also note, that in fact it is possible to construct a deformed classical curve, whose associated closed string amplitudes (computed by the ordinary Eynard-Orantin topological recursion [@eyn-or]) would coincide with refined topological string amplitudes. Such curves have been constructed in [@Sulkowski:2010ux; @Sulkowski:2011qs; @Eynard:2011vs] for toric manifolds without compact 4-cycles, and in that case a classical deformation parameter was identified as $\beta=-\epsilon_1/\epsilon_2$. For example, for ${\mathbb C}^3$ such a $\beta$-deformed mirror curve can be written as [@Eynard:2011vs]: $$A(x,y;\beta) \; = \; x^2 y - (1+y)^{1+\beta} \,,$$ so that for $\beta=1$ it reduces to the mirror curve of ${\mathbb C}^3$ in framing $\frac{1}{2}$. However, this curve is completely different than the refined curve which we found now in ([\[Aq1-C3\]](#Aq1-C3){reference-type="ref" reference="Aq1-C3"}). It is desirable to understand the relation between these two deformed classical curves, and between the two corresponding quantization schemes.

Having found the refined mirror curves, we can finally determine the leading free energy, given by the integral $S_0(x,q_1)=\int\log y\frac{dx}{x}$ on an appropriate curve. For ${\mathbb C}^3$ we integrate over the curve ([\[Aq1-C3\]](#Aq1-C3){reference-type="ref" reference="Aq1-C3"}). The explicit expression in terms of the variable $x$ can be written in framing $f=0$, so that $$S_0 (x,q_1; f=0) \; = \; - (\log x) ( \log q_1) - \textrm{Li}_2 (-q_1 x) \,.
\label{S0C3}$$ In this case, we also clearly see a singularity at $q_1\to 0$. For general framing we can express the exact answer in terms of $y$ variable $$S_0(x,q_1; f) \; = \; -\frac{f}{2}  ( \log y)^2 + (\log y) (\log(1-y q_1)) + \textrm{Li}_2(y q_1) \,.$$

In our next example, namely the conifold, the free energy $S_0(x,q_1)$ is a $Q$-deformation of the ${\mathbb C}^3$ result. An explicit expression in terms of the variable $x$ can again be given in framing $f=0$, so that $$S_0(x,q_1; f=0) \; = \; - (\log x) ( \log q_1) - \textrm{Li}_2 (-q_1 x) + \textrm{Li}_2 (-\frac{Q x}{\sqrt{q_1}}) \,.
\label{S0conifold}$$ In this case we also clearly see a singularity as $q_1\to 0$. For general framing we can express the exact answer in terms of the variable $y$: $$S_0 (x,q_1; f) \; = \; -\frac{f}{2}  ( \log y)^2 + (\log y) \Big(\log\frac{1-y q_1}{1 - \frac{y Q}{\sqrt{q_1}}}\Big) + \textrm{Li}_2(y q_1)
- \textrm{Li}_2 (-\frac{Q y}{\sqrt{q_1}}) \,.$$

Finally, let us comment on the quantizability of the refined classical curves which we found in this section. These curves are, in a sense, more general than the refined $A$-polynomials for knots analysed in section [\[sec:knots\]](#sec:knots){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:knots"}. This is so because, apart from the deformation parameter $q_1$, they in general depend on Kähler parameters $Q_i$. Therefore, the quantizability conditions should impose some constraints on $q_1$ as well as on $Q_i$, and this is indeed what happens.

Much as in the analysis of the refined $A$-polynomials for knots, we can consider the quantization condition ([\[tempered\]](#tempered){reference-type="ref" reference="tempered"}). To this end, we need to construct face polynomials $f(z)$ for all faces of Newton polygons for BPS quantum curves. Such Newton polygons, for both ${\mathbb C}^3$ and the conifold, are shown in figure [1](#fig:NewtonC3Coni){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:NewtonC3Coni"}. These polygons coincide in refined and unrefined cases. In fact, they are quite simple, as each face contains only two lattice points, so that all face polynomials $f(z)$ are linear in $z$. For example, for ${\mathbb C}^3$ they look like $$z-q_1 \,,\qquad z+(-1)^f q_1 \,,\qquad z - (-1)^f \,.$$ Therefore, in the refined ${\mathbb C}^3$ case, we immediately conclude that meeting the constraint ([\[tempered\]](#tempered){reference-type="ref" reference="tempered"}) requires $q_1$ to be a root of unity, much as in the knot theory examples, where the deformation parameter $t$ was forced to be a root of unity. In the conifold case, we find that both $q_1$ and $Q$ must be roots of unity. This imposes an interesting condition on the Kähler parameter: $\log Q$ must be a pure imaginary rational number. We expect that similar conditions arise for refined mirror curves of general manifolds, *i.e.* $q_1$ and all Kähler parameters $Q_i$ in general are required to be roots of unity.

## Refinement as a twisted mass parameter

Now, let us discuss the physical interpretation of the general setup considered in this section and the corresponding interpretation of the classical action $S_0 (u,t)$.

As we already reviewed in the beginning of this section, compactification of type II string theory on a toric Calabi-Yau 3-fold $X$ is a natural arena for a "geometric engineering" of ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ supersymmetric gauge theories in the remaining four space-time dimensions. In this setup, BPS states of the "effective" four-dimensional gauge theory are simply the closed BPS states from the vantage point of the Calabi-Yau 3-fold $X$. Moreover, counting *refined* closed BPS states means that, from the viewpoint of the 4d ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ theory, we keep track of their spin $j_3$.

Much of our interest comes from counting *open* refined BPS states in a slight generalization of this setup, with a D4-brane added: $$\begin{matrix}
{\mbox{\rm space-time:}} & \qquad & {\mathbb R}^4 & \times & X \\
& \qquad & \cup &  & \cup \\
{\mbox{\rm D4-brane:}} & \qquad & {\mathbb R}^2 & \times & L
\end{matrix}
\label{surfeng}$$ Since the extra D4-brane here is supported on a special Lagrangian submanifold $L \subset X$, it preserves half of the supersymmetry and, as explained in [@DGH], yields a "geometric engineering" of a half-BPS surface operator in ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ gauge theory on ${\mathbb R}^4$ (see [@Ramified; @Gknothom; @Rigid] for a purely field theoretic definition of surface operators). In a special case when $X$ is the conifold and $L_K$ is the Lagrangian submanifold associated to a knot $K$, the system [\[surfeng\]](#surfeng){reference-type="eqref" reference="surfeng"} becomes identical to the physical setup [\[theoryB\]](#theoryB){reference-type="eqref" reference="theoryB"} discussed in section [\[sec:knots\]](#sec:knots){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:knots"}, after compactification on a circle from M-theory to type IIA string theory.

From the viewpoint of the ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ gauge theory on ${\mathbb R}^4$, the generating function of open BPS invariants $Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}}$ that plays a central role in this section can be interpreted as the instanton partition function of the gauge theory in the presence of a surface operator [@DGH; @Braverman; @AGGTV; @AFKMY], much like its "closed" counterpart $Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{closed}}$ is the K-theory version of the equivariant instanton counting on ${\mathbb R}^4$ without surface operators [@Nekrasov; @NYlectures].

Let $SO(2)_1 \times SO(2)_2$ be the rotation symmetry of ${\mathbb R}^4$ preserved by the surface operator in [\[surfeng\]](#surfeng){reference-type="eqref" reference="surfeng"}, such that $SO(2)_2$ is the rotation symmetry along the ${\mathbb R}^2 \subset {\mathbb R}^4$ and $SO(2)_1$ is the rotation symmetry of its normal bundle. Note, from the viewpoint of the Euclidean ${\mathcal{N}}=(2,2)$ theory on the surface operator, $SO(2)_2$ is a rotation symmetry (part of the two-dimensional Poincaré group), while $SO(2)_1$ is a global R-symmetry. In fact, both symmetries in this brane system were already discussed in section [\[sec:refinedbraid\]](#sec:refinedbraid){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:refinedbraid"}, where we gave them a name $U(1)_P$ and $U(1)_F$, respectively. In particular, the global R-symmetry is: $$U(1)_F \; \cong \; SO(2)_1
\label{U1Fso2}$$

If, as usual, we denote by $\epsilon_1$ and $\epsilon_2$ the equivariant parameters for $SO(2)_1$ and $SO(2)_2$, respectively, then the generating functions $Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{closed}}$ and $Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}}$ are known to have the following form, see *e.g.* [@KreflWalcher; @KlemmHuang]: $$\begin{aligned}
Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{closed}} (\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)
& = & \exp \Bigl(\, \frac{1}{\epsilon_1 \epsilon_2} {\mathcal{F}}(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2) \, \Bigr) \label{ZclosedW} \\
& = & \exp \Bigl(\, \frac{1}{\epsilon_1 \epsilon_2} {\mathcal{F}}^{(0)}
+ \frac{\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2}{\epsilon_1 \epsilon_2} {\mathcal{F}}^{(1)} + \ldots \, \Bigr) \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ and $$Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}} (\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)
\; = \; \exp \Bigl(\, \frac{1}{\epsilon_2} \widetilde{\cal W} + \ldots  \, \Bigr)
\label{ZopenW}$$ where ${\mathcal{F}}^{(0)}$ is the prepotential of the four-dimensional ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ theory and $\widetilde{\cal W}$ is the twisted superpotential of the two-dimensional ${\mathcal{N}}=(2,2)$ theory on the surface operator. In particular, as explained in [@AGGTV] (see also [@AwataYamada; @TakiMaruyoshi; @AFKMY]), the twisted superpotential is given by the integral of the Seiberg-Witten differential $v du = \log y \frac{dx}{x}$ over a path: $$\widetilde{\cal W} (u) \; = \; \int^u v du$$ on the curve $A(x,y)=0$.

Now, let us explain how our discussion in this paper compares to the Nekrasov-Shatashvili limit and its relation to quantum integrable systems. There are some similarities and some differences, and both are important. First, the limit considered in [@NS] in our notations is $\epsilon_1 = 0$ with $\epsilon_2$ playing the role of the quantization parameter (in the quantum integrable system). This is very similar to the limit [\[reflimit\]](#reflimit){reference-type="eqref" reference="reflimit"} and the discussion in this section, where $\hbar = - \epsilon_2$ also plays the role of the quantization parameter and the only essential difference is that $\epsilon_1$ (equivalently, $q_1 = e^{\epsilon_1}$) is allowed to take any finite values, so that for small $\hbar$ we have $t = - \frac{q_1}{q_2} \sim - q_1$. In this respect, what we consider can be viewed as a refinement of the Nekrasov-Shatashvili limit, *cf.* Figure [\[fig:qqqt\]](#fig:qqqt){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:qqqt"}.

There is an important difference, however, which has to do with the fact that [@NS] consider the partition function of the 4d gauge theory or, from the vantage point of the Calabi-Yau 3-fold $X$, the generating function of *closed* BPS invariants [\[ZclosedW\]](#ZclosedW){reference-type="eqref" reference="ZclosedW"}. In particular, the Yang-Yang function of the quantum integrable system is identified with the following limit of the instanton partition function in the absence of any surface operators [@NS]: $$\lim_{\epsilon_1 \to 0} \, \epsilon_1 \, \log Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{closed}} (\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)
\; = \; \frac{1}{\epsilon_2} {\mathcal{F}}^{(0)} + {\mathcal{F}}^{(1)} + {\cal O }(\epsilon_2)
\label{YangYang}$$ This expansion should not be confused with a similar-looking expansion in [\[ZopenW\]](#ZopenW){reference-type="eqref" reference="ZopenW"}, which describes the behavior of the *open* BPS partition function or, from the gauge theory viewpoint, represents the contribution of a surface operator. In particular, the leading term ${\mathcal{F}}^{(0)}$ in [\[YangYang\]](#YangYang){reference-type="eqref" reference="YangYang"} depends only on the closed string moduli, whereas a similar leading term $\widetilde{\cal W}$ in [\[ZopenW\]](#ZopenW){reference-type="eqref" reference="ZopenW"} depends on both open and closed string moduli. Thus, the variable $u$ in our discussion or, equivalently, $x = e^u$ is an open string modulus.

Keeping these remarks in mind, we can express the refined volume conjecture ([\[VCparamref\]](#VCparamref){reference-type="ref" reference="VCparamref"}b) as a statement that the free energy $\log Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}} (q_1, q_2)$ has a first-order pole in the limit [\[reflimit\]](#reflimit){reference-type="eqref" reference="reflimit"}: $$\log Z_{\text{BPS}}^{\text{open}} (\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)
\; = \; \frac{S_0 (u,t)}{\hbar} + \ldots
\qquad \text{as~~} \boxed{ \phantom{I} q_2 = e^{\hbar} \to 1
\text{~and~} \frac{q_1}{q_2} = - t = \text{finite}~}~ \,,
\label{logZopenSt}$$ where we used [\[qqqt\]](#qqqt){reference-type="eqref" reference="qqqt"}. This, at the same time, is both *open* and *refined* generalization of [\[YangYang\]](#YangYang){reference-type="eqref" reference="YangYang"}. In particular, in the special case $t = -1$ (or, equivalently, $q_1 = 1$) we recover [\[ZopenW\]](#ZopenW){reference-type="eqref" reference="ZopenW"}, with the twisted superpotential $$\widetilde{\cal W} (u) \; = \; S_0 (u,-1) \,.$$ Therefore, we conclude that $S_0 (u,t)$ which appears in the refined volume conjecture ([\[VCparamref\]](#VCparamref){reference-type="ref" reference="VCparamref"}b) is a "refinement" of the twisted superpotential in the ${\mathcal{N}}=(2,2)$ surface operator theory[^1] with a twisted mass for the global symmetry $U(1)_F$ (= rotation in the plane orthogonal to the surface operator), *cf.* [\[ZrefdefP\]](#ZrefdefP){reference-type="eqref" reference="ZrefdefP"}: $$\tilde m_F \; = \; \log (-t) \,.
\label{mftrel}$$ As a simple example, let us consider a two-dimensional ${\mathcal{N}}=(2,2)$ theory with a tower of Kaluza-Klein states obtained by reducing a three-dimensional ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ chiral multiplet on a circle. The twisted superpotential of this theory has the familiar form $\widetilde{\cal W} = \textrm{Li}_2(-t x)$, where we assumed that the 3d chiral multiplet on ${\mathbb R}^2 \times {\bf S}^1$ has charge $+1$ with respect to both $U(1)_F$ as well as the global $U(1)$ symmetry with twisted mass $\log x$. Therefore, in the twisted superpotential [\[S0conifold\]](#S0conifold){reference-type="eqref" reference="S0conifold"} we can recognize a contribution of two chiral multiplets. Similarly, in the expression [\[S0C3\]](#S0C3){reference-type="eqref" reference="S0C3"} for the tetrahedron (or ${\mathbb C}^3$) we recognize contribution of a single chiral multiplet. In general, the number of chiral multiplets is equal to the number of dilogarithms in $S_0 (u,t)$, and the charges of chiral multiplets are simply the powers of $t$ and $x$ in the arguments of these dilogarithms. It is easy to recognize such contributions *e.g.* in [\[Pn-saddle\]](#Pn-saddle){reference-type="eqref" reference="Pn-saddle"}.

To summarize, each chiral multiplet in the D4-brane theory [\[surfeng\]](#surfeng){reference-type="eqref" reference="surfeng"} (or, to be more precise, in the five-brane theory on a circle, *cf.* [\[theoryB\]](#theoryB){reference-type="eqref" reference="theoryB"}) contributes to the twisted chiral superpotential a dilogarithm term: $$\text{tetrahedron}~\Delta
\qquad \leftrightarrow \qquad
\text{chiral}~\phi
\qquad \leftrightarrow \qquad
\begin{array}{l}
\text{twisted superpotential} \\[.1cm]
S_0 (\Delta; \vec u, t) = \textrm{Li}_2( e^{\vec n \cdot \vec u + n_F \, \tilde m_F} )
\end{array}$$ where $n_F$ is the charge of the chiral multiplet under the global R-symmetry [\[U1Fso2\]](#U1Fso2){reference-type="eqref" reference="U1Fso2"} and $\vec n$ denotes the charges of the chiral multiplet under all other flavor symmetries (with twisted mass parameters $\vec u$). Here, we also identified the example of a single chiral multiplet (or ${\mathbb C}^3$) discussed in the present section with a single tetrahedron in examples coming from 3-manifolds, *cf.* [@DGH; @DGSdual; @abmodel; @DGG; @CCV].

More generally, one can consider a three-dimensional ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ theory $\mathcal{T}$ that contains $N_f$ chiral multiplets $\phi_i$, $i=1, \ldots, N_f$ with charges $n^i_a$ under global flavor symmetries $U(1)_a$, $a = 1, \ldots, N$. This could be either a low-dimensional effective field theory in brane systems [\[theoryB\]](#theoryB){reference-type="eqref" reference="theoryB"} and [\[surfeng\]](#surfeng){reference-type="eqref" reference="surfeng"}, or a three-dimensional ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ theory $T_M$ associated to a triangulation of a 3-manifold $M$, $$M \; = \; \bigcup_i \, \Delta_i \,.$$ In either case, the refinement is achieved by assigning each chiral multiplet $\phi_i$ (resp. each tetrahedron $\Delta_i$) one extra charge $n_F^i$ that describes how $\phi_i$ transforms under the R-symmetry $U(1)_F$, which may already be a part of $\prod_a U(1)_a$. Then, using [\[mftrel\]](#mftrel){reference-type="eqref" reference="mftrel"}, we conclude that passing from unrefined theory to refined theory has the effect of introducing the $t$-dependence in the classical "volume functional" (= twisted superpotential) via a simple rule: $$\widetilde{\cal W} (x_i) = \sum_i \textrm{Li}_2 \big( \prod_a x_a^{n_a^i} \big)
\quad \leadsto \quad
S_0 (x_i, t) = \sum_i \; \textrm{Li}_2 \; \Big( (-t)^{n_F^i} \prod_a x_a^{n_a^i} \Big)$$ modulo logarithmic ambiguities that depend on choices of framing, polarization, *etc.* In other words, at least in such models, the essential information about the refinement is contained in a set of charges $\{ n_F^i \}$ that need to be assigned to chiral multiplets or, in the language of 3-manifolds, to tetrahedra $\Delta_i$. While many examples are considered in the present paper (see Table [1](#tab-nF){reference-type="ref" reference="tab-nF"}), a systematic rule for assigning $\{ n_F^i \}$ will be presented in the follow-up work.

[^1]: To be more precise, it is a three-dimensional ${\mathcal{N}}=2$ theory on a circle that is relevant to the K-theoretic version of the vortex partition function [@DGH].
