Our next goal is to describe a very rich structure of the colored superpolynomials that can be used either as an alternative way to compute them or as a tool to verify their correctness. As in the previous discussion and in most of the literature on this subject [@KhPatterns; @KhR1; @DGR], ${\cal P}^R (K;a,q,t)$ stands for the *reduced* superpolynomial of a knot $K$ colored by $R$, and its unreduced version is denoted with a bar.

Suppose, for example, that we wish to compute the $S^2$-colored HOMFLY homology ${\cal H }^{S^2}_{i,j,k} (T^{2,5})$ and the corresponding superpolynomial ${\cal P}^{S^2} (T^{2,5}; a,q,t)$ of the $(2,5)$ torus knot, also known as the knot ${\bf 5}_1$. By definition [\[superPdef\]](#superPdef){reference-type="eqref" reference="superPdef"}, at $t=-1$ the colored superpolynomial reduces to the colored HOMFLY polynomial [\[PfromH\]](#PfromH){reference-type="eqref" reference="PfromH"}, which for the knot $T^{2,5}$ has 25 terms, see *e.g.* [@LinZheng]: $$\begin{aligned}
P^{S^2} (T^{2,5}; a,q)
&=& a^6 \left( q + q^4 + q^5 + q^7 \right) \label{P25S2} \\
&& + a^5 \left( - q^{-2} - q^{-1} - q - 2 q^2 - q^3 - q^4 - 2 q^5 - q^6 - q^7 - q^8 \right) \nonumber \\
&& + a^4 \left( q^{-4} + q^{-1} + 1 + q^2 + q^3 + q^4 + q^5 + q^6 + q^8 \right) \,. \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ Each terms in this expression comes from a certain generator of the triply-graded colored HOMFLY homology ${\cal H }^{S^2}_{i,j,k} (T^{2,5})$, *cf.* Figure [\[fig:superpolunmial_hier\]](#fig:superpolunmial_hier){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:superpolunmial_hier"}. Therefore, we conclude that ${\cal H }^{S^2}_{i,j,k} (T^{2,5})$ must be at least 25-dimensional. How can we restore the homological $t$-grading?

There are many ways to do that, based on the structure of the commuting differentials [\[gradingtabl\]](#gradingtabl){reference-type="eqref" reference="gradingtabl"}. For instance, one way is to pick a relation in the infinite set [\[specN\]](#specN){reference-type="eqref" reference="specN"} labeled by $N \in {\mathbb Z}$, and to study its implications. Thus, in our present example, the relation [\[specN\]](#specN){reference-type="eqref" reference="specN"} with $N=2$ says that the homology of ${\cal H }^{S^2}_{i,j,k} (T^{2,5})$ with respect to the differential $d_2$ should be isomorphic to the $S^2$ colored $sl(2)$ homology ${\cal H }^{sl(2),S^2}_{i,j} (T^{2,5})$, $$\left( {\cal H }^{S^2}_{*,*,*} (K) , d_2 \right)
\; \cong \;
{\cal H }^{sl(2), S^2}_{*,*} (K)
\; \cong \;
{\cal H }^{so(3), V}_{*,*} (K) \,,$$ where in the last isomorphism we used the identification between the symmetric representation $R = S^2$ of $sl(2)$ and the 3-dimensional vector representation of $so(3)$. The latter homology was studied in [@GWalcher], where explicit answers were tabulated for all prime knots with up to 7 crossings.[^1] In particular, for the $(2,5)$ torus knot $T^{2,5}$ the homology ${\cal H }^{so(3), V}_{i,j} (K)$ is also 25-dimensional, and by matching the results of [@GWalcher] with the specialization ${\cal P}^{S^2} (T^{2,5}; a=q^2,q,t)$ one can restore the $t$-grading of every term in [\[P25S2\]](#P25S2){reference-type="eqref" reference="P25S2"}: $$\begin{aligned}
{\cal P}^{S^2} (T^{2,5}; a,q,t)
&=& a^6 \left( q t^6 + q^4 t^8 + q^5 t^8 + q^7 t^{10} \right) \label{superP25S2} \\
&+& a^5 \left( q^{-2} t^3 + q^{-1} t^3 + q t^5 + 2 q^2 t^5 + q^3 t^5 + q^4 t^7 + 2 q^5 t^7 + q^6 t^7 + q^7 t^9 + q^8 t^9 \right) \nonumber \\
&+& a^4 \left( q^{-4} + q^{-1} t^2 + t^2 + q^2 t^4 + q^3 t^4 + q^4 t^4 + q^5 t^6 + q^6 t^6 + q^8 t^8 \right) \, , \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ where we tacitly assumed that the entire $S^2$-colored homology ${\cal H }^{S^2}_{i,j,k} (T^{2,5})$ is indeed 25-dimensional, so that $Q^{sl(2),S^2} = 0$ in [\[superright\]](#superright){reference-type="eqref" reference="superright"} for the present example. This can be easily justified by looking at the other differentials in [\[specN\]](#specN){reference-type="eqref" reference="specN"} or the corresponding Poincaré polynomials [\[superright\]](#superright){reference-type="eqref" reference="superright"}--[\[PRright\]](#PRright){reference-type="eqref" reference="PRright"}.

Everything we saw in this simple example can be easily generalized to other knots (in fact, not just torus knots) and other representations. As the knot $K$ is getting bigger, the size of the colored HOMFLY homology ${\cal H }^{R}_{i,j,k} (K)$ typically grows as well. At the same time, for larger knots and larger homology more differentials act on ${\cal H }^{R}_{i,j,k} (K)$ in an interesting way, thus providing non-trivial constraints. Among the infinite set of differentials in [\[gradingtabl\]](#gradingtabl){reference-type="eqref" reference="gradingtabl"} there are some special ones, which always act non-trivially, no matter how large or small the knot $K$ is. These are the so-called *canceling* differentials which get their name after the fact that, when acting on ${\cal H }^{R}_{i,j,k} (K)$, they cancel almost all of the terms, except a single one, *i.e.* $$\dim \left( {\cal H }^R (K) , d_{\text{canceling}} \right) \; = \; 1 \,.
\label{specNcanc}$$ At first, even the very existence of such differentials might seem very surprising. However, they all usually have a simple origin and interpretation.

Let us consider, for example, the $S^r$-colored HOMFLY homology ${\cal H }^{S^r}_{i,j,k} (K)$ relevant to the present paper. Then, as we already pointed out in the discussion below [\[superwrong\]](#superwrong){reference-type="eqref" reference="superwrong"}, the $sl(1)$ homology ${\cal H }^{sl(1), S^r}_{i,j} (K)$ should be trivial (*i.e.* one-dimensional) for every knot $K$. When combined with [\[specN\]](#specN){reference-type="eqref" reference="specN"}, this basic property implies that the differential $d_1$ must be canceling for the $S^r$-colored HOMFLY homology ${\cal H }^{S^r}_{i,j,k} (K)$.

Another canceling differential in the same theory is $d_{-r}$, whose origin is very similar: the representation $R = \Lambda^r$ of $sl(N)$ is trivial when $N=r$. As a result, ${\cal H }^{sl(r), \Lambda^r}_{i,j} (K)$ should also be one-dimensional for every knot $K$, and from [\[gradchoicea\]](#gradchoicea){reference-type="eqref" reference="gradchoicea"} it follows that $d_{-r}$ is also a canceling differential for ${\cal H }^{S^r}_{i,j,k} (K)$. To summarize, in our standard conventions the canceling differentials in the $S^r$-colored HOMFLY theory have degree $$\deg (d_1) = (-1,1,-1) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \deg (d_{-r}) = (-1,-r,-3) \,.
\label{dcancgrading}$$ These canceling differentials pair up almost all of the terms in the Poincaré polynomial of the homology ${\cal H }^{S^r}_{i,j,k} (K)$ which, therefore, has the structure [\[superright\]](#superright){reference-type="eqref" reference="superright"}: $$\begin{aligned}
{\cal P}^{S^r} (K;a,q,t) &=& a^{rs} q^{-rs} t^{0} + (1+a^{-1}qt^{-1}) Q_1 (K;a,q,t) \nonumber \\
&=&a^{rs}q^{r^2s}t^{2rs}+(1+a^{-1}q^{-r}t^{-3}) Q_{-r} (K;a,q,t) \,,
\label{super_monomial1}
\end{aligned}$$ with a monomial "remainder" $R (a,q,t) = a^i q^j t^k$ and $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (-1,1,-1)$ or $(-1,-r,-3)$ for $d_1$ or $d_{-r}$, respectively. Since [\[super_monomial1\]](#super_monomial1){reference-type="eqref" reference="super_monomial1"} is obtained by taking the Poincaré polynomial of both sides in [\[specNcanc\]](#specNcanc){reference-type="eqref" reference="specNcanc"}, the polynomials $Q_1$ and $Q_{-r}$ have non-negative integer coefficients. Furthermore, the $(a,q,t)$-degrees of the "remainder" are determined by a single integer $s(K)$, the so-called $S$-invariant of the knot $K$ (see [@GS] for details).

The structure [\[super_monomial1\]](#super_monomial1){reference-type="eqref" reference="super_monomial1"} is a "colored generalization" of the familiar property of the ordinary HOMFLY homology [@DGR] that corresponds to $r=1$ and comes equipped with two canceling differentials $d_1$ and $d_{-1}$. In that context, the $S$-invariant is expected to provide a lower bound on the slice[^2] genus $g_* (K)$ of the knot, which in our normalization reads $$|s (K)| \; \le \; g_* (K)$$ and is often tight. The slice genus of the $(p,q)$ torus knot is [@MilnorSing; @Rasmussen]: $$g_* (T^{p,q}) \; = \; \frac{(p-1)(q-1)}{2}
\qquad\qquad
\text{(The Milnor Conjecture)}$$ so that for $(2,2p+1)$ torus knots we expect $$s (T^{2,2p+1}) \; = \; p \,.
\label{2forptorknot}$$

Substituting [\[2forptorknot\]](#2forptorknot){reference-type="eqref" reference="2forptorknot"} into [\[super_monomial1\]](#super_monomial1){reference-type="eqref" reference="super_monomial1"}, it is easy to verify that our result [\[superP25S2\]](#superP25S2){reference-type="eqref" reference="superP25S2"} indeed has the expected structure, thereby, illustrating how a combination of the structural properties that follow from action of differentials can determine ${\cal H }^{R}_{i,j,k} (K)$. In practice, starting with the colored HOMFLY polynomial $P^R (K;a,q)$, usually one needs only a few of the relations like [\[specN\]](#specN){reference-type="eqref" reference="specN"} in order to find its homological lift (*a.k.a.* "categorification"). Then, the rest of the relations can be used as consistency checks. For simple knots (with less than 10 crossings or so) this typically gives a largely overconstrained system, that miraculously has a solution.

Just like it is easier to differentiate a function $f(x)$ rather than to integrate it, it is much easier to use the structure based on the commuting differentials [\[gradingtabl\]](#gradingtabl){reference-type="eqref" reference="gradingtabl"} to verify the correctness of a particular result than to derive it. Thus, even though in principle we could extend the derivation of [\[superP25S2\]](#superP25S2){reference-type="eqref" reference="superP25S2"} to more general torus knots and higher dimensional representation, verifying the correctness of our results [\[Superpolynomial_sym_refined\]](#Superpolynomial_sym_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Superpolynomial_sym_refined"} and [\[Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined\]](#Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined"} is much easier. Indeed, specializing to $a = - q t^{-1}$ and $a = - q^{-r} t^{-3}$ in [\[Superpolynomial_sym_refined\]](#Superpolynomial_sym_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Superpolynomial_sym_refined"} we can verify [\[super_monomial1\]](#super_monomial1){reference-type="eqref" reference="super_monomial1"} in no time!

Similarly, we can run a test on the $\Lambda^r$-colored superpolynomials [\[Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined\]](#Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined"} of the $(2,2p+1)$ torus knots. In view of the mirror symmetry [\[mirror\]](#mirror){reference-type="eqref" reference="mirror"}, this is not really an independent test, but it is still instructive to see how it works. (In particular, it helps to understand the role of mirror symmetry.) As we already discussed earlier, passing from $R = S^r$ to $R^t = \Lambda^r$ can be achieved by changing the sign of $N$. Therefore, if $d_1$ and $d_{-r}$ are canceling differentials in ${\cal H }^{S^r}_{i,j,k} (K)$, then $d_{-1}$ and $d_r$ must be canceling differentials in the $\Lambda^r$-colored HOMFLY homology ${\cal H }^{\Lambda^r}_{i,j,k} (K)$. According to [\[gradingtabl\]](#gradingtabl){reference-type="eqref" reference="gradingtabl"}, the degrees of these differentials are $$\deg (d_{-1}) = (-1,-1,-3) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \deg (d_{r}) = (-1,r,-1) \,,$$ so that [\[specNcanc\]](#specNcanc){reference-type="eqref" reference="specNcanc"} implies the following structure of the $\Lambda^r$-colored superpolynomial, *cf.* [\[superright\]](#superright){reference-type="eqref" reference="superright"} and [\[super_monomial1\]](#super_monomial1){reference-type="eqref" reference="super_monomial1"}: $$\begin{aligned}
{\cal P}^{\Lambda^r} (K; a,q,t) &=& a^{rs} q^{rs} t^{2rs} + (1+a^{-1}q^{-1}t^{-3}) Q_{-1} (K;a,q,t)
\nonumber \\
&=& a^{rs} q^{-r^2s} t^0 + (1+a^{-1}q^{r}t^{-1}) Q_{r} (K;a,q,t) \,.
\label{super_monomial2}
\end{aligned}$$ It is easy to verify that all our results indeed have this remarkable structure. Indeed, using [\[2forptorknot\]](#2forptorknot){reference-type="eqref" reference="2forptorknot"} for $K = T^{2,2p+1}$ and specializing to $a=-q^{-1}t^{-3}$ and $a=-q^{r}t^{-1}$ in ([\[Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined\]](#Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined){reference-type="ref" reference="Superpolynomial_anti-sym_refined"}) we find that, respectively, only $\ell=r$ and $\ell=0$ contributions survive.\
For completeness, let us describe how the same structure looks in the "DGR conventions" [\[Symmetric_DGR_refined\]](#Symmetric_DGR_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Symmetric_DGR_refined"} and [\[Anti-Symmetric_DGR_refined\]](#Anti-Symmetric_DGR_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Anti-Symmetric_DGR_refined"}. The grading of the canceling differentials $d_1$ and $d_{-r}$ for $R = S^r$ can be read off directly from [\[gradingtabl\]](#gradingtabl){reference-type="eqref" reference="gradingtabl"}: $$\deg_{\text{DGR}} \, (d_1) = (-2,2,-1) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \deg_{\text{DGR}} \, (d_{-r}) = (-2,-2r,-2r-1) \,,$$ and, similarly, the gradings of the canceling differentials $d_{-1}$ and $d_{r}$ for $R = \Lambda^r$ are $$\deg_{\text{DGR}} \, (d_{-1}) = (-2,-2,-3) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \deg_{\text{DGR}} \, (d_{r}) = (-2,2r,-1) \,.$$ Substituting these values of $(a,q,t)$-degrees $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ in the general formula [\[superright\]](#superright){reference-type="eqref" reference="superright"}, we arrive to the following structure of the colored superpolynomials in the grading conventions of [@DGR]: $$\begin{aligned}
{\cal P}^{S^r}_{\text{DGR}} (K; a,q,t)
&=&a^{2rs}q^{-2rs}t^{0} + (1+a^{-2}q^{-2}t^{-1})Q_1^{\text{DGR}}(K;a,q,t)
\nonumber \\
&=&a^{2rs}q^{2r^2 s} t^{2r^2 s} + (1+a^{-2}q^{-2r}t^{-2r-1})Q_{-r}^{\text{DGR}}(K;a,q,t) \,,
\\
{\cal P}^{\Lambda^r}_{\text{DGR}} (K; a,q,t)
&=&a^{2rs} q^{2rs} t^{2rs} + (1+a^{-2}q^{-2}t^{-3})Q_{-1}^{\text{DGR}}(K;a,q,t)
\nonumber \\
&=&a^{2rs} q^{-2r^2 s} t^0 + (1+a^{-2}q^{2r}t^{-1})Q_{r}^{\text{DGR}}(K;a,q,t) \,.
\end{aligned}$$ Using $s=p$ for $K=T^{2,2p+1}$, it easy to verify that our results [\[Symmetric_DGR_refined\]](#Symmetric_DGR_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Symmetric_DGR_refined"} and [\[Anti-Symmetric_DGR_refined\]](#Anti-Symmetric_DGR_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Anti-Symmetric_DGR_refined"} exhibit this structure, ensuring the validity of some of the steps in section [\[sec:refinedbraid\]](#sec:refinedbraid){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:refinedbraid"}.\
While the above discussion hopefully makes it fairly convincing how powerful and elegant the structure of differentials is, it is just the tip of an iceberg! Indeed, besides the canceling differentials that we discussed in detail and that, in many cases, alone suffice for deducing the superpolynomials from the colored HOMFLY polynomials, there is yet another class of "universal" differentials found in [@GS]. These differentials are called "colored" because they relate homology theories associated with different representations. The simplest example of a colored differential discussed in [@GS] is a differential that, when acting on the $S^2$-colored HOMFLY homology, leaves behind the ordinary, $S$-colored HOMFLY homology, modulo a simple re-grading: $$\left( {\cal H }^{S^2} (K) , d_{\text{colored}} \right)_{2i,k+2j,2k} \; \cong \; {\cal H }^{\Box}_{i,j,k} (K) \,.$$ As in our discussion of [\[specNcanc\]](#specNcanc){reference-type="eqref" reference="specNcanc"} and [\[super_monomial1\]](#super_monomial1){reference-type="eqref" reference="super_monomial1"}, we can take Poincaré polynomials of both sides to learn the following structure of the $S^2$-colored superpolynomial, $${\cal P}^{S^2} (K; a,q,t) \; = \; {\cal P}(K; a^2,q^2, q t^2) + (1 + q) Q (a,q,t) \,,
\label{coloredPa}$$ which corresponds to the first colored differential on our list [\[gradingtabl\]](#gradingtabl){reference-type="eqref" reference="gradingtabl"}. Similarly, the second colored differential in [\[gradingtabl\]](#gradingtabl){reference-type="eqref" reference="gradingtabl"} acts a little differently and leads to a similar, but different relation: $${\cal P}^{S^2} (K; a,q,t) \; = \; a^{s} {\cal P}(K; a,q^2, t) + (1 + a^{-1} t^{-1}) Q (a,q,t) \,.
\label{coloredPb}$$ Both superpolynomials ${\cal P}^{S^2} (K; a,q,t)$ and ${\cal P}(K; a,q,t)$ that participate in these relations can be easily determined from the corresponding HOMFLY polynomials by using the structure of either $d_2$ or canceling differentials. Indeed, as a yet another illustration of this method, let us explain how it works for the torus knots we are interested in.

In the case of torus knots, the starting point of this construction -- namely, the colored HOMFLY polynomial -- is available *e.g.* from [@LinZheng]. In fact, for $R=S$, the explicit expression for the (uncolored) HOMFLY polynomial of an arbitrary torus knot was written already by Jones [@Jones]. In our conventions, the answer for $(2,2p+1)$ torus knots reads $$P (T^{2,2p+1}; a,q) \; = \; - a^{p+1} \sum_{i=1}^p q^{2i - p - 1} + a^{p} \sum_{i=0}^p q^{2i - p} \,.
\label{T22p1HOMFLY}$$ The corresponding (uncolored) superpolynomials of torus knots have the same structure [@DGR]. In fact, for $T^{2,2p+1}$ torus knots, all of the terms in the superpolynomial ${\cal P}(T^{2,2p+1}; a,q,t)$ are "visible" in the HOMFLY polynomial [\[T22p1HOMFLY\]](#T22p1HOMFLY){reference-type="eqref" reference="T22p1HOMFLY"} and, therefore, can be determined from the structure [\[super_monomial1\]](#super_monomial1){reference-type="eqref" reference="super_monomial1"} associated with two canceling differentials $d_1$ and $d_{-1}$. In our grading conventions [\[dcancgrading\]](#dcancgrading){reference-type="eqref" reference="dcancgrading"}, these canceling differentials for $r=1$ have degree $(-1,1,-1)$ and $(-1,-1,-3)$, respectively. Hence, restoring the powers of $t$ in [\[T22p1HOMFLY\]](#T22p1HOMFLY){reference-type="eqref" reference="T22p1HOMFLY"} consistent with $d_1$ and $d_{-1}$ we get $$\begin{aligned}
{\cal P}(T^{2,2p+1}; a,q,t) & = & a^{p+1} \sum_{i=1}^p q^{2i - p - 1} t^{2i+1} + a^{p} \sum_{i=0}^p q^{2i - p} t^{2i} \nonumber \\
& = & \; a^{p} q^{p} t^{2p} \, \frac{a q^{-1} t (1 - q^{-2p} t^{-2p}) + 1 - q^{-2p-2} t^{-2p-2} }{1 - q^{-2} t^{-2}} \,. \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ Similarly, starting with the $S^2$-colored HOMFLY polynomial (see *e.g.* [@LinZheng]), one can derive the $S^2$-colored superpolynomial for all $(2,2p+1)$ torus knots, $$\begin{aligned}
{\cal P}^{S^2} (T^{2,2p+1}; a,q,t)
& = & \frac{q^4 t^4 (a q^2 t^2)^{2 p} (1 + a t) (q + a t)}{1 - q^2 (1 + q) t^2 + q^5 t^4} \label{PS2p} \\
& - & \frac{q^2 t^2 (1 + q) (a q t)^{2 p} (q + a t) (1 + a q^2 t^3)}{1 - q^2 (1 + q^2) t^2 + q^6 t^4}
+ \frac{(a q^{-1})^{2 p} (1 + a q^2 t^3) (1 + a q^3 t^3)}{1 - q^3 (1 + q) t^2 + q^7 t^4} \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ which, of course, is consistent with our earlier result [\[Superpolynomial_sym_refined\]](#Superpolynomial_sym_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Superpolynomial_sym_refined"}. Substituting these colored superpolynomials into relations [\[coloredPa\]](#coloredPa){reference-type="eqref" reference="coloredPa"} and [\[coloredPb\]](#coloredPb){reference-type="eqref" reference="coloredPb"}, it is easy to verify that they work like a charm.\
Finally, after an exciting discussion of colored and canceling differentials that have a universal nature, let us consider a somewhat more rudimentary differential $d_2$ that, according to [\[specN\]](#specN){reference-type="eqref" reference="specN"}, controls specialization to the $sl(2)$ knot homology, see also Figure [\[fig:superpolunmial_hier\]](#fig:superpolunmial_hier){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:superpolunmial_hier"}. This differential is actually very important for the subject of our paper since it accompanies the specialization to $a=q^2$ and, hence, the definition of the invariant $P_n (q,t)$ that appears in the refined volume conjectures [\[VCquantref\]](#VCquantref){reference-type="eqref" reference="VCquantref"} and [\[VCparamref\]](#VCparamref){reference-type="eqref" reference="VCparamref"}. This invariant is defined as a specialization [\[Pq2special\]](#Pq2special){reference-type="eqref" reference="Pq2special"}: $$P_{n=r+1}(K;q,t) \; := \; {\cal P}^{S^r}(K;a=q^2,q,t)
\label{Pq2special123}$$ and, contrary to [\[superwrong\]](#superwrong){reference-type="eqref" reference="superwrong"}, for $(2,2p+1)$ torus knots appears to coincide with the Poincaré polynomial of the $n$-colored $sl(2)$ knot homology ${\cal H }^{sl(2),V_n} (K)$. Indeed, as one can see directly from [\[PS2p\]](#PS2p){reference-type="eqref" reference="PS2p"}, for the trefoil knot $T^{2,3} = {\bf 3}_1$ the $S^2$-colored HOMFLY homology simply contains no terms that can be killed by $d_2$. For the next torus knot $T^{2,5} = {\bf 5}_1$, there are such terms, but according to our discussion around [\[superP25S2\]](#superP25S2){reference-type="eqref" reference="superP25S2"}, none of them are canceled by $d_2$, *i.e.* $Q^{sl(2),S^2} = 0$ in [\[superright\]](#superright){reference-type="eqref" reference="superright"}, *etc.*

The fact that $P_{n}(T^{2,2p+1};q,t)$ defined in [\[Pq2special123\]](#Pq2special123){reference-type="eqref" reference="Pq2special123"} appears to coincide with the Poincaré polynomial ${\cal P}^{sl(2),V_n} (T^{2,2p+1}; q,t)$ of the $n$-colored $sl(2)$ knot homology has an important implication: it suggests that the homological volume conjectures [\[VCquantref\]](#VCquantref){reference-type="eqref" reference="VCquantref"} and [\[VCparamref\]](#VCparamref){reference-type="eqref" reference="VCparamref"} can be formulated directly in terms of the $sl(2)$ knot homology, as in [\[Pnoptimistic\]](#Pnoptimistic){reference-type="eqref" reference="Pnoptimistic"}, rather than in terms of the specialization of the colored superpolynomial [\[Pq2special\]](#Pq2special){reference-type="eqref" reference="Pq2special"}. For example, such homological version of the generalized volume conjecture [\[VCparamref\]](#VCparamref){reference-type="eqref" reference="VCparamref"} has a nice form $$\sum_{i,j} q^i t^j \dim {\cal H }^{sl(2),V_n}_{i,j} (K)
\; \simeq \;
\exp\left( \frac{1}{\hbar} S_0 (u,t) \,+\,\sum_{k=0}^\infty S_{k+1} (u,t) \, \hbar^{k} \right)$$ and describes the asymptotic growth of the dimensions of the $n$-colored $sl(2)$ homology groups in the limit [\[reflimit\]](#reflimit){reference-type="eqref" reference="reflimit"}: $$q = e^{\hbar} \to 1 \,, \qquad t = \text{fixed} \,, \qquad x \equiv e^u = q^n = \text{fixed} \,.$$ Similarly, the homological version of the quantum volume conjecture [\[VCquantref\]](#VCquantref){reference-type="eqref" reference="VCquantref"} presumably can be formulated in the form of an exact sequence $$0~~
\longrightarrow~~ C^n~~
\longrightarrow^{\kern -13pt \frak{a}^{n}} ~~ C^{n+1}~~
\longrightarrow^{\kern -20pt \frak{a}^{n+1}}~~ C^{n+2}~~

Then, from [\[Superpolynomial_sym_refined\]](#Superpolynomial_sym_refined){reference-type="eqref" reference="Superpolynomial_sym_refined"} we have $$P_{n}(T^{2,2p+1};q,t) =

\left[(-1)^{\ell}q^{\frac{(n-1)(n+1)-\ell(\ell+1)}{2}} t^{\frac{3}{2}(n-1)-\ell}\right]^{2p+1}$$ $$\times
q^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\ell}t^{\frac{1}{2}(1-2p)(n-1)-\ell}
\frac{(q;q)_{n-1}(t^2q;q)_{\ell}(t^2q^2;q)_{2\ell}(-t^3 q^2;q)_{n+\ell-1}(-tq;q)_{n-\ell-1}}
{(-t^3q^2;q)_{n-1}(q;q)_{\ell}(t^2q;q)_{2\ell}(t^2q^2;q)_{n+\ell-1}(q;q)_{n-\ell-1}} \,.
\label{refined_SU(2)}$$
