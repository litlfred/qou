Let C ⊆CP1 be projectively convex, and ﬁx φ ∈SL2(C). If the pole of φ is in C, then dφ preserves strong C-stability. Proof Gauss–Lucas and the fact that CP1\C is projectively convex give the result. Specif- ically, for C-stable p ∈V (n) consider φ · p, which is stable in φ · C ∋∞. Letting B be the complement of C, the dehomogenization of this polynomial is then of degree exactly n with all of its roots in the stereographic projection of φ · B. By projective convexity, φ · B is convex and therefore Gauss–Lucas implies ∂x(φ · p) is φ · C-stable and not identically zero. Applying φ−1 then implies dφ p = (φ−1∂xφ)p is C-stable. ⊓⊔
