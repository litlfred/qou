The Hecke algebra has a basis {C′ w}, over all w ∈Sm, with the elements uniquely determined by the conditions ι(C′ w) = C′ w and C′ w = q−l(w) X y≤w Py,w(q)Ty, where Py,w(q) ∈Z[q2], Pw,w = 1 and deg Py,w < l(w) −l(y) if y̸ = w. The inequality sign under the sum sign refers to the Bruhat partial order on Sm. This proposition is due to Kazhdan and Lusztig [KL1] and admits a direct combinatorial proof.
