Consider the linear category M from Example 2.1.4, built from a ring A. It does not have a zero object (unless the ring A is the zero ring), so it is not additive. A more puzzling question is this: Does M have ﬁnite direct sums? This turns out to be equivalent to whether or not A ∼= A ⊕A as right A-modules. One can show that when A is nonzero and commutative, or nonzero and noetherian, then A̸ ∼= A ⊕A in Mod Aop. On the other hand, if we take a ﬁeld K, and a countable rank K-module N, then A := EndK(N) will satisfy A ∼= A ⊕A.
