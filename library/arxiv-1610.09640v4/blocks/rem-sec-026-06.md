Of course there are many instances of commutative graded rings that do not involve the Koszul sign rule; see e.g. [46] or [105]. In our book we call them algebraically graded rings, and they are studied in Sections 15-17. Strong commutativity has another reason. Its role is to guarantee that the strongly commutative graded polynomial ring K[X] from Example 3.1.23 above will be a graded-free K-module. Without this condition, the square of an odd variable z would be a nonzero 2-torsion element.
