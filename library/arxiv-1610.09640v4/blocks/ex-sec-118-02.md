A NC ring A is called regular if it has ﬁnite global cohomological dimension on both sides. This is very similar to the graded deﬁnition (see Deﬁnition 15.4.1). Let us recall what it means: there is some natural number d such that Exti A(M, N) = 0 for all i > d and all M, N ∈M(A); and also Exti Aop(M ′, N ′) = 0 for all i > d and all M ′, N ′ ∈M(Aop). The smallest such number d is the global cohomological dimension of A. Such rings are easy to ﬁnd; for instance, if K is a ﬁeld, and g is ﬁnite Lie algebra over K, then the universal enveloping ring A := U(g) is regular; the global cohomological dimension of A is d := rankK(g).
