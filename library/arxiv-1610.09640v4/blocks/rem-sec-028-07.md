In the book [46, Section 17, Exercises] there are a few variants of the Koszul complex. Note that in most of the classical literature (including [46] and [105]), the multiplicative structure of K(C; c) has been ignored. Deﬁnition 3.3.13. Let A be a DG ring. The opposite DG ring Aop is the same DG K-module as A, but the multiplication ·op of Aop is the multiplication · of A, reversed and twisted by signs: a ·op b := (−1)i·j ·b·a for a ∈Ai and b ∈Aj. Exercise 3.3.14. Verify that Aop is a DG ring.
