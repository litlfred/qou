The critical cohomological Hall algebra $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{B,W})$ associated to a noncommutative smooth algebra $B$ and potential $W\in B/[B,B]_{\mathop{\mathrm{vect}}}$ was introduced by Kontsevich and Soibelman in [@KS2] as a way of categorifying the theory of Donaldson--Thomas invariants. The meaning of "critical" here is that the Hall algebra is built out of the hypercohomology of the sheaf of *vanishing cycles* of the superpotential $\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(W)$ on the stack of finite-dimensional $B$-modules. This sheaf is supported on the critical locus of $\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(W)$, which is identified with the stack of representations of the Jacobi algebra associated to $B$ and $W$. The critical cohomological Hall algebra can thus be thought of as the Hall algebra categorifying the Donaldson--Thomas theory of representations of this Jacobi algebra. Donaldson--Thomas invariants, first introduced in [@Thomas1], already have an extensive literature. We refer to the sequence of papers by Dominic Joyce [@JoyceI; @JoyceII; @JoyceIII; @JoyceIV; @JoyceMF] for a comprehensive account, or the book [@JoyceDT] by Joyce and Song, and also [@KS1; @KS3] for the more general and abstract account by Kontsevich and Soibelman using motivic vanishing cycles. In all of these treatments of Donaldson--Thomas theory, a key role is played by the integrality conjecture, as proven in [@KS2] in the case of quivers with potential. In this paper we describe and prove the natural categorification of the integrality conjecture in the context of the cohomological Hall algebra for a quiver with potential, and explain how this gives rise to a mathematical definition of the Lie algebra of BPS states, for which the cohomological Hall algebra can be thought of as the positive half of an associated quantum group. See [@HM98] for a Physics-oriented construction of Hall algebras of BPS states. From now on we assume that $B=\mathbb{C} Q$ is the free path algebra of a quiver, that we are given a Bridgeland stability condition $\upzeta$ on the category of $\mathbb{C} Q$-modules, and that $\upmu\in(-\infty,\infty]$ is a slope. We denote by $\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}\subset\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$ the union of the zero vector with the subset of dimension vectors of slope $\upmu$ with respect to the stability condition $\upzeta$. Let $\mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}$ denote the stack of $\upzeta$-semistable $\mathbb{C}Q$-modules with dimension vector in $\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}$. For full generality, we consider a locally closed substack $\mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}}_{\upmu}\subset \mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}$, the closed points of which correspond to $\upzeta$-semistable $\mathbb{C}Q$-modules in a Serre subcategory $\mathcal{S}$ of the category of $\mathbb{C}Q$-modules. When $\mathcal{S}$ is the entire full subcategory of finite dimensional $\mathbb{C}Q$-modules, we will omit all superscripts $\mathcal{S}$. Via natural correspondences there is an algebra structure on the $\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}$-graded Borel--Moore homology of the normalised vanishing cycle complex on $\mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}$, restricted to $\mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}}_{\upmu}$, that we denote $$\label{ua}
\mathop{\mathrm{H}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}\right)\colonequals  \bigoplus_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}\in\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}}\mathop{\mathrm{H}}_{c}\left(\mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}},\upphi_{\mathfrak{Tr}(W)^{\upzeta}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}}\otimes\mathbb{L}^{-\dim(\mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}})/2}\right)^{\vee}$$ as in [@KS2]. These notions are properly defined and explained in Section [\[Qrepsection\]](#Qrepsection){reference-type="ref" reference="Qrepsection"}. This algebra categorifies Donaldson--Thomas theory in the sense that Donaldson--Thomas invariants for the category of $\upzeta$-semistable modules in $\mathcal{S}$ are obtained by considering the class in the Grothendieck group of the $\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}$-graded mixed Hodge structures (equipped with a monodromy action) of the underlying mixed Hodge structure of ([\[ua\]](#ua){reference-type="ref" reference="ua"}). The main conclusion of this paper is that the introduction of the cohomological Hall algebra structure associated to a quiver, a potential and a Serre subcategory $\mathcal{S}$ should be seen as a first step towards forging a connection between the theory of refined Donaldson--Thomas invariants and the theory of quantum enveloping algebras, and that the proof of the integrality conjecture is the motivic shadow of a Poincaré--Birkhoff--Witt theorem for the associated quantum enveloping algebra. In fact we reach this conclusion by providing all of the remaining steps, proving this PBW type theorem, and defining the associated Lie algebra of BPS cohomology. This programme was already articulated in [@KS2] but several components had yet to be found. Firstly, a coproduct turning the algebra $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ into a Hopf algebra had to be defined. A localised coproduct was found in the first author's paper [@Da13], reinforcing the hope that $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ could be turned into a quantum enveloping algebra. Meanwhile the integrality conjecture in the case of free path algebras of quivers with zero potential was reproven by the second author and Markus Reineke in [@Meinhardt14]. We recall that, loosely speaking, the integrality theorem states that the class of $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{0,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ in the Grothendieck group of graded monodromic mixed Hodge structures is equal to that of the free supercommutative algebra generated by $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes\Omega\right)$ for *some* element $\Omega$ for which the graded pieces are finite-dimensional. This is of course much weaker than the claim that we have an isomorphism between these two objects, which in turn is weaker than the existence of a PBW isomorphism. Nevertheless, the proof of [@Meinhardt14] has the attractive feature of explicitly constructing $\Omega$, in terms of intersection complexes. This at least gives us a natural candidate for a choice of $\Omega$ with which to try to upgrade equality in the Grothendieck group to a bona fide isomorphism. In addition, it is proven in [@DaMe4] that due to formal properties of the vanishing cycle functor, the methods of [@Meinhardt14] are enough to prove a very general version of the integrality conjecture, even in the presence of a nonzero potential. Similarly, the proof in [@DaMe4] gives explicit formulae for the class of $\Omega$, and the construction lifts to cohomology, or mixed Hodge modules, which is the lift that we develop in this paper. The claim that there is an isomorphism in the category of $\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}$-graded monodromic mixed Hodge structures between $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes\Omega\right)$ for some $\Omega$ depending on $W,\upmu,\upzeta,\mathcal{S}$ is what we call the "cohomological integrality conjecture". The proof of the cohomological integrality conjecture, and the existence of a coproduct, are the main ingredients of our proof of the PBW theorem.
