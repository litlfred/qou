Now that we have introduced the relative version of Donaldson--Thomas theory, we can state the first main result of this paper, a cohomological refinement of the integrality conjecture.

::: thmx
[]{#ThmA label="ThmA"} Let $Q,W$ be a quiver with potential, let $\upmu\in (-\infty,\infty]$ be a slope, and let $\upzeta$ be a $\upmu$-generic stability condition. Let $\mathfrak{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}}_{\upmu}$ be a locally closed substack of the stack of $\upzeta$-semistable $\mathbb{C}Q$-representations, the closed points of which correspond to the representations in a Serre subcategory $\mathcal{S}$ of the category of $\mathbb{C}Q$-representations. For $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}\setminus\{0\}$ a nonzero dimension vector of slope $\upmu$, define the following element of $\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}})$, the category of monodromic[^1] mixed Hodge modules on the coarse moduli space of $\upzeta$-semistable $\mathbb{C}Q$-representations: $$\mathcal{BPS}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta}\colonequals \begin{cases}\upphi^{\mathop{\mathrm{mon}}}_{\mathcal{T}r(W)^\upzeta_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}}\mathcal{IC}_{\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}}(\mathbb{Q})&\textrm{if  }\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-st}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}\neq\emptyset \\0&\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}$$ and let $\mathcal{BPS}^{\upzeta}_{W,\upmu}$ be the direct sum of all the $\mathcal{BPS}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta}$ for $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}$ of slope $\upmu$ with respect to the stability condition $\upzeta$. Define $$\mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}=\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}\left(\dim_!(\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}\rightarrow \mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}})^*\mathcal{BPS}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta}\right)^{\vee}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta})),$$ where the superscript denotes the dual in the category of monodromic mixed Hodge modules, so in particular, $$\mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta}\cong \mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}\left(\dim_*\mathcal{BPS}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta}\right)$$ by Verdier self-duality of $\mathcal{BPS}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta}$. Define $\mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}$ to be the direct sum of all the $\mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}$ for $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}$ of slope $\upmu$. Then $$\begin{aligned}
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_+}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes \mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}\right)\cong \mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\upmu}) &&\emph{(absolute integrality theorem)}
\end{aligned}$$ in $\mathcal{D}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}))$. Furthermore, working over the coarse moduli space of $\upzeta$-semistable representations $\mathcal{M}_{\upmu}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}$, there is an isomorphism $$\begin{aligned}
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_{\oplus}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes\mathcal{BPS}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta}\right)\cong\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta})&&\emph{(relative integrality theorem)}
\end{aligned}$$ in $\mathcal{D}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}))$.
:::

The definition of $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}$ is given in Section [\[MMHMs\]](#MMHMs){reference-type="ref" reference="MMHMs"}. Requisite notions from the theory of representations of quivers, including the functors $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_{\oplus}}$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_{+}}$, and the definition of $\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}$, are recalled in Section [\[Qrepsection\]](#Qrepsection){reference-type="ref" reference="Qrepsection"}. The functor $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_{+}}$, via the correspondence between monodromic mixed Hodge modules on $\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$ and $\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$-graded monodromic mixed Hodge structures, is the functor that sends an $\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$-graded monodromic mixed Hodge structure $\mathcal{F}$ satisfying $\mathcal{F}_0=0$ to the $\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$-graded monodromic mixed Hodge structure $$\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_+}(\mathcal{F})\colonequals \bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^i(\mathcal{F})$$ given by taking the free $\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$-graded supercommutative algebra generated by $\mathcal{F}$ and then forgetting the algebra structure. We briefly explain the relation to earlier, less "categorical", incarnations of the integrality conjecture in terms of generating functions of weight polynomials, as that is the language in which the integrality conjecture is perhaps most familiar. In the literature, this is most commonly referred to as "refined Donaldson--Thomas theory". Given an element $\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}))$, we define the formal power series $$\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{L},q^{1/2},x_1,\ldots,x_n)\colonequals \sum_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}}\upchi_q(\mathcal{L}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}},q^{1/2})x^{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}},$$ where we have used the weight polynomial $$\upchi_q(\mathcal{L}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}},q^{1/2})\colonequals \sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}} (-1)^i\dim(\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}^W_{j}(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}^i(\mathcal{L}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}})))q^{j/2}.$$ As in Theorem [\[ThmA\]](#ThmA){reference-type="ref" reference="ThmA"} we fix a quiver $Q$, potential $W$, stability condition $\upzeta$, and slope $\upmu$. Although the total cohomology $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ (defined as in ([\[ua\]](#ua){reference-type="ref" reference="ua"})) is not in the bounded derived category $\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}))\subset \mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}))$, since each $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ will typically be nonzero in arbitrarily high cohomological degree, the partition function $$\mathcal{Z}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}),q^{1/2},x_1,\ldots,x_n\right)$$ still makes sense as a formal power series in $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, with coefficients given by Laurent series in $q^{1/2}$. This is because for each $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}\in\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}$, and for each $j\in\mathbb{Z}$, the element $\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}^W_{j}(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}^i(\mathcal{A}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}))$ is nonzero for only finitely many values of $i$; we denote by $\mathcal{D}^{\geq, \mathrm{lf}}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}))\subset \mathcal{D}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}))$ the full subcategory of monodromic mixed Hodge modules satisfying this condition and also the condition that for fixed $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}$ the element $\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}^W_{j}(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}))$ vanishes for $j\ll 0$. Then one way to define the plethystic exponential $$\mathop{\mathrm{EXP}}\colon\mathbb{Z}((q^{1/2}))[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]_+\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}((q^{1/2}))[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]$$ where the subscript "$+$" on the left hand side signifies that we take the subgroup of elements $\sum_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}}a_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}(q^{1/2})x^{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}$ such that $a_0(q^{1/2})=0$, is via the formula for $\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq, \mathrm{lf}}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}\setminus\{0\}))$: $$\label{EXPdef}
\mathop{\mathrm{EXP}}\left(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{L},q^{1/2},x_1,\ldots,x_n)\right)=\mathcal{Z}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_+}(\mathcal{L}),q^{1/2},x_1,\ldots,x_n\right).$$

With the above conventions in place, we may finally state the integrality conjecture in refined DT theory. It is the statement that there exist Laurent polynomials $\Omega^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}(q^{1/2})\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/2}]$ such that $$\label{ics}
\mathcal{Z}(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}),q^{1/2},x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\mathop{\mathrm{EXP}}\left(\sum_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}\in\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}\setminus\{0\}} \Omega^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}(q^{-1/2})x^{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}/(q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2})\right).$$ The formal power series $\Omega_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}(q^{1/2})$ are, by definition[^2], the refined Donaldson--Thomas/Bogomol'nyi--Prasad--Sommerfield[^3] invariants for the slope $\upmu$, stability condition $\upzeta$, and Serre subcategory $\mathcal{S}$. Note that $$\upchi_q(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}},\mathbb{Q})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}},q^{1/2})=-q^{1/2}-q^{3/2}-\ldots=(q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2})^{-1}$$ and so we deduce from ([\[EXPdef\]](#EXPdef){reference-type="ref" reference="EXPdef"}), ([\[ics\]](#ics){reference-type="ref" reference="ics"}) and Theorem [\[ThmA\]](#ThmA){reference-type="ref" reference="ThmA"} the equality $$\Omega^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}(q^{-1/2})=\upchi_q(\mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}},q^{1/2})$$ where $\mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}$ are the cohomological invariants defined in Theorem [\[ThmA\]](#ThmA){reference-type="ref" reference="ThmA"}. Note also that since $\mathop{\mathrm{EXP}}$ is injective, one can always find formal Laurent power series $\Omega_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}(q^{1/2})$ satisfying ([\[ics\]](#ics){reference-type="ref" reference="ics"}). The content of the integrality conjecture, as proved in [@KS2], is that these formal power series are in fact polynomials.

We can deduce the refined integrality conjecture from Theorem [\[ThmA\]](#ThmA){reference-type="ref" reference="ThmA"}; since $\mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}$ is the hypercohomology of a bounded complex of monodromic mixed Hodge modules, its weight polynomial is a genuine Laurent polynomial instead of a formal power series. However, the cohomological lift provided by Theorem [\[ThmA\]](#ThmA){reference-type="ref" reference="ThmA"} is much stronger than the integrality conjecture. It upgrades an equality in the Grothendieck (lambda-)ring of $\mathcal{D}^{\geq, \mathrm{lf}}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}))$ to an isomorphism in that category. This is a surprising result even before introducing the extra structure of the cohomological Hall algebra, since it shows that there is an entire theory of "cohomologically refined" BPS invariants waiting to be explored. For early applications of this theory, we refer the reader to the first author's reproof of the Kac positivity conjecture [@Da13b] (originally proved by Hausel, Letellier and Villegas [@HLRV13]) and proof of the quantum cluster positivity conjecture [@Da16a].

[^1]: See Section [\[MMHMs\]](#MMHMs){reference-type="ref" reference="MMHMs"} for an introduction to this category. It is an enlargement of the ordinary category of mixed Hodge modules, taking account of the monodromy operation on the vanishing cycles functor.

[^2]: The substitution $q^{1/2}\mapsto q^{-1/2}$ arises from the fact that DT invariants are typically defined in terms of compactly supported cohomology, which is the dual to the cohomology theory we consider.

[^3]: Some authors refer to the coefficients in the partition function on the left hand side of ([\[ics\]](#ics){reference-type="ref" reference="ics"}) as (refined) DT invariants, while some have reserved this term to refer to the $\Omega_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta}(q^{1/2})$ appearing on the right hand side. In order to establish some distinction between the two types of invariants, we will refer to the $\Omega_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}}^{\upzeta}(q^{1/2})$ exclusively as BPS invariants, and to the left hand side of ([\[ics\]](#ics){reference-type="ref" reference="ics"}) as the DT partition function.
