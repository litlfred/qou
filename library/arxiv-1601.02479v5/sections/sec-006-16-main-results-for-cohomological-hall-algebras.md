Assume that the stability condition $\upzeta$ is generic. The object $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\upmu})$ is a unital algebra in the category of $\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}$-graded monodromic mixed Hodge structures, which corresponds to a monoid in the category $\mathcal{D}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}))$. The monoidal product in this category is defined by $$\mathcal{F}\boxtimes_+\mathcal{G}\colonequals +_*(\mathcal{F}\boxtimes \mathcal{G}),$$ where $\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathcal{G}$ is the usual external tensor product, and $+\colon\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}\times\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}\rightarrow \mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$ is the addition map. A unital graded algebra structure is determined by a map $$\mathcal{F}\boxtimes_+\mathcal{F}\rightarrow \mathcal{F}$$ satisfying the obvious associativity condition, along with a unit map $\mathbb{Q}_{\{0\}}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}$ from the constant sheaf supported at the monoidal unit $0\in \mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$. Although we prove theorems regarding this monoid, we do so via theorems regarding the monoid $$\label{intrRel}
\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta})\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq, \mathrm{lf}}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}))$$ in the category of monodromic mixed Hodge modules on the coarse moduli space of $\upzeta$-semistable $\mathbb{C}Q$-modules of slope $\upmu$. After restricting to $\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}}_W$ and applying the monoidal functor obtained by taking the direct image along the map $$\dim\colon \mathcal{M}_{\upmu}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{Q_0},$$ the monodromic mixed Hodge module ([\[intrRel\]](#intrRel){reference-type="ref" reference="intrRel"}) becomes isomorphic to the perverse associated graded version of the monoid ([\[ua\]](#ua){reference-type="ref" reference="ua"}). In symbols, there is an isomorphism of algebras $$\label{PAG}
\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}\left(\dim_*\left(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}\left((\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}}_{\upmu}\hookrightarrow \mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu})^!\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta}\right)\right)\right)\cong \mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathfrak{P}}}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})\right).$$ All of our main results regarding $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta})$ stem from the observation that all product and coproduct structures that we consider on $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\upmu})$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathcal{A}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\upmu})$ respect the perverse filtration, giving rise to the algebra structures on the right hand side of ([\[PAG\]](#PAG){reference-type="ref" reference="PAG"}) that are much easier to describe --- precisely, we prove that (in an appropriate sense) the algebras appearing in ([\[PAG\]](#PAG){reference-type="ref" reference="PAG"}) are supercommutative degenerations of the CoHA $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\upmu})$. To define this "appropriate sense", we must take some care over the signs in the symmetrizing morphism $$\textbf{sw}:\mathcal{F}\boxtimes_+ \mathcal{F}\rightarrow \mathcal{F}\boxtimes_+\mathcal{F}.$$ It was noted already in [@KS2] that the cohomological Hall algebra on the graded vector space ([\[baby\]](#baby){reference-type="ref" reference="baby"}), while not commutative, becomes commutative after twisting by a simple system of signs. There are two related ways of expressing this idea, which we develop in tandem in this paper.

Firstly, the algebra *is* commutative, after replacing the usual symmetrizing morphism for the category in which we consider it (involving the Koszul sign rule) by a new one, which depends on $\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$-degree. We consider an adapted symmetrizing morphism ${}^{\uptau}\textbf{sw}$ for the categories of monodromic mixed Hodge modules on $\Uplambda_{\upmu}^{\upzeta}$ and $\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}$, with respect to which Theorems [\[qea\]](#qea){reference-type="ref" reference="qea"} and [\[strongPBW\]](#strongPBW){reference-type="ref" reference="strongPBW"} are stated --- see Section [\[lc_signs\]](#lc_signs){reference-type="ref" reference="lc_signs"} for the provenance of these signs. At the level of elements, the rule is easy to state. Consider the form $\uptau$ induced by $$\label{half_tau}
\uptau\colon \mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'\otimes \mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}''\mapsto \upchi(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'')+\upchi(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}')\upchi(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'')$$ on the $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-vector space $V=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{Q_0}$. Then $${}^{\uptau}\textbf{sw}(a\otimes b)=(-1)^{\uptau(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'')+\lvert a\lvert \cdot\lvert b\lvert}b\otimes a$$ where $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}''$ are the $\mathbb{N}^{Q_0}$-degrees of $a$ and $b$ respectively, and $\lvert a\lvert$ and $\lvert b\lvert$ are their cohomological degrees. In other words, we modify the usual Koszul sign rule by $(-1)^{\uptau(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'')}$. Now we describe the second approach to signs. As in [@KS2 Sec.2.6] and [@Efimov] it is sometimes more convenient to modify the definition of the product in the CoHA $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$, so that the algebra (or in the context of this paper, its perverse associated graded algebra) becomes supercommutative with respect to the usual Koszul sign rule. The form $\uptau$ vanishes on pairs $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}\otimes \mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}$, and so there exists a bilinear form $\uppsi$ on $V$ such that $$\label{pcocdef}
\uppsi(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'')+\uppsi(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}')=\uptau(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'').$$ We define the underlying graded monodromic mixed Hodge structure of $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(^\uppsi\!\!\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ to be the same as that of $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$. We define the multiplication on $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(^{\uppsi}\!\!\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ via $$\label{psitwdef}
a\otimes b\mapsto (-1)^{\uppsi(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}',\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'')}\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\ast_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})(a\otimes b)$$ where $a\in \mathop{\mathrm{H}}(^{\uppsi}\!\!\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}'}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$ and $b\in \mathop{\mathrm{H}}(^{\uppsi}\!\!\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}}''}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$. Associativity of the twisted CoHA follows from associativity for the untwisted CoHA and bilinearity of $\uppsi$.

Our first main result concerning CoHAs is an upgrade of Theorem [\[ThmA\]](#ThmA){reference-type="ref" reference="ThmA"}. It states that the isomorphisms of Theorem [\[ThmA\]](#ThmA){reference-type="ref" reference="ThmA"} can be realised explicitly as PBW-type isomorphisms defined using the structure of the relative, or absolute CoHA, respectively.

::: thmx
[]{#qea label="qea"} Let $\upzeta$ be a $\upmu$-generic Bridgeland stability condition. The localised bialgebra structure on $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}_{W,\upmu})$ induces a (non-localised) Hopf algebra structure on $\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathfrak{P}}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}))$, which is a free commutative algebra with respect to the symmetrizing morphism ${}^{\uptau}\!\textbf{sw}$. There is a canonical PBW isomorphism in $\mathcal{D}^{\geq, \mathrm{lf}}(\mathop{\mathrm{MMHM}}(\mathcal{M}_{\upmu}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}))$: $$\label{PBWiso1}
{}^{\uptau}\!\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_{\oplus}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes\mathcal{BPS}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta}\right)\rightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta})$$ that is realized via a canonical embedding[^1] $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes\mathcal{BPS}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta}\subset \mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta})$ and the relative cohomological Hall algebra multiplication. There is a similar canonical PBW isomorphism over the base $\Uplambda^{\upzeta}_{\upmu}$: $$\label{PBWiso2}
{}^{\uptau}\!\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_+}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes \mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}\right)\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}).$$ and the map is defined via the cohomological Hall algebra product on $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})$. If $\uppsi$ is a bilinear form as in ([\[pcocdef\]](#pcocdef){reference-type="ref" reference="pcocdef"}), then $\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathfrak{P}}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(^{\uppsi}\!\!\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}))$ is a free supercommutative algebra, and there are canonically defined PBW isomorphisms $$\begin{aligned}
\label{PBWiso3}
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_{\oplus}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes \mathcal{BPS}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta}\right)\rightarrow &\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(^{\uppsi}\!\!\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{A}}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta})\\
\label{PBWiso4}
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_+}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{BPS}}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}}\right)\rightarrow&\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(^{\uppsi}\!\!\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu}^{\upzeta,\mathcal{S}})
\end{aligned}$$ to the relative and absolute CoHAs with the $\uppsi$-twisted multiplication.
:::

Note that we do not claim that in the "absolute" cases [\[PBWiso1\]](#PBWiso1){reference-type="eqref" reference="PBWiso1"} and [\[PBWiso3\]](#PBWiso3){reference-type="eqref" reference="PBWiso3"} the canonical isomorphisms of Theorem [\[qea\]](#qea){reference-type="ref" reference="qea"} are isomorphisms of algebras --- in many interesting cases they will not be, hence the moniker "PBW theorem". We expect that the case of symmetric quivers with zero potential, for which it is proved by Efimov in [@Efimov] that the $\uppsi$-twisted cohomological Hall algebra is free supercommutative, is atypical. Our final theorem is a more general PBW type statement for the whole CoHA. Just as Theorem [\[qea\]](#qea){reference-type="ref" reference="qea"} states that the integrality isomorphisms of Theorem [\[ThmA\]](#ThmA){reference-type="ref" reference="ThmA"} can be realised in terms of PBW-type isomorphisms in the relative CoHA, Theorem [\[strongPBW\]](#strongPBW){reference-type="ref" reference="strongPBW"} states that the same is true of the combination of the integrality isomorphisms (for every slope) and the wall crossing isomorphism, for a generic stability condition.

::: thmx
[]{#strongPBW label="strongPBW"} Let $\upzeta$ be a generic stability condition for $Q$. Then there are embeddings[^2] $(\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}\rightarrow\mathcal{M})_* \mathcal{BPS}^{\upzeta}_{W,\upmu}\subset \mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathcal{A}_{W,\upmu})$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{M}_{\upmu}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{BPS}^{\upzeta}_{W,\upmu})\subset \mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{S}}_W)$ such that the resulting morphisms $$\begin{aligned}
\mbox{\larger[3]{$\boxtimes$}}_{\oplus, \infty\xrightarrow{\upmu} -\infty}^{\mathop{\mathrm{tw}}} {}^{\uptau}\!\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_{\oplus}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes (\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}\rightarrow\mathcal{M})_* \mathcal{BPS}^{\upzeta}_{W,\upmu}\right)\rightarrow &\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathcal{A}_W)\\
\mbox{\larger[3]{$\boxtimes$}}_{+, \infty\xrightarrow{\upmu} -\infty}^{\mathop{\mathrm{tw}}}{}^{\uptau}\!\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_+}\left( \mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes \mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{M}_{\upmu}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{BPS}^{\upzeta}_{W,\upmu})\right)\rightarrow &\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_W^{\mathcal{S}})
\end{aligned}$$ induced by the relative and absolute cohomological Hall algebra multiplications, respectively, are isomorphisms. Similarly, if $\uppsi$ is a twist defined as in ([\[pcocdef\]](#pcocdef){reference-type="ref" reference="pcocdef"}), then the morphisms $$\begin{aligned}
\mbox{\larger[3]{$\boxtimes$}}_{\oplus, \infty\xrightarrow{\upmu} -\infty}^{\mathop{\mathrm{tw}}} \mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_{\oplus}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes (\mathcal{M}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}}}_{\upmu}\rightarrow\mathcal{M})_* \mathcal{BPS}^{\upzeta}_{W,\upmu}\right)\rightarrow &\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathcal{A}_W)\\
\mbox{\larger[3]{$\boxtimes$}}_{+, \infty\xrightarrow{\upmu} -\infty}^{\mathop{\mathrm{tw}}}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\boxtimes_+}\left( \mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathop{\mathrm{B\mathbb{C}^*}})_{\mathop{\mathrm{vir}}}\otimes \mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{M}_{\upmu}^{\upzeta\mathop{\mathrm{-ss}},\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{BPS}^{\upzeta}_{W,\upmu})\right)\rightarrow &\mathop{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{A}_W^{\mathcal{S}})
\end{aligned}$$ induced by the same embeddings as above and the $\uppsi$-twisted relative and absolute cohomological Hall algebra multiplications are isomorphisms.
:::

Theorem [\[qea\]](#qea){reference-type="ref" reference="qea"} and [\[strongPBW\]](#strongPBW){reference-type="ref" reference="strongPBW"} together give a very general structural result on the relative and absolute CoHAs, namely that they are obtained by infinite extensions of algebras (indexed by descending slope) that are themselves noncommutative deformations of free supercommutative algebras, with characteristic functions determined by the cohomological BPS invariants.

[^1]: Strictly speaking, these maps are embeddings in the category of $\mathbb{Z}$-graded monodromic mixed Hodge-modules, considered as maps in the derived category of monodromic mixed Hodge modules via the natural embedding of categories.

[^2]: See previous footnote.
