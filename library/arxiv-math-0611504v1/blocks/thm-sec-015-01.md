The morphism HN(B, h, k) = HN(T (B)) does not depend on the choice of T (B) up to sign and multiplication by Nth roots of unity, and HN(B, h, k) = HN(B, h′, k′) if the mod(NZ) reductions of h and h′ (resp. k and k′) are the same, that is, if we have k −k′ ∈H1(˜LF; Z) and k −k′ = 0 ∈H1(˜LF; Z/NZ), and similarly for h and h′. Moreover, there is no sign ambiguity if we restrict to even valued ﬂattenings as in Lemma 2.9 (hence with h = 0). We call HN(B, h, k) the amplitude of (B, h, k).
