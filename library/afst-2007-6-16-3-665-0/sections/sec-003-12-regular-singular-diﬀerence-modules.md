We recall some classical results (a modern proof is given in [vdP-S1]).
The classiﬁcation of regular singular modules over K and K are similar and
we restrict our attention to q-diﬀerence modules M over K. A diﬀerence
module over K is called regular singular if there exists a lattice M 0 ⊂M
over C{z} (i.e., M 0 = C{z}e1 ⊕· · ·⊕C{z}em for some K-basis {e1, . . . , em}
of M) which is invariant under Φ and Φ−1 (or in later terminology, M is pure
of slope 0). Then M can uniquely be written as K ⊗C V where V is a ﬁnite
dimensional vector space over C provided with a linear map A : V →V
such that all its eigenvalues α satisfy |q| < |α| ⩽1. The action of Φ on this
tensor product is given by Φ(a ⊗v) = φ(a) ⊗A(v), for a ∈K and v ∈V .
For a regular singular M we deﬁne Mglobal ⊂M as the set of elements
m such that the C-vector space generated by {Φnm| n ⩾0} has ﬁnite
dimension. Equivalently, m ∈Mglobal, if and only if there exists a non zero
L ∈C[Φ] such that L(m) = 0. Clearly Mglobal is a C[z, z−1]-submodule.
More precisely,

Remarks 1.2. — (1) The unipotent diﬀerence module Um over K (or
over K) is Um := K ⊗C Cm with Φ(f ⊗v) = φ(f) ⊗A(v), where A :
