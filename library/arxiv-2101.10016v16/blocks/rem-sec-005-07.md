An antiautomorphism S of A can be a strong antipode only if it satisfies the following compatibility conditions with the associator, xS(y)z = I, S(x ′ )y ′S(z′ ) = I. (6.9) For example, when A is a bialgebra, that is Φ = I ⊗ I ⊗ I, then the above equations obviously hold and the notion of a strong antipode reduces to the usual notion of antipode of a Hopf algebra. More generally, in the weak case we shall see that equations (6.9) are satisfied by the associator of a weak Hopf algebra, see Sect. 9.
