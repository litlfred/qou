Theorem [\[th:1\]](#th:1){reference-type="ref" reference="th:1"} and Theorem [\[th:2\]](#th:2){reference-type="ref" reference="th:2"} are both based on two new relations for the $q$-Pochhammer symbol, given in Theorems [\[th:Ir\]](#th:Ir){reference-type="ref" reference="th:Ir"} (cf. [\[eq:mro\]](#eq:mro){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:mro"}) and [\[thp\]](#thp){reference-type="ref" reference="thp"}. These relations are proved in Section [\[repsec\]](#repsec){reference-type="ref" reference="repsec"} and both make use of the Abel-Plana summation formula [@Abel1992 p.23], [@Plana1820 p.408], [@Olver1997 Chapter 8.3.1], which is a form of Euler-Maclaurin summation with an explicit form of the error term.

In the case of Theorem [\[th:Ir\]](#th:Ir){reference-type="ref" reference="th:Ir"}, one starts by dividing the product in the definition [\[eq:def-Pochhammer\]](#eq:def-Pochhammer){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:def-Pochhammer"} of $(\ifthenelse{\equal{\gamma \alpha}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(  \gamma \alpha\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{( \gamma \alpha)}{( \gamma \alpha)}{( \gamma \alpha)}})_{r}$ into appropriate intervals and congruence classes. One then take the logarithm and apply the summation formula to the resulting sum of the function $\log(1-\ifthenelse{\equal{}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(  \aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{( )}{( )}{( )}}(z))$. As this function is close to a primitive of $\pi\cot(\pi z)$, which has poles at integers, then through a residue computation one eventually arrives to the dual object $(\ifthenelse{\equal{ \alpha}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(   \alpha\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{(  \alpha)}{(  \alpha)}{(  \alpha)}})_{r'}$.

In the case of Theorem [\[thp\]](#thp){reference-type="ref" reference="thp"}, the reciprocity relation for the $q$-Pochhammer symbol relates $\frac{\overline h}{k}$ and $\frac{\overline k}{h}$. In this case one starts by applying the simple relation $\frac{\overline h}{k}\equiv -\frac{\overline k}{h}+\frac1{hk}~\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft( \textnormal{mod}~1\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{(\textnormal{mod}~1)}{(\textnormal{mod}~1)}{(\textnormal{mod}~1)}$. After some initial manipulations, one is lead to consider sums of the function $\log(1-\cot(\pi \xi)\tan(\pi \xi z))+\cot(\pi \xi)\tan(\pi \xi z)$ for some choices of $\xi\in(0,1/2)$. This is again performed using Abel-Plana summation formula followed by a careful analysis, with particular care needed in the reassembling of various main terms.

In both our reciprocity relations for the $q$-Pochhammer symbol, we show that the error terms extend to holomorphic functions of controlled growth. This is crucial for the applications to the modularity relations for the Kashaev invariant for knots other than $4_1$.

Once the reciprocity formulas for the $q$-Pochhammer symbol are established the proofs of Theorem [\[th:1\]](#th:1){reference-type="ref" reference="th:1"} and Theorem [\[th:2\]](#th:2){reference-type="ref" reference="th:2"}, given in Section [\[pmt1\]](#pmt1){reference-type="ref" reference="pmt1"} and [\[pmt2\]](#pmt2){reference-type="ref" reference="pmt2"} respectively, follow in similar ways. We first split the sums in the definition of the Kashaev invariant into congruence classes (and suitable intervals) and apply the reciprocity relations, reducing the problem to that of estimating certain sums of exponentials of linear combinations of dilogarithms. These sums are very similar to the ones one needs to consider for the the volume conjecture with only two relevant differences: the variables of summation range over some convex space rather than some larger cubic regions, and inside the exponential we have also some new error terms. For all the knots we consider the first difference is easily treated since, as shown in Lemma [\[extra_terms\]](#extra_terms){reference-type="ref" reference="extra_terms"}, the neglected terms are much smaller than the main terms (for other knots, such as $7_2$, a treatment as in [@Ohtsuki7 Section 8] should be possible). In the case of Theorem [\[th:1\]](#th:1){reference-type="ref" reference="th:1"}, the second difference is also surpassed thanks the holomorphicity of the error terms mentioned above, since the complex analytic methods of [@Ohtsuki52; @OhtsukiYokota6; @Ohtsuki7], using Poisson summation and the saddle-point method, go through essentially unchanged. In the case of Theorem [\[th:2\]](#th:2){reference-type="ref" reference="th:2"}, while we still have the holomorphicity of the error terms, the fact that the errors are not $o(1)$ forces us to use positivity to avoid possible cancellations in the main terms, thus restricting the applicability to the $4_1$ knot only.

Theorems [\[theo:cfep\]](#theo:cfep){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:cfep"}, [\[th:pz\]](#th:pz){reference-type="ref" reference="th:pz"} and [\[concor\]](#concor){reference-type="ref" reference="concor"} are proved in Section [\[fise\]](#fise){reference-type="ref" reference="fise"} and all use the reciprocity formulas [\[eq:J0-approxmod\]](#eq:J0-approxmod){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:J0-approxmod"} and [\[eq:frf\]](#eq:frf){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:frf"} (the latter being more crucial) in conjunction with the recent work [@BettinDrappeau] on the distribution of $\Sigma(\alpha)$.

The difference between the reciprocity relations [\[eq:J0-approxmod\]](#eq:J0-approxmod){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:J0-approxmod"} and [\[eq:frf\]](#eq:frf){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:frf"} can be better understood in terms of the continued fraction expansions $[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]$ of $h/k$ (for simplicity we assume $m$ odd). Indeed, [\[eq:J0-approxmod\]](#eq:J0-approxmod){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:J0-approxmod"} relates the values of $\mathcal{J}_{4_1,0}$ at $\ifthenelse{\equal{{[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]}}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(  {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]}\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}}$ and at $\ifthenelse{\equal{{[0;b_\ell,\dots,b_m]}}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(  {[0;b_\ell,\dots,b_m]}\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{( {[0;b_\ell,\dots,b_m]})}{( {[0;b_\ell,\dots,b_m]})}{( {[0;b_\ell,\dots,b_m]})}}$ provided that $b_\ell\to\infty$ for some $\ell\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ with all the other $b_i$ bounded, whereas [\[eq:frf\]](#eq:frf){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:frf"} relates the values of $\mathcal{J}_{4_1,0}$ at $\ifthenelse{\equal{{[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]}}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(  {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]}\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}}$ and at $\ifthenelse{\equal{{[0;b_1,\dots,b_{m-1}]}}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(  {[0;b_1,\dots,b_{m-1}]}\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_{m-1}]})}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_{m-1}]})}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_{m-1}]})}}$ provided that $b_m\to\infty$ and that the other $b_i$ are not too large (for example $\log(b_i)=o(b_m)$ for all $i<m$ would suffice).

Because of its uniformity, [\[eq:frf\]](#eq:frf){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:frf"} can be successfully iterated removing each time the last convergent $b_m$ from $\mathcal{J}_{4_1,0}(\ifthenelse{\equal{{[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]}}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(  {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]}\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}{( {[0;b_1,\dots,b_m]})}})$. We keep doing so untill we reach the last step for which we need to apply [\[eq:J0-approxmod\]](#eq:J0-approxmod){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:J0-approxmod"}. In this process, we pick up a main term of $\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Vol}}(4_1)}{2\pi}b_m$ at each step and thus arrive to [\[eq:cfef\]](#eq:cfef){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:cfef"}. Equation [\[eq:asy\]](#eq:asy){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:asy"} then follows by the law of large numbers for $\Sigma(\alpha)$ established in [@BettinDrappeau].

Theorem [\[th:pz\]](#th:pz){reference-type="ref" reference="th:pz"} follows a similar line, with the difference that in this case the previous argument and Conjecture [\[conj:zagier2\]](#conj:zagier2){reference-type="ref" reference="conj:zagier2"} give that $\log \mathcal{J}_{4_1,0}(\ifthenelse{\equal{\alpha}{}}{{\rm e}}{\operatorname{e}\mathchoice{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\originalleft(  \alpha\aftergroup\egroup\originalright)}{( \alpha)}{( \alpha)}{( \alpha)}})- \frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Vol}}(4_1)}{2\pi}\Sigma(\alpha)$ can be well approximated by a differentiable function. The theorem then follows invoking again [@BettinDrappeau].

Finally, Theorem [\[concor\]](#concor){reference-type="ref" reference="concor"} follows via a simple argument from Theorem [\[th:4\]](#th:4){reference-type="ref" reference="th:4"}, a version of Theorem [\[th:2\]](#th:2){reference-type="ref" reference="th:2"} which becomes useful when there is a middle partial quotient which is extremely large.
