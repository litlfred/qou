First introduce the necessary notations. Let $\omega$ be some primitive $N$-th root of unity for some $N\ge 2$. For any non-negative integer $n<N$ define the symbol: $$(\omega)_n=\cases{1, &if $n=0$;\cr
              \prod_{j=1}^n(1-\omega^j), &$0<n<N$,\cr}$$ Under complex conjugation we have the following properties: $$(\omega)_n^*=(\omega^*)_n,\quad \omega^*\omega=1.$$ It will be convenient to use the "theta"-function, defined on integers: $$\theta(n)=\cases{1, &$0\le n< N$;\cr
                 0,&otherwise,\cr}\quad n\in Z.$$ For any $n\in Z$ denote by $[n]$ the coresponding integer residue modulo $N$, lying in the interval $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$: $$=n\pmod N,\quad 0\le [n]<N,\quad n,[n]\in Z.\label{brac}$$ The evident property of this function is its' periodicity: $$=[n],\quad n\in Z. \label{periodicity}$$ It should be stressed however that the "bracketing" is a map from $Z$ to $Z$ rather than to $Z_N$, the ring of residues modulo $N$. Besides, it does not preserve the addition operation: $$+[n]\ge N\Rightarrow [m]+[n]\ne [m+n].$$ Define a function with four integer arguments: $$W(k,l,m,n)=N\omega^{(l+m)(m+n)}{\theta([k]+[m])\theta([l]+[n])\over
(\omega)_{[k]}(\omega^*)_{[l]}(\omega)_{[m]}(\omega^*)_{[n]}},\quad k,l,m,n\in Z.\label{4W}$$ Using these definitions consider the following indexed set of numbers: $$R(i,j,k,l|a,b,c,d)=\omega^{a-k-j}W(j-i-a,i-l-d,l-k-c,k-j-b),
\label{R}$$ where all indices are integers, and $a,b,c,d$ being constrained to have the unit sum modulo $N$: $$a+b+c+d=1\pmod N.$$ There is a symmetry group of these symbols generated by relations: $$R(i,j,k,l|a,b,c,d)=R(k,l,i,j|c,d,a,b),             \label{symmetry1}$$ and $$R(i,j,k,l|a,b,c,d)=R(-j,-i,-l,-k|a,d,c,b)\omega^{-i-j-k-l},\label{symmetry2}$$ which have a natural geometrical interpretation. Indeed, consider the diagram given by a projection of two non-coplanar segments in $R^3$ to some plane, the images of the segments having an intersection point. Place indices $j$ and $l$ on two ends of the overcrossing segment, and indices $i$ and $k$, on the ends of the other segment, choosing among two possibilities that, where indices $i,j,k,l$ go round the intersection point in the counter-clockwise direction. Place indices $a,b,c,d$ on four sectors also in the counter-clockwise order, starting from the sector bounded by segments with the end labels $i$ and $j$. Associate with this configuration the "Boltzmann" weight ([\[R\]](#R){reference-type="ref" reference="R"}). The above mentioned symmetry group acquires now an interpretation of the symmetry group of the diagram. Namely, relation ([\[symmetry1\]](#symmetry1){reference-type="ref" reference="symmetry1"}) corresponds to the rotation by $\pi$ aroung the intersection point, while ([\[symmetry2\]](#symmetry2){reference-type="ref" reference="symmetry2"}) corresponds to looking at the projection image from the opposite side of the plane.

The next important property of the $R$-symbols is the Yang-Baxter identity, which they satisfy. Using periodicity property ([\[periodicity\]](#periodicity){reference-type="ref" reference="periodicity"}), confine the range, where the indices take their values, to the finite set $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$. Consider an $N^2$-by-$N^2$ $R$-matrix, described by the matrix elements: $$\langle i,j|R|k,l\rangle=R(i,j,k,l|1,0,0,0)\omega^{k+l}.\label{R-matrix}$$ Then, the following YBE holds: $$R_{12}R_{13}R_{23}=R_{23}R_{13}R_{12},\label{YBE}$$ where the standard matrix notations are used: $$R_{12}|i,j,k\rangle=\sum_{i',j'=0}^{N-1}|i',j',k\rangle
\langle i',j'|R|i,j\rangle,$$ and similarly for the other matrices. Later we will argue that the $R$-matrix ([\[R-matrix\]](#R-matrix){reference-type="ref" reference="R-matrix"}) is in fact a cyclic analog of the universal $R$-matrix associated with the quantum group $U_q(sl(2))$ (see the remark in the end of Sect.[\[sec3\]](#sec3){reference-type="ref" reference="sec3"}).

The inverse $R$-matrix is given in terms of the same $R$-symbols: $$\langle i,l|\overline R|k,j\rangle=R(i,j,k,l|,0,0,0,1)\omega^{j+k}.$$ So, in matrix notations we have $$R_{12}\overline R_{12}=1.$$ Thus, taking into account above diagrammatic interpretation as well as the symmetry property ([\[symmetry1\]](#symmetry1){reference-type="ref" reference="symmetry1"}), we have already realizations of two Reidemeister moves, corresponding to the regular isotopy of links. The following relation provides us with the third Reidemeister move, necessary for the ambient isotopy: $$\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\omega^jR(i,j,j,l|0,1,0,0)=\omega^{-i}\delta_{i,l}.$$ In the next section, using these results, we define the "state sum", leading to an ambient isotopy link invariant.
