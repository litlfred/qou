Thus, following from the resurgence relations ([\[eq:asymrel\]](#eq:asymrel){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:asymrel"}), we can say that ${\cal D}^{\{j,k\}}\equiv{\cal D}^{\{0,\dots0,1,0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots\}}$ must have the same expansion as the perturbative part but with the replacements $I_{n}\to I_{n}^{\{j,k\}}$, where $$\begin{aligned} I_{n}^{\{j,k\}} & =I_{n}^{\{j\}}+\frac{2(-1)^{n}}{(2\pi x_{k})^{2n-1}}=I_{n}^{\{k\}}+\frac{2(-1)^{n}}{(2\pi x_{j})^{2n-1}} \end{aligned}$$ The corresponding Stokes constants are $$\begin{aligned} S^{\{j,k\}} & =S^{\{j\}}S^{\{k\}}\frac{(x_{j}-x_{k})^{2}}{(x_{j}+x_{k})^{2}} \end{aligned}$$ where the extra factor is due to the fact that we need to take $\frac{e^{i\pi\ell_{\beta}}}{4\pi x_{k}}\frac{\Phi^{\{j\}}(2i\pi x_{k})}{\Phi^{\{j\}\prime}(-2i\pi x_{k})}$ rather than the same expression with $\Phi$, which defines $S^{\{k\}}$.
