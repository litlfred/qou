In the end, let us recall some facts about exotic spheres (see [15]) . A
k-dimensional compact oriented diﬀerentiable manifold is called a k-sphere
if it is homeomorphic to the k-dimensional standard sphere. A k-sphere
not diﬀeomorphic to the standard k-sphere is said to be exotic. The ﬁrst
exotic sphere was discovered by Milnor in 1956 (see [26]). Two k-spheres
are called equivalent if there exists an orientation preserving diﬀeom orphism
between them. The equivalence classes of k-spheres constitute for k ≥ 5 a
ﬁnite abelian group Θ k under the connected sum operation. Θ k contains the
subgroup bPk+1 of those k-spheres which bound a parallelizable manifold.
bP4m (m ≥ 2) is cyclic of order 2 2m−2(22m−1 − 1) numerator (4 Bm/m), where
Bm is the m-th Bernoulli number. Let gm be the Milnor generator of bP4m.
If a (4 m − 1)-sphere Σ bounds a parallelizable manifold B of dimension 4 m,
then the signature τ (B) of the intersection form of B is divisible by 8 and
