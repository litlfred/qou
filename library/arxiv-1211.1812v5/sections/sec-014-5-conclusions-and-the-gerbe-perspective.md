[]{#sec.concl label="sec.concl"}

In the present paper we proved that superselection structures in curved spacetimes are section categories of presheaves of symmetric tensor *C\**-categories.

As a consequence, several new invariants are assigned to a sector: a holonomy with values in a compact Lie group, characteristic classes, a 1--cochain defining a non-abelian cocycle. As a further novelty, when an embedding is given a Tannaka duality describes the superselection structure in terms of equivariant Hilbert net bundles (or equivariant flat Hermitian bundles, see [@RRV08; @Vas12]), and this interpretation is supported by constructions of nets generated by quantum fields acted upon by group bundles. These constructions (twisted field nets) are very general and can be applied to any (free or interacting) field generating a Haag-Kastler net fulfilling normal commutation relations; moreover, they yield the expected phase factors on the charged fields that, in particular, can arise as holonomies of classical, interacting potentials in accord with the scenario of the Aharonov-Bohm effect (§[\[rem.qed\]](#rem.qed){reference-type="ref" reference="rem.qed"}).

Two points, that are object of a work in progress, remain to be discussed.

The first one is a further discussion of nets of the type studied in §[\[sec.gauge\]](#sec.gauge){reference-type="ref" reference="sec.gauge"} from the viewpoint of sectors: apart from the verification of all the properties of Haag-Kastler net (punctured Haag duality, first of all), we are interested to determine which representations, among those constructed in Theorem [\[thm.gauge1\]](#thm.gauge1){reference-type="ref" reference="thm.gauge1"}, fulfill the selection criterion [@BR08 §3.2], and then to prove that these are in one-to-one correspondence with representations of $\EuFrak{G}$, on the line of [@GLRV01 Theorem 3.14-3.15].

The second point concerns the reconstruction, starting from the Haag-Kastler net $\EuFrak{R}$ and the presheaf $\EuFrak{S}$, of the *C\**-gerbe playing the role of the field net [@DR90].

The program is to construct the local field algebras $F_a$, $a \in \Delta$, as the crossed products

$$F_a \, := \, R_a \rtimes S^\bullet_a$$

in the sense of [@BL Theorem 4.14], that can be applied because the DR-category (with simple units) $S^\bullet_a$ embeds in ${\bf end}R_a$ (which has a non-simple unit because the centre of $R_a$ is not trivial).

The further step is to recognize that there is a unique group gerbe encoding all the eventual precosheaf structures on the family $F$, and this will follow by using universality of the above crossed product.

In this scenario, the inequivalent field systems of Cor.[\[cor.gauge1\]](#cor.gauge1){reference-type="ref" reference="cor.gauge1"} defining the same observable net would find their justification, given by the fact that the involved group net bundles are different manifestations of the same gerbe; but even if the group net bundles have isomorphic duals, only one \"physical\" field net $\EuFrak{F}$ should be selected by a proper set of invariants of $Z^{1,\bullet}(\EuFrak{R})$. The net $\EuFrak{F}$ is expected to contain a subnet $\EuFrak{F}_t$, the one associated with the subcategory $Z^{1,\bullet}_t(\EuFrak{R})$ of $Z^{1,\bullet}(\EuFrak{R})$.

On a similar research line, we would like to mention the paper [@Few12], where a gauge group is defined in a functorial way. The scenario is the one of locally covariant quantum field theory ([@BFV03]), where generic functors are considered instead of nets. In our setting, it seems natural to conjecture that Fewster's gauge group is the Doplicher-Roberts dual of $Z^{1,\bullet}(\EuFrak{R})$.

In the following paragraph we explain what we mean by a gerbe over a poset and give a construction of gerbes of *C\**-algebras by means of quantum fields.

#### Gerbes.

We recall the definition of the sets

$$N_1(\Delta) := \{ \, b = ( b_0 \subseteq |b| \in \Delta ) \, \}
\ \ , \ \
N_2(\Delta) := \{ \, c = ( c_0 \subseteq c_1 \subseteq |c| \in \Delta ) \, \} 
\ ;$$

by [@RRV07 §2.2], there are inclusions

$N_1(\Delta) \subseteq \Sigma_1(\Delta)$, $N_2(\Delta) \subseteq \Sigma_2(\Delta)$.

Let now $G$ be a group. A *$G$-gerbe* over $\Delta$ is a pair

$\check \EuFrak{G}= (i,\delta)_\Delta$,

$$\label{eq.CON1}
i : \Sigma_1(\Delta) \to {\bf aut}G
\ \ , \ \
\delta : \Sigma_2(\Delta) \to G
\ \ : \ \
{\mathrm{ad}}\delta_c \circ i_{\partial_1c} = i_{\partial_0c} \circ i_{\partial_2c}
\ , \
\forall c \in \Sigma_2(\Delta)
\ .$$

The basic idea is that $i$ is a ${\bf aut}G$-cocycle (in the sense of [@RRV07]) only up to inner automorphisms, and the obstacle to get the cocycle relations is encoded by $\delta$.

If $\EuFrak{R}$ is a Haag-Kastler net, then any sector $z \in {\bf obj \ }Z^{1,\bullet}(\EuFrak{R})$ defines a gerbe $\check \EuFrak{G}_z=(i_z,\delta_z)_\Delta$, by means of the cochain $u_z : \Sigma_1(\Delta) \to NG_z$ defined by Theorem [\[thm.gerbe\]](#thm.gerbe){reference-type="ref" reference="thm.gerbe"}:

$$\label{eq.CON1a}
i_z(b) := {\mathrm{ad}}u_z(b) \ \ , \ \ \delta_z(c) := du_z(c)
\ \ , \ \qquad
b \in \Sigma_1(\Delta) \ , \ c \in \Sigma_2(\Delta) \ .$$

A *$\check \EuFrak{G}$-*C\**-gerbe* $\check \EuFrak{F}$ is given by a family of *C\**-dynamical systems

$\alpha_a : G \to {\bf aut}F_a$, $a \in \Delta$,

on which \*-monomorphisms

$\jmath_{a'a} : F_a \to F_{a'}$, $a \subseteq a'$,

are defined in such a way that

$$\label{eq.CON2}
\jmath_{|c| c_1} \circ \jmath_{c_1 c_0} = \alpha_{|c|}(\delta_c) \circ \jmath_{|c| c_0}
\ \ \ , \ \ \
\jmath_{|b| b_0} \circ \alpha_{b_0}(g)  = \alpha_{|b|}(i_b(g)) \circ \jmath_{|b|b_0}
\ ,$$

for any

$b \in N_1(\Delta)$, $c \in N_2(\Delta)$, $g \in G$.

The first equality in ([\[eq.CON2\]](#eq.CON2){reference-type="ref" reference="eq.CON2"}) generalizes ([\[eq.00\]](#eq.00){reference-type="ref" reference="eq.00"}), whilst the second one is analogous to ([\[eq.GA00\]](#eq.GA00){reference-type="ref" reference="eq.GA00"}). Moreover, again by ([\[eq.CON2\]](#eq.CON2){reference-type="ref" reference="eq.CON2"}), the fixed point family

$\EuFrak{R}= (R,\jmath)_\Delta$, $R_a := F_a^G$, $a \in \Delta$,

is a *net* of *C\**-algebras, that we interpret here as the observable net.

When $\delta \equiv 1$ the gerbe $\check \EuFrak{G}$ defines a group net bundle, written $\EuFrak{G}$, and $\check \EuFrak{F}$ is a $\EuFrak{G}$-*net* of *C\**-algebras.

To illustrate how gerbes can arise in quantum field theory we give the following result:

::: thm
[]{#thm.gauge3 label="thm.gauge3"} Let $M$ denote a globally hyperbolic (spin) manifold and $(G_0,\gamma)$ a ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded group. Given a symmetric, separating set $\lambda$ for $G_0$ and a morphism

$$\chi : \pi_1(M) \to N^\lambda G_0 / G_0 \ ,$$

there are a $G_0$-gerbe $\check \EuFrak{G}= (i,\delta)_\Delta$ and a $\check \EuFrak{G}$-*C\**-gerbe

$\check \EuFrak{F}= (F,\jmath)_\Delta$,

defining a fixed-point **net** $\EuFrak{R}= (R,\jmath)_\Delta$ causally represented on a Hilbert net bundle.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We take $\omega \in \Delta$, a path frame $P_\omega$, and define

$$V^\downarrow_b := \chi( p_{\omega \, {\partial_0 b}} * b * p_{{\partial_1 b}\, \omega} ) \in N^\lambda G_0 / G_0
\ \ , \ \
\forall b \in \Sigma_1(\Delta) \ .$$

By homotopy invariance of $\chi$, the relations

$$\label{eq.gauge3.1}
V^\downarrow_{\partial_0c} V^\downarrow_{\partial_2c} = V^\downarrow_{\partial_1c}
\ \ , \ \
c \in \Sigma_2(\Delta)
\ ,$$

are fulfilled (see [@Ruz05 §2.2]). For any $b \in \Sigma_1(b)$ we pick a $V_b \in N^\lambda G_0$ such that $V_b \,{\mathrm{mod}}G_0 = V^\downarrow_b$, so ([\[eq.gauge3.1\]](#eq.gauge3.1){reference-type="ref" reference="eq.gauge3.1"}) implies

$$\label{eq.gauge3.2}
\delta_c := V_{\partial_0c} V_{\partial_2c} V_{\partial_1c}^{-1} \in G_0
\ \ , \ \
c \in \Sigma_2(\Delta)
\ .$$

We define

$\imath_b := {\mathrm{ad}}V_b$, $\forall b \in \Sigma_1(\Delta)$,

which yields, by ([\[eq.gauge3.2\]](#eq.gauge3.2){reference-type="ref" reference="eq.gauge3.2"}), the desired gerbe $\check \EuFrak{G}= (\imath,\delta)_\Delta$. We now consider the normal $( \tau(G_0),\tau(\gamma) )$-net of von Neumann algebras $\EuFrak{F}_*$ of Lemma [\[lem.gauge2\]](#lem.gauge2){reference-type="ref" reference="lem.gauge2"}, extend $\tau$ to $N^\lambda G_0$ as in the proof of Theorem [\[thm.gauge1\]](#thm.gauge1){reference-type="ref" reference="thm.gauge1"} and set $G_* := \tau(G_0)$, so $\tau(NG_0)$ takes values in $N_\gamma G_*$. Recalling the inclusion $N_1(\Delta) \subset \Sigma_1(\Delta)$ we define

$$\jmath_{a'a} : F_a \to F_{a'}
\ \ , \ \
\jmath_{a'a}(t) := \{ {\mathrm{ad}}\tau(V_{(a,a')}) \}(t)
\ \ , \ \
\forall a \subseteq a' \in \Delta
\ , \
t \in F_a
\ .$$

Moreover we consider the gauge actions

$$\alpha_a : G_0 \to {\bf aut}F_a 
\ \ , \ \ 
\alpha_a(g) := {\mathrm{ad}}\tau(g)
\ \ , \ \
a \in \Delta \ , \ g \in G_0 \ .$$

By ([\[eq.gauge3.2\]](#eq.gauge3.2){reference-type="ref" reference="eq.gauge3.2"}) we have

$$\jmath_{|c| c_1} \circ \jmath_{c_1 c_0} = \alpha_{|c|}(\delta_c) \circ \jmath_{|c| c_0}
\ \ , \ \ 
\forall c \in N_2(\Delta) \ .$$

Moreover,

$$\jmath_{|b| b_0} \circ \alpha_{b_0}(g) \ = \
\{ {\mathrm{ad}}\tau(V_b) \} \circ {\mathrm{ad}}\tau(g) \ = \ 
{\mathrm{ad}}\tau ( V_bg ) \ = \
{\mathrm{ad}}\tau (\imath_b(g) \, V_b) \ = \
\alpha_{|b|}(\imath_b(g)) \circ \jmath_{|b| b_0} \ ,$$

so $\check \EuFrak{F}$ is a $\check \EuFrak{G}$-gerbe as desired, with fixed-point net $\EuFrak{R}= (R,\jmath)_\Delta$.

To construct a causal representation of $\EuFrak{R}$ we consider the Hilbert space $H_*$ of Lemma [\[lem.gauge2\]](#lem.gauge2){reference-type="ref" reference="lem.gauge2"} and the subspace $\overline H_*$ of $G_0$-invariant elements. Defining

$\overline U_{a'a}v:= \tau(V_{(a,a')}) v$, $a \subseteq a'$, $v \in \overline H_*$, we find, for any triple $c := \{ a \subseteq a' \subseteq a'' \in \Delta \}$,

$$\overline U_{a''a'} \overline U_{a'a}v =
\tau(V_{(a',a'')} \, V_{(a,a')}) v =
\tau ( \delta_c \, V_{(a,a'')} ) v =
\tau (V_{(a,a'')} ) \, \tau(\delta'_c) v =
\tau (V_{(a,a'')} ) v =
\overline U_{a''a}v \ ,$$

where $\delta'_c := V_{(a,a'')}^{-1} \delta_c V_{(a,a'')} \in G_0$. So

$\overline\EuFrak{H}= (\overline H, \overline U)_\Delta$, $\overline H = \{ \overline H_a \equiv \overline H_* \}$,

is a Hilbert net bundle.

We now define

$$\overline\pi_a : R_a \to B \overline H_a
\ \ , \ \
\overline\pi_a(t)v := tv
\ \ , \ \
\forall v \in \overline H_a \subset H_*
\ , \
a \in \Delta 
\ .$$

Since each $R_a$ is pointwise $G_0$-invariant we find that $tv \in \overline H_a$, so the definition is well-posed. Moreover

$${\mathrm{ad}}\overline U_{a'a} \circ \overline\pi_a(t) = 
\{ {\mathrm{ad}}\tau(V_{(a,a')}) \}(t) = 
\jmath_{a'a}(t) =
\overline\pi_{a'} \circ  \jmath_{a'a}(t) \ ,$$

for any $t \in R_a$, so the pair $(\overline\pi,\overline U)$ is a representation as desired.

Finally, to verify causality (in the sense of ([\[def.norm\]](#def.norm){reference-type="ref" reference="def.norm"})) we note that for any $o \perp a$, $t_1 \in R_a$, $t_0 \in R_o$,

$$[ \pi_a(t_1) \, , \, {\mathrm{ad}}U_p \circ \pi_o(t_0) ]
\ = \
[ t_1 \, , \, {\mathrm{ad}}\tau(V_p)(t_0)  ]
\ \ , \ \
\forall p : o \to a 
\ ,$$

and the last commutator is zero because

$\{ {\mathrm{ad}}\tau(V)\}R_o \subseteq R_o$ for any $V \in N^\lambda G_0$

(recall ([\[def.NG\]](#def.NG){reference-type="ref" reference="def.NG"}) and that $\tau(N^\lambda G_0) \subseteq N_\gamma G_*$).

This suffices to prove causality, as $\EuFrak{R}$ has the same fibres of the fixed-point net of $\EuFrak{F}_*$, which is causal. ◻
:::

It is easily verified that $\chi$ has a lift to $N^\lambda G_0$ if, and only if, there is a choice of $\{ V_b \}$ such that $V$ is a 1-cocycle (see [@Vas12 Theorem 5.5]), that is, if, and only if, $\check \EuFrak{F}$ can be arranged in such a way to be a net.

The point of the previous theorem is that we expect that the morphism $\chi$ is determined by the presheaf structure of $Z^{1,\bullet}(\EuFrak{R})$, on the line of [@Vas12 Theorem 5.3].
