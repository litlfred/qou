Figure [\[seis\]](#seis){reference-type="ref" reference="seis"} is called by some (at least by me) "Devil's tesselation".

In general, there is a tesselation by triangles of the hyperbolic plane with group of symmetries $[n,p,q]$ if and only if $$\label{brig}\frac{1}{n}+ \frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<1.$$ In particular, if $n>3$ and $p,q\geq 3$ then this inequality is satisfied. So most rank three Coxeter groups are the group of symmetries of some hyperbolic tiling. Those Coxeter groups which are not, are either the group of symmetries of a tesselation by triangles of the Euclidean plane[^1] where inequality ([\[brig\]](#brig){reference-type="ref" reference="brig"}) is changed by an equality or they are the group of symmetries of a tesselation by triangles of the sphere like the following

In the spherical case the reversed inequality is satisfied

$$\frac{1}{n}+ \frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}>1.$$

so the triples $[n,p,q]$ that appear here are the"little numbers" (we just need to add to Figures [\[12\]](#12){reference-type="ref" reference="12"}, [\[13\]](#13){reference-type="ref" reference="13"} and [2](#14){reference-type="ref" reference="14"}, the groups $[2,2,n]$ for $n\geq 2$).

[^1]: Essentially those that can be constructed from Figures [\[triang\]](#triang){reference-type="ref" reference="triang"}, [\[check\]](#check){reference-type="ref" reference="check"} and [\[honey\]](#honey){reference-type="ref" reference="honey"}. The groups of symmetries appearing in this fashion are just the three groups $[3,3,3], [4,4,2]$ and $[6,3,2]$.
