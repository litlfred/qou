Why did we us the letter $\mathbb I$ to denote the set $\{R, B_s, B_r, B_{sr}, B_{rs}, B_{srs}\}$? Because they are indecomposable objects. Let us see why.

We will use the following notation. A $\mathrm{min}$ in the subindex of a graded object means the minimal $i$ for which its degree $i$ part is non-zero. For example $(B_s)_{\mathrm{min}}=(B_s)_{-1}$

As we have seen, every element $M\in \mathbb I$ is generated as an $R$-bimodule by the element $1^{\otimes}\in M_{\mathrm{min}}$. Let us suppose that $M=N\oplus P$. Then we have that $M_{\mathrm{min}}=N_{\mathrm{min}}\oplus P_{\mathrm{min}}$ as $\mathbb R$-vector spaces, but the dimension of $M_{\mathrm{min}}$ over $\mathbb R$ is one, so $M_{\mathrm{min}}$ is either $N_{\mathrm{min}}$ or $P_{\mathrm{min}}$ and thus $M$ is either $N$ or $P$. Thus $M$ is indecomposable.

**Caution!** *It is a particularity of the group $S_3$ that all the indecomposable Soergel bimodules are generated by $1^{\otimes}$. For example in $S_4$ the indecomposable $B_{s_2s_1s_3s_2}$ is not generated by $1^{\otimes}$.*
