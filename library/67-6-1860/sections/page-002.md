The quaternionic gauge theory was first studied by Finkelstein et al.® who
obtained a broken SU(2) gauge theory with massive W* and massless photon
fields. Since quaternions are associative, it is feasible to gauge the norm group
SO(4) of quaternions, a bigger symmetry than the automorphism SU(2) em-
ployed by Finkelstein et al. There are two important characteristics of the
quaternionic gauge theory.’ First, the rep content is limited: Only the ir-
reducible reps (2, 2), (3, 1), (, 3), (2, 1) and (1, 2) of SO(4) are realized in terms
of the quaternionic fields. Since quaternions are closed under the product, the
same is true also for composite fields except gauge singlets. Second, the Yang-
Mills sector contains more than SO(4) gauge fields: There exist two more extra
vector fields which would be redundant but for the quaternionic fields repre-
senting the complex irreducible reps (2,1) and (1,2). Since fermions and
complex Higgs mesons in the quaternionic Weinberg-Salam theory are assumed
to belong to them, the extra fields should be promoted to a single Abelian gauge
field which couples to U(1) charge a of the quaternionic fields. The resulting
gauge group is SO(4)X U(1)=SU(2). x SU(2)eX U(1).

A quaternionic Fock space is defined only for gauge singlets.‘ This is due
to the noncommutativity of quaternions. Although gauge singlets are composite
objects, they reduce to elementary quaternionic fields in the lowest order approxi-
