An $n$-braid is a collection of $n$ strands that go downwards monotonically from a set of fixed $n$ points to another set of fixed $n$ points as shown in Figure [1](#fig:braid){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:braid"}.

![braid](braid_small.eps){#fig:braid}

The set of all $n$-braids makes a group $B_n$ with product of braids $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ given by putting $\beta_2$ below $\beta_1$. It is well known (see for example [@Birman:1974]) that $B_n$ is generated by $\sigma_1, \sigma_2,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ (Figure [2](#fig:s_i){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:s_i"}) with relations $\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i$ ($|i-j|>1$) and $\sigma_k\sigma_{k+1}\sigma_k=\sigma_{k+1}\sigma_k\sigma_{k+1}$. See Figure [3](#fig:braid_relation){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:braid_relation"} for the latter relation, which is called the braid relation.

![$i$th generator of the $n$-braid group $B_n$](s_i_small.eps){#fig:s_i}

<figcaption>braid relation</figcaption>

So we have the following group presentation of $B_n$. $$\label{eq:braid_presentation}
  B_n
  =
  \langle
    \sigma_1,\sigma_2,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
    \mid
    \sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i\,\text{($|i-j|>1$)},
    \sigma_k\sigma_{k+1}\sigma_k=\sigma_{k+1}\sigma_k\sigma_{k+1}
  \rangle$$

It is known that any knot or link can be presented as the closure of a braid.

::: theorem
[]{#thm:Alexander label="thm:Alexander"} Any knot or link can be presented as the closure of a braid.
:::

<figure id="fig:braid_presentation">
<p><embed src="braid_small.eps" /> <embed src="closure_small.eps" /></p>
<figcaption>the figure-eight knot is presented as the closure of a braid</figcaption>
</figure>

Here the closure of an $n$-braid is obtained by connecting the $n$ points on the top with the $n$ points on the bottom without entanglement as shown in the middle picture of Figure [4](#fig:braid_presentation){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:braid_presentation"}.

There are many braids that present a knot or link but if two braids present the same knot or link, they are related by a finite sequence of conjugations and (de-)stabilizations. In fact we have the following theorem.

::: theorem
[]{#thm:Markov label="thm:Markov"} If two braids $\beta$ and $\beta'$ give equivalent links, then they are related by a finite sequence of conjugations, stabilizations, and destabilizations. Here a conjugation replaces $\alpha\beta$ with $\beta\alpha$, or equivalently $\beta$ with $\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha$ (Figure $\ref{fig:conjugate}$), a stabilization replaces $\beta\in B_n$ with $\beta\sigma_n^{\pm1}\in B_{n+1}$ (Figure $\ref{fig:stabilization}$), a destabilization replaces $\beta\sigma_n^{\pm1}\in B_{n+1}$ with $\beta\in B_n$.
