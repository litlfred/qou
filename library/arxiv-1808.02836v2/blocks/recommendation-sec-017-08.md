The remaining tetrahedron type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ can occur in all cases and in all places around $f.$ To start suppose $\Delta_2$ is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}};$ one of the $H$--even edges of $\Delta_2$ is $f$ and we denote the other $f'.$ If $\Delta_2$ appears more than once around $f,$ then $f=f'.$ In particular the two faces of $\Delta_2$ containing $f'$ meet $\Delta_1$ in at least two edges and hence cannot be identified with a tetrahedron $\Delta_{2i - 1},$ $2 \leq i \leq m.$ This shows that in the cases $(3,6)$ and $(2,4)$ all tetrahedra must be pairwise distinct by the symmetry of the situation.
