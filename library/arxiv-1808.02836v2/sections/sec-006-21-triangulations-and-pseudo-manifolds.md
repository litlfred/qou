Let~∆ be a ﬁnite union of pairwise disjoint Euclidean 3–simplices with the standard simplicial structure. Every
k–simplex τ in~∆ is contained in a unique 3–simplex στ . A 2–simplex in~∆ is termed a face.
Let Φ be a family of afﬁne isomorphisms pairing the faces in ~∆, with the properties that ϕ∈ Φ if and only if
ϕ−1∈ Φ, and every face is the domain of a unique element of Φ. The elements of Φ are termed face pairings.
Consider the quotient space ˆM =~∆/Φ with the quotient topology, and denote the quotient map p: ~∆→ ˆM.
We make the additional assumption on Φ that for every k–simplex τ in~∆ the restriction of p to the interior of
τ is injective. Then the set of non-manifold points of ˆM is contained in the 0–skeleton, and in this case ˆM is
called a closed 3–dimensional pseudo-manifold. (See Seifert-Threfall [33].) We also always assume that ˆM is
connected. The triple T = (~∆, Φ, p) is called a (singular) triangulation of ˆM.
Let ˆM(k) ={p(τk)| τk⊆~∆} denote the set of images of the k–simplices of~∆ under the projection map. Then
the elements of ˆM(k) are precisely the equivalence classes of k–simplices in ~∆. The elements of ˆM(0) are
termed the vertices of ˆM and the elements of ˆM(1) are the edges. The triangulation is a k–vertex triangulation
if ˆM(0) has size k.
