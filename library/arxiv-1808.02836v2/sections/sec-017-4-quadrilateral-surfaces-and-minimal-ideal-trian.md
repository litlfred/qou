In this section we prove the following result, first stated in the introduction. The essence of the proof is a counting argument taking into account even edges, compression discs for the canonical normal representatives, and types of tetrahedra. This is modelled on the blueprint for closed 3--manifolds [@Jaco-Z2-2017; @Jaco-minimal-2009; @Jaco-Z2-2013; @Nakamura-complexity-2017].

::: reptheorem
thm:sumofnorms Let $\mathcal{T}$ be an ideal triangulation of a cusped hyperbolic 3--manifold $M$ and let $H~\le~H_2 (M,\mathbb{Z}_2)$ be a rank $2$ subgroup. Then $$|\mathcal{T}| \geq \sum \limits_{0 \neq c \in H} || \ c \ ||.$$ Moreover, in the case of equality the triangulation is minimal, each canonical normal representative of a non-zero element in $H\le H_2 (M,\mathbb{Z}_2)$ is taut and meets each tetrahedron in a quadrilateral disc, and the number of tetrahedra in the triangulation is even.
:::

Note that the conclusion that *each canonical normal representative meets each tetrahedron in a quadrilateral disc* is equivalent to saying that *all tetrahedra are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qqq}}$*.

::: proof
*Proof.* It suffices to work with a minimal ideal triangulation. For if we can show any such a triangulation satisfies the desired inequality, then clearly an arbitrary ideal one does also. Moreover, if any ideal triangulation admits equality, then it clearly must be minimal.

Let $\mathcal{T}'$ be a minimal ideal triangulation of a cusped hyperbolic 3--manifold $M.$ Fix a subgroup $H< H_2 (M,\mathbb{Z}_2)$ of rank $2,$ and let $S_i,$ $i=1,2,3,$ be the canonical normal representatives of the non-zero classes $0 \neq \varphi_i \in H.$ Let $\mathcal{T}$ be the minimal triangulation that has the smallest number of tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}$ amongst all minimal triangulations of $M$ that can be obtained from $\mathcal{T}'$ by a sequence of 4-4-moves.

Following the notation used in Section [\[sec:normsurfs\]](#sec:normsurfs){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:normsurfs"}, the union of the normal surfaces $S_i,$ $i = 1,2,3,$ intersects the tetrahedra of $\mathcal{T}$ in $n_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}},$ $n_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}},$ and so on.

Let $d_i$ be the maximal number of pairwise disjoint compression discs for $S_i$, whose union does not separate any component of $S_i.$ We have $|| c_i || \leq - \chi (S_i)$ due to Lemma [\[lem:chi bounds norm for canonical\]](#lem:chi bounds norm for canonical){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:chi bounds norm for canonical"}. Moreover, in the presence of compression discs, we obtain the stronger bound $$\sum \limits_{i=1}^{3} ||\ c_i\ || \le - \sum \limits_{i=1}^{3} (\chi(S_i) +2d_i)$$ since compressions do not change the homology class and do not introduce components that are spheres or projective planes. To simplify the expression, and with a view towards the second part of the theorem, we write $$\sum \limits_{i=1}^{3} ||\ c_i\ ||  = -D - \sum \chi(S_i),$$ where $D\ge 0.$ Here, we take care of the possibility that the canonical surfaces are incompressible but not norm minimising, and we note that each independent compression disc contributes 2 to $D.$

For the sake of contradiction, assume $$|\mathcal{T}| < \sum \limits_{i=1}^{3} ||\ c_i\ ||.$$ Lemma [\[lem:formula for degree 3 edges\]](#lem:formula for degree 3 edges){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:formula for degree 3 edges"} now implies the strict inequality $$\label{eq:main}
  n_{\mathfrak{tt}}+n_{\mathfrak{qtt}}+2 D+\sum_{d=5}^{\infty} (d-4) \mathfrak{e}_d < \mathfrak{e}_3.$$

Hence, proving the first part of Theorem [\[thm:sumofnorms\]](#thm:sumofnorms){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:sumofnorms"} is equivalent to showing that this is impossible. That is, we need to prove that the left hand side of inequality ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) must be at least as large as the right hand side.

The idea of the proof is to counter-balance each contribution to the right hand side with a contribution to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) of equal or larger size. A contribution to the right hand side only arises from a degree three edge, and we have already shown that each such edge is contained in a layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus subcomplex. Any such complex contains at most one edge of degree three. The organising principles for the counting argument are as follows. We identify sets of degree three edges that can be associated with an $H$--even edge $f$ such that $d(f)-4$ is as least as large as the size of the associated set of degree three edges (see Claim 2 and the paragraph directly after the proof of Claim 2). In doing so, we may double count some of the degree three edges, but we make sure not to double count any of edges $f$. For the remaining degree three edges, there are only a small number of special cases, in which we need to also take into account the remaining terms $n_{\mathfrak{tt}}+n_{\mathfrak{qtt}}+2 D$. Here we need to make sure that such contributions to the left hand side are only counted at most once. Moreover, in these special cases the contribution to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) is strictly greater than the contribution to the right hand side.

The following terminology helps organise the counting argument. A *deficit in ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"})* is when the contribution to the left is bigger than to the right, a *balance in ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"})* is when they are equal, and otherwise we have a *gain in ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"})*. Thus, for proving the first part of Theorem [\[thm:sumofnorms\]](#thm:sumofnorms){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:sumofnorms"} it is sufficient to disregard all deficits and balances and show that it is impossible to construct a triangulation exhibiting a gain. For the second part of Theorem [\[thm:sumofnorms\]](#thm:sumofnorms){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:sumofnorms"} we also need to keep track of balances.

Recall that the core tetrahedron of any layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus $\operatorname{LST}$ is a standard one-tetrahedron layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus $\operatorname{LST}^\star(1, 2, 3).$ A canonical normal representative of a class of $H_2 (M,\mathbb{Z}_2)$ necessarily intersects $\operatorname{LST}^\star(1, 2, 3)$ in one particular quadrilateral type, or not at all. This can be seen by enumerating all $0/1$-colourings of edges in $\operatorname{LST}^\star(1, 2, 3)$ such that every face of $\operatorname{LST}^\star(1, 2, 3)$ has zero or two edges of colour $1$. Accordingly, the intersection of $\operatorname{LST}^\star(1, 2, 3)$ with all three canonical normal representatives of non-trivial classes of $H$ must result in a tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ or $\Delta_{\emptyset}$.

It follows that either all tetrahedra in $\operatorname{LST}$ are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ or of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}$ (this follows directly from looking at the pattern of $H$--odd edges in the boundary). Accordingly we say that $\operatorname{LST}$ is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ or $\Delta_{\emptyset}.$ A layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus $\operatorname{LST}$ of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ has one $H$--even boundary edge and the other two boundary edges are $H$--odd.

: All edges of degree 3 in $\mathcal{T}$ are $H$--even.

: Due to Lemma [\[lem:degreethree\]](#lem:degreethree){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:degreethree"}, every edge $e \in \mathcal{T}$ of degree three can be associated to a maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus $\operatorname{LST}_e \subset \mathcal{T}$ containing $e$ in its interior. More precisely, $e$ occurs twice in the core tetrahedron of $\operatorname{LST}_e$ and once in the tetrahedron adjacent to the core. If $\operatorname{LST}_e$ is of type $\Delta_{\emptyset},$ then naturally $e$ must be $H$--even. Hence, let $\operatorname{LST}_e$ be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}.$ Since $e$ occurs twice in the core tetrahedron and the other two edges in the core tetrahedron occur in it one and three times respectively, they only way the core tetrahedron of $\operatorname{LST}_e$ can be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ is for $e$ to be $H$--even.0◻

A layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus $\operatorname{LST}$ has exactly one boundary edge of degree 1 with respect to $\operatorname{LST}.$ We call the other two boundary edges *non-unital*.

: A maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus $\operatorname{LST}$ of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}$ containing an $H$--even edge of degree three either has an interior $H$--even edge of degree $\geq 6$, or non-unital boundary edges $g$ and $f$ with $d_{\operatorname{LST}}(g) = 3$ and $d_{\operatorname{LST}}(f) \geq 5$.

: In a layered solid torus of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}$ every edge is necessarily $H$--even. We can thus ignore the edge-coulourings.

Every layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus $\operatorname{LST}$ in a minimal triangulation $\mathcal{T}$ and containing a degree three edge is obtained from a layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus of type $\operatorname{LST}^{\star} (1,3,4)$ by iteratively layering onto one of the non-unital boundary edges. In $\operatorname{LST}^{\star} (1,3,4)$, the non-unital boundary edges $g$ and $f$ have degrees $d_{\operatorname{LST}}(g) = 3$ and $d_{\operatorname{LST}}(f) = 5$. If we layer on $f$ we create an interior edge of degree six and we are done. If we layer on $g$ we create an interior edge of degree four, the formerly unital boundary edge is now of degree three, and the degree of $g$ -- still being in the boundary -- is increased by two. Layering on the unital edge contradicts minimality, since it creates an edge of degree two. Iterating this procedure proves the claim. 0◻

: Let $\operatorname{LST}\subset \mathcal{T}$ be a maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus that contains an interior edge $e \in \mathcal{T}$ of degree three. Then $\operatorname{LST}$ is incident with an $H$--even edge $f$ such that if $m$ is the number of degree three edges of all maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid tori incident with $f$, then $d(f)-4 \ge m$ (i.e. contributing a deficit or balance in ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"})), unless the following three conditions are satisfied for $\operatorname{LST}$:

1.  all interior $H$--even edges distinct from $e$ are of degree four,

2.  the solid torus is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$, and

3.  the unique $H$--even boundary edge $f$ is of degree one with respect to $\operatorname{LST}.$

We call a maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus that contains an interior edge $e$ of degree three and satisfies the conditions (1)--(3) *internally unbalanced*.

: No interior edge of $\operatorname{LST}$ distinct from $e$ can be of degree less than four (see Lemma [\[lem:degreethree\]](#lem:degreethree){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:degreethree"}). If $\operatorname{LST}$ contains an interior $H$--even edge of degree $> 4,$ then we choose $f$ to be this edge. It follows that $m=1$, and thus $d(f)-4 \ge m$ holds. Hence assume that all interior $H$--even edges distinct from $e$ are of degree four.

Suppose that $\operatorname{LST}$ is of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}$. All edges of $\operatorname{LST}$ are $H$--even and, by the above, all its interior edges apart from $e$ are of degree four. But then, by Claim 1, there exists a boundary edge $f \in \partial \operatorname{LST}$ satisfying $d_{\operatorname{LST}}(f) \geq 5$. Now $m-1$ additional maximal layered solid tori containing degree three edges meet $f$ in one of their $H$--even boundary edges. By Lemma [\[lem:intersection\]](#lem:intersection){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:intersection"}, $f$ is of degree at least $4 + 2m.$ Thus $d(f)-4\ge 2m > m$ and we have a deficit in ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}).

The last case is that $\operatorname{LST}$ is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$. Since $\operatorname{LST}$ is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ it has a unique $H$-even boundary edge $f \in \partial \operatorname{LST}$. If $d_{\operatorname{LST}}(f) > 1,$ then $d_{\operatorname{LST}}(f) \geq 3.$ Again $m-1$ additional maximal layered solid tori containing degree three edges are incident with $f$. By Lemma [\[lem:intersection\]](#lem:intersection){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:intersection"}, $f$ is of degree at least $2 + 2m.$ Thus $d(f) -4 \ge  2m-2.$ So if $m\ge 2$, then $d(f) -4 \ge m.$ If $m=1,$ then we claim that the degree of $f$ is at least five. Suppose it is not. Then the neighbourhood of $f$ is modelled on a layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus having one more tetrahedron than $\operatorname{LST}$ but extra identifications between the boundary edges. This is not possible due to maximality and Lemma [\[lem:lst no folds\]](#lem:lst no folds){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:lst no folds"}. Hence no identifications are possible amongst the edges and so $d(f)\ge 5.$ 0◻

The set of internally unbalanced maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid tori can now be canonically partitioned, by grouping together those which meet along their unique $H$--even boundary edge. Fix one such set of $m$ internally unbalanced maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid tori around a common $H$--even edge $f.$ Its contribution to the right hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) is $m.$ But $f$ must be of degree at least $2m$ by Lemma [\[lem:intersection\]](#lem:intersection){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:intersection"}, and its contribution to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) is $\geq 2m-4.$ Hence, there is a deficit if $m > 4.$ We therefore consider the cases where $m \le 4.$ If $m=4$ and $d(f) > 8,$ we also obtain a deficit.

: Since the degree $d = \operatorname{deg}(f)$ of $f$ is bounded below by four, we obtain at most a balance if: $$(m,d) \in \{ (4,8),(3, 7), (2,6),(1,5) \},$$ and we obtain at most a gain if: $$(m,d) \in \{ (3,6),(2,5),(2,4),(1,4) \}.$$ We call the edges in the above collections the *balancing edges* and the *gaining edges*, respectively.

The maximal potential gain is $2$ in the case $(2,4)$ and it is $1$ in all other cases. Our strategy now is to visit each edge $f$ that gives a potential gain, show that it either achieves a deficit, or (even stronger) that it achieves a deficit for itself and all $H$--even edges in its star.

Before taking a closer look at these cases, we first prove an auxiliary statement. For this and for the remainder of this proof, we denote the tetrahedra around $f$ by $\Delta_1, \ldots , \Delta_d$ (labelled in cyclic order such that $\Delta_i$ shares a triangle with $\Delta_{i+1};$ some tetrahedron may appear in this sequence multiple times). Moreover, without loss of generality and following Lemma [\[lem:intersection\]](#lem:intersection){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:intersection"}, the internally unbalanced maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid torus $\operatorname{LST}_i$ incident to $f$ can be assumed to contain $\Delta_{2i - 1},$ $1 \leq i \leq m.$

: In all four cases $(m,d) \in \{ (3,6),(2,5),(2,4),(1,4) \},$ the edge $f$ is surrounded by $d$ pairwise distinct tetrahedra.

: Tetrahedra contained in distinct internally unbalanced maximal layered $\partial$--punctured solid tori must be pairwise distinct. Hence the tetrahedra $\Delta_{2i - 1},$ $1 \leq i \leq m,$ are pairwise distinct since $\Delta_{2i - 1}$ is contained in $\operatorname{LST}_{i}.$

A tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ cannot occur twice around $f$ because it has only one $H$--even edge and, of course, there can be no tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qqq}}.$ Moreover, only $\Delta_3$ in case $(1,4)$ can be of type $\Delta_{\emptyset},$ and hence a tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}$ cannot occur more than once around $f.$

If $f$ occurs more than once in a tetrahedron $\Delta$ of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}},$ then we must be in $(2,5)$ or $(1,4)$ since $f$ is contained in a face only having $H$--even edges. In case $(2,5)$ we have $\Delta= \Delta_4 = \Delta_5;$ in case $(1,4)$ we either have $\Delta= \Delta_2 = \Delta_3,$ or $\Delta= \Delta_3 = \Delta_4,$ or $\Delta= \Delta_2 =\Delta_4.$ Assume we have one of the three cases where $\Delta = \Delta_i = \Delta_{i+1}.$ Then two faces of $\Delta$ are identified. However, there is only one way to identify two faces of a tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}$ in an orientation preserving manner, and this creates an edge of degree one, which is not possible. Hence we are in case $(1,4)$ and $\Delta= \Delta_2 =\Delta_4.$ Then $\Delta_3$ is necessarily of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}.$ But $\Delta_{\emptyset}$ cannot share two distinct faces with $\Delta_2 = \Delta_4$ because a tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}$ only has one triangle of $H$--even edges.

The remaining tetrahedron type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ can occur in all cases and in all places around $f.$ To start suppose $\Delta_2$ is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}};$ one of the $H$--even edges of $\Delta_2$ is $f$ and we denote the other $f'.$ If $\Delta_2$ appears more than once around $f,$ then $f=f'.$ In particular the two faces of $\Delta_2$ containing $f'$ meet $\Delta_1$ in at least two edges and hence cannot be identified with a tetrahedron $\Delta_{2i - 1},$ $2 \leq i \leq m.$ This shows that in the cases $(3,6)$ and $(2,4)$ all tetrahedra must be pairwise distinct by the symmetry of the situation.

In the case $(2,5)$ this implies that $\Delta_2$ cannot equal $\Delta_4.$ However, $\Delta_2$ also meets a face of $\Delta_3$ and by the same reasoning cannot equal $\Delta_5.$ The only remaining possibility in this case is that $\Delta_4$ equals $\Delta_5.$ Here again, $\Delta_5$ meets $\Delta_1$ in a face, and hence if $\Delta_4$ equals $\Delta_5,$ then $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_3$ share two edges, which is not possible.

It remains to consider the case $(1,4).$ First suppose that $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_3$ are the same tetrahedron. There are two possibilities that are combinatorially equivalent. Taking all the face pairings into account, we conclude that $\Delta_2$ is a 1--tetrahedron $\operatorname{LST}^\star(1, 2, 3).$ But this meets $\Delta_1,$ and hence $\operatorname{LST}_1,$ in three edges, a contradiction. See Figure [1](#fig:distinct1){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:distinct1"} for an illustration of this case.

Hence suppose without loss of generality that $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_4$ are the same tetrahedron. Analysing the two different gluings that arise, we see that $\Delta_2$ is layered on one of the two $H$-odd boundary edges of $\Delta_1.$ But this either contradicts maximality of $\operatorname{LST}_1$ or it contradicts Lemma [\[lem:lst no folds\]](#lem:lst no folds){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:lst no folds"}, see Figure [2](#fig:distinct2){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:distinct2"}.

![Identifying $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_4$ in case $(1,4)$ yields a layering on the boundary of $\operatorname{LST}_1.$ []{#fig:distinct2 label="fig:distinct2"}](distinct_2){#fig:distinct2 width=".45\\textwidth"}

This completes the proof of the claim.0◻

: Since $f$ is $H$--even of degree four, we have to find a contribution to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) of $2$ to obtain a balance and $>2$ to obtain a deficit.

By Claim 3 and by the preliminary observations above, we can assume that all four tetrahedra surrounding $f$ are distinct and tetrahedra $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_3$ are of both of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}.$ If the quadrilaterals in $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_3$ are of distinct colours, $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_4$ must both be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}.$ Hence, they contribute $2$ to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}). Moreover, since these two tetrahedra then do not have any $H$--even edges other than $f,$ they cannot contribute to any other set of maximal layered solid tori united along an $H$--even edge, and their contribution to ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) is global. In particular, there is at most a balance and not a gain. Now one of the surfaces, $S_i,$ links the edge $f$ in an annulus. We can perform a compression of this annulus, using a disc transverse to $f.$ This is indeed a compression disc since the resulting surface is still connected, due to its intersection with the $H$-odd boundary edges of the layered $\partial$--punctured solid tori. Whence in total, we have a deficit of $4.$ We also note that this compression disc is confined to a neighbourhood of $f$ and that the tetrahedra $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_4$ have a *unique* $H$--even edge. Thus, the quadrilateral discs of $S_i$ affected by this compression allow no other compressions of this kind and we may associate the total deficit with $f.$

We can thus assume that $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_3$ are coloured the same. This implies that $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_4$ must both be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ with the same colours as well. In particular, the octahedron that is the boundary of the star of $f$ has eight $H$-odd edges, all of degree two inside the star of $f,$ and a 4--cycle of $H$--even edges, all of degree $1$ inside the star of $f.$ In this case we can perform compressions of two of the surfaces as above. Whence in total we have a contribution of $8$ to the left hand side and a contribution of $2$ to the right hand side. We now allocate a contribution of $3$ to $f$ so that in total we have a deficit of $1$ associated with $f.$ We then associate a contribution of $2.5$ to each of the other two $H$--even edges of $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_4.$ Hence each of these (if it is in our collection of gaining or balancing edges) now has a deficit associated with it and is not visited again. In particular, any further compression discs we find in the star of an $H$--even edge is disjoint from the ones we just found.

This completes the case of gaining edges of type $(2,4)$ showing that they all contribute a deficit. We may thus assume that the maximal potential gain of an edge we have not yet considered is 1.

: Since $f$ is $H$--even of degree six, it contributes $2$ to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}). Hence, we have to find an additional contribution of $1$ to obtain a balance and $>1$ to obtain a deficit.

Since $\operatorname{LST}_1,$ $\operatorname{LST}_2$ and $\operatorname{LST}_3$ are internally unbalanced, $\Delta_1,$ $\Delta_3$ and $\Delta_5$ are all of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}.$ If not all of them are coloured the same, at least two of $\Delta_2,$ $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_6$ must be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}},$ contributing $2$ to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) and we are done (again, note that the two tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ do not have any other $H$--even edges, and their contribution to the star of $f$ is global).

Hence all tetrahedra $\Delta_i,$ $1\leq i \leq 6,$ are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}},$ all with the same colours. This implies that one can perform a compression of two of the surfaces $S_i,$ $i  \in \{1,2,3\},$ by cutting along the two tubes around the $H$--even edge $f,$ and pasting two discs, each intersecting $f$ once (see Figure [3](#fig:compression){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:compression"} for an illustration of this process). The compressed surfaces are still embedded.

Moreover, note that three of the quadrilaterals in the tubes linking edge $f$ are contained in layered solid tori $\operatorname{LST}_j$, $1 \leq j \leq 3$. Hence, each such quadrilateral has its vertices identified in diagonally opposed pairs. In particular, the compressed surfaces still have the same number of connected components, and their components still have non-positive Euler characteristic and Lemma [\[lem:chi bounds norm for canonical\]](#lem:chi bounds norm for canonical){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:chi bounds norm for canonical"} applies. Altogether, the possibility of such compressions yields a contribution of $8$ to the term $2D \geq 4 \sum_{i=1}^{3} d_i$ of the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}).

![Compression along an $H$--even edge surrounded by tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}.$ The picture only shows one of the two parallel surfaces. []{#fig:compression label="fig:compression"}](compression){#fig:compression width=".8\\textwidth"}

We distribute this contribution onto $f$ and the three remaining $H$--even edges of $\Delta_2,$ $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_6$ equally. Thus, all of the four edges receive a sufficient contribution to give a deficit in ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}), and are not visited again (except that they may receive additional deficit from other edges). In particular, any other compression discs we find do not intersect the tetrahedra in the star of $f.$

This completes the case of gaining edges of type $(3,6)$ showing that they all contribute a deficit.

: Since $f$ is $H$--even of degree five, it contributes $1$ to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}). Hence, we have to find an additional contribution of $1$ to achieve a balance and $>1$ to achieve a deficit.

Again, $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_3$ can be assumed to be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}.$ If $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_3$ are of distinct colours, $\Delta_2$ must be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}.$ If there is at least one other tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ incident with $f,$ then there is a contribution to the left hand side of [\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"} of $2$ and we have a deficit. Otherwise there are two distinct tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}$ also incident with $f.$ We obtain a contribution of $2$ to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) from these tetrahedra which needs to be shared with the two other $H$--even edges, $g$ and $h$ of the triangle shared by $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_5.$ We can distribute a contribution of $\frac{2}{3}$ from these tetrahedra to the (possibly not pairwise distinct edges) $f,$ $g$ and $h.$ This yields a total contribution of $\frac{5}{3}>1$ to $f$ and hence a deficit.

Hence, assume that $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_3$ are equally coloured and thus $\Delta_2$ must be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ with matching colours. If any of $\Delta_4$ or $\Delta_5$ is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}},$ the other one must be as well, we obtain a contribution of $2,$ and we have a deficit.

If one of $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_5$ is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ the other one must be as well. As before, this implies that one can perform a compression of two of the surfaces $S_i,$ $i  \in \{1,2,3\},$ by cutting along the two tubes around the $H$--even edge $f.$ Again, there is an additional contribution to the left hand side of $8,$ distributed equally over the four $H$--even edges of $\Delta_2,$ $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_5$ (see Figure [3](#fig:compression){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:compression"}). In particular, each of these edges has an associated deficit.

Hence, we can assume that both $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_5$ are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}},$ both with the same colouring. We obtain a contribution of $2$ to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) which needs to be shared with the two other $H$--even edges, $g$ and $h$ of the triangle shared by $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_5.$ However, for this contribution to be insufficient globally, both $g$ and $h$ must be surrounded by some internally unbalanced maximal layered solid tori, with the tetrahedra surrounding $g$ and $h$ and distinct from $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_5$ all being of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ and of matching colours. This implies that one can perform two compressions of two of the surfaces $S_i,$ $i  \in \{1,2,3\},$ by cutting along the three times two incomplete tubes around the edges $f,$ $g$ and $h.$ This process replaces the two parallel triangles inside $\Delta_4$ and $\Delta_5$ by pairs of parallel normal triangles of all three other types. Again, quadrilaterals in the tubes linking edges which are contained in a layered solid torus have their vertices identified in diagonally opposed pairs. Hence this operation does not produce any new connected components. See Figure [4](#fig:doubleCompression){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:doubleCompression"} for an illustration of this process in the case of one surface (rather than two parallel ones).

![Double compression along a triangle of $H$--even edges. Only one of the two parallel copies of surfaces is drawn. []{#fig:doubleCompression label="fig:doubleCompression"}](doubleCompression){#fig:doubleCompression width="\\textwidth"}

This double compression of two surfaces contributes a total of $16$ to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}). We distribute two to each of $f,$ $g$ and $h,$ as well as to the at most three other $H$--even edges in the neighbourhoods of $f,$ $g$ and $h.$ The extra contribution of at least $4$ is a surplus and not needed. We therefore again have a deficit.

This completes the case of gaining edges of type $(2,5)$ showing that they all contribute a deficit.

: In this case, $f$ does not contribute to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) and we have to find a total contribution of $1$ for a balance and $>1$ for a deficit.

Again, $\Delta_1$ can be assumed to be of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}.$ If any of $\Delta_2,$ $\Delta_3$ and $\Delta_4$ is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ then we again have two cases. Either at least two of these are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ and we obtain a contribution of at least $2$ to the left hand side of ([\[eq:main\]](#eq:main){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:main"}) and we are done, or one of them is of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ and two are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}.$ In this case we distribute the contribution of $2$ to the tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}$ evenly over their three $H$--even edges and obtain a total contribution $>1$ for $f.$ If all $\Delta_i,$ $1 \leq i \leq 4$ are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}},$ there exist compressions for two of the surfaces $S_i,$ $i \in \{1,2,3\},$ with a total contribution of $8,$ which can be distributed equally over the four $H$--even edges contained in $\Delta_2,$ $\Delta_3$ and $\Delta_4.$ This again gives a deficit for all $H$--even edges involved.

There are two cases remaining (up to symmetry). In the first case we have (without loss of generality) $\Delta_2$ of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ and $\Delta_3$ and $\Delta_4$ of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}.$ Here, we fall back onto the double compression case along a triangle of $H$--even edges, as done in the case $m=2,$ $d=5$ (see Figure [4](#fig:doubleCompression){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:doubleCompression"}). This again gives a deficit for all $H$--even edges involved.

In the second case we have $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_4$ of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}$ and $\Delta_3$ of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}.$ In this case, we obtain another minimal triangulation of $M$ by performing a 4-4-move on $f.$ This results in two tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}$ and two of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}.$ But this contradicts our hypothesis that $\mathcal{T}$ locally has the smallest number of tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}.$ In particular, this case does not happen.

This completes the case of gaining edges, showing that they all contribute a deficit.

To sum up, it is not possible to achieve a gain, and hence we have proved the first part of the theorem, establishing the basic lower bound on the number of tetrahedra.

To prove the second part, we make some additional observations regarding the balancing edges.

If the balancing edge $f$ received a contribution $>0$ as a member of the star of a gaining edge, then it has an associated deficit. Hence assume that $f$ has not received such a contribution. If $f$ is incident with a tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ then there is a deficit. If $f$ is incident with a tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}},$ then this tetrahedron has not been counted yet since otherwise $f$ would have received a contribution. We can therefore allocate $1/3$ to each edge incident with this tetrahedron and hence $f$ has a deficit.

*In particular, this shows that associated to the subcomplex that is the union of all internally unbalanced tori and all tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ or $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}$ we have an overall deficit.*

These observations regarding the balancing edges suffice to analyse the case of equality. Hence suppose $|\mathcal{T}| = \sum \limits_{0\neq c \in H} ||\ c\ ||$ and so $$n_{\mathfrak{tt}}+n_{\mathfrak{qtt}}+2 D+\sum_{d=5}^{\infty} (d-4) \mathfrak{e}_d = \mathfrak{e}_3.$$

The above analysis shows that this equality is only possible if there is no deficit since otherwise the right hand side is smaller than the left hand side. In particular, the subcomplex that is the union of all internally unbalanced tori and all tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qtt}}$ or $\Delta_{\mathfrak{tt}}$ must be empty. Whence $n_{\mathfrak{tt}}= n_{\mathfrak{qtt}}=0.$ From the possible face pairings between tetrahedra of the different types, this implies that either all tetrahedra in $\mathcal{T}$ are of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}$ (but then $H$ has rank 0); or all tetrahedra are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qq}}$ (but then $H$ has rank 1); or all are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qqq}}.$ Hence we have the last case and each canonical surface is a quadrilateral surface. Since all tetrahedra are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qqq}},$ there are no $H$--even edges. Whence $\mathfrak{e}_3=0,$ which implies $D=0$ and the three surfaces therefore are taut.

It remains to show that in *every* minimal triangulations of $M$ the canonical normal representatives of non-trivial classes in $H$ are taut and all tetrahedra are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qqq}}$. Recall that $\mathcal{T}$ was obtained from an arbitrary minimal triangulation $\mathcal{T}'$ by 4-4 moves.

We first note that every edge $e$ of $\mathcal{T}$ is contained in at most $d(e) -1$ distinct tetrahedra. Otherwise the unique canonical representative $S$ disjoint from $e$ can be altered by cutting out the tube around $e$ and pasting in two discs orthogonal to $e$. Since $S$ is taut, this operation cannot be a compression of $S$ and thus it must produce two connected components -- one of which must be a normal $2$-sphere. This contradicts the fact that $\mathcal{T}$ is $0$-efficient due to Theorem [\[lem:bdEfficient\]](#lem:bdEfficient){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:bdEfficient"}. But if no edge $e$ of $\mathcal{T}$ is contained in $d(e)$ distinct tetrahedra, it is in particular true that $\mathcal{T}$ does not admit 4-4-moves. Hence $\mathcal{T}'=\mathcal{T}.$ In particular, the minimality assumption on the number of tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\emptyset}$ is true for all minimal triangulations of $M$.

We now show that if all tetrahedra are of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qqq}}$, then the total number of tetrahedra in $\mathcal{T}$ is even. For this we use the fact that $M$ is orientable and we assume that all tetrahedra are coherently oriented. In a tetrahedron of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qqq}}$, every boundary triangle has one 1-even, one 2-even and one 3-even edge and the orientation on the triangle induced by the orientation of the tetrahedron determines a cyclic order of the labels 1, 2, and 3. This is the same cyclic order for all boundary triangles of the same oriented tetrahedron. Hence there are two types of oriented tetrahedra of type $\Delta_{\mathfrak{qqq}}$. Tetrahedra meeting in a triangle must induce opposite cyclic orders on this triangle, see Figure [5](#fig:even){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:even"}. Hence, they are of different orientation types. It follows that the number of tetrahedra is even. ◻
