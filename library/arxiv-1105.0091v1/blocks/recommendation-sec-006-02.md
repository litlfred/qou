In other words, $\mathbb{P}_{n,q,t}=\mathbb{P}_{n,\omega_{q,t}}$, and $$\mathbb{P}_{n,q,t}[\lambda]=(1-q)^{n}\,(\dim \lambda)\,\, s_{\lambda}\left(\frac{[t]-[t-1]}{1-[q]}\right).$$ The $(q,t)$-Plancherel measures have the following symmetry: $$\mathbb{P}_{n,q,t}[\lambda]=\mathbb{P}_{n,q^{-1},t}[\lambda'].$$ Hence, we shall always suppose that $q \in\, ]0,1[$, the other case following by a symmetry.
