[]{#sec: conclusion label="sec: conclusion"} We have now seen that Bring's curve has many interesting properties and appears in a number of quite different settings; with this background now established we can summarise the main new results of the paper. We have:

1.  Constructed an explicit birational map between the HC- and $\mathbb{P}^4$-model of the curve, Proposition [\[prop: HC valid model for Bring\]](#prop: HC valid model for Bring){reference-type="ref" reference="prop: HC valid model for Bring"}.

2.  Constructed an explicit transform ([\[transRRW\]](#transRRW){reference-type="ref" reference="transRRW"}) between the period matrix of Weber and that of R&R.

3.  Provided a complete realisation of the automorphism group of the curve in the HC-model (Propositions [\[autA5\]](#autA5){reference-type="ref" reference="autA5"} and [\[prop: U is an automorphism\]](#prop: U is an automorphism){reference-type="ref" reference="prop: U is an automorphism"}) and the canonical model ([\[can12\]](#can12){reference-type="ref" reference="can12"},[\[can145\]](#can145){reference-type="ref" reference="can145"}).

4.  Unified the functional, algebraic and geometric pictures of the distinguished orbits on the curve (those of size 24, 30, and 60) by explicitly identifying these points (Propositions [\[WPprop\]](#WPprop){reference-type="ref" reference="WPprop"},[\[FCprop\]](#FCprop){reference-type="ref" reference="FCprop"},[\[Vprop\]](#Vprop){reference-type="ref" reference="Vprop"}) and identifying the divisors of the holomoprhic differentials and meromorphic functions associated to the Weierstrass points (Proposition [\[prop: function and divisor associated to WP\]](#prop: function and divisor associated to WP){reference-type="ref" reference="prop: function and divisor associated to WP"}).

5.  Completed the classification of, and relations between, the quotients of Bring's curve (Figures [\[eq: structure of quotient curves\]](#eq: structure of quotient curves){reference-type="ref" reference="eq: structure of quotient curves"} and [\[eq: structure of quotient groups\]](#eq: structure of quotient groups){reference-type="ref" reference="eq: structure of quotient groups"}), finding a previously unidentified elliptic curve in the process ([\[ref: quotient by 3cycle\]](#ref: quotient by 3cycle){reference-type="ref" reference="ref: quotient by 3cycle"}). We identify one of the known quotient curves with the modular curve $X_0(50)$.

6.  Proven in a new way the $\mathbb{Q}$-isogeny class of the Jacobian (Proposition [\[prop: JB Q-isogeny class\]](#prop: JB Q-isogeny class){reference-type="ref" reference="prop: JB Q-isogeny class"}) and established a number of isogenies between (products of) the various Prym varieties associated with the quotients ([\[isogenies\]](#isogenies){reference-type="ref" reference="isogenies"}).

7.  Computed the orbit decomposition of both the odd and even theta characteristics on the curve ([\[eq:oddcharacteristics\]](#eq:oddcharacteristics){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:oddcharacteristics"},[\[eq:evencharacteristics\]](#eq:evencharacteristics){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:evencharacteristics"}) and determined the unique invariant theta characteristic (Proposition [\[invarianttheta\]](#invarianttheta){reference-type="ref" reference="invarianttheta"}) identifying this with the Szëgo divisor (Proposition [\[szegoinvt\]](#szegoinvt){reference-type="ref" reference="szegoinvt"}) and a divisor identified by Burns (Proposition [\[burnsLs\]](#burnsLs){reference-type="ref" reference="burnsLs"}).

At various points we have corrected the literature.

In addition to our written account there are the accompanying notebooks. Throughout we have provided examples of how modern computational tools can simplify calculation and provide insight to aid discovery. The notebooks utilise SageMath, Maple, and Macaulay2 (all interfaced through Sage) and the code may be easily modified for other curves.

Finally we would draw attention to two areas that (for us) merit attention. First are the isomorphisms in Figure [\[eq: structure of quotient curves\]](#eq: structure of quotient curves){reference-type="ref" reference="eq: structure of quotient curves"} we have been unable to explain within the quotient structure of the curve. Perhaps this is something that the modular theory of the Bring's curve can shed light on. The second area deals with the orbit decompositions of the theta characteristics. In the case of even characteristics our construction was purely numeric and not analytic: a solution to this likely involves extending the theory of the Scorza quartic. Further, for both even and odd characteristics, there is a ''threeness ''we observe that we have found no satisfying reason for. We look forward to the clarification of these.
