To do this, we use the fact that any rank 2 tensor can be decomposed into symmetric and antisymmetric parts; for example, $${\mathbb C}^2 \otimes {\mathbb C}^2 \cong{\rm Sym}^2 {\mathbb C}^2 \oplus \Lambda^2 {\mathbb C}^2$$ and this is actually how ${\mathbb C}^2 \otimes {\mathbb C}^2$ decomposes into irreps. ${\rm Sym}^2 {\mathbb C}^2$, as we noted in Section [\[sec:isospin\]](#sec:isospin){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:isospin"}, is the unique 3-dimensional irrep of ${\rm SU}(2)$; its othogonal complement $\Lambda^2 {\mathbb C}^2$ in ${\mathbb C}^2 \otimes {\mathbb C}^2$ is thus also a subrepresentation, but this space is 1-dimensional, and must therefore be the trivial irrep, $\Lambda^2 {\mathbb C}^2 \cong{\mathbb C}$.
