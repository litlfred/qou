[]{#subsec:pointed-gral label="subsec:pointed-gral"}

### 

Let $H$ be a pointed Hopf algebra and set $G = G(H)$. We assume that $H$ is generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements. For $H$ finite-dimensional, it was conjectured that this is always the case [@AS-adv Conjecture 1.4]. So far this is true in all known cases, see [@Ang3; @AGI] and references therein. As explained in Subsection [\[subsec:hopf\]](#subsec:hopf){reference-type="ref" reference="subsec:hopf"}, there exist $g_1, \dots, g_{\theta}\in G$ and $\rho_i \in \operatorname{Irr}C_G(g)$, $1 \le i \le \theta$, such that the infinitesimal braiding of $H$ is $$M({\mathcal O}_{g_1}, \rho_1) \oplus \dots \oplus M({\mathcal O}_{g_\theta}, \rho_\theta).$$

::: lema
[]{#lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode label="lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode"} Let $\omega \in G$. Then the following are equivalent:

1.  $\omega$ is a pivot.

2.  $\omega\in Z(G)$ and $\rho_i(\omega) = \rho_i(g_i)^{-1}$, $1 \le i \le \theta$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* There exist $x_1, \dots, x_{\theta} \in H$ such that $\Delta(x_i) = x_i {\otimes}1 + g_i{\otimes}x_i$, $1 \le i \le \theta$, and $H$ is generated by $x_1, \dots, x_{\theta}$ and $G$ as an algebra. Now ${\mathcal S}^2(x_i) = g_i^{-1}x_ig_i = \rho_i(g_i^{-1}) x_i$. ◻
:::

### 

Let $G$ be a finite group and $\delta_g$ be the characteristic function of the subset $\{g\}$ of $G$. If $M\in{}_{\Bbbk^G}{\mathcal M}$, then $M=\oplus_{g\in\operatorname{Supp}M}M[g]$ where $$M[g]:=\delta_g\cdot M\,\mbox{ and }\,\operatorname{Supp}M:=\{g\in G:M[g]\neq0\}.$$

::: lema
[]{#lema:copointed-omega-square-antipode label="lema:copointed-omega-square-antipode"} Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional copointed Hopf algebra over $\Bbbk^G$ and $\omega=\sum_{g\in G}\omega(g)\delta_g \in G(\Bbbk^G)$. The following are equivalent:

1.  $\omega$ is a pivot.

2.  $\mathcal{S}^2(x) = \omega(g)x$ for all $x\in H[g]$, $g\in G$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Consider $H$ as a $\Bbbk^G$-module via the adjoint action; then $\delta_tx=x\delta_{g^{-1}t}$ for $x\in H[g]$, $g, t\in G$ [@AV 3.1 (b)]. Hence $\omega x\omega^{-1}=\omega(g)x$. ◻
:::
