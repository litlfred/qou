[]{#sec:inv-pivot label="sec:inv-pivot"}

There are many examples of Hopf algebras with involutory pivot.

### 

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebra with $G(H)$ abelian. Then its infinitesimal braiding $V$ is a braided vector space of diagonal type with matrix $(q_{ij})_{1\le i,j \le \theta}$, $\theta = \dim V$. Assume that $q_{ii} = -1$, $1\le i \le \theta$. The list of all braided vector spaces with this property and such that the associated Nichols algebra is finite-dimensional can be easily extracted from the main result of [@He-adv]. We apply Lemma [\[lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode\]](#lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode){reference-type="ref" reference="lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode"} because $H$ is generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements [@Ang3]. Hence, if $V$ belongs to this list, then $H$ is a spherical Hopf algebra with involutory pivot, eventually adjoining a group-like if necessary. The argument also works when $G(H)$ is not abelian but the infinitesimal braiding is a direct sum of one-dimensional Yetter-Drinfeld modules (one often says that the infinitesimal braiding *comes from the abelian case*).

### 

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebra with $G(H)$ not abelian and such that the infinitesimal braiding does not come from the abelian case. In all the examples of such infinitesimal braidings that are known[^1], we may apply Lemma [\[lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode\]](#lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode){reference-type="ref" reference="lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode"} because $H$ is generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements. Also, in all examples except one in [@HLV], the scalar in Lemma [\[lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode\]](#lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode){reference-type="ref" reference="lema:pointed-omega-square-antipode"} (b) is -1. Thus $H$ is a spherical Hopf algebra with involutory pivot, eventually adjoining a group-like if necessary.

### 

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional copointed Hopf algebra over $\Bbbk^G$, with $G$ not abelian. Lemma [\[lema:copointed-omega-square-antipode\]](#lema:copointed-omega-square-antipode){reference-type="ref" reference="lema:copointed-omega-square-antipode"} makes it easy to check whether $H$ has an involutive pivot. For instance, the Hopf algebras $\mathcal{A}_{[\mathbf{a}]}$, for $\mathbf{a}\in\mathfrak{A}_3$, introduced in [@AV], have an involutory pivot. These Hopf algebras are liftings of $\mathcal{B}(V_3)\#\Bbbk^{{\mathbb S}_3}$, so they have dimension 72; but they are not quasi-triangular [@AV].

Also, $\operatorname{Irr}\operatorname{\underline{Rep}}\mathcal{A}_{[0]}$ is infinite. Namely, $\mathcal{A}_{[0]} \simeq
\mathcal{B}(V_3)\#\Bbbk^{{\mathbb S}_3}$ and for $\lambda\in\Bbbk$ we define the $\mathcal{A}_{[0]}$-module $M_\lambda=\langle m_{g}:e\neq g\in{\mathbb S}_3\rangle$ by $$\begin{aligned}
\delta_h\cdot m_g=\delta_{h}(g)m_g\quad\mbox{and}\quad x_{(ij)}\cdot m_g=\begin{cases}
                                                     0 &\mbox{if }\operatorname{sgn}g=-1,\\
                                                     \lambda_{(ij),g}\,m_{(ij)g}&\mbox{if }\operatorname{sgn}g=1,
                                                     \end{cases}
\end{aligned}$$ where $\lambda_{(ij),g}=1$ except to $\lambda_{(12),(123)}=\lambda$. Then $M\in\operatorname{Indec}_q\mathcal{A}_{[0]}$ with $\operatorname{qdim}M_{\lambda}=-1$ for all $\lambda\in\Bbbk$ and $M_\lambda\simeq M_\mu$ iff $\lambda=\mu$. Analogously, we can define $N_\lambda\in\operatorname{Indec}_q\mathcal{A}_{[0]}$ with basis $\langle n_{g}:(12)\neq g\in{\mathbb S}_3\rangle$, $\operatorname{qdim}N_{\lambda}=1$ and which are mutually not isomorphic.

::: Rem
The dual of $\mathcal{A}_{[0]}$ is $\mathcal{B}(V_3)\#\Bbbk{\mathbb S}_3$, which is not pivotal because ${\mathbb S}_3$ is centerless. Compare with the main result of [@p], where it is shown that the dual of a semisimple spherical Hopf algebra is again spherical. In fact, the dual of $\mathcal{A}_{[\mathbf{a}]}$ is not pivotal for any $\mathbf{a}\in\mathfrak{A}_3$. If $\mathbf{a}$ is generic, then $(\mathcal{A}_{[\mathbf{a}]})^*$ has no non-trivial group-likes [@AV2 Theorem 1]. If $\mathbf{a}$ is sub-generic, then the unique non-trivial group-like $\zeta_{(12)}$ of $(\mathcal{A}_{[\mathbf{a}]})^*$ is not a pivot, see [@AV2 Lemma 8] for notations. Namely, if $g\neq(12),e$, then $$\begin{aligned}
\zeta_{(12)}\rightharpoonup\delta_g\leftharpoonup\zeta_{(12)}=\sum_{t,s\in{\mathbb S}_3}\delta_{s}((12))\,\delta_{s^{-1}t}\,\delta_{t^{-1}g}((12))=\delta_{(12)g(12)}\neq\mathcal{S}^2(\delta_g).
\end{aligned}$$
:::

### 

Let $H$ be a spherical Hopf algebra with involutory pivot $\omega$. Then

-   The quantum dimensions are integers.

-   If $\chi$ is a representation of dimension one, then $\operatorname{qdim}\Bbbk_{\chi} = \chi(\omega)$.

-   If $H$ is not semisimple, then at least one module has negative quantum dimension.

-   Assume that there exists $L\in \operatorname{Irr}H$ such that $\operatorname{qdim}L' > 0$ for all $L'\in \operatorname{Irr}H$, $L'\neq L$. Then $\operatorname{qdim}L < 0$.

::: prop
[]{#prop:spherical-involutory label="prop:spherical-involutory"} Let ${\mathcal C}$ be a fusion subcategory of $\operatorname{\underline{Rep}}H$, where $H$ is a spherical Hopf algebra with involutory pivot. Then there exists a semisimple quasi-Hopf algebra $K$ such that ${\mathcal C}\simeq \operatorname{Rep}K$ as fusion categories.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The quantum dimensions are integers, because the pivot is involutory, and positive by [@ENO Corollary 2.10]; here we use that ${\mathcal C}$ is spherical, see Remark [\[obs:spherical-subcat\]](#obs:spherical-subcat){reference-type="ref" reference="obs:spherical-subcat"}. Then [@ENO Theorem 8.33] applies. Indeed, the Perron-Frobenius and quantum dimensions here coincide, see e. g. the proof of [@ENO Proposition 8.23]. ◻
:::

We are inclined to believe, because of some computations in examples, that the quasi-Hopf algebra $K$ in the statement is actually a Hopf algebra quotient of $H$.

[^1]: The list of all known examples is in `http://mate.dm.uba.ar/~matiasg/zoo.html`, see also [@GHV Table 1], except for one example discovered later [@HLV Proposition 36].
