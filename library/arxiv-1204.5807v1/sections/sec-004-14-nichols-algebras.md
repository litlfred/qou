[]{#subsec:nichols label="subsec:nichols"}

Let $H$ be a Hopf algebra. We shall say *braided* Hopf algebra for a Hopf algebra in the braided tensor category ${}^H_H\mathcal{YD}$. Given $V\in {}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}$, the *Nichols algebra* of $V$ is the braided graded Hopf algebra $\mathcal{B}(V)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{B}^n(V)$ satisfying the following conditions:

-   $\mathcal{B}^0(V)\simeq\Bbbk$, $\mathcal{B}^1(V)\simeq V$ as Yetter-Drinfeld modules over $H$;

-   $\mathcal{B}^1(V)={\mathcal P}\big(\mathcal{B}(V)\big)$, the set of primitives elements of $\mathcal{B}(V)$;

-   $\mathcal{B}(V)$ is generated as an algebra by $\mathcal{B}^1(V)$.

The Nichols algebra of $V$ exists and is unique up to isomorphism. We sketch a way to construct $\mathcal{B}(V)$ and prove its unicity, see [@AS-cambr]. Note that the tensor algebra $T(V)$ admits a unique structure of graded braided Hopf algebra in ${}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}$ such that $V \subseteq {\mathcal P}(V)$. Consider the class $\mathfrak S$ of all the homogeneous two-sided Hopf ideals $I \subseteq T(V)$ such that $I$ is generated by homogeneous elements of degree $\geq 2$ and is a Yetter-Drinfeld submodule of $T(V)$. Then $\mathcal{B}(V)$ is the quotient of $T(V)$ by the maximal element $I(V)$ of $\mathfrak S$. Thus, the canonical projection $\pi:T(V)\to \mathcal{B}(V)$ is a Hopf algebra surjection in ${}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}$.

Braided vector spaces of diagonal type with finite-dimensional Nichols algebra were classified in [@He-adv]; the explicit defining relations were given in [@Ang3], using the results of [@Ang2]. Presently we understand that the list of braidings of diagonal type with finite-dimensional Nichols algebra given in [@He-adv] is divided into three parts:

1.  Standard type [@Ang1], comprising Cartan type [@AS-adv];

2.  Super type [@AAY];

3.  Unidentified type [@Ang4].
