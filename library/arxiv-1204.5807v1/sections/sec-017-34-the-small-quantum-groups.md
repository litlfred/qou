We consider finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras attached to the matrix $(q_{ij})_{i,j\in {\mathbb I}}$, analogues of the small quantum groups or Frobenius-Lusztig kernels. We need the following additional data: A finite abelian group $\varGamma$, provided with elements $g_1, \dots, g_\theta \in \varGamma$ and characters $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_\theta \in \operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb Z}}(\varGamma, \Bbbk^{\times})$ such that $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:cartan-datum-finite}
\chi_j(g_i) &= q_{ij}, &  &i,j\in {\mathbb I}.
\end{aligned}$$ We define a structure of Yetter-Drinfeld module over $\Bbbk\varGamma$ on $W\oplus V$ by $$\begin{aligned}
v_i &\in V_{g_i}^{\chi_i}, &  w_i &\in W_{g_i}^{\chi_i^{-1}}, & i&\in {\mathbb I}.
\end{aligned}$$ Let ${\mathfrak {u}}$ be the Hopf algebra $T(W \oplus V) \# \Bbbk\varGamma/ {\mathfrak I}$, where ${\mathfrak I}$ is the ideal generated by ${\mathcal J}(V)$, ${\mathcal J}(W)$ and the relations $$\begin{aligned}
\label{reducedlinking-finite}
&v_iw_{j} - \chi_j^{-1}(g_i) w_{j}v_i - \delta_{ij}(g_i^2 - 1) & &i,j\in {\mathbb I}.
\end{aligned}$$ This is a pointed quasi-triangular Hopf algebra with $\dim {\mathfrak {u}}= \vert \varGamma\vert\dim \mathcal{B}(V)^2$. The freedom to choose the abelian group $\varGamma$ allows more flexibility, but otherwise this is very close to the small quantum groups (with more general Nichols algebras). By choosing $\varGamma$ appropriately, ${\mathfrak {u}}$ is a spherical Hopf algebra. Let ${\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}$ (resp. ${\mathfrak {u}}^-$) be the subalgebra of ${\mathfrak {u}}$ generated by $v_1,\dots,v_{\theta}$ and $\varGamma$ (resp. $w_1,\dots,w_{\theta}$). Consider the morphisms of algebras $\rho_V :\mathcal{B}(V) \to {\mathfrak {u}}$, $\rho_W : \mathcal{B}(W) \to {\mathfrak {u}}$ and $\rho_{\varGamma} : \Bbbk\varGamma\to {\mathfrak {u}}$, given by $\rho_V(v_i) = v_i$, $\rho_W(w_i)= w_i$, $\rho_{\varGamma}(g_i) = g_i$, $i\in {\mathbb I}$. Then

1.  $\rho_V$, $\rho_W$, $\rho_{\varGamma}$ give rise to isomorphisms $\mathcal{B}(W) \simeq {\mathfrak {u}}^-$, $\mathcal{B}(V) \# \Bbbk\varGamma\simeq {\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}$.

2.  The map $\mathcal{B}(V) \otimes \mathcal{B}(W) \otimes \Bbbk\varGamma\to {\mathfrak {u}}$, $v \otimes w \otimes g \mapsto \rho_V(v) \rho_W(w) \rho_{\varGamma}(g)$ is a coalgebra isomorphism.

3.  The multiplication maps ${\mathfrak {u}}^- \otimes {\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}\to {\mathfrak {u}}$ , ${\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}\otimes {\mathfrak {u}}^-  \to {\mathfrak {u}}$ are linear isomorphisms.

See [@Ma Theorem 5.2], [@ARS Corollary 3.8]. Now suppose that one would like to define tilting modules over ${\mathfrak {u}}$, ignoring that this is not a quasi-hereditary algebra. Inducing from ${\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}$, we see that simple modules correspond to characters of $\Gamma$; but the set of simple modules could not be totally ordered and [\[eq:pro-unique\]](#eq:pro-unique){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:pro-unique"} and [\[eq:ind-head-simple\]](#eq:ind-head-simple){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:ind-head-simple"} do not necessarily hold. A first approach to remedy this that might come to the mind is to assume the following extra hypothesis: *There exists a ${\mathbb Z}$-bilinear form $\langle\, , \, \rangle: \varGamma\times \varLambda\to \Bbbk^{\times}$ such that* $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:cartan-datum-weights}
\langle g_i, \alpha_j\rangle &= q_{ij}, &  &i,j\in {\mathbb I}.
\end{aligned}$$ Then we may consider a category that is an analogue of category of representations of the algebraic group $G_1T$ in positive characteristic, or else of its quantum analogue in the literature of quantum groups. Let ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}}$ be the category of finite-dimensional ${\mathfrak {u}}$-modules $M$ with a $\varLambda$-grading $M = \oplus_{\lambda \in \varLambda} M_{\lambda}$, compatible with the action of ${\mathfrak {u}}$ in the sense $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:weight-space}
M_{\lambda} &= \{m\in M: g\cdot m = \langle g, \lambda\rangle m, \quad g\in \varGamma\}, & \lambda \in \varLambda, \\ \label{eq:weight-space-vi}
v_i\cdot M_{\lambda} &= M_{\lambda + \alpha_i},\qquad w_i\cdot M_{\lambda} = M_{\lambda - \alpha_i},  & \lambda \in \varLambda,\quad i\in {\mathbb I}.
\end{aligned}$$ Morphisms in ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}}$ preserve both the action of ${\mathfrak {u}}$ and the grading by $\varLambda$. The category ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}$ is defined analogously. Both categories ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}}$ and ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}$ are spherical tensor categories (up to an appropriate choice of $\varGamma$), with duals defined in the obvious way. There are functors $\operatorname{Res}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}}$, $\operatorname{Ind}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}}$ and $\operatorname{Pro}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}}$ between the categories ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}}$ and ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}$; indeed $$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Ind}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}} T &= {\mathfrak {u}}{\otimes}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}} T \simeq \mathcal{B}(W) {\otimes}T, & \operatorname{Pro}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}} T &= \operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}} ({\mathfrak {u}}, T) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{B}(W), T),
\end{aligned}$$ so that the grading in $\operatorname{Ind}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}} T$, resp. $\operatorname{Pro}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}} T$, arises from that of $\mathcal{B}(W) {\otimes}T$, resp. $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{B}(W), T)$.

Given $\lambda \in \varLambda$, we denote by $\Bbbk_{\lambda}$ the vector space with generator ${\bf 1}_{\lambda}$, considered as object in ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}$ by $$\begin{aligned}
\deg {\bf 1}_{\lambda} &= \lambda, & g \cdot {\bf 1}_{\lambda} &= \langle g, \lambda\rangle {\bf 1}_{\lambda}, & g&\in \varGamma, & v \cdot {\bf 1}_{\lambda} &= 0,& v&\in V.
\end{aligned}$$ Note that $\Bbbk_{\lambda} \simeq \Bbbk_{\mu}$ in ${{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}} {\text -}\operatorname{mod}}$ whenever $\lambda - \mu \in \varGamma^{\perp}$, but they are not isomorphic as objects in ${\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}$ unless $\lambda = \mu$. Clearly, $\operatorname{Irr}{\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}} = \{\Bbbk_{\lambda}: \lambda \in \varLambda\}$.

Consider the modules $\operatorname{Pro}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}}(\Bbbk_{\lambda})$ and $\Delta(\lambda) := \operatorname{Ind}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}}(\Bbbk_{\lambda})$. We know $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:pesos-delta}
\varPi(\Delta(\lambda)) &= \{\lambda - \alpha: \alpha \in \varPi(\mathcal{B}(V))\},
\\ \label{eq:pesos-nabla}
\varPi(\operatorname{Pro}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}}(\Bbbk_{\lambda})) &= \{\lambda + \alpha: \alpha \in \varPi(\mathcal{B}(V))\}.
\end{aligned}$$ Thus, $\Delta(\lambda)$ has a highest weight $\lambda$ and a lowest weight $\lambda - \varrho$, both of multiplicity 1; and $\operatorname{Pro}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}}(\Bbbk_{\lambda})$ has a highest weight $\lambda + \varrho$ and a lowest weight $\lambda$, both of multiplicity 1. For convenience, set $\nabla(\lambda) = \operatorname{Pro}_{{\mathfrak {u}}^{\mathfrak {b}}}^{{\mathfrak {u}}}(\Bbbk_{\lambda - \varrho}) \simeq \Delta(-\lambda + \varrho)^*$, since $\Bbbk_{\lambda}^* \simeq \Bbbk_{-\lambda}$.

The statements [\[subsub:ind-univ\]](#subsub:ind-univ){reference-type="eqref" reference="subsub:ind-univ"}, [\[subsub:simple-ind-quot\]](#subsub:simple-ind-quot){reference-type="eqref" reference="subsub:simple-ind-quot"}, [\[subsub:pro-univ\]](#subsub:pro-univ){reference-type="eqref" reference="subsub:pro-univ"} and [\[subsub:simple-pro-subobj\]](#subsub:simple-pro-subobj){reference-type="eqref" reference="subsub:simple-pro-subobj"} in [\[subsub:pro-ind-generalities\]](#subsub:pro-ind-generalities){reference-type="ref" reference="subsub:pro-ind-generalities"} above carry over to the present setting. We claim that [\[eq:ind-unique\]](#eq:ind-unique){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:ind-unique"}, [\[eq:pro-unique\]](#eq:pro-unique){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:pro-unique"}, [\[eq:ind-head-simple\]](#eq:ind-head-simple){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:ind-head-simple"} and [\[eq:pro-socle-simple\]](#eq:pro-socle-simple){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:pro-socle-simple"} also hold here.

Indeed, let $S\in \operatorname{Irr}{\mathcal C}_{\mathfrak {u}}$ and $\lambda, \mu \in \varPi(S)$ such that $\mu \preceq \tau \preceq \lambda$ for all $\tau\in \varPi(S)$. If $m \in S_{\lambda} - 0$, then $v_i\cdot m =0$ for all $i\in {\mathbb I}$ by [\[eq:weight-space-vi\]](#eq:weight-space-vi){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:weight-space-vi"}, hence $\Bbbk m \simeq \Bbbk_{\lambda}$ and we have $\Delta(\lambda) \twoheadrightarrow S$ and $\varPi(S) \subseteq \{\lambda - \alpha: \alpha \in \varPi(\mathcal{B}(V))\}$ by [\[eq:pesos-delta\]](#eq:pesos-delta){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:pesos-delta"}. Moreover, if $\Delta(\lambda') \twoheadrightarrow S$, then $\lambda \in \varPi(S) \subseteq \{\lambda' - \alpha: \alpha \in \varPi(\mathcal{B}(V))\}$, hence $\lambda' = \lambda$, showing [\[eq:ind-unique\]](#eq:ind-unique){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:ind-unique"}. The proof of [\[eq:ind-head-simple\]](#eq:ind-head-simple){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:ind-head-simple"} is standard: $\Delta(\lambda)$ has a unique maximal submodule, which is the sum of all submodules intersecting trivially $\Delta(\lambda)_{\lambda}$. Now [\[eq:pro-unique\]](#eq:pro-unique){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:pro-unique"} and [\[eq:pro-socle-simple\]](#eq:pro-socle-simple){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:pro-socle-simple"} follow by duality, so that $S \hookrightarrow \nabla(\mu)$.

In conclusion we have the following standard result.

::: prop
[]{#prop:simple-modules label="prop:simple-modules"} If $E(\lambda) :=$ head of $\Delta (\lambda)$, then $\operatorname{Irr}{\mathcal C}_{{\mathfrak {u}}} = \{E(\lambda): \lambda \in \varLambda\}$. If $\mu\in \varGamma^{\perp}$, then $\dim E(\mu)=1$ and $E(\mu) {\otimes}E(\lambda) \simeq E(\lambda) {\otimes}E(\mu)\simeq E(\lambda + \mu)$. There is a bijection $w_0: \varLambda\to \varLambda$ such that $E(\lambda) :=$ socle of $\nabla (w_0(\lambda))$. 0◻
:::

The modules $\Delta(\lambda)$, resp. $\nabla(\lambda)$, are called the Weyl modules, resp. the dual Weyl modules. We may then go on and define good and Weyl filtrations, and tilting modules. However, it is likely that tilting modules are projective, thus with 0 quantum dimension, as is the case for $G_1T$, see [@A-quot 3.4], [@Jantzen].
