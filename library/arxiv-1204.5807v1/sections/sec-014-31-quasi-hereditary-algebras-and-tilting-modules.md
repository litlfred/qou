[]{#sec-app:tilting label="sec-app:tilting"}

Tilting modules work for our purpose because they span a completely reducible category already in $\operatorname{Rep}H$. We think it is worthwhile to recall the main definitions of the theory of (partial) tilting modules over quasi-hereditary algebras, due to Ringel [@Ri]. A full exposition is available in [@Do2].

Let $A$ be an artin algebra. Consider a family $\Theta = (\Theta(1), \dots, \Theta(n))$ of $A$-modules such that $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:ext-0}
\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(\Theta(j), \Theta(i)) &= 0, & j&\geq i.
\end{aligned}$$ We denote by ${\mathcal F}(\Theta)$ the full subcategory of ${{A}{\text -}\operatorname{Mod}}$ with objects $M$ that admit a filtration with sub-factors in $\Theta$. We fix a numbering (that is, a total order) of $\operatorname{Irr}A$: $L(1), \dots, L(n)$. We set $$\begin{aligned}
P(i) &= \text{ projective cover of } L(i),
\\ Q(i) &= \text{ injective hull of } L(i),
\\ \Delta(i) &= P(i)/U(i), \quad \text{ where } U(i) = \sum_{j>i}\quad \sum_{\alpha\in \operatorname{Hom}(P(j), P(i))} \operatorname{Im}\alpha,
\\ \nabla(i) &=  \bigcap_{j>i}\quad \bigcap_{\beta\in \operatorname{Hom}(Q(i), Q(j))} \ker \beta,
\end{aligned}$$ $1\le i \le n$. Let $\Delta = (\Delta(1), \dots, \Delta(n))$, $\nabla = (\nabla(1), \dots, \nabla(n))$; these satisfy [\[eq:ext-0\]](#eq:ext-0){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:ext-0"} and then [@Ri Theorem 1] applies to them.

::: Def
[]{#def:quasi-hereditary label="def:quasi-hereditary"} The artin algebra $A$ is *quasi-hereditary* provided that $_{A}A \in {\mathcal F}(\Delta)$, and $L(i)$ has multiplicity one in $\Delta(i)$, $1 \le i \le n$.
:::

::: Rem
[]{#obs:quasi-hereditary-alternative label="obs:quasi-hereditary-alternative"} Quasi-hereditary algebras were introduced by Cline, Parshall and Scott, see e. g. [@CPS]. There are some alternative definitions.

1.  []{#item:quasi-hereditary-alternative inductive label="item:quasi-hereditary-alternative inductive"} An ideal $J$ of an artin algebra $A$ is *hereditary* provided that

    -   $J\in {{A}{\text -}\operatorname{Mod}}$ is projective,

    -   $\operatorname{Hom}_A(J, A/J) = 0$ (Ringel assumes $J^2 = J$ instead of this),

    -   $JNJ =0$, where $N$ is the radical of $A$.

    It can be shown that $A$ is quasi-hereditary iff there exists a chain of ideals $A = J_0 > J_1 > \dots > J_m =0$ with $J_i/J_{i+1}$ hereditary in $A/J_{i+1}$.

2.  Also, $A$ is quasi-hereditary iff ${{A}{\text -}\operatorname{Mod}}$ is a highest weight category, that is the following holds for all $i$:

    -   $Q(i)/ \nabla(i)\in {\mathcal F}(\nabla)$.

    -   If $(Q(i)/ \nabla(i): \nabla(j)) \neq 0$, then $j>i$.
:::

For completeness, we include the definitions of tilting, cotilting and basic modules, see e.g. [@Ri] and its bibliography. First, a module $T$ is tilting provided that

-   it has finite projective dimension;

-   $\operatorname{Ext}^i(T, T) =0$ for all $i\ge 1$;

-   for any projective module $P$, there should exist an exact sequence $0 \to P \to T_0 \to \dots \to T_m \to 0$, with all $T_j$ in the additive subcategory generated by $T$, denoted $\operatorname{add}T$.

Second, a cotilting module should have

-   finite injective dimension;

-   $\operatorname{Ext}^i(T, T) =0$ for all $i\ge 1$;

-   for any injective module $I$, there should exist an exact sequence $0 \to T_m \to \dots \to T_0 \to I \to 0$, with all $T_j$ in $\operatorname{add}T$.

Lastly, a basic module is one with no direct summands of the form $N\oplus N$, with $N\neq 0$.

Assume that $A$ is a quasi-hereditary algebra and consider the full subcategory $\mathcal{T}= \mathcal{T}_A = {\mathcal F}(\Delta) \cap {\mathcal F}(\nabla)$ of (partial) tilting modules. It was shown in [@Ri Theorem 5] that-- for a quasi-hereditary algebra-- there is a unique basic module $T$, that is tilting and cotilting (in the sense just above), and such that $\mathcal{T}$ coincides with $\operatorname{add}T$. The relation between this $T$ and the partial tilting modules is clarified by the following result.

::: Thm
[]{#thm:tilting-ringel label="thm:tilting-ringel"} [@Ri Corollary 2, Proposition 5] There exist indecomposable (partial) tilting modules $T(i)\in \mathcal{T}$, $1 \le i \le n$, with the following properties:

-   Any indecomposable tilting module is isomorphic to one of them.

-   $T = T(1) \oplus \cdots \oplus T(n)$ is the tilting module mentioned above.

-   There are exact sequences $$\begin{aligned}
    &0 \longrightarrow \Delta(i) \overset{\beta(i)}\longrightarrow  T(i)\longrightarrow X(i)\longrightarrow 0,
    \\
    &0 \longrightarrow Y(i) \longrightarrow T(i)\overset{\gamma(i)}\longrightarrow  \nabla(i)\longrightarrow 0,
    \end{aligned}$$ where $X(i) \in {\mathcal F}(\{\Delta(j): j < i\})$, $Y(i) \in {\mathcal F}(\{\nabla(j): j < i\})$, $\beta(i)$ is a left ${\mathcal F}(\nabla)$-approximation and $\gamma(i)$ is a right ${\mathcal F}(\Delta)$-approximation, $1\le i \le n$ (see [@Ri] for undefined notions). 0◻
:::
