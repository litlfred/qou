Making the trivial non-trivial. Deﬁnition 19.2.12. An action of a compact group on a factor M is called minimal if (M G)′ ∩M = Cid. If G has a minimal action α on M and ρ is an irreducible unitary repre- sentation of G on Ck we may take the action α ⊗Adρ on M ⊗Mk(C). One then deﬁnes the "Wassermann subfactor" (M ⊗1)G ⊆(M ⊗Mk(C))G. The point is that the commutant of (M ⊗1)G in M ⊗Mk(C) is already just Mk(C) by minimality of the action. So the ﬁxed points are indeed factors and the Wassermann subfactor is irreducible. Already for ﬁnite groups this provides lots of examples.
