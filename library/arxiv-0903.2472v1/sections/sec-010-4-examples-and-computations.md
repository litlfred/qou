In the previous sections, we described several ways to approach perturbative Chern-Simons theory with complex gauge group. Our discussion was guided by the aim of computing the all-loop perturbative partition function $Z^{(\rho)} (M;\hbar)$ in the background of a nontrivial flat connection ${\cal A}^{(\rho)}$ labeled by a homomorphism $\rho: \pi_1 (M) \to G_{{\mathbb{C}}}$.

In this section, our goal is to illustrate these methods in concrete examples by calculating perturbative invariants $S_n^{(\rho)}$ (also denoted $S_n^{(\alpha)}(u)$) to high loop order. Which 3-manifolds shall we choose for our examples? On one hand, the approach based on quantization and analytic continuation most directly applies to 3-manifolds with boundary, such as knot complements. (The invariants of closed 3-manifolds without boundary can be obtained by gluing two manifolds, $M_+$ and $M_-$, along a common boundary $\Sigma$, as in [\[mmgluing\]](#mmgluing){reference-type="eqref" reference="mmgluing"}.) On the other hand, the state integral model described in Section [\[sec:statesum\]](#sec:statesum){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:statesum"} does not require $M$ to have a boundary, but in the present form applies only to hyperbolic 3-manifolds. (We believe this state integral model can be extended to arbitrary 3-manifolds, but we do not pursue it here.) Therefore, if we wish to compare the results of different methods, it is convenient to choose examples of 3-manifolds that are knot complements *and* hyperbolic at the same time. In other words, we take $M$ to be a complement of a hyperbolic knot $K$ in the 3-sphere: $$M = {\bf S}^3 \smallsetminus K\,.$$ Among hyperbolic knots with a small number of crossings, the simplest ones are the figure-eight knot (also denoted ${\bf 4_1}$) and the three-twist knot (also denoted ${\bf 5_2}$) shown in Figure [\[fig:knots\]](#fig:knots){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:knots"}. Among all hyperbolic knots, the complement of the figure-eight knot has the least possible volume ${\mbox{Vol\,}}({\bf 4_1}) = 2.0298832128...$, whereas the volume of the ${\bf 5_2}$ knot complement is ${\mbox{Vol\,}}({\bf 5_2}) = 2.8281220883...$. In both cases, the knot group $\pi_1 (M)$ is generated by two elements $a$ and $b$, such that $a^{-1} b a b^{-1} a = ba^{-1} ba b^{-1}$ for the figure-eight knot and $waw^{-1}=b$, with $w = a^{-1} b a^{-1} b^{-1} a b^{-1}$, for the ${\bf 5_2}$ knot. For the figure-eight knot, the trace field $\mathbb{Q}({\rm tr\,}\Gamma)$ is the imaginary quadratic field $\mathbb{Q} (\sqrt{-3})$, while for the ${\bf 5_2}$ knot it is a cubic field with one complex place and discriminant $-23$.

$a)$ figure-8 knot ${\bf 4_1}$, and $b)$ three-twist knot ${\bf 5_2}$. []{#fig:knots label="fig:knots"}

This section consists of two parts. In the first part (Section [\[sec:arith_example\]](#sec:arith_example){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:arith_example"}), we fix the homomorphism $\rho = {\rm geom}$ and compute the perturbative $SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})$ invariants $S_n^{({\rm geom})}$ for the simplest hyperbolic knot complements, $M = {\bf S}^3 \smallsetminus{\bf 4_1}$ and $M = {\bf S}^3 \smallsetminus{\bf 5_2}$. In particular, we shall verify the arithmeticity conjecture formulated in Section [\[sec:loops\]](#sec:loops){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:loops"}. In the second part (Section [\[sec:figeight\]](#sec:figeight){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:figeight"}), we look more closely at the perturbative invariants of the figure-eight knot complement and study how the $S_n^{(\rho)}$'s depend on the homomorphism $\rho$ (equivalently, we study each $S_n^{(\alpha)}(u)$ as a function of $u$ and $\alpha$).
