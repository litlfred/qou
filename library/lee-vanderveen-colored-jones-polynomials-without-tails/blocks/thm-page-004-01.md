If u ≤−3 then C(5, −3, 5, u) is a Manx knot. More precisely, the leading coeﬃcient of JC(5,−3,5,u),n+1(v) is ⌊n/2⌋. The same techniques will also show that the pretzel knot P(5, −3, 5) is Manx but this appears to be the only Manx pretzel knot [9]. We expect that many more Manx knots exist but not in the family C(r, s, t, u) we considered. All Montesinos Manx knots seem to derive from the unique Manx pretzel P(5, −3, 5). Although our Manx knots do not stabilize in the ordinary sense they may still be c-stable.
