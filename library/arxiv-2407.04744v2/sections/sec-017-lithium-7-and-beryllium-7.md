The ${}^7\text{Li}$ and ${}^7\text{Be}$ magnetic f.f.'s receive contributions from $M_1$ and $M_3$ multipoles, as can be seen in Figs. [\[fig:MFF_multipole_IIb\]](#fig:MFF_multipole_IIb){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:MFF_multipole_IIb"}a and [\[fig:MFF_multipole_IIb\]](#fig:MFF_multipole_IIb){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:MFF_multipole_IIb"}b. The $M_3$ contribution becomes dominant in the region $1.0\leq q \leq 2.5$ fm$^{-1}$ where it fills in the diffraction minimum appearing in $M_1$. This is consistent with the findings of Ref. [@Donnelly1984] and references there in, where ${}^7\text{Li}$ is analyzed using phenomenological models. The $M_1$ and $M_3$ contributions are different in the two mirror nuclei. In ${}^7\text{Be}$, $M_3$ has more strength than $M_1$, in contrast to what is observed in ${}^7\text{Li}$.

The available data for ${}^7\text{Li}$ are obtained from Refs. [@Peterson1962; @Goldemberg1963; @Vanniftrik1971; @Lichtenstadt1983]. As can be seen in Fig. [\[fig:MFF_IIb_co\]](#fig:MFF_IIb_co){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:MFF_IIb_co"}d, we are able to explain the shape and magnitude of the experimental magnetic f.f.'s. The cumulative contribution from LO and NLO currents is sufficient to reproduce the peak at $q\simeq0.5$ fm$^{-1}$. Conversely, the region where $q\geq 1$ fm$^{-1}$ is significantly underestimated, unless N3LO currents are incorporated. The N3LO contributions are required to describe the tail of the magnetic f.f.; however, they generate too much strength for the $M_1$ peak, despite accurately predicting the magnetic moment. In the region with $q\geq 1$ fm$^{-1}$, the magnetic f.f. is reasonably reproduced, taking into account the theoretical errors. We note that, with none of the NV2+3 models considered are we able to reproduce the experimental strength of the first peak (see Fig. [\[fig:MFF_IIb_co\]](#fig:MFF_IIb_co){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:MFF_IIb_co"}d), which we overpredict by ${\sim} 25-40\%$. For all the interactions considered, two-body currents give a correction of ${\sim} 25\%$ in the region of the first peak, and up to over ${\sim} 50\%$ in the tail. This is in line with the findings of Ref. [@Donnelly1984], which are based on phenomenological models. In that study, contributions from two-body meson exchange are found to be of the order of $7-15\%$ over most of the $q$ region.

In ${}^7\text{Be}$, the contribution from NLO two-body currents enhances the LO magnetic f.f. by a factor of ${\sim} 2$, this enhancement is even more pronounced when adding the N3LO currents, almost uniformly across the $0.5\lesssim q\lesssim 2.5$ fm$^{-1}$ region. This would make the ${}^7\text{Be}$ magnetic f.f. a perfect candidate for validating models of two-body currents, if that experimental data were available.
