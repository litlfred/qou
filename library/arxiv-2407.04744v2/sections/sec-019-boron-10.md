The experimental data for the ${}^{10}\text{B}$ magnetic f.f. are provided for $0.5\lesssim q \lesssim 2.0$ fm$^{-1}$ region [@Goldemberg1963; @Goldemberg1965; @Rand1966; @Vanpraet1965; @Lapikas1978]. We did not include the data of Refs. [@Goldemberg1963; @Goldemberg1965] in the plots because of the strong disagreement with the more recent experimental results reported in Ref. [@Donnelly1984]. The VMC calculation has a very nice agreement with the data in the region of the main peak (see Fig. [\[fig:MFF_IIb_co\]](#fig:MFF_IIb_co){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:MFF_IIb_co"}l). Contributions coming from two-body currents are very small because they are purely IS. As shown in Fig. [\[fig:MFF_multipole_IIb\]](#fig:MFF_multipole_IIb){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:MFF_multipole_IIb"}i, the main peak is dominated by the $M_1$ multiple, while the secondary peak in the $q\sim2.5-3$ fm$^{-1}$ region is generated by the $M_5$ multipole that also contributes to the filling in of the $M_1$'s diffraction minimum. Note that this secondary peak was hypothesized in Ref. [@Donnelly1984], but it was not found in shell-model calculations performed in that reference. The $M_3$ peak that dominates all the other nuclei in the $q\sim 1.5$ fm$^{-1}$ region is barely visible, as in Ref. [@Donnelly1984].
