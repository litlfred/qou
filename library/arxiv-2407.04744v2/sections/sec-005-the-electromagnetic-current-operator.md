The electromagnetic current operator, ${\bf j}$, is expressed as an expansion in many-body terms as $$\begin{aligned}
{\bf j} &=&  \sum_i {\bf j}_i({\bf q}) + \sum_{i<j} {\bf j}_{ij}({\bf q}) +\dots  \ ,
\end{aligned}$$ where ${\bf q}$ is the momentum transferred to the nucleus. In this work we include up to two-nucleon contributions.

Electromagnetic currents have been extensively studied within several implementations of $\chi$EFT, including covariant perturbation theory [@Park:1995pn], the unitary transformation method [@Kolling:2009iq; @Kolling:2011mt; @Krebs:2016rqz; @Krebs:2019aka], and time-ordered perturbation theory [@Pastore:2008ui; @Pastore:2009is; @Pastore:2011ip; @Piarulli:2012bn; @Schiavilla:2018udt; @Gnech:2022vwr]. In this work, we adopt the operators developed using time-ordered perturbation theory [@Pastore:2008ui; @Pastore:2009is; @Pastore:2011ip; @Piarulli:2012bn; @Schiavilla:2018udt; @Gnech:2022vwr] whose expressions in momentum space are summarized in Ref. [@Gnech:2022vwr]. For ease of reading, we detail the corresponding coordinate space expressions in Appendix [\[app:currr\]](#app:currr){reference-type="ref" reference="app:currr"}.

Figs. [\[fig:currn2lo\]](#fig:currn2lo){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:currn2lo"} and [1](#fig:currn3lo){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:currn3lo"} display schematic representations of the electromagnetic current operator. One-body operators appear at LO and N2LO. These are generated by the disconnected diagrams displayed in panels (a) and (d) of Fig. [\[fig:currn2lo\]](#fig:currn2lo){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:currn2lo"}, respectively. The LO one-body operator consists of the convection and the spin-magnetization currents associated with an individual nucleon. This operator is derived from the non-relativistic reduction of the covariant single-nucleon current; that is to say, by expanding the operator in powers of $p_i/m_N$, where $p_i$ and $m_N$ are the momentum and mass of a nucleon, respectively. Its expression in coordinate space is given in Eq. ([\[eq:jlo\]](#eq:jlo){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:jlo"}) and is denoted with 'LO' in the tables. The one-body operator at N2LO accounts for relativistic corrections to the LO one-body operator, and its expression can be found in Eq. ([\[eq:j1rcc\]](#eq:j1rcc){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:j1rcc"}).

Two-body operators first appear at NLO in the expansion. At this order, they consist of a purely isovector (IV) contribution generated by the seagull and pion-flight diagrams illustrated in panels (b) and (c) of Fig. [\[fig:currn2lo\]](#fig:currn2lo){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:currn2lo"}. The NLO two-body current is denoted with 'NLO' in the tables, and its expression is provided in Eq ([\[eq:nlor2\]](#eq:nlor2){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:nlor2"}).

At N2LO, there is the two-body OPE current involving the excitation of an intermediate $\Delta$-isobar shown in panel (e) of Fig. [\[fig:currn2lo\]](#fig:currn2lo){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:currn2lo"}. In the tables, we report the cumulative contribution at N2LO, that is the sum of panels (d) and (e), and denote it with 'N2LO'. When discussing the two-body magnetic densities, we analyze the N2LO OPE current involving the intermediate $\Delta$-isobar separately, and denote its contribution with 'N2LO($\Delta$)'. This OPE term is purely IV and transverse, thus unconstrained by current conservation with the NV2 interaction.

The contributions at N3LO are shown in Fig. [1](#fig:currn3lo){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:currn3lo"}. Panel (a) displays the OPE current involving a sub-leading term in the $\gamma\pi N$ chiral Lagrangian that generates both an isoscalar (IS) and an IV current. These N3LO contributions of one-pion range are cumulatively denoted with 'N3LO(OPE)' in the tables. When discussing the two-body magnetic densities, we analyze separately the effect of its individual components. Specifically, the IS component---given in Eq. ([\[eq:n3lo.is\]](#eq:n3lo.is){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:n3lo.is"})---is proportional to the unknown LEC $d_9^\prime$, and is denoted with 'N3LO(OPE) IS' in the figures. The IV component involves two unknown LECs, namely, $d_8^\prime$ and $d_{21}^\prime$. The term associated with $d_8^\prime$ is given in Eq. ([\[eq:n3lo.iv.d8\]](#eq:n3lo.iv.d8){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:n3lo.iv.d8"}), and is labeled with 'N3LO(OPE) IV $d_8^\prime$'. While the term involving $d_{21}^\prime$ is given in Eq. ([\[eq:n3lo.iv.d21\]](#eq:n3lo.iv.d21){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:n3lo.iv.d21"}), and is labeled with 'N3LO(OPE) IV $d_{21}^\prime$'.

Currents of two-pion range (without $\Delta$-isobars) are shown in panels (g)--(k) of Fig. [1](#fig:currn3lo){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:currn3lo"} and are denoted with 'N3LO(LOOP)' in the tables and in the figures.

The last contribution at N3LO is represented by the contact term in panel (l) of Fig. [1](#fig:currn3lo){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:currn3lo"}. We distinguish between two kinds of contact contributions, namely the minimal (MIN) and non-minimal (NM) contact currents. The former is linked to the NV2 contact potential at NLO via current conservation; therefore it involves the same LECs entering the NV2. This contribution is denoted with 'N3LO(MIN)' in the tables. The N3LO(MIN) term is broken down into its IS and IV components---denoted as 'N3LO(MIN) IS' and 'N3LO(MIN) IV'---given in Eqs. ([\[eq:jmin.is\]](#eq:jmin.is){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:jmin.is"}) and ([\[eq:jmin.iv\]](#eq:jmin.iv){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:jmin.iv"}), respectively.

Non-minimal contact currents are unconstrained by current conservation and are denoted with 'N3LO(NM)' in the tables. The non-minimal contact currents are proportional to two unknown LECs, $C_{15}^\prime$ and $C_{16}^\prime$. The former enters the IS current given in Eq. ([\[eq:jnm.is\]](#eq:jnm.is){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:jnm.is"}) that generates a contribution labeled as 'N3LO(NM) IS'. The latter enters the IV current given in Eq. ([\[eq:jnm.iv\]](#eq:jnm.iv){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:jnm.iv"}) that gives rise to a contribution labeled as 'N3LO(NM) IV'.

The operators described above involve five unknown LECs, $d_8^\prime$, $d_9^\prime$, $d_{21}^\prime$, $C_{15}^\prime$ and $C_{16}^\prime$ for which we adopt the values obtained from the analysis of Ref. [@Gnech:2022vwr]. In particular, these LECs are constrained to reproduce the magnetic moments of the deuteron and the trinucleon systems, as well as backward-angle deuteron photo-disintegration data. This fitting strategy, denoted as "fit A\" in Ref. [@Gnech:2022vwr], provides excellent agreement with the data, even at large values of momentum transfer, when used to compute magnetic form factors in the hyperspherical harmonics approach. For convenience, we report the values of these LECs in Appendix [\[app:currr\]](#app:currr){reference-type="ref" reference="app:currr"}.
