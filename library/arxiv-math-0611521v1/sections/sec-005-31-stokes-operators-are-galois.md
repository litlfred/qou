We take on the notations of section [\[subsection:filtration\]](#subsection:filtration){reference-type="ref" reference="subsection:filtration"} and consider moreover another object $B$, to which we apply similar notations: graded $B_{0}$, diagonal blocks $B_{j}$ corresponding to slopes $\nu_{j}$ with multiplicities $s_{j}$, etc.

::: lemma
[]{#lemma:tensfonctrestreint label="lemma:tensfonctrestreint"} (i) Assume the following condition: $$\label{equation:nonresonancemixtetensorielle}
\forall i < i', j < j' \;,\; 
q^{{\mathbf Z}} c^{\mu_{i} + \nu_{j}} Sp(A_{i}) Sp(B_{j}) \cap 
q^{{\mathbf Z}} c^{\mu_{i'} + \nu_{j'}} Sp(A_{i'}) Sp(B_{j'}) = \emptyset.$$ Then: $$\label{equation:sommationtensorielle}
S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A \otimes B} = 
(S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}) \otimes (S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{B}).$$ (ii) Assume the following condition: $$\label{equation:nonresonancemixtefonctorielle}
\forall i, j \text{~such that~} \mu_{i} < \nu_{j} \;,\; 
q^{{\mathbf Z}} c^{\mu_{i}} Sp(A_{i}) \cap q^{{\mathbf Z}} c^{\nu_{j}} Sp(B_{j}) = \emptyset.$$ Then, for any morphism $F : A \rightarrow B$, writing $F_{0} = {\text{gr}}F$, we have: $$\label{equation:sommationfonctorielle}
F \; S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A} = S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{B} \; F_{0}.$$
:::

\(i\) From elementary properties of the tensor product, we draw that the diagonal blocks of $A \otimes B$ are the $z^{\mu_{i} + \nu_{j}} A_{i} \otimes B_{j}$ and that $Sp(A_{i} \otimes B_{j}) = Sp(A_{i}) Sp(B_{j})$; thus the right hand side of the equality is a morphism from ${\text{gr}}(A \otimes B) = A_{0} \otimes B_{0}$ to $A \otimes B$ satisfying condition ([\[equation:conditionpolaire\]](#equation:conditionpolaire){reference-type="ref" reference="equation:conditionpolaire"}) on poles: it has to be $S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A \otimes B}$.\
(ii) From the functoriality of the filtration, we know that $F$ only has rectangular blocks relating slopes $\mu_{i} \leq \nu_{j}$, and that $F_{0}$ is made up of those such that $\mu_{i} = \nu_{j}$. It is sensible to call the latter "diagonal blocks". Then, the compositum $(S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{B})^{-1} F \; S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}$ is a (meromorphic) morphism from $A_{0}$ to $B_{0}$, with diagonal $F_{0}$ (since $S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}$ and $S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{B}$ are in ${\mathfrak{G}}$) and with $0$ under the diagonal. Any block $F_{i,j}$ such that $\mu_{i} < \nu_{j}$ has all its poles on $[-c;q]$, and with multiplicities $\leq \nu_{j} - \mu_{i}$; thus, $F_{i,j} = \theta_{c}^{\mu_{i} - \nu_{j}} F'_{i,j}$, where $F'_{i,j}$ is holomorphic on ${\mathbf C}^{*}$ and satisfies: $\sigma_qF'_{i,j} c^{\mu_{j}} A_{j} = c^{\mu_{i}} A_{i} F'_{i,j}$ The same computation (with the Laurent series) as in section [\[subsection:Stokesoperators\]](#subsection:Stokesoperators){reference-type="ref" reference="subsection:Stokesoperators"} shows that, under condition ([\[equation:nonresonancemixtefonctorielle\]](#equation:nonresonancemixtefonctorielle){reference-type="ref" reference="equation:nonresonancemixtefonctorielle"}), this implies $F'_{i,j} = 0$. Therefore $(S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{B})^{-1} F \; S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A} = F_{0}$ and ([\[equation:sommationfonctorielle\]](#equation:sommationfonctorielle){reference-type="ref" reference="equation:sommationfonctorielle"}) holds. $\Box$\
In terms of the fiber functors introduced after theorem [\[theorem:filtration\]](#theorem:filtration){reference-type="ref" reference="theorem:filtration"}, the meaning of the above lemma is that, under proper restrictions to ensure that $S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}$ is well defined at $a$,and that the nonresonancy conditions ([\[equation:nonresonancemixtetensorielle\]](#equation:nonresonancemixtetensorielle){reference-type="ref" reference="equation:nonresonancemixtetensorielle"}) and ([\[equation:nonresonancemixtefonctorielle\]](#equation:nonresonancemixtefonctorielle){reference-type="ref" reference="equation:nonresonancemixtefonctorielle"}) hold for any pair of objects, $A \leadsto S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}(a)$ is an $\otimes$-isomorphism from $\hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}$ to $\omega^{(0)}_{a}$. For any pure $A_{0}$, taking up the previous notations, we therefore define, first its "weighted spectrum" and singular locus: $$\overline{WSp}(A_{0}) = \text{~the subgroup of ${\mathbf{E}_{q}}\times {\mathbf Z}$ generated by~}
\bigcup_{i} \bigl(\overline{Sp(A_{i})} \times \{\mu_{i}\}\bigr),
\quad
\tilde{\Sigma}(A_{0}) = \bigcup_{\mu \not= 0}
\{\overline{c} \in {\mathbf{E}_{q}}\;/\; 
(\mu \overline{c},\mu) \in \overline{WSp}(A_{0})\}.$$

::: prop
Let $<A>$ be the tannakian subcategory of ${\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{(0)}$ generated by $A$. Fix $\overline{c} \not\in \tilde{\Sigma}(A_{0})$ and $a \not\in [-c;q]$. Then $B \leadsto S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{B}(a)$ is an $\otimes$-isomorphism from $\hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}$ to $\omega^{(0)}_{a}$, both being restricted to $<A>$.
:::

Apply the lemma and the formulas giving the slopes of linear constructions in [@JSFIL]. $\Box$\
Now we recall, from [@JSSTO] that, for a pure object $A = A_{0}$, all the $S_{\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}$ are equal (they are indeed equal to the formal Stokes operator $\hat{F}_{A}$ which is actually analytic).

::: thm
With the same restrictions, fix an arbitrary $\overline{c_{0}} \not\in \tilde{\Sigma}(A_{0}) \cup \{\overline{-a}\}$. Then, for all $\overline{c} \not\in \tilde{\Sigma}(A_{0}) \cup \{\overline{-a}\}$, we have, using notation ([\[equation:defoperateurStokes\]](#equation:defoperateurStokes){reference-type="ref" reference="equation:defoperateurStokes"}): $$S_{\overline{c_{0}},\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}(a) \in \mathfrak{St}(A).$$
:::

By the above proposition, it is in the Galois group; by the remark above, it is killed by the functor ${\text{gr}}$. $\Box$\

::: cor
We get a family of elements of Lie-like automorphisms: ${LS_{\overline{c},a}}(A) \underset{def}{=}
\log (S_{\overline{c_{0}},\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}(a)) \in \mathfrak{st}(A)$.
:::

Now, although the functoriality and $\otimes$-compatibility were proved only for $\overline{c} \not\in \tilde{\Sigma}(A_{0}) \cup \{\overline{-a}\}$, the above formula is actually well defined for all $\overline{c} \not\in \Sigma_{A_{0}} \cup \{\overline{-a}\}$. Moreover, from the explicit computation in section [\[subsection:Stokesoperators\]](#subsection:Stokesoperators){reference-type="ref" reference="subsection:Stokesoperators"} (multiplications by powers of $\theta_{c}$ and resolution of recursive equations by inversion of $\Phi_{q^{p} A'_{j},A'_{i}}$), we see that the mapping $\overline{c} \mapsto {LS_{\overline{c},a}}(A)$ is meromorphic on ${\mathbf{E}_{q}}$, with poles on $\Sigma_{A_{0}}$. Moreover, it takes values in the vector space $\mathfrak{st}(A)$ for all $\overline{c}$ except for a denumerable subset: therefore, it takes all its values in $\mathfrak{st}(A)$. Last, taking residues at a pole is an integration process and gives values in the same vector space.

::: thm
Define the $q$-alien derivations by the formula: $${{\dot{\Delta}}_{\overline{c}}}(A) = Res_{\overline{d} = \overline{c}} LS_{\overline{d},a}(A).$$ Then, ${{\dot{\Delta}}_{\overline{c}}}(A) \in \mathfrak{st}(A)$. (In order to alleviate the notation, we do not mention the arbitrary basepoint $a \in {\mathbf C}^{*}$.)
:::

Of course, for $\overline{c} \not\in \Sigma_{A_{0}}$, we have ${{\dot{\Delta}}_{\overline{c}}}(A) = 0$. According to the graduation of $\mathfrak{st}$ described at the end of section [\[subsection:filtration\]](#subsection:filtration){reference-type="ref" reference="subsection:filtration"}, each alien derivation admits a canonical decomposition: $$\label{equation:decompositionalienderivation}
{\dot{\Delta}}= \bigoplus_{\delta \geq 1} {{\dot{\Delta}}^{(\delta)}_{\overline{c}}},$$ where ${{\dot{\Delta}}^{(\delta)}_{\overline{c}}}(A) \in \mathfrak{st}_{\delta}(A)$ has only non null blocks for $\mu_{j} - \mu_{i} = \delta$.

::: thm
The alien derivations are Lie-like $\otimes$-endomorphisms of $\hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}$ over ${\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{(0)}$.
:::

This means first that they are functorial; for all morphisms $F : A \rightarrow B$, one has: $${{\dot{\Delta}}_{\overline{c}}}(B) \circ \hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}(F) =
\hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}(F) \circ {{\dot{\Delta}}_{\overline{c}}}(A).$$ Note that $\hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}(F) = F_{0}(a)$. First assume the previous restrictions on $\overline{c}$. Then, from the lemma [\[lemma:tensfonctrestreint\]](#lemma:tensfonctrestreint){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:tensfonctrestreint"}, we get that $S_{\overline{c_{0}},\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{B}(a) \circ F_{0}(a) =
F_{0}(a) \circ S_{\overline{c_{0}},\overline{c}} \hat{F}_{A}(a)$. Now, the logarithm of a unipotent matrix $P$ being a polynomial of $P$, we have $R \circ Q = Q \circ P \Rightarrow \log R \circ Q = Q \circ \log P$, so that we have ${LS_{\overline{c},a}}(B) \circ F_{0}(a) = 
F_{0}(a) \circ {LS_{\overline{c},a}}(A)$ and we take the residues on both sides. Now that the equality is established outside a denumerable set of values of $\overline{c}$, we can extend it to all values by holomorphy.\
The assertion means, second, Lie-like $\otimes$-compatibility: $${{\dot{\Delta}}_{\overline{c}}}(A \otimes B) = 
1 \otimes {{\dot{\Delta}}_{\overline{c}}}(B) + {{\dot{\Delta}}_{\overline{c}}}(A) \otimes 1,$$ where the left and right $1$ respectively denote the identities of $\hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}(A)$ and $\hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}(B)$. This equality makes sense because $\hat{\omega}^{(0)}_{a}$ is itself $\otimes$-compatible. From the lemma [\[lemma:tensfonctrestreint\]](#lemma:tensfonctrestreint){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:tensfonctrestreint"} we get first that ${LS_{\overline{c},a}}(A \otimes B) = 
{LS_{\overline{c},a}}(A) \otimes {LS_{\overline{c},a}}(A)$. Then, we note that, for any two unipotent matrices $P$ and $Q$, the commuting product $P \otimes Q = (P \otimes 1) (1 \otimes Q) = (1 \otimes Q) (P \otimes 1)$ entails $\log (P \otimes Q) = (\log P) \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes (\log Q)$. The proof is then finished as above. $\Box$
