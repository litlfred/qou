Young diagrams
There is a wealth of detail about the features of Young tableaux in many texts
such as [25, 7, 10]. Here we emphasize certain properties which will be to the
fore in this article.
A partition of n can be represented by a Young diagram; a collection of n
cells arranged in rows, with λ1 cells in the ﬁrst row, λ2 cells in the second row up
to λk cells in the kth row where λ1 ≥λ2 ≥· · · ≥λk > 0 and Pk
i=1 λi = n. We
shall denote both the partition and its Young diagram by λ. The Young diagram
for (0) is the empty diagram. We denote the number of cells in λ by |λ|. The
conjugate λ∨of λ is the Young diagram whose rows form the columns of λ. Any
cell for which a legitimate Young diagram remains after it has been removed will
be called an extreme cell. To each extreme cell we associate an extreme rectangle,
namely those cells above and to the left of it in the Young diagram. When there
is a cell in the ith row and jth column of λ we write (i, j) ∈λ, and refer to (i, j)
as the coordinates of the cell.
We will work with the example ν = (4, 2, 1) throughout this paper.
The
conjugate of ν is ν∨= (3, 2, 1, 1) and |ν| = |ν∨| = 7. The Young diagrams for ν
and ν∨are shown in Fig. 5. There are three extreme cells in ν, marked by a cross
in Fig. 6 with their associated extreme rectangles shaded in. The coordinates of
the extreme cells are (1, 4), (2, 2) and (3, 1) respectively.
Let T ′(λ) be an assignment of the numbers 1 to n to the cells of λ such that
the numbers increase from left to right along the rows and top to bottom down
the columns. We call T ′(λ) a standard tableau. In particular T(λ) will denote the
tableau where the cells of the Young diagram are numbered from 1 to n along
the rows.

We now give some standard combinatorial results about permutations and
Young diagrams, in a context which extends readily to positive permutation
braids and the Hecke algebras.
Let λ and µ be Young diagrams with |λ| = |µ| = n. We say that π ∈Sn
separates λ from µ if no pair of numbers in the same row of T(λ) are mapped by
π to the same row of T(µ). The permutation πλ, for example, separates λ from
its conjugate λ∨.
Write R(λ) ⊂Sn for the subgroup of permutations which preserve the rows
of T(λ). Each R(λ) is generated by some subset of the elementary transpositions
(i i + 1). For example R(ν) is generated by {(12), (23), (34), (56)}.
It is easy to see that if π separates λ from µ then so does ρπσ for any ρ ∈
R(λ), σ ∈R(µ). Conversely, it can be shown that if π separates λ from λ∨then
π = ρπλσ with ρ ∈R(λ) and σ ∈R(λ∨). We say that λ is just separable from λ∨.
If no permutation π ∈Sn separates λ from µ then we call λ and µ inseparable.
Order the Young diagrams by lexicographical ordering of their rows. Thus
λ > µ when there exists t with λt > µt and λi = µi for i < t.

Proof.
By induction on t. Suppose ﬁrst that t = 1, and let π ∈Sn. Since
µ∨has µ1 < λ1 rows, π must map at least two numbers from the ﬁrst row of λ
to the same row of µ∨. Hence π does not separate λ from µ∨.
When t > 1, consider the Young diagrams r(λ) and r(µ), where r(α) is the
Young diagram obtained from α by removing the ﬁrst row. We have r(λ) > r(µ),
and the diagrams ﬁrst diﬀer in the (t−1)st row. Hence, by the induction hypoth-
esis, r(λ) and r(µ)∨are inseparable. It remains to prove that if λ were separable
from µ∨then r(λ) would be separable from r(µ)∨, giving us a contradiction.
Suppose that π separates λ from µ∨. Since λ1 = µ1, the number of cells in
the ﬁrst row of λ is equal to the number of rows of µ∨. Then π must send exactly
one cell from the ﬁrst row of λ to each row of µ∨. We can suppose, without
loss of generality, that it is the ﬁrst cell in each row of µ∨, for if not, there is a
transposition τ ∈R(µ∨) which will switch the ﬁrst cell of the row with the image
of the cell in the ﬁrst row of λ, and πτ will also separate λ from µ∨. Restrict π
to all but the ﬁrst row of λ. Its image will be exactly the cells of r(µ)∨, and the
restricted permutation separates r(λ) from r(µ)∨.
✷

Lemma 3.3 Let π be a permutation which separates λ from λ∨. Then the positive
permutation braid ωπ can be written as ωπ = ωρωπλωσ for some ρ ∈R(λ) and
σ ∈R(λ∨).
Proof.
We already know that we can write π = ρπλσ. It is enough to show
that ωρωπλωσ is a positive permutation braid, for it is then determined as ωπ by
its permutation π.
The key feature of ωπλ is that pairs of strings which start in the same row of
T(λ) or ﬁnish in the same row of T(λ∨) don’t cross. The only pairs which cross in
ωρ or in ωσ are in the same row of T(λ) or T(λ∨) respectively. Consequently each
pair of strings in the product braid ωρωπλωσ crosses at most once. All crossings
are positive, so this is a positive permutation braid.
✷
This is a special case of a property of positive permutation braids which will
be useful later.
Lemma 3.4 In each right coset R(λ)π′ of the subgroup R(λ) there is a unique π
such that ωπ = ωρωπ, where ρ ∈R(λ) and π = ρπ runs through the coset.
Proof.
Choose π so that no two strings which start in the same row of λ
cross. Then ωρωπ is a positive permutation braid.
✷
