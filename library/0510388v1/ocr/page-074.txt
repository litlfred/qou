34. Isometries of H’.

A linear transformation A: R'* > R'® is called an isometry if
< A(x), A(y)> = <x,y>

for all x and y € R'®.

An isometry of R'* takes the two-sheeted hyperboloid <x ,x > = 1 to itself,
but may interchange the two sheets. If it does not interchange these sheets, then in
particular it takes the "upper" sheet H’ to itself. In that case, it also preserves the
Riemannian metric on H’, so is an isometry of H? in the sense of Riemannian
geometry.

One example of an isometry of R'* which preserves H?® is given by the linear
transformation A with matrix

A family of examples of isometries of R'’ which preserve H’ consists of those
linear transformations A which fix identically the xo-axis, and act as Euclidean
rigid motions on the x;x2x3-space.

Compositions of these examples provide all possible isometries of R'* which
take H’® to itself, and in particular provide all possible isometries of H? in the

sense of Riemannian geometry.

Half of these are orientation preserving, and constitute the group SO(1,3).
