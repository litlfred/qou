Recall the canonical filtration on $\varphi(V_n)$ (see [\[canon\]](#canon){reference-type="ref" reference="canon"}). We will compare the identification (see Lemma [\[Mit\]](#Mit){reference-type="ref" reference="Mit"}) of the associated graded with $\bigoplus_{i=-n}^n(Id,\pi,\Id)_*{\mathcal O}(\Gamma_i)\otimes\ _iV_n$ with the identification (see Lemma [\[alg\]](#alg){reference-type="ref" reference="alg"}) $\varphi(V_n)\cong{\mathcal O}\left((\pi,\Id,\Id)^{-1}(A_n)\right)$. To this end we recall some basic facts about $U_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-modules. First, $V_n$ is a free $\mathsf k[\hbar]$-module with a basis $\{v_n,v_{n-2},\ldots,v_{2-n},v_{-n}\}$. The action of $e,h,f\in\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is given by $$hv_i=i\hbar v_i,\ ev_{i-2}=\frac{n+i}{2}\hbar v_i,\
fv_{i+2}=\frac{n-i}{2}\hbar v_i$$ Second, $\CM_\hbar(-1)$ is a free $\mathsf k[\hbar,x]$-module with a basis $\{m_{-1},m_{-3},m_{-5},\ldots\}$. The action of $e,h,f$ is given by $$hm_i=(x+i\hbar)m_i,\ 
em_i=\frac{-i-1}{2}\hbar(x-\frac{i+1}{2}\hbar)m_{i+2},\ 
fm_i=m_{i-2}$$

We have a canonical filtration $0=F^{n+2}\subset F^n\subset F^{n-2}\subset\ldots\subset
F^{2-n}\subset F^{-n}=
\CM_\hbar(-1)\otimes_{\mathsf k[\hbar]}V_n$ by $U_\hbar$-submodules such that $F^i/F^{i+2}=\CM_\hbar(i-1)\otimes\ _iV_n$ (notations of [\[canon\]](#canon){reference-type="ref" reference="canon"}). Recall that $_iV_n$ is spanned by $v_i$. There is a unique vector $s_i\in(\CM_\hbar(-1)\otimes V_n)\otimes_{\mathsf k[\hbar,x]}\mathsf k(\hbar,x)$ such that $es_i=0$, and $s_i\equiv m_{-1}\otimes v_i$ modulo $U_\hbar\langle s_{i+2},s_{i+4},\ldots,s_n\rangle$. Then $F^i=U_\hbar\langle s_i,s_{i+2},\ldots,s_n\rangle\cap
(\CM_\hbar(-1)\otimes V_n)$. The image $\bar{s}_i$ of this vector in the $\psi$-coinvariants $U_\hbar\langle s_i\rangle\otimes_{U_\hbar(\check{\mathfrak n}{}_-)}\psi=
\CM_\hbar(i-1)\otimes_{U_\hbar(\check{\mathfrak n}{}_-)}\psi$ is the generator of $(\Id,\pi,\Id)_*{\mathcal O}(\Gamma_i)\otimes\ _iV_n$. Note that the space of $\psi$-coinvariants is just the quotient modulo the image of $f-1$.

On the other hand, the proof of Lemma [\[alg\]](#alg){reference-type="ref" reference="alg"} shows that $\varphi(V_n)=
(\CM_\hbar(-1)\otimes V_n)\otimes_{U_\hbar(\check{\mathfrak n}{}_-)}\psi$ is generated by the image $\overline{m_{-1}\otimes v_n}$ of $m_{-1}\otimes v_{-n}$ in the space of $\psi$-coinvariants. Thus we have to express $\overline{m_{-1}\otimes v_{-n}}$ in terms of $\bar{s}_{-n},\ldots,\bar{s}_n$.

::: lem
[]{#idiot label="idiot"} $$\overline{m_{-1}\otimes v_{-n}}=\sum_{i=-n}^n
(x+(i-1)\hbar)^{-1}(x+(i-2)\hbar)^{-1}
\ldots\left(x+\frac{i-n}{2}\hbar\right)^{-1}
\bar{s}_i$$
:::

*Proof* : Recall that the space of $\psi$-coinvariants is just the quotient modulo the image of $f-1$. This means we have to prove the following equality in $(\CM_\hbar(-1)\otimes V_n)\otimes_{\mathsf k[\hbar,x]}\mathsf k(\hbar,x)$: $$m_{-1}\otimes v_{-n}=\sum_{i=-n}^n
(x+(i-1)\hbar)^{-1}(x+(i-2)\hbar)^{-1}
\ldots\left(x+\frac{i-n}{2}\hbar\right)^{-1}
f^{\frac{n+i}{2}}s_i$$ For $l=0,1,\ldots,n$ we introduce a new vector $s_i^l$ such that $es_i^l=0$, and $s_i^l\equiv e^l(m_{-1}\otimes v_{i-2l})$ modulo $U_\hbar\langle s_{i+2},s_{i+4},\ldots,s_{n}\rangle$ (evidently, $s_i^l$ is proportional to $s_i=s_i^0$ when $i-2l\geq-n$; otherwise, $s_i^l$ is not defined). Consider the following collection of equalities: $$e^l(m_{-1}\otimes v_{-n})=\sum_{i=-n+2l}^n\hbar^l
(x+(i-1)\hbar)^{-1}(x+(i-2)\hbar)^{-1}
\ldots\left(x+\frac{i-n+2l}{2}\hbar\right)^{-1}
f^{\frac{n-2l+i}{2}}s_i^l$$ Then the $n$-th equality is obvious, while the $l+1$-st equality is equivalent to the $l$-th one by applying $e$ to both sides. Thus the desired equality (for $l=0$) follows by descending induction in $l$. $\Box$

## Cohomology is fully faithful

The following property of the cohomology functor will play an important role in the proof of the main results. Let ${\mathcal{IC}}$ (resp. $\widetilde{\mathcal{IC}}$) denote the full subcategory of semisimple complexes in $D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)$ (resp. $D_{\bf G_O}(\Gr)$).

::: lem
[]{#fullyTop label="fullyTop"} a) The functor $H_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes{{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet:\
{\mathcal{IC}}\to Coh^{{\mathbb G}_m}(N_{(\check{\mathfrak t}{}^*/W)^2}\Delta)$ is a full imbedding.

b\) The functor $H_{\bf G_O}^\bullet:\ 
\widetilde{\mathcal{IC}}\to Coh^{{\mathbb G}_m}(\bT(\check{\mathfrak t}{}^*/W))$ is a full imbedding.
:::

*Proof* is due to V. Ginzburg, see [@G0]. We prove a), and the proof of b) is identical. For $V_1,V_2\in Rep({\check{G}})$ we have $\Ext_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes{{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet(S(V_1),S(V_2))=
\Ext_{G\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet(S(V_1),S(V_2))$. Let us denote by $Res_G^T$ the forgetting functor from the $G\times{{\mathbb G}_m}$-equivariant derived category to the $T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}$-equivariant derived category. Then the Weyl group $W$ acts naturally on $\Ext_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet(Res_G^TS(V_1),Res_G^T(V_2))$, and $\Ext_{G\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet(S(V_1),S(V_2))=
\Ext_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet(Res_G^TS(V_1),Res_G^T(V_2))^W$. On the other hand, we know by Theorem [\[compaThm\]](#compaThm){reference-type="ref" reference="compaThm"} a) that $H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr,S(V_{1,2}))\simeq\varphi(V_{1,2})$, and clearly, $\Hom(\phi(V_1),\phi(V_2))=\Hom(\varphi(V_1),\varphi(V_2))^W$. Thus it suffices to prove that $$\Ext_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet(Res_G^TS(V_1),Res_G^T(V_2))\simeq$$ $$\Hom_{\CO({\frak t}\times({\frak t}/W)\times{\mathbb A}^1)}
\left(H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr,S(V_1)),
H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr,S(V_2))\right)=$$ $$\Hom_{H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)}\left(H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr,S(V_1)),
H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr,S(V_2))\right).$$

Following [@BL], recall the definition of the LHS: we choose finite dimensional approximations $P_i$ to the classifying space of $T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}$, and we have ind-varieties $P_i\Gr$ fibered over $P_i$ with fibers isomorphic to $\Gr$. We also have semisimple perverse sheaves $P_iS(V_{1,2})$ on $P_i\Gr$, and finally $\Ext_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet(Res_G^TS(V_1),Res_G^T(V_2))$ is defined as $\underset{\to}{\lim}\Ext_{P_i\Gr}^\bullet(P_iS(V_1),P_iS(V_2))$. Since $T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}$ is a torus, we can choose $P_i$ to be the products of projective spaces (of increasing dimension). We can choose a generic action of ${{\mathbb G}_m}$ on $P_i\Gr$ (linear along $P_i$, and via a one-parametric subgroup of $T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}$ along $\Gr$) such that the corresponding Bialynicki-Birula decomposition of $P_i\Gr$ is cellular. Then we can apply the Theorem of [@G0] to conclude that $\Ext_{P_i\Gr}^\bullet(P_iS(V_1),P_iS(V_2))\simeq
\Hom_{H^\bullet(P_i\Gr)}(H^\bullet(P_i\Gr,S(V_1)),H^\bullet(P_i\Gr,S(V_2)))$. But the limit of the RHS as $i$ grows is $\Hom_{H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)}\left(H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr,S(V_1)),
H^\bullet_{T\times{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr,S(V_2))\right)$.

The lemma is proved. $\Box$

## Homology

The goal of this subsection is to express equivariant cohomology of arbitrary (not necessarily semisimple) equivariant complexes in terms of Harish-Chandra bimodules, and to prove Theorem [\[TodaTh\]](#TodaTh){reference-type="ref" reference="TodaTh"}. By Theorem [\[free_cohh\]](#free_cohh){reference-type="ref" reference="free_cohh"} we have a monoidal equivalence ${\mathcal{S}}$ between the category $\HC_\hbar^{fr}$ of free asymptotic Harish-Chandra bimodules and the category ${\mathcal{IC}}$ of semisimple complexes in $D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)$.

A standard argument shows any functor on free Harish-Chandra bimodules is representable by a unique up to a unique isomorphism (not necessarily free) Harish-Chandra bimodule. Thus we have a functor from ${\mathfrak{F}}:D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)\to \widetilde \HC_\hbar$ equipped with a functorial isomorphism $$\Hom({\mathcal{S}}(F), \F)\cong \Hom(F,{\mathfrak{F}}(\F));$$ ${\mathfrak{F}}$ is defined uniquely up to a unique isomorphism.

Since ${\mathcal{S}}$ is a full embedding, ${\mathfrak{F}}\circ {\mathcal{S}}\cong\Id$ canonically, i.e. ${\mathfrak{F}}$ restricted to the category of semi-simple complexes is the inverse equivalence to ${\mathcal{S}}$. It is easy to see from the definition that ${\mathfrak{F}}$ is a homological functor; thus it actually lands in the category $\HC_\hbar$ of finitely generated Harish-Chandra bimodules, ${\mathfrak{F}}: D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)\to \HC_\hbar$.

We will say that a functor $F$ between two monoidal categories is quasi-monoidal if a functorial map $F(X)*F(Y)\to F(X*Y)$ is fixed for any objects $X,Y$ of the target category, compatible with the associativity isomorphisms in the two categories in the natural sense.

::: prop
[]{#fufux label="fufux"} a) ${\mathfrak{F}}$ carries a unique quasi-monoidal structure, whose restriction to ${\mathcal{IC}}$ induces the natural monoidal structure on $\Id_{{\HC_\hbar^{fr}}}\cong {\mathfrak{F}}\circ {\mathcal{S}}$.

b\) We have a natural isomorphism $H^\bullet_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}\cong {\kappa_\hbar}\circ {\mathfrak{F}}$ compatible with the (quasi)monoidal structure.
:::

*Proof* a) It is easy to see that $${\mathfrak{F}}(\F)= R\Hom(IC_0, {\mathcal O}({\check{G}})* \F)\cong R\Hom(IC_0,
\F*{\mathcal O}({\check{G}}))$$ canonically (where the last isomorphism comes from the fact that both sides can be identified with $\bigoplus\limits_\lambda 
RHom(V_\lambda^*\otimes IC_\lambda, \F)$). Then the quasi-monoidal structure comes from the coalgebra structure on ${\mathcal O}({\check{G}})$. Uniqueness follows from the fact that for every $\F\in D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}$ we can find a free asymptotic Harish-Chandra bimodule $V$ and a surjection $V\to {\mathfrak{F}}(\F)$; by definition of ${\mathfrak{F}}$ it comes from a map $L={\mathcal{S}}(V)\to \F$. Then, in view of functoriality, for $\F_1,\,\F_2\in D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}$ the map ${\mathfrak{F}}(\F_1)*{\mathfrak{F}}(\F_2)\to
{\mathfrak{F}}(\F_1*\F_2)$ is uniquely determined by the isomorphism ${\mathfrak{F}}(L_1)*{\mathfrak{F}}(L_2)
\to {\mathfrak{F}}(L_1*L_2)$ for $L_i\to \F_i$ ($i=1,2$) as above.

b\) For $\F\in D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)$ we can find an exact sequence $V_1\to V_2\to {\mathfrak{F}}(\F)\to 0$, where $V_i$ are free asymptotic Harish-Chandra bimodules. To this sequence there corresponds a sequence of maps in $D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)$ with zero composition: $L_1\to L_2\to \F$, where $L_i={\mathcal{S}}(V_i)$. We have ${\kappa_\hbar}\circ {\mathfrak{F}}(\F) = CoKer ({\kappa_\hbar}(V_1) \to {\kappa_\hbar}(V_2))=CoKer
(H^\bullet_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(L_1)\to 
H^\bullet_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(L_2))$. The latter module maps canonically to $H^\bullet_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\F)$. Thus we defined a map ${\kappa_\hbar}\circ 
{\mathfrak{F}}(\F)\to H^\bullet_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}
(\F)$. A standard argument shows that this map does not depend on the choice of $V_1,\, V_2$. We have obtained a natural transformation between the two functors. This transformation is an isomorphism on semi-simple complexes. Since both functors are homological (where we use exactness of ${\kappa_\hbar}$, Lemma [\[propKons\]](#propKons){reference-type="ref" reference="propKons"}(a)) and semi-simple objects generate the triangulated category $D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)$, we see that the transformation is an isomorphism. $\Box$

We can clearly extend all of the above to Ind-objects. We will be particularly interested in the Ind-object ${\mathfrak D}=\varinjlim {\mathfrak D}_\lambda$, where ${\mathfrak D}_\lambda$ is the dualizing sheaf of the closure of the ${\bf G_O}$-orbit $\Gr_\lambda$. Notice that $H_\bullet^{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)=H^\bullet_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}({\mathfrak D})$, and the convolution algebra structure on homology comes from the structure of an algebra in the monoidal category $D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}$ on the object ${\mathfrak D}$ and the monoidal structure on the cohomology functor.

We will now describe the corresponding object in the category of Harish-Chandra bimodules. Recall first the duality ${\mathbf D}$ on $\HC_\hbar^{fr}$, ${\mathbf D}:M\mapsto \Hom_{U_\hbar}(M,U_\hbar)$, where $\Hom$ is taken with respect to the right action of $U_\hbar$, and the left action on $M,\, U_\hbar$ is used to define, respectively, the right and the left action on the $\Hom$ module. Let $Forg$ denote the forgetful functor from Harish-Chandra bimodules to vector spaces. The functor $Forg\circ {\mathbf D}$ is represented by the Ind-object, which is readily identified with $U_\hbar \otimes
{\mathcal O}({\check{G}})$, the module of $\hbar$-differential operators on ${\check{G}}$. Furthermore, $U_\hbar \otimes
{\mathcal O}({\check{G}})$ carries another commuting structure of a Harish-Chandra bimodule, and we set ${\mathcal K }={\kappa_\hbar}(U_\hbar\otimes {\mathcal O}({\check{G}}))$, where this second structure of a Harish-Chandra bimodule is used to compute ${\kappa_\hbar}$. Thus ${\mathcal K }$ is an Ind-object in the category of Harish-Chandra bimodules; moreover, it is an algebra Ind-object in this monoidal category.

::: prop
[]{#fufudsK label="fufudsK"} We have a canonical isomorphism of algebra Ind-objects: ${\mathfrak{F}}({\mathfrak D})\cong {\mathcal K }$.
:::

To prove Proposition we need another auxiliary

::: lem
[]{#VerD label="VerD"} We have a canonical isomorphism ${\mathbb D}\circ {\mathcal{S}}\cong {\mathcal{S}}\circ   {\mathfrak C}_{{\check{G}}}\circ {\mathbf D}$, where ${\mathbb D}$, ${\mathfrak C}_{{\check{G}}}$ denote, respectively, Verdier duality

and the functor induced by the canonical outer automorphism of ${\check{G}}$ interchanging conjugacy classes of $g$ and $g^{-1}$, $g\in {\check{G}}$ (the Chevalley involution).
:::

*Proof* : Recall that a monoidal category ${\mathcal C}$ is rigid if for any object $V\in {\mathcal C}$ there exists another object $V^*\in {\mathcal C}$ and morphisms $\iota:{\bf 1}\to V\otimes V^*$ and $\tau:V^*\otimes V\to 
{\bf 1}$ satisfying a certain compatibility constraint, see [@DM]. Given $V$, an object $V^*$ together with morphisms $\iota,\tau$ is unique up to a unique isomorphism if it exists. Thus for a rigid category ${{\mathcal C}}$ we have a canonical (up to a canonical isomorphism) functor ${{\mathcal C}}\mapsto  {{\mathcal C}}^{op}$ sending $V$ to $V^*$. It is immediate to check that monoidal categories ${Perv_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes{{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)}$ and $\HC_\hbar^{fr}$ are rigid categories with duality functors given by ${\mathfrak C}_G\circ {\mathbb D}$ and ${\mathbf D}$ respectively, where the functor ${\mathfrak C}_G$ is induced by the Chevalley involution of $G$. The equivalence between the two categories intertwines the canonically defined dualities. Also, it is well-known that ${\mathcal{S}}\circ {\mathfrak C}_{{\check{G}}}\cong {\mathfrak C}_G\circ
{\mathcal{S}}$ canonically. The Lemma follows. $\Box$

*Proof of Proposition [\[fufudsK\]](#fufudsK){reference-type="ref" reference="fufudsK"}.* The Ind-object ${\mathfrak D}$ represents the functor $\F\mapsto H^\bullet_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}({\mathbb D}(\F))$. In view of Lemma [\[VerD\]](#VerD){reference-type="ref" reference="VerD"} and Proposition [\[fufux\]](#fufux){reference-type="ref" reference="fufux"}(c) we see that the Ind-object ${\mathfrak{F}}({\mathfrak D})$ represents the functor $M\mapsto {\kappa_\hbar}({\mathbf D}(M))$ on the category of free asymptotic Harish-Chandra modules. It is straighforward to see from the definitions that the Ind-object ${\mathcal K }$ represents the same functor. The isomorphism of functors yields an isomorphism of Harish-Chandra bimodules. Since the isomorphism of functors is compatible with the monoidal structure, the isomorphism of Harish-Chandra bimodules is compatible with the algebra structure.

$\Box$

*Proof of Theorem [\[TodaTh\]](#TodaTh){reference-type="ref" reference="TodaTh"}.* By Propositions [\[fufux\]](#fufux){reference-type="ref" reference="fufux"} and [\[fufudsK\]](#fufudsK){reference-type="ref" reference="fufudsK"} we have an isomorphism of algebras $$H^{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}_\bullet(\Gr)=H_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}^\bullet({\mathfrak D})\cong
{\kappa_\hbar}({\mathcal K }).$$ The latter is by definition the algebra of the quantum Toda lattice. $\Box$

## Formality from purity

In this section we combine the above Ext computation with a standard argument which allows one to derive formality of the RHom algebra from purity of the Ext spaces. The result is a description of the derived Satake category, including the version equivariant with respect to the loop rotation.

*Except for some technical details, this section does not contain original contributions of the authors.* We have learned the geometric (respectively, algebraic) ideas exposed here from V. Ginzburg (respectively, L. Positselski) around 1998.

In order to be able to use Frobenius weights we extend the basic setting, and consider ${\bf G_O}$, $\Gr$ etc. over ${\bar{\mathbb F}_q}$, and the categories of equivariant $l$-adic sheaves on $\Gr$.

Consider the following general situation. Let $R$ be a finitely localized ring of integers of a number field $E$, and let $\Fq$ be a finite field quotient of $R$. Let $X_R$ be a flat scheme over $R$ acted upon by a smooth affine group scheme $G_R$, such that the set of orbits is finite.

We denote by $(X_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}},G_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$ (resp. $(X_{\bar E},G_{\bar E})$) the base change of $(X_R,G_R)$ to a geometric point of $R$ over $\Fq$ (resp. over the generic point). We choose a prime $l$ invertible in $R$. Let $D_{G_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}}}(X_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$ (resp. $D_{G_{\bar E}}(X_{\bar E})$) stand for the bounded equivariant constructible derived category of étale ${\overline {{\Bbb Q}_l} }$-sheaves on $X_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}}$ (resp. $X_{\bar E}$) (see e.g. [@BD], 7.4). We choose an isomorphism ${\overline {{\Bbb Q}_l} }\simeq\mathsf k$ (under a technical assumption that $\mathsf k$ has the same cardinality as ${\overline {{\Bbb Q}_l} }$), and an embedding ${\bar E}\hookrightarrow\BC$, and we denote by $(X_\BC,G_\BC)$ the base change of $(X_{\bar E},G_{\bar E})$ to $\BC$. Let $D_{G_\BC}(X_\BC)$ (resp. $D_{G_\BC}^{top}(X_\BC,{\overline {{\Bbb Q}_l} }),\
D_{G_\BC}^{top}(X_\BC)$) stand for the bounded equivariant constructible derived category of étale ${\overline {{\Bbb Q}_l} }$-sheaves on $X_\BC$ (resp. bounded equivariant constructible derived category of sheaves with ${\overline {{\Bbb Q}_l} }$-coefficients, resp. $\mathsf k$-coefficients, *in the classical topology* of $X_\BC$).

::: prop
[]{#bbdd label="bbdd"} There exists a localization $R_{(r)}$ of $R$ such that for any point $R_{(r)}\twoheadrightarrow\Fq$, we have the following chain of natural equivalences: $$D_{G_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}}}(X_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})\stackrel{\alpha}{\to}
D_{G_{\bar E}}(X_{\bar E})\stackrel{\beta}{\to}
D_{G_\BC}(X_\BC)\stackrel{\gamma}{\to}
D_{G_\BC}^{top}(X_\BC,{\overline {{\Bbb Q}_l} })\stackrel{\delta}{\to}
D_{G_\BC}^{top}(X_\BC)$$
:::

*Sketch of proof.* The argument is taken from [@BBD], 6.1. The first equivalence $\alpha$ is constructed in [@BBD], 6.1.9 (existence of good models). To justify the finiteness assumptions of *loc. cit.* we note that the set of isomorphism classes of $G$-equivariant irreducible perverse sheaves on $X$ is finite. Since the equivariant derived categories are not considered in *loc. cit.* we note that according to [@BD], 7.4, to compare the equivariant Exts between equivariant irreducible perverse sheaves on $X$ it suffices to compare the usual Exts between the lifts of these sheaves to $X\times G^n$ (see the canonical spectral sequence (312) of *loc. cit.*). These are calculated by the Künneth formula.

The second equivalence $\beta$ is just the base change from $\bar E$ to $\BC$. The third equivalence $\gamma$ is the classical M. Artin's comparison theorem of étale and classical cohomology, see [@BBD], 6.1.2(B$''$). Finally, $\delta$ is induced by our isomorphism ${\overline {{\Bbb Q}_l} }\simeq\mathsf k$. $\Box$

From now on we will restrict our attention to the equivariant derived categories in our new setting, that is, over ${\bar{\mathbb F}_q}$.

Let $X$ be an algebraic variety over a finite $\Fq$, and let $D(X_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$ stand for the bounded constructible $l$-adic derived category of $X_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}}$. Let $\F$ be a pure weight zero object of the $l$-adic derived category of $X$ of geometric origin. The space $\Ext^i(\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}},\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})=\bigoplus\limits
\Ext^i_j(\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}},\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$ carries a canonical grading by Frobenius weights; here the subindex denotes base change to ${\bar{\mathbb F}_q}$, and $\Ext^i_j$ is the component of weight $j$. Recall that by Deligne Theorem [@W2] $\Ext^i_j=0$ for $j<i$.

Let ${\mathcal E}$ be a graded algebra and $\phi:{\mathcal E}\to \Ext^\bullet(\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}},\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})^{op}$ be a homomorphism sending a graded component ${\mathcal E}^i$ to $Ext^i_i(\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}},\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$.

We will consider the graded algebra ${\mathcal E}$ as a dg-algebra with zero differential.

::: prop
[]{#general label="general"} There exists a canonical functor $\Phi_X:\ D_{perf}({\mathcal E})\to D(X_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$ sending the free module to $\F_{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}$ and inducing the map $\phi$ on Ext groups.
:::

*Sketch of proof.* The complex $\F_{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}$ is semi-simple, i.e. is isomorphic to $\oplus \IC_i[d_i]$, where $\IC_i$ is an irreducible perverse sheaf and $d_i\in {\Bbb Z}$. By Beilinson's Theorem [@Bedr] the $l$-adic derived category contains the derived category of perverse sheaves as a full subcategory, thus $\Ext^\bullet(\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}},\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$ coincides with Ext in the category of perverse sheaves. We can assume without loss of generality that ${\mathcal E}$ is finitely presented; thus the map $\phi$ factors through a map $\phi_{fin}\colon {\mathcal E}\to \Ext^\bullet_{\A} (\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}},\F_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$, where $\A$ is the Serre subcategory in the category of perverse sheaves on $X_{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}$ generated by a finite set of irreducible objects, including $\IC_i$. Moreover, the argument of [@Bedr] (cf. Examples in *loc.cit.*, 1.2, p.28) shows that all irreducible objects of $\A$ can be assumed to be of geometric origin.

We can identify the abelian category $\A$ with the category of finite length $A$-modules, where the pro-finite dimensional algebra (algebra in the tensor category of pro-finite dimensional vector spaces) $A$ is defined by $A=\End(\oplus {\mathcal P}_s)$; here $s$ runs over the (finite) set of isomorphism classes of irreducible objects in $\A$, and ${\mathcal P}_s$ is a pro-object in $\A$ which is a projective cover of the corresponding irreducible object $L_s$, cf. [@B]. We fix an isomorphism $Fr_q^*(L_s)\cong L_s$, which induces a pure weight zero Weil structure on $L_s$ (this is possible because $L_s$ has geometric origin, see [@BBD]). Since projective cover of an irreducible object is unique up to a non-unique isomorphism, we can (and will) fix an isomorphism $Fr_q^*({\mathcal P}_s)\cong {\mathcal P}_s$. Then conjugation with Frobenius is an automorphism of $A$ (which we will also call Frobenius).

By a result of [@BBD], Frobenius acts on $\Ext^1(L_s,L_{s'})$ with positive weights. It follows that Frobenius finite elements are dense in $A$, and they form a graded subalgebra $A^{gr}$ with finite dimensional graded components, where the grading comes from Frobenius weights. Moreover, components of negative degree in $A^{gr}$ vanish, while $A^{gr}_0$ is semisimple. Obviously, $\A$ is identified with the category of finite length $A^{gr}$ modules, on which $A^{gr}_N$ acts by zero for $N\gg 0$.

We now consider the object $L=\oplus L_i[d_i]\in D^b(\A)$ (where $L_i$ corresponds to $\IC_i$) and a dg-algebra $\sD=\RHom_\A(L,L)$ (well defined as an object of the category of dg-algebras with inverted quasi-isomorphisms). Recall that we have an equivalence $D^b(\A)\cong D_{perf}(\sD^{op})$, $M\mapsto \RHom(L,M)$. We lift $L$ to an object $\tilde L=\oplus \tilde L_i[d_i](d_i)$ of the derived category of graded $A^{gr}$-modules, where $\tilde L_i$ is the irreducible $A$-module concentrated in degree zero, and $(d)$ stands for shift of grading by $d$. Then the algebra $\Ext^\bullet(L,L)$ acquires an additional grading, and $\sD$ can be chosen to carry also an additional grading compatible with the grading on $\Ext$'s. We have a homomorphism ${\mathcal E}\to\bigoplus\limits_i\Ext^i_i(L,L)$, where the lower index denotes the additional grading, and the fact that $A^{gr}$ is positively graded implies that $\Ext^i_j(L,L)=0$ for $j<0$. Thus existence of a canonically defined functor $D_{perf}({\mathcal E})\to D^b(\A)$ follows from the standard Lemma [\[gradualg\]](#gradualg){reference-type="ref" reference="gradualg"}a) below.

We leave it as an exercise to the reader to show that the composed functor $\Phi_X:\ D_{perf}({\mathcal E})
\to D^b(\A)\to D(X_{{\bar{\mathbb F}_q}})$ does not depend on the choice of $\A$ up to a canonical isomorphism. $\Box$

We will also have to use functoriality properties of the above construction. We spell these out now.

*Proof. * Let $\CalC\in D_{perf}({\mathcal E})$. Consider a dg-module $B_{X,Y}$ of $\sD'\otimes \sD^{op}$-module defined by $B_{X,Y}:=

It is not hard to see that the composed functor $D_{perf}(\sD)\cong D^b(\A)\overset{F}{\longrightarrow} D^b(\A')\cong D_{perf}((\sD')^{op})$ arises from this bimodule as described in Lemma [\[gradualg\]](#gradualg){reference-type="ref" reference="gradualg"}(b). ## Proof of Theorem [\[polkovnik\]](#polkovnik){reference-type="ref" reference="polkovnik"}

We construct the first equivalence, the second one is similar. We will construct a monoidal functor $\Psi:D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}}) \to D_{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)$, whose restriction to the full subcategory $\HC_\hbar^{fr}\subset D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})$ is identified with ${\mathcal{S}}$ (where the full embedding sends a ${\check{G}}$-equivariant graded $U_\hbar$-module to the same module considered as a dg-module with zero differential). Then $\Psi$ sends a set of generators of the source triangulated category to generators of the target category, and induces an isomorphism on Hom's between the generators, hence it is an equivalence.

It suffices to construct a collection of functors $\Psi_\lambda:  D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})_{\leq \lambda}\to
D_{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr_{\leq \lambda})$, where $\lambda$ is a coweight of $G$, $\Gr_{\leq \lambda}$ is the closure of the corresponding ${\bf G_O}$ orbit on $\Gr$, and $D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})_{\leq \lambda}$ is the full subcategory in $D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})$ generated by the objects $V\otimes {U_\hbar^{[]}}$, where $V$ is an irreducible representation of ${\check{G}}$ with a highest weight $\mu \leq \lambda$. These functors will be compatible for comparable coweights (i.e. we have isomorphisms $\Psi_\mu\cong
 \Psi_\lambda|_{ D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})_{\leq \mu}}$ for $\mu\leq \lambda$, satisfying the obvious compatibility for a triple of coweights $\nu\leq \mu \leq \lambda$).

The action of ${\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}$ on $\Gr_{\leq \lambda}$ factors through a finite dimensional algebraic group $H_\lambda$, and $D_{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr_{\leq \lambda})\cong 
D_H(\Gr_{\leq \lambda})$ naturally. To describe $\Psi_\lambda$ we need to provide the following data: for a smooth $H_\lambda$-equivariant map $X\to \Gr_{\leq \lambda}$, where $H_\lambda$ acts on $X$ freely we need to provide a functor $D^{\check{G}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})_{\leq \lambda}\to D(X/H_\lambda)$, compatible with pull-backs (i.e. a Cartesian section of the category of resolutions of $\Gr_{\leq \lambda}/H_\lambda$ , cf. [@BL], 2.4.3). In view of Theorem [\[free_cohh\]](#free_cohh){reference-type="ref" reference="free_cohh"} we have a map $\End^\bullet(V_{\leq \lambda}\otimes {U_\hbar^{[]}}) {\stackrel{\sim}{\longrightarrow}}
\End^\bullet(I_{\leq \lambda})\to End(I_{\leq \lambda}^X)$; here $V_{\leq \lambda}$ is the sum of all irreducible ${\check{G}}$-modules with a highest weight less or equal than $\lambda$, $I_{\leq \lambda}
\in D_{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr_{\leq \lambda})$ is the sum of IC sheaves of all ${\bf G_O}$ orbits in $\Gr_{\leq \lambda}$, and $I_{\leq \lambda}^X$ is the pull-back of $I_{\leq \lambda}$ to $X$. Thus the required functor is given by Proposition [\[general\]](#general){reference-type="ref" reference="general"} in view of purity of equivariant $\Ext$'s between IC sheaves on the affine Grassmannian, see e.g. [@Ga]. These functors do indeed form a Cartesian section in view of Proposition [\[foon\]](#foon){reference-type="ref" reference="foon"}, cf. Remark [\[pull_push_fun\]](#pull_push_fun){reference-type="ref" reference="pull_push_fun"}. Compatibility between $\Psi_\lambda$ and $\Psi_\mu$ for $\mu\leq \lambda$ is left as an exercise for the reader.

In view of Proposition [\[foon\]](#foon){reference-type="ref" reference="foon"} (cf. Remark [\[pull_push_fun\]](#pull_push_fun){reference-type="ref" reference="pull_push_fun"}), a monoidal structure for the constructed functor would follow if we show that the functors from $[D^{\check{G}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})]^2$, $[D^{\check{G}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})]^3$ to the derived category of sheaves on the convolution space, respectively, triple convolution space, given, respectively, by $(M_1,M_2) \mapsto \Psi(M_1)\boxtimes^{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}} \Psi(M_2)$, and by $(M_1,M_2,M_3) \mapsto \Psi(M_1)\boxtimes^{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}} \Psi(M_2)
\boxtimes^{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}} \Psi(M_3)$ (where $\boxtimes^{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}$ denotes the twisted external product on the convolution space), are compatible with the functors stemming from the construction of Proposition [\[general\]](#general){reference-type="ref" reference="general"}. This follows from Remark [\[multifoon\]](#multifoon){reference-type="ref" reference="multifoon"}. $\Box$
