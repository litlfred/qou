To a differential graded algebra $A$ one can associate the triangulated category $D(A)$ of differential graded modules localized by quasi-isomorphism; and a full triangulated subcategory $D_{perf}(A)\subset D(A)$ of perfect complexes. Thus $D_{perf}(A)$ is the full subcategory in the latter category consisting of perfect complexes (i.e. generated by the free module under cones and direct summands). Given an algebraic group $H$ acting on a dg-algebra $A$, we can consider equivariant dg-modules and localize them by quasi-isomorphisms, arriving at the equivariant version $D^H_{perf}(A)$.

We now consider the "differential-graded versions" ${\Sym^{[]}(\check{\mathfrak g}{})}$, ${U_\hbar^{[]}}$ of the graded algebras $\Sym (\check{\mathfrak g}{})$, $U_\hbar(\check{\mathfrak g}{})$. By definition ${\Sym^{[]}(\check{\mathfrak g}{})}$, ${U_\hbar^{[]}}$ are differential graded algebras with zero differential, which as algebras are isomorphic to $\Sym(\check{\mathfrak g}{})$, $U_\hbar(\check{\mathfrak g}{})$ respectively. The cohomological grading is defined so that elements of $\check{\mathfrak g}{}$ and $\hbar$ have degree two. Recall from section [\[agm\]](#agm){reference-type="ref" reference="agm"} that an asymptotic Harish-Chandra bimodule $M\in\HC_\hbar$ is nothing but a ${\check{G}}$-equivariant ${U_\hbar^{[]}}$-module. Using this identification we can transfer tensor product of asymptotic Harish-Chandra bimodules to a monoidal structure on the category of ${\check{G}}$-equivariant ${U_\hbar^{[]}}$-modules. It gives rise to a monoidal structure on $D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}})$. Similarly, we define a monoidal structure on $D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({\Sym^{[]}(\check{\mathfrak g}{})})$.

::: thm
[]{#polkovnik label="polkovnik"} There exist canonical equivalences of monoidal triangulated categories $D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({U_\hbar^{[]}}) \cong D_{{\bf G_O}\ltimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)$, $D^{{\check{G}}}_{perf}({\Sym^{[]}(\check{\mathfrak g}{})}) \cong D_{{\bf G_O}}(\Gr)$.
:::

The following statement is an immediate consequence of the Theorem, which has the (psychological) advantage of bypassing the notion of a dg-algebra.

::: cor
[]{#bone label="bone"} a) The derived graded Harish-Chandra bimodule category is Koszul dual to a graded version of the loop-rotation equivariant Satake category, i.e. (cf. [@BGS], [@ABG], [@Hu]) there exists a functor $\Phi:D^b(\HC_\hbar) \to D_{{\bf G_O}\rtimes {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\Gr)$, such that

i\) $\Phi(M(1)) \cong \Phi(M)[1]$ in a natural way.

ii\) For $M_1,\, M_2 \in D^b(\HC_\hbar)$, $\Phi$ induces an isomorphism $$\sum\limits_{n,m}Hom(M_1, M_2(n)[m])\cong \sum\limits_k Hom(\Phi(M_1),\Phi(M_2)[k])$$

iii\) The image of $\Phi$ generates the target category as a triangulated category.

Moreover, $\Phi$ carries a natural monoidal structure, and $\Phi|_{\HC_\hbar^{fr}}$ coincides with $S_\hbar$.

b\) The derived category of graded ${\check{G}}$-equivariant $\Sym(\check{\mathfrak g}{})$ modules is Koszul dual to a graded version of the Satake category, i.e. there exists a functor $\Phi':D^b(Coh^{{\check{G}}\times {{\mathbb G}_m}}(\check{\mathfrak g}{}) ) \to D_{{\bf G_O}}(\Gr)$, satisfying the properties similar to those listed in part (a). This functor has a natural monoidal structure.
:::
