The remaining necessary properties to define the $k$-negligible ideals for either quantized or modular tilting modules follow from Lusztig's theory of canonical bases [@Lusztig-book Part 4].

## Weights and Weyl groups

We will use the following notation (similar to [@Parshall-Wang]) in the following:

-   $R$ an irreducible root system in an euclidian space $E$, $R^+$ a fixed set of positive roots, $\phi$ the simple roots.

-   $h$ the Coxeter number of $R$, $\rho$ the half sum of the positive roots.

-   $\alpha^{\vee} = 2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)$ the dual root to $\alpha \in R$.

-   $W$ the finite Weyl group.

-   $X$ the integral weight lattice of $R$.

-   $X^+$ the dominant integral weights.

-   $Q$ the ${\mathbb Z}$-span of $R$.

-   $W_{\ell} = W \rtimes T_{\ell}$ for the group of translations $T_{\ell} \ni t_{\ell}:E \to E$, $x \mapsto x + \ell\lambda$ for $\lambda \in Q$ and an odd integer $\ell$.

-   $d_{\alpha} = (\alpha,\alpha)/(\alpha_0,\alpha_0)$ for the shortest root $\alpha_0$ of $\phi$.

-   $wht(\lambda) = \sum_{\alpha \in \phi} r_{\alpha} d_{\alpha}$ for $\lambda = \sum_{\alpha \in \phi} r_{\alpha} \alpha$.

-   $C_{\ell}$ the fundamental alcove $\{ \lambda \in X^+ \ | \ 0 < \ <\lambda + \rho, \alpha^{\vee}>  \ < \ell \ \text{ for all } \alpha \in R^+\}$ and $\overline{C}_{\ell}$ its closure.

Recall that the affine Weyl group acts on $X$. The fundamental domain for this action is the closed alcove $\overline{C}_{\ell}$. The affine Weyl group $W_{\ell}$ can be identified with the set of alcoves in $X$ by matching $w \in W_{\ell}$ with $w \cdot C_{\ell}$. The alcove corresponding to $w$ is denoted by $C_w$. We denote $W_{\ell}^+ = \{ w \in W \ | \ w \cdot C_{\ell} \subset X^+ \} \subset W$.

## Based modules {#sec:based}

For the notion of a based module $(M,B)$ ($M \in \mathcal{C})$ we refer to [@Lusztig-book 27.1.2]. Based modules form a category $\tilde{C}$ with morphisms as in [@Lusztig-book 27.1.3]. Based modules are closed under direct summands [@Lusztig-book 27.1.2], submodules and quotients [@Lusztig-book 27.1.3].

The tensor product of two based modules $M \otimes M'$ with the naive basis $B \otimes B'$ is not a based module, but there is a modified basis $B \lozenge B'$ with elements $b \lozenge b'$ with $(b,b') \in B \times B'$ which turns $(M \otimes M', B \lozenge  B')$ into a based module [@Lusztig-book 27.3]. We denote by $_{\mathcal{A}}M \otimes M'$ the $\mathcal{A}$-submodule generated by the basis $B \otimes B'$ (where $\mathcal{A} = {\mathbb Z}[v,v^{-1}]$). The modified basis is then an $\mathcal{A}$-basis of $_{\mathcal{A}}M \otimes M'$ [@Lusztig-book Theorem 27.3.3].

The anti-involution $\omega$ of $U$ gives rise to a duality on $\mathcal{C}$, and we denote its dual by $^{\omega}M$. For a based module $(M,B)$ there is a partition of $B$ into subsets $B[\lambda]$ such that $B[0]$ is the base for the space of coinvariants $M_*$. For based modules $M,M'$ we can canonically identify $Hom(M,M')$ with the dual of the space of coinvariants of $M \otimes ^{\omega}M'$. This gives $Hom(M,M')$ the structure of a based module with basis $(B \lozenge ^{\omega}B')[0]$ [@Lusztig-book 27.2.5]. In particular the $\mathcal{A}$-integrality properties of based modules show that $\tilde{C}$ is an $\mathcal{A}$-linear monoidal category.

The simple objects in $\tilde{C}$ are parametrized by $X^+$, i.e. every simple object is isomorphic to $(L(\lambda), B(\lambda),\Psi_{\lambda})$ for some unique $\lambda \in X^+$. Therefore any nontrivial based module admits a filtration with subquotients isomorphic to $(L(\lambda), B(\lambda),\Psi_{\lambda})$ for some $\lambda \in X^+$.

::: lemma
[]{#lem:good-filtration-tensor label="lem:good-filtration-tensor"} [@Kaneda-based Corollary 1.9] Let $M, M'$ be two based modules. Then $M_{\mathcal{A}} \otimes_{\mathcal{A}} M'_{\mathcal{A}}$ admits a filtration of $U_{\mathcal{A}}$-modules with each subquotient isomorphic to some $L_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda), \ \lambda \in X^+$.
:::

We can now base change from $\mathcal{A}$ to $R = \widehat{{\mathbb Q}[v]_{(v-q)}}$. Then the lemma immediately implies that the tensor product of two modules with a Weyl filtration (or good filtration) has a again a Weyl filtration (good filtration).

Let $G_k$ as in section [\[tilting-modular\]](#tilting-modular){reference-type="ref" reference="tilting-modular"} denote a semisimple simply connected algebraic group over a field $\Bbbk$ of characteristic $p$. Then (as in [@Kaneda-based 1.10]) we can view ${\mathbb Z}$ as an $\mathcal{A}$-algebra and obtain a ring isomorphism $$U_{\mathcal{A}}/(K_{\alpha} - 1)_{\alpha \in \Pi} \ \otimes_{\mathcal{A}} {\mathbb Z}\cong Dist(G)$$ for the Chevalley ${\mathbb Z}$-form $G$ of $G_k$. The $Dist(G)$-modules that are free of finite rank over ${\mathbb Z}$ are $G$-modules such that $L_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda) \otimes_{\mathcal{A}} {\mathbb Z}$ is the Weyl module for $G$ of highest weight $\lambda, \ \lambda \in X^+$. Therefore lemma [\[lem:good-filtration-tensor\]](#lem:good-filtration-tensor){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:good-filtration-tensor"} implies in the modular case as well that the tensor product of two modules with a Weyl filtration (or good filtration) has a again a Weyl filtration (good filtration).

::: corollary
The tensor product of two quantized tilting modules over $R$ and the tensor product of two modular tilting modules over the Witt ring $W(k)$ is a tilting module.
:::

## The mod $\mathfrak{m}$ evaluation

We want to realize now $Tilt(U_{q}(\mathfrak{g}), {\mathbb Q}(q))$ and $Tilt(G,k)$ as mod $\mathfrak{m}$ evaluations. In the modular case (by section [\[tilting-modular\]](#tilting-modular){reference-type="ref" reference="tilting-modular"}) we should take a complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic zero with residue field $\Bbbk$ of characteristic $p$. In order to measure the $p$-divisibility, its maximal ideal should be generated by $p$. By [@Serre-local II.Theorem 3] for every perfect field $\Bbbk$ of characteristic $p$, there exists a unique complete discrete valuation ring which is absolutely unramified \[i.e., $p$ is a uniformizing element\] and has $\Bbbk$ as its residue field: namely $W(k)$, the ring of Witt vectors. The results of section [\[tilting-modular\]](#tilting-modular){reference-type="ref" reference="tilting-modular"}, [\[tilting-quantum\]](#tilting-quantum){reference-type="ref" reference="tilting-quantum"} and [0.2](#sec:based){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:based"} imply

::: theorem
[]{#thm:tilting-eval label="thm:tilting-eval"} a) Let $R = \widehat{{\mathbb Q}[v]}_{v-q}$ where $q$ is a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity where $\ell > h$ and $\ell$ is not divisible by 3 if $\mathfrak{g}$ contains $\mathfrak{g}_2$. Then $Tilt(U_q(\mathfrak{g}),{\mathbb Q}(q))$ is the mod $\mathfrak{m}$ evaluation of $Tilt(U_v(\mathfrak{g}), R)$ where $\mathfrak{m} = (v-q)$.\
b) Let $\Bbbk$ be a perfect field of characteristic $p$. Then $Tilt(G_{k},k))$ is the mod $\mathfrak{m}$ evaluation of $Tilt(G_{W(k)}, W(k))$ where $\mathfrak{m} = (p)$.
:::

As both rings are discrete valuation rings, all the $N_I$ are of the form $N_k$ for $k=1,2,\ldots$.

## The nullity in the quantum case

The category $Tilt(U_q(\mathfrak{g}),{\mathbb Q}(q))$ is a braided monoidal category. The categorical dimension $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}(V)$ is often called the quantum dimension of $V$.

::: proposition
([@Parshall-Wang Proposition 2.2, Theorem 3.3]) For $\lambda \in X^+$

1.  We have $$\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V(\lambda) = \prod_{\alpha \in R^+} \frac{q^{-d_{\alpha}(\lambda + \rho,\alpha^{\vee})} - q^{d_{\alpha}(\lambda + \rho,\alpha^{\vee})}}{q^{-d_{\alpha}( \rho,\alpha^{\vee})} - q^{d_{\alpha}(\rho,\alpha^{\vee})}}.$$

2.  $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V(\lambda) \neq 0$ if and only if $\lambda$ is $\ell$-regular..

3.  If $w \in W_{\ell}$ satisfies $w \cdot \lambda \in X^+$, then $$\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V(w \cdot \lambda)  = (-1)^{l(w)} \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V (\lambda)$$ for the length function $l$ on $W$.
:::

The dimension formula can be rewritten as a product $$\prod_{\alpha \in R^+} \frac{[(\lambda +\ \rho, \alpha)]}{[(\rho, \alpha)]}$$ where $[ \ ]$ denotes the $q$ number. We deduce from this formula the following corollary.

::: corollary
[]{#modulenullity label="modulenullity"} If $T(\lambda)=V(\lambda)$ is a simple tilting module, its nullity is equal to the number $n_\ell(\lambda)$ of positive roots $\alpha$ such that $\ell |(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* If $T(\lambda)$ is simple, the nullity is determined by $Tr(id_{T(\lambda)})$. ◻
:::

::: example
The Steinberg module has nullity $|R^+|$. Zeros occur in the dimension formula whenever $(\lambda + \rho, \beta)_{\ell}$ is divisible by $\ell$ for $\beta \in R^+$. For the weight of the Steinberg module this factor equals $(\ell \cdot \rho,\beta)$ and hence each factor for $\beta \in R^+$ is divisible by $\ell$. Since $<St>$ is the smallest thick ideal of $Tilt(U_q(\mathfrak{g}),{\mathbb Q}(q))$, any indecomposable module in $<St>$ has nullity $|R^+|$.
:::

::: example
In the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-case the only nontrivial thick ideal is the ideal of negligible objects (the $N_1$ case). For $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ we have two nontrivial thick ideals (see [@Lusztig-homepage] for pictures of the thick ideals in the rank 2 cases), namely $N_1$ and $<St> = N_3$.
:::

## The nullity in the modular case

The category $Tilt(G,k)$ is a symmetric monoidal category. The categorical dimension $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}(V)$ - also called the $p$-dimension - is the image of the vector space dimension $dim(V)$ under the homomorphism ${\mathbb Z}\to \mathbb{F}_p$, $1_{{\mathbb Z}} \mapsto 1_{\mathbb{F}_p}$.

::: proposition
([@Parshall-Wang Example 1.6, Theorem 3.3]) For $\lambda \in X^+$

1.  $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V(\lambda) \neq 0$ if and only if $\lambda$ is $p$-regular..

2.  If $w \in W_p$ satisfies $w \cdot \lambda \in X^+$, then $$\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V(w \cdot \lambda)  = (-1)^{l(w)} \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V (\lambda).$$
:::

If $T(\lambda)$ is irreducible, the nullity is already determined by $Tr(id_{T(\lambda)})$, i.e. the $p$-divisibility.

::: lemma
If $T(\lambda)$ is irreducible, then $T(\lambda)  \in N_k$ if and only if $p^k | dim T(\lambda)$.
:::

If $M$ is a module of $G$, we denote by $M^{[r]}$ the $G$-module which coincides with $M$ as a vector space on which the $G$-action is given by $$g.m\ =\ F^r(g)m, \hskip 3em m\in M,\quad g\in G,$$ where the action on the right hand side is the original action of $G$ on $M$, and $F$ is the Frobenius map, see [@Jantzen] for more details.

::: lemma
[]{#dimmod label="dimmod"} If $T(\lambda)=V(\lambda)$ is a simple module of the algebraic group $G$ in characteristic $p$, then the tilting module $T(\lambda^{(r)})$ is simple as well, where $\lambda^{(r)}=p^r \lambda + (p^r - 1)\rho$.
:::

$Proof.$ If $T(\lambda)$ is simple, it coincides with the Weyl module $V(\lambda)$ and the simple module $L(\lambda)$. A special case of Steinberg's tensor product theorem [@Jantzen Proposition 3.16] implies that

$$L((p^r - 1)\rho) \otimes L(\lambda)^{[r]} \cong L((p^r - 1)\rho + p^r \lambda),$$ which also implies isomorphisms between the corresponding Weyl and tilting modules. As the dimension of the Steinberg module $St_r=L((p^r - 1)\rho )=V((p^r - 1)\rho )$ is equal to $p^{r|R^+|}$ (see e.g. the inductive formula in [@Jantzen], Section 11.5), it follows from the Weyl dimension formula that then also $$\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}L((p^r - 1)\rho + p^r \lambda)=p^{r|R^+|}\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}L(\lambda)=\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V((p^r - 1)\rho + p^r \lambda),$$ as $L(\lambda)=V(\lambda)$ by assumption. The claim follows from this, as $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}L(\lambda^{(r)})
= \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}V(\lambda^{(r)})$.

::: corollary
\(a\) If $T(\lambda)=V(\lambda)$ is a simple tilting module, its nullity is equal to the number $n_p(\lambda)=\sum_{\alpha>0} k_\alpha$, where the summation goes over all positive roots, and $k_\alpha$ is the largest integer such that $p^{k_\alpha} |(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)$.

\(b\) If $T(\lambda)=V(\lambda)$ is simple and has nullity $n(\lambda)$, then the nullity $n(\lambda^{(r)})$ of $T(\lambda^{(r)})$ is equal to $n(\lambda)+r|R^+|$.
:::

Clearly $T(\lambda) \in N_k$ always implies $p^k | \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}(T(\lambda))$. See section [0.5.2](#sec-modularA1){reference-type="ref" reference="sec-modularA1"} section [0.5.3](#sec:p-div){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:p-div"} for examples that the converse doesn't hold.

### The Steinberg modules in the modular case {#steinberg-cells}

Recall that we fixed a maximal torus $T$ and a Borel subgroup $T \subset B \subset G$. We denote by $F$ the Frobenius morphism. The notation $G_rT$ denotes the scheme $(F^r)^{-1}(T)$ and $G_r = ker(F^r)$.

The Steinberg module $St_r = L((p^r-1)\rho)$ [@Jantzen II.3.18] in $Rep(G)$ is both injective and projective as a $G_rT$ and $G_r$-module [@Jantzen Proposition II.10.2]. For the dimension of $St_r$ we obtain $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}(St_r) = p^{rd}$, where $d$ is the number of positive roots. We consider for any $r \in \mathbb{N}$ the composition $\psi_r$ of monoidal functors $$\xymatrix{Tilt(G,k) \ar[r] & Rep(G) \ar[r]^{res} & Rep(G_r T) \ar[r] & \overline{Rep(G_r T)} }$$ where $res: Rep(G) \to Rep(G_r T)$ denotes the restriction functor and $\overline{Rep(G_r T)}$ is the stable category of $Rep(G_r T)$. We denote the thick ideals that form the kernel of the functors $\psi_r$ by $I_r$ and the tensor ideals by $\mathcal{I}_r$. Clearly this gives an descending chain of thick ideals $$I_1 \supseteq I_2 \supseteq I_3 \supseteq \ldots$$ By [@Coulembier Proposition 5.2.3] and its proof the thick ideal $I_r$ is generated by $St_r$. The key ingredient is the following lemma of Jantzen [@Jantzen Lemma II.E.8]:

::: lemma
Let $\lambda \in X(T)^+$ and $r \in \mathbb{N}_{>0}$. Then $T(\lambda)$ is projective as a $G_r T$-module if and only if $<\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}> \geq p^r - 1$ for all simple roots $\alpha$.
:::

Since $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}St_r = p^{r |R^+|}$ and $St_r$ is simple, we obtain

::: corollary
The nullity of $St_r$ is $r|R^+|$. In particular $<St_r> \subset N_{r|R^+|}$ .
:::

### The modular cases $A_1$ {#sec-modularA1}

For $SL(2)$ the $I_r$ are a complete list of thick ideals [@Coulembier Theorem 5.3.1]. A tilting module $T(m)$ is in $I_r$ if and only if $m \geq p^r - 1$. In particular $I_1 = N$. For $SL(2)$ we have $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}(St_r) = p^{r}$. Therefore $I_r$ is the $r$-th negligible ideal, and every thick ideal is $k$-negligible for some $k$.

It follows from Jensen [@Jensen-Phd Lemma 9.6] that for $p>2$ $$T(\lambda) \in N_k \text{ if and only if } p^k | \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}T(\lambda)$$ where $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}$ refers to the dimension of $T(\lambda)$ as a vector space. In other words, $N_k$ measures the $p$-divisibility of the dimension of $T(\lambda)$.

It is important to assume $p > 2$ here. Indeed the dimensions of the first tilting modules in the $p = 2$ case are $$\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}T(0) = 1, \ \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}T(1) = St_1 = 2, \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}T(2) = 4, \ \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}T(3) = St_3 = 4.$$

Although $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}T(2) = 4$, it is not in $N_2$. Over ${\mathbb Z}_2$ we have $Tr(id_{T(2)}) = 4$, but we can write $T(2) \cong T(1) \otimes T(1)$. Hence there is an endomorphism $f$ of $T(2)$ which permutes the two factors. It is easy to see that $Tr(f) = 2$, hence the trace is not always contained in $(2)^2$ and so $T(2) \notin N_2$.

### $p$-divisibility {#sec:p-div}

The $SL(2)$-case seems to suggest that indeed we have $T(\lambda) \in N_k$ if and only if $p^k | \ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}T(\lambda)$ provided $p > h$ (the Coxeter number). This is false. The following example was communicated to us by Thorge Jensen. For $Sp(4)$ and $p=11$ consider the following tilting modules (weights are expressed via fundamental weights)

$$\begin{tabular}{|c|c|} $T(\lambda)$ & $\ensuremath{\operatorname{dim}}$ \\
\hline
$T(1,18)$ & $7018$ \\
$T(1,20)$ & $10648$ \\
$T(0,22)$ & $8954$ \\
$T(9,20)$ & $56870$
\end{tabular}$$

By computations of Jensen these tilting modules all belong to the same $p$-cell (see Section [\[sec:combi\]](#sec:combi){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:combi"}), but their $p$-valuation is not constant. All these modules belong to $N_2$ but the dimension of $T(1,20)$ is divisible by $11^3$. Therefore the nullity of a $T(\lambda)$ is not simply given by the $p$-divisibility.
