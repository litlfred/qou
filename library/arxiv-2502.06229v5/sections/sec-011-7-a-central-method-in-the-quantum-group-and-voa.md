A central method of proof for parts (b), (c), and (d) is the use of the fundamental
generating representation V and its properties, following primarily the work of Wenzl.
The paper [99] plays a main role especially for the proof of (b), (c). For part (d) we shall
later describe results and related works in the literature on which our proof is based.
Parts (b), (c) of Theorem 3.1 gives coincidence of the two braided tensor structures for
all Lie types (two pairs of associativity morphisms together with their braiding morphisms
satisfying the two hexagonal diagrams) on a specific collection of their variables, in the
two settings of quantum groups and vertex operator algebras. These collections do not
generate additively all the variables of the braiding morphisms or associativity morphisms,
because they do not contain all the irreducible objects.
Coincidence of the associativity morphisms and braiding morphisms on the special ob-
jects is verified in a direct way, relying on similar structural properties of fusion of two
corresponding generating representations in the two cases. In the quantum group case,
fusion arises in the setting of non-semisimple representations of Uq(g) considered in [99],
while in the vertex operator algebra case fusion arises in a semisimple setting. To compare
the braiding and associativity morphisms on the special variables, we use a Drinfeld twist
that we construct trivialising, in a sense, the non-trivial unitary structure in the setting of
representations of quantum groups at roots of unity to that of the affine vertex operator
algebra. This idea emerges in the construction and analysis of the unitary structure of the
fusion category of quantum groups at roots of unity of [99].
The twist takes one unitary structure of a special tensor product arising in the quantum
group setting from [99] to the classical unitary structure corresponding to the compact
real form of g (and this in turn remarkably leads to the construction of a unitary tensor
structure on the module category of the vertex operator algebra Vgk in a uniform way).
This implies that the twist leaves the corresponding structural properties invariant on these
special variables. To see this, we work on the side of the vertex operator algebras and we
compare with the work by Frenkel and Zhu [34]. An abstract concept describing these
common structural properties of the associativity morphisms is introduced in Sect. 9 with
the name of a V-pre-associator of CFT type. The name is motivated by our understanding
of the structure of Huang-Lepowsky associativity morphisms.

Direct verification of coincidence of the two braided tensor structures on all the irre-
ducible objects is more complicated due to multiplicity of fusion and non-semisimplicity
of Uq(g). To this aim rather than pursuing a closer analogy with the original Drinfeld-
Kohno theorem for all objects, we develop two uniqueness theorems, Theorem 8.13 and
its generalization Theorem 8.19 in [10]. This generalization is made necessary by the ac-
tual conclusions reached in our work in the applicative sections on the direct comparison
between special cases of the two associativity morphisms and their corresponding braided
symmetries. Thus the uniqueness theorem 8.19 is formulated with suitable assumptions,
where in the application the abstract notion of V-pre-associator of CFT-type for the fun-
damental representation V is used in a central way both on the side of quantum groups
and that of vertex operator algebras to verify coincidence of these special braid and as-
sociativity morphisms, after application of our Drinfeld-Kohno theorem. But this brief
description of Theorems 8.13 and Theorem 8.19 put together with coincidence of the two
braided tensor structures on special collections of objects proved in part (b), (c), is still
not enough to describe our method of proof to complete the proof of part (d) and with it
the proof of Theorem 3.1.
We need more that we describe in the following sections. The first next step is a some-
what complicated cohomological reduction work done in Theorems 8.13 and 8.19, where we
use cohomology of the pentagon equation and its interplay with the hexagonal equations
defining the braided symmetry for a general semisimple braided tensor category endowed
with a generating object.
The cohomological reduction is useful to reduce the problem to the last step, which
uncovers a central duality property between the fundamental representation of the quantum
group and corresponding representations of the braid group in the intertwining spaces of
the fundamental representation, in the various Lie types. The first example of this is Schur-
Weyl duality in the type A case, and works in the literature that we shall recall greatly
extend this first classical example to to other Lie types and to the quantum cases. We
shall come back to this, but let us anticipate similarities and differences with the original
Drinfeld-Kohno theorem, useful for the proof of (b), (c), that will also useful to clarify how
we were led to uncover the key role of duality of the fundamental representation V , for the
proof of the last step (d).
