Theorem 1.1. Let g be a complex simple Lie algebra and k a positive integer, the level. Let
q = e
iπ
d(k+h∨), with h∨the dual Coxeter number and d the ratio of the square lengths of the
long to the short root. Then the unitary coboundary weak Hopf algebra AW(g, q) induces a
rigid braided tensor category structure on the module category of the affine vertex operator
algebra Vgk.
Furthermore we construct, via the Drinfeld twist method, a braided tensor equivalence
between this category and the modular quantum group fusion category C(g, q) for all Lie
types.
Regarding the comparison with the Huang-Lepowsky ribbon braided tensor structure:
(1) For the Lie types A, B, C, D, G2 we obtain a complete identification.

(2) For the remaining exceptional Lie types we obtain the identification for the tensor
product structure, the ribbon structure, and those braiding and associativity axioms
for morphisms where one or two variables are the fundamental representation V ,
respectively.
The previous result is stated in greater detail as Theorem 3.1. Our starting point for this
theorem was in the context of constructive rigorous conformal field theory, in the setting of
operator algebras. The main references important for us to start to set up the connection
between quantum groups and affine vertex operator algebras, are the work of Wenzl [99],
and the work by Wassermann [98] in the setting of affine Lie algebras. A key concept is
played by the notion of primary field, and its initial term, which determines the primary
field uniquely, as shown by Tsuchiya and Kanie [95]. We use this fact in a fundamental
way to connect quantum group fusion category to representations of the Zhu algebra and
their special tensor products with Wenzl fundamental representation, see Sect. 35 in [10].
Most importantly, we describe a connection between Wenzl and Wassermann work via
Wenzl quantisation curve, and building on their work in [10] we obtain Theorems 2.1, 2.2,
Cor. 2.3, and Theorem 2.4 which compares with the tensor product structure by Huang
and Lepowsky, to which we refer the reader also for important historical references. The
connection from affine Lie algebra setting to Huang-Lepowsky tensor product theory may
be found in Sections 33–38 in [10].
A main idea that we follow is to consider the fundamental representation V of g for
each Lie type as in [99] where the author explains its generating property and good fusion
structure with respect to tensor products with an arbitrary irreducible representation al-
lowing a well-defined truncation procedure to obtain non-negligible objects, with the aim
of studying unitarizability of the fusion category.
As anticipated in the abstract, the restriction to the mentioned Lie types is due to the
fact that we need to use a key property of the theory of quantum groups, namely the
extension to the quantum case of generalized Schur-Weyl duality. Currently, this property
is known to hold for these Lie types. This is part (d) of the same theorem.
The final stage of the proof, detailed in Sect. 9–11, relies on a process of cohomological
reduction and heavily utilizes extensions of quantum Schur-Weyl duality. By explicitly
exploiting the generating property of the braid group representation within the centralizer
algebras of the truncated tensor powers of the fundamental representation, we achieve the
direct equivalence with Huang-Lepowsky braided tensor structure of Rep(Vgk) necessary
to solve Huang’s problem for the classical and G2 Lie types.
The paper is organized as follows. In Sect. 2 we give the main definition of unitary
coboundary weak Hopf algebra and recall the construction of AW(g, q). In Sect. 3 we state
the main result, Theorem 3.1. Sects 4–7 explain the main ideas to prove parts (a)–(d) of
this result. We give details for the completion of the proof of part (d) in Sects 9–11. Sect.
12 concerns connection with Doplicher-Roberts theorem regarding the role of generalized
Schur-Weyl duality. Sect. 13 concerns future developments. We put in the Appendix
historical remarks on the proof of rigidity in module categories of affine Lie algebras and
affine vertex operator algebras at positive integer level.
