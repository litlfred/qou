We also remark that Schur-Weyl duality plays an important role in the abstract duality
theory for compact groups by Doplicher and Roberts. It is thanks to the fact that the usual
permutation symmetry of the category of Hilbert spaces represents all the the intertwining
operators between tensor powers of the defining representation of the unitary groups SU(d),
that their construction of the compact gauge group associated to the category of localized
endomorphisms of the observable algebras in algebraic quantum field theory takes place,
as a closed subgroup G ⊆SU(d).
If one regards affine vertex operator algebras at positive integer levels as models cor-
responding to the case of a gauge group given by the simple compact Lie group SU(d)
in Doplicher-Roberts theory of quantum gauge fields (i.e.versus the case of gauge group
G given by any compact Lie group (a proper closed subgroup of SU(d)), then from this
viewpoint one may expect that duality properties for the fundamental representation V
of a quantum group should play a central role in our approach to FKL theorem. The
analogue of permutation symmetry in our case is its generalization to the braid group
symmetry, and therefore it is natural to consider all the Lie types for which braid group
duality is known to hold for the generating representation V , as a first step towards a
Doplicher-Roberts reconstruction of a field algebra from the local observable algebras in
low dimensional quantum field theory.
It may therefore be somewhat surprising that the need to use such an important property
as generalizations of Schur-Weyl duality to Lie types beyond A and to the quantum case,

has not been noted before as a key property to understand the proof of Theorem 3.1, in

Perhaps similarly surprising is the fact that in our approach we do not invoke modularity.
On the other hand by a private discussion with M. Yamashita, we learned that modularity
is used in their recent work on classification of fusion categories with fusion rules of type
BCD [39].
Braid group duality for the fundamental representation V was the relevant observation
helpful to prove part (d) of Theorem 3.1 with respect to the previous parts of the same
theorem, which could reach the equivalence only partially as stated in the previous parts
(a)–(c). This property was indeed used to apply Theorems 8.13 and 8.19 in [10] and reach
the full braided tensor equivalence for the stated Lie types. We refer the reader to the
historical paper by V. F. R. Jones [61] for a general first introduction to the origin of
quantum groups as solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation.
We find it remarkable the deep application that braid group duality has to quantum
field theory. This application led us to evaluate even more the relevance of the quantum
group viewpoint than as noted up to that point in our study.
We also would like to point out that our results of the categorical characterization of the
class of unitary coboundary weak Hopf algebras that we obtain in Sect. 25, 26, 28 in [10]
extends the notion of symmetric functors that plays a key role in Doplicher-Roberts theory
[20], [21]. In Sect. 28 we explain the relation with the more familiar class of hermitian
ribbon structures.
