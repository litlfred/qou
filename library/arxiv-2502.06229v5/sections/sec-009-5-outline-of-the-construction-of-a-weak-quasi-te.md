Outline of the construction of a weak quasi-tensor structure on the
Zhu algebra A(Vgk) via Wenzl quantisation isomorphism and Drinfeld
twist method.
Let (C, ⊗, ι, α) be a semisimple complex tensor category (possibly braided, rigid, ribbon)
and let
F : C →Vec
be a faithful linear functor. There are two natural ways to make the representation category
Rep(A) of A = Nat0(F) into a tensor category (or a category with the same type of
structure as C) equivalent to C with all the structure.
The first way is traditionally referred to as Tannaka-Krein duality. One needs a (weak,
quasi-) tensor structure (F, G) on F, and then apply Theorem 7.6 in [10] to obtain a
(weak, quasi)-bialgebra structure on A = Nat0(F) (with extra structure) with the desired
equivalence property. The (weak quasi-) tensor structure
(F, F, G) : C →Vec
on a given functor, may be easily identified in some cases, e.g. representation categories of
groups, simple complex Lie algebras, or quantum groups or more generally whenever one
has a tensor category that naturally admits a tensor functor to Vec, as is the example of
the compact quantum groups of Woronowicz. In these cases, Tannakian constructions are
ubiquitous.
But in other cases the structure (F, G) needs to be constructed, and in this case the
situation is perhaps similar to the problem met by Doplicher and Roberts and Deligne
in their abstract duality theories. In Doplicher-Roberts duality for compact groups, the
embedding functor itself F needed to be constructed from an abstract symmetric strict
tensor category satisfying suitable conditions, and the tensor structure of F was trivial.
The Tannakian construction endows A with an associator which depends on both the as-
sociativity morphisms α of C and the structure (F, G), following Majid version for Drinfeld
quasi-Hopf algebras. Thus Tannaka-Krein duality is particularly useful in cases where the
associativity morphisms α are not too complicated, in that in this case it puts emphasis on
F and G. This may allow to understand most of the associativity property of the category
by understanding F and G.
Fusion categories that arise from quantum groups at roots of unity, by the work of many
authors, among them Drinfeld, Jimbo, Lusztig, Andersen, Wenzl, are realized in a natural
way as strict fusion categories. However, in this case tensor functors to Vec do not exist.
A natural linear functor has been naturally constructed in [99] for the roots of unity of
interest for the aims of [10], i.e. those that are related to the affine Lie algebras at positive
integer levels.
Tensor structures on Wenzl functor F do not exist in general, and one has to construct
more general structures, from the inner structure of the category, in order to reduce to the
weak quasi-bialgebra method.
A suitable notion of weak Hopf algebra property is a natural concept to look for, by the
analogy with Drinfeld-Kohno theorem that first motivated the work of physicists Mack and
17
Schomerus in [70]. The adjective weak refers to the need of considering truncated fusion
rules that respect fusion rules of physical models, and rule out representations of Uq(g) at
roots of unity of vanishing quantum dimension. They accordingly introduced the notion of
weak analogues of Drinfeld quasi-Hopf algebras, and called them weak quasi-Hopf algebras,
a key concept that we rely on. They constructed a semisimple weak quasi-Hopf algebra
associated to a suitable model in conformal field theory for sl2 and truncated fusion rules.
However, the weak Hopf property was overlooked by them.
We introduce a notion of weak Hopf algebra in [10]. This property was first noticed in
[9] in the type A.
In Sect. 32 we shall construct a weak tensor structure on Wenzl functor, and derive
a weak Hopf finite dimensional C∗-algebra AW(g, q) associated to fusion categories of
quantum groups at roots of unity for all Lie types, with extra structure, that is the braiding,
unitary structures given by a Drinfeld coboundary matrix, also rigid and ribbon, extending
the work of [9] for slN in turn extending and expanding the work by Mack and Schomerus
for sl2 [70].
We use the weak Hopf algebra property of AW(g, q) as a replacement of the Hopf algebra
property of Uh(g) in Drinfeld-Kohno theorem. This property indeed plays a crucial role
in our work, but in a different way as compared to Drinfeld’s proof of Drinfeld-Kohno
theorem.
In our case, we use the weak Hopf property jointly with fusion and duality
properties of a fundamental generating representation V in the application, to study the
same aim as Drinfeld, comparison of the braided symmetries and associativity morphisms
in the two settings of fusion categories of quantum groups at roots of unity and of module
categories of vertex operator algebras.
The unitary aspects of our weak Hopf algebras AW(g, q) are based on the results of [99],
on the use of Drinfeld coboundary matrix.
The weak tensor property as compared to the weak quasi-tensor property may prove
useful for further constructions of the quantum fields from the observables, with the aim of
extending the work of Doplicher and Roberts in high dimensional algebraic quantum field
theory, as compared to previous attempts in the literature on low dimensional algebraic
quantum field theory, that suffered the difficulties due to a possibly more complicated
associator.
