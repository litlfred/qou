Drinfeld-Kohno Theorem and Theorem 29.4 in [10]
We next outline general ideas that make our strategy possible, useful to prove part (b),
(c) of Theorem 3.1.
As already explained, the starting point of our work is the paper [99]. In that paper, a
fundamental generating representation V is described for each simple Lie algebra g, and
it is used for the construction of the unitary structure in the associated quantum group
fusion category. In our approach we follow the work of Wenzl on the use of the generating
representation V .
21
Secondly, in our approach the notion of coboundary matrix first introduced by Drinfeld
in his work on Drinfeld-Kohno theorem for quasi-Hopf algebras in a formal setting, plays
a major two-fold role.
In our case we adopt the idea of Drinfeld coboundary that we
describe for complex weak quasi-Hopf algebras in an analytic setting. This general theory
is considered in Sections 27, 28, 29, 30.
On one hand the coboundary matrix in an analytic setting induces a unitary structure
which shows striking differences with unitary structures of usual Hopf C∗-algebras, such
as for example the compact quantum groups, where the ∗-involution commutes with the
coproduct. Drinfeld original coboundary is used in [99] in the setting of representations of
the quantized universal enveloping algebra Uq(g) with q a suitable type of root of unity,
to construct the unitary braided tensor structure of the associated fusion category. This
already gives a striking connection between unitary structures and ribbon braided tensor
equivalences established by Drinfeld in Drinfeld-Kohno theorem, for different categories.
Similarities with the original Drinfeld-Kohno theorem regard the treatment of special
braiding morphisms, that is based as in Drinfeld proof on a twist given by a square root
of the Drinfeld coboundary matrix. This approach is particularly useful for braiding on
tensor products of the form Vλ ⊗V or V ⊗Vλ, where multiplicity-free and structural fusion
properties of V allow for a direct verification of the equivalence between the two braided
symmetries on these objects. Moreover in our case on special triples (or n-tuples) where two
(or consecutive n −1-tuples) coordinates are fixed to V , verification of the equivalence of
the two associativity morphisms similarly put in a nutshell reduces to the same properties
of V . We do not use the KZ equation to this aim, or in any part of our work, other than
the fact that the KZ equation was used by Drinfeld, Kazhdan and Lusztig, and by Huang
and Lepowsky in the setting of affine vertex operator algebras, to construct the structure
of a braided tensor category.
Similarities with the original Drinfeld-Kohno theorem also concern the identification
method between the two associativity morphisms, in our case the twisted structure of that
arising from quantum groups and that coming from Huang Lepowsky tensor structure both
on the Zhu algebra. In Proposition 3.16 of [24], Drinfeld identifies the most difficult parts
of the corresponding structures for Drinfeld category using his uniqueness Theorem 3.15
based on cohomology of the same paper. In our case, we similarly develop a uniqueness
theorem but for the associativity morphisms which we verify of our weak Hopf algebras
AW(g, q) and with very different methods, based on the generating representation V for
which we develop cohomological reduction Theorem 8.19 in [10] and extension to quantum
case and different Lie types of Schur-Weyl duality. We discuss this more in the next two
sections.
