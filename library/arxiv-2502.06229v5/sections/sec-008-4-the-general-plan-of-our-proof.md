Part (a) of Theorem 3.1 follows from the application to Zhu’s functor of the general con-
struction. Let us consider a (ribbon, braided) semsimple tensor category C together with
a discrete complex algebra A (a direct sum of full matrix algebras), a linear equivalence
C →Rep(A) and an inverse linear equivalence Rep(A) →C. We wish to use these equiv-
alences to transfer all the structure of C to Rep(A), and make it into a (ribbon, braided)
tensor category in such a way that the given equivalences become (ribbon, braided) tensor
equivalences. The definition of the structure on Rep(A) is very natural and verification
of the axioms of (ribbon, braided) tensor category and the natural transformations on
the equivalences and the needed properties is straightforward. The special case of mak-
ing Rep(A(Vgk)) into a ribbon braided tensor category from Huang-Lepowsky structure
on Rep(Vgk) corresponds to pass from the structure on a category of infinite dimensional
modules to the category of their zero modes. We have explicitly described this construction
in Sect. 37 in [10]. This construction was also considered in [72]. We have used a similar
construction, in the reverse direction, and for a different purpose in Theorems 15.6 and
22.10 of [10].
For part (b), we follow a suitable Drinfeld twist method originally used by Drinfeld for
Drinfeld-Kohno theorem [22], [24], [25], and in our case for the stated categories. This
step splits in various parts. The first main step is the construction of the class of finite
dimensional unitary coboundary weak Hopf C∗-algebras AW(g, q) recalled in Theorem 2.4
for all the Lie types and positive integer levels. This construction extends with different
methods the previos examples of [9] in the type A case. The unitary coboundary structure
of AW(g, q) naturally arises from the unitary structure of the fusion category of quantum
groups at roots of unity, associated to the specific linear fiber functor W : C(g, q) →Vec,
that emerges from the work of Wenzl [99].
The second main step to prove (b) is the
construction of the Drinfeld twist F defined as a suitable square root of R. A third step

consists in the development of an abstract analogue of Drinfeld-Kohno theorem 29.4 in
[10], that we apply to our twist composed with a quantization isomorphism described in
[99] for the unitary structure of irreducible respresentations of the fusion category, between
AW(g, q) and a corresponding quotient of U(g) that identifies with the Zhu algebra A(Vgk).
By application of our analogue of Drinfeld-Kohno theorem we obtain the structure of a
unitary coboundary weak quasi-Hopf algebra on the Zhu algebra A(Vgk) associated to a
corresponding affine vertex operator algebra Vgk. Thus a natural weak quasitensor struc-
ture defined by fusion intertwiners with respect to the action of g compatible with the
fusion rules of modules of the vertex operator algebra. This induces the structure of a
ribbon braided tensor category on the module category of Vgk, via transport from the the
Zhu algebra A(Vgk). In particular, we get an independent construction of the associativ-
ity morphisms and proof of rigidity of Rep(Vgk), directly arising from the quantum group
fusion category via the equivalence induced by the twist.
By construction, this structure is ribbon braided tensor equivalent to the structure
arising from the quantum group fusion category. The equivalence is explicitly described
by a Drinfeld twist analogous to the Drinfeld twist appearing in Drinfeld’s first proof of
Drinfeld-Kohno theorem [24]. In our case, the unitary structure of the fusion category of
quantum groups at roots of unity in [99] is used in the construction of the Drinfeld twist
of AW(g, q).
Part (c) initiates the comparison between Huang-Lepowsky braided tensor structure
on RepHL(Vgk) and our structure on RepQG(A(Vgk)) and the axioms of braided tensor
equivalence is done in [10] by a direct comparison for the special braiding morphisms
and the special associativity morphisms listed in (c) on the Zhu algebra, passing through
(a). Up to this point we do not need any knowledge on the space of intertwiners in the
two categories, the linear equivalence is an isomorphism between any two corresponding
morphism spaces by the Drinfeld twist construction.
The necessary general theory of semisimple weak quasi-Hopf algebras, weak Hopf al-
