The role of braid group duality for the generating object in
determining the full braided tensor structure for the proof of (d).
The uniqueness Theorems 8.13 and 8.19 both apply under a central duality property of
the fundamental representation V with respect to the braid group representation in the
quantum group tensor category, which is at the core of quantum group representation
theory. The first instance of this property is Schur-Weyl duality for the general linear
group. This duality says that the algebra of intertwining operators, from a tensor power
of the vector representation V of the general linear group to itself (also called centralizer
algebras for the tensor powers of the vector representation) is generated by permutation
operators [104].
The generalization of Schur-Weyl duality to Lie types beyond A in classical Lie groups
and quantum groups is an enormous subject developed by many authors, extending from
the classical more familiar cases of type A and vector representation to other Lie types
beyond A, with respect to a specified generating representation V , and to the quantum
group case.
We need the property that the centralizer algebras (V n, V n) with respect to the action
of the Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum group Uq(g) are generated by the natural representation
of the braid group induced by the R-matrix. In our case q is a root of unity as usually
assumed corresponding to the unitary cases of the associated fusion category, specified in
[99], [92], see also Def. 19.1, 20.2 in [10]. The tensor powers V n appearing in our setting
are canonical choices of truncations of the full tensor powers of the quantum group Uq(g)
obtained by induction over n.

The construction of the iterated truncations V n of Uq(g) at the root of unity is explained
in [99] and is the starting point to construct our weak Hopf algebras in Sect. 32. The duality
property between the truncated tensor powers V n of the fundamental representation and
the representation of the braid group is needed as a further property to complete the proof
of FKL theorem on coincidence between associativity morphisms and braiding morphisms
on all pairs and triples and is used in our cohomological theorems 8.13 and 8.19. These
theorems complete with cohomogical arguments what was proved up to that point in our
work, coincidence of these structure maps on pairs and triples where one or two coordinated
are fixed to the fundamental representation V . To do so, the way how our weak Hopf
algebras are described in the application to the quantum group fusion category is also
used.
For all the Lie types, the truncated tensor powers V n are inductively defined over n
by a canonical decomposition, which is into irreducible components for g̸ = E8, of a full
tensor product of representations Vλ ⊗V of Uq(g), with Vλ an arbitrary irreducible of
the semisimplified fusion category. Then one selects irreducible summands with nonzero
quantum dimension. The fusion of the fundamental representation of E8 is described in
[99] as well, and is more delicate.
Returning to the question of whether the braid group representation generates the cen-
tralizer algebras (V n, V n) for the truncated tensor powers of Uq(g) at the root of unity,
this question can be studied in the same way as for the case of a corresponding summand
with q not a root of unity, as in the truncated summand the root of unity property is not
visible. This is explained among other things in Prop. 2.4 in [99], see also Prop 30.2 in [10]
for the same result with a slightly more detailed proof, where an important role is played
by the continuous curve described in that proposition, that passes through generic values
of q. See also Section 3.5 in [99] for the special tensor products Vλ ⊗V of representations of
Uq(g). Thus it suffices to have the duality property between the full algebras (V ⊗n, V ⊗n)
of Uq(g) when q is not a root of unity.
We recall that the fundamental representation V are listed in Sect. 3.5 in [99] for all the
Lie types, and in particular are given by
- The vector representation for Lie types A and C,
- The spinor representation for Lie types B and D. In particular V is the direct sum
of the two spinor representations for type Dn with n even.
- The 7-dimensional representation for Lie types G2.
The Lie types A and C for the vector representation are well known. If q is not a root
of unity, the centralizer algebras (V ⊗n, V ⊗n) in the type A case are described by Hecke
algebras, a quotient of the complex braid group algebra by well known generators and
relations. The first result in this direction is due to Jimbo [59]. For the Lie types B, C,
D, when V is the vector representation, Murakami and Birman and Wenzl introduced and
studied what are known as the BMW algebras [77], [6]. A nice exposition of the Hecke
and BMW algebras may be found in the book by Goodman, de la Harpe and Jones [38].

In the application of Theorems 8.13 and 8.19, we work on the side of the quantum group
fusion category and by the results above, our assumptions on braid group duality for the
fundamental representation of our weak Hopf algebras AW(g, q) constructed in Sect. 31
in [10] are verified for the defining structure arising from Tannakian construction on the
quantum group fusion category C(g, q)) and Wenzl functor.
