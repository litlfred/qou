Main tools to prove (d): cohomological reduction of a braided
tensor structure of a category with a generating object V to braid
group duality of V
Theorem 8.13 and Theorem 8.19 show uniqueness of the associativity morphisms (and also
braiding morphisms in the latter case) under some assumptions. These assumptions all
aim to determine the associativity morphisms uniquely via data from the braiding, but
lead to analysis of a different nature in the application.
The first assumption requires knowledge that in our application, via the methods pre-
viously alluded to (i.e. Wenzl isomorphism given by a continuous curve describing quanti-
zation of all the irreducible representations of the fusion category [99] and recalled also in
[10], combined with application of our Drinfeld twist) one gets a braided symmetry coin-
ciding, for all pairs of objects, with the braided symmetry associated to Huang-Lepowsky
braided tensor category structure.
Moreover in Theorem 8.13 we require that the associativity morphisms coincide on the
special triples. These assumptions allow a cohomological analysis on the braided symmetry
of a tensor category with a generating object. This analysis does not suffice to complete
the proof Theorem 8.13. Before describing the further key property of the fundamental
representation that we need, we add some more comments on our methods regarding this
part.
In the application on the other hand, while as discussed in the previous parts we reach
coincidence of associativity morphisms on special triples thanks to the structure of Huang-
Lepowsky associativity morphisms and Drinfeld twist method, regarding the braiding mor-
phisms we limit ourselves to verify the coincidence of the two braided symmetries on the
special pairs of objects where one of the variables is fixed to V . These special braiding
morphisms generate all the other braiding morphisms, but the generating property of these
special morphisms depends also on the associativity morphisms. This poses the need of a
more detailed analysis to obtain a uniqueness theorem for both braiding and associativity
morphisms based on knowledge of their coincidence on special variables that in our ap-
proach we can verify directly. This is the aim of Theorem 8.19, with respect to Theorem
8.13, which on the other hand is useful to present the main ideas of how a full braided
symmetry determines the associativity morphisms in case of a generating representation.
The assumptions on coincidence of the associativity morphisms and braided symmetry
as required in Theorem 8.19 are verified in the proof of Theorem 3.1 on the side of vertex
operator algebras. For the associativity morphisms, methods are similar to those used to

The proof of (d) of Theorem 3.1 is reduced to the proof of (c) and application of The-
orem 8.19. More in detail, for part (d) we use the fact that the special variables of the
associativity morphisms and braided symmetry generate all the triples multiplicatively,
in the sense that all the components except one in the variables of the associativity and
braiding morphisms are taken by the generating representation in each Lie type. In the
proof of Theorem 8.19, and the antecedent Theorems 8.13, we explain the generating prop-
erty of these special values of the associativity and braiding morphisms with the pentagon
equation of associativity morphisms and the hexagonal equations of a braided symmetry
in a semisimple tensor category with a generating object but, as anticipated, another im-
portant property concerning the braid group representations in the category is needed, to
determine the associativity morphisms uniquely.
