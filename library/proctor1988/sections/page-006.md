The odd symplectic groups are not semisimple. So the standard highest
weight theory of irreducible finite dimensional representations of simple Lie
algebras does not apply. But there is another (and largely forgotten) view of
representations of classical groups which is fruitful for the odd symplectic groups.
In The Classical Groups Weyl realized all irreducible finite dimensional represen-
tations of SLy, Sp2,, and Oy (+simply connected Oy) as irreducible trace free
tensor representations. Our main result (Theorem 2.1 in the case Sp2,4 1 =Sp2n,1)
describes the weights of indecomposable trace free tensor representations of
Sp2n+1 With Young tableaux. From this the most interesting result (Theorem
2.2) of this paper can be easily obtained: We give a character formula for these
tensor representations of Sp,,,-, which looks very much like the Weyl character
formulas for Sp.,, SOont1> and SO,,. The denominator of this formula can
be written as a product over the positive roots of the system BC,,_,. In fact,
in a certain sense (Section 2), the weights of the adjoint representation of Sp2,-1
form the root system BC, _,. So the groups Sp,,,-, can be thought of as “miss-
ing” classical Lie groups which account for the “leftover” non-reduced root
systems BC, _,.

I.M. Gelfand and A.V. Zelevinsky independently formulated a different defi-
nition [GeZv] of odd symplectic group at about the same time (1983) or slightly
before the definition above was made. Although their definition was made in
the same spirit as ours (to fill a gap in a framework established by existing
classical groups), the resulting group is different. Altogether there are four candi-
dates for the name “odd symplectic group”. The competing definitions are given
at the end of the paper in Section 9.
