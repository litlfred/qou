§ 1. Introduction 
The unification of Einstein's general relativity with quantum field theory is a difficult 
problem, which physicists have so far failed to solve. This is because Einstein gravity 
theory is non-renormalizable in the usual perturbation theoretical sense. If we quantize 
general relativity, the structure of space-time itself is subject to quantum fluctuation, so 
that the usual concepts in the field theory, e.g., the particle, energy, etc., will cease to be 
well-defined. 
Recently, lattice gauge theory has enabled non-perturbative study of non-abelian 
gauge theory, which shows quark confinement in the strong coupling region where the 
usual perturbation technique is not applicable. This is beyond the reach of the usual 
perturbative technique and shows that there is a phenomena which does not follow from 
perturbation theory. 
In traditional quantum gravity approach, only small deviation from some assumed 
back ground has been treated. However, in this method the essential features of gravity 
theory will not appear. Non-perturbative studies of quantum gravity may be of funda-
mental importance. As one method of which, it is expected to be efficient to study 
quantum gravity on a lattice.

In §2, we present the lattice action of Einstein-Cartan gravity theory. It is shown that· 
the naive continuum limit of the lattice action is, in fact, the action of the Einstein-Cartan 
gravity theory in the continuum. In §3, we show that our lattice action has reflection 
positivity, which implies the existence of positive Hilbert space and positive Hamiltonian 
from the Osterwalder-Schrader construction. In §4, we perform cluster expansion and 
show that for sufficiently large value of the coupling constant the infinite volume limit 
exists. The final section is devoted to the discussion on the relation between the Rieman-
nian structure in classical general relativity and our formulation of quantum gravity on 
a flat lattice.
