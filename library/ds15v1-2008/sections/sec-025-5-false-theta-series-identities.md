Notice that although f(a, b) = f(b, a), in general, Ψ(a, b)̸ = Ψ(b, a).
L.J. Rogers [37] studied q-series expansions for many instances of f(±qα, ±qβ) and
Ψ(±qα, ±qβ), which he called theta (resp. false theta) series of order (α + β)/2.
A false theta series identity for the series Ψ(±qα, ±qβ) arises from the same Bailey pair
as the Rogers-Ramanujan type identity with product f(±qα, ±qβ)/ϕ(−q). A designation
of the form (F. n) means that the identity is the false theta analog of (S. n), the nth
identity in Slater’s list [41]. (Slater did not record any false theta function identities.)
