Separating QED and QCD effects
Here we show how to separate the electromagnetic and strong contributions in an isospin splitting, i.e.
∆MX = ∆QEDMX + ∆QCDMX,
where the ﬁrst and second terms are proportional to α and δm, respectively. It is clear, that it is sufﬁcient to
decompose the kaon mass squared difference,

since the separation for the other splittings can be obtained from this decomposition through Eq. (S37). Any
such decomposition has an ambiguity of O (αδm), which is NLO in isospin breaking, i.e. one order higher than
the one at which we work. This arises from the scheme dependence in the deﬁnition of δm. In (10) we proposed
a separation based on the difference of connected meson masses, ∆M 2 = M 2
¯dd−M 2
¯uu. Using chiral perturbation
theory (59), ∆M 2 can be shown to be proportional to δm up to corrections of O (α2, αδm, (δm)2, αmud), which
are also NLO if we make the very reasonable assumption that O (mud) ∼O (δm). Though this technique is
sufﬁcient for the electroquenched approximation used in previous work, it has a drawback here, in that these
connected mesons are not in the spectrum of the full theory. One could use this approach in a partially quenched
framework, but we want to avoid this in the present ab initio setup.
Here we show that, within the precision of our calculation, an alternative separation can be applied. The
method is based only on observed particles. Since the Σ+ and Σ−have the same charge squared and the same
spin, their electromagnetic self-energies are identical at O (α), if these particles are assumed to be point-like.
In that case, ∆MΣ = MΣ−−MΣ+ comes purely from δm. Because these particles are composite, there are
corrections to this result. We now argue that they are no larger than the 0.2 MeV overall uncertainty on our de-
termination of ∆MΣ. In the seminal quark mass review (63), Gasser and Leutwyler ﬁnd ∆MΣ = 0.17(30) MeV
by evaluating the Cottingham formula (for a recent discussion see (64)). Using ∆M 2 = 0 to deﬁne the electro-
magnetic contribution to splittings we found ∆QEDMΣ = −0.08(12)(34) MeV in our electroquenched lattice
computation (10), which is consistent with 0, albeit within relatively large uncertainties. Repeating this analy-
sis in the fully unquenched theory we ﬁnd ∆QEDMΣ = 0.18(12)(6) MeV, again consistent with 0 within a little
more than one standard deviation. All of these determinations provide quantitative evidence that our proposed
QCD-QED separation convention, i.e. using
∆QEDMΣ = 0,
is adequate within the precision of the present paper. This choice establishes a ﬁrst benchmark against which
future results can be compared. It can certainly be reﬁned when calculations, such as the one presented here,
become more precise.
