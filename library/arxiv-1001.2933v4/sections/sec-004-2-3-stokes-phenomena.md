2.3 Stokes Phenomena
Once one expresses the integration cycle C in terms of critical point cycles for real values
of the Chern-Simons coupling constant s (or of the appropriate parameters in the other
examples) via a formula C =
P
σ
nσJσ, the next step is to make an analytic continuation
in s. There is no problem in defining the critical points pσ and the corresponding Lefschetz
thimbles Jσ for any generic value of s. In fact, in Chern-Simons theory, the critical point
equation (2.14) is independent of s (except at s = ±iℓ, where t or et vanishes). The Jσ do
depend on s (their definition by steepest descent makes use of the action, which depends
on s), but their definition ensures that the path integral over Jσ is always convergent, even
when s has an imaginary part.
One can find the right coefficients in the expansion C =
P
σ
nσJσ by starting at real s,
where one knows what to do, and then continuously evolving in s. Here, however, one runs
into the main subtlety of the theory: Stokes phenomena. As one varies s, one crosses Stokes
curves at which both the Lefschetz thimbles Jσ and the coefficients nσ jump, in such a way
that the homology cycle P
σ
nσJσ is locally constant. Such jumping can occur when there
are paths of steepest descent that connect two critical points. The flow by steepest descent
has a conserved quantity (the imaginary part of the holomorphic function whose real part is
taken as the Morse function), and a flow between two critical points is possible only when
this quantity takes equal values at the two critical points. This is reviewed in section 3.
After taking the Stokes phenomena into account, one can obtain a recipe for how the nσ
evolve as s varies in the complex plane. This then gives, finally, a way to define the analytic
continuation in s of the Chern-Simons path integral for the case that the gauge group is a
complex Lie group.
