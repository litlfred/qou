Here we will finally apply all this machinery to some specific examples. We will consider
SU(2) Chern-Simons theory at level k on S
3
, with a knot K labeled by the n-dimensional
representation of SU(2). We denote as Jn(q), where q = exp(2πi/(k+2)), the (unnormalized)
Chern-Simons path integral in the presence of this knot. Jn(q) is essentially the colored Jones
polynomial, though our normalization is not the most common one in the mathematical
literature. (As explained at the end of section 2.5.3, the two normalizations differ by whether
one chooses to divide by the Chern-Simons path integral for the unknot.)
We consider in detail two illustrative and relatively simple knots – the trefoil knot and
the figure-eight knot (fig. 12). For background on these examples, see [17]. The trefoil knot
is a basic example of a larger class of knots known as torus knots. They can be understood
as orbits of a U(1) action on S
3
, and this leads to exceptionally simple formulas for their
quantum invariants, as has been explained recently via path integrals [34]. We expect similar
behavior for other torus knots, though we consider only the trefoil in detail. However, torus

We will study the behavior of Jn(q) in the limit of large n, k with fixed ratio γ = n/k.
We call this the semiclassical limit. For physical values of n and k – that is, positive integers
– this limit can be analyzed by standard methods. One simply sums over contributions of
real saddle points of the path integral; for physical values of n and k, the expansion around
such real saddle points suffices for computing Jn(q) to all orders in an asymptotic expansion
in 1/k (or in 1/n with γ fixed). The virtue of the Morse theory machinery of this paper
is that it enables us to understand what happens in the semiclassical limit after analytic
continuation away from integer values of k (and of n, if one so chooses).
