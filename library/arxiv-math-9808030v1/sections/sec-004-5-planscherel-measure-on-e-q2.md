The comultiplication $$\label{eq: plan11}\Delta :\Phi (E_q(2))\rightarrow \Phi (E_q(2))\otimes \Phi
(E_q(2))$$ defines the regular representations of the quantum group $E_q(2)$ in $\Phi
(E_q(2))$. Since the scalar product in $\Phi (E_q(2))$ is defined by means of the invariant integral this representation is unitary. The linear space $\Phi (E_q(2))$ is common invariant dense domain for the set of linear operators ${\cal R}(\phi )$, ${\cal L}(\phi )$, $\phi \in U_q(e(2))$. Since the representation ([\[eq: plan11\]](#eq: plan11){reference-type="ref" reference="eq: plan11"}) of $E_q(2)$ is unitary the representatives of ${\cal R}(E_{+}E_{-})$, ${\cal R}(H)$, ${\cal L}(H)$ will be at least symmetric operators in $\Phi (E_q(2))$. From ([\[eq: c6\]](#eq: c6){reference-type="ref" reference="eq: c6"}), ( [\[eq: c8\]](#eq: c8){reference-type="ref" reference="eq: c8"}) as well as from $${\cal R}(E_{+}E_{-})t_{ij}^p=p^2t_{ij}^p$$ we see that the matrix elements $t_{ij}^p$ are eigenfunctions of these operators. Thus, the eigenfunctions $t_{ij}^p$ and $t_{i^{\prime }j^{\prime
}}^{p^{\prime }}$ corresponding to different eigenvalues are orthogonal $$(t_{ij}^p,t_{i^{\prime }j^{\prime }}^{p^{\prime }})_L=c_{ij}^l(p)\delta
_{ii^{\prime }}\delta _{jj^{\prime }}\delta (p-p^{\prime }),$$ $$(t_{ij}^p,t_{i^{\prime }j^{\prime }}^{p^{\prime }})_R=c_{ij}^r(p)\delta
_{ii^{\prime }}\delta _{jj^{\prime }}\delta (p-p^{\prime }),$$ where $c_{ij}^l(p)$ and $c_{ij}^r(p)$ are the normalization constants. Due to the existence of the delta function the matrix elements of the unitary irreducible representations of $E_q(2)$ in $\Phi (E_q(2))$ do not belong to $\Phi (E_q(2))$. This is natural feature of the non--compact groups.

Since $\tau (t_{ij}^p)=q^{2(i+j)} t_{ij}^p$ from ([\[plan8\]](#plan8){reference-type="ref" reference="plan8"}) and the above orthogonality conditions we obtain $$\label{eq: plan14}c_{ij}^l(p)=q^{2(i+j)}c_{ij}^r(p).$$ From the explicit expressions ([\[irep12\]](#irep12){reference-type="ref" reference="irep12"}) and ([\[irep13\]](#irep13){reference-type="ref" reference="irep13"}) for the matrix elements we have $$t_{ij}^{pp_0}=\beta (t_{ij}^p)$$ with $\beta$ being the automorphism in the quantum group $E_q(2)$ defined as $$\beta (z)=p_0z,\ \ \ \beta (\delta )=\delta ,\ \ \ \beta (z^*)=p_0z^*;\ \ \
\ p_0\in (0,\ \infty ).$$ To preserve the unitarity of $E_q(2)$ representations we choose $p_0$ to be real and positive. Using the explicit expressions for the Hermitian forms ( [\[plan6\]](#plan6){reference-type="ref" reference="plan6"}) and ([\[plan7\]](#plan7){reference-type="ref" reference="plan7"}) we arrive at $$( t_{ij}^p, t_{i^{\prime }j^{\prime }}^{p^{\prime}}) _{R,L}= p_0^2(
t_{ij}^{pp_0}, t_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime }}^{p^{\prime }p_0})_{R,L}$$ which implies $$c_{ij}^{r,l}(p)=p_0c_{ij}^{r,l}(pp_0)$$ for any $p_0\in (0,\infty )$ and $i,j\in Z$. Thus the normalization coefficients can be represented as $$c_{ij}^{r,l}(p)=\frac{1}{p}a_{ij}^{r,l},$$ where the coefficients $a_{ij}^{r,l}$ do not depend on $p$.

From the unitarity of the left regular representation we have $$({\cal R}(E_\pm^n) t_{ij}^p, t_{i^\prime j^\prime }^{p^\prime})_R
=(t_{ij}^p, {\cal R}(E_\mp^n)t_{i^\prime j^\prime }^{p^\prime})_R.$$ The above relation implies $$a_{i,j}^r=a^r_{i \pm n,j}$$ for any $n\in Z_0$. Thus the coefficients $a_{i,j}^r$ do not depend on the first subindex $i$. $$a_{i,j}^r=c^r_j$$ In a similar fashion using the unitarity of the right regular representation we get $$a_{i,j}^l=c^l_i$$ which is independent of its second subindex. By the virtue of ([\[eq:
plan14\]](#eq:
plan14){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:
plan14"}) we have $$c_i^l=q^{2(i+j)}c_j^r,$$ which is solved by $c_i^l=cq^{2i}$ and $c_j^r=cq^{-2j}$ with $c=constant$. Thus the matrix elements of the unitary irreducible representations satisfy the following orthogonality conditions $$\label{eq: plan15}( t_{ij}^p, t_{i^{\prime }j^{\prime }}^{p^{\prime }})_R=
\frac{cq^{-2j}}p\delta _{ii^{\prime }}\delta _{jj^{\prime }} \delta
(p-p^{\prime }),$$ and $$\label{eq: plan16}(t_{ij}^p\mid t_{i^{\prime }j^{\prime }}^{p^{\prime }}) _L=
\frac{cq^{2i}}p\delta _{ii^{\prime }}\delta _{jj^{\prime }}\delta
(p-p^{\prime }),$$ which implies that the Planscherel measure on $E_q(2)$ is $p$.

For any function $f\in \Phi (E_q(2))$ which can be expressible as the linear combination of the matrix elements $t_{ij}^p$ we have $$f=\frac 1c\int_0^\infty pdp\sum_{i,j=-\infty }^\infty q^{2j}\hat
f_{ij}^pt_{ij}^p,$$ where $$\hat f_{ij}^p=(f,t_{ij}^p)_R.$$

**A. Quantum Group $SU_q(2)$ and Algebra $U_q(su(2))$**

The quantum group $SU_q(2)$ or the $*$--Hopf algebra $A(SU_q(2))$ is the algebra of polynomials of the coordinate functions $x$, $x^*$, $u$ and $u^*$ satisfying the relations ([\[com\]](#com){reference-type="ref" reference="com"}) and the coalgebra operations $$\Delta (x)=x\otimes x -qu\otimes u^*, \ \ \ \Delta (u)=x\otimes u +u\otimes
x^*,$$ $$\varepsilon (x)=1, \ \ \ \varepsilon (u)=0$$ and the antipode $$S(x)=x^*, \ \ \ S(x^*)=x, \ \ \ S(u)=-qu, \ \ \ S(v)=-q^{-1}v.$$

The quantum algebra $U(su_q(2))$ is generated by the elements $${\cal E_\pm}, \ k_\pm=q^{\pm H/4}$$ satisfying the relations $$= \frac{k^2-k^{-2}}{q-q^{-1}}, \ \ \ k{\cal E}
_\pm = q^{\pm 1}{\cal E}_\pm k$$ involutions $$({\cal E}_\pm)^*={\cal E}_\mp , \ \ \ \ k^*=k$$ and co--algebra operations $$\Delta_U ({\cal E}_\pm )={\cal E}_\pm \otimes k +k^{-1}\otimes {\cal E}_\pm
, \ \ \ \Delta_U (k)=k\otimes k,$$ $$\varepsilon_U ({\cal E}_\pm )=0 , \ \ \ \ \varepsilon_U (k )=1$$ and antipode $$S_U ({\cal E}_\pm )=-q^{\pm 1}{\cal E}_\pm , \ \ \ \ S_U (k )=k^{-1}.$$ The quantum algebra $U_q(su(2))$ is in the non--degenerate duality with the quantum group $SU_q(2)$

**B. $E_q(2)$ from $SU_q(2)$ by Contraction**

Substituting $$\label{eq: contr1}x\rightarrow x_0, \ \ \ u\rightarrow \frac{1}{r}u_0,$$ in the formulas (II.1) and (A.1)$\rightarrow$(A.3) we get the relations $$\begin{aligned}
u_0u^*_0=u^*_0u_0, \ \  u_0x_0=qx_0u_0, \ \ \ qu_0x^*_0=x^*_0u_0, \nonumber \\ 
 x_0x^*_0+\frac{1}{r^2}u_0u^*_0=1,\ \ \ x^*_0x_0+\frac{q^2}{r^2}u_0u^*_0=1,
\end{aligned}$$ the coalgebra operations $$\Delta x_0=x_0\otimes x_0 - \frac{q}{r^2}u_0\otimes u^*_0, \ \ \ \Delta
u_0=x_0\otimes u_0+ u_0\otimes x^*_0,$$ $$\varepsilon (x_0)=1,\ \ \ \varepsilon (u_0)=0$$ and the antipode $$S(x_0)=x^*_0,\ \ S(u_0)=-qu_0.$$ Taking $r\rightarrow \infty$ limit in the above formulae and putting $$\label{eq: contr2}z=iqx_0 u_0, \ \ \ \delta =x_0^2$$ we arrive at $$\label{eq: contr3}zz^*=q^{-2}z^*z,\ \ \ z\delta =q^2\delta z,\ \ \ z^*\delta
=q^2\delta z^*,$$ $$\label{eq: contr4}\delta ^{*}=\delta ^{-1},$$ $$\label{eq: contr5}\Delta (z^*)=z^*\otimes 1+\delta^{-1} \otimes z^*, \ \ \
\Delta (z)=z\otimes 1+\delta \otimes z, \ \ \ \Delta (\delta )=\delta\otimes
\delta ,$$ $$\label{eq: contr6}\varepsilon (\delta )=1,\ \ \ \varepsilon (z)=0,$$ and $$\label{eq: contr7}S(\delta )=\delta ^{-1},\ \ \ S(z)=-\delta ^{-1}z,\ \ \
S(z^*)=-\delta z^*.$$ The above relations define the quantum Euclidean group $E_q(2)$ [@cel].

Due to the duality one obtains $U_q(e(2))$ from $U_q(su(2))$ also by contraction. Substituting $$\label{eq: contr10}{\cal E}_\pm \rightarrow r E_\pm, \ \ \ k\rightarrow k$$ in (A.5)$\rightarrow$(A.9) and taking $r\rightarrow\infty$ limit one gets the relations $$\label{eq: contr12}[ E_{+}, E_{-}]=0, \ \ \ k E_\pm =q^{\pm 1}E_\pm k,$$ the involution $$\label{eq: contr13} E_\pm^*=E_\mp, \ \ \ k^*=k,$$ the coalgebra operations $$\Delta_U ( E_\pm )= E_\pm \otimes k +k^{-1}\otimes E_\pm , \ \ \ \Delta_U
(k)=k\otimes k,$$ $$\varepsilon_U ( E_\pm )=0 , \ \ \ \ \varepsilon_U (k )=1$$ and the antipode $$S_U ( E_\pm )=-q^{\pm 1} E_\pm , \ \ \ \ S_U (k )=k^{-1},$$

**C. Unitary Representations of $E(2)$ from $SU(2)$ by Contraction [@9]**

Matrix elements $t^p_{kj}$, $p\in (0, \ \infty )$, $-\infty < k, \ j < \infty$, of the unitary irreducible representation of $E(2)$ has the following integral representation $$t_{kj}^p(g_E) = \frac{e^{-i (k\phi +j(\zeta -\phi))}}{2\pi }
\int_0^{2\pi}e^{ip\rho \cos \psi } e^{i(k-j)\psi }d\psi ,$$ where $$g_E= \left ( 
\begin{array}{ccc}
\cos\zeta & -\sin\zeta & \rho\cos\phi \\ 
\sin\zeta & \cos\zeta & \rho\sin\phi \\ 
0 & 0 & 1 
\end{array}
\right ).$$ Matrix elements $t^l_{ij}$, $l\in\frac{1}{2}Z_0$, $-l \leq i,j\leq l$, of the unitary irreducible representation of $SU(2)$ are given by $$t_{kj}^l(g_S)=e^{-i(k\phi +j\phi ^\prime ) }P_{kj}^l(\cos \theta ),$$ where $P_{kj}^l$ is the Jacobi polynomial which has the following integral representation $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq: c1} P_{kj}^l(\cos\theta )=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{(l-j)!(l+j)!}{(l-k)!(l+k)!}} \int_0^{2\pi} d\psi
e^{ij\psi}
 (i\sin(\theta /2)e^{i\psi /2} 
+\cos(\theta /2)e^{-i\psi /2} )^{l-k} \nonumber \\  
(i\sin(\theta /2)e^{-i\psi /2} +\cos(\theta /2)e^{i\psi /2})^{l+k} \ \ \ \ \ 
\ \  \ \   \ \ \ \ 
\end{aligned}$$ and $$\label{eq: c0}g_S= \left( 
\begin{array}{cc}
e^{i(\phi +\phi^\prime) /2}\cos \theta /2 & ie^{i(\phi-\phi^\prime ) /2}\sin
\theta /2 \\ 
ie^{i(\phi^\prime -\phi) /2}\sin \theta /2 & e^{-i(\phi +\phi^\prime)
/2}\cos \theta /2 
\end{array}
\right)$$ Putting $\theta =p\rho /l$ in ([\[eq: c1\]](#eq: c1){reference-type="ref" reference="eq: c1"}) for $l>>1$ we have $$P_{kj}^l(\cos(p\rho /l))=\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} d\psi
e^{i(j-k)\psi}d\psi (1 +\frac{ip\rho }{2l}e^{-i\psi /2} )^{l-k} (1 +\frac{
ip\rho }{2l}e^{i\phi /2} )^{l+k}.$$ By the virtue of $\lim_{l\rightarrow \infty} (1+x/l)^l = e^x$ we obtain $$\lim_{l\rightarrow \infty}P_{kj}^l(\cos(p\rho /l))= \frac{1}{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}e^{ip\rho \cos \psi} e^{i(k-j)\psi }d\psi.$$ Hence the matrix elements of the unitary irreducible representation of the Euclidean group $E(2)$ with weight $p$ can be obtained from those of $SU(2)$ by the following contraction formula $$\label{eq: c2}t_{kj}^p (\phi , \rho , \zeta )= \lim _{l\rightarrow \infty
}t_{kj}^l(\phi ,p\rho /l,\zeta-\phi ).$$
