The VMC wave functions are based on one-body parts that have a shell-model
structure, namely four nucleons in an α core coupled to ( A − 4) one-body ℓ = 1
wave functions. However the low-lying states of 8Be exhibit a rotational spectrum
and are believed to be well approximated as two α’s rotating around their common
center of mass. It is possible to recover this picture from th e VMC wave functions
by a modiﬁed Monte Carlo density calculation (19).
The standard Monte Carlo method for computing one-body dens ities,ρ(r), is to
make a random walk that samples |Ψ( r1, r2, · · ·, rA)|2 and to bin r1, r2, · · ·, rA for
each conﬁguration in the walk. The density is then proportio nal to the number of
samples in each bin. In the case of a J = 0 nucleus, this “laboratory” density will
necessarily be spherically symmetric.

When the above procedure is applied to the three lowest 8Be states, a dramatic
intrinsic structure is revealed, as shown in Fig. 11 for the g round state. The ﬁgure
shows contours of constant density plotted in cylindrical c oordinates. The left side
of the ﬁgure shows the standard, lab frame, density calculat ion. For the J = 0
ground state, this is spherically symmetric as shown. The ri ght side of the ﬁgure
shows the intrinsic density. It is clear that the intrinsic d ensity has two peaks, with
the neck between them having only one-third the peak density ; we regard these as
twoα’s. This assignment is strengthened by making the same const ruction for the
J = 2 + and 4 + states; although the laboratory densities for these states (inM =J
states) are quite diﬀerent, the intrinsic densities are, wit hin statistical errors, the
same as the J = 0 intrinsic density (19).
If the 0 +, 2 +, and 4 + states are generated by rotations of a common deformed
structure, then their electromagnetic moments and transit ion strengths should all
be related to the intrinsic moments which can be computed by i ntegrating over
the projected body-ﬁxed densities. This is explored in Ref. (19) and a generally
consistent picture is shown.
