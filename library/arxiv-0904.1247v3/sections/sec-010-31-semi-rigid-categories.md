We continue here in the general setting $St_\mathbb C$ of stable presentable $\infty$-categories. Given a monoidal category $A\in Alg(St_\mathbb C)$, its multiplication map $\mu:A\otimes A\to A$ can be regarded as a map of $A$-bimodules, or equivalently $A^e=A\otimes A^{op}$ modules, where the left (respectively, right) $A$-action on $A\otimes A$ is via the first (respectively, second) factor.

We will be interested in monoidal categories with enough duality in the following sense:

::: definition
A monoidal category $A\in Alg(St_\mathbb C)$ is said to be *semi-rigid* if $A$ is compactly generated by objects that are both right and left dualizable.
:::

The definition is a variant of the standard notion of rigid tensor category, for which the following $\infty$-refinement can be found in [@DGcat]:

::: definition
A monoidal category $A\in  Alg(St_\mathbb C)$ is said to be *rigid* if

1.  The multiplication map $\mu:A\otimes A\to A$ is quasi-proper.

2.  The natural lax $A^e$-linearity of the right adjoint $\mu^r$ is strict.

3.  The unit $1_A\in A$ is compact.
:::

The terminology "semi-rigid\" is justified by the following assertion.

::: prop
A semi-rigid category with compact unit is rigid. Conversely, a compactly generated rigid category is semi-rigid.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The first part follows from Lemmas [\[quasiproper action\]](#quasiproper action){reference-type="ref" reference="quasiproper action"} and [\[perfect adjoints\]](#perfect adjoints){reference-type="ref" reference="perfect adjoints"} below, the second is [@DGcat 6.1.1]. ◻
:::

::: lemma
[]{#quasiproper action label="quasiproper action"} If $A\in  Alg(St_\mathbb C)$ is a semi-rigid category, the multiplication morphism $\mu:A\otimes A\to A$ is quasi-proper, and so its right adjoint $\mu^r:A\to A\otimes A$ is continuous. More generally, for any $A$-module $M$, the action map $A\otimes M\to M$ is quasi-proper. The unit morphism $e:\operatorname{Vect}_\mathbb C\to A$ is quasi-proper if and only if in addition $A$ is rigid.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* For the first assertion, let $a\in A$ be compact (hence dualizable), $m\in M$ compact, and $\{n_i\}$ an arbitrary small diagram in $M$.

Then we calculate using Lemma [\[internal\]](#internal){reference-type="ref" reference="internal"}: $$\begin{aligned}
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{M}}(a\star m , \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_i n_i))
&\simeq&  \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{M}}(m, {}^\vee a\star (\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_i n_i))\\
&\simeq&  \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{M}}(m, \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_i ({}^\vee a\star n_i))\\
&\simeq&  {\lim}_i \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{M}}(m, {}^\vee a\star n_i)\\
&\simeq&  {\lim}_i \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{M}}(a\star m, n_i)
\end{aligned}$$ Hence the action of $A$ on $M$ is quasi-proper, and so by Lemma [\[quasi-proper functors\]](#quasi-proper functors){reference-type="ref" reference="quasi-proper functors"}, its right adjoint continuous.

Since $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_A(1_A, -):A\to \operatorname{Vect}_\mathbb C$ is the right adjoint to $e:\operatorname{Vect}_\mathbb C\to A$, the second assertion also follows from Lemma [\[quasi-proper functors\]](#quasi-proper functors){reference-type="ref" reference="quasi-proper functors"}. ◻
:::

### Linearity of adjoints

An important technical aspect of working with semi-rigid categories is that adjoints of linear functors between modules are canonically linear (compare [@DGcat Corollary 6.2.4]):

::: lemma
[]{#perfect adjoints label="perfect adjoints"} Let $A \in  Alg(St_\mathbb C)$ be a semi-rigid monoidal category.

Let $\mathcal C,\mathcal D\in St_\mathbb C$ be compactly generated $A$-modules, and $F:\mathcal C\to{\mathcal D}$ a quasi-proper (respectively, accessible limit-preserving) $A$-linear functor. Then the right (respectively, left) adjoint of $F$ is canonically $A$-linear and continuous as well.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* If $F:\mathcal C\longleftrightarrow \mathcal D: G$ form an adjoint pair, and $F$ is equipped with an $A$-linear structure, then $G$ automatically commutes with the $A$-action in a lax sense, i.e. up to a natural transformation. Thus we need to check that these natural transformations are isomorphisms, which we do by writing objects as colimits of compact generators: $$\begin{aligned}
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal C}}(\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_i c_i, G( \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j a_j\star d))
&\simeq& \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal D}}(F(\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_i c_i), \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_J a_j\star d)\\
&\simeq& \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal D}}(\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_i F(c_i), \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j a_j\star d)\\
&\simeq& \rm{lim}_i \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal D}}(F(c_i), \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j a_j\star d)\\
&\simeq& \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j {\rm{lim}}_i \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal D}}(F(c_i),  a_j\star d)\\
&\simeq& \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j {\rm{lim}}_i \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal D}}( a_j^\vee \star F(c_i), d)\\
&\simeq& \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j {\rm{lim}}_i \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal D}}(F( a_j^\vee \star c_i), d)\\
&\simeq& \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j {\rm{lim}}_i \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal C}}( a_j^\vee \star c_i, G(d))\\
&\simeq& \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j {\rm{lim}}_i \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal C}}(c_i,  a_j\star G(d))\\
&\simeq& \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal C}}(\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_i c_i, \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_j a_j\star G(d))
\end{aligned}$$

The argument for left adjoints of $A$-linear functors is identical. ◻
:::

::: cor
Let $A \in  Alg(St_\mathbb C)$ be a semi-rigid monoidal category.

Passing to Ind-categories and passing to right adjoints induce canonical equivalences between the following three $\infty$-categories:

1.  the category of $A_c$-modules in small idempotent-complete stable categories with morphisms exact functors;

2.  the category of $A$-modules in compactly generated stable categories with morphisms quasi-proper functors;

3.  the opposite of the category of $A$-modules in compactly generated stable categories with morphisms accessible continuous and cocontinuous functors.
:::

::: remark
This corollary allows us to work either with plain stable presentable categories $St$ or in the $\mathbb C$-linear setting in calculating limits and colimits of categories.
:::

### Adjoints

It follows from the above discussion that a semi-rigid monoidal category $A\in Alg(St_\mathbb C)$ defines a comonoidal category $A^r\in Coalg(St_\mathbb C)$ with the same underlying category $A^r=A$ and comultiplication $\mu^r$ the right adjoint of the product $\mu$ on $A$. Given an $A$-module $M$, the diagram defining its module structure defines, by taking right adjoints, a diagram in the opposite category exhibiting $M$ as a comodule for $A^r$. Moreover, this comodule structure is in fact functorial for all morphisms of $A$-modules. To see this, note that given an action $a_M:A\otimes M\to M$, the coaction of $A^r$ can be described as the composition $$\xymatrix{a_M^r: M\ar[r]^-{\sim} & A\otimes_A M \ar[r]^-{\mu^r\otimes{\rm id}} & A\otimes A\otimes_A M\ar[r]^-{\sim} & A\otimes M }$$ where we use semi-rigidity to ensure that $\mu^r$ is a morphism of right $A$-modules. This construction evidently commutes with all morphisms of $A$-modules. In other words, we have an equivalence $$\mathop{\mathrm{Mod}}_A(St_\mathbb C) \simeq \mathop{\mathrm{Comod}}_{A^r}(St_\mathbb C)$$ commuting with the natural forgetful functors to $St_\mathbb C$.
