Conjecture 1. Let ρ ∈ R0 be an irreducible SL2(C)-representation of a hyperbolic knot in S3. If
ρ(µ) and ρ(λ) are parabolic, then t(ρ) /∈ Q.
Remark. (1) If ρ = ρ0 is the discrete faithful representation of the hyperbolic s tructure, then we
know ρ(µ) and ρ(λ) are parabolic and t(ρ0) is called the cusp constant and the cusp polynomial is
the minimum polynomial for t(ρ0) over Q. Moreover {1, t(ρ0)} generates a lattice of C. Therefore,
t(ρ0) /∈ R. For more detail, see [CL, Section 6] . So t(ρ) can be thought of as the generalization of
the cusp constant.
(2) The conjecture can not be extended to non-hyperbolic knot s. There is an example in [Ril1,
Section 5] of an irreducible parabolic representation ρ of alternating torus knot such that t(ρ) ∈ Z.
The conjecture is true for the ﬁgure-eight knot. In fact, for the ﬁgure-eight knot, by Proposition
5.4, if ρ satisﬁes the condition of the conjecture, then it must be the discrete faithful representation
of the hyperbolic structure. By the remark, t(ρ) /∈ R.
Proposition 4.6. Suppose the above conjecture is true. Let γ = pµ + qλ ∈ H1(∂MK ; Z) with p, q
non-zero coprime integers. Let χρ ∈ Zγ be the character of an irreducible representation ρ. Then
ρ(γ) = ±I if and only if the trace of ρ(µ) is not equal to ±2.
Proof. If the trace of ρ(µ) is not equal to ±2, then ρ(µ) is not parabolic or ±I. Since ρ(µ) and ρ(γ)
commute, ρ(γ) is not parabolic. Since χρ ∈ Zγ , σ(ρ(γ)) = ±2. Thus, we obtain ρ(γ) = ±I.
Next suppose that the trace of ρ(µ) is equal to ±2. We show that ρ(γ) ⁄= ±I. There are two
cases.

sentation of π1(S3) in P SL2(C). It is trivial. Therefore, the image of ρ in SL2(C) is contained in
{±I}. So ρ is reducible. This contradiction shows that Case I can not ha ppen.

Proposition 4.7. Suppose χ ∈ Zγ is the character of a reducible representation ρ. Then χ(µ) ⁄= ±2
and ρ(γ) = ±I.
Proof. Let m be an eigenvalue of ρ(µ). By [CCGLS, Proposition 6.2], m2 must be a root of the

Theorem 4.9. The integer b(p, q) is a well-deﬁned invariant of the 3-manifold K(p/q) resulting
from the Dehn ﬁlling on the hyperbolic knot complement MK . It is always positive.
Proof. As mentioned above, the set tD(E(p, q)) ∩ r(X0) is ﬁnite and hence b(p, q) < ∞. On the
other hand, because χρ0(µ) = ±2, χρ0 is always contained in this set, where ρ0 ∈ R0 is the discrete
faithful representation of the hyperbolic metric. Therefo re, b(p, q) > 0.
The intersection between tD(E(p, q)) and r(X0) is taking place in the surface X(∂MK ) described
in Proposition 4.2. If two hyperbolic knots K1 and K2 are homeomorphic, then they have the iso-
morphic fundamental groups of their complements in S3, hence they have isomorphic X0. Therefore,
they have the same b(p, q). Thus b(p, q) is an invariant of K(p/q) depending only on the hyperbolic
knot K and the Dehn surgery coeﬃcient p/q. □

The invariant λ(p, q) is for the hyperbolic knot and its ( p, q) Dehn surgery. An SL2(C) knot in-
variant obtained from the character variety of 1-dimension al components is given by the ﬁrst author
in [Li]. The construction in [Li] was purely topological by c hoosing generic smooth perturbations
and generic values of χ(µ). The topological deﬁnition of the Casson-type invariant i s not easy to
calculate. Our invariant λ(p, q), deﬁned via the intersection multiplicity, is easier or at least very
explicit for computation. It can also be interpreted as the i ntersection cycles of the appropriate
cohomology classes of X(∂M ).
Proposition 4.13. If λ(p, q) > 0, then π1(K(p/q)) is non-cyclic.
Proof. (1) If λ(p, q) > 0, then there exists ρ ∈ R0 such that its character χρ ∈ S(p, q). Hence
Fµ (χρ) ⁄= 0, and Fγ (χρ) = 0. By [CGLS, Proposition 1.5.2], there exists a represent ation from
π1(K(p/q)) to P SL2(C) with non-cyclic image. The result follows. □
