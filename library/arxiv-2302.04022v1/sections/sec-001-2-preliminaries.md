A partition of a positive integer n is a sequence of positive integers γ = ( γ1, γ2, . . . , γm)
satisfying γ1 ≥ γ2 ≥ · · · ≥ γm and n = γ1 + γ2 + · · · + γm. We use γ ⊢ n to indicate that
γ is a partition of n and let ci(γ) denote the number of terms in γ which are equal to i. A
Young diagram is a ﬁnite collection of boxes arranged in left-justiﬁed row s, with the row sizes
weakly decreasing. The Young diagram associated to the part ition γ = (γ1, γ2, . . . , γm) is the one
that has m rows and γi boxes on the i-th row. Since there is a clear one-to-one correspondence
between partitions and Young diagrams, we use the two terms i nterchangeably.
Every permutation σ of Sn has a decomposition into disjoint cycles. The cycle type of σ is
the partition of n whose parts are the lengths of the cycles in its decompositio n. It is widely
known that two elements of Sn are conjugates if and only if they have the same cycle type. Th is
means that the conjugacy classes of Sn are characterized by the cycle types and thus correspond
to the partitions of n. Denote by C(Sn, γ) the conjugacy class of Sn that corresponds to the
partition γ ⊢ n. We use sgn( γ) to denote the sign of the permutations in C(Sn, γ).
For each partition ζ ⊢ n, we use ρζ to denote the Specht module of Sn that corresponds
to ζ. It is well known that ˆSn = {ρζ | ζ ⊢ n} is a complete list of inequivalent irreducible
representations of Sn. The Specht modules ρ(n) and ρ(1n) are called the trivial and the sign
representations of Sn, respectively. The standard representation of Sn just refers to ρ(n−1, 1).
Let χζ (·) and ˜χζ (·) denote the character and normalized character of ρζ , respectively. We have
χ(n)(σ) = ˜χ(n)(σ) = 1 and χ(1n)(σ) = ˜χ(1n)(σ) = sgn( σ) for any σ ∈ Sn. As χζ (·) (respectively,
˜χζ (·)) is a class function on Sn, we use χζ (γ) (respectively, ˜χζ (γ)) to indicate the value of χζ (·)
(respectively, ˜χζ (·)) on the conjugacy class C(Sn, γ).
