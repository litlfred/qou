2. Hyperbolic structures on compression bodies
Let S be a closed, orientable (possibly disconnected) surface with genus at least 1,
and let I denote the interval [0, 1].
Deﬁnition 2.1. A compression body C is either a handlebody, or the result of
attaching 1-handles to S × I along S × {1} such that the result is connected. The
negative boundary is S ×{0} and is denoted ∂−C; if C is a handlebody then ∂−C = ∅.
The positive boundary is ∂C∖∂−C and is denoted ∂+C.
The compression bodies that we study here are those for which ∂−C is a torus
and ∂+C is a genus (n + 1) surface, with n ≥1 an integer. We call this the (1; n + 1)-
compression body and denote it by C(1; n + 1) or just C when there is no confusion.
The numbers 1 and n + 1 refer to the genera of the boundary components. Thus
C(1; n + 1) is obtained by attaching n 1-handles to T2 × I. See Figure 1.
The fundamental group of C(1; 2) is π1(C(1; 2)) = (Z × Z) ∗Z, because C(1; 2)
deformation retracts to the union of a torus and an arc γ1 at the core of the 1-handle;
see Figure 1. This arc is called the core tunnel of C(1; 2). We will denote the
generators of the Z × Z factor by α and β, and the generator of the free Z by γ1.
More generally, for the (1; n + 1)-compression body, the fundamental group is
π1(C(1; n + 1)) = (Z × Z) ∗Z ∗· · · ∗Z,
