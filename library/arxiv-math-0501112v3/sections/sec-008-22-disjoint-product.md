[]{#subsec:disjoint label="subsec:disjoint"} Usually by a product of normalized conjugacy classes $\Sigma_{k_1,\dots,k_m}$ we understand their product as elements of the group algebra ${\mathbb{C}}(S_q)$. However, sometimes it is convenient to consider their disjoint product defined by $$\label{eq:disjoint} \Sigma_{k_1,\dots,k_m} \bullet
\Sigma_{l_1,\dots,l_n}= \Sigma_{k_1,\dots,k_m,l_1,\dots,l_n}.$$

::: remark
The readers familiar with the notion of partial permutations of Ivanov and Kerov [@IvanovKerov1999] will see that [\[eq:disjoint\]](#eq:disjoint){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:disjoint"} is compatible with the following definition of the product $\alpha_1 \bullet \alpha_2$, when $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ are partial permutations: $$\label{eq:definition-of-disjoint} \alpha_1\bullet \alpha_2 =
\begin{cases} \alpha_1 \alpha_2 & \text{if supports of $\alpha_1$
and $\alpha_2$ are disjoint,} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}$$ Further discussion can be found in Section [\[subsec:algebraofconjugacy\]](#subsec:algebraofconjugacy){reference-type="ref" reference="subsec:algebraofconjugacy"}.
:::
