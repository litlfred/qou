Lemma 4 Let X and X' be closed braids, in general having distinct braid
indices. Assume that X' is a preferred longitude for X. Then X = X'.

Proof: Since X’ is a preferred longitude for X there is a Seifert surface F
with OF = X and an annulus AC F, with 0A = X — X'. (Here the minus
sign denotes a reversal of the order of X'.) Using the tools which were re-
viewed in §2 above, we may assume that F has maximal Euler characteristic
and is a Markov surface. Therefore F admits a tiling. Since X’ is a closed
braid we may further assume that X’ C F is everywhere transverse to the
leaves in the foliation. Our plan is to use the tiling on F and the induced
tiling on AMF to prove that X = X’.

Recall that F is a union of tiles of type aa, ab and bb. Let us examine how
X' intersects the tiles in the tiling of F. Since F is tangent to fibers of 1 at its
singular points, whereas X’ (being a closed braid) is everywhere transverse
to fibers of , it follows that X’ never passes through a singular point in the
tiling, also it is oriented so that it travels clockwise (resp. counterclockwise)
whenever it goes around a negative (resp. positive) vertex in the tiling,
when we are viewing the positive side of F. Also, whenever subarcs of X’
are locally parallel to subarcs of X these subarcs are oriented coherently. It
follows that for each of the three tile types there are four different ways that
X' can pass through a tile T. These are illustrated in Figure 7(b). We have
assigned little numbers 1,2,3,4 to the four possible components of X‘NT
in each tile T.
