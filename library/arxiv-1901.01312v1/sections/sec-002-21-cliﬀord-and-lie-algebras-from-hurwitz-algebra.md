Unlike Lie and Cliﬀord algebras, there are only a ﬁnite number of Hurwitz algebras 4. These
algebras are very generative. Given a Hurwitz algebra identiﬁed with a spinor space, one acts on
this spinor space with the algebra itself, from the left, right, or from both sides. Sometimes this
does not make a diﬀerence, and sometimes it does. Considering all the repeated nested actions
generate the adjoint algebras, which are speciﬁc Cliﬀord algebras. The bivectors of a Cliﬀord
algebra close under commutation, providing a representation of the Lie algebra that preserves
the metric of the associated pseudo-orthogonal space.
The small set of Hurwitz algebras thus provide a means of selecting (or rather a mechanism for
generating) very speciﬁc Cliﬀord and Lie algebras from an otherwise inﬁnite sets. Particularly
interesting is the case of the complex octonions. Although this algebra is not associative, the
adjoint algebras C𝓁(7, 0)∼=Cl(6) are.
