The extensive works of Dixon [12, 13, 14] focus on deriving the SM from tensor products of
Hurwitz algebra. The approach is to reduce spinor spaces of Hurwitz algebras (as well as
their associated action algebras) into sets of orthogonal subspaces. This is achieved by ﬁnding
projection operators within the algebras, corresponding to primitive idempotents obtained from
a resolution of the identity. In the case of C⊗ O the projectors can be used to split the algebra
into four orthogonal subspaces, each aSU (3) multiplet. This is because the automorphism group
that keeps e7 ﬁxed is SU (3). Each spinor in C⊗ O is resolved into 1⊕ 3⊕ 3⊕ 1 with respect
to this chosen SU (3) automorphism group[12]. The same algebra admits a U(1) generator that
produces the correct hypercharges.
4 We use the term Hurwitz algebra here instead of normed division algebra because, the reduction of spinor
spaces considered by Dixon [12], and the construction of minimal ideals considered by Furey [15] depend on the
existence of nilpotents and primitive idempotents. These do not exist in normed division algebras, but do in their
split algebra cousins. For example, when working in C⊗ H or C⊗ O one uses a primitive idempotent to select a
split basis to work with.
