Let us consider now the Hopfion solutions in the topological sector of degree two.

We can see that, as in previously considered case of the $Q=1$ Hopfion, for relatively large value of the mass parameter $m$ the spacial distribution of the energy density follows the location curves $\mathcal{C}_1 = \vec \phi^{-1}(1,0,0)$ and $\mathcal{C}_{-1} = \vec \phi^{-1}(-1,0,0)$. Indeed, in Fig. [1](#q2combine){reference-type="ref" reference="q2combine"}, left plot, we display the energy density isosurfaces of the $Q=2$ configuration together with the isosurfaces of the preimages of the vector $\vec \phi=(\pm 0.9, 0,0)$. Clearly, for higher values of the energy density, left plot, the energy density isosurface has a form of twisted bead necklace with four beads. This structure is similar to the energy density isosurface of the corresponding four-component solution of the Skyrme model with Heisenberg type potential [@Gudnason:2015nxa], which however, is not twisted.

We can consider now the $Q=2$ Hopfion solution in the model ([\[lagr\]](#lagr){reference-type="ref" reference="lagr"}) with the potential ([\[pot-Nitta\]](#pot-Nitta){reference-type="ref" reference="pot-Nitta"}). As it is seen in Fig. [2](#q2al1o23){reference-type="ref" reference="q2al1o23"}, in this case the energy density isosurfaces also represent two buckled tubular loops twisted two times around each other. However, similar to the corresponding $Q=1$ solution above, cf. Fig. [\[q1al1o23\]](#q1al1o23){reference-type="ref" reference="q1al1o23"}, the energy is not equally distributed among these loops (see table [\[hopftable2\]](#hopftable2){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable2"}), one of them is thinner than another, it disappears as the value of the energy density increases, see left plot in Fig. [2](#q2al1o23){reference-type="ref" reference="q2al1o23"}. Interestingly, for higher values of the energy density the corresponding tube is not decomposed into four isolated beads, as it happens in the model with Heisenberg type potential above.

To classify solutions of the Faddeev-Skyrme model with symmetry breaking potential we introduce new notations, which generalize the above-mentioned classification by Sutcliffe [@Sutcliffe:2007ui]. Since the spacial position of maxima of the energy distribution corresponds to the location curves $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_{-1}$, which could be of different types, the configuration with linking number $Q$ can be denoted as $$Q\left(\mathcal{C}_1 \between \mathcal{C}_{-1}\right)_{\mathcal{C}}$$ where the subindex corresponds to the type of the loop $\mathcal{C} = \vec \phi^{-1}(0,0,-1)$, which is the antipodal to the vacuum. In this notations, for example, the configuration of degree two is denoted as $2(\widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1\between \widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1)_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{2,1}}$. Further remark is that for larger values of the mass parameter $m$, the loop $\mathcal{C}$ in the sector of degree $Q$ becomes symmetric with respect to the dihedral group $D_{Q}$. This is a symmetry of the truncated $c=0$ model.

Note that in our numerical simulation we find another solution of the Faddeev-Skyrme model with potential $m^2{\phi_1}^2$ in this sector, $2(\widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1\between \widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1)_{\widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{1,2}}$, see Table [\[hopftable\]](#hopftable){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable"}. This configuration represents a local minimum, its energy is about $16 \%$ higher than the energy of the global minimum, see Table [\[energytable\]](#energytable){reference-type="ref" reference="energytable"}. However, as the parameter $\alpha$ in ([\[potential\]](#potential){reference-type="ref" reference="potential"}) varies, this configuration becomes a saddle point, our algorithm is unable to find this solution in the model with potential ([\[pot-Nitta\]](#pot-Nitta){reference-type="ref" reference="pot-Nitta"}) (see Table [\[energytable\]](#energytable){reference-type="ref" reference="energytable"}). Indeed, it was noticed that the Hopfion of that type, which in the usual $m=0$ Faddeev-Skyrme model represents an unstable configuration of two charge one solitons stacked one above the other [@Hietarinta:1998kt; @Ward:1998pj], it has the energy around $13\%$ above the global minimum [@Sutcliffe:2007ui].
