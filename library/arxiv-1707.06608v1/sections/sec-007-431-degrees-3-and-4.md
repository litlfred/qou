A peculiar feature of the Faddeev-Skyrme model is that for a given degree $Q$, there are usually several different stable static soliton solutions of similar energy, furthermore, the number of solutions grows with $Q$. We considered both the configurations with minimal energies and the local minima in a given sector. Results of our numerical simulations are summarized in Table [\[hopftable\]](#hopftable){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable"}.

For Hopf degree $Q=3$ the minimum-energy configuration in the model with the symmetry breaking potential $m^2{\phi_1}^2$ is presented in Fig. [1](#q3combine){reference-type="ref" reference="q3combine"}. Similar to the corresponding solutions in the sectors of degrees one and two, cf. Figs. [\[q2combine\]](#q2combine){reference-type="ref" reference="q2combine"}, [\[q1combine\]](#q1combine){reference-type="ref" reference="q1combine"}, we displayed there the energy isosurface of the $Q=3$ configuration $3(\widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1\between \widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1)_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1}$ together with the location curves of the field components. Clearly, the energy density distribution follows the location curves $\mathcal{C}_1 = \vec \phi^{-1}(1,0,0)$ and $\mathcal{C}_{-1} = \vec \phi^{-1}(-1,0,0)$, as above. Note that for the higher values of the energy, the energy density isosurface has a form of a bead necklace with six beads. The antipodal to the vacuum curve $\mathcal{C} = \vec \phi^{-1}(0,0,-1)$, which is bounding these beads, is planar but it is not exactly circular. Interestingly it has the hexagonal shape possessing dihedral symmetry $D_3$, see Fig. [1](#q3combine){reference-type="ref" reference="q3combine"}, bottom-left plot.

As said above, the Hopfion configuration can be constructed from planar Skyrmions located in the plane transverse to the direction of the loop in 3d [@Kobayashi:2013xoa]. It is known, however that the structure of the multisoliton solution of the planar Skyrme model is very sensitive to the choice of the potential [@Weidig:1998ii; @Leese:1989gj; @Hen:2007in; @Ward:2003un; @Jaykka:2010bq]. In particular, it was proposed to consider the potential [@Jaykka:2011ic; @Winyard:2013ada], which explicitly breaks the $O(3)$ symmetry of the planar Skyrme model to the dihedral group $D_3$. In the baby Skyrme model with the potential $V=m^2(\phi_1-c)^2$ a single Skyrmion is splitted into two partons with the same topological charge $Q\simeq1/2$, as $c=0$ and into constituents with fractional charges $Q=1/3, 2/3$, as $c=1/3$. Hence the appearance of two tubes in the energy density distribution of the Faddeev-Skyrme model with the potential ([\[pot-Nitta\]](#pot-Nitta){reference-type="ref" reference="pot-Nitta"}), can be related with decomposition of the planar solitons. Non-equal distribution of the topological charge between these lumps is reflected in different thickness of the corresponding location curves $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_{-1}$, see Table [\[hopftable2\]](#hopftable2){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable2"}.

In the sector of degree $Q=3$ we also found another solution $3(\widetilde A_1\between \widetilde A_1)_{\widetilde A_1}$ (Table [\[hopftable\]](#hopftable){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable"}). Since the energies of both solutions are very close, we are not able to identify which configuration represents a global minimum, our algorithm suggests that the configuration with hexagonal symmetry of the location curve of the preimage of the point $\vec \phi=(0, 0, -0.9)$ has slightly lower energy.

Considering the structure of the $Q=3$ Hopfion solutions in the ([\[lagr\]](#lagr){reference-type="ref" reference="lagr"}) with the potential ([\[pot-Nitta\]](#pot-Nitta){reference-type="ref" reference="pot-Nitta"}), we found that the energy density isosurfaces again represents two buckled tubular loops with interlinking number three, see Table [\[hopftable2\]](#hopftable2){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable2"}. Thus, as before, the location curves $\mathcal{C}_1 = \vec \phi^{-1}(1,0,0)$ and $\mathcal{C}_{-1} = \vec \phi^{-1}(-1,0,0)$ define the spacial distribution of the energy density. Similar to the corresponding $Q=1,2$ solutions we discussed above, the energy is not equally distributed among these tubes. This observation holds for all configurations in the sectors of higher degrees.

At degree four in the model ([\[lagr\]](#lagr){reference-type="ref" reference="lagr"}) with potential $V=m^2{\phi_1}^2$ we found solution of three different types, see Table [\[hopftable\]](#hopftable){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable"}. The minimal energy configuration is of the type $4(\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_{1,1}\between\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_{1,1})_{\widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{2,2}}$. The energy density isosurfaces of this very symmetric Hopfion represent four tubular loops interlinked with each other, see the corresponding plot in the Table [\[hopftable\]](#hopftable){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable"}.

The configuration of the form $4(\widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{1,1}\between \widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{1,1})_{\widetilde \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{4,1}}$ has less symmetry, it is a local minimum of the energy functional. In this sector we also found the dihedrally symmetric Hopfion $4(\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{1,1}\between \ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{1,1})_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{4,1}}$, its energy is even higher. Similar to the corresponding solutions in the sectors of lower degrees, for large values of the energy, the energy density isosurface forms a bead necklace with 8 beads and the bounding planar curve $\mathcal{C} = \vec \phi^{-1}(0,0,-1)$ possessing dihedral symmetry $D_4$.

$Q=4$ Hopfions in the model ([\[lagr\]](#lagr){reference-type="ref" reference="lagr"}) with mixed potential ([\[pot-Nitta\]](#pot-Nitta){reference-type="ref" reference="pot-Nitta"}) are of similar forms, see Table [\[hopftable2\]](#hopftable2){reference-type="ref" reference="hopftable2"}. Surprisingly, we found that lowest energy configuration in this sector is $4(\widetilde\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1\between\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_{1,1})_{\widetilde\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{4,1}}$. More symmetric solution $4(\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1\between\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_1)_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{4,1}}$ then has almost the same energy as the configuration $4(\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_{1,1}\between\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_{1,1})_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{A}}_{2,2}}$, so it is difficult to identify which state is the first local minimum. Remind that the former configuration represents the global minimum energy solution in the massless model [@Sutcliffe:2007ui].
