It is a known fact that many standard (integer) linear programming formulations of relevant problems in optimization show a lot of symmetry. In this situation a standard branch-and-cut framework repeatedly enumerates symmetric solutions, and sometimes this renders such methods useless. To address these issues the last decade saw a number of approaches to devise algorithms specialized to symmetric optimization problems. We mention a few: Margot suggests to solve symmetric integer linear programs (ILPs) via a pruned branch-and-cut approach involving techniques from computational group theory [@Margot02; @Margot03]. A recent improvement in this direction is "orbital branching" devised by Ostrowski et al. [@OstrowskiEtAl09]. Friedman [@Friedman07] as well as Kaibel and Pfetsch [@KaibelPfetsch08] treat symmetric ILPs by shrinking the domain of feasibility by cutting off symmetric solutions. Gatermann and Parrilo apply results from representation theory and invariant theory to semidefinite programming [@GatermannParrilo04], which includes linear programming as a special case. Our approach is close in spirit to this paper. See also the survey of Margot [@Margot10] for a general overview of symmetric integer linear programming.

This is how our paper is organized: first we analyze linear programs with an arbitrary finite group of linear automorphisms. Most results in this section are known. A first key observation, Theorem [\[thm:barycenter_ineqs\]](#thm:barycenter_ineqs){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:barycenter_ineqs"}, is that symmetric linear programming can be reduced to linear programming over the fixed space of the automorphism group. Sections [\[sec:ILP\]](#sec:ILP){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:ILP"} and [\[sec:layers\]](#sec:layers){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:layers"} translate these results to the context of integer linear programming. In the sequel we concentrate on groups acting as signed permutations on the standard basis of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$. Section [\[sec:searching\]](#sec:searching){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:searching"} contains our main contribution: our *Core Point Algorithm* [\[algo:core_point\]](#algo:core_point){reference-type="ref" reference="algo:core_point"} can solve an integer linear program in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ whose group of linear automorphisms contains the alternating group of degree $n$ (acting as signed permutations) in $O(mn^2)$ time, where $m$ is the number of constraints. This is in sharp contrast with the known NP-completeness of the general integer linear programming feasibility problem. While our algorithm only works for ILPs with an exceptionally high degree of symmetry we believe that this is a first step towards an entire new class of algorithms dealing with symmetry in ILPs. Suitable generalizations are the subject to ongoing research. In Section [\[sec:finding\]](#sec:finding){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:finding"} we discuss algorithms to determine groups of automorphisms of integer linear programs. This leads to algorithmic problems which turn out to be graph-isomorphism-complete. The final Section [\[sec:experiments\]](#sec:experiments){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:experiments"} contains experimental results. One of the ILP classes that we investigated computationally is motivated by work of Pokutta and Stauffer on lower bounds for Gomory-Chvátal ranks [@PokuttaStauffer10]. Section [\[sec:hypertruncated\]](#sec:hypertruncated){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:hypertruncated"} explains the construction.
