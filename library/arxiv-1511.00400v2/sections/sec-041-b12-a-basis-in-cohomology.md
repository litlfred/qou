To describe a basis for $H_i(M^3; \varrho^{k_1,k_2})$ when $i=1,2$, recall the cap product defined in Equation [\[eqn:cap\]](#eqn:cap){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:cap"}. $$\cap\colon H^0(T^2; \varrho^{k_1,k_2})\times H_i(T^2; \mathbb{C})\to H_i(T^2; 
\varrho^{k_1,k_2}).$$ Let $i\colon T^2\to M^3$ denote the inclusion, and $i_*\colon 
H_j(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2} )\to H_j(M^3;\varrho^{k_1,k_2} )$, the induced map.

::: prop
[]{#prop:basis label="prop:basis"} Let $M^3$ and $T^2$ be as above, $k_1\neq k_2$, $k_1+k_2$ even. Choose $a\in  
H^0(T^2; \varrho^{k_1,k_2})$, $a\neq 0$. Then:

(a) []{#item:h2 label="item:h2"} $i_*(a\cap [T^2])$ is a basis for $H_2(M^3;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})$, where $[T^2]\in H_2(T^2;\mathbb{Z})$ is a fundamental class.

(b) []{#item:h1 label="item:h1"} $i_*(a\cap [\gamma])$ is a basis for $H_1(M^3;\varrho^{k_1,0})$ or $H_1(M^3;\varrho^{0,k_2})$, for any $[\gamma]\in H_1(T^2;\mathbb{Z})$, $[\gamma]\neq 0$.

(c) []{#item:csratio label="item:csratio"} If $\varrho$ denotes a lift of the holonomy of the complete structure, for any pair of peripheral curves $0\neq [\gamma_1],[\gamma_2] \in H^1(T^2;\mathbb{Z})$ and for $a\in H^0(T^2; \varrho^{k,0})$ $$a\cup [\gamma_2]=\operatorname{cs}(\gamma_2,\gamma_1) a\cap [\gamma_1]$$ in $H_1(T^2; \rho^{k,0})$.

(d) []{#item:h1dif label="item:h1dif"} When $k_1k_2\neq 0$, the cap product $$\cap\colon H^0(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})\times  H_1(T^2;\mathbb{C})\to H_1 
    (T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})$$ is the trivial map (i.e. identically zero).
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* For Item [\[item:h2\]](#item:h2){reference-type="eqref" reference="item:h2"}, $a\cap [T^2]$ is a basis for $H_ 2(T^2; \varrho^{k_1,k_2})$, by Poincaré duality and Lemma [\[lemma:inv\]](#lemma:inv){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:inv"}. Item [\[item:h1\]](#item:h1){reference-type="eqref" reference="item:h1"} is proved in [@MFP12b] for $k_2=0$. It holds true for $k_1=0$ by complex conjugation.

To prove the other two items, we choose a cell decomposition of the torus from a square with opposite edges identified. Namely there is a $2$-cell $e^2$ represented by a square, whose sides are two copies of the $1$-cells: $e_1^1$ and $e_2^1$ (with respective homology classes $[e_1^1]=[{m}]$ and $[e_2^1]=[{l}]$ respectively) and the vertices are four copies of the $0$-cell. Choose lifts to the universal covering so that $$\label{eqn:partial}
\partial \tilde e^2= (1-l)\tilde e^1_1 + (m-1)\tilde e^2_1,$$ where $m$ and $l$ generate $\pi_1 T^2$. We may assume that $$\varrho(m)=
\pm \begin{pmatrix}
 1 & 1 \\
 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\qquad
\textrm{ and }
\qquad
\varrho(l)=
\pm \begin{pmatrix}
 1 & \eta \\
 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}$$ where $\eta =\operatorname{cs}(l,m)\in\mathbb{C}-\mathbb{R}$.

We prove now [\[item:csratio\]](#item:csratio){reference-type="eqref" reference="item:csratio"}. $\operatorname{Sym}^k$ acts on the space of degree $k$ homogeneous polynomials on $X$ and $Y$; the one dimensional space invariant by $\varrho^{k}=\varrho^{k,0}$ is generated by $a= X^k$ (Lemma [\[lemma:inv\]](#lemma:inv){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:inv"}). By [\[eqn:partial\]](#eqn:partial){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:partial"}: $$\partial( X^{k-1}Y\otimes \tilde e^2) = -\eta  \, a \otimes \tilde e^1_1 +   a \otimes \tilde e^2_1 \\
$$ which in cohomology translates to $-\eta\, a\cap [m]+ a\cap [l]=0$. This proves [\[item:csratio\]](#item:csratio){reference-type="eqref" reference="item:csratio"} for a system of generators $\{[m],[l]\}$ of $H_1(T^2;\mathbb{Z})$, and it holds in general by linearity.

We prove finally [\[item:h1dif\]](#item:h1dif){reference-type="eqref" reference="item:h1dif"}. $\operatorname{Sym}^{k_1}\otimes\overline{\operatorname{Sym}^{k_2} }$ acts on the space of degree $k_1$ homogeneous polynomials on $X$ and $Y$, multiplied by degree $k_2$ homogeneous polynomials on $\bar  X$ and $\bar  Y$; the one dimensional space invariant by $\varrho^{k_1,k_2}$ is generated by $a= 
X^{k_1} \bar  X^{k_2}$. By [\[eqn:partial\]](#eqn:partial){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:partial"}: $$\begin{aligned}
\partial( X^{k_1-1}Y\bar  X^{k_2}\otimes \tilde e^2) &= -\eta  \, a \otimes \tilde e^1_1 +   a \otimes \tilde e^2_1 \\
\partial( X^{k_1}\bar  X^{k_2-1}Y\otimes \tilde e^2) &= -\bar  \eta  \, a \otimes \tilde e^1_1 +   a \otimes \tilde e^2_1 
\end{aligned}$$ Namely, in homology $-\eta  \, a \cap [m] + a\cap [l]=-\bar  \eta  \, a \cap [m] + a\cap [l]=0$. As $\eta\not\in \mathbb{R}$, the claim follows. ◻
:::
