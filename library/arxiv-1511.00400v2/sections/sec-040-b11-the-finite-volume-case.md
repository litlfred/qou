We next discuss the consequences in the finite volume case of Theorem [\[thm:strong_acyclicity\]](#thm:strong_acyclicity){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:strong_acyclicity"}. The following corollary does not assume finite volume.

::: cor
[]{#cor:notL2 label="cor:notL2"} Let $M^3$ be an orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold and $\widetilde{\mathrm{hol}}$ a lift of its holonomy. For $k_1\neq k_2$ every closed $L^2$-form in $\Omega^*(M^3; E_{k_1,k_2})$ is exact. In particular every element in $H^* (M^3;   \varrho^{k_1,k_2}
)$ is represented by a form that is not $L^2$.
:::

In order to apply this corollary, we need first to compute the homology and cohomology of the peripheral torus. All the information on the dimension is given by $H^0(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})$, which is the set of invariants of the module $\mathbb{C}^{(k_1+1)(k_2+1)}$ by the action of $\varrho^{k_1,k_2}(\pi_1 T^2)$.

The following implies Item (a) of Proposition [\[prop:basis complete\]](#prop:basis complete){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:basis complete"}.

::: lemma
[]{#lemma:inv label="lemma:inv"} Let $M^3$ be as above and let $T^2\subset M^3$ be the peripheral torus. The invariant subspace of the peripheral group is $$H^0(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})\cong \left\{
  \begin{array}{ll}
   0 &  \textrm{if } k_1+k_2 \textrm{ is odd,} \\
   \mathbb{C}& \textrm{if } k_1+k_2 \textrm{ is even.}
  \end{array}
  \right.$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The lift of the holonomy restricted to $\pi_1T^2\cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ is written as $$(n_1,n_2)\mapsto (-1)^{\epsilon(n_1,n_2)} \begin{pmatrix}
                   1 & n_1 + n_2 \operatorname{cs}\\
                   0 & 1
                  \end{pmatrix}$$ where $\operatorname{cs}\in\mathbb{C}-\mathbb{R}$ is the cusp shape in Definition [\[def:cs\]](#def:cs){reference-type="ref" reference="def:cs"} and $\epsilon\colon\mathbb{Z}^2\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is a surjection (here it is relevant that $\epsilon$ is non-trivial). From the representation, it is straightforward that if $k_1+k_2$ is odd, then there is an element whose eigenvalues are all $(-1)$, and for $k_1+k_2$ even, the subspace of invariants is generated by the monomial $X^{k_1+1} \bar X ^{k_2+1}$. ◻
:::

From Poincaré duality we get information on $H^2$, but also on $H^1$ because $\chi(T^2)=0$. We also know the dimension of the homology groups by duality. Thus we have:

::: cor
[]{#cor:Ht2 label="cor:Ht2"} Let $M^3$ and $T^2$ as above.

(a) If $k_1+k_2$ is odd then $$H^*(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})\cong 
                    H_*(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})=0\, .$$

(b) If $k_1+k_2$ is even, then $$\dim H^i(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})=\dim H_i(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})= 
                          \left\{
                          \begin{array}{ll}
                          1 &  \textrm{for } i=0,2, \\
                          2 &  \textrm{for } i=1, \\
                          0 &  \textrm{otherwise. }
                          \end{array}\right.$$
:::

::: thm
[]{#prop:dimHM label="prop:dimHM"} Let $M^3$ be a hyperbolic orientable 3-manifold with a single cusp, and let $T^2$ be a peripheral torus.

(a) []{#item:odd label="item:odd"} When $k_1+k_2$ is odd, then $H_*(M^3; 
    \varrho^{k_1,k_2})=0$.

(b) When $k_1+k_2$ is even with $k_1\neq k_2$, then

    (i) $H_i(M^3; \varrho^{k_1,k_2} )=0$ for $i\neq 1,2$,

    (ii) $H_2(M^3; 
            \varrho^{k_1,k_2})\cong H_2(T^2; \varrho^{k_1,k_2})\cong \mathbb{C}$,

    (iii) $H_1(M^3; \varrho^{k_1,k_2})\cong  \mathbb{C}$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The group $H^0(M^3 ; \varrho^{k_1,k_2})$ vanishes, as this is the subspace of fixed vectors, and both $\varrho$ and $\operatorname{Sym}^{k_1}\otimes \overline{\operatorname{Sym}^{k_2}}$ are irreducible. By Corollary [\[cor:notL2\]](#cor:notL2){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:notL2"}, the map $H^i(M^3 ; T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})\to H^i(M^3;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})$ vanishes. Thus $H^i(M^3;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})\to H^i(T^2;\varrho^{k_1,k_2})$ is injective, by the long exact sequence in cohomology of the pair. With Poincaré duality and duality between homology and cohomology, we get Item [\[item:odd\]](#item:odd){reference-type="eqref" reference="item:odd"}. Poincaré duality also yields that $H^1(T^2; \varrho^{k_1,k_2})\to H^2(M^3,T^2;  \varrho^{k_1,k_2})$ is surjective. Then the lemma follows from the long exact sequence in cohomology and the duality between homology and cohomology. ◻
:::
