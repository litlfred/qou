Figure 3.3 illustrates the subdivision of F into subsets 1’, 1”, 2 and
3. Using it, we are almost ready to define our complexity function.
Notice that we have already standardized the geometry in the following
ways:
1. F is a Bennequin surface with respect to H and H’.
2. W and W’ are admissible words.
3. A is disjoint from the digons, by construction.
4. The standard foliation of the interior of Q is singularity-free.
We now need to standardize the intersections of fibers of H’ with the
digons:

Lemma 3.3. Let Q be the union of all the digons. We may assume

that
(i) The intersections of A’ with F UQ are finite in number and

transverse.

(ii) The non-standard foliation of F UQ. is radial near each pierce
point.

(iii) All but finitely many fibers H, of H’ meet FUQ transversally.
Those which do not (the Q-singular fibers) are each tangent to FUQ
at exactly one point in the interior of Q.
