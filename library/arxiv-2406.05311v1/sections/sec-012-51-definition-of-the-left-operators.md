[]{#SS:monoid label="SS:monoid"}

We first recall the results of [@BS-Monoid] concerning saturated chains in the classical $k$-Bruhat order and then extend part of its approach to the quantum $k$-Bruhat order.

### Classical left operators {#SS:classical-left-operators}

Let ${\mathcal F}_n$ be the free monoid generated by the $\binom{n}{2}$ symbols $\{{\color{RoyalBlue}{\mathbf v}_{a,b}} \mid a<b \mbox{ with }a,b\in[n]\}$. Let $S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ be the symmetric group $S_n$ with an absorbing element ${\mathbf 0}$ adjoined. We often omit the comma between indices, thus ${\mathbf v}_{ab}={\mathbf v}_{a,b}$.

Both the Grassmannian--Bruhat order and the $k$-Bruhat order lead to actions of ${\mathcal F}_n$ on $S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$. The action of ${\mathcal F}_n$ on $S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ induced by the Grassmannian--Bruhat order is defined on generators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ for $a<b$ in $[n]$ by ${\mathbf v}_{ab}\cdot{\mathbf 0}={\mathbf 0}$, and for $\zeta\in S_n$,

$${\mathbf v}_{ab} \cdot \zeta\ =\ \begin{cases}
            (a,b) \zeta, & \text{ if }\zeta{\prec\!\!\!\cdot\,}(a,b)\zeta,\\
            {\mathbf 0},& \text{ otherwise,}
       \end{cases}$$

and extended via composition to ${\mathcal F}_n$. For $k< n$, the action of ${\mathcal F}_n$ on $S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ induced by the $k$-Bruhat order is similarly defined on generators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ for $a<b$ in $[n]$ by ${\mathbf v}_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}{\mathbf 0}={\mathbf 0}$, and for $u\in S_n$, $${\mathbf v}_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u\ =\ \begin{cases}
            (a,b) u, & \text{ if }u \lessdot_k (a,b)u,\\
            {\mathbf 0},& \text{ otherwise.}
       \end{cases}$$

Let ${\mathcal M}_n$ be the monoid with ${\mathbf 0}$ that is the quotient of ${\mathcal F}_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ by the following relations.

$$\label{Eq:monoid-relations}
    \begin{array}{rrclll}
        (i)   & {\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}           & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{ab}                     &  & \text{($b<c$ or $a<c<d<b$)} \\ [0.5ex]
        (ii)  & {\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}           & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{ab}\ \equiv\ {\mathbf 0}&  & \text{($a\leq c<b\leq d$)}  \\ [0.5ex]
        (iii) & {\mathbf v}_{bc}{\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{ac} & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{bd}{\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{bc}                 &  & \text{($a<b<c<d$)}          \\ [0.5ex]
        (iv)  & {\mathbf v}_{ac}{\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{bc} & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{bc}{\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{bd}                 &  & \text{($a<b<c<d$)}          \\ [0.5ex]
        (v)   & {\mathbf v}_{bc}{\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{bc} & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{bc}{\mathbf v}_{ab}\ \equiv\ {\mathbf 0}&  & \text{($a<b<c$)}
    \end{array}$$

If a composition ${\mathbf v}$ is equivalent to ${\mathbf 0}$ (${\mathbf v}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$), then we say that and if ${\mathbf v}$ is not equivalent to ${\mathbf 0}$, then we say that . We summarize the main results of [@BS-Monoid].

::: proposition
[]{#Prop:monoid label="Prop:monoid"} Let $e$ denote the identity element of $S_n$.

1.  The map ${\mathcal F}_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}\to S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ defined by ${\mathbf x}\mapsto {\mathbf x}\cdot e$ is surjective, factors through ${\mathcal M}_n$, and induces a bijection between ${\mathcal M}_n$ and $S_n \cup \{{\mathbf 0}\}$.

2.  For $\zeta\in S_n$, the fiber $\{{\mathbf x}\in{\mathcal F}_n\mid {\mathbf x}\cdot
                e = \zeta\}$ is in bijection with the set of saturated chains in $[e,\zeta]_\preceq$.

3.  []{#Prop_3 label="Prop_3"} For $u,w\in S_n$ and $k<n$, the set $\{ {\mathbf x}\in{\mathcal F}_n\mid {\mathbf x}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=w\}$ is in bijection with the set of saturated chains in $[u,w]_k$.
:::

### Quantum left operators {#SS:left-quantum-operators}

Unlike what we just described, we do not know of an analog of the Grassmannian--Bruhat order or of the monoid ${\mathcal M}_n$ for the quantum Bruhat order on $S_n$. Some structure does extend, most notably the two- and three-term relations in [\[Eq:monoid-relations\]](#Eq:monoid-relations){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:monoid-relations"} and certain symmetries. With the goal of an analog of Proposition [\[Prop:monoid\]](#Prop:monoid){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:monoid"}[\[Prop_3\]](#Prop_3){reference-type="eqref" reference="Prop_3"}, we define as follows. For $1\leq k<n$, $\{a,b\}\subseteq [n]$ with $a\ne b$, and $u\in S_n$ define

$$\begin{aligned}
 \label{Eq:Def-k-action}
    {\color{RoyalBlue}{{\mathbf v}}_{ab}} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u
    &=
    \begin{cases}
        (a,b) u, \hphantom{q_{i,j}} & \text{if $a<b$ and $u \lessdot_k^q (a,b)u\,,$}            \\[0.4ex]
          q_{i,j} (a,b) u\,, & \text{if $a>b$ and  $u\lessdot_k^q q_{i,j} (a,b) u$\,,\ \ 
           ($u(i)=a$, $u(j)=b$)} \\[0.4ex]
        {\mathbf 0}\,, & \text{otherwise\,,}
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}$$

and extend to $S_n[q] \cup \{{\mathbf 0}\}$ by setting ${\color{RoyalBlue}{\mathbf v}_{ab} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}(q^\alpha u) \vcentcolon= q^\alpha \, ({\mathbf v}_{ab} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u)}$. Note that if ${{\mathbf v}}_{ab} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u\neq{\mathbf 0}$, then $u\lessdot_k^q{{\mathbf v}}_{ab} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u$ is a cover, and all covers in the quantum Bruhat order occur in this way.

::: remark
[]{#Rem:k-action label="Rem:k-action"} We make a few remarks and definitions about these operators.

1.  The operators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ with $a<b$ are those of §[0.0.1](#SS:classical-left-operators){reference-type="ref" reference="SS:classical-left-operators"}, and are subject to the relations in [\[Eq:monoid-relations\]](#Eq:monoid-relations){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:monoid-relations"}.

2.  We say that ${{\mathbf v}}_{ab}$ is if $a<b$ and if $a>b$.

3.  []{#Rem:k-action:zero label="Rem:k-action:zero"} A composition ${\mathbf v}$ of operators (${\color{RoyalBlue}{\mathbf v}\equiv {\mathbf 0}}$) if for every $u\in S_n$ and $1\leq k<n$, we have that ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u= {\mathbf 0}$, using [\[Eq:Def-k-action\]](#Eq:Def-k-action){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:Def-k-action"}.

4.  []{#Rem:k-action:not-zero label="Rem:k-action:not-zero"} If we do not have ${\mathbf v}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$ in this sense, then ${\mathbf v}$ is . In this case, there exist $u,w\in S_n$, $1\leq k<n$, and $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$ such that ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u= q^\alpha w$. Note that $u\leq_k^q q^\alpha w$.

5.  []{#Rem:k-action:chains label="Rem:k-action:chains"} In the situation of [\[Rem:k-action:not-zero\]](#Rem:k-action:not-zero){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:not-zero"}, where ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$, if ${\mathbf v}={\mathbf v}_{a_rb_r}\dotsb{\mathbf v}_{a_2b_2}{\mathbf v}_{a_1b_1}$, then there exist $w_0=u,w_1,\dotsc,w_r=w$ in $S_n$ and $\alpha_0=0, \alpha_1,\dotsc,\alpha_r=\alpha$ in ${\mathbb N}^{n-1}$ such that for all $1\leq i<j\leq r$,

    $$\label{Eq:sub-chain}
          {\mathbf v}_{a_jb_j} \dotsb {\mathbf v}_{a_ib_i} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}(q^{\alpha_{i-1}}w_{i-1})\ =\ q^{\alpha_j} w_j\,.$$

    In particular, the composition ${\mathbf v}_{a_jb_j} \dotsb {\mathbf v}_{a_ib_i}$ is nonzero. We also have that $$u\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_1}w_1\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_2}w_2
          \ \lessdot_k^q\ \dotsb\  \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_r}w_r=q^\alpha w$$ is a saturated chain in the interval $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$.

6.  []{#Rem:k-action:zero_compose label="Rem:k-action:zero_compose"} A consequence of the definition [\[Rem:k-action:zero\]](#Rem:k-action:zero){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:zero"} and the observation [\[Rem:k-action:chains\]](#Rem:k-action:chains){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:chains"} is that if ${\mathbf v}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$, then ${\mathbf v}''{\mathbf v}{\mathbf v}'\equiv{\mathbf 0}$ for any operators ${\mathbf v}',{\mathbf v}''$.

7.  []{#Rem:k-action:uk_equiv label="Rem:k-action:uk_equiv"} In the situation of [\[Rem:k-action:not-zero\]](#Rem:k-action:not-zero){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:not-zero"}, with ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$, suppose that we have an operator ${\mathbf v}'$ such that ${\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$. Then we say that the operators ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf v}'$ are . We explore this equivalence relation in Sections [\[SS:Shape-equivalence\]](#SS:Shape-equivalence){reference-type="ref" reference="SS:Shape-equivalence"} through [\[S:degreeTwo\]](#S:degreeTwo){reference-type="ref" reference="S:degreeTwo"}.

    In this case, by the same reasoning as [\[Rem:k-action:chains\]](#Rem:k-action:chains){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:chains"}, ${\mathbf v}'$ represents a saturated chain in $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$. When ${\mathbf v}\neq{\mathbf v}'$, the operators induce distinct chains. As $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$ is a ranked poset, ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf v}'$ have the same length, so that $(u,k)$-equivalence is ranked.

8.  Suppose that $u\leq_k^q q^\alpha w$ and we have a saturated chain in $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$, $$u\ =\ q^{\alpha_0}w_0\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_1}w_1\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_2}w_2
          \ \lessdot_k^q\ \dotsb\  \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_r}w_r=q^\alpha w\,,$$ For each $i=1,\dotsc,r$, consider an operator ${\mathbf v}_{a_ib_i}$ satisfying ${\mathbf v}_{a_ib_i}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}q^{\alpha_{i-1}}w_{i-1}=q^{\alpha_i}w_i$. Then we have that $(w_{i-1})^{-1}w_i=(s,t)$ with $s\leq k<t$, $a_i=w_{i-1}(s)$, and $b_i=w_{i-1}(t)$. Furthermore, if $a_i<b_i$, then $q^{\alpha_i}=q^{\alpha_{i-1}}$, but if $a_i>b_i$, then $q^{\alpha_i}=q_{s,t}q^{\alpha_{i-1}}$. Hence, the operator ${\color{RoyalBlue}{\mathbf v}}\vcentcolon={\mathbf v}_{a_rb_r}\dotsb {\mathbf v}_{a_1b_1}$ is such that ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$.

9.  []{#Rem:k-action:subword label="Rem:k-action:subword"} Finally, suppose that ${\mathbf v}''{\mathbf v}{\mathbf v}'$ is a nonzero operator and ${\mathbf v}''{\mathbf v}{\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$. If we write ${\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\beta x$ and ${\mathbf v}{\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\gamma y$, then ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}x = q^{\gamma-\beta} y$. Suppose that ${\mathbf u}$ is $(x,k)$-equivalent to ${\mathbf v}$, so that ${\mathbf u}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}x = q^{\gamma-\beta} y$. Then ${\mathbf v}''{\mathbf v}{\mathbf v}'$ is $(u,k)$-equivalent to ${\mathbf v}''{\mathbf u}{\mathbf v}'$.

Items [\[Rem:k-action:zero_compose\]](#Rem:k-action:zero_compose){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:zero_compose"} and [\[Rem:k-action:subword\]](#Rem:k-action:subword){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:subword"} show that equivalence of operators may be induced by subwords.$\diamond$
:::

A consequence of Remark [\[Rem:k-action\]](#Rem:k-action){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action"} is the following generalization of Proposition [\[Prop:monoid\]](#Prop:monoid){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:monoid"}[\[Prop_3\]](#Prop_3){reference-type="eqref" reference="Prop_3"}.

::: proposition
[]{#Prop:chains_and_operators label="Prop:chains_and_operators"} Suppose that $u\leq_k^q q^\alpha w$. Then there is a bijection between saturated chains in the interval $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$ and nonzero operators ${\mathbf v}$ such that ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$, and this set of operators forms a single $(u,k)$-equivalence class of nonzero operators.
:::
