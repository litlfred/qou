[]{#left-operators label="left-operators"}

We will prove the following quantum analog of the first part of Proposition [\[P:peaklessChains\]](#P:peaklessChains){reference-type="ref" reference="P:peaklessChains"}.

::: theorem
[]{#Th:nonZero label="Th:nonZero"} Let $u,w\in S_n$ and $q^\alpha w\in S_n[q]$ be such that $[u,q^\alpha w]^q_k$ is a minimal interval. Then there is a hook partition $\lambda$ with $|\lambda|=\ell(q^\alpha w)-\ell(u)$ such that $C^{q^\alpha w}_{u,v(\lambda,k)}$ is nonzero.
:::

Our proof of Theorem [\[Th:nonZero\]](#Th:nonZero){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:nonZero"} is given at the end of this section. We sketch the organization of this section and that proof. We first extend the approach in [@BS-Monoid] (see also [@Benedetti_Bergeron]) of studying saturated chains in the $k$-Bruhat order using operators. We introduce operators ${\mathbf v}$ with an action $\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}$ on $S_{n}[q]$ and an equivalence relation in Section [0.1](#SS:monoid){reference-type="ref" reference="SS:monoid"} that encodes saturated chains in the quantum $k$-Bruhat order as follows: Let ${\mathbf v}$ be an operator, $u,w\in S_n$, and $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$ be such that $u\leq^q_k {\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u = q^\alpha w$. Elements of the equivalence class of ${\mathbf v}$ are in bijection with chains in $[u,q^\alpha w]^q_k$. The operator ${\mathbf v}$ is if $q^\alpha=1$, in which case ${\mathbf v}$ is an operator from [@BS-Monoid].

We show in Section [0.3](#SS:diagrammatic-notation){reference-type="ref" reference="SS:diagrammatic-notation"} that these operators and the equivalence relation exhibit symmetries that are enjoyed by the quantum $k$-Bruhat order, the most important being Postnikov's cyclic shift [@Postnikov_symmetry], which induces a cyclic symmetry of the coefficients $C^{q^\alpha w}_{u,v}$. Relations of degree two are studied in Section [0.5](#S:degreeTwo){reference-type="ref" reference="S:degreeTwo"}. In Section [0.6](#crossing-products){reference-type="ref" reference="crossing-products"} we associate a graph to an operator ${\mathbf v}$, and show that if ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$ and if the interval $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$ is minimal, then the graph of ${\mathbf v}$ is a (noncrossing) forest. In Section [0.7](#ss:Paths){reference-type="ref" reference="ss:Paths"} we study operators whose graph is a path. In Section [0.8](#SS:trees){reference-type="ref" reference="SS:trees"}, we use induction to prove that a noncrossing forest ${\mathbf v}$ is equivalent to a composition $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{C}$ of what we term a row ($\mathbf{R}$) and a column ($\mathbf{C}$), and that ${\mathbf v}$ (and hence $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{C}$) may be cyclically shifted to a classical operator ${\mathbf v}'$, which is also a composition $\mathbf{R}'\mathbf{C}'$ of a row $\mathbf{R}'$ and a column $\mathbf{C}'$ in the classical sense of [@Benedetti_Bergeron]. The product $\mathbf{R}'\mathbf{C}'$ corresponds to a peakless chain in the classical interval corresponding to ${\mathbf v}'$. Given this, Postnikov's cyclic symmetry [@Postnikov_symmetry Thm. 4] and Proposition [\[pieri-formulas-version-2\]](#pieri-formulas-version-2){reference-type="ref" reference="pieri-formulas-version-2"} enable us to deduce Theorem [\[Th:nonZero\]](#Th:nonZero){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:nonZero"}.

## Definition of the left operators {#SS:monoid}

We first recall the results of [@BS-Monoid] concerning saturated chains in the classical $k$-Bruhat order and then extend part of its approach to the quantum $k$-Bruhat order.

### Classical left operators {#SS:classical-left-operators}

Let ${\mathcal F}_n$ be the free monoid generated by the $\binom{n}{2}$ symbols $\{{\color{RoyalBlue}{\mathbf v}_{a,b}} \mid a<b \mbox{ with }a,b\in[n]\}$. Let $S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ be the symmetric group $S_n$ with an absorbing element ${\mathbf 0}$ adjoined. We often omit the comma between indices, thus ${\mathbf v}_{ab}={\mathbf v}_{a,b}$.

Both the Grassmannian--Bruhat order and the $k$-Bruhat order lead to actions of ${\mathcal F}_n$ on $S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$. The action of ${\mathcal F}_n$ on $S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ induced by the Grassmannian--Bruhat order is defined on generators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ for $a<b$ in $[n]$ by ${\mathbf v}_{ab}\cdot{\mathbf 0}={\mathbf 0}$, and for $\zeta\in S_n$,

$${\mathbf v}_{ab} \cdot \zeta\ =\ \begin{cases}
            (a,b) \zeta, & \text{ if }\zeta{\prec\!\!\!\cdot\,}(a,b)\zeta,\\
            {\mathbf 0},& \text{ otherwise,}
       \end{cases}$$

and extended via composition to ${\mathcal F}_n$. For $k< n$, the action of ${\mathcal F}_n$ on $S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ induced by the $k$-Bruhat order is similarly defined on generators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ for $a<b$ in $[n]$ by ${\mathbf v}_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}{\mathbf 0}={\mathbf 0}$, and for $u\in S_n$, $${\mathbf v}_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u\ =\ \begin{cases}
            (a,b) u, & \text{ if }u \lessdot_k (a,b)u,\\
            {\mathbf 0},& \text{ otherwise.}
       \end{cases}$$

Let ${\mathcal M}_n$ be the monoid with ${\mathbf 0}$ that is the quotient of ${\mathcal F}_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ by the following relations.

$$\label{Eq:monoid-relations}
    \begin{array}{rrclll}
        (i)   & {\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}           & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{ab}                     &  & \text{($b<c$ or $a<c<d<b$)} \\ [0.5ex]
        (ii)  & {\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}           & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{ab}\ \equiv\ {\mathbf 0}&  & \text{($a\leq c<b\leq d$)}  \\ [0.5ex]
        (iii) & {\mathbf v}_{bc}{\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{ac} & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{bd}{\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{bc}                 &  & \text{($a<b<c<d$)}          \\ [0.5ex]
        (iv)  & {\mathbf v}_{ac}{\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{bc} & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{bc}{\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{bd}                 &  & \text{($a<b<c<d$)}          \\ [0.5ex]
        (v)   & {\mathbf v}_{bc}{\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{bc} & \equiv & {\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{bc}{\mathbf v}_{ab}\ \equiv\ {\mathbf 0}&  & \text{($a<b<c$)}
    \end{array}$$

If a composition ${\mathbf v}$ is equivalent to ${\mathbf 0}$ (${\mathbf v}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$), then we say that and if ${\mathbf v}$ is not equivalent to ${\mathbf 0}$, then we say that . We summarize the main results of [@BS-Monoid].

::: proposition
[]{#Prop:monoid label="Prop:monoid"} Let $e$ denote the identity element of $S_n$.

1.  The map ${\mathcal F}_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}\to S_n\cup\{{\mathbf 0}\}$ defined by ${\mathbf x}\mapsto {\mathbf x}\cdot e$ is surjective, factors through ${\mathcal M}_n$, and induces a bijection between ${\mathcal M}_n$ and $S_n \cup \{{\mathbf 0}\}$.

2.  For $\zeta\in S_n$, the fiber $\{{\mathbf x}\in{\mathcal F}_n\mid {\mathbf x}\cdot
                e = \zeta\}$ is in bijection with the set of saturated chains in $[e,\zeta]_\preceq$.

3.  []{#Prop_3 label="Prop_3"} For $u,w\in S_n$ and $k<n$, the set $\{ {\mathbf x}\in{\mathcal F}_n\mid {\mathbf x}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=w\}$ is in bijection with the set of saturated chains in $[u,w]_k$.
:::

### Quantum left operators {#SS:left-quantum-operators}

Unlike what we just described, we do not know of an analog of the Grassmannian--Bruhat order or of the monoid ${\mathcal M}_n$ for the quantum Bruhat order on $S_n$. Some structure does extend, most notably the two- and three-term relations in [\[Eq:monoid-relations\]](#Eq:monoid-relations){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:monoid-relations"} and certain symmetries. With the goal of an analog of Proposition [\[Prop:monoid\]](#Prop:monoid){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:monoid"}[\[Prop_3\]](#Prop_3){reference-type="eqref" reference="Prop_3"}, we define as follows. For $1\leq k<n$, $\{a,b\}\subseteq [n]$ with $a\ne b$, and $u\in S_n$ define

$$\begin{aligned}
 \label{Eq:Def-k-action}
    {\color{RoyalBlue}{{\mathbf v}}_{ab}} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u
    &=
    \begin{cases}
        (a,b) u, \hphantom{q_{i,j}} & \text{if $a<b$ and $u \lessdot_k^q (a,b)u\,,$}            \\[0.4ex]
          q_{i,j} (a,b) u\,, & \text{if $a>b$ and  $u\lessdot_k^q q_{i,j} (a,b) u$\,,\ \ 
           ($u(i)=a$, $u(j)=b$)} \\[0.4ex]
        {\mathbf 0}\,, & \text{otherwise\,,}
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}$$

and extend to $S_n[q] \cup \{{\mathbf 0}\}$ by setting ${\color{RoyalBlue}{\mathbf v}_{ab} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}(q^\alpha u) \vcentcolon= q^\alpha \, ({\mathbf v}_{ab} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u)}$. Note that if ${{\mathbf v}}_{ab} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u\neq{\mathbf 0}$, then $u\lessdot_k^q{{\mathbf v}}_{ab} \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u$ is a cover, and all covers in the quantum Bruhat order occur in this way.

When ${\mathbf v}\neq{\mathbf v}'$, the operators induce distinct chains. As $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$ is a ranked poset, ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf v}'$ have the same length, so that $(u,k)$-equivalence is ranked.

8.  Suppose that $u\leq_k^q q^\alpha w$ and we have a saturated chain in $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$, $$u\ =\ q^{\alpha_0}w_0\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_1}w_1\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_2}w_2

9. A consequence of Remark [\[Rem:k-action\]](#Rem:k-action){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action"} is the following generalization of Proposition [\[Prop:monoid\]](#Prop:monoid){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:monoid"}[\[Prop_3\]](#Prop_3){reference-type="eqref" reference="Prop_3"}.

::: proposition
[]{#Prop:chains_and_operators label="Prop:chains_and_operators"} Suppose that $u\leq_k^q q^\alpha w$. Then there is a bijection between saturated chains in the interval $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$ and nonzero operators ${\mathbf v}$ such that ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$, and this set of operators forms a single $(u,k)$-equivalence class of nonzero operators.
:::

## Flattening and $(u,k)$-equivalence {#SS:Shape-equivalence}

Equivalence among classical operators, [\[Eq:monoid-relations\]](#Eq:monoid-relations){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:monoid-relations"} and Proposition [\[Prop:monoid\]](#Prop:monoid){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:monoid"}, depends only upon the relative order of the indices, and not their actual values. We establish a version for quantum operators. We begin with some definitions.

Let $a=(a_1,\dotsc,a_t)$ be a sequence of integers and set $m\vcentcolon=\#\{a_1,\dotsc,a_t\}$, the number of distinct integers in this sequence. The of $a$ is the sequence $(c_1,\dotsc,c_t)$ where each $c_i\in[m]$ and $c_i>c_j$ if and only if $a_i>a_j$. For example, the flattening of $(3,6,1,6,8,3,1)$ is $(2,3,1,3,4,2,1)$. Sequences are if they have the same flattening. The flattening of an operator ${\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}\dotsb{\mathbf v}_{a_1 b_1}$ is ${\mathbf v}_{c_r d_r}\dotsb{\mathbf v}_{c_1 d_1}$, where $(c_r,d_r,\dotsc,c_1,d_1)$ is the flattening of $(a_r,b_r,\dotsc,a_1,b_1)$. Operators ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf v}'$ are if they have the same flattening. This extends the notion of shape-equivalence of permutations $\zeta$ from Section [\[SS:GBO\]](#SS:GBO){reference-type="ref" reference="SS:GBO"}.

The of an operator ${\mathbf v}={\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}\dotsb{\mathbf v}_{a_1 b_1}$ is $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})\vcentcolon=\{a_r,b_r,\dotsc,a_1,b_1\}$. The operator ${\mathbf v}$ is if the factorization of $\zeta=\zeta({\mathbf v})=(a_r,b_r)\dotsb(a_1,b_1)$ into transpositions has minimal length in that $r=\#\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\zeta)-s(\zeta)$. As $r$ is the minimal possible number of transpositions whose product is $\zeta$, we have that $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\zeta({\mathbf v}))=\{a_r,b_r,\dotsc,a_1,b_1\}=\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})$. Furthermore, if ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$, then as $\zeta({\mathbf v})=wu^{-1}$, the interval $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$ is minimal in the sense of Section [\[sec:Qminim\]](#sec:Qminim){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:Qminim"}. Later, in the proof of Theorem [\[Th:nonZero\]](#Th:nonZero){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:nonZero"} in Section [0.10](#sec:final){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:final"}, we will show that the permutation $\zeta({\mathbf v})$ is a minimal permutation in the sense of Section [\[minimal-permutations\]](#minimal-permutations){reference-type="ref" reference="minimal-permutations"}.

Shape-equivalence preserves equivalence to ${\mathbf 0}$ and for minimal operators, $(u,k)$-equivalence.

::: theorem
[]{#Th:shape-equivalence label="Th:shape-equivalence"} Let ${\mathbf v}$ be an operator with flattening ${\mathbf v}'$. Then ${\mathbf v}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$ if and only if ${\mathbf v}'\equiv{\mathbf 0}$.

Suppose that ${\mathbf v}$ is nonzero and minimal, and that ${\mathbf u}$ is an operator that is $(u,k)$-equivalent to ${\mathbf v}$ for some $u\in S_n$ and $k<n$. Then ${\mathbf u}$ is minimal and $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})=\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf u})$. Furthermore, if ${\mathbf v}'$ is the flattening of ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf u}'$ the flattening of ${\mathbf u}$, then there exist $x\in S_m$ and $l<m$, where $m=\#\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})$, such that ${\mathbf v}'$ is $(x,l)$-equivalent to ${\mathbf u}'$.
:::

We deduce Theorem [\[Th:shape-equivalence\]](#Th:shape-equivalence){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:shape-equivalence"} at the end of this subsection after establishing some preliminary lemmas and definitions. Let $s\in[n]$. Define the truncation map ${\color{RoyalBlue}\tau_s}\colon[n]\to[n{-}1]$ by

$$\tau_s(j)\ =\ \left\{ \begin{array}{rcl} j&\ &\mbox{if }j<s\\ j{-}1&&\mbox{if } j\geq s\end{array}\right. \ .$$

This extends to operators. For distinct $a,b,s\in[n]$, define ${\color{RoyalBlue}\tau_s({\mathbf v}_{a,b})}\vcentcolon={\mathbf v}_{\tau_s(a),\tau_s(b)}$. If ${\mathbf v}$ is an operator and $s\not\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})$, then we may apply $\tau_s$ to every operator ${\mathbf v}_{a,b}$ in ${\mathbf v}$, and we define $\tau_s({\mathbf v})$ to be the composition of the $\tau_s({\mathbf v}_{a,b})$. For example, $\tau_3({\mathbf v}_{45}{\mathbf v}_{51}{\mathbf v}_{25})={\mathbf v}_{34}{\mathbf v}_{41}{\mathbf v}_{24}$.

For $u\in S_n$ and $r\in[n]$, let ${\color{RoyalBlue}u/r}\in S_{n-1}$ be the permutation obtained by deleting the $r$th column and $u(r)$th row of the permutation matrix of $u$. In one-line notation, this is obtained by omitting the $r$th value of $u$ and then flattening, so that $(41\underline{3}652)/3=31542$. Omitting the $s$th component of $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$ gives $\alpha/s\in{\mathbb N}^{n-2}$, which is the sequence $(\alpha_1,\dotsc,\alpha_{s-1},\alpha_{s+1},\dotsc,\alpha_{n-1})$. Thus $(3,1,\underline{2},4,0)/3 = (3,1,4,0)$.

We also have an expansion map ${\color{RoyalBlue}\iota_s}\colon[n{-}1]\to[n]$ defined by

$$\iota_s(j)\ =\ \left\{ \begin{array}{rcl} j&\ &\mbox{if }j<s,\\ j{+}1&&\mbox{if } j\geq s.\end{array}\right.$$

This extends to operators in the same way as $\tau_s$ extended to operators. For $r,s\in[n{+}1]$ and $v\in S_n$, define a permutation $u=\varepsilon_{r,s}(v)\in S_{n+1}$ by $u(r)=s$ and $u/r=v$. For $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$, define $\varepsilon_{r,s}(\alpha)\vcentcolon=(\alpha_1,\dotsc,\alpha_{r{-}1},s,\alpha_r,\dotsc,\alpha_{n-1})\in{\mathbb N}^{n}$.

::: lemma
[]{#L:relative-labels label="L:relative-labels"} Let ${\mathbf v}$ be a nonzero operator with ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$.

1.  []{#L:r-l:i label="L:r-l:i"} For $s\not\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})$, set $r=u^{-1}(s)$. Then $r=w^{-1}(s)$ and $\tau_s({\mathbf v}) {\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}}_{\tau_r(k)} (u/r) = q^{\alpha/r} w/r$.

2.  []{#L:r-l:ii label="L:r-l:ii"} Suppose that $r\leq u^{-1}(a)$ for all $a\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})$ or $r>u^{-1}(a)$ for all $a\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})$. Then for any $s\in[n{+}1]$, $\iota_s({\mathbf v})\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{\iota_r(k)} \varepsilon_{r,s}(u) = q^{\varepsilon_{r,0}(\alpha)}\varepsilon_{r,s}(w)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Both statements follow (by composition) from the case when ${\mathbf v}={\mathbf v}_{ab}$ is a single operator. As $u\lessdot_k q^\alpha w={\mathbf v}_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u$, we have $w=(a,b)u=u(i,j)$, where $i=u^{-1}(a)\leq k < u^{-1}(b)=j$ and either $q^\alpha=1$ when $a<b$ or $q^\alpha=q_{i,j}$ when $a>b$. The statements follow from the definitions of $\tau_s$, $\iota_s$, $/r$, $\varepsilon_{r,s}$, and the characterizations of covers in Propositions [\[nothing-in-between-is-in-between\]](#nothing-in-between-is-in-between){reference-type="ref" reference="nothing-in-between-is-in-between"} and [\[prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers\]](#prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers"}, in a case-by-case analysis depending upon the relative positions of $s$ with $a,b$ and $r$ with $i,j$. While we omit that analysis, we illustrate three cases in Example [\[Ex:three_cases\]](#Ex:three_cases){reference-type="ref" reference="Ex:three_cases"} below.

Note that when $a<b$, ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ is classical, which is treated in [@BS-Bruhat; @BS-Monoid]. ◻
:::

::: example
[]{#Ex:three_cases label="Ex:three_cases"} Let $u=154\raisebox{-1pt}{\hspace{0.55pt}\color{magenta}\rule{0.75pt}{11pt}}\hspace{0.55pt}32$. Then ${\mathbf v}_{53}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_3 u = q_2q_3 13452$. ::: corollary
[]{#C:single_equivalences label="C:single_equivalences"} Let ${\mathbf v}$ be a composition of operators.

1.  []{#C:SE_1 label="C:SE_1"} For $s\in[n]\smallsetminus\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})$, we have that ${\mathbf v}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$ if and only if $\tau_s({\mathbf v})\equiv{\mathbf 0}$.

2.  For $s\in[n{+}1]$, we have that ${\mathbf v}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$ if and only if $\iota_s({\mathbf v})\equiv{\mathbf 0}$.

3.  []{#C:SE_3 label="C:SE_3"} Suppose that ${\mathbf v}$ is nonzero and ${\mathbf v}'$ is a composition that is $(u,k)$-equivalent to ${\mathbf v}$ for some $u\in S_n$ and $k<n$.

    1.  []{#C:SE_3_i label="C:SE_3_i"} Let $s\in[n]\smallsetminus(\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})\cup\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v}'))$ and set $r\vcentcolon= u^{-1}(s)$. Then $\tau_s({\mathbf v})$ and $\tau_s({\mathbf v}')$ are $(u/r,\tau_r(k))$-equivalent.

    2.  For any $s\in[n{+}1]$ and any $r$ such that either $r\leq u^{-1}(a)$ for all $a\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})\cup\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v}')$ or $r>u^{-1}(a)$ for all $a\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})\cup\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v}')$, we have that $\iota_s({\mathbf v})$ and $\iota_s({\mathbf v}')$ are $(\varepsilon_{r,s}(u), \iota_r(k))$-equivalent.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The third statement holds by Lemma [\[L:relative-labels\]](#L:relative-labels){reference-type="ref" reference="L:relative-labels"}. This also proves the (contrapositive of) the implication $\Leftarrow$ in the first two statements. As in (1), ${\mathbf v}=\iota_s(\tau_s({\mathbf v}))$ and in (2), $s\not\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\iota_s({\mathbf v}))$ and ${\mathbf v}=\tau_s(\iota_s({\mathbf v}))$, the other directions also hold. ◻
:::

::: proof
*Proof of Theorem [\[Th:shape-equivalence\]](#Th:shape-equivalence){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:shape-equivalence"}.* As flattening is induced by applying truncation operators, the first statement of Theorem [\[Th:shape-equivalence\]](#Th:shape-equivalence){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:shape-equivalence"} follows from Corollary [\[C:single_equivalences\]](#C:single_equivalences){reference-type="ref" reference="C:single_equivalences"} [\[C:SE_1\]](#C:SE_1){reference-type="ref" reference="C:SE_1"}.

Suppose that ${\mathbf v}$ is nonzero and minimal. Let ${\mathbf u}$ be an operator that is $(u,k)$-equivalent to ${\mathbf v}$. Then there exist $w\in S_n$ and $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$ such that ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u={\mathbf u}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$. As $\zeta({\mathbf v})=wu^{-1}$, $\zeta({\mathbf u})=\zeta({\mathbf v})$. As $(u,k)$-equivalence is ranked, both ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf u}$ involve the same number of elementary operators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$. This implies that ${\mathbf u}$ is minimal and that $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})=\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf u})$.

Consequently, any sequence of truncation operators that flattens ${\mathbf v}$ also flattens ${\mathbf u}$. The existence of $x$ and $l$ in the final part of Theorem [\[Th:shape-equivalence\]](#Th:shape-equivalence){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:shape-equivalence"} follows by applying Corollary [\[C:single_equivalences\]](#C:single_equivalences){reference-type="ref" reference="C:single_equivalences"} [\[C:SE_3\]](#C:SE_3){reference-type="ref" reference="C:SE_3"} [\[C:SE_3_i\]](#C:SE_3_i){reference-type="ref" reference="C:SE_3_i"} along this same sequence of truncation operators. ◻

## Symmetries of the quantum $k$-Bruhat order {#sec:actions-on-left-operators}

Operations that preserve the $k$-Bruhat order (its symmetries) played a role in many results in [@BS-Bruhat]. We identify three operations preserving the quantum $k$-Bruhat order, which are symmetries of that order. These induce three operations on the operators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ that preserve equivalence to ${\mathbf 0}$ and $(u,k)$-equivalence, and which are fundamental to our proof of Theorem [\[Th:nonZero\]](#Th:nonZero){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:nonZero"}. We first define these operations and state the main result of this section before studying the quantum $k$-Bruhat order.

Let $\mathfrak o$ be the $n$-cycle $(1, 2, \dotsc, n)$. For a composition ${\mathbf v}={\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}\cdots {\mathbf v}_{a_1 b_1}$, define $${\color{RoyalBlue}\mathfrak o({\mathbf v})}\ =\ \mathfrak o({\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}\cdots {\mathbf v}_{a_1 b_1})
    \   \vcentcolon=\
    {\mathbf v}_{\mathfrak o(a_r),\mathfrak o(b_r)} \cdots {\mathbf v}_{\mathfrak o(a_1),\mathfrak o(b_1)}\,.$$ This cyclically shifts indices. Note that $\mathfrak o({\mathbf v}_{n,b}) = {\mathbf v}_{1,b+1}$ and $\mathfrak o({\mathbf v}_{a,n}) = {\mathbf v}_{a+1,1}$, interchanging classical and quantum operators, while other applications of $\mathfrak o$ preserve the type of the operator. When $n = 3$, we have $\mathfrak o= (1,2,3)$ and $$\arraycolsep=0.1cm
  \begin{array}{ccccccccccc}
    \valuediagram[padding=1, xmin=1, xmax=3]{{2,3}} &
     \raisebox{-3pt}{$\xrightarrow{\,\mathfrak o\,}$}&
     \valuediagram[padding=1]{{3,1}} &
     \raisebox{-3pt}{$\xrightarrow{\,\mathfrak o\,}$}&
     \valuediagram[padding=1, xmax=3]{{1,2}}
    &\raisebox{-2pt}{\quad and\quad}&
     \valuediagram[padding=1, xmin=1, xmax=3]{{3,2}} &
     \raisebox{-3pt}{$\xrightarrow{\,\mathfrak o\,}$}&
     \valuediagram[padding=1]{{1,3}} &
     \raisebox{-3pt}{$\xrightarrow{\,\mathfrak o\,}$}&
     \valuediagram[padding=1, xmax=3]{{2,1}}
   \\[1ex]
     {{\mathbf v}}_{23}&& {{\mathbf v}}_{31}&& {{\mathbf v}}_{12}&&
     {{\mathbf v}}_{32}&& {{\mathbf v}}_{13}&& {{\mathbf v}}_{21}
 \end{array}$$ Each triple cycles back to the original operator with another application of $\mathfrak o$. Let $w_0$ be the longest element in $S_n$, so that $w_0(i)=n{+}1{-}i$ for $i\in[n]$. Define $${\color{RoyalBlue}w_0({\mathbf v})}\ =\ w_0({\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}\cdots {\mathbf v}_{a_1 b_1})
  \ \vcentcolon=\
  {\mathbf v}_{w_0(b_r), w_0(a_r)} \cdots {\mathbf v}_{w_0(b_1), w_0(a_1)}\,.$$ This replaces an index $c$ by $w_0(c)=n{+}1{-}c$ and reverses the order of indices on each ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$. It preserves quantum and classical operators as $a<b\Leftrightarrow n{+}1{-}b<n{+}1{-}a$. Finally, define ${\color{RoyalBlue}\rho({\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}\cdots {\mathbf v}_{a_1b_1})}\vcentcolon= {\mathbf v}_{a_1b_1}\cdots{\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}$, which reverses the order of the operators.

We introduce notation to quantify how these operations act on monomials in the $q_i$. If $q^\alpha=q_1^{\alpha_1}\dotsb q_{n-1}^{\alpha_{n-1}}$, then set ${\color{RoyalBlue}w_0(q^\alpha)}\vcentcolon={\color{RoyalBlue}q^{w_0(\alpha)}}=q_1^{\alpha_{n-1}}\dotsb q_{n-1}^{\alpha_{1}}$. For $\mathfrak o$, this requires Laurent monomials in the $q_i$. When $i<j$, we defined $q_{i,j}$ to be $q_iq_{i+1}\dotsb q_{j-1}$. When $i>j$, set $q_{i,j}=q_{j,i}^{-1}$. For $u,w\in S_n$, define ${\color{RoyalBlue}q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}}\vcentcolon=q_{w^{-1}(n),u^{-1}(n)}$, which keeps track of how the monomial $q^\alpha$ in elements of $S_n[q]$ is affected by the cyclic shift. Note that $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}=q_{1,u^{-1}(n)}\cdot q_{w^{-1}(n),1}$, which shows that it is multiplicative in that $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}=q^{\mathfrak o(u,v)}\cdot q^{\mathfrak o(v,w)}$, for any $u,v,w\in S_n$. Observe that for $u\in S_n$ and $i<j$, if we set $w\vcentcolon= u(i,j)$, then

$$\label{Eq:qOcycle}
   q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}\ =\ \left\{ \begin{array}{rcl} 
      q_{i,j}^{-1} &\quad&\mbox{if } u(i)=n\,,\\
      q_{i,j} &\quad&\mbox{if } u(j)=n\,,\\
      1 &\quad&\mbox{otherwise}\,. \end{array}\right.$$

For $u\in S_n$, we will often write $\mathfrak ou$ for $\mathfrak o(u)$.

::: lemma
[]{#L:symmetry_qkBO label="L:symmetry_qkBO"} Let $u\in S_n$, $1\leq i\leq k<j\leq n$, and $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$. Set ${\color{RoyalBlue}w}\vcentcolon= u(i,j)$. Then the following are equivalent:

$$\begin{aligned}
    & (1)\ u\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^\alpha w, 
    & &(3)\ w_0uw_0\ \lessdot_{n-k}^q\ q^{w_0(\alpha)} w_0 w w_0, \\
    & (2)\ \mathfrak ou\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} q^\alpha \mathfrak ow, 
    & &(4)\ w w_0\ \lessdot_{n-k}^q\ q^{w_0(\alpha)} u w_0.
 
\end{aligned}$$
:::

That the operator $\mathfrak o$ preserves the quantum $k$-Bruhat order in this way follows from results of Postnikov [@Postnikov_symmetry]; for the order it is his Corollary 12 and the exponents $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}$ are related to his operator $O$ referred to as "twisted cyclic shift" in *loc. cit.* We deduce the following corollary concerning the action of the operators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$.

::: corollary
[]{#C:symmetries-q-ops label="C:symmetries-q-ops"} Let $u,w\in S_n$, $q^\alpha w\in S_n[q]$, and $a\neq b$ in $[n]$. The following are equivalent.

$$\begin{aligned}
    & (1)\ u\ \lessdot_k^q\ {\mathbf v}_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u = q^\alpha w,
    & &(3)\ w_0uw_0\ \lessdot_{n-k}^q\ \ w_0({\mathbf v}_{ab})\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} w_0uw_0 =  q^{w_0(\alpha)} w_0 w w_0,\\
    & (2)\ \mathfrak ou\ \lessdot_k^q\ \mathfrak o({\mathbf v}_{ab})\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}\mathfrak ou = q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} q^\alpha \mathfrak ow,
    & &(4)\ w w_0\ \lessdot_{n-k}^q\ {\mathbf v}_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} ww_0 = q^{w_0(\alpha)} u w_0.
 
\end{aligned}$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof of Lemma [\[L:symmetry_qkBO\]](#L:symmetry_qkBO){reference-type="ref" reference="L:symmetry_qkBO"}.* We will freely use Propositions [\[nothing-in-between-is-in-between\]](#nothing-in-between-is-in-between){reference-type="ref" reference="nothing-in-between-is-in-between"} and [\[prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers\]](#prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers"} which characterize the covers $\lessdot_k$ and $\lessdot_k^q$. Note that if $q^\alpha=1$, then $u\lessdot_k u(i,j)=w$ is a classical cover, otherwise $u\lessdot_k^q  q_{i,j}u(i,j)=q^\alpha w$, so that $q^\alpha=q_{i,j}$.

Suppose first that $q^\alpha=1$, so that $u\lessdot_k u(i,j)=w$. Thus, if $a=u(i)$ and $b=u(j)$, then $a<b$ and if $i<l<j$ then either $u(l)<a$ or $b<u(l)$. As $w_0(c)=n{+}1{-}c$, we have that $w_0u w_0 \lessdot_{n-k} w_0 w w_0$ and $w w_0 \lessdot_{n-k} u w_0$. Thus (1) implies both (3) and (4) when $q^\alpha=1$.

Still supposing that $q^\alpha=1$, if $u(j)=b<n$, then $\mathfrak ou(j)=b+1\leq n$ and we see that $\mathfrak ou \lessdot_k \mathfrak ow$. As $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}=1$, we have $\mathfrak ou \lessdot^q_k q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} \mathfrak ow$. If $n=b=u(j)$, then for every $i<l<j$, $u(l)<a=u(i)$. We have that $\mathfrak ou(i)=a+1>\mathfrak ou(j)=\mathfrak o(n)=1$ and if $i<l<j$, then $\mathfrak ou(i)=a{+}1>\mathfrak ou(l)>1=\mathfrak ou(j)$, which implies that $\mathfrak ou \lessdot^q_k q_{i,j} \mathfrak ow$. By [\[Eq:qOcycle\]](#Eq:qOcycle){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:qOcycle"}, $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}=q_{i,j}$ and we again have that $\mathfrak ou \lessdot^q_k q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} \mathfrak ow$. Thus when $q^\alpha=1$, (1) implies (2). Similar arguments show that (1) implies (2$'$), which is the statement (2), but where we use $\mathfrak o^{-1}$ in place of $\mathfrak o$ and replace $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}$ by $q^{\mathfrak o(\mathfrak o^{-1}(w),\mathfrak o^{-1}(u))}$.

Suppose now that $q^\alpha\neq 1$, so that $q^\alpha=q_{i,j}$ and $u\lessdot^q_k q_{i,j} w$. Then $a=u(i)>b=u(j)$ and if $i<l<j$, then $a>u(l)>b$. Multiplying by $w_0$ on the right shows that $$w w_0\ \lessdot^q_{n-k}\ q_{n+1-j,n+1-i} w w_0 (n{+}1-j,n{+}1-i)\ =\  w_0(q_{i,j})\, u w_0\,,$$ and further multiplying by $w_0$ on the left shows that $$w_0uw_0\  \lessdot^q_{n-k}\  q_{n+1-j,n+1-i} w_0 u w_0  (n{+}1-j,n{+}1-i)\ =\   w_0(q_{i,j})\, w_0 w w_0\,.$$ Thus (1) implies both (3) and (4) in this case.

Let us now consider (2). If $a\neq n$, then as before, $\mathfrak ou \lessdot_k^q  q_{i,j}\mathfrak ow=q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} q_{i,j}\mathfrak ow$, as $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}=1$. If $a=n$, then $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}=q_{i,j}^{-1}$ [\[Eq:qOcycle\]](#Eq:qOcycle){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:qOcycle"}. If $i<l<j$, then $n>u(l)>b=u(j)$, and we have $n\geq u(l)+1 = \mathfrak ou(l)>\mathfrak ou(j)=b+1$. Thus $\mathfrak ou \lessdot_k \mathfrak ou(i,j)=\mathfrak ow= q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} q_{i,j} \mathfrak ow$. Thus (1) implies (2). Similar arguments show that (1) implies (2$'$), which is the statement (2), but where we use $\mathfrak o^{-1}$ in place of $\mathfrak o$ and $q^{\mathfrak o(\mathfrak o^{-1}(w),\mathfrak o^{-1}(u))}$ in place of $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}$.

These arguments show that if $u\lessdot^q_k q^\alpha u(i,j)=q^\alpha w$, then (1) implies each of (2), (2$'$), (3), and (4). The implications $(3)\Rightarrow(1)$ and $(4)\Rightarrow(1)$ follow as $u\mapsto u w_0$ and $u\mapsto w_0 u w_0$ are involutions. Similarly, $\mathfrak o^{-1}\mathfrak ou=u$ and $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}q^{\mathfrak o(w,u)}=1$, thus $(1)\Rightarrow(2')$ for $\mathfrak ou \lessdot^q_k q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} q^\alpha\mathfrak ow$ is $(2)\Rightarrow(1)$. This completes the proof that the four statements are equivalent. ◻
:::

We combine the equivalences of Corollary [\[C:symmetries-q-ops\]](#C:symmetries-q-ops){reference-type="ref" reference="C:symmetries-q-ops"} with the interpretation of equivalent operators corresponding to different chains in intervals of Remark [\[Rem:k-action\]](#Rem:k-action){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action"} [\[Rem:k-action:chains\]](#Rem:k-action:chains){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:chains"} and [\[Rem:k-action:uk_equiv\]](#Rem:k-action:uk_equiv){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:uk_equiv"}.

::: lemma
[]{#lemma:actions label="lemma:actions"}

The following statements are equivalent:

1.  []{#l:actions:a label="l:actions:a"} ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf v}'$ are $(u,k)$-equivalent with ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u = {\mathbf v}' \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u = q^\alpha w$.

2.  []{#l:actions:b label="l:actions:b"} $\mathfrak o{\mathbf v}$ and $\mathfrak o{\mathbf v}'$ are $(\mathfrak ou, k)$-equivalent, with ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}\mathfrak ou={\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}\mathfrak ou = q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} q^\alpha \mathfrak ow$.

3.  []{#l:actions:c label="l:actions:c"} $w_0 {\mathbf v}$ and $w_0{\mathbf v}'$ are $(w_0uw_0, n{-}k)$-equivalent with $w_0{\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} w_0uw_0=w_0{\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} w_0 uw_0 = q^{w_0(\alpha)} w_0 w w_0$.

4.  []{#l:actions:d label="l:actions:d"} $\rho {\mathbf v}$ and $\rho {\mathbf v}'$ are $(ww_0, n{-}k)$-equivalent with $\rho{\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} w w_0=\rho{\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} ww_0 = q^{w_0(\alpha)} u w_0$.
:::

Before proving the lemma, we observe that a composition being nonzero is preserved by $\mathfrak o$, $w_0$, and $\rho$, and the same for being zero.

::: corollary
[]{#C:symmetries_bzero label="C:symmetries_bzero"} A composition ${\mathbf v}$ is equivalent to zero if and only if one (and hence all) of $\mathfrak o{\mathbf v}$, $w_0{\mathbf v}$, and $\rho{\mathbf v}$ is equivalent to zero.
:::

::: proof
*Proof of Lemma [\[lemma:actions\]](#lemma:actions){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:actions"}.* Let $u,w\in S_n$, $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$, and ${\mathbf v},{\mathbf v}'$ be compositions of left operators such that $u<_k^q {\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u={\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u = q^\alpha w$. By Remark [\[Rem:k-action\]](#Rem:k-action){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action"}[\[Rem:k-action:chains\]](#Rem:k-action:chains){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:chains"}, ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf v}'$ each correspond to a chain in $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$. We apply the equivalences of Corollary [\[C:symmetries-q-ops\]](#C:symmetries-q-ops){reference-type="ref" reference="C:symmetries-q-ops"} to each step of those chains. For [\[l:actions:c\]](#l:actions:c){reference-type="ref" reference="l:actions:c"}, we have $w_0 uw_0 <_{n-k} w_0{\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} w_0uw_0 =  w_0{\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} w_0uw_0 = q^{w_0(\alpha)}w_0 ww_0$. For [\[l:actions:d\]](#l:actions:d){reference-type="ref" reference="l:actions:d"}, note that the chain is reversed and this shows that $w w_0 <_{n-k} \rho{\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} w w_0 =  \rho{\mathbf v}'\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{n-k} w w_0 = q^{w_0(\alpha)}u w_0$.

The argument for [\[l:actions:b\]](#l:actions:b){reference-type="ref" reference="l:actions:b"} is slightly more involved as the action of $\mathfrak o$ on monomials depends upon the cover. Let $u<_k^q {\mathbf v}u =  q^{\alpha} w$ correspond to the saturated chain in $[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q$,

$$u\ =\ q^{\alpha_0}w_0\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_1}w_1\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_2}w_2
      \ \lessdot_k^q\ \dotsb\  \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\alpha_r}w_r=q^\alpha w\,.$$

By Lemma [\[L:symmetry_qkBO\]](#L:symmetry_qkBO){reference-type="ref" reference="L:symmetry_qkBO"}(2), applying $\mathfrak o$ to $q^{\alpha_i}w_i\lessdot_k^q q^{\alpha_{i+1}}w_{i+1}$ gives $q^{\alpha_i}\mathfrak ow_i\lessdot_k^q q^{\mathfrak o(w_i,w_{i+1})}q^{\alpha_{i+1}}\mathfrak ow_{i+1}$. If we set ${\color{RoyalBlue}q^{\beta_i}}\vcentcolon=q^{\mathfrak o(u,w_i)}q^{\alpha_i}$, then, as $q^{\mathfrak o(u,w_{i+1})}=q^{\mathfrak o(u,w_i)}q^{\mathfrak o(w_i,w_{i+1})}$, we obtain the saturated chain

$$\mathfrak ou\ =\ q^{\beta_0}\mathfrak ow_0\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\beta_1}\mathfrak ow_1\ \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\beta_2}\mathfrak ow_2
      \ \lessdot_k^q\ \dotsb\  \lessdot_k^q\ q^{\beta_r}\mathfrak ow_r=q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)}q^\alpha \mathfrak ow\,$$

in the interval $[\mathfrak ou,q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} q^\alpha \mathfrak ow]_k^q$, which completes the proof. ◻
:::

We have the following corollary. Here $\textit{op}$ applied to a poset gives the opposite poset, reversing all inequalities.

::: corollary
[]{#C:equivalences label="C:equivalences"} Let $u,w\in S_n$ and $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$ be such that $u <_k^q q^\alpha w$. Then we have the following isomorphisms of posets, $$[u,q^\alpha w]_k^q
    \ \simeq\ 
    [\mathfrak ou, q^{\mathfrak o(u,w)} q^\alpha \mathfrak ow]_k^q
     \ \simeq\ 
    [w_0 uw_0,q^{w_0(\alpha)} w_0 w w_0]_{n-k}^q
    \ \simeq\ 
    \textit{op}\left([ww_0,q^{w_0(\alpha)} u w_0]_{n-k}^q\right)\,.$$
:::

## Degree two relations {#S:degreeTwo}

Given a composition ${\mathbf v}$ involving quantum operators, in Section [0.1](#SS:monoid){reference-type="ref" reference="SS:monoid"} we developed the notions of equivalence to zero (${\mathbf v}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$), of ${\mathbf v}$ being nonzero (${\mathbf v}\not\equiv{\mathbf 0}$), and the notion of $(u,k)$-equivalence for nonzero compositions ${\mathbf v}$. We do not however have a notion of equivalence of operators similar to the stronger one of [@BS-Monoid] for classical operators.

By Theorem [\[Th:shape-equivalence\]](#Th:shape-equivalence){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:shape-equivalence"}, a composition ${\mathbf v}$ being equivalent to ${\mathbf 0}$ only depends upon its shape-equivalence class. Furthermore, if ${\mathbf v}$ is minimal and ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha w$, then $(u,k)$-equivalence to ${\mathbf v}$ only depends on shape-equivalence (and not specifically on $u$ or $k$). Consequently, we will simply write $\equiv$ for equivalence to ${\mathbf 0}$ and $(u,k)$-equivalence of minimal compositions.

A composition ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}$ is minimal when $\{a,b\}\neq\{c,d\}$. We identify all relations among minimal compositions that preserve equivalence. These relations are depicted in Figures [4](#fig:crossing-operators){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:crossing-operators"},  [5](#fig:degree-2-nonzero-relations){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:degree-2-nonzero-relations"},  [6](#fig:more-degree-2-zero-relations){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:more-degree-2-zero-relations"}, and  [7](#fig:touching-orbit){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:touching-orbit"}. This uses the notion of crossing and noncrossing sets from Section [\[sec:classic-mn-rule\]](#sec:classic-mn-rule){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:classic-mn-rule"}.

::: lemma
[]{#Lem:Rel label="Lem:Rel"} The following describe all relations among minimal compositions of two left operators; those not of the form ${\mathbf v}_{ab} {\mathbf v}_{ba}$ or ${\mathbf v}_{ab} {\mathbf v}_{ab}$.

1.  If $\{a,b\}\cap\{c,d\}=\emptyset$, then the operators commute, ${{\mathbf v}}_{ab}{{\mathbf v}}_{cd} \equiv {{\mathbf v}}_{cd}{{\mathbf v}}_{ab}$.

    1.  []{#Rel:1:i label="Rel:1:i"} If $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c,d\}$ are crossing, then ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}  \equiv {\mathbf 0}$.

    2.  []{#Rel:1:ii label="Rel:1:ii"} If $a<d<c<b$ or $a>b>c>d$, then ${\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{ab}$ and ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}$ are both zero, $$\valuediagram[scale=1.1]{{1,4},{3,2}}\equiv
                     \valuediagram[scale=1.1]{{2,1},{4,3}}\equiv
                     \valuediagram[scale=1.1]{{3,2},{1,4}}\equiv
                     \valuediagram[scale=1.1]{{4,3},{2,1}}\equiv {\mathbf 0}\,.$$

    3.  []{#Rel:1:iii label="Rel:1:iii"} In all other cases when $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c,d\}$ are noncrossing, ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}\equiv{\mathbf v}_{cd}{\mathbf v}_{ab}$, and both are nonzero.

2.  If $\#(\{a,b\}\cap\{c,d\})=1$, then the operators ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ and ${\mathbf v}_{cd}$ do not commute.

    1.  []{#Rel:2:i label="Rel:2:i"} For $a<b<c$, each of the six compositions, ${\mathbf v}_{ba}{\mathbf v}_{ac}$, ${\mathbf v}_{cb}{\mathbf v}_{ba}$, ${\mathbf v}_{ac}{\mathbf v}_{cb}$, ${\mathbf v}_{ac}{\mathbf v}_{ba}$, ${\mathbf v}_{ba}{\mathbf v}_{cb}$, and ${\mathbf v}_{cb}{\mathbf v}_{ac}$ is equivalent to ${\mathbf 0}$.

    2.  []{#Rel:2:ii label="Rel:2:ii"} For any distinct $a,b,c$, both ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{ac}$ and ${\mathbf v}_{ba}{\mathbf v}_{ca}$ are equivalent to ${\mathbf 0}$.

    3.  []{#Rel:2:iii label="Rel:2:iii"} The six remaining compositions with $\#(\{a,b\}\cap\{c,d\})=1$ are nonzero and pairwise inequivalent, and are depicted in Figure [8](#fig:nonzero-touching-operators){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:nonzero-touching-operators"}.
:::

::: remark
The composition ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{ab}$ is zero. When $a<b$, this is by [\[Eq:monoid-relations\]](#Eq:monoid-relations){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:monoid-relations"}(*ii*). The case $a>b$ follows from this by cyclic shift (applying $\mathfrak o$) and Corollary [\[C:symmetries_bzero\]](#C:symmetries_bzero){reference-type="ref" reference="C:symmetries_bzero"}.

The composition ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{ba}$ is nonzero and ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{ba}\not\equiv {\mathbf v}_{ba}{\mathbf v}_{ab}$. If $a<b$, then ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{ba}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u$ equals $q_k u$ if $u(k)=b$ and $u(k{+}1)=a$ and is ${\mathbf 0}$ otherwise. If $a>b$, then ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{ba}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u$ equals $q_k u$ if $u(k)=a$ and $u(k{+}1)=b$ and is ${\mathbf 0}$ otherwise.$\diamond$
:::

::: proof
*Proof of Lemma [\[Lem:Rel\]](#Lem:Rel){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem:Rel"}.* Consider compositions ${\mathbf v}_{ab}{\mathbf v}_{cd}$ with $\{a,b\}\cap\{c,d\}=\emptyset$. Each operator may be either quantum or classical, there are 24 choices in all.

The four compositions in (1)[\[Rel:1:ii\]](#Rel:1:ii){reference-type="ref" reference="Rel:1:ii"} form a single orbit under $\mathfrak o$ by Lemma [\[lemma:actions\]](#lemma:actions){reference-type="ref" reference="lemma:actions"}, and it suffices to show that one is equivalent to ${\mathbf 0}$. As in Section [\[cohomology-ring-of-the-flag-manifold\]](#cohomology-ring-of-the-flag-manifold){reference-type="ref" reference="cohomology-ring-of-the-flag-manifold"}, write permutations in one-line notation, placing " $\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\hspace{0.55pt}\color{magenta}\rule{0.75pt}{9pt}}\hspace{0.55pt}$ " just after the $k$th position. Thus $52\raisebox{-1pt}{\hspace{0.55pt}\color{magenta}\rule{0.75pt}{11pt}}\hspace{0.55pt}6134\in S_6$ has $k=2$, and $(\dotsc a\dotsc \raisebox{-1pt}{\hspace{0.55pt}\color{magenta}\rule{0.75pt}{11pt}}\hspace{0.55pt}b \dotsc)$ is a permutation $w$ with $w^{-1}(a)\leq k$ and $w(k{+}1)=b$.

Let $a<b<c<d$ be the values involved in the first composition in (1)(ii) of operators, $\valuediagram{{1,4},{3,2}}={\mathbf v}_{cb}{\mathbf v}_{ad}$, and suppose that $u\in S_n$ is such that ${\mathbf v}_{cb} {\mathbf v}_{ad}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u\neq{\mathbf 0}$. Then $u\lessdot_k (a,d)u$, and writing $v \vcentcolon= (a,d)u$, we have that $v$ has the form $(\dotsc d\dotsc \raisebox{-1pt}{\hspace{0.55pt}\color{magenta}\rule{0.75pt}{11pt}}\hspace{0.55pt}\dotsc a \dotsc)$, with neither $b$ nor $c$ appearing between $d$ and $a$. Since ${\mathbf v}_{cb}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}v\neq{\mathbf 0}$, not only must $c$ be left of $\raisebox{-1pt}{\hspace{0.55pt}\color{magenta}\rule{0.75pt}{11pt}}\hspace{0.55pt}$ and $b$ right of $\raisebox{-1pt}{\hspace{0.55pt}\color{magenta}\rule{0.75pt}{11pt}}\hspace{0.55pt}$, but every value $e$ of $v$ at a position between those of $c$ and $b$ satisfies $b<e<c$. But this forces $c$ and $b$ to lie between $a$ and $d$ in $v$, a contradiction. Thus ${\mathbf v}_{cb} {\mathbf v}_{ad}\equiv{\mathbf 0}$.

## Crossing products

For a composition ${\mathbf v}={\mathbf v}_{a_rb_r}\cdots {\mathbf v}_{a_1b_1}$, let $\min({\mathbf v})$ and $\max({\mathbf v})$ be the minimum and maximum elements of its support, $\{a_r,b_r,\dots, a_1,b_1\}$.

A composition ${\mathbf v}$ defines a (multi)graph whose vertices are its support. It has one edge between $a$ and $b$ for each operator ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ appearing in ${\mathbf v}$. We say that ${\mathbf v}$ is if its graph is connected, that ${\mathbf v}$ is a if its graph is a path with $a_1$ or $b_1$ as an endpoint and $\#(\{a_i,b_i\}\cap\{a_{i+1},b_{i+1}\})=1$ for all $i$. It is a if its graph is tree. A composition of two connected compositions is if their supports are disjoint and crossing. Note that noncrossing products of trees are minimal. Indeed, each tree is minimal, so a noncrossing product of trees is a noncrossing product of minimal compositions of operators, and hence is minimal. Figure [8](#fig:nonzero-touching-operators){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:nonzero-touching-operators"} shows all nonzero paths of length two and Figure [3](#F:symmetry-chains){reference-type="ref" reference="F:symmetry-chains"} shows some compositions that are trees but not paths. Figure [4](#fig:crossing-operators){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:crossing-operators"} shows all crossing compositions ${\mathbf v}_{ab} {\mathbf v}_{cd}$, and all are equivalent to zero.

::: lemma
[]{#L:crossing-supports label="L:crossing-supports"} A crossing composition of connected minimal operators is equivalent to zero.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Suppose that ${\mathbf v}$ and ${\mathbf v}'$ are connected compositions whose supports are disjoint and crossing. Then there exists $l<r$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})$ and $l'<r'$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v}')$ with $\{l,r\}$ and $\{l',r'\}$ crossing. We may assume that $l < l'$, which implies that $l < l' < r < r'$. As the graph of ${\mathbf v}$ is connected, it contains a sequence of edges connecting $l$ to $r$. Similarly, the graph of ${\mathbf v}'$ contains a sequence of edges connecting $l'$ to $r'$.

In the sequence of edges connecting $l$ to $r$, let $(a,b)$ be the first edge with exactly one endpoint between $l'$ and $r'$---this corresponds to an operator ${\mathbf v}_{ab}$ in ${\mathbf v}$. Thus either $l'$ is between $a$ and $b$, or $r'$ is between $a$ and $b$. Suppose that $r'$ is between $a$ and $b$. Let $(c,d)$ be the first edge in the path from $l'$ to $r'$ with exactly one endpoint between $a$ and $b$---it corresponds to an operator ${\mathbf v}_{cd}$ in ${\mathbf v}'$. Thus $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c,d\}$ are crossing. (If $l'$ is between $a$ and $b$, a similar argument gives an edge $(c,d)$ such that $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c,d\}$ are crossing.)

By Lemma [\[Lem:Rel\]](#Lem:Rel){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem:Rel"}(1)(ii), ${\mathbf v}_{ab} {\mathbf v}_{cd} \equiv{\mathbf 0}$. As $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})\cap \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v}')=\emptyset$, each operator in ${\mathbf v}$ commutes with each operator in ${\mathbf v}'$ by Lemma [\[Lem:Rel\]](#Lem:Rel){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem:Rel"}(1). By Remark [\[Rem:k-action\]](#Rem:k-action){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action"} [\[Rem:k-action:subword\]](#Rem:k-action:subword){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:subword"}, these commutations give equivalent operators. Writing ${\mathbf v}=\mathbf{A} {\mathbf v}_{ab} \mathbf{B}$ and ${\mathbf v}'=\mathbf{C}  {\mathbf v}_{cd}  \mathbf{D}$, we have $${\mathbf v}{\mathbf v}'
        \ =\  \big(\mathbf{A} {\mathbf v}_{ab} \mathbf{B}
          \big)
          \big(\mathbf{C}  {\mathbf v}_{cd}  \mathbf{D}
          \big)
       \  \equiv\  \mathbf{AC} \big({\mathbf v}_{ab} {\mathbf v}_{cd}\big)\mathbf{BD}\  \equiv\  {\mathbf 0}\,.$$ The first equivalence is by commutation of operators and the second is by Remark [\[Rem:k-action\]](#Rem:k-action){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action"}[\[Rem:k-action:zero_compose\]](#Rem:k-action:zero_compose){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:zero_compose"}. ◻
:::

We single out a special case of Lemma [\[L:crossing-supports\]](#L:crossing-supports){reference-type="ref" reference="L:crossing-supports"}.

::: corollary
[]{#corollary-classical-product-times-quantum label="corollary-classical-product-times-quantum"} Suppose that ${\mathbf u}$ is a connected composition of classical operators that is minimal, $a, b \notin \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf u})$, $a<b$, and ${\mathbf u}{\mathbf v}_{ba} \not\equiv {\mathbf 0}$. If $l = \min({\mathbf u})$ and $r = \max({\mathbf u})$, then $$\begin{gathered}
        a < b < l < r
        \quad\text{or}\quad
        l < r < a < b
        \quad\text{or}\quad
        l < a < b < r
        \quad\text{or}\quad
        a < l < r < b\ .






    
\end{gathered}$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The graphs of ${\mathbf u}$ and ${\mathbf v}_{ba}$ are connected, as that of ${\mathbf v}_{ba}$ is a single edge. Since they have disjoint support and ${\mathbf u}{\mathbf v}_{ba} \not\equiv {\mathbf 0}$, Lemma [\[L:crossing-supports\]](#L:crossing-supports){reference-type="ref" reference="L:crossing-supports"} implies their supports are noncrossing. ◻
:::

We deduce that a crossing product of a classical tree (no cycle in its graph and no repeated edges) and a quantum operator is zero.

::: corollary
[]{#T*vq=0 label="T*vq=0"} Let $\mathbf{T}$ be a composition of classical operators whose graph is a tree. If $a<b$ with $\min(\mathbf{T}) < a < \max(\mathbf{T})$ and $a \notin \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T})$, then $\mathbf{T}{\mathbf v}_{ba} \equiv {\mathbf 0}$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Note that $\mathbf{T}$ is minimal. Suppose that $\mathbf{T}{\mathbf v}_{ba} \not\equiv {\mathbf 0}$. Then there exist $k<n$ and a permutation $u\in S_n$ such that $\mathbf{T}{\mathbf v}_{ba}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u\neq{\mathbf 0}$. Suppose that ${\mathbf v}_{ba}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u = q_{i, j} v$. Then $i=u^{-1}(b)\leq k <j=u^{-1}(a)$ and $a=v(i)$. By Proposition [\[prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers\]](#prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:quantum-k-bruhat-covers"}, if $i<l<j$, then $a<u(l)=v(l)<b$, and by Remark [\[Rem:k-action\]](#Rem:k-action){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action"}[\[Rem:k-action:chains\]](#Rem:k-action:chains){reference-type="ref" reference="Rem:k-action:chains"}, $\mathbf{T}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}v\neq{\mathbf 0}$.

Since $\min(\mathbf{T})<a<\max(\mathbf{T})$, $a\not\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T})$, and $\mathbf{T}$ is a classical tree, there is an operator ${\mathbf v}_{cd}$ in $\mathbf{T}$ with $c<a<d$. Write $\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{T}'' {\mathbf v}_{cd} \mathbf{T}'$ and set $w \vcentcolon= \mathbf{T}' \mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}v\neq {\mathbf 0}$. As $\mathbf{T}'$ is classical, $v\leq_k w$. By Proposition [\[nothing-in-between-is-in-between\]](#nothing-in-between-is-in-between){reference-type="ref" reference="nothing-in-between-is-in-between"}, if $l\leq k$, then $w(l)\geq v(l)$ and we have $a=w(i)$ as $a\not\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}')$.

We also have ${\mathbf v}_{cd}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}w\neq{\mathbf 0}$, so that $w\lessdot_k (c,d)w$. Consider the position $l(\leq k)$ of $c$ in $w$, $c=w(l)$. As $c<a<d$ and $d$ is to the right of $k$ (and $i$), we cannot have $l<i$, by Proposition [\[nothing-in-between-is-in-between\]](#nothing-in-between-is-in-between){reference-type="ref" reference="nothing-in-between-is-in-between"}. If $i<l$, then $a<v(l)\leq w(l)=c$, contradicting $c<a$. Thus $\mathbf{T}{\mathbf v}_{ba}\equiv {\mathbf 0}$. ◻
:::

::: theorem
[]{#thm:quantum_path label="thm:quantum_path"} Every path is either a row or a column and a path contains at most one quantum operator. Paths can be cyclically shifted to a classical row or to a classical column. The only path that can be shifted to both consists of a single operator.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We split the proof into four parts. Let ${\mathbf v}$ be a path, which is nonzero.

*(1) Paths consisting only of classical operators.* Suppose the path ${\mathbf v}= {\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}\dotsb{\mathbf v}_{a_1 b_1}$ is a composition of classical operators (for all $i$, $a_i<b_i$). Then every pair of adjacent compositions ${\mathbf v}_{a_{i+1},b_{i+1}}{\mathbf v}_{a_i,b_i}$ is nonzero. Suppose that $a_1$ is an endpoint. As ${\mathbf v}\not\equiv{\mathbf 0}$, the relations [\[Eq:monoid-relations\]](#Eq:monoid-relations){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:monoid-relations"}(*ii*) imply that $a_{i+1}=b_i$ for all $i$. Similarly, if $b_1$ is an endpoint, then we must have $a_i=b_{i+1}$ for all $i$. Thus, ${\mathbf v}$ is either a connected classical row or a connected classical column. If ${\mathbf v}= {\mathbf v}_{a_r b_r}\dotsb{\mathbf v}_{a_1 b_1}$ with for example, $a_i<b_i$ and $a_{i+1}=b_i$ for all $i$, then we showed that ${\mathbf v}\not\equiv {\mathbf 0}$, and so ${\mathbf v}$ is a connected classical row. If instead $a_i=b_{i+1}$, then applying $\rho$ or $w_0$ shows that ${\mathbf v}$ is a connected classical column. Such paths are shown in [\[Eq:classicalRowColumn\]](#Eq:classicalRowColumn){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:classicalRowColumn"} in Figure [9](#fig:paths){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:paths"}.

*(2) Composition of a connected classical column/row with a quantum operator.* Suppose that the path ${\mathbf v}={\mathbf v}_{a_ra_{r-1}} \dotsb {\mathbf v}_{a_2 a_1}{\mathbf v}_{a_1b} \cdot {\color{BrickRed}{\mathbf v}_{ba}}$ is a composition of a connected classical column with single quantum operator ${\color{BrickRed}{\mathbf v}_{ba}}$. Then $a_r<a_{r-1}<\dotsb<a_2<a_1<b$, $a<b$, and $a\not\in\{a_r,\dotsc,a_2,a_1\}$. By Corollary [\[T\*vq=0\]](#T*vq=0){reference-type="ref" reference="T*vq=0"}, we must have $a < a_r < \dotsb < a_1 < b$. Such a path is illustrated in [\[Eq:InitialLemma\]](#Eq:InitialLemma){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:InitialLemma"} on the right in Figure [9](#fig:paths){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:paths"}.

Applying $\mathfrak o^{n{+}1{-}a_r}$ to ${\mathbf v}$ cyclically shifts it to a classical column. We illustrate this.

$$\valuediagram[scale=1., critical windows={(1,2)}]{{5,1}, {4,5}, {3,4}, {2,3}}
    \ \raisebox{5pt}{$\xrightarrow{\;\mathfrak o\;}$}\
     \valuediagram[scale=1., critical windows={(2,3)}]{{1,2}, {5,1}, {4,5}, {3,4}}
    \ \raisebox{5pt}{$\xrightarrow{\;\mathfrak o\;}$}\
     \valuediagram[scale=1., critical windows={(3,4)}]{{2,3}, {1,2}, {5,1}, {4,5}}
    \ \raisebox{5pt}{$\xrightarrow{\;\mathfrak o\;}$}\
     \valuediagram[scale=1., critical windows={(4,5)}]{{3,4}, {2,3}, {1,2}, {5,1}}
    \ \raisebox{5pt}{$\xrightarrow{\;\mathfrak o\;}$}\
     \valuediagram[scale=1.]{{4,5}, {3,4}, {2,3}, {1,2}}$$

If instead ${\mathbf v}={\mathbf v}_{a_ra_{r-1}} \dotsb {\mathbf v}_{a_2 a_1}{\mathbf v}_{a_1b} \cdot {\color{BrickRed}{\mathbf v}_{ba}}$ is the composition of a classical connected row and a quantum operator ${\color{BrickRed}{\mathbf v}_{ba}}$, then we may apply $w_0$ to convert it to the composition of a connected classical column and a quantum operator. As $w_0\mathfrak o=\mathfrak o^{n-1}w_0$, arguments for a column imply this for rows.

*(3) Paths may contain at most one quantum operator.* Suppose that ${\mathbf v}$ contains at least two quantum operators. By Figures  [6](#fig:more-degree-2-zero-relations){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:more-degree-2-zero-relations"} and [7](#fig:touching-orbit){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:touching-orbit"}, no two quantum operators are adjacent. Restrict ${\mathbf v}$ to two of its quantum operators and the classical operators between them. This segment remains a nonzero path. Applying $w_0$ if necessary, we may assume that the classical segment is a column, so that ${\mathbf v}$ has the form

$${\mathbf v}\ =\ {\color{BrickRed}{\mathbf v}_{b a_r}}\,{\mathbf v}_{a_r a_{r-1}}\dotsb {\mathbf v}_{a_2 a_1}\, {\color{BrickRed}{\mathbf v}_{a_1 c}} \,,\quad\mbox{where }\
    a_r<b\,, \ a_r<\dotsb<a_2<a_1\,,\ \mbox{ and }c<a_1 \,.$$

Applying Part *(2)* to the segment omitting ${\mathbf v}_{b a_r}$ shows that $c<a_r$. Applying $\rho$ and then $\omega_0$ to the segment omitting ${\mathbf v}_{a_1 c}$, Part *(2)* shows that $a_1<b$. Applying $\mathfrak o^{n+1-b}$ to ${\mathbf v}$ gives: $$\mathfrak o^{n+1-b}{\mathbf v}\ =\ {\mathbf v}_{\alpha_r 1}\,{\mathbf v}_{\alpha_r \alpha_{l-1}}\dotsb
   {\mathbf v}_{\alpha_2 \alpha_1}\, {\color{BrickRed}{\mathbf v}_{\alpha_1,\gamma}}\,,\quad\mbox{where }\
    1<\gamma<\alpha_r<\dotsb<\alpha_2<\alpha_1 \,.$$ $$\valuediagram[scale=1.25, xmin=1, xmax=9]
   {{6,2}, {5,6},{4,5},{3,4}, {8,3}}
   \quad \raisebox{10pt}{$\xrightarrow{\ \mathfrak o^{n+1-b}\ }$} \quad
   \valuediagram[scale=1.25, xmin=1, xmax=9]{{8,4}, {7,8},{6,7},{5,6}, {1,5}}$$ As $1<\gamma<\alpha_r$, this is zero by Corollary [\[T\*vq=0\]](#T*vq=0){reference-type="ref" reference="T*vq=0"} and Part *(2)*, thus ${\mathbf v}$ cannot contain two quantum operators.

*(4) Paths containing a single quantum operator.* Let ${\mathbf v}$ be a path containing a single quantum operator ${\mathbf v}_{ba}$ ($a<b$). Arguing as above, we may suppose ${\mathbf v}$ that takes the form $${\mathbf v}=
    {\mathbf v}_{b_m b_{m-1}} \cdots {\mathbf v}_{b_2 b_1} {\mathbf v}_{b_1 b}
    \, {\color{BrickRed}{\mathbf v}_{ba}}\, 
    {\mathbf v}_{a a_{l}} {\mathbf v}_{a_l a_{l-1}} \dotsb{\mathbf v}_{a_2 a_1}$$ with $a < a_l < \dotsb < a_{2} < a_1$ and $b_{m} < \cdots < b_{1} < b$. If $a_1<b_m$, then applying $\mathfrak o^{n{+}1{-}b_m}$ to ${\mathbf v}$ cyclically shifts it to a classical column, which completes the proof in this case. This is illustrated on the right in [\[Eq:quantumRowColumn\]](#Eq:quantumRowColumn){reference-type="eqref" reference="Eq:quantumRowColumn"} in Figure [9](#fig:paths){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:paths"}.

Suppose that $b_{m}\leq a_1$. Since the graph of ${\mathbf v}$ is a path, $b_{m}\not\in\{a_1,\dotsc,a_r\}$. We have $a<b_{m}$ by Part *(2)*, so there is some $i$ with $a_{i+1}<b_{m}<a_i$ (if $i=l$, then $a=a_{l+1}$). Then the cyclic shift $\mathfrak o^{n-b_{m}}{\mathbf v}$ is a path with two quantum operators coming from the shifts of ${\mathbf v}_{a_{i+1} a_i}$ and ${\mathbf v}_{b_m b_{m-1}}$, as illustrated by $$\valuediagram[baseline=2ex]  {{3,5},{2,3},{1,2}, {7,1}, {6,7},{4,6}}
   \xrightarrow{\;\mathfrak o\;}
  \valuediagram[baseline=2ex]  {{4,6},{3,4},{2,3},{1,2}, {7,1}, {5,7}}
   \xrightarrow{\;\mathfrak o\;}
  \valuediagram[baseline=2ex]  {{5,7},{4,5},{3,4},{2,3},{1,2},  {6,1}}
   \xrightarrow{\;\mathfrak o\;}
  \valuediagram[baseline=2ex]  {{6,1}, {5,6},{4,5},{3,4},{2,3}, {7,2}}\ .$$ But this is a contradiction, as a nonzero path has at most two quantum operators. ◻

## Products of trees {#SS:trees}

A nonzero composition ${\mathbf v}=\mathbf{T}_k \cdots \mathbf{T}_2 \mathbf{T}_1$ is a if each $\mathbf{T}_i$ is a tree and each pair of trees has noncrossing supports. A forest is minimal, so that each pair of trees commute, and commuting them gives an equivalent operator.

::: theorem
[]{#main-theorem label="main-theorem"} Suppose that ${\mathbf v}$ is a forest. Let $u\in S_n$ and $k<n$ be such that ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u\neq{\mathbf 0}$. Then ${\mathbf v}$ is $(u,k)$-equivalent to a composition $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{C}$ of a row $\mathbf{R}$ and a column $\mathbf{C}$. Moreover, there exists $r \in {\mathbb N}$ such that $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{C})=\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C})$ is classical, and $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C})\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}\mathfrak o^r(u)\neq{\mathbf 0}$.
:::

The proof is split into two parts.

### The classical case

The case of Theorem [\[main-theorem\]](#main-theorem){reference-type="ref" reference="main-theorem"} in which all the operators are classical follows from the study of minimal permutations in [@BS-iso Sect. 6.2], and the discussion in Section [\[minimal-permutations\]](#minimal-permutations){reference-type="ref" reference="minimal-permutations"}. Recall that ${\mathcal L}$ is the rank function in the Grassmannian--Bruhat order on $S_n$.

::: proposition
[]{#BS2002 label="BS2002"} If ${\mathbf v}$ is a composition of classical operators that is a forest, then it is equivalent to a composition $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{C}$ of a classical row and a classical column.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Suppose that $\zeta=(a_r,b_r)\dotsb(a_1,b_1)$ is the product of transpositions whose corresponding graph is a forest. Using that this graph is a forest, induction on $r$ shows that $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\zeta)=\{a_r,b_r,\dotsc,a_1,b_1\}$. (*A priori*, it is only a subset.) The same induction shows that $\zeta$ is a product of disjoint cycles, with each cycle corresponding to a tree in the graph of $\zeta$.

Now suppose that ${\mathbf v}={\mathbf v}_{a_r,b_r}\dotsb{\mathbf v}_{a_1,b_1}$ is a nonzero tree with corresponding permutation $\zeta\vcentcolon=(a_r,b_r)\dotsb(a_1,b_1)$, which is a cycle--so $s(\zeta)=1$--and which satisfies ${\mathcal L}(\zeta)=r$. Since ${\mathbf v}$ is a tree, $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}({\mathbf v})=\{a_r,b_r,\dotsc,a_1,b_1\}$, which equals $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\zeta)$, has cardinality $r{+}1$. Thus ${\mathcal L}(\zeta)=\#\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\zeta)-s(\zeta)$, and we conclude that $\zeta$ is a minimal cycle.

When ${\mathbf v}$ is a forest, its corresponding permutation $\zeta$ is a noncrossing product of minimal cycles, and is therefore a minimal permutation. By Proposition [\[P:peaklessChains\]](#P:peaklessChains){reference-type="ref" reference="P:peaklessChains"}, the interval $[e,\zeta]_\preceq$ has a peakless chain. Under the identification of chains in $[e,\zeta]_\preceq$ with compositions equivalent to ${\mathbf v}$ from Proposition [\[Prop:monoid\]](#Prop:monoid){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:monoid"}(*2*), this peakless chain corresponds to a composition $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{C}$ of a classical row and a classical column. ◻
:::

### Proof of Theorem [\[main-theorem\]](#main-theorem){reference-type="ref" reference="main-theorem"}

We use induction on the number of operators in ${\mathbf v}$. Let ${\mathbf v}_{ef}$ be its rightmost operator so that ${\mathbf v}=\mathbf{F}{\mathbf v}_{ef}$, where $\mathbf{F}$ is a forest. As ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u$ is nonzero, ${\mathbf v}_{ef}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u$ is nonzero. Then there exist $\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{n-1}$ and $v\in S_n$ such that ${\mathbf v}_{ef}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u=q^\alpha v$, and $\mathbf{F}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}v$ is nonzero. By induction, $\mathbf{F}$ is $(v,k)$-equivalent to $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{C}$, where $\mathbf{R}$ is a row and $\mathbf{C}$ is a column, and there exists $r \in {\mathbb N}$ such that $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{F})$ is $(\mathfrak o^r v, k)$-equivalent to $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C})$, where the later is classical. Thus

$$\mathfrak o^r({\mathbf v}) \text{ is } (\mathfrak o^r u,k)\text{- equivalent to } \mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C}){\mathbf v}_{cd}\,,$$

where $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})$ is a classical row, $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C})$ is a classical column, and ${\mathbf v}_{cd}=\mathfrak o^r({\mathbf v}_{ef})$.

If $c<d$, then $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C}){\mathbf v}_{cd}$ is a classical forest and by Proposition [\[BS2002\]](#BS2002){reference-type="ref" reference="BS2002"}, it is $(\mathfrak o^r u, k)$-equivalent to the composition of a classical row and a classical column, $\mathbf{R}' \mathbf{C}'$. But then ${\mathbf v}$ is $(u, k)$-equivalent to $\mathfrak o^{-r}(\mathbf{R}')\mathfrak o^{-r}(\mathbf{C}')$, which is a composition of a row and a column that admits a cyclic shift that is classical.

Suppose now that $c>d$. Set $a=d$ and $b=c$ so that ${\mathbf v}_{cd}={\mathbf v}_{ba}$ with $a<b$, to conform to our running notation for quantum operators. Since $\mathbf{F}$ is a forest, $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C})$ is a classical forest. Grouping components of $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C})$ together gives noncrossing (classical) trees $\mathbf{T}_1,\dotsc,\mathbf{T}_m$ such that $\mathfrak o^r({\mathbf v})$ is $(\mathfrak o^r u, k)$-equivalent to $(\mathbf{T}_m\dotsb\mathbf{T}_1){\mathbf v}_{ba}$. The proof of Theorem [\[main-theorem\]](#main-theorem){reference-type="ref" reference="main-theorem"} follows from Lemma [\[cyclic-shift-classical-trees-times-quantum\]](#cyclic-shift-classical-trees-times-quantum){reference-type="ref" reference="cyclic-shift-classical-trees-times-quantum"} below, and since ${\mathbf v}$ is a forest, an application of Proposition [\[BS2002\]](#BS2002){reference-type="ref" reference="BS2002"}.

::: lemma
[]{#cyclic-shift-classical-trees-times-quantum label="cyclic-shift-classical-trees-times-quantum"} Let $a<b$ and suppose that

1.  $\mathbf{T}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{T}_m$ are disjoint, noncrossing, classical trees;

2.  $\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1 {\mathbf v}_{ba} \not\equiv {\mathbf 0}$; and

3.  the graph of $\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1 {\mathbf v}_{ba}$ is a forest.

Then $\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1 {\mathbf v}_{ba}$ admits a cyclic shift that is a product of classical operators.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We have three cases according to $\#(\{a,b\}\cap \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1))$.

Suppose that $\emptyset=\{a,b\}\cap \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1)$. Since for each $i \in [m]$, we have $\mathbf{T}_i {\mathbf v}_{ba}\not\equiv {\mathbf 0}$, Corollary [\[T\*vq=0\]](#T*vq=0){reference-type="ref" reference="T*vq=0"} implies that either $\max(\mathbf{T}_i)<a$ or $a<\min(\mathbf{T}_i)$, for each $i\in[m]$. Hence, $\mathfrak o^{-a}(\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1 {\mathbf v}_{ba})$ is a composition of classical operators. We present an example.

$$\begin{array}{c}
      \valuediagram[scale=1, critical windows={(5,6)}]{
                    {12,5},
                    {10,11}, {9,10},
                    {1,3}, {3,4}, {2,3},
                    {14,15}, {15,16}, {13,15},
                    {6,7}, {7,8}}
    \end{array}
 
        \xrightarrow{~~\mathfrak o^{11}~}
    \begin{array}{c}
      \valuediagram[scale=1]{
                    {7,16},
                    {5,6}, {4,5},
                    {12,14}, {14,15}, {13,14},
                    {9,10}, {10,11}, {8,10},
                    {1,2}, {2,3}}
     \end{array}$$

Suppose that $1=\#(\{a,b\}\cap \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1))$. Applying $\omega_0$ if necessary, we may assume that $b\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1)$. As the trees have disjoint support, $b$ lies in the support of exactly one tree. As the trees commute, we may replace this operator by an equivalent operator (obtained by commuting the trees) and assume that $b \in \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_m)$. By Corollary [\[T\*vq=0\]](#T*vq=0){reference-type="ref" reference="T*vq=0"}, $a < \min(\mathbf{T}_m)$ for otherwise $\mathbf{T}_m {\mathbf v}_{ba} \equiv {\mathbf 0}$, contradicting that $\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1{\mathbf v}_{ba}$ is nonzero.

Since $\emptyset=\{a,b\}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_{m-1}\dotsb\mathbf{T}_1)$, the previous argument implies that for $i\in[m{-}1]$ either $\max(\mathbf{T}_i)<a$ or $a<\min(\mathbf{T}_i)$. Thus $\mathfrak o^{-a}(\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1 {\mathbf v}_{ba})$ is a composition of classical operators. We present an example. $$\begin{array}{c}
         \valuediagram[scale=1, critical windows={(5,6)}]{
             {11,5},
             {7,8}, {6,7},
             {1,3}, {3,4}, {2,3},
             {14,15}, {15,16}, {13,15},
             {9,11}, {11,12}, {10,11}}
     \end{array}
       \xrightarrow{~~\mathfrak o^{11}~}
     \begin{array}{c}
         \valuediagram[scale=1]{
             {6,16},
             {2,3},{1,2},
             {12,14},{14,15},{13,14},
             {9,10},{10,11},{8,10},
             {4,6},{6,7},{5,6}}
     \end{array}$$

Finally, suppose that $\{a,b\}\subset \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1)$. As the trees are disjoint, each of $a$ and $b$ lies in the support of a unique tree. These cannot be the same tree, for if $a,b\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_i)$, then the graph of $\mathbf{T}_i {\mathbf v}_{ba}$ is not a tree, contradicting that $\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1 {\mathbf v}_{ba}$ is a forest.

As the $\mathbf{T}_i$ commute, we may replace this operator by an equivalent operator and assume that $a \in \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_{m})$ and $b \in \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_{m-1})$. Let $i\in[m{-}1]$. As $\mathbf{T}_i v_{ba}\not\equiv{\mathbf 0}$, Corollary [\[T\*vq=0\]](#T*vq=0){reference-type="ref" reference="T*vq=0"} implies that either $\max(\mathbf{T}_i)<a$ or $a<\min(\mathbf{T}_i)$.

If $\max(\mathbf{T}_i)<a$, then as $a\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_m)$, we have $\max(\mathbf{T}_i)<\max(\mathbf{T}_m)$.

If $a<\min(\mathbf{T}_i)$, then as $a\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathbf{T}_m)$ and the trees $\mathbf{T}_i$ and $\mathbf{T}_m$ are noncrossing, we have $\max(\mathbf{T}_m)<\min(\mathbf{T}_i)$.

Since $a\leq \max(\mathbf{T}_m) < b$, this implies that $\mathfrak o^{-\max(\mathbf{T}_{m})}(\mathbf{T}_m \cdots \mathbf{T}_1 {\mathbf v}_{ba})$ is a composition of classical operators, which completes the proof. We present an example. $$\begin{array}{c}
         \valuediagram[scale=1, critical windows={(7,8)}]{
             {11,6},
             {2,3},{1,2},{2,4},
             {14,16},{13,14},{14,15},
             {10,11},{9,10},{10,12},{8,9},
             {5,6},{6,7}}
     \end{array}
       \xrightarrow{~~\mathfrak o^{-7}~}
     \begin{array}{c}
         \valuediagram[scale=1]{
             {4,14},
             {10,11},{9,10},{10,12},
             {7,9},{6,7},{7,8},
             {3,4},{2,3},{3,5},{1,2},
             {13,14},{14,15}}
     \end{array}
     \qedhere$$ ◻
:::

## Proof of Theorem [\[Th:nonZero\]](#Th:nonZero){reference-type="ref" reference="Th:nonZero"} {#sec:final}

Let $[u,q^\alpha w]^q_k$ be a minimal interval, and consider a composition ${\mathbf v}$ corresponding to some saturated chain in $[u,q^\alpha w]^q_k$. Then ${\mathbf v}\mathop{\mathrm{{\scriptstyle \bullet}}}_{k}u= q^\alpha w$. As explained in Section [\[sec:Qminim\]](#sec:Qminim){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:Qminim"}, as $[u,q^\alpha w]^q_k$ is a minimal interval, $\ell(q^\alpha w)-\ell(u)=\#\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(wu^{-1}) - s(wu^{-1})$. This implies that the graph of ${\mathbf v}$ contains no cycles, and is therefore a forest. As ${\mathbf v}$ is nonzero, it is a forest.

By Theorem [\[main-theorem\]](#main-theorem){reference-type="ref" reference="main-theorem"}, ${\mathbf v}$ is $(u,k)$-equivalent to a composition of operators of the form $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{C}$ where $\mathbf{R}$ is a row and $\mathbf{C}$ is a column, and there exists a suitable cycle shift $\mathfrak o^r$ such that applying it to $[u,q^\alpha w]^q_k$ gives a classical interval with a classical chain $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C})$ that is peakless. Let $a$ be its height and $a+b-1=\ell(q^\alpha w)-\ell(u)$ its length.

By Postnikov's cyclic symmetry [@Postnikov_symmetry Thm. 4] (and using the notation of [@Postnikov_symmetry]), $$C_{v(\lambda,k),u,w_0w}\ =\ q^{\beta(r,u,w)}C_{v(\lambda,k),\mathfrak o^{r} u,\mathfrak o^{-r}w_0w}\ =\ 
   q^{\beta(r,u,w)}C_{\mathfrak o^{r} u,v(\lambda,k),w_0\mathfrak o^{r}w}\,,$$ where $q^{\beta(r,u,w)}$ is some monomial in the $q_i$'s determined by $u$, $w$ and the power $r$ in $\mathfrak o^r$. Translating the notation of [@Postnikov_symmetry] to our notation gives $$C_{u,v,w_0w}\ =\ \sum_\alpha q^\alpha N_{u,v}^{w,\alpha}=\ \sum_\alpha C_{u,v}^{q^\alpha w} \,.$$

Thus if $\lambda=(b,1^{a-1})$ is the hook partition, then the peakless chain $\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{R})\mathfrak o^r(\mathbf{C})$ in the classical interval $[\mathfrak o^{r} u,\mathfrak o^{r}w]_k$ implies that $$C_{u,v(\lambda,k)}^{q^\alpha w}\ =\ C_{\mathfrak o^{r} u,v(\lambda,k)}^{ q^{\beta(r,u,w)}q^\alpha \mathfrak o^{r}w}\ =\
   C_{\mathfrak o^{r} u,v(\lambda,k)}^{ \mathfrak o^{r}w} \ne 0\,,$$ by Proposition [\[P:peaklessChains\]](#P:peaklessChains){reference-type="ref" reference="P:peaklessChains"} and Lemma [\[P:classical_hook\]](#P:classical_hook){reference-type="ref" reference="P:classical_hook"}. This completes the proof.$\Box$
