A more interesting model of string theory is obtained when we specify a set of 'boundary conditions' or 'D-branes' for the open strings. For a mathematician this just means a set of labels for the boundary points of objects. So fix a set $\Lambda$ of labels, and consider the category $2\textsf{Cob}_{\text{oc}}^\Lambda$ of open-closed cobordisms such that the objects are compact, oriented one-manifolds with the boundary points labelled with elements of the set $\Lambda$, and morphisms having their internal boundaries labelled compatibly with their boundaries. Here is an example of a morphism from the union of the circle and the interval labelled $(B,A)$, to the interval labelled $(B,A)$. $$\raisebox{-.45\height}{}$$

Now a $\Lambda$-labelled open-closed TQFT is a symmetric monoidal functor to some appropriate target category which we will again take to be the category of vector spaces or the category of graded vector spaces. Moreover, the category $2\textsf{Cob}_{\text{oc}}^\Lambda$ is similarly generated by morphisms as listed above, but now they must all be labelled, and the relations are just labelled versions of the previous relations. Thus we can similarly classify $\Lambda$-labelled open-closed TQFTs. Once again the image of the circle is a commutative Frobenius algebra, $\textsf{C}$. However, rather than getting a single vector space $\textsf{O}$ associated to an interval, we get a vector space $\textsf{O}_{BA}$ associated to each ordered pair $(B,A)$ of elements of $\Lambda$; so we do not get a single Frobenius algebra, but rather something which could be called a 'Frobenius algebra with many objects' or a 'Frobenius algebroid', but, for the reason explained below, such a thing is commonly known as a Calabi-Yau category. It is a category in the following sense. We take the category whose objects are parametrized by $\Lambda$ and, for $A,B\in \Lambda$, the morphism set $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(A,B)$ is taken to be $\textsf{O}_{BA}$ (this is consistent with us reading diagrams from right to left). The composition $\mu_{CBA}\colon\textsf{O}_{CB}\otimes\textsf{O}_{BA}\to\textsf{O}_{CA}$ is given by the image of the appropriately labelled version of the morphism pictured. $$\raisebox{-.45\height}{}$$ The Frobenius or Calabi-Yau part of the structure is a --- possibly graded --- perfect pairing $\textsf{O}_{AB}\otimes\textsf{O}_{BA}\to \textsf{k}$: the grading degree of this map is called the dimension of the Calabi-Yau category.

So to specify a labelled open-closed 2d TQFT it suffices to specify a commutative Frobenius algebra $\textsf{C}$, a Calabi-Yau category $\textsf{O}$ and an algebra map $i_A\colon \textsf{C}\to \textsf{O}_{AA}$ with central image, for each object $A$, such that the labelled version of the Cardy Condition holds.
