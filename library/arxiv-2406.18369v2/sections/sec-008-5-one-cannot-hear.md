Milnor’s example from 1964 (see §3) is the first example of a pair of isospectral
non-isometric Riemannian manifolds. It was partly this that inspired Kac’s arti-
cle. One year after Kac’s article Kneser found two 12-dimensional flat tori which
are isospectral but non-isometric [74]. Kitaoka then found, expressed in number-
theoretic language, two isospectral non-isometric 8-dimensional flat tori in 1977
[73]. In the same year Perlis constructed algebraic number fields which are arith-
metically equivalent but non-isometric [96]. Ejiri continued this development in
1979 by constructing non-flat, compact irreducible Riemannian manifolds which
are isospectral but non-isometric [46]. In the next year Vigneras gave examples of
hyperbolic surfaces which are isospectral but non-isometric [115], and Ikeda gave
examples of isospectral non-isometric lens spaces [62]. In 1984 Gordon and Wilson
constructed continuous families of isospectral non-isometric manifolds [55]. Before
that, however, Hsia showed in 1981 that the forms in a genus of positive ternary
quadratic forms are not classified by the set of integers they represent [61], and in
the same year Bérard and Berger gave a survey of pairs of isospectral non-isometric
manifolds that had been found up to that point [11]. Urakawa also gave examples
of bounded manifolds of dimension not less than four which are isospectral but
non-isometric in 1982 [113].
A particularly important milestone for solving the problem proposed by Kac
was achieved in 1985 when Sunada provided a new approach of proving that two
isospectral Riemannian manifolds are non-isometric. Sunada may not have realized
the importance of this result at the time as he simply described it as “a geometric
analogue of a routine method in number theory” [110]. This result was improved by
Buser in 1986 as he proved thatdimVg > 0 for a larger class ofg-values, whereVg
represents a specific space of isospectral non-isometric Riemannian manifolds [21].
At this point developments were made rapidly, and in 1987 Brooks constructed a
manifold of negative curvature and two different potentialsΦ1 and Φ2 such that
∆+Φ 1 and ∆+Φ 2 have the same spectrum [17]. In the same year, Brooks and Tse
constructed surfaces which are isospectral but non-isometric [20], and DeTurck and
Gordon constructed isospectral deformations of Riemannian metrics on manifolds
on whose coverings certain nilpotent Lie groups act [40]. Following that year,
Brooks showed how to use Sunada’s method to construct even more isospectral
non-isometric manifolds [18].
1989 was another important year in terms of developments of the subject. First,
Brooks, Perry and Yang studied isospectral sets of conformally equivalent metrics
[19] and showed how one can use that to obtain isospectral non-isometric sets
[19]. Then, Gordon, DeTurck and Lee generalized the methods by Sunada and
gave a general method for constructing isospectral non-isometric manifolds [41].
In the same year, Gordon studied which geometric and topological properties of
a Riemannian manifold that are determined by their spectrum and constructed
different manifolds with the same spectrum [52]. In yet the same year, Bérard
found isospectral but non-isometric Riemann varieties [10]. In 1990 Schiemann
found two isospectral non-isometric four-dimensional flat tori [103]. Following that

1992 was the year when Kac’s problem was finally solved. First, Gordon, Webb
andWolpertusedideasfromSunadatoconstructapairofisospectral, non-isometric
domains in a hyperbolic setting and in the 2-sphere [53]. Later that year, they were
in fact able to construct two isospectral manifolds in the Euclidean plane which
are not isometric, thus showing that one cannot hear the shape of a drum [54].
The story doesn’t end here, however, partly because one was still interested in the
properties of domains that are spectral invariants, and partly because one can ask
similar questions in more general spaces. For example, in the same year Conway
introduced simple notation for orbifolds [34], and together with Sloane he found
isospectral non-isometric five- and six-dimensional flat tori and an infinite family
of four-dimensional flat tori [36]. Conway continued his work in 1994 together with
Buser, Doyle, and Semmler by giving more examples of isospectral non-congruent
domainsintheplane, anda“simplemethodofproof” [22]. Oneyearlater, Chapman
constructed several examples of isospectral but non-isometric domains [28]. Then,
in 1999 Schueth constructed isospectral pairs for which the fourth-order curvature
invariants differ, thus showing that those invariants are not determined by the
spectrum [106]. More such examples were given by the same author in 2001 [107].
At this point mathematicians began focusing more on finding spectral invariants
rather than examples of isospectral non-isometric manifolds. As a consequence,
the progress on finding more isospectral non-isometric manifolds slowed down by
a significant amount. However, in 2006 Band, Shapira, and Smilansky presented
and discussed isospectral quantum graphs which are not isometric [7]. In the same
year, Levitin, Parnovski and Polterovich constructed2d isospectral non-isometric
domains in Rd by usingmixed boundary conditions [81]. In 2011, using theory of
invariants Hein and Cervino proved that every pair in the family that Conway and
Sloane constructed in [36] are non-isometric whenever the parameters are pairwise
different [26]. In the same year, Bari showed that for every n ≥ 9 there is a
pair of isospectral non-isometric orbifold lens spaces of dimensionn [9], and in 2016
Lauret showed that the same is true forn≥ 5. In 2012 Linowitz constructed infinite
towers of compact, orientable Riemannian manifolds which are isospectral but not
isometric at each stage [82]. In 2013 Lauret, Miatello, and Rossetti constructed
Riemannian manifolds that are isosepctral but have non-isomorphic cohomology
groups [78]. Three years later the same three authors found examples of non-
isometric lens spaces which arep-isospectral, i.e. the eigenvalue spectra of their
Hodge-Laplacian operators acting onp-forms are the same, for everyp [79]. DeFord
and Doyle gave many more such examples in 2018 [39]. In 2021, Gómez-Serrano
and Orriols proved that “any three eigenvalues do not determine a triangle” [51].
This demonstrated the negative part of a conjecture by Antunes and Freitas [6] that
was also mentioned by Laugesen and Siudeja [77], Grieser and Maronna [56] as well
as Lu and Rowlett [84]. They proved the existence of two triangles which are not
isometric to each other for which the first, second and fourth Dirichlet eigenvalues
coincide. In 2022 Bérard and Webb showed that one cannot hear the orientability of
a surface by constructing two flat surfaces with boundary with the same Neumann
spectrum, one orientable, the other non-orientable [12]. Although numerous results
have shown that isospectrality does not necessarily imply isometry, if one restricts
