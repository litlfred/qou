The Coxeter group of type $H_2=I_2(5)$ is just the dihedral group of a regular pentagon. That is the group generated by two reflections $s_1, s_2$ with relation $$(s_1s_2)^5=1.$$ If we let $G$ be the Coxeter group of type $H_2$ and take the complex field $\mathbb{C}$ as our base field, then the group algebra $\mathbb{C}[G]$ is generated by $s_1,s_2$ with the defining relations $$s_1^2=1=s_2^2,\quad s_2s_1s_2s_1s_2=s_1s_2s_1s_2s_1.$$ Set our monomial order to be the degree-lexicographic order with $s_2>s_1$. Denote by $R$ the set of quadratic relations and the braid relation, that is, $$R=\{s_1^2-1,\ s_2^2-1,\ s_2s_1s_2s_1s_2-s_1s_2s_1s_2s_1\}.$$ Then the group algebra $\mathbb{C}[G]$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}\langle s_1,s_2\rangle /I$, where $I$ is the two-sided ideal generated by $R$ in the free associative algebra $\mathbb{C}\langle s_1,s_2\rangle$. From now on, we identify $\mathbb{C}[G]$ with $\mathbb{C}\langle s_1,s_2\rangle /I$. We say that the algebra $\mathbb{C}[G]$ is defined by $R$.

Since the number of $R$-standard monomials is 10, the dimension of $\mathbb{C}[G]$, we know that $R$ is already a Gröbner-Shirshov basis for $\mathbb{C}[G]$.

::: prop
The $R$-standard monomials form the $\mathbb{C}$-basis elements for the type $H_2$. We enumerate as follows : $$\label{std_monomials_H2}
1,\ s_1,\   s_2,\ s_1s_2,\  s_2s_1,\    s_1s_2s_1,\ s_2s_1s_2,\ s_1s_2s_1s_2,\  s_2s_1s_2s_1,\  s_1s_2s_1s_2s_1.$$
:::

 

Now we consider the Coxeter group $G$ of type $H_3$, which is called the full icosahedral group. It is the group generated by three reflections $s_1, s_2, s_3$ with braid relations $$(s_1s_2)^5=(s_2s_3)^3=1=(s_1s_3)^2.$$ Let $G$ be the Coxeter group of type $H_3$. Then the group algebra $\mathbb{C}[G]$ is generated by $s_1, s_2, s_3$ with defining relations $$s_1^2=1=s_2^2=s_3^2,\quad s_2s_1s_2s_1s_2=s_1s_2s_1s_2s_1,\ s_3s_2s_3=s_2s_3s_2,\ s_3s_1=s_1s_3.$$ We take the same monomial order as above with $s_3> s_2>s_1$, and set $$\label{defining_relations_H3}
R=\{s_1^2-1,\ s_2^2-1,\ s_3^2-1,\ s_2s_1s_2s_1s_2-s_1s_2s_1s_2s_1,\ s_3s_2s_3-s_2s_3s_2,\ s_3s_1-s_1s_3\}.$$ Then $\mathbb{C}[G]$ is identified with $\mathbb{C}\langle s_1,s_2,s_3\rangle /I$, where $I$ is the two-sided ideal generated by $R$ in the free associative algebra $\mathbb{C}\langle s_1,s_2,s_3\rangle$.

::: lem
The following relations hold in $\mathbb{C}[G]$ : []{#Lem_H3 label="Lem_H3"}

(a) $(s_3s_2s_1)s_3-s_2(s_3s_2s_1)=0$,

(b) $(s_3s_2s_1)s_3s_2-s_2(s_3s_2s_1s_2)=0$,

(c) $(s_3s_2s_1)^2-s_2(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)=0$,

(d) $(s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3s_2-s_2(s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3=0$,

(e) $(s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3s_2s_1-s_2(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)s_3=0$,

(f) $(s_3s_2s_1s_2)^2-s_2(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)s_3s_2=0$,

(g) $(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)^2-s_2(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)s_3s_2s_1s_2=0$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* (a) We calculate that $$(s_3s_2s_1)s_3=(s_3s_2s_3)s_1=(s_2s_3s_2)s_1$$ by the braid relations $s_1s_3=s_3s_1$ and $s_3s_2s_3=s_2s_3s_2$.

(b)$\sim$(c)

Just multiplying the previous relation from the right by $s_2$, and by $s_1$, we have the relations.

(d)$\sim$(f) We apply $s_2s_3s_2=s_3s_2s_3$ and $s_1s_3=s_3s_1$ to get our relations.

\(g\) We obtain that $$\begin{aligned}
s_3s_2s_1s_2(s_1s_3)s_2s_1s_2s_1&=&s_3s_2s_1s_2s_3(s_1s_2s_1s_2s_1)=s_3s_2s_1(s_2s_3s_2)s_1s_2s_1s_2\\
&=&s_3s_2s_1s_3s_2s_3s_1s_2s_1s_2=s_2(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)s_3s_2s_1s_2,
\end{aligned}$$ using the braid relations $s_1s_3=s_3s_1$, $s_1s_2s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2s_1s_2$ and $s_2s_3s_2=s_3s_2s_3$. ◻
:::

In the following theorem, we have a monomial basis of $\mathbb{C}[G]$. Note that this is different from the result in [@LeeDI6] since we have the reverse index of generators.

::: thm
We denote by $\widehat{R}$ the set of defining relations $R$ combined with the relations in Lemma [\[Lem_H3\]](#Lem_H3){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem_H3"}. Then $\widehat{R}$ forms a Gröbner-Shirshov basis for $\mathbb{C}[G]$. The corresponding $\widehat{R}$-standard monomials are of the form $$M_2M_3,$$ where $M_2$ is among the standard monomials ([\[std_monomials_H2\]](#std_monomials_H2){reference-type="ref" reference="std_monomials_H2"}) and $M_3$ is one of the following : $$\begin{aligned}
\label{std_monomials_H3}
&1,\ s_3,\ s_3s_2,\ s_3s_2s_1,\ s_3s_2s_1s_2,\ s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1,\ (s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3,\ (s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)s_3,\\
&(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)(s_3s_2), (s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)(s_3s_2s_1), (s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)(s_3s_2s_1s_2), (s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)(s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3.\nonumber
\end{aligned}$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We have two ways of proving this theorem.

\(I\) Counting the number of $\widehat{R}$-standard monomials:

It can be easily checked that the product of each $mM$ and $s_i$ $(i=1,2,3)$ is also one of the monomials $mM$ by the relations in $\widehat{R}$.

We enumerate all $\widehat{R}$-standard monomials in $\mathbb{C}\langle s_1,s_2,s_3\rangle$, which are in the above. The number of $\widehat{R}$-standard monomials is $$10\times 12=120,$$ which is exactly equal to the order of $G$ (See [@Humphreys90 §2.13]), the dimension of $\mathbb{C}[G]$ as a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space. Hence, Theorem [\[cor-1\]](#cor-1){reference-type="ref" reference="cor-1"} leads us that the set $\widehat{R}$ is a Gröbner-Shirshov basis for $\mathbb{C}[G]$.

\(II\) Checking that $\widehat{R}$ is closed under composition:

Note that the set $\widehat{R}$ consists of thirteen polynomials. Following Theorem [\[cor-1\]](#cor-1){reference-type="ref" reference="cor-1"} (a), we show that $\widehat{R}$ is closed under composition. It is proved by checking that all possible compositions between any two polynomials reduce trivially.

For example, let $p=(s_3s_2s_1)s_3-s_2(s_3s_2s_1)$ in Lemma [\[Lem_H3\]](#Lem_H3){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem_H3"} (a) and $q=s_3s_2s_3-s_2s_3s_2$ in ([\[defining_relations_H3\]](#defining_relations_H3){reference-type="ref" reference="defining_relations_H3"}), and consider the composition

::: center
$(p,q)_w$with $w=s_3s_2s_1s_3s_2s_3$.
:::

Then we check that $$p(s_2s_3)-(s_3s_2s_1)q
=-s_2(s_3s_2s_1)(s_2s_3)+(s_3s_2s_1)s_2s_3s_2=0$$ by Lemma [\[Lem_H3\]](#Lem_H3){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem_H3"} (d).

In this way, we check that all possible compositions reduce to 0. Therefore, the set of relations $\widehat{R}$ is closed under composition. ◻
:::

. (1) We have the multiplication table between the $\widehat{R}$-standard monomials. For example, we multiply $(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)(s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3$ by $s_1$ from the right, to obtain: $$(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)(s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3\ s_1=(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)^2s_3=s_2(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)(s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3$$ by the commutative relation $s_3s_1=s_1s_3$ and the relation (g) in Lemma [\[Lem_H3\]](#Lem_H3){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem_H3"}.

\(2\) We find that the set of the above $12$ monomials in ([\[std_monomials_H3\]](#std_monomials_H3){reference-type="ref" reference="std_monomials_H3"}) forms the set of right coset representatives of the subgroup of type $H_2$ in the Coxeter group of type $H_3$.

\(3\) The monomial $$s_1s_2s_1s_2s_1(s_3s_2s_1s_2s_1)(s_3s_2s_1s_2)s_3$$ is the longest element in $G$, which is of length $15$, the number of positive roots in a root system of type $H_3$.

\(4\) The full icosahedral group $G$ contains the icosahedral group as a normal subgroup of index $2$. Note that the subgroup of rotational symmetries of a regular icosahedron is isomorphic to the $5$th alternating group $A_5$ of order $60$. Since the elements of $A_5$ are of even degree, we have an explicit monomial expression for $60$ elements of the icosahedral group.

\(5\) An algorithm using rewriting rules is sketched for obtaining complete presentations of Coxeter groups [@Borges-Trenard_Perez-Roses; @LeChenadec].

\(6\) A noncrystallographic root lattice induces a quasicrystal structure, using the cut and project method, which realizes the structure as a projection of a certain strip by a root map in a higher dimensional lattice [@ChenMoodyPatera].
