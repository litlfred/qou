Let us now construct a non-obvious lift of the action of $W$ on $D^b(A\operatorname{\!-modgr}\nolimits)$ to an action of $B_W$ on $K^b(A\operatorname{\!-modgr}\nolimits)$.

### 

For $s\in S$, we define the complex of $(A,A)$-bimodules $$F_s=F_{{\mathbf{s}}}=0\to A\otimes_{A^s}A\buildrel \varepsilon'_s\over \longrightarrow A\to 0$$ where $A$ is in degree $1$ and $\varepsilon'_s$ is the multiplication.

Since $A=A^s\oplus A^s\alpha_s$, the morphism of $A^{\mathrm{en}}$-modules $$A_s\to A\otimes_{A^s}A(1),\
  a\mapsto a\otimes\alpha_s- a\alpha_s\otimes 1$$ induces an isomorphism $$f_{{\mathbf{s}}}:A_s\buildrel \sim\over\to F_s(1) \text{ in }D^b(A^{\mathrm{en}}\operatorname{\!-modgr}\nolimits).$$

### 

For $w\in W$, let $\Delta_{\le w}=\bigcup_{w'\le w}\Delta_{w'}$ and $D_w=k[\Delta_{\le w}]$. Note that $D_s=A\otimes_{A^s}A$ for $s\in S$.

Given $w'\le w$, we have a canonical quotient map $D_w\to D_{w'}$ given by restriction of functions. We have $$\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(D_w,D_{w'})=
\begin{cases}
k\cdot{\mathrm{can}}& \text{ if } w'\le w \\
0 & \text{ otherwise}
\end{cases}$$

### 

In the next lemma, $0\to L\to M\to 0$ denotes a complex with $L$ in degree $0$.

::: lemma
[]{#declength2 label="declength2"} Assume $W$ is a finite dihedral group, *i.e.*, $\dim V=2$, $S=\{s,t\}$ and $m_{st}<\infty$. Let $x\in W$ such that $tx>x$. Then,

-   $D_s(0\to D_{tx}\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{can}}}D_x\to 0)\simeq
    (0\to D_x(-1)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\nolimits} D_x(-1)\to 0)\oplus
    (0\to D_{stx}\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{can}}} D_x\to 0)$.

-   $F_sD_x\simeq
    (0\to D_x\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\nolimits} D_x\to 0)\oplus
    (0\to D_x(-1)\to 0\to 0)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let us recall some constructions and results of Soergel [@Soe4 Lemma 4.5, Proposition 4.6 and their proofs]. Since $2m_{st}$ is invertible, then given $u,u'$ two distinct reflections of $W$, we have $\ker(u+\operatorname{id}\nolimits)\not=\ker(u'+\operatorname{id}\nolimits)$.

Given $I$ an ideal of $A\otimes A$ invariant under $s\times 1$, we put $((A\otimes A)/I)^+=((A\otimes A)/I)^{(s\times 1)}$.

Let $r$ be the reflection of $W$ such that $rx<x$ and $rx\not<tx$. Then, $\Delta_{x}+\Delta_{rx}$ is a hyperplane of $V\times V$ and let $\beta\in V^*\times V^*$ be a linear form with kernel this hyperplane. Let $M$ (resp. $N$) be the $(A^s\otimes A)$-submodule of $D_{tx}$ generated by the image of the elements $\beta$ (resp $1$) of $A\otimes A$. Then, $D_{tx}=M\oplus N$, $M\simeq D_x^+(-1)$ and $N\simeq D_{stx}^+$ as $(A^s\otimes A)$-modules.

Let $M'$ (resp. $N'$) be the $(A^s\otimes A)$-submodule of $D_x$ generated by $\alpha_s\otimes 1$ (resp. $1$). Then, $D_x=M'\oplus N'$, $M'\simeq D_x^+(-1)$ and $N'=D_x^+$ as $(A^s\otimes A)$-modules. Denote by $p:D_x\to M'$ the projection.

Let us show now that $\beta\not\in (V^*)^s\times V^*$. Equivalently, we need to show that $(\Delta_x+\Delta_{rx})\cap (k\alpha_s\times 0)=0$. This amounts to proving that $\operatorname{im}\nolimits(\operatorname{id}\nolimits-r)\not=k\alpha_s$. But this holds, since $r\not=s$.

Let us now come to our problem. Since $\beta\not\in (V^*)^s\times V^*$, it follows that the image of $\beta$ in $(A\otimes A)/(A^s\otimes A)$ is a generator as $(A^s\otimes A)$-module. Consequently, the restriction of $pf:D_{tx}\to M'$ to $M$ is surjective, hence it is an isomorphism (we denote by $f:D_{tx}\to D_x$ the canonical map). $$\xymatrix{
M\ar@{^{(}->}[r] \ar@/^2pc/[rrr]^\sim& D_{tx}\ar@{->>}[r]^-f &
 D_x\ar@{->>}[r]^p & M' \\
&& A\otimes A\ar@{->>}[ul] \ar@{->>}[u] \ar@{->>}[r] &
 A\otimes A/A^s\otimes A \ar@{->>}[u] \\
&& A^s\beta A\ar@{->>}[uull] \ar@{->>}[ur]\ar@{^{(}->}[u]
}$$

Finally, the multiplication map $A\otimes_{A^s}D_y^+\buildrel \sim\over\to D_y$ is an isomorphism for any $y\in W$ with $sy<y$. We have shown that the complex $A\otimes_{A^s}(0\to D_{tx}\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{can}}}D_x\to 0)$ is isomorphic to the direct sum of the complex $0\to D_x(-1)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\nolimits} D_x(-1)\to 0$ and a complex $D=0\to D_{stx}\xrightarrow{\phi} D_x\to 0$. Note that $\phi=r\cdot{\mathrm{can}}$ for some $r\in k$ and we need to prove that $r\not=0$. The complex $0\to D_{tx}\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{can}}}D_x\to 0$ has zero homology in degree $1$, hence the same is true for $D$. It follows that $r\not=0$.

Let us now prove the second assertion. The multiplication map $A\otimes_{A^s}D_x^+\to D_x$ is an isomorphism. Since $D_x=D_x^+\oplus M'$ and $M'\simeq D_x^+(-1)$, we obtain the second part of the Lemma. ◻
:::

::: prop
[]{#tressebimod label="tressebimod"} Take $s\not=t\in S$ with $m_{st}<\infty$. We have braid relations $$\underbrace{F_sF_tF_s\cdots}_{m_{st}\text{ terms}}\simeq
\underbrace{F_tF_sF_t\cdots}_{m_{st}\text{ terms}}$$ in $K^b(A\otimes A)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We have a decomposition $V=V_1\oplus V_2$ under the action of $\langle s,t\rangle$, with $V_1=V^{\langle s,t\rangle}$. For the $(A,A)$-bimodules involved in the Proposition, the right and left actions of $k[V_1]$ are identical. So, we get the Proposition for $V$ from the Proposition for $V_2$ by applying the functor $k[V_1^*]\otimes_k -$. It follows we can assume $\dim V=2$. So, we assume $W$ is finite dihedral with $S=\{s,t\}$. We put $s_+=s$ and $s_-=t$.

Let $m=m_{st}$ and consider $i\le m$ and $\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}$. Let $\sigma^\varepsilon_i=s_\varepsilon s_{-\varepsilon}s_\varepsilon\cdots$ ($i$ terms) and $D^\varepsilon_i=D_{\sigma^\varepsilon_i}$. We put $D^\varepsilon=D_{s_\varepsilon}$. Consider the simplicial scheme over $V\times V$ : $$\Delta_1\rightrightarrows \Delta_{\le s_+}\coprod\Delta_{\le s_-}
 \rightrightarrows \Delta_{\le s_+s_-}\coprod\Delta_{\le s_-s_+}
 \rightrightarrows\cdots\rightrightarrows
 \Delta_{\le \sigma^\varepsilon_{i-1}}\coprod\Delta_{\le\sigma^{-\varepsilon}_{i-1}}
\to \Delta_{\le\sigma_i^\varepsilon}$$ where the maps are the inclusions.

We now define $F_i^\varepsilon$ as the complex of $(A,A)$-bimodules coming from the structural complex of sheaves of this simplicial scheme : $$F_i^\varepsilon=
0\to D_i^\varepsilon\xrightarrow{\tiny
\left(\begin{matrix}+\\+\end{matrix}\right)}
 D_{i-1}^\varepsilon\oplus D_{i-1}^{-\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\tiny
\left(\begin{matrix}+&-\\+&-\end{matrix}\right)}
D_{i-2}^\varepsilon\oplus D_{i-2}^{-\varepsilon}\to \cdots\to
D^+\oplus D^- \xrightarrow{\tiny
\left(\begin{matrix}+&-\end{matrix}\right)}
 D_1\to 0$$ where the sign denotes the multiple of the canonical map considered (we put $D_i^\varepsilon$ in degree $0$). We have $H^r(F_i^\varepsilon)=0$ for $r>0$, since $\Delta_{\le \sigma_r^+}\cap \Delta_{\le \sigma_r^-}=
\Delta_{\le \sigma_{r-1}^+}\cup \Delta_{\le \sigma_{r-1}^-}$ and we have an exact sequence $$0\to k[\Delta_{\le \sigma_r^+}\cup \Delta_{\le \sigma_r^-}]
\xrightarrow{\tiny\left(\begin{matrix}+\\+\end{matrix}\right)}
k[\Delta_{\le \sigma_r^+}]\oplus k[\Delta_{\le \sigma_r^-}]
\xrightarrow{\tiny \left(\begin{matrix}+&-\end{matrix}\right)}
k[\Delta_{\le \sigma_r^+}\cap \Delta_{\le \sigma_r^-}]\to 0.$$

The complex $F_1^\varepsilon$ is isomorphic to $F_{s_\varepsilon}$. We will now show by induction on $i$ that $F_{s_\varepsilon}F_i^{-\varepsilon}$ is homotopy equivalent to $F_{i+1}^\varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon=\pm$. This will prove the Proposition, since $F_m^+\simeq F_m^-$.

Let us consider the complex $C=F_{s_\varepsilon}F_i^{-\varepsilon}$. This is the total complex of the double complex $$\xymatrix{
D^\varepsilon D_i^{-\varepsilon} \ar[r]\ar[d] & D^\varepsilon D_{i-1}^{\varepsilon}\oplus
 D^\varepsilon D_{i-1}^{-\varepsilon} \ar[r]\ar[d] & D^\varepsilon D_{i-2}^{\varepsilon}\oplus
 D^\varepsilon D_{i-2}^{-\varepsilon}\ar[r]\ar[d] & \cdots\ar[r] & D^\varepsilon D_1\ar[d] \\
D_i^{-\varepsilon} \ar[r] & D_{i-1}^{\varepsilon}\oplus D_{i-1}^{-\varepsilon}
\ar[r] & D_{i-2}^{\varepsilon}\oplus D_{i-2}^{-\varepsilon}\ar[r] & \cdots\ar[r] & D_1
}$$

By Lemma [\[declength2\]](#declength2){reference-type="ref" reference="declength2"}, the complex $0\to D^\varepsilon D_r^{-\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{can}}} D^\varepsilon D_{r-1}^{\varepsilon}\to 0$ is isomorphic to the direct sum of $0\to D_{r-1}^\varepsilon(-1)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\nolimits}D_{r-1}^\varepsilon(-1)\to 0$ and of $0\to D_{r+1}^\varepsilon\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{can}}} D_{r-1}^\varepsilon\to 0$. Also, the complex $0\to D^\varepsilon D_r^\varepsilon\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{can}}}D_r^\varepsilon\to 0$ is isomorphic to the direct sum of $0\to D_r^\varepsilon\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\nolimits}D_r^\varepsilon\to 0$ and of $0\to D_r^\varepsilon(-1)\to 0\to 0$. It follows that $C$ is homotopy equivalent to a complex $$C'=0\to D_{i+1}^\varepsilon\to D_i^{\varepsilon}\oplus D_i^{-\varepsilon}\to\cdots\to D_1\to 0,$$ where the maps remain to be determined. Since $F_{s_\varepsilon}$ has non zero homology only in degree $0$ and that homology is free as a right $A$-module, it follows that the homology of $C$ vanishes in degrees $>0$.

To conclude, we have to show that a complex $X$ with the same terms as $F_i^\varepsilon$ and with zero homology in degrees $>0$ is actually isomorphic to $F_i^\varepsilon$. We have $$X=
0\to D_i^\varepsilon\xrightarrow{\tiny
\left(\begin{matrix}a_i\\c_i\end{matrix}\right)}
 D_{i-1}^\varepsilon\oplus D_{i-1}^{-\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\tiny
\left(\begin{matrix}a_{i-1}&b_{i-1}\\c_{i-1}&d_{i-1}\end{matrix}\right)}
D_{i-2}^\varepsilon\oplus D_{i-2}^{-\varepsilon}\to \cdots\to
D^+\oplus D^- \xrightarrow{\tiny
\left(\begin{matrix}c_1&d_1\end{matrix}\right)}
 D_1\to 0$$ where the coefficients are in $k$ and the maps are corresponding multiples of the canonical maps.

Take $r\le i$ minimal such that there is an entry of $\tiny \left(\begin{matrix}a_r&b_r\\c_r&d_r\end{matrix}\right)$ that vanishes. Assume for example $c_r=0$. Then, $a_{r-1}a_r=0$, hence $a_r=0$. We have $b_rc_{r+1}=d_rc_{r+1}=b_rd_{r+1}=d_rd_{r+1}=0$. If $b_r=d_r=0$, then $X$ is the sum of the subcomplex with zero terms in degrees $\le i-r$ and the subcomplex with zero terms in degrees $>i-r$. Otherwise, $c_{r+1}=d_{r+1}=0$, hence $X$ splits as the direct sum of the subcomplex $\cdots\to D_{r+1}^\varepsilon\oplus D_{r+1}^{-\varepsilon}\to D_r^\varepsilon\to 0$ and the subcomplex $0\to D_r^{-\varepsilon}\to D_{r-1}^\varepsilon\oplus D_{r-1}^{-\varepsilon}\to\cdots$. Now, a morphism $D_r^{-\varepsilon}\to D_{r-1}^\varepsilon\oplus D_{r-1}^{-\varepsilon}$ is never injective, for the support of the left term is strictly larger than the support of the right term. Consequently, the complex $X$ has non-zero homology in degree $i-r$, which is a contradiction. We have proven that none of the coefficients $a_r,b_r,c_r,d_r$ can be zero.

Let $Z$ be the closed subvariety of the affine space of coefficients $a_r,b_r,c_r,d_r$ that define a complex (*i.e.*, $\tiny \left(\begin{matrix}a_r&b_r\\c_r&d_r\end{matrix}\right)
\left(\begin{matrix}a_{r+1}&b_{r+1}\\c_{r+1}&d_{r+1}\end{matrix}\right)=0$) and let $Z^0$ be its open subset corresponding to non-zero coefficients. We have an isomorphism $Z^0\buildrel \sim\over\to({\mathbf{G}}_m)^{2i-1}$, $h:(a_r,b_r,c_r,d_r)_r\mapsto (a_r,c_r)_r$. The action of $({\mathbf{G}}_m)^{2i}$ on the terms of the complex induce an action on $Z$. The corresponding action on $Z^0\simeq
({\mathbf{G}}_m)^{2i-1}$ has a unique orbit. It follows that $X$ is isomorphic to $F_i^\varepsilon$. ◻
:::

### 

Let us define the complex of $A^{\mathrm{en}}$-modules $$F_{{\mathbf{s}}^{-1}}=0\to A\buildrel \eta_s\over \longrightarrow A\otimes_{A^s}A(1) \to 0$$ where $A$ is in degree $-1$ and $\eta_s(a)=a\alpha_s\otimes 1+a\otimes\alpha_s$.

::: lemma
[]{#inverse label="inverse"} The complexes $F_{{\mathbf{s}}}$ and $F_{{\mathbf{s}}^{-1}}$ are inverse to each other in $K^b(A^{\mathrm{en}}\operatorname{\!-modgr}\nolimits)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let ${\mathcal{C}}=K^b(A^s\operatorname{\!-modgr}\nolimits)$ and ${\mathcal{D}}=K^b(A\operatorname{\!-modgr}\nolimits)$. Let $F=A\otimes_{A^s} ?$, $G=A\otimes_A?$ and $\Phi=?(1)$. The morphisms of $(A^s,A^s)$-bimodules $$\varepsilon_s:A(1)\to A^s, 1\mapsto 0\text{ and }\alpha_s\mapsto 1
\ \ \text{ and}\ \ \eta'_s:A^s\to A, 1\mapsto 1$$ together with $\eta_s$ and $\varepsilon'_s$ previously defined give rise to adjoint pairs $(F,G)$ and $(G,F\Phi)$.

We have a split exact sequence of $(A^s,A^s)$-bimodules $$0\to A^s\xrightarrow{\eta'_s}A\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_s}A^s\to 0,$$ hence we deduce the Lemma from Proposition [\[inversibilitetriang\]](#inversibilitetriang){reference-type="ref" reference="inversibilitetriang"}. ◻
:::

By Proposition [\[tressebimod\]](#tressebimod){reference-type="ref" reference="tressebimod"} and Lemma [\[inverse\]](#inverse){reference-type="ref" reference="inverse"}, we have already obtained an action "up to isomorphism" of $B_W$ on $K^b(A)$ :

::: prop
[]{#actionuptoiso label="actionuptoiso"} The map $s\mapsto F_{{\mathbf{s}}}$ extends to a morphism from $B_W$ to the group of isomorphism classes of invertible objects of $K^b(A^{\mathrm{en}}\operatorname{\!-modgr}\nolimits)$.
:::
