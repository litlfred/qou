One of the key points in the proof of Theorem [\[thma\]](#thma){reference-type="ref" reference="thma"} is a characterization of the following stacks.

::: defn
A *pseudo-affine* stack is a quasi-compact fpqc stack with quasi-affine diagonal such that all quasi-coherent sheaves on it are generated by global sections. In other words a pseudo-affine stack is a quasi-compact fpqc stack $\stX$ such that $\{\odi{\stX}\}$ generates $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}(\stX)$.

A map $f\colon\stY'\arr\stY$ of fibered categories is called *pseudo-affine* if for all maps $T\arr\stY$ from an affine scheme the fiber product $T\times_{\stY}\stY'$ is pseudo-affine.
:::

A quasi-affine scheme is pseudo-affine. We will prove a pseudo-affine stack which is algebraic is quasi-affine. This result appeared before in [@Gross2013 Proposition 3.1]. We will show that pseudo-affine stacks are indeed just arbitrary intersection of quasi-compact open subsets of an affine scheme (see [\[prop:arbitrary intersection of quasi-compact open is pseudo-affine\]](#prop:arbitrary intersection of quasi-compact open is pseudo-affine){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:arbitrary intersection of quasi-compact open is pseudo-affine"}). In general a pseudo-affine sheaf is not quasi-affine. An example is the sheaf intersection of all the complement of closed points in $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}k[x,y]$, where $k$ is a field (see [\[exa:counterexample pseudo-affine\]](#exa:counterexample pseudo-affine){reference-type="ref" reference="exa:counterexample pseudo-affine"}).

We start with a first characterization of pseudo-affine stacks.

::: prop
[]{#lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections label="lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections"} Let $\stX\arrdi{\pi}\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}R$ be a quasi-compact fpqc stack with quasi-affine diagonal. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.  the stack $\stX$ is pseudo-affine;

2.  the map $\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\shF\arr\shF$ is surjective for all $\shF\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX$;

3.  the stack $\stX$ is equivalent to a sheaf and there exists a flat monomorphism $\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$, where $B$ is a ring.

In this case the map $p\colon\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi{\stX})$ is a flat monomorphism, $p_{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\stX)$ is fully faithful and $p^{*}p_{*}\simeq\id$. Moreover if $\stX\times_{\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi{\stX})}k\neq\emptyset$ for all geometric points $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}k\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi{\stX})$ then $p$ is an isomorphism.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* $2)\then1)$. Given $\shF\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX$, take a surjective map $R^{(I)}\arr\pi_{*}\shF$. In this case the composition $$\odi{\stX}^{(I)}\simeq\pi^{*}R^{(I)}\arr\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\shF\arr\shF$$ is surjective.

$1)\then2)$. A sheaf $\shF\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX$ is generated by global sections and the image of $\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\shF\arr\shF$ contains all of them.

$3)\then1)$. Denote by $p\colon\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ the flat monomorphism. We are going to show that $\delta_{\shF}\colon p^{*}p_{*}\shF\arr\shF$ is an isomorphism for all $\shF\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX$. Arguing as in $2)\then1)$ this will conclude the proof. By hypothesis there exists a representable fpqc covering $h\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}C\arr\stX$ and we must prove that $h^{*}\delta_{\shF}$ is an isomorphism. Let $f=ph\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}C\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ be the composition and consider the commutative diagram $$\begin{tikzpicture}[xscale=2.0,yscale=-1.0]     \node (A0_0) at (0, 0) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}C$};     \node (A0_1) at (1, 0) {$\stX\times_B C$};     \node (A0_2) at (2, 0) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}C$};     \node (A1_1) at (1, 1) {$\stX$};     \node (A1_2) at (2, 1) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$};     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{s}$} (A0_1);     \path (A0_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$} (A0_2);     \path (A1_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{p}$} (A1_2);     \path (A0_2) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{f}$} (A1_2);     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{h}$} (A1_1);     \path (A0_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{t}$} (A1_1);   \end{tikzpicture}$$ Since $\alpha$ is a monomorphism with a section, $\alpha$ and $s$ are inverses of each other. The pullback along $t$ of the adjoint map $\delta_{\shF}\colon p^{*}p_{*}\shF\to\shF$ is $$t^{*}p^{*}p_{*}\shF=\alpha^{*}(f^{*}p_{*}\shF)\to\alpha^{*}\alpha_{*}(t^{*}\shF)\to t^{*}\shF$$ The first map is an isomorphism because $f$ is flat and therefore $f^{*}p_{*}\shF\to\alpha_{*}t^{*}\shF$ is an isomorphism (see [@Tonini2014 Prop 1.15]). The second map is an isomorphism because $\alpha$ is an isomorphism. Pulling back again along $s$ we obtain the result.

$1)\then3)$. Set $B=\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi{\stX})$ and $p\colon\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ the induced map. Notice that $\L_{B}(\{\odi{\stX}\},B)\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B$ and under this isomorphism $$\Omega^{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX\arr\L_{B}(\{\odi{\stX}\},B)\text{ and }\shF_{*,\{\odi{\stX}\}}\colon\L_{B}(\{\odi{\stX}\},B)\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX$$ correspond to $p_{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B$ and $p^{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX$ respectively. Since $\{\odi{\stX}\}$ generates $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}(\stX)$ by hypothesis we can apply [\[thm:main thm main paper\]](#thm:main thm main paper){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:main thm main paper"} and conclude that $p_{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B$ is fully faithful, $p^{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B\to\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}(\stX)$ is exact, that is $p\colon\stX\to\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ is flat by [@Tonini2014 Prop 1.14], and $p^{*}p_{*}\simeq\id$.

We want to show that $\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ is fully faithful or, equivalently, that the diagonal $\stX\arr\stX\times_{B}\stX$ is an equivalence. Let $h\colon V=\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}C\arr\stX$ be a representable fpqc covering and consider the Cartesian diagrams $$\begin{tikzpicture}[xscale=2.5,yscale=-1.2]     \node (A0_0) at (0, 0) {$\stX_V$};     \node (A0_1) at (1, 0) {$\stX$};     \node (A0_2) at (2, 0) {$V$};     \node (A0_3) at (3, 0) {$\stX$};     \node (A1_0) at (0, 1) {$V$};     \node (A1_1) at (1, 1) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$};     \node (A1_2) at (2, 1) {$\stX\times_B V$};     \node (A1_3) at (3, 1) {$\stX\times_B \stX$};     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A0_1);     \path (A0_2) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{h}$} (A0_3);     \path (A1_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{f}$} (A1_1);     \path (A0_3) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{\Delta}$} (A1_3);     \path (A0_2) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{s}$} (A1_2);     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_0);     \path (A0_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{p}$} (A1_1);     \path (A1_2) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{\id\times h}$} (A1_3);   \end{tikzpicture}$$ where $f=ph\colon V\to\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$. We are going to prove that $\stX_{V}\to V$ is an isomorphism, but first we show how to conclude the proof from this. Since $s$ is a section of $\stX_{V}\to V$ it would be an isomorphism too. Since the horizontal arrow in the second diagram is an fpqc covering, by descent it would follow that $\Delta$ is an equivalence. Moreover if if $\stX\times_{B}k\neq\emptyset$ for all geometric points then $f\colon V\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ would be an fpqc covering. The fact that $\stX_{V}\to V$ is an equivalence would therefore imply that $p\colon\stX\to\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ is an equivalence.

Thus our goal is to show that $\stX_{V}\to V$ is an isomorphism. Since $p$ is flat also $f$ is flat. In particular $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi{\stX})=B$ implies $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi{\stX_{V}})=C$. Since $\stX_{V}\arr\stX$ is affine and $\{\odi{\stX}\}$ generates $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}(\stX)$ it follows that $\{\odi{\stX\times_{B}V}\}$ generates $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}(\stX\times_{B}V)$ (see [@Tonini2014 Rem 7.3]). Thus $\stX_{V}$ is pseudo-affine.

Discussion above shows that we can assume that $p\colon\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ has a section, that we still denote by $s\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B\arr\stX$, and we have to show that it is an isomorphism.

We first prove that $p_{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B$ is an equivalence. It suffices to show that, if $M\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B$, then the map $\gamma_{M}\colon M\arr p_{*}p^{*}M$ is an isomorphism. Notice that $p^{*}\gamma_{M}$ is a section of the map $(p^{*}p_{*})p^{*}M\arr p^{*}M$ which is an isomorphism. So $p^{*}\gamma_{M}$ and $\gamma_{M}=s^{*}p^{*}\gamma_{M}$ are isomorphisms. Since $p_{*}s_{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Mod}}}B$ is the identity, we can conclude that $s_{*}\simeq p^{*}$ and $s^{*}\simeq p_{*}$. Consider the Cartesian diagram $$\begin{tikzpicture}[xscale=1.5,yscale=-1.2]     \node (A0_0) at (0, 0) {$U$};     \node (A0_1) at (1, 0) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}C$};     \node (A1_0) at (0, 1) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$};     \node (A1_1) at (1, 1) {$\stX$};     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{g}$} (A0_1);     \path (A1_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{s}$} (A1_1);     \path (A0_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{h}$} (A1_1);     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{t}$} (A1_0);   \end{tikzpicture}$$ where $h$ is a representable fpqc covering. Since $\stX$ has quasi-affine diagonal it follows that $U$ is a quasi-affine scheme. Moreover $$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi U)\simeq g_{*}t^{*}\odi B\simeq h^{*}s_{*}\odi B\simeq h^{*}p^{*}\odi B\simeq C$$ Thus $g\colon U\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}C$ and, by descent, $s\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B\arr\stX$ are open immersions. We finish by proving that $g$ is surjective. Let $Z$ be the complement of $U$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}C$ with reduced structure. We have $$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi Z)\simeq p_{*}h_{*}\odi Z\simeq s^{*}h_{*}\odi Z=t_{*}g^{*}\odi Z=0$$ Thus $Z$ is empty as required. ◻
:::

Taking an affine atlas of $\stX$ and applying [\[lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections\]](#lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections"} we can also conclude that $f^{*}f_{*}\shF\arr\shF$ is surjective for all $\shF\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stY$. In particular if $\shD\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX$ generates $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stX$ then $f^{*}\shD$ generates $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\stY$ (see [@Tonini2014 Rem 7.3]).

::: rem
[]{#rem:fpqc descent of pseudo-affine label="rem:fpqc descent of pseudo-affine"} Consider a Cartesian diagram $$\begin{tikzpicture}[xscale=1.6,yscale=-1.2]     \node (A0_0) at (0, 0) {$Y$};     \node (A0_1) at (1, 0) {$\stY$};     \node (A1_0) at (0, 1) {$X$};     \node (A1_1) at (1, 1) {$\stX$};     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A0_1);     \path (A1_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_1);     \path (A0_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_1);     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_0);   \end{tikzpicture}$$ of pseudo-algebraic fpqc stacks such that $X\to\stX$ is a representable fpqc covering. If $Y\to X$ is pseudo-affine then so is $\stY\to\stX$.

By standard arguments of descent we can assume $\stX=\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B$ and $X=\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}B'$ affine. So $Y$ is pseudo-affine by hypothesis. Since $Y\to\stY$ is affine it follows that $\stY$ is quasi-compact with affine diagonal. In particular, since $B\arr B'$ is flat, we have $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi Y)\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi{\stY})\otimes_{B}B'$ and therefore we can assume $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi{\stY})=B$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{H}}}^{0}(\odi Y)=B'$. In this case $Y\arr X$ is flat and fully faithful and, since $\stX$ and $\stY$ are fpqc stacks, it follows that also $\stY\arr\stX$ is flat and fully faithful.

::: thm
[]{#thm:flat mono are quasi-affine label="thm:flat mono are quasi-affine"} A quasi-compact flat monomorphism of algebraic stacks is quasi-affine.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We have to prove that if $\stX\to\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}R$ is a flat monomorphism and $\stX$ is quasi-compact algebraic space then $\stX$ is a quasi-affine scheme. First we observe that $\stX$ is separated because $\stX\to\stX\times_{R}\stX$ is an isomorphism. Thus we can apply [\[lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections\]](#lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections"} and conclude that $\stX$ is pseudo-affine. Moreover by [@SP014 Tag 0B8A] the space $\stX$ is actually a scheme. By [@EGAII Prop 5.1.2] we conclude that $\stX$ is quasi-affine. ◻
:::

::: cor
[]{#cor:pseudo-affine algebraic is quasi-affine label="cor:pseudo-affine algebraic is quasi-affine"}A pseudo-affine algebraic stack is a quasi-affine scheme.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Use [\[lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections\]](#lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:recognizing affine rings from global sections"} and [\[thm:flat mono are quasi-affine\]](#thm:flat mono are quasi-affine){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:flat mono are quasi-affine"}. ◻

The following property is known for algebraic stacks (see [@Gross2013 Corollary 5.11] and [@Totaro2002 Proposition 1.3]).

::: cor
[]{#cor:quasi-affine and resolution implies affine label="cor:quasi-affine and resolution implies affine"} A quasi-compact fpqc stack $\stX$ with quasi-affine diagonal and with the resolution property has affine diagonal.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Since $\stX$ is pseudo-algebraic the category $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Loc}}}\stX$ is essentially small. Thus we can consider a set $\shR$ of representatives of isomorphism classes of locally free sheaves over $\stX$. Given $\E\in\shR$ we define the sheaf $$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}(\E)=\bigsqcup_{n\in\N}\mathop{\mathrm{\underline{\textup{Iso}}}}_{\stX}(\odi{\stX}^{n},\E)\colon(\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Aff}}}/\stX)^{\text{op}}\arr\sets\comma\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}(\E)(U\arrdi f\stX)=\bigsqcup_{n\in\N}\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Iso}}}_{U}(\odi U^{n},f^{*}\E)$$ The map $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}(\E)\arr\stX$ is an affine fpqc covering: $\stX=\sqcup_{n}\stX_{n}$ where $\stX_{n}$ is the locus where $\E$ has rank $n$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}(\E)_{|\stX_{n}}=\mathop{\mathrm{\underline{\textup{Iso}}}}_{\stX}(\odi{\stX}^{n},\E)\to\stX_{n}$ is a $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Gl}}}_{n}$-torsor. In particular $g\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}=\prod_{\E\in\shR}\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}(\E)\arr\stX$ is also an affine fpqc covering and $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}$ is quasi-compact with quasi-affine diagonal.

Since $g$ is affine by [\[rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps\]](#rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps){reference-type="ref" reference="rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps"} $g^{*}\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Loc}}}\stX$ generates $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}$. On the other hand if $\E\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Loc}}}\stX$ then by construction $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}$ is a (finite) disjoint union of open substacks over which $g^{*}\E$ is free, which implies that $g^{*}\E$ is generated by global sections. We can conclude that $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}$ is a pseudo-affine sheaf and, by [\[rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps\]](#rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps){reference-type="ref" reference="rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps"}, that it has affine diagonal.

In particular, taking an affine atlas of $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Fr}}}$, we get an affine map faithfully flat map $V\to\stX$ from an affine scheme. In particular $V\times_{\stX}V$ is affine and $V\times_{\stX}V\to V\times V$ is an affine map. Since this last map is the base change of the diagonal $\stX\to\stX\times\stX$ along the fpqc covering $V\times V\to\stX\times\stX$ we can conclude that the diagonal $\stX\to\stX\times\stX$ is affine as well. ◻
:::

We prove the following result to compare our results with [@Savin2006].

::: prop
[]{#rem: cases for resolution property label="rem: cases for resolution property"} Let $G$ be a flat and affine group scheme over $R$ such that $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}G$ has the resolution property. Then:

1.  If $U$ is a pseudo-affine sheaf over $R$ with an action of $G$ then $[U/G]$ has the resolution property.

2.  If $\stX=[X/G]$ for a quasi-compact scheme $X$ and there exists $\shL\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Pic}}}(\stX)$ whose pullback to $X$ is very ample relatively to $R$ then $\stX$ has the resolution property.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* 1) By [\[rem:absolute relative pseudo-affine\]](#rem:absolute relative pseudo-affine){reference-type="ref" reference="rem:absolute relative pseudo-affine"} and [\[rem:fpqc descent of pseudo-affine\]](#rem:fpqc descent of pseudo-affine){reference-type="ref" reference="rem:fpqc descent of pseudo-affine"} the map $[U/G]\arr\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}G$ is pseudo-affine. By [\[rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps\]](#rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps){reference-type="ref" reference="rem: generating subcategory under pseudo-affine maps"} we obtain the conclusion.

2\) Consider the Cartesian diagrams $$\begin{tikzpicture}[xscale=2.2,yscale=-1.2]     \node (A0_0) at (0, 0) {$U$};     \node (A0_1) at (1, 0) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}R$};     \node (A1_0) at (0, 1) {$X$};     
\node (A1_1) at (1, 1) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_R \Gm$};     
\node (A1_2) at (2, 1) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}R$};     \node (A2_0) at (0, 2) {$\stX$};     
\node (A2_1) at (1, 2) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_R(G\times \Gm)$};     
\node (A2_2) at (2, 2) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_R G$};     
\path (A0_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_1);     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A0_1);     \path (A2_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A2_1);     \path (A1_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_1);     \path (A1_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_2);     \path (A1_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A2_0);     \path (A1_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A2_1);     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_0);     
\path (A2_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A2_2);     
\path (A1_2) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A2_2);   \end{tikzpicture}$$ It follows that $\stX\simeq[U/G\times\Gm]$. Now consider the Cartesian diagrams $$\begin{tikzpicture}[xscale=2.2,yscale=-1.2]     \node (A0_0) at (0, 0) {$U$};     \node (A0_1) at (1, 0) {$\A^n_R - \{0\}$};     \node (A0_2) at (2, 0) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Spec}}}R$};     \node (A1_0) at (0, 1) {$X$};     \node (A1_1) at (1, 1) {$\PP^n_R$};     \node (A1_2) at (2, 1) {$\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_R \Gm$};     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A0_1);     \path (A0_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_1);     \path (A1_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{i}$} (A1_1);     \path (A1_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_2);     \path (A0_2) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_2);     \path (A0_0) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A1_0);     \path (A0_1) edge [->]node [auto] {$\scriptstyle{}$} (A0_2);   \end{tikzpicture}$$ where $i\colon X\to\PP_{R}^{n}$ is the immersion induced by $\shL$. Since $X$ is quasi-compact and $\PP_{R}^{n}$ is separated it follows that $U$ is quasi-compact. Moreover $U\to\A_{R}^{n}$ is an immersion and, since $\A_{R}^{n}$ is affine, it is quasi-compact. From [@SP014 Tag 01QV] we see that $U$ is a quasi-compact open of a closed subscheme of $\A_{R}^{n}$ and therefore it is quasi-affine.

Thus by 1) we just have to show that $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}(G\times\Gm)$ has the resolution property. Let $a\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}(G\times\Gm)=\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}G\times\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}\Gm\to\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}G$ be the projection and $\shN$ the pullback to $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}(G\times\Gm$) of the canonical invertible sheaf of $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}\Gm$. Given $\shF\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}(G\times\Gm)$) there is a canonical map $$\bigoplus_{n\in\Z}a^{*}a_{*}(\shF\otimes\shN^{-\otimes n})\otimes\shN^{\otimes n}\to\shF$$ Going fpqc locally on $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}G$ and using the usual representation theory of $\Gm$ we see that the above map is an isomorphism. It is therefore clear that the sheaves of the form $a^{*}\shG\otimes\shN^{\otimes n}$ for $\shG\in\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{Loc}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}G)$ generates $\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{QCoh}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{\textup{B}}}_{R}(G\times\Gm$). ◻
