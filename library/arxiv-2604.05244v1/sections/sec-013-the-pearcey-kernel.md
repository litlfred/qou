Just as the Airy kernel arises from the fold normal form $\zeta^3/3-u\zeta$, one expects the cusp normal form [\[eq:cusp-phase\]](#eq:cusp-phase){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:cusp-phase"} to produce the *Pearcey kernel* [@Pearcey1946]. In its standard integrable representation [@TracyWidomPearcey; @BleherKuijlaars2007; @AdlerFerrariVanMoerbeke2010], $$K_{\rm P}^{(\gamma)}(\sigma,\sigma')
  = \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^2}\int_\Gamma d\zeta\int_\Sigma d\eta\;
  \frac{\exp\!\big[\tfrac{1}{4}\zeta^4-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\zeta^2-\sigma\zeta\big]\,
        \exp\!\big[-\tfrac{1}{4}\eta^4+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\eta^2+\sigma'\eta\big]}
       {\zeta-\eta},
  \label{eq:pearcey-kernel}$$ where $\Gamma$ and $\Sigma$ are steepest-descent contours chosen so that the integrals converge. The kernel [\[eq:pearcey-kernel\]](#eq:pearcey-kernel){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:pearcey-kernel"} is a two-point correlation kernel that governs a determinantal point process, just as $K_{\mathop{\mathrm{Ai}}}$ does. It reduces to the Airy kernel in the neighborhood of each fold edge when $\gamma>0$, and to the pure cusp kernel when $\gamma=0$.
