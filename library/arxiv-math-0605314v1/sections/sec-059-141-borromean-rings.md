[]{#sec:borromean-rings label="sec:borromean-rings"}

As in Section [\[sec:invariant\]](#sec:invariant){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:invariant"}, let $B\in \BT^0_3$ denote the Borromean tangle. The closure $A=\cl(B)$ is the Borromean rings.

::: proposition
[]{#r4 label="r4"} For $i,i'\ge 0$, we have $$(1\otimes \trq^{P'_{i}}\otimes \trq^{P'_{i'}})(J_B) =\delta _{i,i'}(-1)^i\sigma _i .$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let $U_h^0\cong\modQ [H][[h]]$ denote the $h$-adic closure of the $\modQ [[h]]$-subalgebra of $U_h$ generated by $H$. Recall that the *Harish-Chandra map* of $U_h$ is the continuous $\modQ [[h]]$-module homomorphism $\varphi\zzzcolon U_h \rightarrow  U_h^0$ determined by $\varphi(F^iH^jE^k)=\delta _{i,0}\delta _{k,0}H^j$ for $i,j,k\ge 0$.

By [\[eq:7\]](#eq:7){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:7"}, for any $x,x'\in \modR _{\modQ [[h]]}$, we have $$(\varphi\otimes \trq^x\otimes \trq^{x'})(J_B)
       =(\varphi\otimes \trq^x\otimes \trq^{x'})
      (\sum_{m_1,m_2,m_3,n_1,n_2,n_3\ge 0}
    B_{m_1,m_2,m_3,n_1,n_2,n_3}),$$ where $B_{m_1,m_2,m_3,n_1,n_2,n_3}$ denotes the summand in [\[eq:7\]](#eq:7){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:7"}. It follows from properties of $\varphi$ and $\trq^x$ that $(\varphi\otimes \trq^x\otimes \trq^{x'})(B_{m_1,m_2,m_3,n_1,n_2,n_3})=0$ unless $n_1=n_3=0$ and $m_1=m_3=m_2+n_2$. Hence, setting $k=m_1$ and $l=n_2$, we have $$\begin{split}
      &(\varphi\otimes \trq^x\otimes \trq^{x'})(J_B)\\
      &=\sum_{k\ge 0}\sum_{l=0}^k
      (\varphi\otimes \trq^x\otimes \trq^{x'})(B_{k,l,k,0,k-l,0})\\
      &=\sum_{k\ge 0}\sum_{l=0}^k
      (-1)^{l}  q^{ -\hf (k-l)(k-l+1)-l -4kl+2k^2 }\\
    &\quad
    \varphi(e^k   \tF^{(k)} K^{-2k+2l})
    \trq^x(e^{k-l}    \tF^{(k)} e^lK^{-2k})
    \trq^{x'}(\tF^{(l)} e^k    \tF^{(k-l)}K^{-2k} ).
    \end{split}$$ Using the identity $\trq^x(yy')=\trq^x(y'S^2(y))$ for $y,y\in U_h$, we obtain $$\begin{split}
      &\trq^x(e^{k-l}    \tF^{(k)} e^lK^{-2k})
      \trq^{x'}(\tF^{(l)} e^k    \tF^{(k-l)}K^{-2k} )\\
      &=q^{-2k+2l+3kl+l^2}\bbq{k}{l}
      \trq^x(\tF^{(k)}K^{-2k}e^k)
      \trq^{x'}(\tF^{(k)}K^{-2k}e^k).
    \end{split}$$ Now, set $x=P_{i}$ and $x'=P_{i'}$. By Lemma [\[lem:6\]](#lem:6){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:6"}, we have for $i\ge 0$ $$\trq^{P_i}(\tF^{(k)}K^{-2k}e^k)
      =\delta _{i,k}v^{k} q^{-k^2} \{k\}_q!.$$ Moreover, using [\[eq:60\]](#eq:60){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:60"}, we obtain $$\varphi(e^k   \tF^{(k)} K^{-2k+2l})
    =\varphi(e^k   \tF^{(k)}) K^{-2k+2l}
    =\{H\}_{q,k}  K^{-2k+2l}.$$ Using the above formulas, we obtain $$\begin{split}
      &(\varphi\otimes \trq^{P_{i}}\otimes \trq^{P_{i'}})(J_B)
      =\delta _{i,i'}
    \Bigl(\sum_{l=0}^{i}
    (-1)^{l}
    q^{\hf l(l+3)-\hf i(i+3)}
    \bbq{i}{l}
    K^{2l-2i}
    \Bigr)
    \{H\}_{q,i}
     (\{i\}_q!)^2.
    \end{split}$$ Using the identities $$\begin{gathered}
      \sum_{l=0}^{i}(-1)^{l}q^{\hf l(l+3)-\hf i(i+3)}
      \bbq{i}{l}K^{2l-2i}
      =\BBq{-H-2}{i},\\
      \varphi(\sigma _i)
      = \BB{H}{i} \BB{H+i+1}{i}
      = (-1)^i\BB{H}{i} \BB{-H-2}{i},
  
\end{gathered}$$ we obtain $$\begin{split}
      &(\varphi\otimes \trq^{P_{i}}\otimes \trq^{P_{i'}})(J_B)
      =\delta _{i,i'}
    \BBq{-H-2}{i}\BBq{H}{i}
     (\{i\}_q!)^2\\
    &\quad =\delta _{i,i'} \BB{-H-2}{i}\BB{H}{i}
     (\{i\}!)^2
    =\delta _{i,i'}(-1)^i\varphi(\sigma _i) (\{i\}!)^2.
    \end{split}$$ Hence, we have $(\varphi\otimes \trq^{P'_{i}}\otimes \trq^{P'_{i'}})(J_B)=\delta _{i,i'}(-1)^i\varphi(\sigma _i)$. Then the assertion follows from the well-known fact that $\varphi$ is injective on the center $Z(U_h)$. ◻
:::

Propositions [\[r4\]](#r4){reference-type="ref" reference="r4"} implies the following.

::: corollary
[]{#r2 label="r2"} For $i,j,k\ge 0$, we have $$\begin{gathered}
    \label{e3}
    J_{A}(P'_i, P'_j, P'_k)
    =\begin{cases}
    (-1)^i{\BB{2i+1}{i+1}}/{\{1\}} & \text{if $i=j=k$},\\
    0 & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}\\
    \label{e10}
    J_{A}(\V_i, \V_j, \V_k)
    =\sum_{p=0}^{\min(i,j,k)}(-1)^p
    \bb{i+1+p}{2p+1} \bb{j+1+p}{2p+1}\bb{k+1+p}{2p+1}
    (\{p\}!)^2\{2p+1\}_{2p}.
  
\end{gathered}$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* For [\[e3\]](#e3){reference-type="eqref" reference="e3"}, use Proposition [\[r22\]](#r22){reference-type="ref" reference="r22"}. For [\[e10\]](#e10){reference-type="eqref" reference="e10"}, use [\[e37\]](#e37){reference-type="eqref" reference="e37"}. ◻
:::

Corollary [\[r2\]](#r2){reference-type="ref" reference="r2"} was first stated using the Kauffman bracket in [@H:iias2000] without proof. One can easily modify the arguments in [@Masbaum] to obtain a skein-theoretic proof of Corollary [\[r2\]](#r2){reference-type="ref" reference="r2"}.
