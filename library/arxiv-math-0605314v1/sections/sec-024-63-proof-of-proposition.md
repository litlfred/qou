### The homomorphism $\xi \zzzcolon \modR _{\modQ [[h]]}\rightarrow Z(U_h)$ {#sec:isomorphism-ztu}

Let $c_+$ denote the $2$-component bottom tangle depicted in Figure [\[fig:c+\]](#fig:c+){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:c+"}. We have $$J_{c_+}= (S\otimes 1)(R_{21}R)=\sum S(\alpha )S(\beta ')\otimes \alpha '\beta \in \Inv(\Uhx2),$$ where $R=\sum\alpha \otimes \beta =\sum\alpha '\otimes \beta '$.

Define a continuous $\modQ [[h]]$-algebra homomorphism $  \xi \zzzcolon \modR _{\modQ [[h]]} \rightarrow  Z(U_h)$ by $$\xi (y) :=  (1\otimes \trq^y)(J_{c_+})
  =\sum S(\alpha )S(\beta ')\trq^y(\alpha '\beta )$$ for $y\in \modR _{\modQ [[h]]}$. Indeed, we can verify $$\label{e35}
  \xi (VV')=\xi (V)\xi (V')$$ graphically as depicted in Figure [\[fig:T01\]](#fig:T01){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:T01"}. We can also verify $\xi (\V_1)=C$ by computation. Since the $C^i$, $i\ge 0$, are linearly independent in $Z(U_h)$, it follows that $\xi$ is injective.

### The Hopf link pairing $\langle ,\rangle \zzzcolon \modR _{\Zvv}\times \modR _{\Zvv}\rightarrow \Zvv$ {#sec:pairing-hhh}

Define a symmetric bilinear form $$\langle ,\rangle \zzzcolon \modR _{\modQ [[h]]}\times \modR _{\modQ [[h]]}\rightarrow \modQ [[h]]$$ by $$\label{eq:85}
  \langle x,y\rangle :=(\trq^x\otimes \trq^y)(J_{c_+})= \trq^x(\xi (y)) = J_H(x,y),$$ for $x,y\in U_h$, where $H=H_1\cup H_2=\cl(c_+)$ denotes the $0$-framed Hopf link with linking number $-1$.

It is well known (see [@Reshetikhin-Turaev:invariants; @Kirby-Melvin]) that for $m,n\ge 0$, we have $$\langle \V_m, \V_n\rangle  = [(m+1)(n+1)].$$ Therefore, $\langle ,\rangle$ restricts to a bilinear form $$\langle ,\rangle \zzzcolon \modR _{\Zvv}\times \modR _{\Zvv}\rightarrow \Zvv.$$ We also need the induced bilinear form $$\langle ,\rangle \zzzcolon \modR _{\modQ (v)}\times \modR _{\modQ (v)}\rightarrow \modQ (v).$$

### The elements $S_n$ {#sec:elements-checkn}

Let $\modS$ denote the $\Zqq$-subalgebra of $\modR _{\Zqq}$ generated by $\V_1^2$. We have $\modS =\Span_{\Zqq}\{\V_{2i}\zzzvert i\ge 0\}$.

For $n\ge 0$, set $$S_n
  =\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\V_1^2-(v^i+v^{-i})^2)
  =\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\V_2-(q^i+1+q^{-i}))
  \in \modS .$$ (The elements corresponding to $S_n$ in the Kauffman bracket skein module of solid torus have been introduced in [@H:iias2000].) Clearly, we have $$\label{e90}
  \xi (S_n)=\sigma _n$$ for $n\ge 0$. Observe that $S_n$ is a monic polynomial in $\V_1^2$ of degree $n$. Therefore, $\modS$ is spanned over $\Zqq$ by the elements $S_n$, $n\ge 0$.

::: proposition
[]{#r23 label="r23"} If $m,n\ge 0$, then we have $$\begin{gathered}
    \label{e21} \langle P_m, S_n\rangle  = \delta _{m,n}\BB{2m+1}{2m}.
  
\end{gathered}$$ (As a consequence, the map $\langle ,\rangle \zzzcolon \modR _{\Zvv}\times \modS \rightarrow \Zvv$ is nondegenerate.)
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* For $k\ge 0$, set $\V'_k=\V_k/[k+1]$. The map $$\langle -,\V'_k\rangle \zzzcolon \modR _{\Zvv}\rightarrow \Zvv,\quad  x\mapsto \langle x,\V'_k\rangle$$ is a $\Zvv$-algebra homomorphism.

We will prove $$\label{eq:37} \langle P_m, \V_{2n}\rangle  =[2n+1]\{n+m\}_{2m}.$$ For $p\ge 0$, we have $$\langle \V_1-v^{2p+1}-v^{-2p-1},\V'_{2n}\rangle 
    =v^{2n+1}+v^{-2n-1}-v^{2p+1}-v^{-2p-1}=\{n-p\}\{n+p+1\}.$$ Since $\langle -,\V'_{2n}\rangle$ is a $\Zvv$-algebra homomorphism, we have $$\langle P_m,\V'_{2n}\rangle 
    =\prod_{p=0}^{m-1}\langle \V_1 - v^{2p+1}-v^{-2p-1},\V'_{2n}\rangle 
    =\prod_{p=0}^{m-1}\{n-p\}\{n+p+1\}=\{n+m\}_{2m}.$$ Therefore, we have [\[eq:37\]](#eq:37){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:37"}. Similarly, we can prove $$\label{eq:40} \langle \V_m,S_n\rangle  = \{m+n+1\}_{2n+1}/\{1\}.$$

By [\[eq:37\]](#eq:37){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:37"}, $\langle P_m,\V_{2n}\rangle =0$ when $0\le n<m$. Therefore, we have $\langle P_m,S_n\rangle =0$ if $0\le n<m$. By [\[eq:40\]](#eq:40){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:40"}, $\langle \V_m,S_n\rangle =0$ when $0\le m<n$. Hence, we have $\langle P_m,S_n\rangle =0$ when $0\le m<n$. By [\[eq:37\]](#eq:37){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:37"}, $$\langle P_m,S_m\rangle =\langle P_m,\V_{2m}\rangle =[2m+1]\BB{2m}{2m}=\BB{2m+1}{2m},$$ since $S_m-\V_{2m}$ is a linear combination of $\V_{2i}$ for $i=0,1,\ldots,m-1$. This completes the proof. ◻
:::

::: remark
[]{#thm:91 label="thm:91"} One can prove [\[eq:100\]](#eq:100){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:100"} as follows. Assume $\V_n=\sum_{j=0}^na_{n,j}P_j$, where $a_{n,j}\in \Zvv$ for $j=0,\ldots,n$. Applying $\langle -,S_i\rangle$ to the both sides, we obtain $\langle \V_n,S_i\rangle =\sum_{j=0}^n a_{n,j}\langle P_j,S_i\rangle$. By [\[eq:40\]](#eq:40){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:40"} and [\[e21\]](#e21){reference-type="eqref" reference="e21"}, we have $\{n+i+1\}_{2i+1}/\{1\}=a_{n,i}\BB{2i+1}{2i}$, hence $a_{n,i}=\bb{n+i+1}{2i+1}$.
:::

### Proof of Proposition [\[r22\]](#r22){reference-type="ref" reference="r22"} {#sec:proof-prop-refr22-1}

For each $n\ge 0$ we have $\xi (S_n) = \sigma _n$. Therefore, by [\[e90\]](#e90){reference-type="eqref" reference="e90"}, [\[eq:85\]](#eq:85){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:85"} and [\[e21\]](#e21){reference-type="eqref" reference="e21"}, we have $$\trq^{P_m}(\sigma _n)
  =\trq^{P_m}(\xi (S_n))
  =\langle P_m,S_n\rangle 
  =\delta _{m,n}\BB{2m+1}{2m},$$ hence the assertion.
