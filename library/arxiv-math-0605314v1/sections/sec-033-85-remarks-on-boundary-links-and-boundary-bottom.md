[]{#sec:remarks-bound-links label="sec:remarks-bound-links"}

In this subsection, we give some remarks on the case of boundary links. The reader may skip this subsection since it is not necessary in the rest of the paper.

Let $\bUqv$ denote the $\modZ [q,q^{-1}]$-subalgebra of $\Uqv$ generated by the elements $K^2$, $K^{-2}$, $e$ and $f:=(q-1)FK$. Define a decreasing filtration $\modF _k(\bUqv)$, $k\ge 0$, by $$\modF _k(\bUqv)=\bUqv\cap \modF _p(\Uqv)=\bUqv\cap \modF _p(\Uq).$$ Let $\bUqvt$ denote the completion in $U_h$ of $\bUqv$ with respect to the filtration $\modF _k(\bUqv)$, i.e., we set $$\bUqvt=\operatorname{Image}\Bigl(\varprojlim_k\bUqv/\modF _k(\bUqv)\rightarrow U_h\Bigr).$$ Clearly, $\bUqvt$ is a $\modZ [q,q^{-1}]$-subalgebra of $\tUqv$. In the similar way to the case of $\tUqvn$, we can define the completed tensor products $\bUqvt\;^{\tOn}=\bUqvt\;\tO\cdots\tO\bUqvt\subset \Uhn$. Let $\Inv(\bUqvtn)$ denote the invariant part of $\bUqvtn$. We have $$\Inv(\bUqvtx1)=\Inv(\bUqvt)=Z(\bUqvt)=Z(\tUqv).$$ Here one can easily verify the last identity using [@H:uqsl2 Section 9].

A bottom tangle $T=T_1\cup \cdots\cup T_n$ with $0$-framings in a cube $[0,1]^3$ is called a *boundary bottom tangle* if there are $n$ disjoint, connected, oriented surface $F_1,\ldots,F_n$ in $[0,1]^3$ such that for each $i=1,\ldots,n$ the boundary $\partial F_i$ of $F_i$ is the union of $T_i$ and the line segment in $\partial [0,1]^3$ bounded by the two endpoints of $T_i$. (See also [@H:universal Section 9.3].)

The following conjecture generalizes the case $n=1$ of Theorem [\[thm:8\]](#thm:8){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:8"}. (Note that a bottom knot is a $1$-component, boundary bottom tangle.)

::: conjecture
[]{#r81 label="r81"} Let $T\in \BT_n$ be an $n$-component, boundary bottom tangle. Then we have $J_T\in \Inv(\bUqvtn)$.
:::

Here we give an outline of a possible proof of Conjecture [\[r81\]](#r81){reference-type="ref" reference="r81"}. We have not worked out all the details yet, but the following idea seems useful. The main tool is [@H:universal Theorem 9.9], which reduces the problem to showing that $Y^{\otimes g}(J_{T'})\in \bUqvtx g$ for any bottom tangle $T'\in \BT_{2g}$ (with any linking matrix), $g\ge 0$. Here $Y\zzzcolon U_h\ho U_h\rightarrow U_h$ is the commutator morphism for the transmutation of $U_h$ (see [@H:universal Section 9.3] for the definition).

Conjecture [\[r81\]](#r81){reference-type="ref" reference="r81"} implies the following variant of Theorem [\[r29\]](#r29){reference-type="ref" reference="r29"}.

::: conjecture
[]{#r20 label="r20"} Let $L$ be an $m$-component, $0$-framed, boundary link. For $i=1,\ldots,m$, let $x_i\in \modP _{k_i}$, $k_i\ge 0$. Then we have $$\label{e88}
    J_L(x_1,\ldots,x_m)
    \in \frac{\BBq{2k_1+1}{k_1+1}}{\{1\}_q}I_{k_2}I_{k_3}\cdots I_{k_m},$$ Here, for $k\ge 0$, $I_k$ is the ideal in $\Zqq$ generated by the elements $\{k-l\}_q!\{l\}_q!$, $l=0,\ldots,k$. (One can permute $k_1,\ldots,k_m$ in [\[e88\]](#e88){reference-type="eqref" reference="e88"}.)
:::

::: proof
*Proof that Conjecture [\[r81\]](#r81){reference-type="ref" reference="r81"} implies Conjecture [\[r20\]](#r20){reference-type="ref" reference="r20"}.* We can express $L$ as the closure of an $m$-component, boundary bottom tangle $T$. We have $$y:=(\id\otimes \trq^{x_2}\otimes \cdots\otimes \trq^{x_m})(J_T)
    \in I_{k_2}I_{k_3}\cdots I_{k_m}(\bUqv)\tilde{},$$ since $J_T$ is an infinite sum of elements of $(\bUqv)^{\otimes m}$ by Conjecture [\[r81\]](#r81){reference-type="ref" reference="r81"} and we have $\trq^{x_i}(\bUqv)\subset I_{k_i}$ by Lemma [\[lem:6\]](#lem:6){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:6"}. By [\[e1\]](#e1){reference-type="eqref" reference="e1"}, we have $$J_L(x_1,\ldots,x_m)=(\trq^{x_1}\otimes \cdots\otimes \trq^{x_m})(J_T)=\trq^{x_1}(y).$$ The rest of the proof is similar to that for Theorem [\[r29\]](#r29){reference-type="ref" reference="r29"}. ◻
:::

::: remark
[]{#r21 label="r21"} One can prove partial results of Conjecture [\[r20\]](#r20){reference-type="ref" reference="r20"} by focusing on the divisibility of link invariants by powers of $q-1$ or $q+1$. It is not difficult to prove using the theory of Goussarov-Vassiliev finite type link invariants that if $L$ is an $m$-component, boundary link, then $J_L(P_{k_1},\ldots,P_{k_m})$ is divisible by $(q-1)^{2(k_1+\cdots+k_m)}$ (hence $J_L(P'_{k_1},\ldots,P'_{k_m})$ is divisible by $(q-1)^{k_1+\cdots+k_m}$). One can also prove that $J_L(P_{k_1},\ldots,P_{k_m})$ is divisible by $(q+1)^{k_1+\cdots+k_m}$. The latter assertion follows from the fact that the Jones polynomial (with colors $\V_1$) of $n$-component, boundary link is divisible by $(q+1)^n$, which we plan to prove in a paper in preparation [@H:spanning] using skein theory.
:::
