The bilinear correlators in the Gaussian Hermitian matrix model are generated by the action of the $W$-operators $\hat W^{(-)}$ on the Schur function $S_R$ as functions of $P_k:={\rm Tr}\,H^k$ [@MMNek], $$\begin{aligned}
\label{da}
\Big<S_Q\{\hat W^{(-)}_k\}\cdot S_R\{P_k\}\Big>=
{\displaystyle{S_{R/Q}\{\delta_{k,2}\}S_R\{N\}}\over \displaystyle{S_R\{\delta_{k,1}\}}}\nonumber\\
\hat W^{(-)}_k:={\rm Tr}\,\left({\partial\over\partial H}\right)^k
\end{aligned}$$ where $H$ is the matrix that is integrated over in the matrix model, and, by the matrix derivative, we imply the derivative w.r.t. matrix elements of the transposed matrix: $\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial H}\right)_{ij}=\frac{\partial}{\partial H_{ji}}$.

As we demonstrated in [@MMd], the averages ([\[da\]](#da){reference-type="ref" reference="da"}) can be reduced to a correlator of the form $$\begin{aligned}
\Big<S_Q\{\hat W^{(-)}_k\}\cdot S_R\{P_k\}\Big>=\Big<K_Q\{P_k\}\cdot S_R\{P_k\}\Big>
\end{aligned}$$ where the polynomials $K_R$ form a complete basis, and celebrate the property $$\begin{aligned}
\Big<K_R\cdot K_Q\Big>=\displaystyle{S_{R}\{N\}\over \displaystyle{S_R\{\delta_{k,1}\}}}\delta_{RQ}
\end{aligned}$$ Examples of these polynomials can be found in [@MMd Appendix]. They are obtained by using integration by parts and the action of the $S_Q\{\hat W^{(-)}_k\}$-operators on the Gaussian measure.

Note that, throughout the paper [@MMd], we discussed another basis of polynomials, $K_\Delta$, the two being related by the Fröbenius formula $$\begin{aligned}
K_R=\sum_\Delta{\psi_R(\Delta)\over z_\Delta}K_\Delta\nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ where $z_\Delta$ is the standard symmetric factor of the Young diagram (order of the automorphism) [@Fulton].
