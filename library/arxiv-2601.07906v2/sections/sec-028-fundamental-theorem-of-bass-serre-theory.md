Fundamental theorem of Bass-Serre theory
The main observation of Bass-Serre theory is that one can recover both the tree T
as well as the original group G acting on it from the corresponding graph of groups
Γ. Thus, the data of a graph of groups is equivalent to the tree with a group action.
We already saw how to construct the graph of groups of Γ from G acting on T.
We will in the following explain how to go in the other direction and reconstruct G
and the tree T from Γ. In the literature, the group G is commonly referred to as the
fundamental group of the graph of groups, and it is denoted by G = π1(Γ). The name
and notation stems from a close connection to topology that we explain below. The
tree T constructed from a graph of groups is called the Bass-Serre covering tree.
The fundamental group of a graph of groups.
This group is defined as the
fundamental group of an auxiliary topological space X, which is obtained by gluing
a number of pathconnected spaces Xv, v ∈V, along edge spaces Xe, e ∈E, defined
as follows. For each edge e ∈E in the graph of groups, Xe+ and Xe−overlap in a
common subspace Xe = Xe+ ∩Xe−, which is identified in the gluing. Ignoring the
subtleties resulting from the choice of basepoint, one chooses the spaces Xv and Xe
such that π1(Xv) ∼= Gv and π1(Xe) ∼= Ge. The boundary monomorphisms ∂±
