The main observation of Bass-Serre theory is that one can recover both the treeT
as well as the original groupGacting on it from the corresponding graph of groups
Γ. Thus, the data of a graph of groups is equivalent to the tree with a group action.
We already saw how to construct the graph of groups of Γ fromGacting onT.
We will in the following explain how to go in the other direction and reconstructG
and the treeTfrom Γ. In the literature, the groupGis commonly referred to as the
fundamental group of the graph of groups, and it is denoted byG=π 1(Γ). The name
and notation stems from a close connection to topology that we explain below. The
treeTconstructed from a graph of groups is called theBass-Serre covering tree.

the other groups, the spanning tree is the whole graph since there are no loops.
