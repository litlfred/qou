From now on, we will assume that either LA is split, or L = k and k is perfect. One consequence
of this assumption is that we can identify the Grothendieck groups Ro(LA) and Ro(kA). We then have

Theorem 1.5 The modular reduction induces an additive map dg : Rj (KA) > Rj (LA) such that
do([KV]) = [kV], where V is an OA-lattice and [kV] is regarded as an element of RJ (LA) via the

The map dg is called the decomposition map associated with the specialisation 6: R > L. Note that
the map dg depends only on @ and not on the choice of the valuation ring O. The matrix Dg of this
map with respect to the bases of Ro(K A) and Ro(LA) consisting of the classes of the simple modules
is called the decomposition matrix associated with 0. The rows of Dg are labelled by Irr(A A) and the
columns of Dg are labelled by Irr(LA).

The following result gives a criterion for dg to be trivial. It is known as “Tits’s deformation theorem”.
For its proof, the reader may refer, for example, to Theorem 7.4.6].

Theorem 1.6 Assume that KA and LA are split. If LA is semisimple, then K A is also semisimple and
the decomposition map dg is an isomorphism which preserves isomorphism classes of simple modules. In
particular, we have a bijection Irr(K A) + Irr(LA).
