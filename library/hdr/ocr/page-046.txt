5.4.2 The Calogero—Moser partition and Kazhdan—Lusztig cells
In an effort to develop a generalised Kazhdan-Lusztig cell theory, Bonnafé and Rouquier

used the Calogero—Moser partition to define Calogero—Moser cells for all complex reflection groups. An
advantage of this, quite geometric, approach is that the Calogero—Moser partition exists naturally for all
complex reflection groups. It also implies automatically the existence of a semicontinuity property for
cells, a property that was conjectured and proved in some cases for Kazhdan—Lusztig cells by Bonnafé
[Bo2]. However, Calogero—Moser cells are very hard to compute and their construction depends on an
“uncontrollable” choice. After very long computations, it has been confirmed that the Calogero-Moser
cells coincide with the Kazhdan—Lusztig cells for some finite Coxeter groups of rank 2: the Weyl groups
Ao, Bo, Go and the dihedral groups I2(m) with m odd {Bo3]; there is still a lot of work that

needs to be done.
