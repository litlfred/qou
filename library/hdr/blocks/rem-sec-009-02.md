We have G(1, 1, n) ∼= Sn, G(2, 1, n) ∼= Bn, G(2, 2, n) ∼= Dn, G(m, m, 2) ∼= I2(m), G23 ∼= H3, G28 ∼= F4, G30 ∼= H4, G35 ∼= E6, G36 ∼= E7, G37 ∼= E8. Let W ⊂GL(h) be a complex reﬂection group. Benard [Ben] and Bessis [Bes] have proved (using a case-by-case analysis) that the ﬁeld K generated by the traces on h of all the elements of W is a splitting ﬁeld for W. The ﬁeld K is called the ﬁeld of deﬁnition of W. If K ⊆R, then W is a ﬁnite Coxeter group, and if K = Q, then W is a Weyl group.
