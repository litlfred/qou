The standard procedure of determining $n$ is to investigate the interaction Lagrangian which is relevant to the decay $\pi^0\to\gamma\gamma$ by expanding $U={\mathbbm 1}+i\mbox{diag}(\pi^0,-\pi^0,0)/F_0+\cdots.$ However, as pointed out by Bär and Wiese, when considering the electromagnetic interaction for an arbitrary number of colors one should replace the ordinary quark charge matrix by $$Q=\left( \begin{array}{rrr} \frac{2}{3}&0&0\\ 0&-\frac{1}{3}&0\\ 0&0&-\frac{1}{3} \end{array} \right) \to \left( \begin{array}{ccc} \frac{1}{2N_c}+\frac{1}{2}&0&0\\ 0&\frac{1}{2N_c}-\frac{1}{2}&0\\ 0&0&\frac{1}{2N_c}-\frac{1}{2} \end{array} \right).$$ The corresponding effective Lagrangian for $\pi^0\to\gamma\gamma$ decay, $${\cal L}_{\pi^0\gamma\gamma}=-\frac{n}{N_c} \frac{e^2}{32\pi^2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu \rho\sigma}{\cal F}_{\mu\nu}{\cal F}_{\rho\sigma}\frac{\pi^0}{F_0},$$ results in the decay rate $$\Gamma_{\pi^0\to\gamma\gamma}= \frac{\alpha^2 M^3_{\pi^0}}{64 \pi^3 F^2_0}\,\frac{n^2}{N_c^2} =7.6\,\mbox{eV}\times\left(\frac{n}{N_c}\right)^2$$ in good agreement with the experimental value $(7.7\pm 0.6)$ eV for $n=N_c$.
