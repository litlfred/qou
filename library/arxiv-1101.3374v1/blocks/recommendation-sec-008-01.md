For $u\in C^\infty(T^3)$, with $$u(\textup{\bf x})\ = \ \sum_{\textup{\bf n}\in\mathbb Z^3} b_{\textup{\bf n}}e^{i\textup{\bf n}\,\raisebox{-.3ex}{\Large$\hskip-.02in\cdot$\hskip.005in}\textup{\bf x}}\,,$$ we have $$\Delta u(\textup{\bf x})\ = \ \sum_{\textup{\bf n}\neq{\bf 0}}|\textup{\bf n}|^2b_{\textup{\bf n}}e^{i\textup{\bf n}\,\raisebox{-.3ex}{\Large$\hskip-.02in\cdot$\hskip.005in}\textup{\bf x}}.$$ Because the $(0,0,0)$-coefficient of $\Delta u$ is zero, this shows why in order to solve $\Delta u=f$ we must restrict $f$ to be in $C^\infty_0(T^3)$.
