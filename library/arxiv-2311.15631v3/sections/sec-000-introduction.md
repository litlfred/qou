Throughout the paper, $\mathcal C$ denotes a fusion category (FC) over the field $\mathbb C$ of complex numbers (see [@EGNO15] for definitions). Its simple objects are denoted $c_i$'s with $i=1,\dots,\text{rank}(\mathcal C)$. We denote its fusion ring by $K(\mathcal C)$. A pivotal structure $a:id_{\mathcal C}\cong(-)^{**}$ defines left and right quantum (or categorical) traces $\text{tr}^{L/R}(a):1\to1$. A pivotal structure is called spherical if $\forall c\in\mathcal C$, $\text{tr}^L(a_c)=\text{tr}^R(a_{c^*})$. In an FC $\mathcal C$ with a spherical structure $a$, a (quantum) dimension of an object $c\in\mathcal C$ is defined as $d_c:=\text{tr}^L(a_c)$. Its squared sum defines categorical (or global) dimension $$D^2(\mathcal C):=\sum_{i=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathcal C)}d_{c_i}^2.\label{categoricaldim}$$ Note that for a given categorical dimension, there are two $D(\mathcal C)$'s, one positive and one negative. If an FC is equipped with a braiding $c$, in order to avoid confusion, we especially write the braided fusion category (BFC) $\mathcal B$ and simple objects $b_i$'s. A spherical BFC is called pre-modular fusion category (pre-MFC). This is our ambient category. If the braiding in a pre-MFC is non-degenerate, the category is called modular or modular fusion category (MFC).

With a braiding, we can study commutative algebras. An algebra in an FC $\mathcal C$ is a triple $(A,\mu,u)$ of an object $A\in\mathcal C$, multiplication morphism $\mu:A\otimes A\to A$, and unit morphism $u:1\to A$ obeying associativity and unit axioms. A category $\mathcal C_A$ of right $A$-modules consists of pairs $(m,p)$ where $m\in\mathcal C$ and $p:m\otimes A\to m$ subject to coherence conditions. (A category $_A\mathcal C$ of left $A$-modules is defined analogously.) An algebra is called separable if $\mathcal C_A$ is semisimple. An algebra $A\in\mathcal B$ in a BFC is called commutative if $$\mu\cdot c_{A,A}=\mu.\label{commutativealg}$$ A commutative separable algebra is called étale. Any étale algebra $A$ decomposes to a direct sum of connected ones [@DMNO10] where $A\in\mathcal C$ is called connected if $\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal C(1,A)=1$. An example of connected étale algebra is the unit $A\cong1\in\mathcal B$ giving $\mathcal B_A\simeq\mathcal B$. A BFC $\mathcal B$ without nontrivial connected étale algebra is called completely anisotropic. The goal of this paper is to classify connected étale algebras[^1] in pre-MFCs. Our main results are summarized as the

What is central in our classification method [@KK23GSD] is the Frobenius-Perron dimensions. They are defined as follows. An FC $\mathcal C$ has Grothendieck ring $\text{Gr}(\mathcal C)$. It admits a non-negative integer matrix representation (NIM-rep). The matrix $(N_i)_{jk}:={N_{ij}}^k$ consists of $\mathbb N$-coefficients $$c_i\otimes c_j\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathcal C)}{N_{ij}}^kc_k.\label{NIM}$$ Since they have non-negative entries, we can apply the Perron-Frobenius theorem to get the largest positive eigenvalue $\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal C}(c_i)$ called Frobenius-Perron dimension of $c_i$ (or $N_i$). Its squared sum defines Frobenius-Perron dimension of an FC $$\text{FPdim}(\mathcal C):=\sum_{i=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathcal C)}(\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal C}(c_i))^2.\label{FPdimC}$$ An FC $\mathcal C$ with $D^2(\mathcal C)=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal C)$ is called pseudo-unitary, and unitary if $\forall c\in\mathcal C$, $d_c=\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal C}(c)$. The Frobenius-Perron dimension defines a Lagrangian algebra. A connected étale algebra $A$ in a BFC $\mathcal B$ is called Lagrangian if $(\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal B}(A))^2=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B)$. With the Frobenius-Perron dimension at hand, our classification method [@KK23GSD] consists of three steps:

1.  Find a maximal rank $r_\text{max}$ on $\text{rank}(\mathcal B_A)$,

2.  List up candidate fusion categories for $\mathcal B_A$,

3.  Check which of them satisfy axioms.

A maximal rank on $\mathcal B_A$ is given by $r_\text{max}=\lfloor\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B)\rfloor$ [@KK23GSD]. This is because $\forall c\in\mathcal C$, $\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal C}(c)\ge1$ [@ENO02; @EGNO15], and for a BFC $\mathcal B$ and a connected étale algebra $A\in\mathcal B$, Frobenius-Perron dimensions obey [@KO01; @ENO02; @DMNO10] $$\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal B}(A)=\frac{\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B)}{\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B_A)}.\label{FPdimA}$$ Since $\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal B}(A)\ge1$, we obtain $$1\le\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B_A)\le\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B),\label{fpdimeq}$$ and $$\text{rank}(\mathcal B_A)\le r_\text{max}=\lfloor\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B)\rfloor.\label{rmax}$$ In our setup, $\mathcal B_A$ is known to be a fusion category.[^2] Hence, we list up candidate fusion categories $\mathcal C$'s for $\mathcal B_A$ in the second step. The candidates should satisfy three conditions: i) $\text{rank}(\mathcal C)\le r_\text{max}=\lfloor\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B)\rfloor$, ii) $(1\le)\text{FPdim}(\mathcal C)\le\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B)$, and iii) $\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal B}(A)=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal B)/\text{FPdim}(\mathcal C)$. The first two conditions are coming from ([\[rmax\]](#rmax){reference-type="ref" reference="rmax"},[\[fpdimeq\]](#fpdimeq){reference-type="ref" reference="fpdimeq"}). The third condition is coming from ([\[FPdimA\]](#FPdimA){reference-type="ref" reference="FPdimA"}). Since $A$ is a direct sum of simple objects in $\mathcal B$, its Frobenius-Perron dimension is given by a linear sum of those of simple objects with $\mathbb N$-coefficients $$\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal B}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathcal B)}n_i\text{FPdim}_{\mathcal B}(b_i)\quad(n_i\in\mathbb N).\label{fpdimAlinearsum}$$ It turns out that these conditions are strong enough to rule out many fusion categories. In the third step, we check which candidate fusion categories satisfy axioms of connected étale algebras. In particular, we have to check ([\[commutativealg\]](#commutativealg){reference-type="ref" reference="commutativealg"}). For that purpose, we check a necessary condition $$\mu\cdot c_{A,A}\cdot c_{A,A}=\mu\label{necessarycommutative}$$ for an algebra to be commutative. With conformal dimension $h_i$ of $b_i$, the double braiding is given by $$c_{A,A}\cdot c_{A,A}\cong\sum_{i,j=1}^n(\iota_i\otimes\iota_j)\cdot c_{a_j,a_i}\cdot c_{a_i,a_j}\cdot(p_i\otimes p_j),\label{cAAcAA}$$ where $p_i:A\to a_i$ and $\iota_i:a_i\to A$ are product projections and coproduct injections of a direct sum $A\cong a_1\oplus a_2\oplus\cdots\oplus a_n$, respectively. The double braiding $c_{a_j,a_i}\cdot c_{a_i,a_j}$ of simple objects $a_i,a_j$ can be computed from the formula[^3] $$c_{a_j,a_i}\cdot c_{a_i,a_j}=\sum_{k=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathcal B)}{N_{ij}}^k\frac{e^{2\pi ih_k}}{e^{2\pi i(h_i+h_j)}}id_k.\label{doublebraiding}$$ If one finds $A$ does not satisfy ([\[necessarycommutative\]](#necessarycommutative){reference-type="ref" reference="necessarycommutative"}), one can drop it from the list of candidates. If a candidate does pass the necessary condition, we need to check the axiom $\mu\cdot c_{A,A}=\mu$ seriously. Just like a double braiding, the half-braiding is given by $$c_{A,A}\cong\sum_{i,j=1}^n(\iota_j\otimes\iota_i)\cdot c_{a_i,a_j}\cdot(p_i\otimes p_j).\label{cAA}$$ To find $c_{a_i,a_j}$, in principle, we have to solve hexagon equations, but 'diagonal' ones $c_{b,b}$'s for self-dual simple objects $b\cong b^*$ can be computed from the formula [@K05] $$c_{b,b}\cong e^{-2\pi ih_b}\nu_2(b)id_{b\otimes b},\label{cbb}$$ where $\nu_2(b)$ is the Frobenius-Schur indicator [@FRS92].[^4]

Another fact we use to check an existence of connected étale algebra $A\in\mathcal B$ is that $\mathcal B_A$ is a left $\mathcal B$-module category [@O01]. A left $\mathcal C$-module category (or module category over $\mathcal C$) is a quadruple $(\mathcal M,\triangleright,m,l)$ of a category $\mathcal M$, an action (or module product) bifunctor $\triangleright:\mathcal C\times\mathcal M\to\mathcal M$, and natural isomorphisms $m_{-,-,-}:(-\otimes-)\triangleright-\cong-\triangleright(-\triangleright-)$ and $l:1\triangleright\mathcal M\simeq\mathcal M$ called module associativity constraint and unit constraint, respectively. They are subject to associativity and unit axioms as usual. An essential fact about module categories is that since $c\triangleright m$ is an object of $\mathcal M$, it can be decomposed to a direct sum of simple objects with $\mathbb N$-coefficients. Thus, actions of (left) $\mathcal C$-module categories form NIM-reps. If one finds no $r$-dimensional NIM-rep, one can rule out candidates $A\in\mathcal B$ giving $\mathcal B_A$'s with rank $r$. On the other hand, if one finds $r$-dimensional NIM-reps, we can use it to constrain forms of $A$'s. In order to get additional constraints, we use the internal Hom [@O01]. For $m_1,m_2\in\mathcal M$ over $\mathcal C$, the internal Hom $\underline{\text{Hom}}(m_1,m_2)\in\mathcal C$ is defined, if exists, by a natural isomorphism $$\forall c\in\mathcal C,\quad\mathcal M(c\triangleright m_1,m_2)\cong\mathcal C(c,\underline{\text{Hom}}(m_1,m_2)).\label{inthom}$$ It has a natural isomorphism $$\forall c\in\mathcal C,\forall m_1,m_2\in\mathcal M,\quad\underline{\text{Hom}}(m_1,c\triangleright m_2)\cong c\otimes\underline{\text{Hom}}(m_1,m_2).\label{inthomprop}$$ We will use this fact to translate actions $\triangleright$ of $\mathcal C$ on $\mathcal M$ to monoidal products $\otimes$ in $\mathcal C$. For every $m\in\mathcal M$, $\underline{\text{Hom}}(m,m)$ has a canonical structure of an algebra in $\mathcal C$. Furthermore, it is known [@O01; @EGNO15] that the functor $$F:=\underline{\text{Hom}}(m,-):\mathcal M\to\mathcal C_{\underline{\text{Hom}}(m,m)}\quad(m\in\mathcal M)\label{Ffunc}$$ sending $m'\in\mathcal M$ to $\underline{\text{Hom}}(m,m')\in\mathcal C$ gives $\mathcal M\simeq\mathcal C_{\underline{\text{Hom}}(m,m)}$.
