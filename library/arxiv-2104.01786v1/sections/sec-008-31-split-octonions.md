Split octonions $\tilde{{\mathbb O}}$ are similarly generated by the identity and seven imaginary octonions $$\begin{aligned}
y= y_0 + y_1 \tilde{e}^1 +\ldots + y_7 \tilde{e}^7.
\end{aligned}$$ To distinguish the split from the usual octonions we will denote them either with a letter with a tilde, or use letter $y$ (with an index if necessary) rather than $x$. The unit split octonions satisfy $$\begin{aligned}
(\tilde{e}^{5,6,7})^2=-1, \qquad (\tilde{e}^{1,2,3,4})^2=1.
\end{aligned}$$ The split octonion conjugation again changes the sign of all the imaginary units. This means that the split octonion quadratic form $$\begin{aligned}
\label{Q-split}
|y|^2_s:= y\bar{y} = (y_0)^2 - (y_1)^2 - (y_2)^2 - (y_3)^2 - (y_4)^2 + (y_5)^2 + (y_6)^2 + (y_7)^2
\end{aligned}$$ is of the split signature $(4,4)$. It will be convenient to rewrite this quadratic form in a matrix notation. We introduce a diagonal matrix $\tau = {\rm diag}(-1,1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1)$, which described a reflection in the $1,2,3,4$ plane. If we represent a split octonion by a 8-dimensional column with entries $y_0,y_1,\ldots, y_7$, the quadratic form is $$\begin{aligned}
\label{norm-split}
|y|^2 = -y^T \tau y.
\end{aligned}$$ This form of writing will be useful later.

The split octonion cross-product is again most conveniently encoded by a 3-form, which we choose to be $$\begin{aligned}
\label{C-split}
C = \tilde{e}^{567} - \tilde{e}^5\wedge (\tilde{e}^{41}-\tilde{e}^{23}) - \tilde{e}^6\wedge (\tilde{e}^{42}-\tilde{e}^{31}) - \tilde{e}^7\wedge (\tilde{e}^{43}-\tilde{e}^{12}).
\end{aligned}$$ Note that the only change as compared to ([\[C\]](#C){reference-type="ref" reference="C"}) is that the signs in front of the last 3 terms changed. This means that the cross-product in the $\tilde{e}^{5,6,7}$ plane is unchanged to the case of usual octonions. This plane, together with ${\mathbb I}$, generates a copy of the quaternions ${\mathbb H}$. The signs of all other cross-products get reversed as compared to ${\mathbb O}$.

## The Clifford algebra ${\rm Cliff}_{4,3}$

Operators $\tilde{E}^i := L_{ \tilde{e}^i}$ of left multiplication by a unit split imaginary octonion generate the Clifford algebra ${\rm Cliff}_{4,3}$ $$\begin{aligned}
{\rm Cliff}_{4,3} = {\rm Im}(\tilde{{\mathbb O}}).
\end{aligned}$$ We can represent them explicitly as the following $8\times 8$ matrices $$\begin{aligned}
\label{E-split}
& \tilde{E}^1 &= S_{12}+S_{38}-S_{47}+S_{56} \\ \nonumber
& \tilde{E}^2 &= S_{13}-S_{28}+S_{46}+S_{57} \\ \nonumber
& \tilde{E}^3 &= S_{14}+S_{27}-S_{36}+S_{58} \\ \nonumber
& \tilde{E}^4 &= S_{15}-S_{26}-S_{37}-S_{48} \\ \nonumber
& \tilde{E}^5 &= -E_{16}+E_{25}+E_{34}-E_{78} \\ \nonumber
& \tilde{E}^6 &= -E_{17}-E_{24}+E_{35}+E_{68} \\ \nonumber
& \tilde{E}^7 &= -E_{18}+E_{23}+E_{45}-E_{67} .
 
\end{aligned}$$ These satisfy the Clifford algebra relations $$\begin{aligned}
 \tilde{E}^i \tilde{E}^j + \tilde{E}^j \tilde{E}^i = 2\eta^{ij},
 
\end{aligned}$$ where $\eta={\rm diag}(1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1)$ is the metric of signature $(4,3)$. Note that we have reversed the signs here as compared to ([\[cliff-rels\]](#cliff-rels){reference-type="ref" reference="cliff-rels"}), this will be convenient later when we put ${\mathbb O}$ and $\tilde{{\mathbb O}}$ together. This extra sign is the same as the one required in ([\[signature\]](#signature){reference-type="ref" reference="signature"}). The matrices $S_{ij}$ are symmetric matrices with the identity in $i$th row and $j$th column, as well as in $j$th row and $i$th column. It is then clear that the matrices $\tilde{E}^{1,2,3,4}$ are symmetric, while $\tilde{E}^{5,6,7}$ are anti-symmetric. Note that $\tilde{E}^{5,6,7}=E^{5,6,7}$. Again we have the property that $\tilde{E}^i \tilde{{\mathbb I}} = \tilde{e}^i$, where both $\tilde{{\mathbb I}}, \tilde{e}^i$ are viewed as 8-component columns.

## Lie algebra ${\mathfrak so}(4,3)$

Forming the products of pairs of distinct Clifford generators we get the representation of a general Lie algebra in terms of $8\times 8$ matrices $$\begin{aligned}
\label{Lie-so43}
 X_{{\mathfrak so}(4,3)} = \sum_{i<j} \tilde{\omega}_{ij} \tilde{E}^i \tilde{E}^j.
 
\end{aligned}$$ These matrices no longer have a definite symmetry. Instead, the invariance of the quadratic form ([\[norm-split\]](#norm-split){reference-type="ref" reference="norm-split"}) can be written as the property $$\begin{aligned}
X_{{\mathfrak so}(4,3)}^T \tau + \tau X_{{\mathfrak so}(4,3)}=0.
\end{aligned}$$ Here $\tau$ is the matrix of the quadratic form ([\[norm-split\]](#norm-split){reference-type="ref" reference="norm-split"}).
