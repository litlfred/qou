Spin(4, 3) and the split octonions
3.1
Split octonions
Split octonions ˜O are similarly generated by the identity and seven imaginary octonions
y = y0 + y1˜e1 + . . . + y7˜e7.
(22)
To distinguish the split from the usual octonions we will denote them either with a letter with
a tilde, or use letter y (with an index if necessary) rather than x. The unit split octonions
satisfy
(˜e5,6,7)2 = −1,
(˜e1,2,3,4)2 = 1.
(23)
10
The split octonion conjugation again changes the sign of all the imaginary units. This means
that the split octonion quadratic form
|y|2
s := y¯y = (y0)2 −(y1)2 −(y2)2 −(y3)2 −(y4)2 + (y5)2 + (y6)2 + (y7)2
(24)
is of the split signature (4, 4). It will be convenient to rewrite this quadratic form in a matrix
notation. We introduce a diagonal matrix τ = diag(−1, 1, 1, 1, 1, −1, −1, −1), which described
a reﬂection in the 1, 2, 3, 4 plane. If we represent a split octonion by a 8-dimensional column
with entries y0, y1, . . . , y7, the quadratic form is
|y|2 = −yTτy.
(25)
This form of writing will be useful later.
The split octonion cross-product is again most conveniently encoded by a 3-form, which we
choose to be
C = ˜e567 −˜e5 ∧(˜e41 −˜e23) −˜e6 ∧(˜e42 −˜e31) −˜e7 ∧(˜e43 −˜e12).
(26)
Note that the only change as compared to (9) is that the signs in front of the last 3 terms
changed. This means that the cross-product in the ˜e5,6,7 plane is unchanged to the case of
usual octonions. This plane, together with I, generates a copy of the quaternions H. The signs
of all other cross-products get reversed as compared to O.
