The matrix $A$ should be seen as a $2\times 3$ rectangle of $n\times n$ sub-matrices as in Figure \ref{ABmatrices:fig}-(left), where: \begin{itemize} \item each column of $A$ corresponds to a pair of opposite edges of one tetrahedron, more precisely the columns $i, i+n,$ and $i+2n$ correspond the opposite edges of type 1,2,3 of $\Delta_i$; \item the first $n$ rows of $A$ correspond to the tetrahedra $\Delta_1,\ldots, \Delta_n$; \item the last $n$ rows of $A$ correspond to the edges $e_1,\ldots, e_n$. \end{itemize} When $i\leqslant n$, the entry $A_{ij}$ is 1 or 0 depending on whether $\Delta_i$ contains the $j$-th edge pair.
