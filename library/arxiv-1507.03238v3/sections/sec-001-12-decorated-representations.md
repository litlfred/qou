overview assuming for simplicity thatM has a single torus boundary component (the general
case and precise deﬁnitions are recalled in Section 2.2). LetG be either PSL(2, C) or SL(2, C).
A decorationof a representationρ: π1(M)→G may be thought of as a choice of a point inCP 1
stabilized byρ(π1(∂M )). In particular, a representation generically has two decorations1, but
may have only one (ifρ is boundary-unipotent), inﬁnitely many (ifρ(π1(∂M )) is trivial), or none
at all (ifρ is not boundary-Borel). Any decorated representation determines shapes satisfying
Thurston’s gluing equations.
Since CP 1 = SL(2, C)

B we shall sometimes refer to a decoration as aB-decoration. If ρ is
boundary-unipotent, a (stronger) decoration by points inSL(2, C)
/
P exists which we refer to as
a P-decoration.
In the language of decorations, Thurston’s gluing equation variety parametrizesgenerically
B-decoratedboundary-Borel PSL(2, C)-representations, where a decoration is generic if the cor-
responding solution to Thurston’s gluing equations is non-degenerate. Whether or not a repre-
sentation has a generic decoration depends on the choice of triangulation.
