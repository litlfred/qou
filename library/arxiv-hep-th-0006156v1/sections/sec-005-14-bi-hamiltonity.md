Historically, the Magri bracket brought about the notion of "raising and lowering" which soon became paramount for the whole Hamiltonian theory of soliton equation. In our case, one lifts $L_0$ to $$L_0^\uparrow=\int{\tfrac14}\:u^2dx$$ to turn the free motion ([\[cr\]](#cr){reference-type="ref" reference="cr"}) into the KdV equation $$\dot u={\tfrac12}u'''-3uu'
=\{L_0^\uparrow,u\} ,$$ then drops the Magri bracket to that of Zakharov-Faddeev's $$\{u(x),u(y)\}_\downarrow=2\delta'(x-y)$$ to restore the free motion in a different guise $$\dot u=-u'=\{L_0^\uparrow,u\}_\downarrow .$$ Although not quite relevant to the Liouville equation, this makeshift may be useful anyway, for the corresponding lower guise of the Volterra system $$\dot u_n=u_n(u_{n-1}-u_{n+1})
=\left\{\sum u_m,u_n\right\}_\downarrow$$ features a more quantization-friendly bracket $$\{u_m,u_n\}_\downarrow=u_mu_n
(\delta_{m+1,n}-\delta_{m-1,n}).$$ We happen to already know [@fadVol93] how to turn this one into a noncommutative algebra and to arrange there a "free motion in discrete time". So, in the rest of this section we shall be looking at that motion, then we shall try to guess how it could be lifted back to the Magri--Virasoro case.
