    \{u_m,u_n\}={\tfrac14}\:u_mu_n\big((4-u_m
    &-u_n)(\delta_{m+1,n}-\delta_{m-1,n})        \\
    &-u_{\frac12(m+n)}(\delta_{m+2,n} -\delta_{m-2,n}) ,
  \end{aligned}$$ with $u_n\sim1-\Delta^2u(n\Delta)$. In the traditional difference-differential scheme of things it has to do with Volterra's venerable preys and predators $$\tag{\ref{cr}\('\)}
   \dot u_n=u_n(u_{n-1}-u_{n+1})=
   \left\{\sum\log u_m,u_n\right\},$$ otherwise known as the lattice KdV equation. It should be noted however that the latter only emerges in a rather tricky continuous limit, while the simplest limit leads to the free equation ([\[cr\]](#cr){reference-type="ref" reference="cr"}). Which is a little unfortunate, for however essential a part has the Volterra system been playing in the soliton theory, there must be something wrong about a nonlinear equation emulating a linear one. Apparently, discrete space and continuous time do not get along, still, the KdV connection may teach us a thing or two.
