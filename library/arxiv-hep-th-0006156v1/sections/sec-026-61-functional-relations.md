Function ([\[ncqdl\]](#ncqdl){reference-type="ref" reference="ncqdl"}) satisfies the 'inversion' relation $$\label{inversion}
e_b(z)e_b(-z)=e^{i\pi z^2
-i\pi(1+2c_b^2)/6},$$ and a pair of functional equations $$\label{shift}
e_b(z-ib^{\pm1}/2)=(1+e^{2\pi zb^{\pm1} })
e_b(z+ib^{\pm1}/2).$$ The latter equations enable us to extend the definition of the QDL to the entire complex plane.

When $b$ is real or a pure phase, function $e_b(z)$ is *unitary* in the sense that $$\label{qdlunitarity}
\overline{e_b(z)}=1/e_b(\bar z).$$ If selfadjoint operators $\mathsf P$ and $\mathsf X$ in $L^2(\mathbb R)$ satisfy the Heisenberg commutation relations $$\label{heisen}
[\mathsf P,\mathsf X]=\frac{1}{2\pi i},$$ the following operator five term identity holds: $$\label{pent}
e_b(\mathsf P)e_b(\mathsf X)=e_b(\mathsf X)e_b(\mathsf P+\mathsf X)
e_b(\mathsf P).$$ For real $b$ this can be proved in the $C^*$-algebraic framework [^1]. We will prove it for complex $b$. The case of real $b$ then will follow by continuity.

[^1]: S.L. Woronowicz: private communication, 1998
