Appendix A. Coxeter Groups and Their Characters
A.1.
Our main reference for this appendix is the book [GP].
A Coxeter group is a group W that admits a ﬁnite presentation of the form
W = ⟨s ∈S : (st)ms,t = 1⟩,
where ms,s = 1 and ms,t ≥1 for all s, t ∈S. We say that W is irreducible iﬀit
is both nontrivial and not a product of smaller Coxeter groups. The pair (W, S) is
called a Coxeter system, and S is called its set of simple reﬂections. We say
that |S| is the rank of W.
If w ∈W, then an expression for w is a sequence (s1, . . . , sℓ) of elements of
S such that w = s1 · · · sℓ. The reduced expressions for w are those of minimal
length. The Bruhat order on W is the partial order where w′ ≤w iﬀsome reduced
expression for w′ is a subsequence of a reduced expression for w, not necessarily a
contiguous one. The common length of the reduced expressions for w is called the
Bruhat length of w and denoted |w|.
If W is ﬁnite, then there is a unique element w0 ∈W of maximal Bruhat length,
known as the longest element of (W, S) [GP, §1.5].
