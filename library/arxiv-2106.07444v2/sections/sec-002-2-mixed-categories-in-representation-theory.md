##

## 

Let $W$ be the Weyl group of $G$. Henceforth, we fix a Coxeter presentation of $W$ (see §[\[subsec:coxeter-group\]](#subsec:coxeter-group){reference-type="ref" reference="subsec:coxeter-group"}), so that $W$ is endowed with a Bruhat order. As in §[\[subsec:representation\]](#subsec:representation){reference-type="ref" reference="subsec:representation"}, we write $\hat{W}$ for the set of irreducible characters of $W$. In particular, $1 \in \hat{W}$ is the trivial character.

It will also be convenient to set $$\begin{aligned}
r &= \rk_\bb{Z} \bb{X},\\
N &= \tfrac{1}{2} |\Phi|.
\end{aligned}$$ We have $\dim G = r + 2N$.

## 

Let $\cal{B}_0$ be the flag variety of $G_0$, *i.e.*, the variety that parametrizes its Borel subgroups. Let $G_0$ act on $\cal{B}_0$ by right conjugation.

We stratify $\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0$ by its diagonal $G_0$-orbits. The poset formed by the orbits under the closure relation is isomorphic to the poset formed by $W$ under the Bruhat order. For each $w \in W$, let $$\begin{aligned}
j_w : O_{w, 0} \to \cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0
\end{aligned}$$ denote the inclusion of the corresponding orbit. We have $\dim O_w = |w| + N$, where $|w|$ is the Bruhat length of $w$. Thus the (shift-twisted) intersection complex $$\begin{aligned}
\mathit{IC}_w = j_{w, !\ast}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell\langle |w| - r - N\rangle \in \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)
\end{aligned}$$ is a simple perverse sheaf of weight zero on $[(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]$.

::: df
Let $\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0) \subseteq \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$ be the full, replete, additive subcategory generated by the objects $\mathit{IC}_w\langle n\rangle$ with $w \in W$ and $n \in \bb{Z}$.
:::

::: lem
[]{#lem:frob-invariance-bruhat label="lem:frob-invariance-bruhat"} $\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$ is Frobenius-invariant, hence $\SHom$-pure.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* [@ar Lem. 7.8] proves a Frobenius-invariance statement for any variety equipped with an affine even stratification. Their argument generalizes to the stacks in [@sun] as long as the same condition holds on the stratification.

Fix a Borel $B_0 \subseteq G_0$. The stratification of the coset space $B_0\backslash G_0$ into right $B_0$-orbits, *i.e.*, into double cosets, is affine even. We have an isomorphism $$\begin{aligned}
\simeq [(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0],
\end{aligned}$$ so the stacky version of [@ar Lem. 7.8] implies the desired result. ◻
:::

## 

The **convolution** on $\sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$ is the monoidal product $$\begin{aligned}
(-) \star (-) : \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0) \times \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0) \to \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)
\end{aligned}$$ defined by $$\begin{aligned}
K \star L = \mathit{pr}_{1, 3, \ast}(\mathit{pr}_{1, 2} \times \mathit{pr}_{2, 3})^\ast (K \boxtimes L)\langle r + N\rangle,
\end{aligned}$$ where $\mathit{pr}_{1, 2}, \mathit{pr}_{1, 3}, \mathit{pr}_{2, 3} : \cal{B}_0^3 \to \cal{B}_0^2$ are the three projection maps.

We claim that $\star$ is weight-preserving by the results of [@deligne_1980]. Indeed, $\mathit{pr}_{1, 3}$ is proper, and it can be checked that the map of stacks $[\cal{B}_0^3/G_0] \to [\cal{B}_0^2/G_0] \times [\cal{B}_0^2/G_0]$ induced by $\mathit{pr}_{1, 2} \times \mathit{pr}_{2, 3}$ is smooth of relative dimension $\dim G - \dim \cal{B} = r + N$. More strongly, we have [@by Prop. 3.2.5]:

::: lem
The convolution $\star$ restricts to a monoidal product on $\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$.
:::

::: rem
[]{#rem:by label="rem:by"} Let $\sf{C}(\cal{B} \times \cal{B})$ be the the essential image of $\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$ in $\sf{D}_G^b(\cal{B} \times \cal{B})$. Prior to [@by], it was known that the convolution on $\sf{D}_G^b(\cal{B} \times \cal{B})$ preserves the class of objects in $\sf{C}(\cal{B} \times \cal{B})$, *cf.* [@springer_1982 Prop. 2.13]. As explained in [@by Rem. 3.2.6], the force of the lemma is to assert that for all $K, L \in \sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$, the object $K \star L$ is semisimple *before* its pullback to $\cal{B} \times \cal{B}$. The proof involves developing a Frobenius-enriched version of some of the results of [@springer_1982].
:::

The convolution on $\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$ induces one on $\sf{K}^b(\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0))$. In the case where *$G$ is semisimple*, we define the **Hecke category** of $W$ to be the resulting monoidal triangulated category, and abbreviate $$\begin{aligned}
\begin{split}
\sf{H}_W
&= \sf{K}^b(\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)).
\end{split}
\end{aligned}$$ By Remark [\[rem:compatible\]](#rem:compatible){reference-type="ref" reference="rem:compatible"}, the convolution on $\sf{H}_W$ is compatible with realization functors in the sense that the diagram $$\label{eq:convolution-realization}
\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=(current bounding box.center), >=stealth]
\matrix(m)[matrix of math nodes, row sep=2em, column sep=2em, text height=2ex, text depth=0.5ex]
{   \sf{H}_W \times \sf{H}_W
        &\sf{H}_W\\
    \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0) \times \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)
        &\sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)\\
        };
\path[->,font=\scriptsize, auto]
(m-1-1)     edge node{$\star$} (m-1-2)
(m-2-1)     edge node{$\star$} (m-2-2)
(m-1-1)     edge node[left]{$\rho \times \rho$} (m-2-1)
(m-1-2)     edge node{$\rho$} (m-2-2);
\end{tikzpicture}$$ commutes.

::: rem
This definition of the Hecke category originates in work of Beilinson, Ginzburg, Soergel, and others on Koszul duality patterns in the geometry of mixed complexes. See [@bgs §4] and the references therein.
:::

##  {#subsec:decat}

Let $H_W$ be the Iwahori--Hecke algebra of $W$ (see §[\[subsec:hecke\]](#subsec:hecke){reference-type="ref" reference="subsec:hecke"}). Below, we review how $\sf{H}_W$ decategorifies to $H_W$.

If $\sf{A}$ is an additive category, then we write $[\sf{A}]_\oplus$ for its *split* Grothendieck group. The elements of $[\sf{A}]_\oplus$ are the isomorphism classes $[K]$ of the objects $K$, and the relations are $[K \oplus L] = [K] + [L]$ for all objects $K, L$. If $\sf{A}$ is given a monoidal product $\star$, then $[\sf{A}]_\oplus$ forms a ring under the multiplication $[K] \cdot [L] = [K \star L]$.

We endow $[\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)]_\oplus$ with the action of $\bb{Z}[\bm{q}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]$ in which $\bm{q}^{\frac{1}{2}}$ acts by $\langle -1\rangle$. For each $K \in \sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$, let $$\begin{aligned}
\chi_w(K) = \sum_{i, j}
{(-1)^{i + j}} \bm{q}^{\frac{j}{2}} \dim \gr_j^{\bm{w}} \ur{H}^i(j_w^\ast K),
\end{aligned}$$ the virtual weight polynomial of the stalk of $K$ over $[O_{w, 0}/G]$. Thm. 2.8 of [@springer_1982] implies if $G$ is *semisimple*, then the map $$\begin{aligned}
\begin{array}{rcl}
{[\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)]_\oplus} &\to &H_W\\
{[K]} &\mapsto &\displaystyle\sum_{w \in W} \bm{q}^{\frac{|w|}{2}} \sigma_w \chi_w(K)
\end{array}
\end{aligned}$$ is a morphism of $\bb{Z}[\bm{q}^{\pm \frac{1}{2}}]$-algebras. In fact, it is an isomorphism, and if $\gamma_w$ is the image of $[\mathit{IC}_w]$, then the set $\{\gamma_w\}_{w \in W}$ forms a free $\bb{Z}[\bm{q}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]$-linear basis of $H_W$, *cf.* [@ww_2011 407]. The element $\gamma_w$ is called the **Kazhdan--Lusztig element** attached to $w$.

If $\sf{T}$ is a triangulated (additive) category, then we write $[\sf{T}]_\triangle$ for its *triangulated* Grothendieck group. The elements of $[\sf{T}]_\triangle$ are the isomorphism classes of the objects of $\sf{T}$, and the relations are $[\cal{K}] = [\cal{K}'] + [\cal{K}'']$ for each exact triangle $\cal{K}' \to \cal{K} \to \cal{K}'' \to \cal{K}'[1]$ in $\sf{T}$.

If $\sf{A}$ is any additive category, then there is an isomorphism $[\sf{K}^b(\sf{A})]_\triangle \xrightarrow{\sim} [\sf{A}]_\oplus$ that sends any complex to the alternating sum of its terms [@rose]. In particular, $$\begin{aligned}
\begin{split}
[\sf{H}_W]_\triangle 
&\simeq [\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)]_\oplus.
\end{split}
\end{aligned}$$ Since the convolutions on these categories are compatible with each other, this is an isomorphism of rings, not just groups. We endow $[\sf{H}_W]_\triangle$ with the action of $\bb{Z}[\bm{q}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]$ in which $\bm{q}^{\frac{1}{2}}$ acts by $\langle -1\rangle$. Then we obtain a $\bb{Z}[\bm{q}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]$-algebra isomorphism $[\sf{H}_W]_\triangle \xrightarrow{\sim} H_W$ that again sends $[\mathit{IC}_w] \mapsto \gamma_w$.

::: rem
[]{#rem:iwahori label="rem:iwahori"} Recall that $q^{\frac{1}{2}} \in \bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell^\times$ is a fixed square root of $q = |\bb{F}|$. In [@iwahori], Iwahori gave an isomorphism of $\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$-algebras: $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:iwahori}
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell\otimes H_W|_{\bm{q}^{1/2} = q^{1/2}} \simeq \End_{G^F}(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell[\cal{B}^F]).
\end{aligned}$$ The isomorphism $H_W \simeq [\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)]_\oplus$ is an integral version in the following sense. We have a commutative diagram $$\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=(current bounding box.center), >=stealth]
\matrix(m)[matrix of math nodes, row sep=2em, column sep=2.5em, text height=2ex, text depth=0.5ex]
{   {[\sf{C}(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)]_\oplus}
        &H_W\\
    \bb{Z}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}][\cal{B}^F \times \cal{B}^F]^{G^F}
        &H_W|_{\bm{q}^{1/2} = q^{1/2}}\\
        };
\path[->,font=\scriptsize, auto]
(m-1-1)     edge node{$\sim$}(m-1-2)
(m-1-1)     edge (m-2-1)
(m-1-2)     edge (m-2-2)
(m-2-1)     edge node{$\sim$} (m-2-2);
\end{tikzpicture}$$ where the left-hand arrow is induced by the function-sheaf dictionary. Iwahori's isomorphism can be factored as $$\begin{aligned}
\End_{G^F}(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell[\cal{B}^F]) \xrightarrow{\sim} \bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell[\cal{B}^F \times \cal{B}^F]^{G^F} \xrightarrow{\sim} \bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell\otimes H_W|_{\bm{q}^{1/2} = q^{1/2}},
\end{aligned}$$ where the second arrow is induced by the bottom arrow of the diagram.
:::

##  {#section-4}

Let $G_0$ act on itself by right conjugation. There is a $G_0$-stable stratification of $G_0$ by locally-closed subvarieties [@lusztig_1984_intersection §3.1] [@lusztig_1985_1 §3.11], now known as Jordan classes. Let $$\begin{aligned}
\mathit{act}: G_0 \times \cal{B}_0 &\to \cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0,\\
\mathit{pr}: G_0 \times \cal{B}_0 &\to G_0
\end{aligned}$$ be defined by $$\begin{aligned}
\mathit{act}(g, B) &= (g^{-1}Bg, B),\\
\mathit{pr}(g, B) &= g.
\end{aligned}$$ The maps above are $G_0$-equivariant with respect to the diagonal $G_0$-actions on $G_0 \times \cal{B}_0$ and $\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0$. Moreover, we can stratify $G_0 \times \cal{B}_0$ so that $\mathit{act}$, *resp.* $\mathit{pr}$, is stratification-preserving with respect to the Bruhat stratification of $\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0$, *resp.* the Jordan stratification of $G_0$.

Following [@lusztig_1985_1], we define the **character functor** $$\begin{aligned}
\sf{CH} : \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0) \to \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(G_0)
\end{aligned}$$ to be the composition $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:horocycle-transform}\begin{split}
\sf{CH} = \bigoplus_i {}^p\cal{H}^i[-i] \circ \mathit{pr}_\ast\, \mathit{act}^!\langle r + N\rangle.
\end{split}
\end{aligned}$$ Since $\mathit{act}$ is smooth of relative dimension $r + N$ and $\mathit{pr}$ is proper, $\sf{CH}$ preserves the class of pure complexes of weight zero [@deligne_1980]. We define a **mixed unipotent character sheaf** to be a simple perverse sheaf $E \in \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(G_0)$ such that $E\langle -i\rangle$ is a subquotient of $\sf{CH}(\mathit{IC}_w)$, *resp.* $\xi\sf{CH}(\mathit{IC}_w)$, for some $w \in W$ and $i \in \bb{Z}$. This means $E$ is also pure of weight zero.

Recall that by [@bbd Prop. 5.3.9], every indecomposable pure perverse sheaf takes the form $E \otimes M$, where $E$ is a simple perverse sheaf and $M$ is the pullback of a sheaf on $\mathit{pt}_0$ that corresponds to a $\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell[F]$-module on which $F$ acts by a single unipotent Jordan block. Thus, a simple perverse sheaf $E \in \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(G_0)$ is a mixed unipotent character sheaf if and only if $E\langle -i\rangle \otimes M$ occurs as a direct summand of $\sf{CH}(\mathit{IC}_w)$ for some $w, i, M$ as above.

::: df
Let $\sf{C}(G_0) \subseteq \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(G_0)$ be the full, replete, additive subcategory generated by the objects $E \otimes M$, where $E$ is a mixed unipotent character sheaf and $M$ is the pullback of a pure object of $\sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\mathit{pt}_0)$ of weight zero.
:::

::: rem
The objects $\xi \sf{CH}(\mathit{IC}_w) \in \sf{D}_G^b(G)$ coincide with the objects $\bar{K}_w^\cal{L}$ studied by Lusztig in [@lusztig_1985_3] when $\cal{L}$ is the trivial local system on a fixed maximal torus of $G$. They also coincide with the objects $\bb{K}_w$ studied by Webster--Williamson in [@ww_2011] up to a shift-twist.
:::

##  {#section-5}

The character functor is a monoidal trace on $\sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)$ in the following sense. Abbreviate $$\begin{aligned}
\sf{CH}(- \star -) \vcentcolon= \sf{CH} \circ ((-) \star (-)) : \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)^2 \to \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(G_0).
\end{aligned}$$ The result below can be deduced from a paper of Lusztig [@lusztig_2015], identifying the monoidal center of $\sf{D}_G^b(\cal{B} \times \cal{B})$ with a certain category of unmixed unipotent character sheaves, but we give a self-contained proof.

::: lem
[]{#lem:monoidal-trace label="lem:monoidal-trace"} We have ${\sf{CH}(- \star -) \circ (\mathit{pr}_2 \times \mathit{pr}_1)^\ast}\, \simeq \,{\sf{CH}(- \star -)}$, where $\mathit{pr}_2 \times \mathit{pr}_1$ is the involution of $[(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]^2$ that swaps the factors.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Observe that $\sf{CH}(- \star -)$ is induced by pullback and pushforward along the solid arrows in the diagram below: $$\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=(current bounding box.center), >=stealth]
\matrix(m)[matrix of math nodes, row sep=2.5em, column sep=2.5em, text height=2ex, text depth=0.5ex]
{   {[(G_0 \times \cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]}
        &{[(G_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]}
        &{[G_0/G_0]}\\
    {[(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]}
        &{[(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]}
        &\\
    {[(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]^2}\\
        };
\path[->,font=\scriptsize, auto]
(m-1-2)     edge node[above]{$\mathit{pr}$}( m-1-3)
            edge node[right]{$\mathit{act}$} (m-2-2)
(m-2-1)     edge node[below]{$\mathit{pr}_{1,3}$} (m-2-2)
            edge node[left]{$\mathit{pr}_{1,2} \times \mathit{pr}_{2,3}$} (m-3-1);
\path[->,font=\scriptsize, densely dotted]
(m-1-1)     edge (m-1-2)
            edge (m-2-1);
\end{tikzpicture}$$ Let the square in the diagram be cartesian. By proper base change across that square, we see that $$\begin{aligned}
\sf{CH}(- \star -) = \bigoplus_i {}^p\cal{H}^i[-i] \circ \Psi_\ast \circ \Phi^\ast \langle 2(r + N)\rangle,
\end{aligned}$$ where the functors $$\begin{aligned}
\Phi : [(G_0 \times \cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0] &\to [(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]^2,\\
\Psi : [(G_0 \times \cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0] &\to [G_0/G_0]
\end{aligned}$$ are defined by $$\begin{aligned}
\Phi([g, B, B']) &= ([g^{-1}B'g, B], [B, B']),\\
\Psi([g, B, B']) &= [g].
\end{aligned}$$ Now consider the diagram $$\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=(current bounding box.center), >=stealth]
\matrix(m)[matrix of math nodes, row sep=1em, column sep=2.5em, text height=2ex, text depth=0.5ex]
{   {[(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]^2}
        &{[(G_0 \times \cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]}
        &\\
    {}
        &
        &{[G_0/G_0]}\\
    {[(\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]^2}
        &{[(G_0 \times \cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0)/G_0]}
        &\\
        };
\path[->,font=\scriptsize, auto]
(m-1-2) edge node[above]{$\Phi$} (m-1-1)
(m-1-2.south east)   edge node[above]{$\Psi$} (m-2-3)
(m-3-2) edge node[above]{$\Phi$} (m-3-1)
(m-3-2.north east)   edge node[below]{$\Psi$} (m-2-3)
(m-1-1) edge node[left]{$\mathit{pr}_2 \times \mathit{pr}_1$} (m-3-1)
(m-1-2) edge node{$\Xi$} (m-3-2);
\end{tikzpicture}$$ in which $\Xi([g, B, B']) = [g, g^{-1}B'g, B]$. We can check that the square on the left and the triangle on the right commute. (The former is where it is essential to use orbits, not orbit representatives.) Thus the whole diagram commutes, giving us the desired equivalence of functors. ◻
:::

We expect that the functor $\sf{CH}$ categorifies the map from the Iwahori--Hecke algebra to its cocenter, by way of the function-sheaf dictionary (see Remark [\[rem:iwahori\]](#rem:iwahori){reference-type="ref" reference="rem:iwahori"}).

##  {#section-6}

Let $\tilde{G}_0$ be defined by the cartesian diagram: $$\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=(current bounding box.center), >=stealth]
\matrix(m)[matrix of math nodes, row sep=2em, column sep=3em, text height=2.5ex, text depth=0.5ex]
{   \tilde{G}_0
        &G_0 \times \cal{B}_0\\
    O_{1, 0} = \cal{B}_0
        &\cal{B}_0 \times \cal{B}_0\\
        };
\path[->,font=\scriptsize, auto]
(m-1-1)     edge (m-1-2)
(m-1-1)     edge (m-2-1)
(m-1-2)     edge node{$\mathit{act}$} (m-2-2)
(m-2-1)     edge node{$j_1$} (m-2-2);
\end{tikzpicture}$$ The composition $p' : \tilde{G}_0 \to G_0 \times \cal{B}_0 \to G_0$ is a small, hence proper, map called the **Grothendieck--Springer map**. The **Grothendieck--Springer sheaf** is $$\begin{aligned}
\cal{G} = p'_\ast\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell.
\end{aligned}$$ By base change and smallness, $\cal{G} \simeq \sf{CH}(\mathit{IC}_1)$.

The Grothendieck--Springer map restricts to a Galois $W$-cover over the regular semisimple locus of $G$. Therefore, the decomposition of $\cal{G}$ into indecomposable perverse sheaves takes the form $$\begin{aligned}
\cal{G} \simeq \bigoplus_{\phi \in \hat{W}} \cal{G}_\phi \otimes V_\phi,
\end{aligned}$$ where each $\cal{G}_\phi$ is a simple perverse sheaf and $V_\phi$ is the irreducible representation of $W$ over $\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$ of character $\phi$. We pin down $\cal{G}_\phi$ up to unique isomorphism by declaring that Frobenius acts trivially on $V_\phi$.

::: df
Let $\sf{C}_{\hat{W}}(G_0) \subseteq \sf{C}(G_0)$ be the full, replete, additive subcategory generated by the objects $\cal{G}_\phi \otimes M$, where $\phi \in \hat{W}$ and $M$ is the pullback of a pure object of $\sf{D}_G^b(\mathit{pt}_0)$ of weight zero.
:::

::: rem
The work in Section [\[sec:steinberg\]](#sec:steinberg){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:steinberg"} will show that $\sf{C}_{\hat{W}}(G_0)$ and thus $\sf{C}(G_0)$ are not realizable, let alone $\SHom$-pure.
:::

::: rem
If $G$ is of type $A$, meaning $W = S_n$ for some $n$, then every (unmixed) unipotent character sheaf on $G$ is isomorphic to a summand of $\xi\cal{G}$ (*e.g.*, by Lusztig's classification of character sheaves). So in this case, we have $\sf{C}_{\hat{W}}(G_0) \simeq \sf{C}(G_0)$. In other types, a unipotent character sheaf that does not occur as a summand of $\xi \cal{G}$ is said to be **cuspidal**.
:::

##  {#section-7}

Let $i : \cal{U}_0 \to G_0$ be the inclusion of the unipotent locus. The action of $G_0$ on itself by conjugation restricts to an action on $\cal{U}_0$. We stratify $\cal{U}_0$ by its $G_0$-orbits. Every orbit is a Jordan class of $G_0$, so the inclusion $i : \cal{U}_0 \to G_0$ is stratification-preserving. We have $\dim \cal{U} = 2N$, from which $\dim \cal{U} - \dim G = -r$.

::: df
[]{#df:unipotent label="df:unipotent"} Let $\sf{C}(\cal{U}_0) \subseteq \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\cal{U}_0)$ be the full, replete, additive subcategory generated by the objects $i^\ast E \otimes M$, where $E$ is a mixed unipotent character sheaf and $M$ is the pullback of a pure object of $\sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\mathit{pt}_0)$ of weight zero.
:::

Since the map $i$ is merely a closed immersion, there is a priori no reason that $i^\ast$ sends unipotent character sheaves to well-behaved objects. Nevertheless:

::: thm
[]{#thm:lusztig-restriction label="thm:lusztig-restriction"} If $E$ is a mixed unipotent character sheaf, then there exist a $G_0$-orbit $j_C : C_0 \to \cal{U}_0$ and a pure $\ell$-adic local system $\cal{L} \in \sf{D}_{G, m}^b(C_0)$ of weight zero such that $i^\ast E \simeq j_{C, !\ast} \cal{L}\langle n_C\rangle$ for some $n_C \in \bb{Z}$ that only depends on $C$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The unmixed version of this statement is [@lusztig_1984_intersection Thm. 6.5(c)]. Below, we explain how to deduce our mixed version. The notation $\mathit{IC}(-, -)$ will mean the intersection complex of a local system (*not* necessarily shifted to be perverse).

First, $\xi E$ is an object of $\sf{D}_G^b(G)$ that, in Lusztig's notation, takes the form $\phi_!K[\dim \bar{Y}]$, where $\phi$ is a map $X \to \bar{Y} \subseteq G$ and $K = \mathit{IC}(X, \bar{\cal{E}}_1)$ for some local system $\bar{\cal{E}}_1$ over $X$ [@lusztig_1984_intersection §4]. As long as we start with $\bb{F}$-structures on $X, Y, \phi, \bar{\cal{E}}_1$ such that $\bar{\cal{E}}_1$ is pure of weight zero, the construction descends to $G_0$ to yield $E = \phi_!K\langle \dim \bar{Y}\rangle$. In the notation of [@lusztig_1984_intersection §6.6], we have $i^\ast K \simeq K^a$, where $K^a = \mathit{IC}(X_0^a, \bar{\cal{E}}_1^a)$ and $\bar{\cal{E}}_1^a = i^\ast \bar{\cal{E}}_1$ (and the subscript $0$ means the $\bb{F}$-structure on $X^a$ coming from the $\bb{F}$-structure we chose on $X$). Therefore, $i^\ast E \simeq \phi_!^a K^a \langle \dim \bar{Y}\rangle$. Since $\phi^a$ is proper, $\bar{\cal{E}}_1^a$ being pure implies $\phi_!^a K^a$ being pure of the same weight [@deligne_1980], *cf.* (6.6.1) of [@lusztig_1984_intersection].

In our situation, $E$ is a unipotent character sheaf, which means that $\bar{\cal{E}}_1$ is a constant sheaf [@lusztig_1985_1 §3.1, §4.4]. Therefore, $\bar{\cal{E}}_1^a$ is the constant sheaf on $X_0^a$, hence pure of weight zero. Therefore $i^\ast E$ is pure of weight zero. Now, the rest of [@lusztig_1984_intersection Thm. 6.5(c)] implies that at the unmixed level, $i^\ast E$ is a shift of some intersection complex $j_{C, !\ast}\cal{E}$. Since $\cal{E}$ is the shifted restriction of $i^\ast E$ to $C$, it must be pure. So up to twisting $\cal{E}$, we can find a pure local system $\cal{L}$ of weight zero such that $i^\ast E \simeq j_{C, !\ast} \cal{L}\langle n\rangle$ for some $n$. Finally, [@lusztig_1984_intersection Thm. 6.5(c)] also implies that $n$ only depends on $\dim \bar{Y}^a$ and $\dim C$, and the former is determined by a variety $C_1$ that only depends on $C$. ◻
:::

##  {#section-8}

Let $p : \tilde{\cal{U}}_0 \to \cal{U}_0$ be defined by the cartesian diagram: $$\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=(current bounding box.center), >=stealth]
\matrix(m)[matrix of math nodes, row sep=2em, column sep=3em, text height=2.5ex, text depth=0.5ex]
{   \tilde{\cal{U}}_0
        &\tilde{G}_0\\
    \cal{U}_0
        &G_0\\
        };
\path[->,font=\scriptsize, auto]
(m-1-1)     edge (m-1-2)
(m-1-1)     edge node[left]{$p$} (m-2-1)
(m-1-2)     edge node{$p'$} (m-2-2)
(m-2-1)     edge node{$i$} (m-2-2);
\end{tikzpicture}$$ Then $p$ (or rather, $p^\red : \tilde{\cal{U}}_0^\red \to \cal{U}_0$) is a semismall resolution of singularities called the **Springer resolution**. The **Springer sheaf** is $$\begin{aligned}
\cal{S} = p_\ast \bar{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell\langle -r\rangle.
\end{aligned}$$ By proper base change, $\cal{S} = i^\ast \cal{G}\langle -r\rangle$. Setting $\cal{S}_\phi = i^\ast \cal{G}_\phi \langle -r\rangle$, we have that $$\begin{aligned}
\cal{S} \simeq \bigoplus_{\phi \in \hat{W}} \cal{S}_\phi \otimes V_\phi
\end{aligned}$$ is the decomposition of $\cal{S}$ into indecomposable perverse sheaves.

::: df
Let $\sf{C}_{\hat{W}}(\cal{U}_0) \subseteq \sf{C}(\cal{U}_0)$ be the full, replete, additive subcategory generated by the objects $\cal{S}_\phi \otimes M$, where $\phi \in \hat{W}$ and $M$ is the pullback of a pure object of $\sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\mathit{pt}_0)$ of weight zero.
:::

::: lem
[]{#lem:unipotent label="lem:unipotent"} $\sf{C}(\cal{U}_0)$ and thus $\sf{C}_{\hat{W}}(\cal{U}_0)$ are $\SHom$-pure.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* $\sf{C}(\cal{U}_0)$ is closed under $\langle n\rangle$ for all $n$ because the same is true of the class of pure objects of weight zero in $\sf{D}_{G, m}^b(\mathit{pt}_0)$. It remains to check that $\SHom^0(i^\ast E, i^\ast E')$ is concentrated in weight zero for all mixed unipotent character sheaves $E, E'$.

In the notation of Theorem [\[thm:lusztig-restriction\]](#thm:lusztig-restriction){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:lusztig-restriction"}, we can write $i^\ast E \simeq j_{C, !\ast} \cal{L}\langle n_C\rangle$ and $i^\ast E' \simeq j_{C', !\ast} \cal{L}'\langle n_{C'}\rangle$ for some $C, C', \cal{L}, \cal{L}', n_C, n_{C'}$. It remains to show that $$\begin{aligned}
\begin{split}
&\SHom^0(j_{C, !\ast} \cal{L}\langle n_C\rangle, j_{C', !\ast} \cal{L}'\langle n_{C'}\rangle) \\
&= \SHom^{d}(j_{C, !\ast} \cal{L}\langle \dim C\rangle, j_{C', !\ast} \cal{L}'\langle \dim C'\rangle)(\tfrac{d}{2})
\end{split}
\end{aligned}$$ is concentrated in weight zero, where $d = (n_{C'} - \dim C') - (n_C - \dim C)$. Note that $j_{C, !\ast} \cal{L}\langle \dim C\rangle$ and $j_{C', !\ast} \cal{L}'\langle \dim C'\rangle$ are both pure perverse of weight zero. So the desired claim follows from [@rr Cor. 4.8]. ◻
:::
