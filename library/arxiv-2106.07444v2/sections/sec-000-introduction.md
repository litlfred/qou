Introduction
0.1. Summary. Let V be a ﬁnite-dimensional vector space over a subﬁeld of R,
and let W be a ﬁnite group of automorphisms of V generated by reﬂections. Cox-
eter showed that W admits a remarkable presentation in terms of generators and
relations [ Co]. Via this presentation, the group ring of W can be deformed to a
Z[q± 1
2 ]-algebra known as the Iwahori–Hecke algebra HW . One can further deﬁne
an inﬁnite group BrW , generalizing the braid groups studied in geometric topolo gy,
such that W and HW are respective quotients of BrW and Z[q± 1
2 ][BrW ].
If W = Sn, the symmetric group on n letters, then BrW = Brn, the classical
braid group on n strands. Any braid β can be closed up, end-to-end, to yield a
topological link ˆβ in 3-dimensional space. If β,β ′ ∈ Brn are conjugate, then ˆβ, ˆβ′
are isotopic. Ocneanu, building on work of Jones, used this o bservation to construct
an isotopy invariant of links called the HOMFLY series out of class functions on
the groups Brn. These functions, in turn, factor through functions called Markov
traces on the algebras HSn [HOMFLY, J]. Y. Gomi discovered that the latter admit
a uniform generalization to any ﬁnite Coxeter group W [G].
It is now known that HW is the (triangulated) Grothendieck ring of a monoidal
triangulated category that we will call the Hecke category HW . Gomi’s Markov
trace is categoriﬁed by an additive functor
HHH : HW → Vect3,
where Vect3 denotes the category of triply-graded vector spaces. As W runs over
the symmetric groups, these functors can be used to construc t a link invariant
valued in Vect3, originally constructed by Khovanov–Rozansky [ KR, Kh]. More
precisely, there is a map from braids β ∈ BrW to objects R(β) ∈ HW , due to
Rouquier [ R06], and HHH(R(β)) can be used to construct an isotopy invariant of
ˆβ that decategoriﬁes to its HOMFLY series.
The most general deﬁnition of HW is purely algebraic: the homotopy category of
Soergel bimodules over Sym( V) [EW14, EW16]. However, in representation theory,
we are especially interested in the case where V is the span of a root lattice and
W is the Weyl group of the corresponding semisimple algebraic group G over an
algebraically closed ﬁeld, say, ¯F for a ﬁnite ﬁeld F. Here, the Hecke category can
be deﬁned in terms of the geometry of G, or rather, its split form G0 over F. We
are led to hope that HHH(R(β)) might be expressed in terms of the (co)homology
of an explicit variety over F attached to β.
In the series of papers [ WW08, WW11, WW17], Webster–Williamson relate
HHH to the weight grading on the G-equivariant cohomology of certain varieties
with maps to G. The most inexplicit part of their work is a procedure they ca ll
“chromatography,” which turns an object of the mixed derived category Db
G,m(G0) =

HHH(R(β)) to a combination of several varieties, rather than to a sin gle one. These
varieties encode the term-by-term structure of R(β), when R(β) is viewed as a
complex of Soergel bimodules.

0.3.1. The appearance of the ring AW suggests that AH is closely related to a
functor on HW called the horizontal trace, which [ QRS, GW, GHW] studied in the

additive functor under which K⋆ L and L⋆ K become isomorphic for all objects
K, L ∈ HW .

irreducible summand.)
