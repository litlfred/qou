We study the question whether functions of negatively dependent random variables satisfy concentration bounds, similar to functions of independent random variables. Many tools are known for functions of independent random variables --- e.g., martingales, Talagrand's inequality, the Kim-Vu inequality, and the entropy method for self-bounding functions. It is also known that Chernoff-Hoeffding bounds for (linear functions of) independent random variables generalize to negatively dependent variables, assuming a relatively weak property known as negative cylinder dependence. A natural question therefore arises, whether more sophisticated concentration bounds also generalize to some form of negative dependence. In this paper, we discuss the question of concentration bounds for Lipschitz functions[^1] on $\{0,1\}^n$, under certain forms of negative dependence.

[^1]: A function $f:\{0,1\}^n \rightarrow {\mathbb R}$ is $c$-Lipschitz, if each variable can affect the value additively by at most $c$.
