The Kauffman state-sum for the Jones polynomial comes directly from this formalism for TLy.
One of the goals of this work, only partially realized, is to find an analogous formalism for the
Alexander polynomial.

Alas, the story for the Alexander polynomial is not so simple. The Super Temperley-Lieb
algebra ST Lag is defined as the intertwiner algebra for the quantum group associated to glI(1|1).
Hence, the Markov trace on Ha giving the Alexander polynomial on braids factors through ST Lg.
This algebra has Wedderburn components indexed by hook partitions. However, the ideal that
consists of components that are not hooks is not cellular in the dominance order on partitions, and
so STLq does not inherit the cellular structure from Hg and the map Hg -» ST La is quite subtle.
We can obtain a basis for STL, but it has no clear connection to the standard basis for Hg, and so
it is not obvious how to calculate the Markov trace directly. In fact, because the transition matrix
from the standard basis for Hg to our basis for ST Lg is only defined upon extending scalars to Q(v),
it is no longer clear that the Alexander invariant is a Laurent polynomial in Z[v,v7+]. The main
result in this section is a presentation of the relations for STLq using some of the combinatorics
of the symmetric group adapted to Ha.

This is why in Chapter IV we start again using Young’s seminormal form for Hy. To
begin, STLq is presented as a diagram algebra in the basis of matrix units of its Wedderburn
components. As a result, a lot of complicated information is packaged in the idempotents in Hg
that project onto STLq. Using character formulas of Geck and Jacon ([16], [17]} for the irreducible
representations of Hq and building upon the work of Jones ((/24]), a combinatorial procedure is
deduced for calculating the trace, and hence the Alexander polynomial, from a braid diagram
directly. This state-sum has the appeal of being computable in terms of certain labeled diagrams
(the basis for STLyz) that come from resolving crossings in the original braid. The trade-off for
changing basis so drastically is the aforementioned difficulty that the formula gives the Alexander
polynomial as a sum of rational functions involving quantum integers, obscuring the fact that the
invariant is actually a Laurent polynomial.
