As observed in Section 2, the improvement of Theorem [\[teo1\]](#teo1){reference-type="ref" reference="teo1"} respect to the old Penrose-Ruelle bound is manifestly evident, as far as general stable and tempered pair potentials are concerned. There have been however recent works [@MPS; @dLP; @dLPY] which also obtain improvements on the convergence radius of the Mayer series for systems of particles interacting via a restricted class of stable and tempered pair potentials. In particular, in a recent paper [@dLPY], which is a development of an early work by Basuev [@Ba2], the authors obtain new bounds for the Mayer series convergence radius which are better than any precedent bound given in the literature (see in [@dLPY] the new bounds of Theorem 5, formula (4.5)), as far as the particles in the system interact via a so-called Basuev potential (see Definition 3 in [@dLPY]). This is a class of potentials which includes the classical case of the Lennard-Jones type.

\
It is worth to say that, at least for the particular case of the Lennard-Jones potential, the optimal bounds presented in [@dLPY] (obtained also using a recent result by one of us [@Y]) are slightly better than those obtained here.

\
Indeed, as far as the Lennard-Jones potential $V(r)={1\over r^{12}}-{2\over r^{6}}$ is concerned, the calculations given in Sec 5.3 of [@dLPY] show that, at $\b=1$ the lower bound for the convergence radius of the Mayer series of the Lennard-Jones gas is surely greater than $${e^{-({B_{_{\rm LJ}}}+1)}\over
7.4 }$$ where $B_{LJ}$ is the Lennard Jones stability constant. The same calculation using instead the bounds of Theorem [\[teo1\]](#teo1){reference-type="ref" reference="teo1"} yields for the same convergence radius a lower bound surely smaller than $${e^{-({B_{_{\rm LJ}}}+1)}\over
8.08}$$ The results of this paper, as well as those given in [@Ba2; @dLP; @MP; @dLPY], show that direct methods based on tree-graph identities are, in the end, much more effective than the old indirect methods of [@Pe63; @Ru63] based on K-S equations. On the other hand, the fact that the bound obtained in [@dLPY] beats our bound of Theorem [\[teo1\]](#teo1){reference-type="ref" reference="teo1"} for some particular cases of stable and tempered pair potentials raises the question of whether it is possible to further improve the bounds of Theorem [\[teo1\]](#teo1){reference-type="ref" reference="teo1"} in the general case.
