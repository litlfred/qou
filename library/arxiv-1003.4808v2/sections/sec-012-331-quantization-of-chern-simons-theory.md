Consider Chern-Simons theory with gauge group $G$ --- either compact or complex --- on a knot complement $M = \widetilde{M}\backslash K$, with $\partial M = T^2$. The phase space ${\cal M}$ associated to $T^2$ is simply the space of flat $G$-connections on $T^2$, modulo gauge equivalence. Since a flat connection is completely determined by the conjugacy classes of its holonomies, we have

$$\begin{aligned}
 {\cal M}&= {\cal M}_{{\rm flat}} (G;T^2) \\
 &= \left\{ \text{representations :}\;\;\pi_1(T^2)\to G \right\}/\text{conjugation}\,.
\end{aligned}$$

The fundamental group $\pi_1(T^2)\simeq {\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}$ is abelian, generated by the meridian and longitude of the torus. The holonomies along these loops can therefore be simultaneously diagonalized [^1]

into the maximal torus $\mathbf{T}\subset G$. Coordinates on ${\cal M}$ are then given by the $2r$ independent eigenvalues $(m_1,...,m_r)$ and $(\ell_1,...,\ell_r)$ of the meridian and longitude holonomies, where $r$ is the rank of $G$. We must also divide by the Weyl group ${\cal W}$ of $G$, which simultaneously permutes both sets of eigenvalues, to obtain

$${\cal M}\simeq (\mathbf{T}^r\times \mathbf{T}^r)/{\cal W}= \mathbf{T}^{2r}/{\cal W}\,.$$

For example, for a compact group $G = SU(n)$ the phase space is ${\cal M}= (S^1)^{2(n-1)}/S_n$, where $S_n$ is the symmetric group on $n$ elements. Similarly, for its complexification $G_{\mathbb{C}}= SL(n,{\mathbb{C}})$, the phase space is ${\cal M}= ({\mathbb{C}}^*)^{2(n-1)}/S_n$. In general, ignoring subtleties in high codimension that are not pertinent to quantization, the relation between compact and complex phase spaces can be described as

$${\cal M}_{{\rm flat}} (G_{\mathbb{C}};\Sigma) = \left[{\cal M}_{{\rm flat}} (G;\Sigma)\right]_{\mathbb{C}}\simeq T^*{\cal M}_{{\rm flat}} (G;\Sigma)\,.$$

(In particular, the last relation is only a birational equivalence.)

### Compact theory

In order to quantize ${\cal M}$, we need a symplectic structure. In *compact* Chern-Simons theory, it is given by

$$\omega = \frac14 \int_{T^2} {\rm Tr}\big[\delta{\cal A}\wedge \delta{\cal A}\big]\,. \label{w0}$$

This can be expressed more concretely in coordinates $\{m_i,\ell_i\}=\{e^{u_i},e^{v_i}\}$ as

$$\omega = \sum_i d\log m_i\wedge d\log \ell_i = \sum_idu_i\wedge dv_i\,.$$

The holonomy variables $u_i$ and $v_i$ function as "positions" and "momenta," respectively. Now, the parameter $\hbar=i\pi/k$ that appeared naturally in the discussion of the volume conjecture in Section [\[sec:gen\]](#sec:gen){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:gen"} is rescaled from the standard geometric quantization parameter $\hbar$ of Section [\[sec:quant\]](#sec:quant){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:quant"} by a factor of $i$. In terms of $k$, the quantization condition [\[wquant\]](#wquant){reference-type="eqref" reference="wquant"} simply takes the form $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}$. The integer $k$ is identified as the Chern-Simons level, modulo the shift mentioned in Footnote [\[foot:k\]](#foot:k){reference-type="ref" reference="foot:k"}.

The last ingredient we need to describe the Hilbert space ${\cal H}$ is a choice of polarization. For clarity, let us take $G=SU(2)$ to be of rank one, and let us choose the polarization $\partial/\partial v = 0$, so that ${\cal H}_{T^2}$ essentially consists of periodic and Weyl-invariant functions of $u$, $f(u) = f(u+2\pi i) = f(-u)$. Being somewhat more careful, and thinking of these not as functions but as sections of the line bundle with curvature $\frac{k}{\pi}\omega$, one finds that the simultaneous periodicity in the momentum $v$ and the position $u$ restricts $u$ to take values in $\frac{i\pi}{k}{\mathbb{Z}}$. Therefore, a function $f(u)$ only takes nonzero values at $k+1$ distinct points $u=0,\frac{i\pi}{k},\frac{2 i\pi}{k}...,i\pi$, and the space ${\cal H}_{T^2}$ is finite-dimensional. For general compact semi-simple $G$, the Hilbert space ${\cal H}$ takes the form [@EMSS; @axelrod-witten]

$${\cal H}_{T^2} \simeq \frac{\Lambda_w}{{\cal W}\ltimes k\Lambda_r}\,.$$

where $\Lambda_w,\,\Lambda_r$ are the weight and root lattices of $G$. In other words, ${\cal H}_{T^2}$ is the set of weights (hence representations) in a level-$k$ affine Weyl chamber.

Given a "wavefunction" $Z(M;u;\hbar)\in{\cal H}_{T^2}$ associated to the knot complement $M=\widetilde{M}\backslash K$ (with $\partial M=T^2$), the partition function $Z^G(\widetilde{M},K;R_\lambda;\hbar)$ for $K\in \widetilde{M}$ colored by representation $R_\lambda$ is simply given by evaluating $Z(M;u;\hbar)$ at $u=i\pi\frac{\lambda^*+\rho^*}{k}$ as in [\[uR\]](#uR){reference-type="eqref" reference="uR"}. For example, in the case of $SU(2)$ theory, we evaluate $Z(M;u;\hbar)$ at $u = i\pi N/k$ (and normalize by the partition function of $S^3$) to find the colored Jones polynomial $J_N(K;q)$. The single wavefunction $Z(M;u;\hbar)$ in ${\cal H}_{T^2}$ comprises the entire family of colored Jones polynomials $J_N(K;q)$, $N\in{\mathbb{Z}}$.

How is such a wavefunction obtained in the compact theory? For any three-manifold $M$, there is a Lagrangian submanifold ${\cal L}\subset {\cal M}$ corresponding to the semi-classical "state" $M$. This manifold ${\cal L}$ is simply defined as the set of flat connections on $T^2$ that can extend to a flat connection on all of $M$. It is the so-called $G$-character variety of $M$ and can be described by a set of polynomial equations in the eigenvalues $\ell_i$ and $m_i$:

$${\cal L}\,:\quad A_j(\ell,m) = 0\,.$$

Depending on whether we restrict to $\ell,m\in S^1$ or $\ell,m\in{\mathbb{C}}^*$, these same equations describe flat $G$ or $G_{\mathbb{C}}$ connections. In the rank-one case, there is just a single equation, the A-polynomial of the knot complement. Upon quantization, the functions $A_j$ get mapped to quantum operators

$$\widehat{A}_j(\widehat{\ell},\widehat{m},q=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{\hbar}}) := {\cal O}_{A_j}\,,$$

where $\widehat{\ell}_i :={\cal O}_{\ell_i} = e^{\widehat{v}_i}$ and $\widehat{m}_i :={\cal O}_{m_i} =e^{\widehat{u}_i}$ act on ${\cal H}_{T^2}$ as

$$\widehat{\ell}_i\,Z(u) = Z(u_i+\hbar)\quad\text{(shifting only $u_i$)}\,,\qquad \widehat{m}_i\,Z(u) = e^{u_i} Z(u)\,.$$

In terms of the colored Jones polynomial $J_N(K,q)$, this means $\widehat{\ell}J_N(K,q) = J_{N+1}(K,q)$ and $\widehat{m}J_N(K,q) = q^{N/2}J_N(K,q)$. The wavefunction $Z(M;u)$ must satisfy [@gukov-2003; @DGLZ]

$$\widehat{A}_j\,Z(M;u) = 0 \qquad \forall\,j\,, \label{Acpt}$$

which leads to a set of recursion relations on polynomial invariants of the knot $K$. In the mathematical literature, such a recursion relation for the colored Jones polynomial (*i.e.* in the case of $G=SU(2)$) is known as the *AJ conjecture* [@garoufalidis-2004; @Gar-Le] (also *cf.* [@Gar-twist]).

### Complex theory

Now, we would like to relate partition functions in Chern-Simons theory with compact gauge group $G$ to Chern-Simons theory with complex gauge group $G_{\mathbb{C}}$. In the case of complex gauge group, the phase space is ${\cal M}={\cal M}_{{\rm flat}} (G_{\mathbb{C}};T^2)=\big(({\mathbb{C}}^*)^r\times({\mathbb{C}}^*)^r\big)/{\cal W}$, and the full symplectic structure induced by Chern-Simons theory is

$$\omega = \frac{\tau}{2}\,\omega_0 + \frac{\tilde{\tau}}{2}\,\overline{\omega_0}\,, \label{wcx}$$

with $\omega_0 = \frac14\int_{T^2} {\rm Tr}(\delta {\cal A}\wedge\delta{\cal A})$ as in [\[w0\]](#w0){reference-type="eqref" reference="w0"}. The connection ${\cal A}$ now takes values in ${\frak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}$, and a priori there are two independent coupling constants $\tau$ and $\tilde{\tau}$. These are the analog of the level $k$ in the compact theory; we include them here in the definition of $\omega$. Since ${\cal M}$ is noncompact, the quantization condition [\[wquant\]](#wquant){reference-type="eqref" reference="wquant"} is less restrictive, only fixing $\tau+\tilde{\tau}\in {\mathbb{Z}}$.

The noncompactness of ${\cal M}$ changes the nature of the Hilbert space ${\cal H}$ --- as in the case of the harmonic oscillator, it is no longer finite-dimensional. Choosing a polarization $\partial/\partial v=0$, we can effectively take ${\cal H}$ to consist of Weyl-invariant square-integrable functions $f(u,\bar{u}) \in L^2(({\mathbb{C}}^*)^{r})$. However, the fact that [\[wcx\]](#wcx){reference-type="eqref" reference="wcx"} is a simple sum of holomorphic and antiholomorphic pieces means that at a perturbative level any wavefunction $Z(M;u)\in{\cal H}$ will *factorize* into holomorphic and antiholomorphic components. Put more concretely, the exact wavefunction $Z(M;u,\bar{u};\hbar=\frac{2\pi i}{\tau},\tilde{\hbar}=\frac{2\pi i}{\tilde{\tau}})$ corresponding to complex Chern-Simons theory on the knot complement $M$ can be written as [@DGLZ; @Wit-anal]

$$Z(M;u,\bar{u};\hbar,\tilde{\hbar}) = \sum_{\alpha,\bar{\alpha}} n_{\alpha,\bar{\alpha}}\, Z^\alpha_{\rm pert}(M;u;\hbar)\, \overline{Z}^{\bar{\alpha}}_{\rm pert}(M;\bar{u};\tilde{\hbar})\,, \label{sumGc}$$

for some coefficients $n_{\alpha,\bar{\alpha}}$, where, as $\hbar\to 0$, each component $Z^\alpha_{\rm pert}(M;u)$ can be expressed as a perturbative series

$$Z^\alpha_{\rm pert}(M;u) = \exp\left( -\frac{1}{\hbar}S_0(u) +\frac{\delta}{2}\log\hbar+S_1(u)+ \hbar\,S_1(u)+\ldots\right)\,,\qquad \hbar = \frac{2\pi i}{\tau}\,\,. \label{Zcxpert}$$

Each partition function $Z^\alpha_{\rm pert}(M;u;\hbar)$ corresponds to complex Chern-Simons theory in the background of a fixed flat connection on $M$ that has meridian holonomy eigenvalues $m=e^u$. The set of such connections, labelled by $\alpha$, is nothing but the (finite) set of solutions $\{v^\alpha(u)\}$ (mod $2\pi i$) to the equations

$$A_j(\ell,m) = 0$$

at fixed $m=e^u$. In the case of $SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})$ theory, one of these flat connections is the geometric one, corresponding to a hyperbolic metric on $M$.

Since the complex phase space ${\cal M}$ is just the complexification of the phase space of the compact theory, the quantization of the functions $A_j(\ell,m)$ is formally identical to the quantization in the compact case. In other words, the operators ${\cal O}_{A_j}=\widehat{A}_j(\hat{\ell},\hat{m},q=e^{2\hbar})$ are identical to those of the compact theory. Every component $Z^\alpha_{\rm pert}(M;u;\hbar)$ must therefore satisfy [@gukov-2003; @DGLZ]

$$\widehat{A}_j(\widehat{\ell},\widehat{m},e^{2\hbar})\,Z^\alpha_{\rm pert}(M;u;\hbar) = 0\,\qquad \forall\,j,\alpha\,,  \label{Acx}$$

with $\widehat{\ell}_iZ(M;u;\hbar) = Z(M;u_i+\hbar;\hbar)$ (in other words $\widehat{v}_i=\hbar\partial_{u_i}$) and $\widehat{m}_iZ(M;u;\hbar) = u^{u_i}Z(M;u;\hbar)$. In particular, at leading order in $\hbar$, we can write

$$Z^\alpha_{\rm pert}(M;u;\hbar) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{\gamma_\alpha} \theta +\ldots\right)\,, \label{Zcts}$$

where $\theta \sim - \sum_iv_i\,du_i$ is a Liouville 1-form and $\gamma_\alpha$ is a path on the complex variety ${\cal L}= \{ A_j=0\}$ ending at the point $(e^{v^\alpha(u)},e^u)$, as in Figure [\[fig:lm\]](#fig:lm){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:lm"}b. Now that $u$ is a continuous parameter in the complex theory, this integral expression makes complete sense.

[^1]: If $G$ is not compact, there may be elements that are not so diagonalizable, but they form lower-dimensional components of ${\cal M}$ which should not be considered in the quantization.
