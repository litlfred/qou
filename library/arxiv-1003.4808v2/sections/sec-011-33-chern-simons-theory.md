Finally, we arrive at our goal, Chern-Simons theory. Let us recall for a second why we began discussing quantization in the first place. In Section [\[sec:cut\]](#sec:cut){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:cut"}, we reviewed how partition functions in TQFT could be obtained by cutting and gluing three-manifolds. We explained that the partition function of a manifold with a knot is equivalent to the partition function of the corresponding knot complement, projected onto appropriate boundary conditions in ${\cal H}(T^2)$ as in [\[uR\]](#uR){reference-type="eqref" reference="uR"}. To make complete sense of this, however, and to actually calculate partition functions, we must understand what ${\cal H}(T^2)$ really is. Using Section [\[sec:quant\]](#sec:quant){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:quant"} we are finally in a position to do so.
