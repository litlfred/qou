[]{#subsect:algebraic_int_regular_trace label="subsect:algebraic_int_regular_trace"} If $q=p^e$ is a power of a prime number, the previous identities may be interpreted in the setting of the representation theory of the group of invertible $n \times n$ matrices $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$. Indeed, it is a well-known fact [@Iwa64] that the relations defining $\mathscr{H}_{n,q}$ are those of the convolution algebra $$\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q) \backslash \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)/ \mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)] = \left\{f \in \mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)]\,\,|\,\,\forall b,b' \in \mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q),\,\, f(bgb')=f(g) \right\},$$ where $\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$ is the Borel subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$, containing the invertible upper-triangular matrices.

Now, consider the $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$-module of functions on the flag variety $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)/ \mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$ --- or, in other words, right $\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$-invariant complex functions on $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$. Since $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$ acts transitively on the flag variety, a $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$-endomorphism $u$ of this module is entirely determined by a function $h \in \mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)]$ such that: $$u(\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)})=\sum_{g \in G} h(g)\,\mathbb{1}_g.$$ Moreover, the condition $u \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)}\, \mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)/\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)]$ implies that $h$ is left and right $\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$-invariant. As a consequence, we have an isomorphism $$\mathscr{H}_{n,q} \stackrel{\text{\tiny iso}}{\simeq}\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)\backslash \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q) / \mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)] \stackrel{\text{\tiny iso}}{\simeq}\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)}\,\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)/\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)],$$ whence a decomposition of the $(\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q),\mathscr{H}_{n,q})$-bimodule $\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)/\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)]$ similar to the classical Schur-Weyl duality between $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})$ and $\mathfrak{S}_n$: $$_{\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q) \curvearrowright }\big\{\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)/\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)]\big\}_{\curvearrowleft \mathscr{H}_{n,q}} =\sum_{\lambda \in \mathscr{Y}_n} U_\lambda(q) \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} S^\lambda(q).$$ The $U_\lambda(q)$ are called unipotent modules of $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$. Each $U_\lambda(q)$ is irreducible and of complex dimension $$\dim U_\lambda(q) = D_\lambda(q) =q^{b(\lambda)} \, \frac{\{n!\}_q}{\prod_{\oblong \in \lambda} \{h(\oblong)\}_q}.$$ Thus, the natural probability measure $M_{n,q}$ on the set of characters of $\mathscr{H}_{n,q}$ is also the natural probability measure on the set of unipotent characters of $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_q)$. Moreover, the regular trace $\tau_{q}$ is just the restriction of the normalized trace of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}\, \mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})/\mathrm{B}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})]$ to the subalgebra of $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})$-morphisms, that is to say, $\mathscr{H}_{n,q}$.

::: remark
In a much more general context, given a (connected, non-twisted) Chevalley group $G^{F}$ over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$ and a cuspidal irreducible character $\rho$ of a Levi subgroup $L^{F}$, the decomposition in irreducibles of the $G^{F}$-module $\mathrm{R}_{L^{F}}^{G^{F}}(\rho)$ obtained by parabolic induction yields a probability measure on the set of characters of a multiple-parameter Hecke algebra of the ramification group $$W(\rho)=\{w \in \mathop{\mathrm{Norm}}(L^{F})/L^{F}\,\,|\,\,\rho \circ w = \rho\}.$$ This ramification group is a Coxeter group and a subgroup of the relative Weyl group $N(L^{F})/L^{F}$; moreover, the multiple-parameter Hecke algebra is in fact isomorphic to the group algebra $\mathbb{C}W(\rho)$ (see [@HL80; @Lus84; @Geck93]), so the probability measure is in fact over the isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of $W(\rho)$. The case we are studying in this paper is when $G^{F}=\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})$, $L^{F}=(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{n}$, $\rho=1$ is the trivial character and $W(\rho)=W=\mathfrak{S}_{n}$.
:::
