In a quantum ﬁeld theory, a matter particle is represented by a fermion ﬁeld
ψ which, in the case of a gauge theory, needs to be in some representation of G.
It is added (or: coupled) to the theory by adding to the YM Lagrangian a term
corresponding to the Lagrangian of the free particle (this includes a parameter, the
particle mass m), and another term corresponding to its interaction with the YM
ﬁeld. In SYM the ﬁeld needs to be in a representation ρ of G, tensored with an
N = 2 multiplet, to preserve the SUSY. (The relevant multiplet, which is built from
the fermion ψ, is called a hypermultiplet; we have already encountered the other
basic type, the N = 2 vector multiplet, which contained the scalar Higgs ﬁeld ϕ.) If
the number of particles added is not too large, the theory remains asymptotically
free (β < 0) or becomes scale invariant (β = 0), and in either case retains many
properties of the pure SYM theory. There still is an r-dimensional moduli space B.

It is natural to ask whether the four dimensional, N=2 SYM theory with ar-
bitrary gauge groups G and collection of particles ρi with β ≤0 can be similarly
solved in geometric terms via families of abelian varieties and meromorphic one-
forms. This question will occupy us in the remainder of this work.
