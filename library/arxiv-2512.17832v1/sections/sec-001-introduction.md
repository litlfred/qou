Remarkable measurements from the
collisions of large nuclei, Au + Au at the Relativistic
Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) and Pb + Pb at the Large
Hadron Collider (LHC), show that O(104) particles yield
signs of collective behavior in the form of, e.g., near-
perfect fluidity [1–3], strangeness enhancement [4, 5],
quarkonium suppression [6–8], and, in particular, the en-
ergy loss of high-momentum particles [9–11], indicating
the production of a novel state of matter, the quark-gluon
plasma (QGP). Shocking results from high-multiplicity
p+Pb collisions at LHC [12–21] and p/d/3He+Au colli-
sions at RHIC [22, 23] with O(102) particles show many
of these same signs of non-trivial emergent many-body
behavior consistent with the formation of a QGP in this
system. In the condensed matter context, non-trivial col-
lective motion has been observed in cold fermionic atom
systems of O(10) particles [24]. As shown in Fig. 1 (bot-
tom), the one glaring missing signature for QGP forma-
tion in asymmetric small systems in high-energy physics
is jet quenching; i.e. the measurement of the attenua-
tion of high momentum particles is inconsistent with en-
ergy loss model predictions [25–27]. This missing link,
in addition to substantial theoretical and experimental
difficulties in asymmetric systems [25, 28–31], motivated
the collision of symmetric small systems at LHC with a
similar multiplicity to central p + Pb collisions [32–35].
Preliminary results from O + O [36, 37] and Ne + Ne
[38] collisions indicate non-trivial final-state partonic en-
ergy loss of high momentum particles. This raises the
question: what is the smallest possible system in which
non-trivial, emergent, many-particle dynamics can be ob-
served by the suppression of high-momentum particles,
i.e. what is the smallest system in which jet quenching
can be discovered?

nuclear parton distribution function (nPDF) effects. In

Our perturbative
quantum chromodynamics (pQCD)-based energy loss
model [44] includes elastic and inelastic energy loss chan-
nels, small system corrections to these channels, and a
rigorous statistical calibration against large system data
at RHIC and LHC. Our baseline predictions are from
state-of-the-art, next-to-leading-order (NLO) nPDF cal-
culations, rigorously constrained to an unprecedented
breadth of data [45]. In this work we consider theoretical
uncertainties associated with the NLO nPDF production
baseline [33, 34, 45–47], theoretical uncertainties associ-
ated with the energy loss [44, 48, 49], and the expected
statistical and systematic uncertainties on measurements
at LHC [36–38]. In this way we can provide a realistic
and quantitative estimate for the discovery potential of
energy loss in small systems.
In Fig. 1 (top), we visualize the distinguishability be-
tween final-state energy loss and nPDF-only effects for
small systems from 10B + 10B to 3He + 3He. Also shown
in Fig. 1 (top) is the quantitative agreement of our energy
loss model with experimental data for symmetric systems
from Pb+Pb to O+O. We find that the isotopes with the
highest discovery potential for energy loss in extremely
small systems are 6Li + 6Li, which results in an expected
significance of 2.3 (3.0) in minimum bias (0–10% central-
ity) collisions, and 3He + 3He, which yields an expected
significance of 1.7 (2.6) in minimum bias (0–10% cen-
trality) collisions.

particles and 6Li + 6Li ∼50, similar to the O(10) parti-
cles of [24]. Surprisingly, 4He + 4He is currently not well
suited for observing jet quenching in small systems. To
