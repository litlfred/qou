Our hypotheses on $q$, $\ell$ and $k$ in this paragraph will be the same as in Proposition [\[xxsec6.4\]](#xxsec6.4){reference-type="ref" reference="xxsec6.4"}. Let $G$ be a connected complex semisimple algebraic group of dimension $d.$ The definition of the *quantised function algebra* $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ is given in the case where $q$ is an indeterminate over $\mathbb{C}$ in [@BG2 I.7.5], and in the case where $q$ is a primitive $\ell$th root of 1 in $\mathbb{C}$ in [@BG2 III.7.1].

::: proposition
Let $G$, $d$, $q$, $\ell$ and $k$ be as stated above. Let $A = \mathcal{O}_q(G).$

1.  $A$ is AS-regular of dimension $d.$

2.  $\nu = {\xi}S^2$, $A$ has rigid dualising complex $^{\nu}A^1 [d]$, $\mathop{\mathrm{tHdim}}A=\mathop{\mathrm{tHcdim}}A=d$, and twisted Poincaré duality holds.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* (a) Suppose first that $q$ is a root of unity. By [@BG1 Theorem 2.8] $A$ is noetherian PI, Auslander regular and Cohen Macaulay of global dimension $d$, and everything is proved in ([\[xxsec6.2\]](#xxsec6.2){reference-type="ref" reference="xxsec6.2"}). Suppose now that $q$ is an indeterminate. It is proved in [@BG1 Proposition 2.7] that $\mathop{\mathrm{gldim}}A$ is finite, and indeed the global dimension is $d$ by [@GZ Theorem 0.1]. By [@GZ Theorem 0.1], $A$ is Auslander-regular and Cohen-Macaulay. By Lemma [\[xxsec6.1\]](#xxsec6.1){reference-type="ref" reference="xxsec6.1"}, $A$ is AS-regular.

\(b\) This follows from Proposition [\[xxsec4.5\]](#xxsec4.5){reference-type="ref" reference="xxsec4.5"} and Corollaries [\[xxsec5.1\]](#xxsec5.1){reference-type="ref" reference="xxsec5.1"} and [\[xxsec5.2\]](#xxsec5.2){reference-type="ref" reference="xxsec5.2"}. ◻
:::
