Proposition. Let g be a Lie algebra over k of ﬁnite dimension d, and let A be its
universal enveloping algebra U (g).
(a) S2 = id A.
(b) A is an AS-regular Hopf algebra with gldim A = d.
(c) [Ye2] The rigid dualising complex of A is (A ⊗ ⋀ d g)[d]. Thus the Nakayama
automorphism ν = ξ of A is the inverse of the winding automorphism Ξ[ χ],
where χ is the representation of g on ⋀ d g. That is,
ν(x) = x + tr(adg(x))
for x ∈ g.
(d) If tr(adg(x)) ⁄= 0, then ν is not diagonalisable.
(e) If g is semisimple, then ν is the identity and hence Hdim A = Hcdim A = d.
(f ) Hd(A, Aν ) ∼= Z(A) ⁄= 0 , and H d(A, ν A) ∼= A/[A, A], so that tHcdim A =
tHdim A = d. The twisted Poincar´ e duality of Corollary 5.2 holds for A.
Proof. (a) Recall that the antipode is given by S(x) = −x for x ∈ g, so S2 the
identity.
(b) That the global dimension is d is [CE, Theorem XIII.8.2]. It is well-known
that A is Auslander-regular for all g. The assertion follows from Lemma 6.1.
(c) The description of rigid dualising complex of A is the main result of [Ye2].
(d) This follows from (c).
(e) If g is semisimple, then tr(ad g(x)) = 0 for all x ∈ g. By (c), ν is the identity.
The assertion follows from Theorems 3.4(c) and 5.3(c).
(f) This follows from Corollaries 5.1 and 5.2. □

and cohomology follows for U (g) from Lemma 3.3. This retrieves the classical
duality for Lie algebra (co)homology which dates back to the work of Koszul [Ko]
and Hazewinkel [Ha].
