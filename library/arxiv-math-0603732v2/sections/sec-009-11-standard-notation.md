moment. When we are working with quantum groups it will sometimes be necessary
to assume that k contains C(q). Unless stated otherwise all vector spaces are
over k, and an unadorned ⊗ will mean ⊗k. An algebra or a ring always means
a k-algebra with associative multiplication mA and with unit 1. Every algebra
homomorphism is a unitary k-algebra homomorphism. All A-modules will be by
default left modules. Let Aop denote the opposite algebra of A and let Ae denote the
enveloping algebra A ⊗ Aop. The category of left [resp. right] A-modules is denoted
by A-Mod [resp. Aop-Mod]. So an Ae-module - that is, an object of Ae-Mod - is
the same as an A-bimodule central over k.
When A is a Hopf algebra we shall use the symbols ∆, ǫ and S respectively for
its coproduct, counit and antipode. The coproduct of a ∈ A will be denoted by
∆( a) = ∑ a1 ⊗ a2. For details concerning the above terminology, see for example
[Mo, Chapter 1]. In many places we assume that
the antipode S of the Hopf algebra A is bijective.(1.1.1)
Some of the results in this paper are valid more generally, but the bije ctivity of S is
satisﬁed by all the examples which will concern us. Recently Skryabin proved the
following.
Proposition. [Sk] Let A be a noetherian Hopf algebra. If one of the following hold,
then S is bijective.
(a) A is semiprime.
(b) A is aﬃne PI.
Thus (1.1.1) is a reasonable condition and may be satisﬁed by all noeth erian
Hopf algebras.
