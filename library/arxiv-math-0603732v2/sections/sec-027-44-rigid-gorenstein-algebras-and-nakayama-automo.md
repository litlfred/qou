The definition of the Van den Bergh condition leads us naturally to the following

::: definition
Let $A$ be an algebra with finite injective dimension $d$.

1.  $A$ is *rigid Gorenstein* if there is an algebra automorphism $\nu$ such that $$\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}^i_{A^e}(A,A^e)= \begin{cases} 0 & i\neq d\\
    {^1A^\nu} &i=d\end{cases}$$ as $A$-bimodules.

2.  The automorphism $\nu$ is called the *Nakayama automorphism* of $A$.

3.  The *Nakayama order* of $A$, denoted by $o(A)$, is the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $\nu^n$ is inner, or $\infty$ if no such $n$ exists.
:::

By Proposition [\[xxsec4.3\]](#xxsec4.3){reference-type="ref" reference="xxsec4.3"}, $A$ is rigid Gorenstein if and only ${^\nu A^1}[d]$ is a rigid dualising complex of $A$. The terminology in (b) generalises standard usage from the classical theory of Frobenius algebras, as - for example - in [@Ya Section 2.1]. For a Frobenius algebra $A$ has as its rigid dualising complex the $k$-dual $A^{\ast}$ of $A$ [@Ye3 Proposition 5.9], and $A^{\ast} \cong {^{\nu}A^1}$, by [@Ya Theorem 2.4.1].

The following observations are clear from the definition and (2.3.2).

::: proposition
1.  The Nakayama automorphism is determined up to multiplication by an inner automorphism of $A.$

2.  The Nakayama automorphism acts trivially on $Z(A).$
:::
