The composite map QH∗(M) →HF ∗(L, L) →QH∗(M) is given by quantum cup-product with the fundamental class [L]. Sketch. The composite counts pairs of disks, each with one interior marked point, and with an incidence condition at their boundary marked points on L. Gluing, one obtains a disk with boundary on L and two interior marked points lying on the real diameter, but no boundary marked point. The relevant one-dimensional moduli space (with modulus the distance d between the interior marked points) has another boundary component, where d →0 rather than d →∞; this is geometrically realised by a degeneration to a disk attached to a sphere bubble
