Let $\EuScript{A}$ be an $A_{\infty}$-category. There is a two-stage purely algebraic operation which formally enlarges $\EuScript{A}$ to yield a more computable object: first, passing to twisted complexes to give $Tw \, \EuScript{A}$; second, idempotent-completing to give $\Pi(Tw\,\EuScript{A}) = Tw^{\pi}\EuScript{A}$. Twisted complexes themselves form the objects of a non-unital $A_{\infty}$-category $Tw(\EuScript{A})$, which has the property that all morphisms can be completed with cones to sit in exact triangles. Idempotent completion, or split-closure, includes objects quasi-representing all cohomological idempotents, and is discussed further in Section [\[Section:AlgebraicInterlude\]](#Section:AlgebraicInterlude){reference-type="ref" reference="Section:AlgebraicInterlude"}. We write $nu$-$fun(\EuScript{A},\EuScript{B})$ for the $A_{\infty}$-category of non-unital functors from $\EuScript{A}$ to $\EuScript{B}$; $mod$-$\EuScript{A}$ for $nu$-$fun(\EuScript{A}^{opp},Ch)$, where $Ch$ is the $dg$-category (viewed as an $A_{\infty}$-category with vanishing higher differentials) of chain complexes of ${\mathbb K}$-vector spaces. Given $Y \in$ Ob$\,\EuScript{A}$ and an $\EuScript{A}$-module $\EuScript{M}$, we define the *algebraic twist* $\EuScript{T}_Y\EuScript{M}$ as the module $$\EuScript{T}_Y\EuScript{M}(X) = \EuScript{M}(Y)\otimes hom_{\EuScript{A}}(X,Y)[1] \oplus \EuScript{M}(X)$$ (with operations we shall not write out here). The twist is the cone over the canonical evaluation morphism $$\EuScript{M}(Y) \otimes \EuScript{Y}\rightarrow \EuScript{M}$$ where $\EuScript{Y}$ denotes the Yoneda image of $Y$. For two objects $Y_0, Y_1 \in$ Ob$\, \EuScript{A}$, the essential feature of the twist is that it gives rise to a canonical exact triangle in $H(\EuScript{A})$ $$\cdots \rightarrow Hom_{H(\EuScript{A})}(Y_0,Y_1) \otimes Y_0 \rightarrow Y_1 \rightarrow T_{Y_0}(Y_1) \stackrel{[1]}{\rightarrow} \cdots$$ (where $T_{Y_0}(Y_1)$ is any object whose Yoneda image is $\EuScript{T}_{Y_0}(\EuScript{Y}_1)$). Note that if $\EuScript{A}$ decomposes into a collection of orthogonal subcategories, and if $Y_0$ lives purely in one of these, the algebraic twist by definition acts trivially on all the other summands.

The twist functor $\EuScript{T}_L \in nu$-$fun(\EuScript{F}(M),\EuScript{F}(M))$ plays an essential role when $L$ is *spherical*, meaning that $Hom_{H(\EuScript{F}(M))}(L,L) \cong H^*(S^n)$. Suppose $L\subset M$ is a Lagrangian sphere; the *geometric twist* $\tau_L$ is the autoequivalence of $\EuScript{F}(M)$ defined by the positive Dehn twist in $L$. On the other hand, since $M$ is spherically monotone, such a sphere gives rise to a well-defined (though not *a priori* non-zero) object of the Fukaya category by the classical work of Oh [@Oh], hence an algebraic twist functor in the sense described above.

::: Proposition
[]{#Prop:twists label="Prop:twists"} If $L\subset M$ is a Lagrangian sphere, equipped with the non-trivial *Spin* structure if $L \cong S^1$, then the geometric twist and the algebraic twist are quasi-isomorphic in $nu$-$fun(\EuScript{F}(M),\EuScript{F}(M))$.
:::

This is [@FCPLT Corollary 17.17] for exact symplectic manifolds; the argument carries over *mutatis mutandis* to the monotone case (see [@WW:triangle] for a detailed account, but note most of their work is needed only for the strictly more difficult case of fibred Dehn twists). Seidel also proves that if $\EuScript{G}: \EuScript{A}\rightarrow \EuScript{B}$ is an $A_{\infty}$-functor, then for $Y\in \EuScript{A}$, $$\label{Eqn:TwistFunctor}
\EuScript{G}\circ \EuScript{T}_Y \ \simeq \ \EuScript{T}_{\EuScript{G}(Y)}\circ \EuScript{G}\ \in nu{\--}fun(\EuScript{A},\EuScript{B}).$$

::: Corollary
[]{#Cor:TwistDeterminesSphere label="Cor:TwistDeterminesSphere"} A Lagrangian sphere $L \subset M$ is determined up to quasi-isomorphism in $\EuScript{F}(M)$ by its associated twist functor $\EuScript{T}_L$ together with the natural transformation $\operatorname{id}\rightarrow \EuScript{T}_L[-1]$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* In general: if $Y_1$ and $Y_2 \in \EuScript{A}$ are spherical and $\EuScript{T}_{Y_1}$ and $\EuScript{T}_{Y_2}$ are quasi-isomorphic as objects of $nu$-$fun(mod$-$\EuScript{A},mod$-$\EuScript{A})$, by an isomorphism which entwines the natural transformations $\operatorname{id}\rightarrow \EuScript{T}_{Y_i}[-1]$, then $Y_1$ and $Y_2$ are quasi-isomorphic objects of $\EuScript{A}$. In the geometric situation at hand, the natural transformation of functors $\operatorname{id}\rightarrow \EuScript{T}_L[-1]$ arises from counting holomorphic sections of a Lefschetz fibration over the annulus with a unique interior critical point having vanishing cycle the given Lagrangian sphere. The cone on this natural transformation is the evaluation map, whose image lies in the subcategory of $mod$-$\EuScript{A}$ generated by the object $Y$ itself; indeed, all elements in the image are twisted complexes of the form $V\otimes Y$, for graded vector spaces $V$. The only spherical such object is $Y$ itself, for reasons of rank, hence $\operatorname{id}\rightarrow \EuScript{T}_Y[-1]$ determines $Y$. ◻
:::

We will later need a split-generation criterion for finite collections of Lagrangian spheres, which is a minor variant on an argument due to Seidel [@Seidel:HMSgenus2]. Let $M$ be a closed symplectic manifold and $\{V_1,\ldots, V_k\}$ a collection of Lagrangian spheres in $M$ for which there is a *positive relation*, i.e. some word $w$ in the positive Dehn twists $\tau_{V_j}$ is equal to the identity in $\operatorname{Symp}(M)/\textrm{Ham}(M)$; if $M$ is simply-connected, this quotient is the mapping class group $\pi_0\operatorname{Symp}(M)$. The word $w$, strictly speaking together with a choice of Hamiltonian isotopy from that product of twists to the identity, defines a Lefschetz fibration $\EuScript{W}\rightarrow S^2$ with fibre $M$, where $w$ encodes the monodromy homomorphism of the fibration. We suppose that the Lagrangian spheres all lie in the $\lambda$-summand $\EuScript{F}(M;\lambda)$. Fix a homotopy class of sections $\beta$ of $\EuScript{W}\rightarrow S^2$; the moduli space of $J$-holomorphic sections in this homotopy class has complex virtual dimension $$\langle c_1(T^{vt}(\EuScript{W}), \beta \rangle + \dim_{{\mathbb C}}(M).$$ Suppose now the moduli spaces of sections define pseudocycles, or more generally carry a virtual class. Evaluation at a point therefore defines, by Poincaré duality, a cycle in $H^{ev}(M;{\mathbb C})$ depending on $\beta$, and arranging these for different homology classes of section defines an element of $H^{ev}(M;\Lambda_{{\mathbb R}})$, or of $H^{ev}(M;{\mathbb C})$ in the fibre-monotone case, which we call the cycle class $\EuScript{C}(w)$ of the word $w$.

::: Proposition
[]{#Prop:Split-Generate-basic label="Prop:Split-Generate-basic"} If for some positive relation $w$ in the $\tau_{V_j}$ quantum cup-product by the cycle class $\EuScript{C}(w)$ is nilpotent as an operation on $QH^*(M;\lambda)$, the spheres $\{V_j\}$ split-generate $D^{\pi}\EuScript{F}(M;\lambda)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* By the correspondence between algebraic and geometric Dehn twists, Proposition [\[Prop:twists\]](#Prop:twists){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:twists"}, there are exact triangles $$\cdots \rightarrow  HF(V,K)\otimes V \rightarrow K \rightarrow \tau_V(K) \stackrel{[1]}{\longrightarrow} \cdots$$ for any Lagrangian $K \subset M$ lying in the $\lambda$-summand of the category. Concatenating the triangles for the $\tau_{V_j}$ occuring in $w$ defines a natural map $$\label{eqn:concatenate}
K \rightarrow \prod_{i_j\in I} \tau_{V_{i_j}} K \cong K$$ defined by an element of $HF^{ev}(K,K)$. Recall from the construction of the long exact sequence in Floer cohomology [@Seidel:triangle], cf. the proof of Corollary [\[Cor:TwistDeterminesSphere\]](#Cor:TwistDeterminesSphere){reference-type="ref" reference="Cor:TwistDeterminesSphere"}, that the natural map $\operatorname{id}\mapsto \EuScript{T}_L[-1]$ arises from counting sections of a Lefschetz fibration over an annulus with a single critical point. It is well-known that one can achieve transversality for holomorphic curves without fibre components using almost complex structures which make the fibration map pseudo-holomorphic. The gluing theorem [@Seidel:triangle Proposition 2.22] implies that the concatenation of such maps counts holomorphic sections of the Lefschetz fibration given by sewing several such annuli together. The map in Equation [\[eqn:concatenate\]](#eqn:concatenate){reference-type="ref" reference="eqn:concatenate"} is accordingly given by the image of the cycle class $r(\EuScript{C}(w)) \in HF^{ev}(K,K)$ under the natural restriction map $r: H^{ev}(M) \rightarrow HF^{ev}(K,K)$. If $\EuScript{C}(w)$ vanishes, one map in the concatenated exact triangle vanishes, and we therefore see that $K$ is a summand in an iterated cone amongst the objects $\{V_{i_j}\}$. If the cycle class is not trivial but quantum product with the cycle class is nilpotent, we can run the same argument after taking an iterated fibre sum of the Lefschetz fibration with itself. ◻
:::

::: Proposition
[]{#Prop:Split-Generate label="Prop:Split-Generate"} If the vanishing cycles $\{V_j\}$ all lie in the summand $\EuScript{F}(M;\lambda)$ and for some positive relation $w$ in the $\tau_{V_j}$ the cycle class $\EuScript{C}(w)$ is a multiple of $c_1(M)-\lambda\operatorname{id}$, then the $\{V_j\}$ split-generate $D^{\pi}\EuScript{F}(M;\lambda)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The image of the cycle class under the natural map $QH^*(M) \rightarrow HF(L,L)$ is zero for any $L \in \textrm{Ob} \, \EuScript{F}(M;\lambda)$, by definition of that summand of the category, cf. Equation [\[Eq:quantumcap\]](#Eq:quantumcap){reference-type="ref" reference="Eq:quantumcap"}. The proof now proceeds as before. ◻
:::

This is particularly useful when $\lambda =0$, for the nilpotent summand.

## Functors from quilts[]{#Section:Quilts label="Section:Quilts"}

Geometrically, functors between Fukaya categories are obtained from counts of quilted holomorphic surfaces, using the theory developed by Mau, Wehrheim and Woodward [@WW1; @WW2; @MWW:Announce; @Mau]. They begin by defining an extended category $\EuScript{F}^{\#}(M)$ which comes with a fully faithful embedding $\EuScript{F}(M) \hookrightarrow \EuScript{F}^{\#}(M)$; objects of the extended category are generalised Lagrangian submanifolds, which comprise an integer $k\geq 1$ and a sequence of symplectic manifolds and Lagrangian correspondences $$(\{pt\}=M_0, M_1,\ldots, M_k=M); \qquad L_{i,i+1} \subset M_i^-\times M_{i+1}, \ 0\leq i\leq k-1$$ where $(X,\omega)^-$ is shorthand for $(X,-\omega)$. These form the objects of a category $H(\EuScript{F}^{\#}(M))$, in which morphisms are given by quilted Floer cohomology groups. A *quilted $(d+1)$-marked disc* is a disc $D\subset {\mathbb C}$ with boundary marked points $\{z_0, z_1, \ldots, z_d\}$ and with a distinguished horocycle -- the seam -- at the point $z_0$. Any quilted Riemann surface whose boundary components are labelled by Lagrangian submanifolds and whose seams are labelled by Lagrangian correspondences determines an elliptic boundary value problem. This problem studies a collection of holomorphic maps, one defined on each subdomain of the surface, subject to Lagrangian boundary conditions as prescribed by the labelling data along boundaries and seams. There is an $A_{\infty}$-category $\EuScript{F}^{\#}(M)$ underlying the cohomological category $H(\EuScript{F}^{\#}(M))$, in which the higher order operations count suitable quilted disks.

The moduli space $\mathcal{M}_{d,1}$ of *nodal stable* quilted $(d+1)$-marked discs (which we shall not define) is a convex polytope homeomorphic to the multiplihedron. The codimension one boundary faces of the multiplihedron correspond to the terms of the quadratic $A_{\infty}$-functor equation $$\begin{gathered}
\sum_{i_j, k} \mu_{{\mathscr B}}^k({\mathcal F}(a_n,\ldots,a_{i_1}),{\mathcal F}(a_{i_1-1},\ldots, a_{i_2}), \ldots, {\mathcal F}(a_{i_k-1},\ldots a_1)) \\
= \sum_d (-1)^{\maltese_{j-d}} {\mathcal F}(a_n,\ldots, a_{j}, \mu_{{\mathscr A}}^d (a_{j-d},\ldots, a_{j-d-1}), a_{j-d},\ldots a_1) 
\end{gathered}$$ for an $A_{\infty}$-functor ${\mathcal F}: {\mathscr A}\rightarrow {\mathscr B}$. Mau's gluing theorem [@Mau] shows that the counts of nodal stable quilted discs indeed reflect the combinatorial boundary structure of the $\mathcal{M}_{d,1}$ and hence the $A_{\infty}$-functor equations. It follows that to every monotone, oriented and spin Lagrangian correspondence $L^{\flat} \subset M^-\times N$, there is a ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded $A_{\infty}$-functor ${\mathcal F}_{L^{\flat}}: \EuScript{F}^{\#}(M) \rightarrow \EuScript{F}^{\#}(N)$ defined on objects by $${\mathbb L}\longrightarrow  {\mathcal F}_{L^{\flat}}({\mathbb L}) \quad \textrm{taking} \quad 
(L_1, L_{23}, \ldots, L_{k-1,k}) \,   \mapsto \,  (L_1,L_{23},\ldots, L_{k-1,k},L^{\flat})$$ and on morphisms and higher products by a signed count of quilted discs. The upshot is the following.

::: Theorem
[]{#Thm:QuiltsGiveFunctors label="Thm:QuiltsGiveFunctors"} Let $M$ and $N$ be monotone symplectic manifolds. The association $L^{\flat} \mapsto {\mathcal F}_{L^{\flat}}$ defines a ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded $A_{\infty}$-functor $$\Phi: \EuScript{F}(M^- \times N) \ \longrightarrow \ nu\mbox{-}fun(\EuScript{F}^{\#}(M),\EuScript{F}^{\#}(N)).$$
:::

We will combine this general theory with an argument from [@Abouzaid-Smith], which uses properties of Yoneda embeddings to deduce that, in special cases, the Mau-Wehrheim-Woodward functor is actually fully faithful.

::: Corollary
[]{#Cor:QuiltsQH label="Cor:QuiltsQH"} In the situation of Proposition [\[Prop:Split-Generate\]](#Prop:Split-Generate){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:Split-Generate"}, the Hochschild cohomology $$HH^*(D^{\pi}\EuScript{F}(M;\lambda)) \cong QH^*(M;\lambda)$$ is isomorphic to the $\lambda$-generalised eigenspace of quantum cohomology.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We will use quilts to resolve the $\lambda$-summand of the diagonal $\Delta \subset M \times M$, which in turn will yield information on Hochschild cohomology. Recall from Proposition [\[Prop:twists\]](#Prop:twists){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:twists"} that the Dehn twist acts on the Fukaya category via an algebraic twist, hence sits in a canonical exact triangle of functors $$\rightarrow id \rightarrow \EuScript{T}_L \rightarrow Hom(L,\cdot)\otimes L \stackrel{[1]}{\longrightarrow}$$ in $nu$-$fun(\EuScript{F}(M),\EuScript{F}(M))$. This triangle is the image under the Mau-Wehrheim-Woodward functor $\Phi$ of a triangle $$\label{Eqn:LiftTriangle}
\rightarrow \Delta_M \rightarrow \Gamma(\tau_L) \rightarrow L\times L \stackrel{[1]}{\longrightarrow}$$ with $\Delta_M$ the diagonal and $\Gamma(\tau_L)$ the graph of the geometric Dehn twist, cf. [@WW:triangle Theorem 7.2]. The functor $\Phi$ is in general not fully faithful. In the setting of Proposition [\[Prop:Split-Generate\]](#Prop:Split-Generate){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:Split-Generate"}, we let $\EuScript{A}(M)$ denote the subcategory generated by the vanishing cycles $\{V_j\}$ and let $\EuScript{A}(M\times M)$ denote the subcategory generated by product Lagrangians $V_i \times V_j$. We define $\EuScript{A}^{\oplus} \subset \EuScript{F}^{\#}(M)$ to comprise those generalised correspondences $$(\{pt\}=M_0, M_1,\ldots, M_k=M); \qquad L_{i,i+1} \cong V_{i_0} \times V_{i_1} \subset M_i^-\times M_{i+1}, \ 0\leq i\leq k-1$$ so each correspondence lies in $\EuScript{A}(M\times M)$. Lemma 7.5 of [@Abouzaid-Smith] shows $Tw(\EuScript{A}) \simeq Tw(\EuScript{A}^{\oplus})$, and moreover that $\Phi$ induces a functor $$\Phi: \EuScript{A}(M \times M) \rightarrow nu\-- fun(Tw^{\pi}\EuScript{A}^{\oplus}(M),Tw^{\pi}\EuScript{A}^{\oplus}(M))$$ which is a fully faithful embedding [@Abouzaid-Smith Lemma 7.8]. Note that this argument relies on the existence of chain-level units in the Fukaya category satisfying $$\mu^2(e,e) = e; \quad \mu^k(e,\ldots,e) = 0 \qquad \textrm{for} \ k>2.$$ In [@Abouzaid-Smith] these existed for grading reasons; in general, to obtain such units one must pass from the Fukaya category to its category of modules, in the manner of [@Abouzaid Section 4]. In the situation at hand, $Tw^{\pi}\EuScript{A}(M) \simeq Tw^{\pi}(\EuScript{F}(M;\lambda))$, by Proposition [\[Prop:Split-Generate\]](#Prop:Split-Generate){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:Split-Generate"}. We now go back to the proof of that Proposition; the argument, lifted by $\Phi$ to the category $\EuScript{F}(M\times M)$ as in Equation [\[Eqn:LiftTriangle\]](#Eqn:LiftTriangle){reference-type="ref" reference="Eqn:LiftTriangle"}, leads to a collection of exact triangles which concatenate to one of the shape $$\Delta_M \rightarrow \Gamma(\prod_{i_j} \tau_{V_{i_j}}) \rightarrow \langle V\times V' \rangle$$ where the third term is built out of product Lagrangians of the shape $V_i \times \tau_{V_j}(V_k)$ and the first arrow is given by quantum product by the cycle class $\mathcal{C}(w)$. Since $HF(\Delta_M, \Delta_M) \cong QH^*(M)$ as a $QH^*(M)$-module, the first arrow in this triangle vanishes on the idempotent summand of $\Delta_M$ corresponding to $QH^*(M;\lambda)$. In other words, that idempotent summand $\Delta^{\lambda}$ of the diagonal is split-generated by product Lagrangians, and lies in the split-closure of the category $\EuScript{A}(M\times M)$. At this point, we infer that $\Phi$ restricted to the extended category $$\EuScript{A}^{\Delta^{\lambda}}(M\times M)$$ which comprises product Lagrangians and the relevant summand of the diagonal, is also a fully faithful embedding. Since the diagonal maps to the identity functor of $Tw^{\pi}\EuScript{A}(M)\subset Tw^{\pi}\EuScript{F}^{\#}(M)$, this implies that $$HF((\Delta, \Delta);\lambda) \cong Hom_{nu-fun}(\operatorname{id}, \operatorname{id})$$ where the RHS is computed in endofunctors of $Tw^{\pi}\EuScript{A}(M) \simeq Tw^{\pi}\EuScript{F}(M;\lambda)$. But then the natural transformations of the identity functor exactly compute Hochschild cohomology. ◻
:::

For any endofunctor $\EuScript{G}: \EuScript{A}\rightarrow \EuScript{A}$ of an $A_{\infty}$-category, there is a Hochschild cohomology group $HH^*(\EuScript{G}) = H(hom_{fun(\EuScript{A},\EuScript{A})}(\EuScript{G},\operatorname{id})$, generalising the Hochschild cohomology of the category which arises for $\EuScript{G}= \operatorname{id}$. Symplectomorphisms of a monotone symplectic manifold act naturally on the monotone Fukaya category. Write $HF(\phi)$ for the fixed-point Floer cohomology of a symplectomorphism $\phi$, so $HF(\operatorname{id}) \cong QH^*(M)$ by the Piunikhin-Salamon-Schwarz isomorphism. As usual, the group $HF(\phi)$ is a module for quantum cohomology, hence has an eigenspace splitting into summands indexed by $\textrm{Spec}(\ast c_1(M))$.

::: Corollary
[]{#Cor:HFisHH label="Cor:HFisHH"} In the situation of Proposition [\[Prop:Split-Generate\]](#Prop:Split-Generate){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:Split-Generate"}, for any $\phi \in \operatorname{Symp}(M)$ in the subgroup generated by Dehn twists in the vanishing cycles $V_j$ inducing a functor $\EuScript{G}_{\phi}$ of $\EuScript{F}(M;\lambda)$, one has $$HF(\phi;\lambda) \cong HH^*(\EuScript{G}_{\phi}).$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We use the standard identification $HF(\phi) \cong HF(\Gamma_{\phi}, \Delta)$ where $\Gamma_{\phi} \subset M\times M$ denotes the graph. The $\lambda$-summand of this graph is resolved by suitable product Lagrangians built from the vanishing cycles, as in Corollary [\[Cor:QuiltsQH\]](#Cor:QuiltsQH){reference-type="ref" reference="Cor:QuiltsQH"}. The proof now proceeds as before, using fullness and faithfulness of the Mau-Wehrheim-Woodward functor in this setting. ◻
:::

::: Remark
For any subcategory $\EuScript{A}\subset \EuScript{F}(M)$, there are always natural open-closed string maps $$HH_*(\EuScript{A},\EuScript{A}) \rightarrow QH^*(M) \rightarrow HH^*(\EuScript{A},\EuScript{A}).$$ Abouzaid [@Abouzaid] proves (in the exact case, and for wrapped Floer cohomology) that *if the unit is in the image of the first map, then $\EuScript{A}$ split-generates the Fukaya category*. Such split-generation results are often proved by showing that the diagonal is resolved by products, in the manner of Beilinson's classical argument for sheaves on projective space. In our case, we know from Proposition [\[Prop:Split-Generate\]](#Prop:Split-Generate){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:Split-Generate"} that the appropriate $\EuScript{A}$ split-generates, without knowing that the diagonal is resolved by products. Quilts enable us to refine split-generation to this more geometric fact, which in turn has consequences for quantum cohomology.
:::

Suppose $L_0^{\flat} \subset M^-\times N$ and $L_1^{\flat}\subset N^-\times P$ are Lagrangian correspondences. Their geometric composition is given by pulling $L_0^{\flat}$ back to $M\times N\times N\times P$, intersecting with the diagonal $M \times \Delta_N \times P$, and pushing forward to $N^-\times P$; in general, although the intersection can be made transverse, the push-forward is only immersed.

::: Theorem
[]{#Thm:QuiltsCompose label="Thm:QuiltsCompose"} Given correspondences $L_0^{\flat} \subset M^-\times N$ and $L_1^{\flat}\subset N^-\times P$ with the geometric composition $L_1^{\flat} \circ L_0^{\flat} \subset M^-\times P$ smooth, embedded and transversely cut out, there is a quasi-isomorphism of functors $${\mathcal F}_{L_1^{\flat}\circ L_0^{\flat}} \ \simeq \  {\mathcal F}_{L_1^{\flat}} \circ {\mathcal F}_{L_0^{\flat}}.$$
:::

The construction of a morphism in the functor-category between ${\mathcal F}_{L_1^{\flat}\circ L_0^{\flat}}$ and ${\mathcal F}_{L_1^{\flat}} \circ {\mathcal F}_{L_0^{\flat}}$ comes from counting "biquilted\" disks with two interior seams (two parallel horocycles). The quasi-isomorphism statement is a difficult result which relies on a delicate strip-shrinking argument for the associated cohomological functors, proved in detail in [@WW2]. A different proof of the cohomological isomorphism has recently been given by Lekili and Lipyanskiy [@LekiliLipyanskiy].

## Grading and deformations[]{#Sec:Indices label="Sec:Indices"}

A one-parameter *deformation of $A_{\infty}$-categories* is an $A_{\infty}$-category $\EuScript{A}_q$ over ${\mathbb K}[[q]]$, with $\mu^d_{\EuScript{A}_q} = \mu^d_{\EuScript{A}} + O(q)$, i.e. $$\label{Eqn:Deformation}
\mu^d_{\EuScript{A}_q} = \mu^d_{\EuScript{A}} + q\mu^d_{\EuScript{A}_q,1} + q^2\mu^d_{\EuScript{A}_q,2} + \cdots$$ with each $\mu^d_{\EuScript{A}_q,j}$ comprising ${\mathbb K}$-linear maps $$hom_{\EuScript{A}}(X_{d_1},X_d) \otimes \cdots \otimes hom_{\EuScript{A}}(X_1,X_2) \rightarrow hom_{\EuScript{A}}(X_1,X_d)[2-d]$$ for any $(X_1,\ldots, X_d) \in$(Ob$\, \EuScript{A})^d$. The operations $\mu^d_{\EuScript{A}_q}$ should be $q$-adically convergent (for us they will always vanish for $q \gg 0$). In applications, such deformations of Fukaya categories will arise from (partial) compactifications of symplectic manifolds $M\subset \bar{M}$, obtained by adding in a divisor $\Delta = \bar{M} \backslash M$ disjoint from the Lagrangian submanifolds under consideration. The operation $\mu_{\EuScript{F}(\bar{M}),j}^d$ will count pseudoholomorphic $d$-gons with multiplicity $j$ over $\Delta$, so setting $q=0$ corresponds to working in the open part $M=\bar{M}\backslash \Delta$. Assuming that one can achieve transversality within the class of almost complex structures which make $\Delta \subset \bar{M}$ a pseudoholomorphic submanifold, positivity of intersections ensures that the $\mu^d_{\EuScript{A}_q}$ are compatible with the filtration on Floer cochain complexes in $\bar{M}$ by powers of $q$. Under suitable monotonicity hypotheses on $\bar{M}$, one can further set $q=1$ since all series converge.

The formal parameter $q$ of a deformation arising from a partial compactification of an open symplectic manifold with $c_1=0$ typically has non-zero degree.

::: Lemma
[]{#Lem:DeformationDegree label="Lem:DeformationDegree"} If the first Chern class $c_1(\bar{M}) = r[\Delta]$, with $r\in {\mathbb Q}$, then $deg(q) = 2r$.
:::

This is a consequence of the index theorem for the Cauchy-Riemann equations with totally real boundary conditions, see [@FCPLT Section 11]. It is most familiar in the case when $c_1(\bar{M})=0$, and the deformed category is ${\mathbb Z}$-graded, or when $\Delta \subset \bar{M}$ is an anticanonical divisor and the deformation parameter has degree 2 (for instance, deforming the cohomology of the zero-section $T^n \subset ({\mathbb C}^*)^n$ from an exterior algebra to the Clifford algebra on compactifying to ${\mathbb C}{\mathbb P}^n$, with $\Delta$ the toric anticanonical divisor).

Suppose then $c_1(\bar{M})$ is effective, that $c_1(M) = 0$, and fix a holomorphic volume form $\eta \in H^0(K_{\bar{M}})$ with *poles* of order $r$ along the divisor $\Delta \subset \bar{M}$. We denote by $\iota^{\eta}$ the absolute indices for generators of Floer complexes between graded Lagrangian submanifolds (which are assumed to lie within the open part $M$). If $L'$ is obtained as a Hamiltonian perturbation of $L$ for a Hamiltonian function whose restriction to $L$ is Morse, the index $i^{\eta}$ of a point of $L\cap L'$ is equal to the Morse index of the corresponding critical point. Suppose now one has a holomorphic polygon $u:D\rightarrow \bar{M}$ with Lagrangian boundary conditions in $M$. In this setting, $$\label{Eqn:IndexFormula}
\textrm{vdim}(u) = i^{\eta}(x_0) - \sum_{j=1}^k i^{\eta}(x_k) + k -2 + 2r \Delta \cdot \mathrm{im}(u)$$ for a holomorphic $(k+1)$-gon with one outgoing boundary puncture $x_0$ (the sign of the last term corresponds to the fact that $\eta$ has poles on $\Delta = r\cdot PD[c_1(\bar{M})]$, and the virtual dimension is increased by adding *positive* Chern number components).

## Formality[]{#Section:Formality label="Section:Formality"}

A natural and recurring question studying Fukaya categories is to determine the $A_{\infty}$-structure on an algebra $A=\oplus_{i,j} HF(L_i,L_j)$, where the $\{L_i\}$ are some finite collection of Lagrangians distinguished by the geometry in some fashion. In general this is a rather intractable problem, but in the case when $A$ is smooth and commutative, $A_{\infty}$-structures up to gauge equivalence correspond to classical Poisson structures, by Kontsevich's formality theorem. The particular case of this that pertains to exterior algebras was used by Seidel in computing $\EuScript{F}(\Sigma_2)$, and will play an important role later.

Let $A$ be a ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded algebra. We will be interested in ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded $A_{\infty}$-structures on $A$ which have trivial differential. Such a structure comprises a collection of maps $$\{ \alpha^i: A^{\otimes i} \rightarrow A \}_{i\geq 2}$$ of parity $i$; usually we will assume that the product agrees with the given product on $A$, and consider $(\alpha^i)_{i\geq 3}$. We introduce the mod 2 graded Hochschild cochain complex $$CC^{\bullet+1}(A,A) \ = \ \prod_{i \geq 2} Hom^{(\bullet+i)}(A^{\otimes i},A)$$ where $Hom^{(\bullet +i)}$ means we consider homomorphisms of parity $\bullet+i$. This carries the usual differential $d_{CC^{\bullet}}$ and the Gerstenhaber bracket $[\cdot, \cdot]$.

::: Remark
[]{#Rem:Pronilpotent label="Rem:Pronilpotent"} The shift in grading follows the convention of [@Seidel:HMSgenus2], since we will later imitate some arguments from that paper. The shift fits with a view of $A_{\infty}$-structures as defined by formal super-vector fields, but disagrees with the usual conventions of the bar complex. One typically incorporates a formal parameter $q$ of even degree into $CC^{\bullet+1}(A,A)$, which makes the resulting Lie algebra $\frak{g} = CC^{\bullet+1}[[q]]$ filtered pronilpotent. This is necessary to guarantee convergence of formal gauge transformations; $L_{\infty}$-quasi-isomorphisms of filtered pronilpotent $L_{\infty}$-algebras induce bijections between equivalence classes of Maurer-Cartan elements. In the sequel, we will encounter gauge transformations on exterior algebras $\Lambda^*({\mathbb C}^3)$ arising from formal diffeomorphisms of ${\mathbb C}^3$ which can be seen explicitly to act on $A_{\infty}$-structures defined over ${\mathbb C}$, cf. Equation [\[Eqn:exp-gamma\]](#Eqn:exp-gamma){reference-type="ref" reference="Eqn:exp-gamma"}. Geometrically, the formal parameter $q$ can be incorporated as that of a deformation obtained by partial compactification, in the sense of Equation [\[Eqn:Deformation\]](#Eqn:Deformation){reference-type="ref" reference="Eqn:Deformation"}. The power of $q$ in any given expression is therefore determined by Lemma [\[Lem:DeformationDegree\]](#Lem:DeformationDegree){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem:DeformationDegree"}, and can be reconstructed from the other geometric data. To simplify notation, we omit this parameter throughout, compare to [@Seidel:HMSgenus2 Sections 4,5].
:::

$A_{\infty}$-structures $(\alpha^i)$ on $A$ which extend its given product correspond to elements of $CC^{1}(A,A)$ satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation $$\label{Eqn:Maurer-Cartan}
\partial \alpha + \frac{1}{2} [\alpha, \alpha] = 0\qquad \textrm{with} \quad \alpha^1 = \alpha^2 = 0.$$ The first terms of the Maurer-Cartan equation give $$\label{Eqn:Maurer-Cartan-FirstTerms}
\partial \alpha^3 = 0; \ \partial \alpha^4 + \frac{1}{2}[\alpha^3,\alpha^3]=0; \ \partial \alpha^5 + [\alpha^3,\alpha^4]=0, \ldots$$ Elements $(g^i)_{i\geq 1}$ of $CC^0(A,A)$, comprising maps $A^{\otimes i} \rightarrow A$ of parity $i-1$, act by gauge transformations on the set of Maurer-Cartan solutions $(\alpha^i)_{i\geq 2}$, at least when suitable convergence conditions apply. Suppose that $A$ is finite-dimensional in each degree; let $g \in CC^0(A,A)$ have constant term $g^0 \in A^1$ vanishing. Define $$\label{Eqn:exp-gamma}
\begin{cases}
\phi^1 = \mathrm{id} + g^1 + \frac{1}{2}g^1 g^1 + \cdots = \exp(g^1), \\
\phi^2 = g^2 + \frac{1}{2} g^1 g^2 + \frac{1}{2} g^2(g^1 \otimes \mathrm{id}) +
\frac{1}{2} g^2(\mathrm{id} \otimes g^1) + \textstyle\frac{1}{3} g^2(g^1 \otimes g^1) + \cdots, \\
\vdots
\end{cases}$$ The term $\phi^j$ is the sum of all possible concatenations of components of $g$ to yield a $j$-linear map. For a term involving $r$ components, which may be ordered in $s$ different ways compatibly with their appearance in the concatenation, the constant in front of the associated term is $s(r!)^{-1}$, which ensures convergence of $\phi^j$. If $\alpha$ and $\tilde\alpha$ are Maurer-Cartan elements which are related by $(g^i)$, the associated $A_\infty$-structures are related by $\phi$, which is an $A_\infty$-isomorphism. There are obvious simplifications to the formulae when $g^1=0$, which will be the case for gauge transformations acting trivially on cohomology relating solutions with $\alpha^2=0$.

::: Lemma
[]{#Lem:DeformedHH label="Lem:DeformedHH"} Let $\EuScript{A}$ denote the $A_{\infty}$-algebra defined by the Maurer-Cartan element $\alpha \in CC^1(A,A)$, with $H(\EuScript{A}) = A$. The Hochschild cohomology $HH^*(\EuScript{A},\EuScript{A})$ is the cohomology of the complex $CC^{\bullet}(A,A)$ with respect to the twisted differential $d_{CC^{\bullet}} + [\cdot, \alpha]$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The Maurer-Cartan equation for $\alpha$ implies that the twisted differential does square to zero. The Lemma follows on comparing the resulting complex with the bar complex defining Hochschild cohomology of an $A_{\infty}$-algebra given in Equation [\[Eqn:Hochschild\]](#Eqn:Hochschild){reference-type="ref" reference="Eqn:Hochschild"}. ◻
:::

Kontsevich's remarkable result [@Kontsevich], specialised to the case of relevance in the sequel, is then:

::: Theorem
[]{#Thm:KontsevichFormality label="Thm:KontsevichFormality"} When $A=\Lambda(V)$ is an exterior algebra, with its natural ${\mathbb Z}_2$-grading placing $V$ in degree 1, there is an $L_{\infty}$-quasi-isomorphism $$\Phi: CC^{\bullet}(A,A) \longrightarrow Sym^{\bullet}(V^{\vee}) \otimes \Lambda(V)$$ between the Hochschild complex and its cohomology, the Lie algebra of polyvector fields.
:::

::: Remark
[]{#Rem:Quasi-Inverse label="Rem:Quasi-Inverse"} In fact, this $\Phi$ is strictly left-inverse to an explicit quasi-isomorphism $$\Psi: \operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}(V^{\vee}) \otimes \Lambda(V) \rightarrow CC^{\bullet}(\Lambda(V),\Lambda(V))$$ defined by Kontsevich. The components of $\Psi$ are linear combinations $\Psi^i = \sum \lambda^i \otimes \mathcal{U}_{\Gamma^i}$, where $\mathcal{U}_{\Gamma^i}$ is a multilinear operation indexed by a certain class of graphs $\{\Gamma^i\}$, and the coefficients $\lambda^i$ are explicit integrals over configuration spaces of points in the upper half-plane.
:::

The Lie algebra of polyvector fields on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ is the algebra $$Sym^{\bullet}(V^{\vee}) \otimes \Lambda(V) \ = \ {\mathbb C}[[V]] \otimes \Lambda(V).$$ The Lie algebra structure comes from the Schouten bracket $$\begin{aligned} {}
& [f \,\xi_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge \xi_{i_k}, g \,\xi_{j_1} \wedge \cdots \xi_{j_l}] = \\
& \textstyle \qquad \sum_q (-1)^{k-q-1} f \, (\partial_{i_q}g) \,
\xi_{i_1}  \wedge \cdots \wedge \widehat{\xi_{i_q}} \wedge \cdots
\wedge \xi_{i_k} \wedge \xi_{j_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge \xi_{j_l} + \\ & \textstyle \qquad
 \sum_q (-1)^{l-q+ (k-1)(l-1)} g \,(\partial_{j_q}f)\, \xi_{j_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge \widehat{\xi_{j_q}} \wedge \cdots \wedge \xi_{j_l} \wedge \xi_{i_1} \wedge
 \cdots \wedge \xi_{i_k}.
\end{aligned}$$ The quasi-isomorphism $\Phi$ identifies gauge equivalence classes of Maurer-Cartan solutions. In particular, since the Schouten bracket vanishes on functions, any formal function $W\in{\mathbb C}[[V]]$ defines a solution of Maurer-Cartan, hence an $A_{\infty}$-structure on $\Lambda(V)$. Lemma [\[Lem:DeformedHH\]](#Lem:DeformedHH){reference-type="ref" reference="Lem:DeformedHH"} and the Formality Theorem [\[Thm:KontsevichFormality\]](#Thm:KontsevichFormality){reference-type="ref" reference="Thm:KontsevichFormality"} imply that the Hochschild cohomology of a ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded $A_{\infty}$-structure on $\Lambda(V)$ defined by an element $\alpha \in HH^1$ is given by the cohomology of the ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded complex $$({\mathbb C}[[V]]\otimes \Lambda^{ev}(V))\  \xrightleftharpoons[{\ [\cdot, \alpha]\ }]{{\ [\cdot, \alpha]\ }} \ ({\mathbb C}[[V]]\otimes \Lambda^{odd}(V)).$$

The following Example will recur in the discussion of Fukaya categories of quadric hypersurfaces in Section [\[Section:Quadric\]](#Section:Quadric){reference-type="ref" reference="Section:Quadric"}.

::: Example
[]{#Ex:AinftyExterior label="Ex:AinftyExterior"} Let $V$ have dimension 1 and $A=\Lambda(V)$, with basis $1, x$ say. Any formal function $W\in{\mathbb C}[[x]]$ of degree $\geq 2$ defines an $A_{\infty}$-structure on $A$, which induces the given product if and only if the degree 2 component vanishes. Up to formal change of variables (gauge transformation), any $$W(x) = x^k + O(x^{k+1}) \ \simeq \ y^k \quad \textrm{via} \ y = x(1+\alpha_1x + \alpha_2x^2 + \cdots)$$ is equivalent to a monomial, hence the resulting $A_{\infty}$-structures are equivalent to those defined by monomial functions. Equation [\[Eqn:HHPolyFields\]](#Eqn:HHPolyFields){reference-type="ref" reference="Eqn:HHPolyFields"} simplifies to the complex $$0 \longrightarrow {\mathbb C}[[x]] \otimes \langle dx\rangle   \xrightarrow{\ \iota_{dW} \ } {\mathbb C}[[x]] \longrightarrow 0$$ with cohomology ${\mathbb C}[[x]]/\langle W'(x) \rangle$ given by the Jacobian ring, so the Hochschild cohomology of $\EuScript{A}_k = (A, W=x^k)$ has rank $k-1$.
:::

::: Remark
[]{#Rem:HH label="Rem:HH"} For the ring $R={\mathbb C}[x]/ \langle x^2-1 \rangle$, the Hochschild cohomology[^1] group of the category of ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded modules has rank 2 if $deg(x)$ is even and rank 1 if $deg(x)$ is odd (the latter fact is the computation of Example [\[Ex:AinftyExterior\]](#Ex:AinftyExterior){reference-type="ref" reference="Ex:AinftyExterior"} in the special case $k=1$, where the underlying product on $A$ is itself deformed). This comes down to the fact that the group $$Hom_{R\otimes R}(R\otimes R, R)$$ of bimodule homomorphisms has rank 2 in the first case, comprising the elements $1\otimes 1 \mapsto \alpha 1 + \beta x$, but only rank 1 in the second, since for a ${\mathbb Z}_2$-graded map one must have $1\otimes 1 \mapsto \alpha 1$. From another viewpoint, when $deg(x)$ is even, $R$ is semisimple, splitting into two orthogonal idempotent summands, but when $deg(x)$ is odd the idempotents are not of pure degree and there is no such splitting.
:::

## The Fukaya category of a curve[]{#Section:Curve label="Section:Curve"}

Take $\Sigma_g\cong C \stackrel{p}{\rightarrow} {\mathbb P}^1$ to be the unique curve branched over the points $\{0, \xi^j, \ 1\leq j\leq 2g+1\}$, where $\xi$ is a primitive $(2g+1)$-st root of unity. We can exhibit embedded circles in $C$ via matching arcs amongst these points, i.e. the projections of ${\mathbb Z}_2$-invariant curves to ${\mathbb P}^1$. For instance, when $g=2$, 5 Lagrangian submanifolds $\{L_j\}$ of $C$ are determined by the arcs of the pentagram on the left of Figure [1](#Figure:Pentagram){reference-type="ref" reference="Figure:Pentagram"} (the vertices are the $5$th roots of unity); more generally there is a spikier $(2g+1)$-gram defining $2g+1$ associated spheres permuted by the obvious cyclic symmetry. In an act of shameless innumeracy, we will refer to all these figures as *pentagrams*. If we divide $C$ by the lift of this cyclic group action from ${\mathbb P}^1$ to $C$, all the circles $\{L_j\}$ are identified with a single immersed circle $\bar{L} \subset S^2_{orb}$ in an orbifold $S^2$ with 3 orbifold points each of order $2g+1$ (these come from the unique preimage of $0\in{\mathbb P}^1$ in $C$ and the two preimages of $\infty \in {\mathbb P}^1$). This immersed curve actually bounds no teardrops, hence Floer theory can be defined unproblematically for it, or one can work equivariantly upstairs.

![Lagrangian spheres from the $g=2$ pentagram and its $g=3$ cousin; on the left one radial path and one basic path are also dotted.[]{#Figure:Pentagram label="Figure:Pentagram"}](Pentagram2-3.eps){#Figure:Pentagram}
:::

For any symplectic surface $\Sigma_g, \omega_{\Sigma}$ of genus $g\geq 2$, let $\sigma: ST\Sigma \rightarrow \Sigma$ be the projection from the unit tangent bundle. Seidel defines the *balanced* Fukaya category, an $A_{\infty}$-category linear over ${\mathbb C}$, with respect to an auxiliary choice of one-form $\theta \in \Omega^1(ST\Sigma)$ which is a primitive for the pullback of the symplectic form, $d\theta = \sigma^*\omega_{\Sigma}$. (Such forms exist since, by the Gysin sequence, $H^2(ST\Sigma,{\mathbb R}) = 0$ for $g(\Sigma) \geq 2$.) Objects of the balanced Fukaya category are simple closed curves $L \subset \Sigma$ for which $\int_L \iota_L^*\theta = 0$, where $\iota_L: L \rightarrow ST\Sigma$ is the tangent lift of $L\hookrightarrow \Sigma$ (these curves should be equipped with orientations and $Spin$ structures as usual). Homotopically trivial curves are never balanced; there is a unique balanced representative up to Hamiltonian isotopy in every other homotopy class of embedded curve. The balancing condition is an analogue of monotonicity; it implies that energy and index are correlated for holomorphic curves with balanced boundary conditions, which in turn means that the balanced category $\EuScript{F}(\Sigma)$ is defined over ${\mathbb C}$. Different choices of $\theta$ yield quasi-isomorphic categories, and the mapping class group $\Gamma_g$ acts by autoequivalences of $D^{\pi}\EuScript{F}(\Sigma_g)$, cf. [@Seidel:HMSgenus2].

::: Remark
[]{#Remark:2objects-1 label="Remark:2objects-1"} An isotopy class of essential simple closed curve $\gamma \subset \Sigma$ defines two objects in $\EuScript{F}(\Sigma)$ modulo shifts, namely the balanced representative of $\gamma$ equipped with either $Spin$ structure. Label these $\gamma$ and $\bar{\gamma}$ for the non-trivial respectively trivial $Spin$-structure. Then $$\label{Eqn:TwoSpinChoices}
HF(\gamma,\gamma) \cong {\mathbb C}\cong HF(\bar{\gamma},\bar{\gamma}); \quad HF(\gamma, \bar{\gamma}) = 0.$$ One can equivalently regard $\bar{\gamma}$ as the pair $(\gamma, \xi)$ where $\xi \rightarrow \gamma$ is the non-trivial line bundle with holonomy in ${\mathbb Z}_2$, from which point of view one sees that the group $H^1(\Sigma;{\mathbb Z}_2)$ acts on $\EuScript{F}(\Sigma)$, tensoring by flat line bundles.
:::

::: Remark
[]{#Remark:GpActingOnCurve label="Remark:GpActingOnCurve"} One can combine the actions of diffeomorphisms and tensoring by flat line bundles into an action of the canonical split extension $\widehat{\Gamma}_{split}(\Sigma_g)$ of $\Gamma_g$ by $H^1(\Sigma;{\mathbb Z}_2)$. Strictly, this is only a *weak* action, meaning that the functors $\EuScript{G}_{g_i}$ associated to group elements $g_i$ satisfy $\EuScript{G}_{g_1}\EuScript{G}_{g_2} \cong \EuScript{G}_{g_1g_2}$ (but we do not keep track of coherence amongst these isomorphisms). We will suppress this point in the sequel, but it would probably enter in reconciling the appearance of $\widehat{\Gamma}_{split}$ with Remark [\[Remark:GpActingOnModuliSpace\]](#Remark:GpActingOnModuliSpace){reference-type="ref" reference="Remark:GpActingOnModuliSpace"}; compare to [@FCPLT Remark 10.4].
:::

We collect a number of other facts from the work of Seidel [@Seidel:HMSgenus2] and Efimov [@Efimov]: let $V$ be a 3-dimensional vector space over ${\mathbb K}= {\mathbb C}$.

1.  The pentagram circles $\{L_1,\ldots, L_{2g+1}\}$, each equipped with the non-trivial $Spin$ structure, split-generate $\EuScript{F}(\Sigma_g)$ (this is an application of Proposition [\[Prop:Split-Generate-basic\]](#Prop:Split-Generate-basic){reference-type="ref" reference="Prop:Split-Generate-basic"});

2.  The Floer cohomology algebra $A=\bigoplus_{i,j=1}^{2g+1} HF(L_i, L_j) \cong \Lambda^*(V) \rtimes {\mathbb Z}/(2g+1)$, where $V$ is graded in degree 1 and the exterior algebra inherits the obvious ${\mathbb Z}/2$-grading;

3.  The $A_{\infty}$-structure $\EuScript{A}$ on $A=H(\EuScript{A})$ is characterised up to $A_{\infty}$-equivalence as follows: the operation $\mu^d = \mu^d_0 + \mu^d_1 + \ldots$ decomposes as a sum of pieces $\mu^d_i$ of degrees $6-3d+(4g-4)i$; and there is a basis $\{x_1, x_2, x_3\}$ for $V$ such that, if $e$ generates $\Lambda^0(V)$, then $$\label{Eqn:AinftyCurve}
    \mu^3_0 (x_1,x_2,x_3) = -e; \quad \mu^{2g+1}_1 (x_i, \ldots, x_i) = e \ \textrm{for} \  i\in \{1,2,3\}$$

4.  Concretely, the operation $\mu^d_i$ counts holomorphic polygons $u$ which, projected to ${\mathbb P}^1$, have $i = mult_u(\infty) + (1/2) mult_u(0)$.

The critical statement (3) is a "finite determinacy\" theorem, and at heart is an application of Theorem [\[Thm:KontsevichFormality\]](#Thm:KontsevichFormality){reference-type="ref" reference="Thm:KontsevichFormality"}. Unfortunately, we will not be able to use the result in quite this form: even working over ${\mathbb C}^*\subset {\mathbb P}^1$, the Fukaya category of the relative quadric $Z_{\neq 0,\infty}$ which appears later is not ${\mathbb Z}$-graded. However, Seidel and Efimov deduce this finite determinacy statement from the result in singularity theory that a power series with an isolated singularity at the origin is determined up to formal change of variables by a finite part of its Taylor series. This result does not rely on gradings, so we will reduce ourselves to applying that directly.

[^1]: Classically, $HH^*$ would be 2-periodic and we're just taking $HH^0$ and $HH^1$.
