Moreover, these maps must satisfy the following universal property: for any maps $f \colon S \rightarrow X$ and $g \colon T \rightarrow X$, there must be a unique map $h \colon S + T \rightarrow X$ making this diagram commute: $$\dgARROWLENGTH=0.5\dgARROWLENGTH \begin{diagram}[S + T] \node{S} \arrow{se,b}{i} \arrow{ese,t}{f} \\ \node[2]{S+T} \arrow{e,t}{h} \node{X} \\ \node{T} \arrow{ne,t}{j} \arrow{ene,b}{g} \end{diagram}$$ In other words, a map from a disjoint union of $S$ and $T$ to some set contains the same information as a pair of maps from $S$ and $T$ to that set.
