The refined local Langlands correspondence for a $p$-adic field $F$ posits an injection from irreducible smooth representations of $G(F)$, for $G$ reductive and quasi-split, to enhanced $L$-parameters $(\phi, \rho)$ where $\phi: \mathop{\mathrm{WD}}_{F} \rightarrow \prescript{L}{}{G}$ (here $\mathop{\mathrm{WD}}_{F} = W_{F} \times \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ is the $\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}_2$ form of the Weil--Deligne group) and $\rho$ is an algebraic irreducible representation of the centralizer group $S_{\phi}$ of the image of $\phi$. The image of the refined correspondence map should be contained in the set of pairs $(\phi, \rho)$ such that $\rho \in \mathop{\mathrm{Irr}}(\pi_0(S_{\phi}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma_F}))$. Vogan ([@VoganLLC]) proposed that in order to upgrade the correspondence to contain $\mathop{\mathrm{Irr}}(\pi_0(S_{\phi})) \subset \mathop{\mathrm{Irr}}(S_{\phi})$ in its image, one should enlarge the domain by adding in irreducible representations of inner forms of $G$. In [@BMO], Oi and the first named author showed that if a refined local Langlands correspondence exists for $G$ and its Levi subgroups, one can upgrade it to a bijection with the entire target $\mathop{\mathrm{Irr}}(S_{\phi})$ if one further allows irreducible representations of Levi subgroups of $G$ on the automorphic side. The question of exactly how to parametrize inner forms of $G$ (and possibly its Levi subgroups) in this context is a subtle one because the cohomology group $H^1(F, G_{\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}})$ is not sufficiently rigid, in the sense that translating by $G_{\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}}$-coboundaries identifies non-isomorphic representations of $G(F)$. However, there are many options for sufficiently rigid cohomology sets including the 'pure inner forms' given by $H^1(F, G)$, the 'extended pure inner forms' classified by the Kottwitz set $B(G)_{\mathop{\mathrm{bas}}}$, and finally 'rigid inner forms' defined by Kaletha in [@Kalannals] and corresponding to basic classes $H_{\mathop{\mathrm{bas}}}^1(\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}, G)$ of the rigid gerbe denoted $\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}$ in this paper. This last set has the advantage that it surjects onto $H^1(F, G_{\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}})$, whereas the others do not in general. For a nice discussion of this topic, we recommend the survey article [@KalSurvey].

One advantage that the Kottwitz set $B(G)_{\mathop{\mathrm{bas}}}$ (or more generally $B(G)$) has is that there is an explicit Tannakian category, namely that of $F$-isocrystals, $\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}_F$, whose objects are in bijection with $\coprod_n B(\mathrm{GL}_n)$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$. The category of $F$-isocrystals appears naturally in Dieudonné theory and hence has connections to local Shimura varieties. By the Tannakian formalism, we can thus view the isocrystal gerbe $\mathcal{E}_{\text{iso}}$ (cf. [@LR] where this is called the Dieudonné gerbe), whose algebraic cohomology is $B(G)$, as the Galois gerbe obtained from the gerbe of fiber functors of $\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}_{F}$ and, in the other direction, identify the tensor category $\mathop{\mathrm{Rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{iso}})$ with $\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}_{F}$ (see [@Taibi25 §7.1] for a summary of this formalism).

The goal of the present work is to construct a Tannakian category of "Rigid Isocrystals" $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_{F}$ which can be canonically identified with $\mathop{\mathrm{Rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}})$ and whose objects are classified by $\coprod_n H^1(\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}, \mathrm{GL}_n)$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Considered as a Galois gerbe, $\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}$ is an extension of $\Gamma_{\overline{F}/F}$ by $u(\overline{F})$, where $u$ is the pro-multiplicative group $\varprojlim_{E/F, n} \mathrm{Res}_{E/F} (\mu_n)$, where the limit is takes over all finite Galois extensions $E/F$ and all natural numbers $n$. We alert the reader that this group was originally defined in [@Kalannals] with $u'$ in place of $u$, where $$u' = \varprojlim_{E/F,n} \frac{\mathrm{Res}_{E/F} (\mu_n)}{\mu_{n}},$$ but one can use $u$ as well because the natural map $u \to u'$ is an isomorphism (see Remark [\[rem: Kalbbandvariant\]](#rem: Kalbbandvariant){reference-type="ref" reference="rem: Kalbbandvariant"}). Kaletha works with the $u'$-presentation of this group because it gives the correct object over $\mathbb{R}$ as well. Since we only consider $p$-adic fields, it suffices to consider the $u$-presentation.

The point of our article is to explain that since the gerbe $\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}$ can be constructed from the isocrystal gerbe with band $\mathbb{G}_m$ by a relatively simple sequence of operations, the same is true at the level of Tannakian categories. Let us describe the operations we need to consider. Beginning with the isocrystal gerbe with band $\mathbb{G}_m$, we pass to the subgroup $\mu_n$. At the level of Tannakian categories, this is a quotient operation and is realized by passing to a certain module category. After this, we take products to get a gerbe with band $\prod\limits_{\Gamma_{E/F}} \mu_{n,E}$ and then descend this gerbe to produce one with band $\mathrm{Res}_{E/F} (\mu_n)$. Finally, we take limits over $E, n$. By understanding just these four operations, products, modules, limits, and descent, we can produce a Tannakian category for the gerbe $\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}$.

::: thm
There is a Tannakian category $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_{F}$ whose associated gerbe of fiber functors represents $[\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}]$ [^1] and is described as follows. Its objects consists of tuples $(V, \tilde{\Phi}, n, E)$ where $n \in \mathbb{N}$, and $E/F$ is a finite Galois extension. The other components of this tuple are given as follows:

-   $(V,\mathcal{D})$ is a vector space over $L_{E}$ ,the completion of the maximal unramified extension of $E$, equipped a $\prod\limits_{\Gamma_{E/F}} \frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}$-grading $\mathcal{D}$ and an action of the ring $R_{E/F} := L_{E}[\prod \limits_{\Gamma_{E/F}} \mathbb{Z}]$ which is additive on the gradings and makes $V$ a finitely-generated $R_{E/F}$-module;

-   $\tilde{\Phi}$ is a semilinear action of $W_{L_E/F}$ on $V$ that acts in the natural way on the grading $\mathcal{D}$ and is compatible with the action of $W_{L_{E}/F}$ on $R_{E/F}$ (cf. §[\[sec:Descent\]](#sec:Descent){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:Descent"} for the description of this action) ;

-    There is an $L_E$-basis of the $(a_{\sigma})_{\sigma}$-graded piece of $V$ such that an $n$th power $\mathfrak{f}^n_E$ of a choice $\mathfrak{f}_{E}$ of the Frobenius of $E$ in $W_{L_{E}/F}$ acts by $\prod\limits_{\sigma \in \Gamma_{E/F}} \sigma(\varpi_E)^{a_{\sigma}}$, for $\varpi_E$ a fixed choice of anti-uniformizer.

Morphisms betweem two such objects in $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_{F}$ are $W_{L_{E}/F}$-equivariant graded morphisms of $R_{E/F}$-modules.
:::

In the final section, we discuss the structure of the Tannakian category $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_{F}$, which is semisimple, and give a number of examples of its simple objects. We point out that the situation differs fairly dramatically from that of $\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}_F$ concerning the role of basic objects---in $\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}_{F}$ (resp. $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_{F}$) these are the objects whose graded pieces are concentrated in one $\mathbb{Q}$- (resp. $\prod_{\Gamma_{E/F}} \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$-) degree and are important because they give inner forms of $\mathrm{GL}_{n}$. For $\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}_{F}$, the simple objects are all basic and are classified according to the Dieudonné--Manin classification by a rational slope $r/s \in \mathbb{Q}$ written in reduced form. A basic isocrystal is simple if $s$ equals the dimension of the underlying $L$-vector space.

In our situation, the simple objects of $\mathop{\mathrm{Rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}})$ need not be basic, but instead come from certain basic classes of $H^1(\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}, M)$ for $M$ an elliptic twisted Levi subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n$. Recall that twisted Levi subgroups are $F$-rational subgroups that become Levi subgroups upon base-change and are precisely the centralizers of $F$-rational tori. A twisted Levi subgroup is *elliptic* if the split rank of its center equals that of $G$, and the elliptic twisted Levi subgroups of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ are all isomorphic to $\mathrm{Res}_{E/F}\mathrm{GL}_s$ with $s[E \colon F] =n$. The set of basic classes of such a twisted Levi are in bijection with tuples $(a_i) \in (\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F}(E,\overline{F})}$ such that $\sum\limits_i a_i \in \frac{1}{s} \mathbb{Z}/ \mathbb{Z}$. In this paper we explain how to explicitly construct the simple object in $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_{F}$ corresponding to each such $E$ and $(a_{i})$. More precisely, we prove:

::: thm
Every isomorphism class of simple objects in $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_{F}$ may be represented by a pair $(E, (a_{i}))$ where $E/F$ is a finite separable field extension and the tuple $(a_{i})$ is an element of $(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F}(E,\overline{F})}$ which is not inflated from a tuple in $(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F}(K,\overline{F})}$ for any proper subextension $F/K/E$. For a fixed collection of finite extensions $\{E\}$ which have the same Galois closure $\widetilde{E}$ and are an orbit under $\Gamma_{\widetilde{E}/F}$, the group $\Gamma_{\widetilde{E}/F}$ acts on all such pairs involving $E' \in \{E\}$, and two arbitrary pairs give the same isomorphism class if and only if the extensions $E_{1}, E_{2}$ are related in this manner and the pairs lie in the same $\Gamma_{\widetilde{E}/F}$-orbit.
:::

In the case of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{iso}}}$, the explicit linear-algebraic description of $\mathop{\mathrm{Rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{iso}})$ has many uses, such as in the definition of crystalline cohomology, Dieudonné theory for $p$-divisible groups, and in the definition of the stack $\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}_G$ in Zhu's ([@Zhu25]) geometrization of the local Langlands correspondence. It is reasonable to ask analogous questions of $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_F$; namely if there is a natural cohomology theory valued in this Tannakian category and if this category leads to a more refined geometrization of the local Langlands correspondence. We do not take up these questions here, but we do relate $\mathrm{RigIsoc}_F$ to Fargues' category $\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}_{F}^e$ of extended isocrystals [@Fargues] and remark that steps towards a refined geomerization are taken in that paper.

[^1]: Of course, one can always take the Tannakian category $\mathop{\mathrm{Rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}})$ and recover $\mathcal{E}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Kal}}}$ as the associated gerbe, but the point of this result is to give a more explicit Tannakian category.
