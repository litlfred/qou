There are no critical points in X but there are two in S: the South pole and
the North pole. In each case the normal slice is an interval minus a point, so the
normal data is homotopy equivalent to (5°, S°), where S° is two points, one
higher than the other, and S° is the lower of the two points. For the South pole,
which has Morse index 0, the tangential data is a point, so the attachment is
(S°,S°), which is the addition of a disconnected point. Figure D.6 illustrates
that for -1 < a < 1, the space X<, is in fact the union of two contractible
components. The North pole has Morse index 2, so the tangential data at the
North pole is (D?, 0D’), a polar cap modulo its boundary. Taking the product
with the normal data gives two polar caps modulo all of the lower one and
the boundary of the upper one. This is the upper polar cap sewn down along
its boundary, the boundary being a point in one of the components. Thus, one
component becomes a sphere and the other remains contractible. 4

Suppose we have a closed space Y c R¢ whose complement X we view as
a stratified space with Morse function h. If p is a critical point for / in some
stratum S then there is a local coordinatization of Y as S X Bp, where By is a
small ball of dimension d — k and k is the dimension of S. The set Bp \ Y is
this ball minus the origin, so it is a cone over L(p) with vertex p. Any chain in
By \ Y may be brought arbitrarily close to p.
