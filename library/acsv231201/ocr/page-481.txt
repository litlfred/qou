Remark. Because we work with complex vector spaces, H,.(C) is sometimes
called homology with coefficients in C to distinguish it from the analogous
construction with integer coefficients. While the theory with integer coeffi-

To discuss the homology of a manifold, we must define an appropriate chain
complex. One natural candidate is the chain complex defined by smooth chains
together with the boundary map discussed in Section A.3 of Appendix A. For
many purposes, however, it is convenient to relax our smoothness condition.
If M is any Hausdorff topological space then a singular n-simplex in M is a
continuous (not necessarily smooth) map 7 : A” — M from the standard n-
simplex A” to M, and a singular n-chain in M is a complex linear combination
of singular n-simplices in M. Just as for smooth chains, we may use the natural
ordering of the faces of A” to define a canonical boundary map through (A.3.1),
taking a singular n-simplex to a singular (n— 1)-simplex and extending linearly
to singular chains. It is a foundational result in homology theory that the ho-
mology of a manifold is unchanged whether one studies smooth or singular
chains.
