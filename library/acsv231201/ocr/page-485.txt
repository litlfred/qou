The difficulties with singular homology for general topological spaces still
arise for A and simplicial complexes. However, the additional structure present
allows us to define a more rigid notion of homology. If X is a simplicial com-
plex then the simplicial homology of X is the homology defined by the chain
complex C whose n-chains are complex linear combinations of the n-simplices
in X, with the boundary map again defined by (A.3.1). Simplicial homology is
crucial for calculation, due to the following result.

Proposition B.5. [f X is a simplicial complex then the nth singular homol-
ogy group of X is isomorphic to the nth simplicial homology group of X. The
simplicial homology groups of X are algorithmically computable.

Proof See [Hat02, Theorem 2.27] for the statement on the equivalence of
the homologies, and [Mun84, Theorem 11.5] for an algorithm to determine
simplicial homology by representing the linear boundary maps of X as matrices
and computing their Smith normal forms. ia)

Among other corollaries, Proposition B.5 implies that a space which can
be represented by a A-complex with a finite number of simplices has finitely
generated homology groups. Although simplicial complexes have more struc-
ture than A-complexes, it can often be more efficient to represent a space as a
A-complex compared to representing it as a simplicial complex.
