In order to make the multivariate division algorithm deterministic, it is im-
portant to check divisibility of LT(f) by LT(g;) in the order the g; appear in L,
as the remainder returned by the division algorithm can be different for differ-
ent orderings of the g;. Related to this technicality is a large problem: even if
f can be written as a polynomial combination of the g;, meaning f lies in the
ideal J = (g1,...,m), it is possible for the multivariate division algorithm to
return a non-zero remainder. The trick to working around this difficulty is to
compute a particularly nice generating set for the ideal J = (g1,..., gm), which
also allows us to resolve many other questions about polynomial ideals.

Grobner bases

Fix a monomial order > and let / be an ideal in K[z], where K is algebraically
closed.

Definition 8.3. A Grébner basis for I with respect to the monomial order > is
a generating set G = {g1,..., gx} for J with the property that for any non-zero
f € 1 the leading term LT(f) is divisible by LT(g;) for some i. The basis is a
reduced Grébner basis if for all i # j no monomial of g; is divisible by LT(g;).

Every non-zero ideal has a Grébner basis with respect to any ordering, re-
duced Grébner bases are unique and algorithmically computable, and algo-
rithms to find them have been implemented in most computer algebra systems.
The size and computation time of finding a Grobner basis can depend heavily
on the term order used. If G is a Grobner basis for J and f € K[z] then

The first two items here follow directly from the properties of Grébner bases,
while the third item is Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz [CLOO7, Section 4.1]. The
fourth item is proved in [CLOO7, Section 3.5] and the final item, sometimes
known as the elimination theorem, can be found in [CLO07, Section 3.1]. The
elimination theorem, which shows how lexicographical Grébner bases general-

us to conveniently encode the solutions of a polynomial system.
