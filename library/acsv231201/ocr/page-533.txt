Stratifications

As a first attempt to perform calculus on an algebraic variety V, one might
partition V into a finite disjoint union of smooth sets and then work on each
piece of the partition. Although such smooth partitions can be easily under-
stood, and easily computed using standard algebro-geometric techniques, they
are not sufficient for our (and many other) purposes. The problem is that the
pieces in an arbitrary smooth partition may not fit together nicely; among other
difficulties, this means the local behavior of V near points in the same piece of
the partition can be very different.

This definition is well crafted: the conditions are easy to fulfill — for ex-
ample, every algebraic variety admits a Whitney stratification, see [Whi65b,
Theorem 18.11] or [Hir73, Theorem 4.8] — and the conditions have strong con-
sequences (for instance, they are strong enough for stratified Morse theorems
to hold). Stratifications of algebraic varieties are also effectively computable.
