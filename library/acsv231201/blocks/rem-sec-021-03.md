The function (1−z)−1/2 is analytic on the slit plane C\{x ∈R : x ≥1}, which we may view as half of the Riemann surface R obtained by gluing two copies of the slit plane, with the upper half of one attaching along the slit to the lower half of the other. The change of variables z = 1 −y2 maps from R to C. The saddle point contour ˜E, when mapped to the z-plane, comes in to +1 along one copy of the slit, does a U-turn, and goes back along the other copy of the slit.
