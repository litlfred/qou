When φ is one-to-one, attachments are more or less the same as unions; that is, both X and Y naturally embed in the attachment and their union covers the attachment. In general, an attachment can be thought of as a union of a space X with a quotient Y/φ, where points y1, y2 ∈Y0 are identiﬁed if φ(y1) = φ(y2). While the two spaces in a union play symmetric roles, the attachment of a space Y to a space X is described asymmetrically. This coincides with the framework of ﬁltered spaces built up by successive attachments, described in the next appendix. Thus attachments, while not entirely new, provide a useful way to build up a space.
