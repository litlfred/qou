Let Q1(x, y) = xy −z3 and Q2(x, y) = xy. Both varieties contain the origin, where they have leading homogeneous term xy. However, the origin is a multiple point of VQ2 but not a multiple point of VQ1. This can be established, for instance, by varying a line parallel to the z-axis through x = y = α as α traces out a small circle around the origin. The three roots are the three values of α2/3, and permute cyclically when α makes one loop around the origin. Hence the local monodromy group is not the trivial group.
