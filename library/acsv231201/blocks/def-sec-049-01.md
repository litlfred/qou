In this section we always assume that V is smooth and Q is square-free, so that for every (x, y) ∈V, at least one of Qx(x, y) and Qy(x, y) is non-zero, and that the set of critical points is ﬁnite. If Q is not square-free then our arguments characterizing the singularities that determine asymptotics still hold when Q is replaced by its square-free part. In any number of variables, a potential program to determine asymptotics is the following. 1. Explicitly compute a cycle representing the intersection class. 2. Try to push the cycle below each critical point, starting at the highest.
