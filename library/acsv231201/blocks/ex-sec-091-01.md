33. The 2-sphere from Example C.29 is a cell complex with one 2-cell and one 0-cell; as noted above, up to homotopy equivalence, there is only one choice for the attachment map. The 2-torus from Example C.30 is a cell complex with one 0-cell, two 1-cells, and one 2-cell. Up to homotopy equivalence, the one skeleton must be the wedge of two circles. There are a number of ways to attach a 2-cell to a wedge of two circles, and the right attaching map can be worked out by knowing what the boundary (i.e., the level set ε below the maximum height) looks like. ◁
