[]{#sub.habmat label="sub.habmat"}

In Section [\[sec.RQMC\]](#sec.RQMC){reference-type="ref" reference="sec.RQMC"}, we saw how successive refinements of the original quantum modularity conjecture [\[QMCb\]](#QMCb){reference-type="eqref" reference="QMCb"} led to a whole matrix $\mathbf{J}^{(K)}(x)$ of generalized Kashaev invariants and to a collection of matrices of formal power series having  as their constant term. The existence of these new matrices and the description of their properties is the main content of this paper. We emphasize that, although the refined QMC which led to the definition of these matrices and to the means of finding them numerically is still conjectural, the matrices themselves are well-defined, at least in terms of a chosen triangulation: Their first columns are trivial (a one followed by $r$ zeros). Their second columns were defined in Section [\[sec.Phi\]](#sec.Phi){reference-type="ref" reference="sec.Phi"} in terms of the original Kashaev invariant and of the perturbative series defined in [@DG; @DG2]. The further columns of the $\mathbf\Phi$-matrix can also be given by Gauss-type integrals like those in [@DG; @DG2], and in principle one could also always find explicit formulas for the $\mathbf{J}$ matrix, as has been written out for the $4_1$ knot in detail in Section [\[sub.4.3\]](#sub.4.3){reference-type="ref" reference="sub.4.3"} (equations [\[Q0for41\]](#Q0for41){reference-type="eqref" reference="Q0for41"} and [\[Q1for41\]](#Q1for41){reference-type="eqref" reference="Q1for41"}) and will be discussed more generally in Sections [\[sub.qholo\]](#sub.qholo){reference-type="ref" reference="sub.qholo"}--[\[sub.statesum\]](#sub.statesum){reference-type="ref" reference="sub.statesum"} in the context of $q$-holonomic systems, with full details for the knot $5_2$. In general, it is not known that these quantities are topological invariants, since their definitions depend a priori on the choice of an ideal triangulation and are believed but not proven to be invariant under Pachner moves. But we expect this invariance to be true, and in any case the new matrices are completely computable in practice, as we seen, and have extremely interesting properties. In this subsection, we look at the properties that are directly visible, and in the following one at the deeper properties of the associated cocycle $W$.

**Extension property.** From their very definitions, the matrices $\mathbf{J}$ and $\mathbf\Phi$ (now omitting the knot from the notation) both have a $(1+r)\times(1+r)$ block triangular form $$\label{Jblock}
\mathbf{J}(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & Q(x) \\
 0 & \mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(x) \end{pmatrix} , \qquad
\mathbf\Phi_\alpha(h) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \mathcal{Q}\bigl(\mathbf{e}(\alpha){\rm e}^{-h}\bigr) \\
0 & \mathbf\Phi^{\text{red}}_\alpha(h) \end{pmatrix} ,$$ where is the vector of length $r$ whose entries are given by the periodic functions found in Section [\[sec.RQMC\]](#sec.RQMC){reference-type="ref" reference="sec.RQMC"}, is the corresponding function in terms of ${q=\mathbf{e}(x)}$ (which we believe to be elements of the Habiro ring $\mathcal H\otimes\mathbb Q$ and therefore to be defined not just at roots of unity, but also at infinitesimal deformations of roots of unity), and $\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}$ and $\mathbf\Phi^{\text{red}}_\alpha$ are $r\times r$ matrices with rows and columns indexed by the elements of $\mathcal{P}^{\text{red}}_K$. We are mainly interested in the larger matrices, but we will sometimes want to consider the "reduced" matrices separately because they sometimes occur separately (notably in Section [\[sub.quad\]](#sub.quad){reference-type="ref" reference="sub.quad"}, where only $\mathcal{P}^{\text{red}}_K$ occurs in [\[quadrel\]](#quadrel){reference-type="eqref" reference="quadrel"}, and in the statements below about the inverse matrices of $\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}$ and $\mathbf\Phi_\alpha^{\text{red}}$). This block triangular property, trivial though it is, should have a deeper meaning as the statement that the $(r+1)$-dimensional objects associated to a knot $K$ (specifically, the $q$-holonomic modules that will be the subject of Section [\[sec.NewHolonomy\]](#sec.NewHolonomy){reference-type="ref" reference="sec.NewHolonomy"}), with a basis parametrized by the set of representations $\mathcal P_K$, are in fact extensions of $r$-dimensional objects with a basis parametrized by $\mathcal{P}^{\text{red}}_K$ by something one-dimensional.

**Behavior under complex conjugation.** The next point is the following compatibility with complex conjugation, namely $$\label{Jsym1}
\overline{\mathbf{J}^{\sigma,\sigma'}(-x)}
= \begin{cases} \hphantom{i}\mathbf{J}^{\sigma,\sigma'}(x)& \text{if $\sigma$ is real,} \\
 -{\rm i}\mathbf{J}^{\overline\sigma,\sigma'}(x)&\text{if $\sigma$ is not real,}\end{cases}$$ where "$\sigma$ real" means $\sigma=\overline\sigma$. In matrix form this becomes $$\label{Jsym}
\overline{\mathbf{J}(-x)} = B \mathbf{J}(x) ,$$ where $B$ is the unimodular symmetric unitary matrix with $B^{(\sigma,\sigma')}$ equal to 1 if ${\sigma'=\sigma=\overline\sigma}$, to $-{\rm i}$ if $\sigma'=\overline\sigma\ne\sigma$, and to 0 if $\sigma'\ne\overline\sigma$. The symmetry [\[Jsym1\]](#Jsym1){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jsym1"} has as the special case ${(\sigma,\sigma')=(\sigma_0,\sigma_1)}$ the behavior $\overline{J(-x)} = J(x)$ of the Kashaev invariant for rational numbers $x$ under complex conjugation, which holds because the colored Jones polynomials have real (even integer) coefficients or alternatively because $J$ is an element of the Habiro ring. The same argument applies conjecturally to all $J^{(\sigma_0,\sigma)}$, since they also belong to the Habiro ring, and if we use the full RQMC it also suffices to establish the general case. Actually, equation [\[Jsym\]](#Jsym){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jsym"} can be strengthened to $$\label{Jsymb}
\overline{\mathbf\Phi_{-\alpha}\big(\overline h\big)} = B  \mathbf\Phi_{\alpha}(h),$$ which specializes at $h=0$ to [\[Jsym\]](#Jsym){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jsym"}. We remark that equation [\[Jsymb\]](#Jsymb){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jsymb"} remains true if we replace both $\mathbf\Phi$'s by their completions $\mathbf{\widehat\Phi}$ (except for the top rows, which differ by a factor of ${\rm i}$), because ${\overline{V(\sigma)}=V(\overline\sigma)}$ for all $\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{red}}_K$.

**Unimodularity.** The next statement, generalizing equation [\[FirstBilin\]](#FirstBilin){reference-type="eqref" reference="FirstBilin"}, is that the matrices $\mathbf{J}$ and even $\mathbf\Phi^{\text{red}}$, are experimentally found to be unimodular. More precisely, this is definitely true for the $4_1$ and $5_2$ knots, for which we have closed formulas for all of the entries of the Habiro-like matrices and can compute numerically; for other knots, we are convinced, and willing to conjecture, that the determinant is $\pm1$, but we have no really convincing reason except aesthetics that it should always be $+1$. The unimodularity implies in particular that the $\mathbf{J}$-matrices are always invertible, a fact that is of course crucial even to define the cocycle $W$ in [\[Wdef\]](#Wdef){reference-type="eqref" reference="Wdef"}. Notice that it is compatible with [\[Jsym\]](#Jsym){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jsym"} and [\[Jsymb\]](#Jsymb){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jsymb"}, since the matrix $B$ is unimodular.

**Inverse/Unitarity.** The final property that we want to mention, again only conjectural, is more surprising. This is that the inverse of $\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}$ (but not of the full matrix $\mathbf{J}$, for which we have no corresponding guess) can be given explicitly by the formula $$\label{Orthogonality1}
\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(x)^t \mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(-x) = \overline{B^{\text{red}}} ,$$ where we have set $B=\bigl(\smallmatrix 1&0\\0&B^{\text{red}}\endsmallmatrix\bigr)$. In fact, even this statement can be strengthened, namely to $$\label{Orthogonality2}
\mathbf\Phi^{\text{red}}_\alpha(h)^t \mathbf\Phi^{\text{red}}_{-\alpha}(-h) = \overline{B^{\text{red}}} ,$$ which specializes to [\[Orthogonality1\]](#Orthogonality1){reference-type="eqref" reference="Orthogonality1"} when we set $h=0$. In view of [\[Jsym\]](#Jsym){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jsym"}, the first of these equations can be rewritten as $$\overline{\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(x)^t} B^{\text{red}}\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(x) = B^{\text{red}},$$ which we see as a kind of unitarity or rather sesqui-unitarity, since if $B^{\text{red}}$ were the identity matrix they would simply say that the matrices $\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(x)$ and $\mathbf\Phi^{\text{red}}_\alpha(h)$ are unitary.

Note that equation [\[Orthogonality2\]](#Orthogonality2){reference-type="eqref" reference="Orthogonality2"} remains true also if we replace $\Phi$ by $\widehat\Phi$, since the volume factors cancel, and also that the very special case $\sigma=\sigma'=\sigma_1$, for which the right-hand side of [\[Orthogonality2\]](#Orthogonality2){reference-type="eqref" reference="Orthogonality2"} vanishes, is just the quadratic relation [\[quadrel\]](#quadrel){reference-type="eqref" reference="quadrel"} that was discussed in Section [\[sub.quad\]](#sub.quad){reference-type="ref" reference="sub.quad"}. It is also worth remarking explicitly that the expression on the left of [\[Orthogonality2\]](#Orthogonality2){reference-type="eqref" reference="Orthogonality2"} is a priori an element of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_c)[[h]]$ ($c=\mathrm{den}(\alpha)$), at least if the predicted algebraic properties of the power series as discussed in Section [\[sub.field\]](#sub.field){reference-type="ref" reference="sub.field"} are true, because the extra factors (root of unity and $c$-th root of an $S$-unit in $F_{\sigma''}(\zeta_c)$) cancel in the products $\Phi^{(\sigma'',\sigma)}(h)\Phi^{(\sigma'',\sigma')}(-h)$ and because the sum over $\sigma''$ implicit in the matrix multiplication gets us from $F_{\sigma''}(\zeta_c)$ down to $\mathbb Q(\zeta_c)$. Finally, we should mention that equation [\[Orthogonality1\]](#Orthogonality1){reference-type="eqref" reference="Orthogonality1"} also gives us a formula for the inverse of the full matrix $\mathbf{J}(x)$, because of the block triangular form of the latter as given in [\[Jblock\]](#Jblock){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jblock"}, namely $$\mathbf{J}(x)^{-1}= \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -Q(x)B^{\text{red}}\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(-x)^t \\
 0 & B^{\text{red}}\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(-x)^t \end{pmatrix} ,$$ in which the elements of the top row are bilinear in the entries of $\mathbf{J}(x)$ and $\mathbf{J}(-x)$ rather than merely linear as is the case for the other rows.

However, the real interest to us of the final point above is not just that there are explicit formulas for the inverses of the matrices $\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}$ (or even $\mathbf{J}$) and $\mathbf\Phi$, but above all that the inverse of $\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}$ is expressed *linearly* (more correctly, sesquilinearly) in terms of the entries of the matrix itself. This means in particular that the entries of the cocycle $W^{\text{red}}$ (the bottom right $r\times r$ block of $W$) are expressed *bilinearly* in terms of those of $\mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}$. This remark will come into its own in the sequel [@GZ:qseries], where this reduced cocycle will arise in a completely different way as a bilinear combination in functions of $q={\rm e}^{2\pi\tau}$ and $\widetilde q={\rm e}^{-2\pi/\tau}$ as a consequence of the factorization of state integrals.

We end this subsection by listing the properties of the function $W_\gamma$ that it inherits by virtue of its definition [\[Wdef\]](#Wdef){reference-type="eqref" reference="Wdef"} from the corresponding properties of $\mathbf{J}$ listed above. These will become important in the next subsection, when we extend $W_\gamma$ from $\mathbb Q$ to $\mathbb R$.

The "extension property" is immediate: the matrix $W_\gamma(x)$ has the block triangular form for an $r\times r$ "reduced" matrix $W_\gamma^{\text{red}}(x)$ which is again a cocycle. The complex conjugation property for $W$ takes the form $$\label{Wsym1}
\overline{W_\gamma(x)} = W_{\varepsilon\gamma\varepsilon}(-x)$$ for all $\gamma\in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and $x \in \mathbb Q$, where $\varepsilon=\bigl(\smallmatrix-1&0\\\hphantom{-}0&1\endsmallmatrix\bigr)$. (Note that $\varepsilon\gamma\varepsilon\in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$.) This is a consequence of the following short calculation using [\[Jsym\]](#Jsym){reference-type="eqref" reference="Jsym"} and the easy conjugation behavior of the automorphy factor : $$\overline{W_\gamma(x)} = \overline{\mathbf{J}(x)^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbf{j}}_\gamma(-x)\mathbf{J}(\gamma(-x))} =
\mathbf{J}(x)^{-1} B^{-1} \overline{\widetilde{\mathbf{j}}_\gamma(-x)} B \mathbf{J}(-\gamma(x)) = W_{\varepsilon\gamma\varepsilon}(-x) .$$ A nice consequence of [\[Wsym1\]](#Wsym1){reference-type="eqref" reference="Wsym1"} is that we can now extend equation [\[WRQMC\]](#WRQMC){reference-type="eqref" reference="WRQMC"}, which described the asymptotics of $W_\gamma(X)$ as $X$ tends to infinity with bounded denominator on the assumption of the RQMC, to give the corresponding asymptotic behavior of $W_\gamma(X)$ also as $X\to-\infty$: $$\label{NegativeX}
W_\gamma(X) \approx B \mathbf{\widehat\Phi}_{a/c}{\left(\frac{2\pi {\rm i}}{c(cX+d)}\right)}^{^{-1}},
\qquad X\to-\infty .$$ The third property is that the determinant of $W_\gamma(x)$ is given by $$\det W_\gamma(x) = |j(\gamma,x)|^{-3/2} ,$$ where $j(\gamma,x)$ is defined as $cx+d$ for $\gamma=\bigl(\smallmatrix a&b\\c&d\endsmallmatrix\bigr)$. This follows from the (conjectural) unimodularity of $\mathbf{J}$, the definition of , and the fact that vanishes ("Galois descent"). Finally, from [\[Orthogonality1\]](#Orthogonality1){reference-type="eqref" reference="Orthogonality1"} we immediately deduce the corresponding formula for the inverse matrix of $W^{\text{red}}_\gamma(x)$: $$W^{\text{red}}_\gamma(x)^{-1} = \overline{B^{\text{red}}} W^{\text{red}}_{\varepsilon\gamma\varepsilon}(-x)^t .$$ In this connection, we note that [\[Wdef\]](#Wdef){reference-type="eqref" reference="Wdef"} and [\[Orthogonality1\]](#Orthogonality1){reference-type="eqref" reference="Orthogonality1"} also imply that $$W^{\text{red}}_\gamma(x) = \big(\overline{B^{\text{red}}}\big)^{-1} \mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(-\gamma x)^t \widetilde{\mathbf{j}}^{\text{red}}_\gamma(x) \mathbf{J}^{\text{red}}(x)
  .$$ In other words, $W^{\text{red}}$ is bilinear in the entries of the matrices $\mathbf{J}$, an important property that is also shared by the functions defined by state integrals.
