[]{#sub:dg schemes label="sub:dg schemes"}

Because the quotient in [\[eqn:defhilbert\]](#eqn:defhilbert){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:defhilbert"} is taken in the sense of GIT, the Hilbert scheme is a quasi-projective variety. But let us neglect its interesting structure as a topological space, and describe its ring of functions locally. By definition, the locus of cyclic triples $(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}$ is an open subset of affine space, and the moment map [\[eqn:defmoment\]](#eqn:defmoment){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:defmoment"} gives rise to a section of the trivial $\mathfrak{g}$ bundle: $$\mu \in \Gamma \left( \mathcal{O}_{(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}} \otimes \mathfrak{g}\right)$$ over $(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}$. We may write down the Koszul complex corresponding to this section: $$\left( \wedge^\bullet \mathfrak{g}, \mu \right) := \left[ \mathcal{O}_{(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}} \otimes \wedge^{\dim G} \mathfrak{g}\stackrel{\mu}\longrightarrow ... \stackrel{\mu}\longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}} \otimes \mathfrak{g}\stackrel{\mu}\longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}} \right]$$ Since the Hilbert scheme is smooth, this complex is exact except at the rightmost cohomology group, where it is isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}_{\mu^{-1}(0)^\text{cyc}}$. Moreover, since all the maps are $G$--equivariant, we may write locally: $$\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Hilb}_n} \stackrel{\mathrm{q.i.s.}}\cong \left( \wedge^\bullet \mathrm{adj}_\mathfrak{g}, \mu \right) = \left[ \wedge^{\dim G} \mathrm{adj}_\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{\mu}\longrightarrow ... \stackrel{\mu}\longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}/G} \right]$$ where $\mathrm{adj}_\mathfrak{g}$ denotes the vector bundle on $(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}/G$, obtained by descending the trivial vector bundle $\mathfrak{g}$ on $\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V$, endowed with the $G$--action by conjugation. One may write down the analogous Koszul complex for the map $\nu$ of [\[eqn:def nu\]](#eqn:def nu){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:def nu"}, but observe that: $$\label{eqn:koszul flag}
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{FHilb}_n(\mathbb{C}^2)} \textbf{ is not } \stackrel{\mathrm{q.i.s.}}\cong \left( \wedge^\bullet \mathrm{adj}_\mathfrak{n}, \nu \right) := \left[ \wedge^{\dim N} \mathrm{adj}_\mathfrak{n}\stackrel{\nu}\longrightarrow ... \stackrel{\nu}\longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V \times \text{Fl})^\text{cyc}/G} \right]$$ (recall that $\mathrm{adj}_\mathfrak{n}$ denotes the vector bundle on $(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V \times \text{Fl})^\text{cyc}/G$, obtained by descending the vector bundle $\mathrm{Adj}_\mathfrak{n}$ on $\text{Fl}$, endowed with the $G$--action by conjugation). The fact that the Koszul complex [\[eqn:koszul flag\]](#eqn:koszul flag){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:koszul flag"} is not exact anymore boils down to the fact that $\mathrm{FHilb}_n(\mathbb{C}^2)$ is not a local complete intersection, and so we choose to work instead with the dg scheme: $$\label{eqn:def dg zero}
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{FHilb}_n^\text{dg}(\mathbb{C}^2)} := \left( \wedge^\bullet \mathrm{adj}_\mathfrak{n}, \nu \right)$$ Note that we think of the left hand side as a sheaf of dg algebras, given precisely by the complex in [\[eqn:koszul flag\]](#eqn:koszul flag){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:koszul flag"} supported on the smooth scheme $(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V \times \text{Fl})^\text{cyc}/G$, which is nothing but a flag variety bundle over the smooth scheme $(\mathfrak{g}\times \mathfrak{g}\times V)^\text{cyc}/G$. This will allow us to ignore the subtleties of the topology of dg schemes.
