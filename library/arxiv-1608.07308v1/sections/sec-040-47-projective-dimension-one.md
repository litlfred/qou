For the setting of this paper, we will need a version of Proposition [\[prop:lift0\]](#prop:lift0){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:lift0"} when the vector bundle $\mathcal{V}$ is replaced by the quotient: $$0 \longrightarrow\mathcal{W}\stackrel{\psi}\longrightarrow \mathcal{V}\longrightarrow \mathcal{Q}\longrightarrow 0$$ where $\mathcal{W}$ is another vector bundle. More precisely, we are interested in the case when: $$Y \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}^\vee$$ is the (derived) zero locus of the section: $$\label{eqn:section}
s : \pi^*(\mathcal{W}) \stackrel{\psi}\longrightarrow \pi^*(\mathcal{V}) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}(1)$$ where $\pi$ is the map in the following diagram: $$\label{eqn:iota}
\includegraphics{4_29.png}$$ To simplify the geometry, we make the following very important assumption: $$\label{eqn:avramov}
\text{the ideal of }Y \stackrel{j}\hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}^\vee \text{ is generated by a regular sequence in Im }s$$ which entails that the embedding $\psi$ cuts out $Y$ as a complete intersection in $\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}^\vee$. One could do without this assumption, but that would require one to replace $Y$ with the dg scheme determined by the exterior power of the section $s$. In other words, we must require the following quasi-isomorphism in the derived category of $\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}^\vee$: $$\label{eqn:rivers}
\mathcal{O}_Y \cong \left[ ... \stackrel{s}\longrightarrow \wedge^k \pi^*(\mathcal{W}) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-k) \stackrel{s}\longrightarrow ... \stackrel{s}\longrightarrow \mathcal{O}\right]$$ In order to construct the lift $\iota''$ in [\[eqn:iota\]](#eqn:iota){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:iota"}, we must first construct the arrow $\iota'$, and for this we invoke Proposition [\[prop:lift0\]](#prop:lift0){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:lift0"}. Then the following Proposition says precisely when the arrow $\iota'$ thus defined factors through $Y$.

::: proposition
[]{#prop:lift label="prop:lift"} Suppose that $Y \stackrel{j}\hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}$ as in [\[eqn:iota\]](#eqn:iota){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:iota"} and that the map $\iota$ is constructed. The datum of the extension $\iota''$ is equivalent to an invertible object $F \in \mathcal{C}$ together with an arrow: $$\label{eqn:datum}
\iota^* \mathcal{Q}\stackrel{\beta}\longrightarrow F$$ in $\mathcal{C}$. This gives $\mathcal{C}$ the structure of a category over $Y$ if and only if: $$\label{eqn:koszul}
\left[ ... \stackrel{\beta}\longrightarrow \iota^*\left(\wedge^k \mathcal{Q}\right) \otimes F^{-k} \stackrel{\beta}\longrightarrow ... \right] \stackrel{\emph{h.e.}}\cong 0$$ The map $\iota''$ is birational if and only if $\iota_*$ gives rise to an isomorphism: $$\label{eqn:properproj}
S^k \mathcal{Q}\cong \iota_*(F^k) \qquad \forall \ k\geq 0$$
:::

Note that if we interpret $Y$ as a dg scheme whose structure sheaf is the dg algebra in the right hand side of [\[eqn:rivers\]](#eqn:rivers){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:rivers"}, we must replace $\mathcal{Q}$ in [\[eqn:datum\]](#eqn:datum){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:datum"}, [\[eqn:koszul\]](#eqn:koszul){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:koszul"}, [\[eqn:properproj\]](#eqn:properproj){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:properproj"} with the two term complex $[\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}]$. Making sense of the symmetric and exterior powers of such a complex is rather straightforward homological algebra, which we relegate to the Appendix.

::: proof
*Proof.* As we have seen in Proposition [\[prop:lift0\]](#prop:lift0){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:lift0"}, the existence of a monoidal functor: $${\iota'}^*: D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}^\vee)) \rightarrow K^b(\mathcal{C})$$ implies the datum of an invertible object $F \in \mathcal{C}$ (the image of $\mathcal{O}(1)$) together with an arrow $\iota^*\mathcal{V}\rightarrow F$ in $\mathcal{C}$ (the image of the tautological morphism). The question is when does the functor ${\iota'}^*$ factor through: $$D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}^\vee)) \stackrel{j^*}\longrightarrow D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))$$ $$M \mapsto M \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}^\vee}} \mathcal{O}_Y =  \left[ ... \stackrel{s}\longrightarrow \wedge^k \pi^*(\mathcal{W}) \otimes M(-k) \stackrel{s}\longrightarrow ... \stackrel{s}\longrightarrow M \right]$$ where in the last equality we have used the assumption [\[eqn:avramov\]](#eqn:avramov){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:avramov"}. In particular, we have: $$\includegraphics{before_4_35.png}$$ This implies that the functor ${\iota''}^*$ must take the composition $\pi^*(\mathcal{W}) \stackrel{\psi}\hookrightarrow \pi^*(\mathcal{V}) \rightarrow \mathcal{O}(1)$ to zero, and hence the map $\alpha$ of [\[eqn:datum0\]](#eqn:datum0){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:datum0"} must factor through a map $\beta$ as in [\[eqn:datum\]](#eqn:datum){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:datum"}. Sending the Koszul complex of $\beta$ though the functor $j^*$ gives rise to the Koszul complex of $\alpha$, which must be sent to 0 by [\[eqn:koszul0\]](#eqn:koszul0){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:koszul0"}. Therefore, we conclude that the existence of the extension ${\iota''}^*$ requires [\[eqn:koszul\]](#eqn:koszul){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:koszul"}. Finally, recall that being birational is equivalent to $\iota''_*\mathbf{1} \cong \mathcal{O}_Y$. The projection formula implies that $\iota''_*(F^\bullet) \cong \mathcal{O}_Y(k)$, and applying $j_*$ to this isomorphism yields: $$\iota'_*(F^\bullet) \cong \left[ ... \stackrel{s}\longrightarrow \wedge^k \pi^*(\mathcal{W}) \otimes \mathcal{O}(\bullet-k) \stackrel{s}\longrightarrow ... \right]$$ Applying $\pi_*$ to the above isomorphism implies: $$\label{eqn:luch}
\iota_*(F^\bullet) \cong \Big[ \wedge\mathcal{W}\otimes S \ \mathcal{V}\Big]^\bullet$$ where the differential in the right hand side of [\[eqn:luch\]](#eqn:luch){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:luch"} is given by the map $\psi:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow \mathcal{V}$. As in Example [\[ex:important\]](#ex:important){reference-type="ref" reference="ex:important"}, the right hand side is a resolution of $S^\bullet \mathcal{Q}$, hence we obtain [\[eqn:properproj\]](#eqn:properproj){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:properproj"}. ◻
:::

## Deducing Conjecture [\[conj:1\]](#conj:1){reference-type="ref" reference="conj:1"} from Conjecture [\[conj:main\]](#conj:main){reference-type="ref" reference="conj:main"} {#sub:proof}

The categorical setup presented in this section allows one to deduce the main conjecture from Conjecture [\[conj:main\]](#conj:main){reference-type="ref" reference="conj:main"} (a)--(c). We will proceed by induction on $n$, so let assume that the functors [\[eqn:funk\]](#eqn:funk){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:funk"} are well-defined for some fixed $n$. Our task is to construct functors: $$K^b(\mathrm{SBim}_{n+1}) \xtofrom[{\iota}_{n+1}^*]{\iota_{n+1*}}   D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{FHilb}^\text{dg}_{n+1}))$$ given the functors: $$K^b(\mathrm{SBim}_{n})[x_{n+1}] \xtofrom[\iota_n^*]{\iota_{n*}} D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{FHilb}^\text{dg}_{n} \times \mathbb{C}) )$$ obtained from the inductive hypothesis and tensoring with the extra variable $x_{n+1}$. We define the composed functors: $$\iota_* \ : \ K^b(\mathrm{SBim}_{n+1}) \xtofrom[I]{\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}}  \mathrm{SBim}_{n}[x_{n+1}] \xtofrom[{\iota_n}^*]{\iota_{n*}} D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{FHilb}^\text{dg}_{n} \times \mathbb{C})) \ : \ \iota^*$$ According to Proposition [\[prop:dg scheme\]](#prop:dg scheme){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:dg scheme"}, we have $\mathrm{FHilb}^\text{dg}_{n+1} = \mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}_n$, where $\mathcal{E}_n$ is the complex on $\mathrm{FHilb}^\text{dg}_n \times \mathbb{C}$ from [\[eqn:complex\]](#eqn:complex){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:complex"}. Relation [\[eqn:two step\]](#eqn:two step){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:two step"} states that this complex has projective dimension 1, and we can therefore apply Proposition [\[prop:lift\]](#prop:lift){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:lift"}. To do so, we must exhibit an invertible object $F \in \mathrm{SBim}_{n+1}$ and a morphism: $$\iota^* \mathcal{E}_n \stackrel{\beta}\longrightarrow F$$ in $K^b(\mathrm{SBim}_n)$. We will choose $F = L_{n+1}$ and take the morphism $\beta$ to be the adjoint of [\[eqn:conjmain\]](#eqn:conjmain){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:conjmain"}: $$\mathcal{E}_n = \iota_*(L_{n+1}) = \iota_{n*} \left( \mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(L_{n+1}) \right)$$ The full statement of [\[eqn:conjmain\]](#eqn:conjmain){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:conjmain"} allows one to prove that $S^k(\mathcal{E}_n) = \iota_*(L_{n+1}^k)$, which establishes the fact that $\mathrm{SBim}_{n+1}$ is birational over $\mathrm{FHilb}_{n+1}$ by [\[eqn:properproj\]](#eqn:properproj){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:properproj"}. To complete the proof of Conjecture [\[conj:1\]](#conj:1){reference-type="ref" reference="conj:1"} one needs to also check that [\[eqn:koszul\]](#eqn:koszul){reference-type="eqref" reference="eqn:koszul"} holds, which is part (c) of Conjecture [\[conj:main\]](#conj:main){reference-type="ref" reference="conj:main"}.
