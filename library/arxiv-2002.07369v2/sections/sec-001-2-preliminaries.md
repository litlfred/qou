[]{#pre label="pre"} Let $K$ be a field, $K\langle X\rangle$ be the non-commutative polynomial algebra generated by $X$ where $X=\{x_1,...,x_n\}$ is a finite set. Let $X^*=\{u|u=x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_m},m\ge 0, i_1,\cdots,i_m \in \{1,\cdots,n\}\}$ be the monoid generated by $X$, and define the identity by the empty word which is denoted by $1$. Denote the degree of a word $w\in X^*$ by $|w|$.

::: definition
A total order $\sigma$ on the set $X^*$ is called well-ordering, if every non-empty subset of $X^*$ has a minimal element under the order $\sigma$.
:::

::: definition
A well-ordering $\sigma$ on the set $X^*$ is called monomial, if for every $u,v\in X^*$, $$u<_\sigma v \iff w_1uw_2<_\sigma w_1vw_2,\forall w_1,w_2\in X^*.$$
:::

::: definition
The deg-lex order on the $X^*$ is defined as follows:

$\forall ~ w_1=x_{i_1}...x_{i_m},w_2=x_{j_1}...x_{j_s}\in X^*$, $$w_1<_{deg-lex}w_2 \iff or\mbox{ } m<s, or \mbox{ }m=s,w_1<_{lex}w_2.$$
:::

::: example
For the set $X=\{x_1,x_2,x_3\}$, under the deg-lex order, we have $$1<x_1<x_2<x_3<x_1^2<x_1x_2<x_1x_3<x_2x_1<x_2^2<x_2x_3<\cdots ,$$ obviously, under the deg-lex order, $X^*$ is a well-ordering set, and the deg-lex order is monomial.
:::

Under the monomial order $\sigma$ on $X^*$, every polynomial $f\in K\langle X\rangle$ can be uniquely represented as $$f=\sum_{i}c_iw_i,$$ where $c_i\in K\setminus\{0\},w_i\in X^*$ and $w_i<_{\sigma}w_j,\mbox{ if } i<j$.

::: definition
-   The monomial $c_1w_1$ is called the leading term of $f$ with respect to $\sigma$ and denoted by $LT_\sigma(f)$.

-   $c_1\in  K\setminus\{0\}$ is called the leading coefficient of $f$ with respect to $\sigma$ and denoted by $LC_\sigma(f)$.

-   $w_1\in X^*$ is called the leading word of $f$ with respect to $\sigma$ and denoted by $LW_\sigma(f)$ or $\bar{f}$. The polynomial $f$ is called monic if $LC_\sigma(f)=1$.
:::

::: definition
For $g\in K\langle X \rangle,w\in X^*$, the $K-$module endomorphism $$r_g:K\langle X\rangle \rightarrow K\langle X\rangle$$ defined by $$\begin{aligned}
r_g(w)=
\begin{cases}
w-agb, &\mbox{ if }\exists~a,b\in X^*,\mbox{ s.t. }w=a\bar{g}b;\\
w,   &\mbox{ if }\forall~a,b\in X^*,\mbox{ s.t. }w\ne a\bar{g}b;
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}$$ is called a reduction by $g$.
:::

For a subset $S=\{g_1,\ldots,g_n\}\in K\langle X\rangle$, $f\in K\langle X\rangle$ is called a monomial which can be reduced to $0$ by $S$, if $f$ can be reduced to $0$ by a finite sequence of reduction $r_1,r_2,...,r_m(r_i\in \{r_g|g\in S\})$, then denote it by $f\xrightarrow{S}0$. If not, we denote it by $f \xrightarrow{S} \neg0$.

::: definition
Under a monomial order $\sigma$ on $X^*$, given two monic polynomials $f,g\in K\langle X \rangle$, the set $(f,g)$ defined by $$\begin{aligned}
(f,g)=\{fb-ag|a,b\in X^*,\bar{f}b=a\bar{g},|\bar{f}|+|\bar{g}|>|\bar{f}b|\}\cup \{f-agb|a,b\in X^*,\bar{f}=a\bar{g}b\}
\end{aligned}$$ is called the composition set of $f$ and $g$.
:::

::: remark
Generally, $(f,g)$ is not equal to $(g,f)$.
:::

Under a monomial order $\sigma$, let $S$ be a subset of $\{g\in K\langle X\rangle |LC(g)=1\}$, the ideal generated by $S$ is denoted by Ideal$(S)$.

::: definition
$S$ is called the Gröbner--Shirshov basis of Ideal$(S)$ under the monomial order $\sigma$, if for any $f,g\in S$, all the elements of $(f,g),(g,f)$ can be reduced to $0$ by $S$.
:::

::: definition
A Gröbner-Shirshov basis $S=\{f_1,...,f_n\}\subset K\langle X\rangle$ under the monomial order $\sigma$ is called reduced if $\forall ~1\leq i,j\leq n,i\ne j$, $\bar{f_i}\ne a\bar{f_j}b$ for $\forall ~a,b\in X^*$.
:::

Let $A=\mbox{sgp}\langle X|R \rangle$ be a semigroup presentation. Under a monomial order $\sigma$, $R=\{u_i=v_i|u_i,v_i\in X^*,v_i<_{\sigma}u_i\},$ corresponds to a subset $S=\{u_i-v_i|u_i,v_i\in X^*,v_i<_{\sigma}u_i\}\subset K\langle X\rangle.$ The ideal generated by $S$ is denoted by Ideal$(S)$. The set $GB(A)$ got by the following Shirshov algorithm is called the Gröbner-Shirshov basis of the semigroup $A$.\

::: algorithm
$k=1, S_k=S$ $GB(A)=S_k$.
:::

It's worth to mention that the number of the loop processes is not always finite and $GB(A)$ is not always a finite set.

In 2001, Bokut computed the Gröbner-Shirshov bases of the Coxeter groups of the types $A_n,B_n,D_n$ in [@MR1857281]. We will set out the Gröbner-Shirshov bases for the coxeter groups of the types $G_2,F_4,E_6,E_7$ and $E_8$ in the following sections. It is easy to calculate the Gröbner-Shirshov bases for the Coxeter group of type $G_2$ and $F_4$. We just list them as the appendix without proof.

It is significant that the presentation of group determines the computation of the Gröbner-Shirshov bases under the fixed order. In the following sections, we rearrange the generator, get a new presentation of Coxeter groups. It is worth to mention this new presentation of group is the key step to get Gröbner-Shirshov bases simpler and quicker than other presentations.

# $Gr\ddot{o}bner-Shirshov$ bases of the Coxeter groups of the types $E_6,E_7,E_8$ {#e}

In this section, we first give the new presentations of finite Coxeter groups of types $E_6,E_7$ and $E_8$. Via this new presentations, we get the $Gr\ddot{o}bner-Shirshov$ bases of the Coxeter groups of the types $E_6,E_7,E_8$.

::: definition
[]{#initial label="initial"} Define the presentations of finite Coxeter groups of types $E_n, n=6,7,8$ as follows: $$\label{E7-1}
\begin{aligned}
\theta_i^1&=x_i^2-1 ,&7-n\leq i\leq 5;\\
\theta_i^2&=x_{i+1}x_ix_{i+1}-x_ix_{i+1}x_i ,&7-n\leq i\leq 3;\\
\theta_{i,j}^3&=x_ix_j-x_jx_i ,&7-n\leq j+1<i\leq 4;\\
\theta_i^4&=x_{5}x_i-x_ix_{5}, &i\ne 6,5,3;\\
\theta^5&=x_{5}x_{3}x_{5}-x_{3}x_{5}x_{3};& \\
\varepsilon^1&=x_6^2-1;&\\
\varepsilon^2&=x_6x_{4}x_6-x_{4}x_6x_{4} ;&\\
\varepsilon_i^3&=x_6x_i-x_ix_6 ,&i\ne 4,6.\\
\end{aligned}$$
:::

The Coxeter-Dynkin diagram related to this presentation is as follows.

::: center
:::

We denote that $$\begin{aligned}
x_{i,j}&=x_ix_{i-1}x_{i-2}...x_{j}, i>j; \quad x_{ii}=x_i, \quad x_{i,i+1}=1;\\
x_{i,j}^{'}&=x_ix_{i-2}x_{i-3}...x_{j},i>j+1; \quad x_{i,i-1}^{'}=x_{i},\quad x_{i,i}^{'}=1.
\end{aligned}$$ The relations of the presentations of $E_n$ are called the initial relations of $E_n$.
