Farey fractions were first introduced in \cite{SS19} for the case $q=3$.  A Farey fraction modulo $n$ is a pair $(a,c)$ where $(a,c,n)=1$ and where we identify $\frac{a}{c}$ with $\frac{-a}{-c}.$  We think of these fractions as being points on $\mathbb{H}/\Gamma(n)$. Two Farey fractions mod $n$,  $(a,c)$  and $ (b,d)$  are adjacent when $ad-bc\equiv \pm 1  \; \text{mod}\;n$. 
 
 \begin{example} {$n=5.$} The Farey fractions mod 5 are 
   
   $$\frac{1}{0}, \frac{2}{0}, \frac{0}{1}, \frac{1}{1}, \frac{2}{1}, \frac{3}{1}, \frac{4}{1}, \frac{0}{2},\frac{1}{2} , \frac{2}{2}, \frac{3}{2} , \frac{4}{2}.$$
   
   We join $\frac{a}{c}$ and $\frac{b}{d}$ by a edge if and only if $ad-bc\equiv \pm 1 mod\ 5$
   

   
   \end{example}

There are 12 vertices and by drawing the diagram we see that we get a regular icosahedron.
