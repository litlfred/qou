of the box diagram for particle scattering are those which give the v olume of a tetrahedron
in hyperbolic 3-space (or its analytic continuation).
In the 3-dimensional [28] studies of statistical physics, one-loop F eynman diagrams
yield logarithms; in the 4 space-time dimensions of relativistic quantum ﬁeld theory
(QFT), they yield dilogarithms [29]. A connection between the dilogs of QFT and those
of hyperbolic geometry was considered in [30]. The achievement of [27 ] is to derive a
fairly simple relation between the value of any scalar box diagram, in 4 s pace-time di-
mensions, and the volume of an explicit tetrahedron in a 3-space of c onstant curvature.
There is nothing ideal about this tetrahedron: in general it has 6 es sential dihedral angles,
determined by the 10 physical quantities in the problem: the 4 exter nal masses, the 4
internal masses, and the Mandelstam variables s and t, related to the energy at which
the process occurs and the angle of scatter. Already one sees a n ice simpliﬁcation, with
9 dimensionless ratios of physical quantities collapsing to 6 essential dihedral angles. So
far, however, the question of number theory does not arise, sinc e in the generic physical
situation the kinematic quantities are real variables, and hence no a lgebraic number ﬁeld
is implied for the arguments of the dilogarithms.
