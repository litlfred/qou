Previously, we have described factorization homology for $n$-disk algebras in spaces, chain complexes, and spectra, when the monoidal structure is given by products, and the resulting homology theories give rise to twisted mapping spaces and usual homology theories. Factorization homology behaves very differently, and with greater sensitivity to manifold topology, when the monoidal structure on chain complexes or spectra is given by tensor product or smash product -- this case is closest to the physical motivation given in the introduction. We will consider this case in this section, focusing on some of the most common classes of $n$-disk algebra structures, which are either commutative, freely generated by a module, or freely generated by a Lie algebra.

## Factorization homology with coefficients in commutative algebras

We begin by examining commutative algebras in $\mathcal V$, otherwise known as $\mathcal E_\infty$-algebras in $\mathcal V$. Note first that a commutative algebra in $\mathcal V$ is equivalent to a symmetric monoidal functor ${\sf Fin}\rightarrow\mathcal V$ from finite sets with disjoint union. So restriction along the connected components functor $[-]: \mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^B_n\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_n \xrightarrow{[-]} {\sf Fin}$ defines a forgetful functor $${\sf fgt}\colon \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V)\longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^B_n}(\mathcal V)~.$$ We have the following consequence of $\otimes$-excision, where $\mathcal V$ is a symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category which is $\otimes$-presentable. To phrase this result we utilize that the $\infty$-category $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V)$ is tensored over spaces: $$\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Spaces}}}\times \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V) \xrightarrow{\otimes} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V)~,\qquad (X,A)\mapsto \mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}\bigl(X\to \ast \xrightarrow{\{A\}} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V)\bigr)~.$$

::: prop
[]{#tensor label="tensor"} The following diagram among $\infty$-categories commutes: $$\xymatrix{
\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal M}\mathsf{fld}}}^B_n \times \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V) \ar[r]^-{U\times {\sf id}}\ar[d]^-{{\sf id}\times {\rm fgt}} 
& 
\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Spaces}}\times \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V)\ar[r]^-\otimes&\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V)\ar[d]
\\
\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal M}\mathsf{fld}}}^B_n \times\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^B_n}(\mathcal V)\ar[rr]_\int &&\mathcal V
}$$ where $U$ is the underlying space functor and the right downward arrow is the standard forgetful functor. In particular, there is a natural equivalence $$\int_M A~\simeq~ M\otimes A$$ between the factorization homology of $M$ with coefficients in $A$ and the tensor of the commutative algebra $A$ with the underlying space of $M$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The functor $-\otimes A\colon \mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal M}\mathsf{fld}}}_n^B \to \mathcal V$ carries each contractible manifold to the underlying object of the commutative algebra $A$. For $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ a finite sequence of commutative algebras in $\mathcal V$, the $I$-fold coproduct in $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(\mathcal V)$ is the pointwise tensor product $\underset{i\in I} \bigotimes A_i$ (see Proposition 3.2.4.7 of [@dag]). It follows that this functor $-\otimes A$ is symmetric monoidal. From the defining expression of factorization homology as a colimit, there results a natural transformation $$\int_- A \longrightarrow -\otimes A$$ between symmetric monoidal functors $\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal M}\mathsf{fld}}}_n^B\to \mathcal V$, which evaluates as an equivalence on objects of $\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_n^B$. Lemma [\[excision\]](#excision){reference-type="ref" reference="excision"} grants that the domain of this natural transformation satisfies $\otimes$-excision. Because a collar-gluing $M'\underset{M_0\times \mathbb R}\bigcup M'' \cong M$ determines a pushout of underlying spaces $M'\underset{M_0}\coprod M'' \simeq M$, the codomain of this natural transformation too satisfies $\otimes$-excision. That the natural transformation evaluates on each $B$-framed $n$-manifold $M$ as an equivalence then follows by induction on a handle decomposition on $M$. ◻
:::

In other words, the factorization homology $\int_M A$ has a natural structure of a commutative algebra when $A$ is commutative, and this commutative algebra has a universal property: for each commutative algebra $C$ in $\mathcal V$ there is a natural equivalence from the space of commutative algebra maps $$\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Map}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}\bigl(\int_MA,C\bigr)~\simeq ~\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Map}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Com}}}}(A,C)^M~,$$ to the space of maps from $M$ to the space of commutative algebra maps. By formal properties of left adjoints and tensors, this has the immediate corollary.

::: cor
For each symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category $\mathcal V$ which is $\otimes$-presentable, there is a natural equivalence in $\mathcal V$: $$\int_M \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Sym}}(V) ~\simeq ~\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Sym}}(M\otimes V)~.$$
:::

In particular, if $\mathcal V$ is the $\infty$-category of chain complexes with tensor product, then there is an equivalence $\int_M\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Sym}}(V) \simeq \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Sym}}(\mathsf C_\ast(M,V))$ for each chain complex $V$. We now push the above result slightly further for the two special classes of commutative algebras arising from the cohomology of spaces and the cohomology of Lie algebras. The study of the latter has benefitted greatly from conversations with Kevin Costello and Dennis Gaitsgory, and a full development of these ideas will amount to a forthcoming work.

::: prop
[]{#prop.5.3 label="prop.5.3"} Let $M$ be an $n$-manifold, and let $X$ be a nilpotent $n$-connective space of finite type over $R$ such that $\pi_nX$ is finite. There is a natural equivalence of chain complexes $$\int_M \mathsf C^\ast(X, R)~ \simeq~ \mathsf C^\ast(X^M,R)$$ between the factorization homology of $M$ with coefficient in the $R$-cohomology of $X$ and the $R$-cohomology of the space of maps from $M$ to $X$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The two sides are evidently equivalent in the case where $M$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^n$, so to establish the result it suffices, as usual, to check by induction over a handle decomposition of $M$. Given a handle decomposition $N\underset{S^{k}\times\mathbb R^{n-k}}\bigcup\mathbb R^n\cong M$, we have a homotopy pullback diagram of spaces $$\label{nilpotent-square}
\xymatrix{
X^M\ar[r]\ar[d]&\ar[d] X^{\mathbb R^n}\\
X^{N}\ar[r]&X^{S^{k}\times\mathbb R^{n-k}}\\}$$ which gives rise to a natural map in $R$-homology $$\mathsf C_\ast(X^{M},R)\longrightarrow \mathsf C_\ast(X^{N},R)\underset{\mathsf C_\ast(X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}},R)}\bigotimes\mathsf C_\ast(X^{\mathbb R^n},R)$$ from the homology of the mapping spaces to the cotensor product of the comodules $\mathsf C_\ast(X^{N},R)$ and $\mathsf C_\ast(X^{\mathbb R^n},R)$ over the coalgebra $\mathsf C_\ast(X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}},R)$. This map is an equivalence exactly if the homological Eilenberg--Moore, or Rothenberg--Steenrod, spectral sequence for this homotopy Cartesian diagram converges. By Dwyer [@dwyer], the convergence of this Eilenberg--Moore spectral sequence is assured if the base $X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}}$ is connected and the action $$\pi_1 \bigl(X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}}, f\bigr)
\circlearrowright
\pi_\ast\bigl({\sf fiber}_f(X^{\mathbb R^n}\rightarrow X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}})\bigr)$$ is nilpotent for a choice of basepoint $f \in X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}}$. Since $X$ is $n$-connective, for $k<n$ any map $f:  S^k \rightarrow X$ is nullhomotopic, and therefore the base $X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}} \simeq X^{S^k}$ is connected. We can thus take $f$ to be the constant map valued at the basepoint of $X$, and so identify ${\sf fiber}_f(X^{\mathbb R^n}\rightarrow X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}}) \simeq \Omega^{k+1}X$. We now show the action of $\pi_1 \bigl(X^{S^k}\bigr)$ on $\pi_\ast \Omega^{k+1} X$ is nilpotent. Consider the fiber sequence $\Omega^{k+1} X \to X^{S^k} \xrightarrow{{\sf ev}_\ast} X$. This fibration admits a section, given by the constant maps. Consequently, there is an identification as a semi-direct product: $$\pi_1\bigl(X^{S^k}\bigr)
\cong
\pi_1X \ltimes\pi_1\Omega^k X
\cong
\pi_1X \ltimes\pi_0 \Omega^{k+1} X
~.$$ Through this identification, the action of $\pi_1 \bigl(X^{S^k}\bigr)$ on $\pi_\ast \Omega^{k+1} X$ is the unique action that extends the standard actions of $\pi_1X$ and of $\pi_0 \Omega^{k+1}X$ on $\pi_\ast \Omega^{k+1}X$. By assumption, the action of $\pi_1X$ on $\pi_{\ast+k+1}X \cong \pi_\ast \Omega^{k+1} X$ is nilpotent. In the case that $k=0$, the same assumption grants that the action of $\pi_0 \Omega^{k+1} X$ on $\pi_\ast \Omega^{k+1}X$ is nilpotent. In the case that $k>0$, the action of $\pi_0 \Omega^{k+1} X$ on $\pi_\ast \Omega^{k+1}X$ is automatically nilpotent due to commutativity. Nilpotence of the action of $\pi_1 \bigl(X^{S^k}\bigr)$ on $\pi_\ast \Omega^{k+1} X$ follows. Consequently, the natural map in $R$-homology above is an equivalence.

The remainder of this argument is checking that we have imposed sufficient finiteness conditions to ensure the convergence in cohomology as well as homology. Dualizing, we obtain an equivalence $$\Bigl(\mathsf C_\ast(X^{N},R)\underset{\mathsf C_\ast(X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}},R)}\bigotimes\mathsf C_\ast(X^{\mathbb R^n},R)\Bigr)^\vee \overset{\sim}\longrightarrow \mathsf C^\ast(X^{M},R)~.$$ Since $\pi_nX$ is finite, the mapping space $X^K$ has finitely many components for any $n$-dimensional finite CW complex $K$. Because the source spaces, $M$, $N$, $S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}$, and $\mathbb R^n$, all have have the homotopy types of finite $n$-dimensional CW complexes, we obtain that all these mapping spaces have finitely many components. Since they are additionally finite CW complexes and $X$ is finite type, the homology groups of the mapping spaces $\mathsf H_i(X^K,R)$ are finite rank over $R$, and therefore $\mathsf C_*(X^K,R)$ is its own double dual: the map $\mathsf C_*(X^K,R)\rightarrow\mathsf C^*(X^K,R)^\vee$ is an equivalence. Likewise, there is an equivalence between the dual of the tensor product and the cotensor product $$\Bigl( \mathsf C^\ast(X^{N},R)\underset{\mathsf C^\ast(X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}},R)}\bigotimes\mathsf C^\ast(X^{\mathbb R^n},R)\Bigr)^\vee \simeq \mathsf C_\ast(X^{N},R)\underset{\mathsf C_\ast(X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}},R)}\bigotimes\mathsf C_\ast(X^{\mathbb R^n},R)\simeq \mathsf C_\ast(X^M,R)$$ -- this can be seen by commuting duality with the colimit to obtain a limit of a cosimplicial object, then comparing termwise. Continuing, one then concludes the equivalence $$\mathsf C^\ast(X^M,R) \simeq \mathsf C^\ast(X^N,R)\underset{\mathsf C^\ast(X^{S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}},R)}\bigotimes \mathsf C^\ast(X^{\mathbb R^n},R)~,$$ thereby finishing the proof. ◻
:::

::: remark
See [@gtz1] for a closely related approach to the study of mapping spaces, in which one approaches the cohomology of a mapping space as a Hochschild homology-type invariant of the cohomology of the target.
:::

## Factorization homology with coefficients in free $n$-disk algebras

We next turn to the factorization homology of free $n$-disk algebras, a topic studied in more detail in §2 of [@aft2].

Denote by $\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V)$ the augmented $n$-disk algebra freely generated by $V\in \mathcal V$, regarded as a trivial $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Top}}}(n)$-module. Let $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M,\partial M)$ denote the quotient of $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)$ by the subspace of all configurations in which at least one point lies in the boundary of $M$.

::: prop
[]{#free label="free"} Let $M$ be an $n$-manifold, possibly with boundary. Let $V\in \mathcal V$ be an object of a symmetric monoidal $\mathop{\mathrm{\infty}}$-category which is $\otimes$-presentable. There is an equivalence $$\int_M \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V) ~\simeq~ \coprod_{i\geq 0} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M,\partial M)\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes V^{\otimes i}$$ between the factorization homology of an $n$-manifold $M$, possibly with boundary, with coefficients in $\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V)$ and the coproduct of the configuration spaces of $M$ labeled by $V$ quotient the subspace where at least one point lies in the boundary of $M$.
:::

The argument below is a special case of one in [@af1].

::: proof
*Proof.* The following equivalences $$\int_M \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V) \simeq \mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{U\in\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\partial}_{n/M}}\coprod_{i\geq 0}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(U,\partial U)\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes V^{\otimes i}\simeq \coprod_{i\geq 0}\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{U\in\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\partial}_{n/M}}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(U,\partial U)\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes V^{\otimes i}$$ follow from the commutativity of colimits. To conclude the result it therefore suffices to show that for each $i$ the canonical morphism $$\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{U\in\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^\partial_{n/M}}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(U,\partial U)\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes V^{\otimes i}
\longrightarrow 
\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M,\partial M)\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes V^{\otimes i}$$ in $\mathcal V$ is an equivalence. By the assumed distributivity in the $\otimes$-presentability condition, this follows if the natural $\Sigma_i$-equivariant map of $\Sigma_i$-spaces $$\label{compose-coend}
\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{U\in\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^\partial_{n/M}}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(U, \partial U)\longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M, \partial M)$$ is an equivalence, which we now show.

We first consider the case that the boundary of $M$ is empty, so that the natural $\Sigma_i$-equivariant map $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M) \xrightarrow{\cong} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M,\partial M)$ is a homeomorphism, and the natural functor $\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_{n/M}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^\partial_{n/M}$ is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories. In this case we are to show that the $\Sigma_i$-equivariant map of $\Sigma_i$-spaces $$\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{U\in \mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_{n/M}} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(U)\longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)$$ is an equivalence. After Proposition [\[EEd-vs-EE\]](#EEd-vs-EE){reference-type="ref" reference="EEd-vs-EE"}, it is enough to show that the $\Sigma_i$-equivariant map of $\Sigma_i$-topological spaces $$\label{hyper}
\underset{U\in {\sf Disk}_{n/M}}\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(U) \longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)$$ is a weak homotopy equivalence from the homotopy colimit. Each map $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(U)\to \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)$ comprising this homotopy colimit is an open embedding. Also, for each element $\{1,\dots,i\}\xrightarrow{c} M$ of $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)$, choosing mutually disjoint Euclidean neighborhoods about each $c(j)\in M$ demonstrates that $c$ lies in the image of at least one such open embedding. Therefore this augmented diagram is an open cover of $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)$. This open cover of $M$ has the property that each finite intersection of its terms is covered by terms contained in this finite intersection. This is to say that this open cover of $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)$ is in fact a hypercover. That the map ([\[hyper\]](#hyper){reference-type="ref" reference="hyper"}) is a weak homotopy equivalence follows from Corollary 1.6 of [@dugger-isaksen].

Now suppose $\partial M$ is not empty. Fix a collar-neighborhood $\partial M \times \mathbb R_{\geq 0} \hookrightarrow M$. Such a collar-neighborhood determines the top horizontal arrow in the diagram of topological spaces $$\xymatrix{
\underset{\emptyset \neq I \subset \{1,\dots,i\}}\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_{\{1,\dots,i\}\smallsetminus I}(\partial M)\times \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_{I}(\mathring{M})  \ar[rr]  \ar[d]
&&
\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(\mathring{M})  \ar[d]
\\
\ast \ar[rr]
&&
\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M,\partial M)
}$$ which commutes up to homotopy -- here, the homotopy colimit is indexed by the opposite of the poset of non-empty subsets of $\{1,\dots,i\}$. This collar-neighborhood also gives that this diagram is a weak homotopy pushout. The result for this case of non-empty boundary thus follows from the previous case of empty boundary applied to $\partial M$ and to $\mathring{M}$, using that homotopy colimits commute with one another. ◻
:::

::: remark
The preceding result has as a consequence that factorization homology is not a homotopy invariant of a closed $n$-manifold, since the homotopy type of configuration spaces is known to be sensitive to simple homotopy equivalence by [@simple].
:::

From Proposition [\[free\]](#free){reference-type="ref" reference="free"} and some reasoning on stable splittings of configuration spaces, one can deduce the following result. For the previous proposition, we required the monoidal structure of $\mathcal V$ to distribute over colimits; for convenience, we next assume the underlying $\infty$-category of $\mathcal V$ is stable.

::: prop
[]{#splits label="splits"} Let $\mathcal V$ be a symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category which is $\otimes$-presentable and whose underlying $\infty$-category is stable. For any $V\in\mathcal V$ and any nonnegative integer $m<n$, there is an equivalence in $\mathcal V$: $$\int_{S^{m}\times\mathbb R^{n-m}}\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_{n}(V)~ \simeq~ \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V) \otimes\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_{n-m}(\Sigma^m V)~.$$
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We first consider the case that $\mathcal V= \bigl({\sf Spectra}, \wedge\bigr)$ and $V=\Sigma^\infty X$ is the suspension spectrum of a pointed connected space $X$. There is a natural equivalence of spaces $$\int_{S^m\times\mathbb R^{n-m}}\Omega^n\Sigma^n X~\underset{\rm Cor~\ref{non-abel}}\simeq~ (\Omega^{n-m}\Sigma^nX)^{S^m}~\simeq~ \Omega^n\Sigma^n X\times \Omega^{n-m}\Sigma^n X$$ where the first equivalence is by nonabelian Poincaré duality and the second is by the standard trivialization of each fiber sequence $\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Map}}_*(K,G)\rightarrow G^K\rightarrow G$ whose base is equipped with the structure of a group-like $\mathcal E_1$-space. (It is this second step that requires the strict inequality $m<n$.) Passing to suspension spectra, Proposition [\[free\]](#free){reference-type="ref" reference="free"} begets the further equivalence $$\bigvee_{k\geq 0} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_k(S^m\times\mathbb R^{n-m})\underset{\Sigma_k}\otimes\Sigma^{\mathop{\mathrm{\infty}}}X^{\otimes k}\simeq \Bigl(\bigvee_{i\geq 0} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(\mathbb R^n)\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes\Sigma^{\mathop{\mathrm{\infty}}}X^{\otimes i} \Bigr)\otimes\Bigl(\bigvee_{j\geq 0} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_j(\mathbb R^{n-m})\underset{\Sigma_j}\otimes\Sigma^{\mathop{\mathrm{\infty}}}(\Sigma^m X)^{\otimes j}\Bigr)~.$$ Collecting coefficients of terms which are homogeneous in $X$ determines a $\Sigma_k$-equivariant stable homotopy equivalence $$\Sigma^\infty_\ast\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_k(S^m\times\mathbb R^{n-m})\simeq\coprod_{i+j=k}\Bigl(\Sigma_k\underset{\Sigma_i}\times\Sigma^\infty_\ast\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(\mathbb R^n) \Bigr)\otimes\Bigr(\Sigma_k\underset{\Sigma_j}\times\Sigma^\infty_\ast\bigl(\Sigma^{mj}_+\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_j(\mathbb R^{n-m})\bigr)\Bigl)$$ where $\Sigma_k\underset{\Sigma_l}\times -$ is induction from $\Sigma_l$-spectra to $\Sigma_k$-spectra.

Now consider the general case for $\mathcal V$, according to the hypothesis. After Proposition [\[free\]](#free){reference-type="ref" reference="free"}, both sides of the equivalence split as coproducts in homogeneous terms $V^{\otimes k}$, so it suffices to show that the coefficients of these terms are degreewise equivalent. Inspecting, we thus seek an equivalence in $\mathcal V$: $$\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_k(S^m\times\mathbb R^{n-m})\underset{\Sigma_k}\otimes V^{\otimes k}
~\simeq~
\bigoplus_{i+j=k}\Bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(\mathbb R^n)\underset{\Sigma_i} \otimes V^{\otimes i} \Bigr)\otimes\Bigr(\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_j(\mathbb R^{n-m})\underset{\Sigma_j}\otimes (\Sigma^m V)^{\otimes j}\Bigl)~.$$ This equivalence follows from the conclusion of the previous paragraph upon tensoring with $V^{\otimes k}$ and taking balanced $\Sigma_k$-coinvariants. ◻
:::

::: remark
The equivalence in the above proposition can be upgraded to an equivalence of $(n-m)$-disk algebras if the righthand side is given a twisted algebra structure, using a natural action of $\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_{n-m}(\Sigma^m V)$ on $\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V)$.
:::

The calculations to this point allow the following interesting description of the bar construction on a free $n$-disk algebra. Let $\mathcal V$ be a symmetric monoidal $\mathop{\mathrm{\infty}}$-category which is $\otimes$-presentable.

::: prop
[]{#bar label="bar"} For any object $V\in\mathcal V$ in a symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category which is $\otimes$-presentable, there is an natural equivalence $${\sf Bar}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V)\bigr)~\simeq~\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_{n-1}(\Sigma V)$$ between the bar construction on the free $n$-disk algebra on $V$ and the free $(n-1)$-disk algebra generated by the suspension of $V$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Via Example [\[ex.assoc\]](#ex.assoc){reference-type="ref" reference="ex.assoc"}, each augmented associative algebra $A\to \mathop{\mathrm{\mathbbm{1}}}$ in $\mathcal V$ determines a symmetric monoidal functor $A\colon \mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\partial, \sf or}_1\to \mathcal V$. Applying $\otimes$-excision in this simplest case of the collar-gluing $[-1,1)\underset{(-1,1)}\bigcup (-1,1]\cong [-1,1]$, we have that the bar construction ${\sf Bar}(A)$ is identifiable as the factorization homology over the closed 1-disk: $${\sf Bar} (A) ~\simeq~\int_{\mathbb D^1} A~.$$ Proposition [\[free\]](#free){reference-type="ref" reference="free"} gives the first and last of the following identifications $$\begin{aligned}
\nonumber
\underset{\mathbb D^1\times\mathbb R^{n-1}}\int \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V) 
&
\simeq 
&
\coprod_{i\geq 0} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(\mathbb D^1\times\mathbb R^{n-1}, \partial \mathbb D^1\times\mathbb R^{n-1})\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes V^{\otimes i} 
\\
\nonumber
&
\simeq 
&
\coprod_{i\geq 0} \Sigma^i\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(\mathbb R^{n-1})\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes V^{\otimes i} 
\\
\nonumber
&
\simeq 
&
\coprod_{i\geq 0} \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(\mathbb R^{n-1})\underset{\Sigma_i}\otimes(\Sigma V)^{\otimes i} 
\\
\nonumber
&
\simeq 
&
\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_{n-1}(\Sigma V)~.
\end{aligned}$$ The second identification follows from the $\Sigma_i$-equivariant equivalence of spaces $$\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(\mathbb D^1\times M, \partial \mathbb D^1\times M)\simeq \mathbb D^i\times \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)\big/\partial\mathbb D^i\times \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)\simeq \Sigma^i \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Conf}}}_i(M)$$ in the case $M=\mathbb R^{n-1}$. The third equivalence is a coproduct of a composite of two equivalences: $\Sigma^iX\otimes V^{\otimes i}\simeq X\otimes\Sigma^i(V^{\otimes i})\simeq X\otimes(\Sigma V)^{\otimes i}$. The first of these equivalences uses that tensoring with spaces preserves colimits among spaces -- an assertion which is direct from definitions. The second of these equivalences directly uses the assumption that the symmetric monoidal structure of $\mathcal V$ distributes over colimits. ◻
:::

::: remark
In [@cotangent], it was proved that ${\sf Bar}^n \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V)\simeq \mathop{\mathrm{\mathbbm{1}}}\oplus \Sigma^n V$, the free $0$-disk algebra on the $n$th suspension of $V$. This can now be seen as an application of Proposition [\[bar\]](#bar){reference-type="ref" reference="bar"} iterated $n$ times. This result is well-known in the case of $n=1$: the bar construction for the tensor algebra on $V$ is $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathbbm{1}}}\oplus \Sigma V$. Our result is also entirely to be expected given the example of $n$-fold loop space, where for a connected pointed space $V$, we can calculate ${\sf Bar}\bigl( \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n(V)\bigr)\simeq \mathsf B\Omega^n\Sigma^nV\simeq \Omega^{n-1}\Sigma^nV\simeq \mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_{n-1}(\Sigma V)$. As such, this result could have been proved longed ago, as it fits naturally into works such as [@may] and [@cohen]. We note lastly that the limiting statement as $n$ increases gives the well-known equivalence ${\sf Bar}\bigl({\sf Sym}(V)\bigr)\simeq {\sf Sym}(\Sigma V)$.
:::

This result has an important consequence for the relation between the $\mathop{\mathrm{\infty}}$-categories of augmented $n$-disk algebras and augmented $(n-1)$-disk algebras:

::: theorem
[]{#baradj label="baradj"} Let $\mathcal V$ be a symmetric monoidal $\mathop{\mathrm{\infty}}$-category which is $\otimes$-presentable. There is an adjunction $${\sf Bar}: \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_n^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf fr}}}}^{\sf aug}(\mathcal V)\leftrightarrows \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_{n-1}^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf fr}}}}^{\sf aug}(\mathcal V): \Omega$$ where the functors are given by the bar construction and by a functor $\Omega$ which, on underlying objects of $\mathcal V$, is the based loop functor.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We first show that the bar construction defines a functor ${\sf Bar}\colon \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_n^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf fr}}}}^{\sf aug}(\mathcal V)\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_{n-1}^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf fr}}}}^{\sf aug}(\mathcal V)$. This is so in as much as ${\sf Bar}\simeq\int_{\mathbb D^1\times\mathbb R^{n-1}}$ is the object in $\mathcal V$ underlying the augmented $\mathcal E_{n-1}$-algebra $\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\sf fr}_{n-1} \xrightarrow{\mathbb D^1\times - } \mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal M}\mathsf{fld}}}^{\partial,\sf fr}_n \xrightarrow{\int A} \mathcal V$. We will now argue that this functor carries colimit diagrams to colimit diagrams.

Proposition [\[bar\]](#bar){reference-type="ref" reference="bar"} gives a commutative diagram among $\infty$-categories: $$\xymatrix{
\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf fr}}}_{n}}^{\sf aug}(\mathcal V)\ar[rr]^{{\sf Bar}}&& \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf fr}}}_{n-1}}^{\sf aug}(\mathcal V)\\
\mathcal V\ar[u]^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_n}\ar[rr]^\Sigma && \mathcal V\ar[u]_{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Free}}_{n-1}}.\\}$$ As a consequence, the functor ${\sf Bar}$ preserves coproducts of free $\mathcal E_n$-algebras. We next argue that ${\sf Bar}$ preserves sifted colimits. Using that the symmetric monoidal structure of $\mathcal V$ distributes over colimits, it is enough to argue that factorization homology $\int_{M}\colon \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}^{\sf aug}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\sf fr}_n}(\mathcal V) \to \mathcal V$ carries sifted colimit diagrams to colimit diagrams, for each framed $n$-manifold $M$ possibly with boundary.

So let $A\colon J\to \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\sf fr}_n}^{\sf aug}(\mathcal V)$ be a diagram of augmented $\mathcal E_n$-algebras in $\mathcal V$, indexed by a sifted $\infty$-category $J$. The canonical arrow $\underset{j\in J}\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}~ {\sf Bar} (A_j) \longrightarrow {\sf Bar}( \underset{j\in J}\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}A_j)$ in $\mathcal V$ is a composite $$\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{j\in J} \mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{U\in \mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\partial,\sf fr}_{n/M}} A_j(U)~ \simeq ~\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{U\in \mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\partial, \sf fr}_{n/(M}} \mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{j\in J} A_j(U) \longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{U\in \mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}^{\partial, \sf fr}_{n/M}} (\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}_{j\in J} A_j)(U)$$ where the outer objects are in terms of the defining expression for factorization homology, the left equivalence is through commuting colimits, and the right arrow is a colimit of canonical arrows. Again using that the symmetric monoidal structure of $\mathcal V$ distributes over colimits, each arrow $\underset{j\in J}\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}A_j(U) \to (\underset{j\in J}\mathop{\mathrm{\sf colim}}A_j)(U)$ is an equivalence if and only if it is for $U$ connected. This is the case provided the forgetful functor ${\sf ev}_{\mathbb R^n}\colon \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_n^{\sf aug}(\mathcal V) \to \mathcal V$ preserves sifted colimits. This assertion is Proposition 3.2.3.1 of [@dag].

Continuing, we conclude that ${\sf Bar}$ preserves all coproducts, since any coproduct is a geometric realization of coproducts of free algebras (this is a consequence of the $\infty$-categorical Barr--Beck Theorem 4.7.4.5 of [@dag]; see §4.7 thereof for a general discussion). Now, coproducts and geometric realizations generate all colimits, and we conclude that ${\sf Bar}$ is a colimit preserving functor from $n$-disk algebras to $(n-1)$-disk algebras.

To complete the proof, both of the $\infty$-categories in the adjunction are presentable (see Corollary 3.2.3.3 of [@dag]). The adjoint functor theorem (Corollary 5.5.2.9 of [@topos]) can thus be applied to conclude that ${\sf Bar}$ is a left adjoint. The diagram above is therefore a commutative diagram of left adjoints, and therefore their right adjoints commute. Consequently, ${\sf Bar}$ has a right adjoint which, at the level of objects of $\mathcal V$, agrees with based loops $\Omega$, which is right adjoint to suspension $\Sigma$. ◻
:::

::: remark
We interpret Theorem [\[baradj\]](#baradj){reference-type="ref" reference="baradj"} in terms of Koszul duality, after [@gk] and [@priddy]. Given the calculation of the *Koszul dual* operad $\mathbb D\mathcal E_{n} \simeq \mathcal E_n[-n]$, computed at the level of homology by Getzler and Jones [@getzlerjones] and computed in chain complexes by Fresse [@fressekoszul], these functors should be equivalent to restriction and induction along the Koszul dual of the map $\mathcal E_{n-1} \rightarrow\mathcal E_{n}$. However, Theorem [\[baradj\]](#baradj){reference-type="ref" reference="baradj"} is more general: it holds unstably (for instance, when $\mathcal V$ is $\mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Spaces}}}$), whereas this operadic form of Koszul duality would require $\mathcal V$ to be stable.
:::

## Factorization homology from Lie algebras

We now discuss factorization homology of $n$-disk algebras coming from Lie algebras. Our results are closely analogous to those above about the factorization homology of $n$-disk algebras coming from topological spaces. For simplicity, we assume our Lie algebras are defined over a fixed field $k$ of characteristic zero.

As we proceed, we make use of the fact that Lie algebras in ${\sf Mod}_k$ admit totalizations, and therefore the $\infty$-category of such is cotensored over pointed spaces in a natural and standard way: $(X,\mathfrak{g})\mapsto {\mathfrak{g}}^X$. For $M$ an $n$-manifold, we notate $\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Map}}_{\sf c}(M , \mathfrak{g}) := {\mathfrak{g}}^{M^+}$, where $M^+$ is the 1-point compactification. One can describe this as $\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(M,\frak g)\simeq \mathsf C_{\sf c}^\ast(M,\frak g)$, the compactly supported cochains of $M$ with coefficients in $\frak g$. See also [@owen] and [@kevinowen] for a discussion of the following.

::: prop
[]{#env label="env"} For $\mathfrak{g}$ a Lie algebra over $k$, there is a natural equivalence of chain complexes over $k$, $$\int_M \mathsf C_\ast^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(\mathbb R^n,\mathfrak{g})\bigr)~ \simeq~ \mathsf C^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}_\ast\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(M, \mathfrak{g})\bigr)~,$$ between the factorization homology of the Lie algebra chains of $\Omega^n {\mathfrak{g}}$ and the Lie algebra chains of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}^{M^+}$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Lie algebra chains defines a functor between $\infty$-categories $\mathsf C^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}_\ast: \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}({\sf Mod}_k) \rightarrow{\sf Mod}_k$. This functor carries finite products of Lie algebras to finite tensor products of $k$-modules, which is to say that it is symmetric monoidal. Furthermore, this functor preserves geometric realizations, so Lemma [\[switch\]](#switch){reference-type="ref" reference="switch"} applies to give a natural identification: $\int_M \mathsf C_\ast^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(\mathbb R^n,\mathfrak{g})\bigr)\simeq  \mathsf C_\ast^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}\bigl(\int_M\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(\mathbb R^n,\mathfrak{g})\bigr)$. The equivalence $\int_M\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(\mathbb R^n,\mathfrak{g})\simeq \mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(M,\mathfrak{g})$ now follows from the argument of nonabelian Poincaré duality (Corollary [\[non-abel\]](#non-abel){reference-type="ref" reference="non-abel"}), only here we apply it in the usual abelian setting.[^1] ◻
:::

::: remark
The $n$-disk algebra $\mathsf C_\ast^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(\mathbb R^n,\mathfrak{g})\bigr)$ has an interesting separate interpretation that we state here, and prove as a separate work. There is a forgetful functor from $\mathcal E_n$-algebras in chain complexes over $k$ to Lie algebras over $k$ (see [@cohen] for an account at the level of homology). The adjoint functor theorem (Corollary 5.5.2.9 of [@topos]) applies to this functor, and so there is an adjunction $$\mathsf U_n \colon \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}({\sf Mod}_k)~ \rightleftarrows~ \mathop{\mathrm{\mathsf{Alg}}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_n^{\sf fr}}({\sf Mod}_k)\colon {\sf fgt}~.$$ In the case $n=1$, this left adjoint $\mathsf U_1$ agrees with the familiar universal enveloping algebra functor. In general, there is an identification of $\mathop{\mathrm{{\mathcal D}\sf isk}}_n^{\sf fr}$-algebras, $$\mathsf U_n \mathfrak{g}~\simeq~  \mathsf C_\ast^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(\mathbb R^n, \mathfrak{g})\bigr)~,$$ through which Proposition [\[env\]](#env){reference-type="ref" reference="env"} can be reformulated as an equivalence of chain complexes over $k$: $$\int_M\mathsf U_n\mathfrak{g}~\simeq~ \mathsf C^{\mathop{\mathrm{\sf Lie}}}_\ast\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{{\sf Map}_{\sf c}}}(M,\mathfrak{g})\bigr)~.$$
:::

[^1]: See [@af2] for a complete account, where nonabelian Poincaré duality is an instance of a version of Poincaré/Koszul duality for *Cartesian-presentable $\infty$-categories*.
