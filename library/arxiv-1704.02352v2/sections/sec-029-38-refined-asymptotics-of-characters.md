[]{#subsec:refinedasymptotics label="subsec:refinedasymptotics"} In order to find more subtle relationships between the asymptotics of various quantities appearing in [\[def:approx-factorization-charactersB\]](#def:approx-factorization-charactersB){reference-type="ref" reference="def:approx-factorization-charactersB"} we need an analogue of Equation [\[eq:Kerov-polynomial-gamma0\]](#eq:Kerov-polynomial-gamma0){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:Kerov-polynomial-gamma0"} between the character $\mathop{\mathrm{Ch}}_l$ and the free cumulants in the generic case $\alpha\neq 1$. We present below two such formulas: the one from [0.0.1](#sec:rough-estimate){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:rough-estimate"} is conceptually simpler and will be sufficient for the scaling when $\alpha$ is fixed; in the case of the double scaling limit we will need a more involved formula from [0.0.2](#sec:top-degree-by-Sniady){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:top-degree-by-Sniady"}.

### The rough estimate {#sec:rough-estimate}

We start with the formula expressing the top-degree part of the normalized Jack character $\mathop{\mathrm{Ch}}_l$ modulo terms divisible by $\gamma^2$ which follows follows from [@Lassalle2009 Section 11] combined with the degree bounds of the first-named author and Féray [@DolegaFeray2014 Proposition 3.7] as well as from [@Sniady-AsymptoticsJack Theorem A.3]: $$\begin{gathered}
\label{eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle}
\mathop{\mathrm{Ch}}_l=
\bigg[ \mathcal{R}_{l+1}+ \\
+\gamma \sum_{i\geq 1} \sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_i=l}
\frac{l}{i} 
(k_1-1) \cdots (k_i-1) \mathcal{R}_{k_1} \cdots \mathcal{R}_{k_i}+ \\
{+ (\text{terms divisible by $\gamma^2$ })\bigg]+ }
\\
(\text{terms of degree at most $l-1$}).
\end{gathered}$$

### Closed formula for top-degree part of Jack characters {#sec:top-degree-by-Sniady}

Let us fix an integer $l\geq 1$. We will view the symmetric group $\mathfrak{S}(l)$ as the set of permutations of the set $[l]:=\{1,\dots,l\}$ and its subgroup $$\mathfrak{S}(l-1):=\big\{ \sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(l) : \sigma(l)=l \big\}$$ as the set of permutations of the same set $[l]$ which have $l$ as a fixpoint. Consider the set $$\begin{gathered}
\mathcal{X}_l= \big\{ (\sigma_1,\sigma_2) \in \mathfrak{S}(l)\times \mathfrak{S}(l) : \\
\text{the group generated by $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ acts transitively on the set $[l]$}
\big\}.   
\end{gathered}$$ The group $\mathfrak{S}(l-1)$ acts on $\mathcal{X}_l$ by coordinate-wise conjugation: $$\pi\cdot (\sigma_1,\sigma_2) := \big(\pi \sigma_1 \pi^{-1}, \pi \sigma_2 \pi^{-1}\big).$$ The orbits of this action define an equivalence relation $\sim$ on $\mathcal{X}_l$; the corresponding equivalence classes have a natural combinatorial interpretation as *non-labeled, rooted, bicolored, oriented maps with $l$ edges* which is out of scope of the current paper (see [@Sniady-AsymptoticsJack Section 1.4.3] for details).

For a permutation $\pi$ we denote by $C(\pi)$ the set of its cycles.

We say that a triple $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,q)$ is an *'expander'* [@Sniady-AsymptoticsJack Appendix A.1], see also [@DolegaFeraySniady2008], if $\sigma_1,\sigma_2\in\mathfrak{S}(l)$ are permutations and $q\colon C(\sigma_2)\to\{2,3,\dots\}$ is a function on the set of cycles of $\sigma_2$ with the following two properties: $$\sum_{c\in C(\sigma_2)} q(c) = |C(\sigma_1)|+|C(\sigma_2)|$$ and for every set $A\subset C(\sigma_2)$ such that $A\neq \emptyset$ and $A\neq C(\sigma_2)$ we have that $$\begin{gathered}
\#\big\{ c\in C(\sigma_1): \text{$c$ intersects at least one of the cycles in $A$} \big\} > \\
\sum_{d\in A} \big[ q(d)-1  \big].
\end{gathered}$$

The following is a refined version of the formula [\[eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle\]](#eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle"}.

::: lemma
[]{#lem:top-degree-jack-character label="lem:top-degree-jack-character"} For each integer $l\geq 1$ the expansion of the character $\mathop{\mathrm{Ch}}_l$ as a polynomial in the variables $\gamma,\mathcal{R}_2,\mathcal{R}_3,\dots$ is given by $$\begin{gathered}
    \label{eq:Sniady-proved-this}
    \mathop{\mathrm{Ch}}_l= \\
    \sum_{[(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)]\in \mathcal{X}_l/\sim} \gamma^{l+1-|C(\sigma_1)|-|C(\sigma_2)|} 
    \sum_{\substack{q\colon C(\sigma_2)\to\{2,3,\dots\} \\
            \text{$(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,q)$ is an expander}}}
    \prod_{c\in C(\sigma_2)} \mathcal{R}_{q(c)} + \\
    +\text{(terms of degree at most $l-1$)}.
    
\end{gathered}$$ where the first sum runs over the representatives of the equivalence clases.
:::

### The scaling when $\alpha$ is constant {#sec:concrete-alpha-constant}

Part [\[item:equivalent-enhanced\]](#item:equivalent-enhanced){reference-type="ref" reference="item:equivalent-enhanced"} of [\[def:approx-factorization-charactersB\]](#def:approx-factorization-charactersB){reference-type="ref" reference="def:approx-factorization-charactersB"} concerns the trivial case $\ell=1$ which one can easily prove from scratch, based on [\[eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle\]](#eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle"}. We shall present a detailed proof only for a specific case which will be useful in applications (more specifically, for the proof of [\[theo:clt\]](#theo:clt){reference-type="ref" reference="theo:clt"} from [\[sec:proof-of-CLTCLT\]](#sec:proof-of-CLTCLT){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:proof-of-CLTCLT"}) and we shall assume that the refined asymptotics of characters specified in part [\[item:equivalent-enhanced\]](#item:equivalent-enhanced){reference-type="ref" reference="item:equivalent-enhanced"} of [\[def:approx-factorization-charactersB\]](#def:approx-factorization-charactersB){reference-type="ref" reference="def:approx-factorization-charactersB"} holds true for the quantity [\[eq:AFPPartitions\]](#eq:AFPPartitions){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:AFPPartitions"}. For a specific choice of the constants $$\begin{aligned}
\label{eq:quick-convergence-1-B}
\chi_n(l)              &= a_{l+1}\ n^{-\frac{l-1}{2}} + b_{l+1} \ n^{-\frac{l}{2}} + o\left(  n^{-\frac{l}{2}} \right)

\label{eq:quick-convergence-2-B}
\mathbb{E}_{\chi_n}(\mathop{\mathrm{Ch}}_l)     &= a_{l+1}\ n^{ \frac{l+1}{2}} + b_{l+1}\ n^{\frac{l}{2}}+ o\left(  n^{ \frac{l}{2}} \right)
\qquad \text{for $l\geq 1$,}\\
\label{eq:quick-convergence-3-B}
\mathbb{E}_{\chi_n}(\mathcal{R}_{l+1})  &= a'_{l+1}\ n^{ \frac{l+1}{2}} + b'_{l+1} \ n^{\frac{l}{2}} + o\left(  n^{ \frac{l}{2}} \right)

### The double scaling limit {#subsubsect:DoubleScaling}

In the double scaling limit $g\neq 0$ considered in [\[sec:hypothesis-double-scaling\]](#sec:hypothesis-double-scaling){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:hypothesis-double-scaling"} the reasoning presented in [0.1.1](#sec:concrete-alpha-constant){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:concrete-alpha-constant"} above remains valid if one replaces all the references to [\[eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle\]](#eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:kerov-polynomial-subtle"} by [\[lem:top-degree-jack-character\]](#lem:top-degree-jack-character){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:top-degree-jack-character"}. \sum_{\substack{q\colon C(\sigma_2)\to\{2,3,\dots\} \\
        \text{$(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,q)$ is an expander}}}
\prod_{c\in C(\sigma_2)} a'_{q(c)},$$ while [\[eq:co-sie-dzieje-z-b\]](#eq:co-sie-dzieje-z-b){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:co-sie-dzieje-z-b"} takes the form $$\begin{gathered}

    \sum_{\substack{q\colon C(\sigma_2)\to\{2,3,\dots\} \\

    +\sum_{[(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)]\in \mathcal{X}_l/\sim} g^{l+1-|C(\sigma_1)|-|C(\sigma_2)|} 
    \times 
} 
\\ 
\times

    C(\sigma_2)\setminus\{c\}} a'_{q(c')}.
