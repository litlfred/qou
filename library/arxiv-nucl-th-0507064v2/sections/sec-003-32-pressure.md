III.
RESULTS
A.
Virial Coeﬃcients
We ﬁrst calculate the second virial coeﬃcient bn. We
use the T = 1 np phase shifts obtained from the Nijmegen
partial wave analysis [35]. This neglects the small charge
dependences in nuclear interactions. We have included
all partial waves with L ⩽6 and the resulting total total
phase shift δtot(E) is shown in Fig. 1. The total phase
shift displays both the prominent low-energy 1S0 reso-
nance and the signiﬁcance of higher partial waves. We
observe that δtot(E) is very weakly dependent on energy
with δtot ≈65 degrees over the entire range E ⩽350 MeV
of the Nijmegen partial wave analysis. As shown in Fig. 1,
the decrease of the 1S0 phase shift due to the large ef-
fective range is compensated by the contributions from
higher angular momenta. For reference, in the unitary
limit of a S-wave resonance (as = ±∞), the phase shift
is energy-independent with δ1S0 = 90 degrees.
In the unitary limit, the second virial coeﬃcient is inde-
pendent of the temperature and given by b2 = 3/25/2 =
0.53 [14]. For neutron matter, our results for the virial co-
eﬃcient bn and its derivative T b′
n(T ) are listed in Table I.
In addition to the full results, we also give the virial co-
eﬃcient obtained only from the S-wave scattering length
anp and including the eﬀective range contribution. As
expected, bn is dominated by the large S-wave scattering
length, but eﬀective range and higher partial wave contri-
butions are signiﬁcant even for these low temperatures.
As a result of the weak energy dependence of δtot(E), we
ﬁnd that the second virial coeﬃcient is approximately
independent of temperature over a wide range, and con-
sequently T b′
n ≈0. The value we obtain for bn ≈0.31 is
40% reduced compared to the universal value b2 = 0.53.
