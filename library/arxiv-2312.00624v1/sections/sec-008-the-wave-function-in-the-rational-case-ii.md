[]{#sec: rational3 label="sec: rational3"} In Section [\[sec: rational1\]](#sec: rational1){reference-type="ref" reference="sec: rational1"}, we showed that the exact wave function $\chi(u; \hbar)$ in Eq. [\[exactconj\]](#exactconj){reference-type="eqref" reference="exactconj"} at rational values of $\hbar$ is determined by the functions $\Upsilon^{(\alpha)} (X)$, defined in Eq. [\[upsilon\]](#upsilon){reference-type="eqref" reference="upsilon"}, and their derivatives with respect to $q$, up to the constants $\mathsf{C}_\alpha$ and the exponential prefactors containing $s^{(\alpha)}_{0,1}(x)$. We will now prove that the functions $\Upsilon^{(\alpha)} (X)$ can be obtained by solving directly the quantum $A$-polynomial in closed form. We will focus on $q$-difference equations of order two and three. These are the relevant cases for the examples studied in Section [\[sec: examples\]](#sec: examples){reference-type="ref" reference="sec: examples"}. The matrix formalism presented below was used in [@szabolcs] for a second-order $q$-difference equation in the context of quantum mirror curves.
