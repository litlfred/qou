Following the conventions of [@ak], the state integral for the $\bm{4}_1$-knot takes the form $$\label{41ak}
\chi_{\bm{4}_1}(u;\hbar) = \frac{1}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} {\rm e}^{-\frac{x}{2}-\frac{x}{2 {\mathsf{b}}^2}-\frac{{\rm i}x^2}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}^2}} \int_{{\mathbb R}+ {\rm i}0} \frac{\Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left(\frac{x-y}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} \right)}{\Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left(\frac{y}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} \right)} {\rm e}^{\frac{{\rm i}x y}{\pi {\mathsf{b}}^2}} dy \, ,$$ where $x = 2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}u$ and $\hbar = 2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}^2$, as before, while $\Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}(z)$ denotes the Faddeev's quantum dilogarithm. We refer to Appendix [\[app: faddeev\]](#app: faddeev){reference-type="ref" reference="app: faddeev"} for its definition and some useful properties. Let us define the function $$f(y) = \frac{\Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left(\frac{x-y}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} \right)}{\Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left(\frac{y}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} \right)} {\rm e}^{\frac{{\rm i}x y}{\pi {\mathsf{b}}^2}} \, ,$$ where we consider $x \in {\mathbb R}$ fixed. The singularities of $f(y)$ are poles located at the points $$\label{y-poles}
\begin{align}
y &= x - \pi {\rm i}\left(2 M + 1 \right) - \pi {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}^2 \left(2 N + 1 \right) \, , \\
y &= -  \pi {\rm i}\left(2 M + 1 \right) - \pi {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}^2 \left(2 N + 1 \right) \, , 
\end{align}$$ where $M, N \in {\mathbb N}$, as dictated by the singular structure of Faddeev's quantum dilogarithm in Appedix [\[app: faddeev\]](#app: faddeev){reference-type="ref" reference="app: faddeev"}. Therefore, $f(y)$ is a holomorphic function in the upper half-plane. Let us now fix the rational value $${\mathsf{b}}^2 = \frac{P}{Q} \, ,  \quad  P,Q \in {\mathbb Z}_{>0} \; \; \text{coprime} \, ,$$ and observe that the function $f(y)$ satisfies the relation $$\label{f-shiftP}
\begin{aligned}
f(y + 2 \pi {\rm i}P) = f(y+2 \pi {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}^2 Q) &= \Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left(\frac{x-y}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} - {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}Q \right) \Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left(\frac{y}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} + {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}Q \right)^{-1} {\rm e}^{\frac{{\rm i}x y}{\pi {\mathsf{b}}^2}- 2 x Q} \\
&= f(y) \left( 1 + {\rm e}^{Q(x-y) - \pi {\rm i}P Q} \right) \left( 1 + {\rm e}^{Q y + \pi {\rm i}P Q} \right) {\rm e}^{-2 x Q} \, ,
\end{aligned}$$ and similarly $$\label{f-shiftM}
\begin{aligned}
f(y - 2 \pi {\rm i}P) = f(y-2 \pi {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}^2 Q) &= \Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left(\frac{x-y}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} + {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}Q \right) \Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left(\frac{y}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} - {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}Q \right)^{-1} {\rm e}^{\frac{{\rm i}x y}{\pi {\mathsf{b}}^2}+ 2 x Q} \\
&= f(y) \left( 1 + {\rm e}^{Q(x-y) + \pi {\rm i}P Q} \right)^{-1} \left( 1 + {\rm e}^{Q y - \pi {\rm i}P Q} \right)^{-1} {\rm e}^{2 x Q} \, , 
\end{aligned}$$ where we have applied the periodicity formulae for $\Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}(z)$ at rational values in Eqs. [\[phibrat-perP\]](#phibrat-perP){reference-type="eqref" reference="phibrat-perP"}, [\[phibrat-perM\]](#phibrat-perM){reference-type="eqref" reference="phibrat-perM"}. Since $P,Q \in {\mathbb Z}_{>0}$ and thus ${\rm e}^{\pi {\rm i}P Q} = {\rm e}^{- \pi {\rm i}P Q}$, it follows from Eqs. [\[f-shiftP\]](#f-shiftP){reference-type="eqref" reference="f-shiftP"}, [\[f-shiftM\]](#f-shiftM){reference-type="eqref" reference="f-shiftM"} that $f(y)$ satisfies the functional equation $$f(y + 2 \pi {\rm i}P) f(y - 2 \pi {\rm i}P) = f(y)^2 \, .$$ It is straightforward to verify that $$f(y) (f(y)-f(y+2 \pi {\rm i}P)) \ne 0 \, , \quad \forall y \in {\mathbb R}+ {\rm i}0 \, ,$$ and therefore we can use Lemma 2.1 from [@garou-kas] to write $$\label{int-lemma}
\int_{{\mathbb R}+ {\rm i}0} f(y) dy = \left( \int_{{\mathbb R}+ {\rm i}0} - \int_{{\mathbb R}+ {\rm i}0 + 2 \pi {\rm i}P} \right) \frac{f(y)}{1- f(y+2 \pi {\rm i}P)/f(y)} dy \, .$$ Let us now compute the integral on the RHS of Eq. [\[int-lemma\]](#int-lemma){reference-type="eqref" reference="int-lemma"} applying the residue theorem. We start by using Eq. [\[f-shiftP\]](#f-shiftP){reference-type="eqref" reference="f-shiftP"} to express the integrand in the form $$\label{integrand}
\frac{f(y)}{1- f(y+2 \pi {\rm i}P)/f(y)} = \frac{f(y) {\rm e}^{2 x Q}}{G(x,y)} \, ,$$ where we have introduced the function $$G(x,y) = {\rm e}^{2 x Q} - \left( 1 + {\rm e}^{Q(x-y) - \pi {\rm i}P Q} \right) \left( 1 + {\rm e}^{Q y + \pi {\rm i}P Q} \right) \, .$$ Since the numerator in Eq. [\[integrand\]](#integrand){reference-type="eqref" reference="integrand"} is holomorphic in the upper half of the complex plane, the relevant singularities of the integrand are the solutions to $G(x,y) = 0$ at fixed $x \in {\mathbb R}$ which lie within the domain of integration. Specifically, these are $$\label{y-sing}
y_{\pm, k}(x) = 2 \pi {\rm i}\frac{k}{Q} - {\rm i}\pi P - \frac{1}{Q} \log \left( -\frac{1 + {\rm e}^{x Q} - {\rm e}^{2 x Q} \pm \sqrt{\Delta({\rm e}^{x Q})}}{2 {\rm e}^{x Q}} \right) \, ,$$ where $k \in {\mathbb Z}$ such that $0 \le k \le P Q -1$, the function $\Delta(X)$ is defined by $$\label{Delta}
\Delta(X) = 1- 2 X - X^2 - 2 X^3 + X^4 \, ,$$ and the branch of the logarithm is chosen in such a way that $\Im (y_{\pm,0}(x)) \in (0, \, 2 \pi/Q]$ for each value of $x \in {\mathbb R}$. Note that $\Delta(X)$ is the same function introduced in Eq. [\[classicD\]](#classicD){reference-type="eqref" reference="classicD"}, which appears in the formula for the solutions to the classical $A$-polynomial of the figure-eight knot in Eq. [\[classicP\]](#classicP){reference-type="eqref" reference="classicP"} and in the formula for the function $\varphi_Q(X)$ obtained via the matrix formalism of Section [\[sec: 3rd-order\]](#sec: 3rd-order){reference-type="ref" reference="sec: 3rd-order"} in Eq. [\[8varphiQ\]](#8varphiQ){reference-type="eqref" reference="8varphiQ"}. Besides, the functions $y_{\pm, k}(x)$ only depend on $x$ through ${\rm e}^{x Q}=X^Q$.

We denote $y_{\pm}(x) = y_{\pm, 0}(x)$ for simplicity, so that $$y_{\pm, k}(x) = y_{\pm}(x) + 2 \pi {\rm i}\frac{k}{Q} \, , \quad 0 \le k \le P Q -1 \, .$$ For $y_{\pm, k}^{(\epsilon)}(x) =y_{\pm, k}(x) + \epsilon$ and $\epsilon\rightarrow 0^+$, we have the asymptotic expansions $$\frac{1}{G\left( x, y_{\pm, k}^{(\epsilon)}(x) \right)} = \mp \frac{1}{\epsilon Q \sqrt{\Delta({\rm e}^{x Q})}} + {\cal O}(\epsilon^0) \, , \quad 0 \le k \le P Q -1 \, .$$ Note that the leading-order term in the expansion above does not depend on the integer $k$. The integral in Eq. [\[int-lemma\]](#int-lemma){reference-type="eqref" reference="int-lemma"} can then be evaluated via the sum of residues of the integrand in Eq. [\[integrand\]](#integrand){reference-type="eqref" reference="integrand"} at the singularities in Eq. [\[y-sing\]](#y-sing){reference-type="eqref" reference="y-sing"}. We find in this way that $$\label{int-sol}
\begin{aligned}
\int_{{\mathbb R}+ {\rm i}0} f(y) dy &= 2 \pi {\rm i}\sum_{k=0}^{P Q - 1} \underset{y(x) = y_{\pm, k}(x)}{\text{Res}} \left( \frac{f(y) {\rm e}^{2 x Q}}{G(x,y)} \right)  \\
&= - \frac{2 \pi {\rm i}{\rm e}^{2 x Q} }{Q \sqrt{\Delta({\rm e}^{x Q})}} \sum_{k=0}^{P Q - 1}  {\rm e}^{-\frac{2 x k}{P}} \left( {\rm e}^{F_k(y_+(x))} - {\rm e}^{F_k(y_-(x))} \right) \, ,

\end{aligned}$$ where we have introduced the notation $$\label{Fk}
F_k(y(x)) = \frac{{\rm i}Q x y(x)}{\pi P} + \log \Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left( \frac{x - y(x)}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} - {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}\frac{k}{P} \right) - \log \Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}\left( \frac{y(x)}{2 \pi {\mathsf{b}}} + {\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}\frac{k}{P} \right) \, ,$$ for $0 \le k \le P Q -1$. Therefore, substituting Eq. [\[int-sol\]](#int-sol){reference-type="eqref" reference="int-sol"} into Eq. [\[41ak\]](#41ak){reference-type="eqref" reference="41ak"}, we obtain that the state-integral invariant of the figure-eight knot at rational values is given exactly by $$\label{state-fact}
\chi_{\bm{4}_1}(u;\hbar) = \frac{{\rm e}^{\frac{x^2 Q}{2 \pi {\rm i}P}+x\left( 2 Q -\frac{1}{2}-\frac{Q}{2 P}\right)} }{{\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}Q \sqrt{\Delta({\rm e}^{x Q})}} \sum_{k=0}^{P Q - 1}  {\rm e}^{-\frac{2 x k}{P}} \left( {\rm e}^{F_k(y_+(x))} - {\rm e}^{F_k(y_-(x))} \right) \, .$$ We conclude by noting that $\Phi_{{\mathsf{b}}}(z)$ for ${\mathsf{b}}^2 \in {\mathbb Q}_{>0}$ can be expressed explicitly in terms of more elementary functions [@garou-kas]. The relevant formula is written in Eq. [\[phib-rat\]](#phib-rat){reference-type="eqref" reference="phib-rat"} and can be used to simplify the computational implementation of Eq. [\[state-fact\]](#state-fact){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-fact"} for arbitrary $P, Q \in {\mathbb Z}_{>0}$ coprime.

We will now comment on the most notable features of the exact expression for the non-perturbative wave function in Eq. [\[state-fact\]](#state-fact){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-fact"}. It will be useful to introduce $$\label{state-pieces}
 \chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(\pm)}(u;\hbar) = \frac{{\rm e}^{\frac{x^2 Q}{2 \pi {\rm i}P}+x\left( 2 Q -\frac{1}{2}-\frac{Q}{2 P}\right)} }{{\rm i}{\mathsf{b}}Q \sqrt{\Delta({\rm e}^{x Q})}} \sum_{k=0}^{P Q - 1}  {\rm e}^{-\frac{2 x k}{P}} {\rm e}^{F_k(y_{\pm}(x))} \, ,$$ where again $x \in {\mathbb R}$, so that we write $$\label{difference}
\chi_{\bm{4}_1}(u;\hbar) = \chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(+)}(u;\hbar) - \chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(-)}(u;\hbar) \, .$$ Observe that the $S$-duality, which acts in the rational case via $P \leftrightarrow Q$ and $x \rightarrow x Q/P$, leaves $\Delta({\rm e}^{x Q})$ unchanged while transforming $y_{\pm, k}(x)$ into $y_{\pm, k}(x) Q/P$, and therefore the closed formula for the state integral of the $\bm{4}_1$-knot in Eq. [\[state-fact\]](#state-fact){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-fact"} is $S$-invariant, as expected. Similarly, we also find that its components in Eq. [\[state-pieces\]](#state-pieces){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-pieces"} are themselves invariant under $S$-duality. Moreover, we have that $$\Im(\chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(+)}(u;\hbar)) = \Im( \chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(-)}(u;\hbar)) \, ,$$ while both $\Re( \chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(\pm)}(u;\hbar) )$ and $\Im( \chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(\pm)}(u;\hbar) )$ are even functions of $x \in {\mathbb R}$ with singularities located at the points $$x = \frac{1}{Q} \log \left( \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2} \right) \, ,$$ which correspond to the zeros of $\Delta({\rm e}^{x Q})$ in Eq. [\[Delta\]](#Delta){reference-type="eqref" reference="Delta"} and, as such, do not depend on the value of $P$. The discontinuities of $\Re( \chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(\pm)}(u;\hbar) )$ cancel with each other to give a smooth difference function in Eq. [\[difference\]](#difference){reference-type="eqref" reference="difference"}, which is itself symmetric under $x \rightarrow -x$. Remarkably, we find that the total state integral $\chi_{\bm{4}_1}(u;\hbar)$ is a real-valued smooth square-integrable function of $x \in {\mathbb R}$, while both functions $\chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(\pm)}(u;\hbar)$ are neither real-valued, smooth, or square-integrable when considered separately. To illustrate these properties with an example, we show in Fig. [1](#fig: state-unit){reference-type="ref" reference="fig: state-unit"} the functions in Eqs. [\[state-fact\]](#state-fact){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-fact"}, [\[state-pieces\]](#state-pieces){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-pieces"} for the benchmark choice $P=Q=1$. The same features and similar functional profiles are verified for various choices of $P, Q \in {\mathbb Z}_{>0}$ coprime. We show in Fig. [2](#fig: state-full){reference-type="ref" reference="fig: state-full"} the full non-perturbative wave function for a sample of small values of ${\mathsf{b}}^2 \in {\mathbb Q}_{>0}$.

We observe that $\chi_{\bm{4}_1}(u;\hbar)$ has a Gaussian-type profile centered at $x=0$ with lateral oscillations around the $x$-axis, which are quickly dampened. The peak at the origin can be evaluated directly from Eqs. [\[y-sing\]](#y-sing){reference-type="eqref" reference="y-sing"}, [\[Fk\]](#Fk){reference-type="eqref" reference="Fk"}, and [\[state-fact\]](#state-fact){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-fact"} for arbitrary $P, Q \in {\mathbb Z}_{>0}$ coprime, yielding a result which is left unchanged by $P \leftrightarrow Q$, as expected from the $S$-invariance of the state integral and evident in Fig. [2](#fig: state-full){reference-type="ref" reference="fig: state-full"}. In particular, the highest peak corresponds to the choice $P=Q=1$ and amounts to $$\chi_{\bm{4}_1}(0; 2 \pi) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \sinh \left( \frac{V}{2 \pi} \right) \, , \quad V = 2 \, \Im \left( \text{Li}_2 \left( {\rm e}^{\frac{\pi {\rm i}}{3}}\right)\right) = 2.0298832 \dots \, ,$$ where $\text{Li}_2(z)$ is the standard dilogarithm and $V$ is the volume of the $\bm{4}_1$-knot, as expected [@garou-kas]. As ${\mathsf{b}}^2>1$ increases, the shape of the function $\chi_{\bm{4}_1}(u;\hbar)$ widens, while for decreasing ${\mathsf{b}}^2<1$, the shape tightens, and in both cases the peak lowers.

We point out that the structure of the exact wave function at rational values for the figure-eight knot obtained here is tantalizingly close to what has been found in the spectral theory of quantum mirror curves. The case of $\hbar=2 \pi$, corresponding to ${\mathsf{b}}^2=1$, was studied in detail in [@mz-wv; @mz-wv2]. As in Eq. [\[difference\]](#difference){reference-type="eqref" reference="difference"}, the wave functions in [@mz-wv; @mz-wv2] are the sums of two individual functions corresponding to two different choices of branch cut in the mirror curve. Each component function is singular when considered alone. Still, the singularities cancel each other in the sum, and one obtains a square-integrable wave function in the end, similar to the one plotted in Fig. [1](#fig: state-unit){reference-type="ref" reference="fig: state-unit"}. We observe that, in the case of the quantum mirror curves studied in [@mz-wv; @mz-wv2], one is working with exponentiated Heisenberg operators, and the resulting wave functions do not satisfy the nodal theorem for the Schrödinger operator typical of confining potentials, since the ground state changes sign in a small region. This same feature is also evident in our plots for the exact wave functions of the $\bm{4}_1$-knot at rational points shown in Fig. [1](#fig: state-unit){reference-type="ref" reference="fig: state-unit"} and Fig. [2](#fig: state-full){reference-type="ref" reference="fig: state-full"}. Such a property suggests that the wave function occurring in complex CS theory is a ground state for an appropriate spectral definition of the quantum $A$-polynomial. We will return to this point in the conclusions.

Finally, let us stress that the results above provide a third method for the computation of the exact wave function at rational values of $\hbar$ to be compared with the previous two presented in Section [\[sec: main\]](#sec: main){reference-type="ref" reference="sec: main"}. We conclude by verifying numerically that our closed formula in Eq. [\[state-fact\]](#state-fact){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-fact"} for the state integral of the figure-eight knot at rational values matches the results of Section [\[sec: 41\]](#sec: 41){reference-type="ref" reference="sec: 41"}. Specifically, the two components $\chi_{\bm{4}_1}^{(\pm)}(u;\hbar)$ in Eq. [\[state-pieces\]](#state-pieces){reference-type="eqref" reference="state-pieces"} can be identified with the two terms in the holomorphic/antiholomorphic block decomposition in Eq. [\[chi41\]](#chi41){reference-type="eqref" reference="chi41"}, which correspond to the conjugate and geometric classical branches, respectively. This identification will be formally clarified in the discussion in the following section.
