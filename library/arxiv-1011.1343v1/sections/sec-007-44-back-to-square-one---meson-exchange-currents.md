As told in section 1, I started my career calculating meson-exchange currents based on the method of unitary transformations. It is quite a nice turn of events that in chiral nuclear EFT one is now able to do this in a controlled and systematic manner, thanks to the power counting. In our approach, Stefan Kölling, Hermann Krebs and Evgeny Epelbaum have taken up the task - the two-pion exchange electromagnetic current based on this method is published in [@Kolling:2009iq] and together with the Cracow group led by Henryk Witala and Jacek Golak a thorough investigation of electro-nuclear processes is underway, for a first application to deuteron photodisintegration see [@Rozpedzik:2010kc]. However, the group around Rocco Schiavilla, using old-fashioned time-ordered perturbation theory, has taken the lead and performed calculations of the MECs of one- and two-pion range and applied this to magnetic moments of light nuclei [@Pastore:2008ui]. Most recently, they have calculated thermal neutron capture on $d$ and $^3$He [@Girlanda:2010vm]. The resulting radiative capture cross sections $\sigma_{nd}^\gamma$ and $\sigma_{n ^3He}^\gamma$ and the photon circular polarization parameter $R_c$, resulting from the capture of polarized neutrons on the deuteron, are shown in Fig. [1](#fig:MEX){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:MEX"} in comparison to two state-of-the-art conventional approaches. As the authors note, these processes are not the best or simplest to illustrate the convergence pattern of chiral nuclear EFT. So much more work is needed here and will be done.
