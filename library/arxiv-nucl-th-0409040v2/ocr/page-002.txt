The scattering of two particles with short-range interactions at sufficiently low energy
is determined by their S-wave scattering length a. Generically, the scattering length a is
comparable to the typical low-energy length scale of the system ¢ which, for short-range
interactions, is of the order of the range of the potential. In special cases, however, the
scattering length can be much larger, |a| >> ¢, due to a fine-tuning of the parameters in
the underlying interaction potential. Such a fine-tuning can be accidental as in the case of
two nucleons at low energy, or it can be controlled experimentally by varying an external
parameter such as a magnetic field in the case of Feshbach resonances for atomic systems.
