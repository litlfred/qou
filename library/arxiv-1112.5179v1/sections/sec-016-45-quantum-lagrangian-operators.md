Just as the index of the tetrahedron theory $T_\Delta$ is annihilated by two difference operators [\[teteqs\]](#teteqs){reference-type="eqref" reference="teteqs"}

$$\big(\hat p_{\pm}+\hat x_{\pm}{}^{-1}-1\big) \mathcal{I}_\Delta(m,e;q) \,=\, 0\,,$$

we find that the index of any 3-manifold theory $T_M$ satisfies pairs of difference equations

$$\hat \mathcal{L}_M^{(i)}(\hat p_\pm,\hat x_\pm;q^{\pm1})\cdot\mathcal{I}_M(m,e;q) \,=\, 0\,, \label{Meqs}$$

with $\hat x,\hat p$ as in Section [\[sec:ops\]](#sec:ops){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:ops"}. Generally, there are just as many pairs of equations as pairs of electric and magnetic flavor charges $(m_i,e_i)$ for $T_M$. Thus, once one knows $\mathcal{I}_M(m,e;q)$ at finitely many values of $(m,e)$, [\[Meqs\]](#Meqs){reference-type="eqref" reference="Meqs"} completely determine the index everywhere. Moreover, the difference equations [\[Meqs\]](#Meqs){reference-type="eqref" reference="Meqs"} govern the asymptotics of the index in a fairly simple way --- for example, the behavior of $\mathcal{I}_M(m,e)$ at large charges $(m,e)$, or as $q\to 1$. The equations always come in mutually commuting $\pm$ pairs due to the $\rho$ symmetry of the index.

Physically, difference equations for the index arise from identities in the algebra of line operators acting on $T_M$; these line operators will be the focus of Section [\[sec:3d4d\]](#sec:3d4d){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:3d4d"}. For now, we can understand the difference operators geometrically and combinatorially. The notation $\hat \mathcal{L}_M$ in [\[Meqs\]](#Meqs){reference-type="eqref" reference="Meqs"} is meant to be suggestive. Indeed, geometrically, the operators $\hat \mathcal{L}_M$ are just quantizations of the classical Lagrangians $\mathcal{L}_M$ that describe the subset of flat $SL(2,{\mathbb C})$ connections on the boundary $\partial M$ that can be extended as flat connections in the bulk:

$$\mathcal{L}_M = \{\text{flat conn$^{s}$ on $\partial M$ that extend to $M$}\}\quad \subset \; \mathcal{P}_{\partial M} \label{classLM}$$

(*cf.* [\[tetLag\]](#tetLag){reference-type="eqref" reference="tetLag"}). Such a Lagrangian is generically cut out by $\tfrac12 \dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal{P}_{\partial M}$ polynomial equations $\mathcal{L}_M^{(i)}(x,p)=0$ in the complex coordinates $(x_i,p_i)$ on $\mathcal{P}_{\partial M}$, and each of these equations leads to a pair of operators $\hat\mathcal{L}_M^{(i)}(\hat p_\pm,\hat x_\pm;q)$.

To explicitly construct the operators $\hat \mathcal{L}_M^{(i)}$, we can translate the gluing rules for the index $\mathcal{I}_M(m,e)$ from Section [\[sec:rules\]](#sec:rules){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:rules"} into gluing rules for operators. We find the following:

1.  For a triangulation $M=\{\Delta_i\}_{i=1}^N$, begin with $N$ pairs of operators

    $$\hat \mathcal{L}_{\Delta_i}(\hat x_\pm,\hat p_\pm;q^{\pm 1}) = \hat p_{i\,\pm}+\hat x_{i\,\pm}^{-1}-1\,. \label{tetiops}$$

    Each pair annihilates a tetrahedron index $\mathcal{I}_{\Delta_i}(m_i,e_i)$.

2.  The collection of all $N$ pairs [\[tetiops\]](#tetiops){reference-type="eqref" reference="tetiops"} annihilates the product index $\mathcal{I}_{\{\Delta_i\},\{\Pi_i\}}(m,e)$. We can say that the $\hat \mathcal{L}_{\Delta_i}$'s define a left ideal in the algebra of operators generated by $\{\hat x_{i\,\pm},\hat p_{i\,\pm}\}_{i=1}^N$, with nontrivial commutation relations

    $$\hat p_{i\,+}\hat x_{i\,+}=q\,\hat x_{i\,+}\hat p_{i\,+}\,,\qquad
    p_{i\,-}\hat x_{i\,-}=q^{-1}\hat x_{i\,-}\hat p_{i\,-}\,.$$

    All elements of this left ideal annihilate the product index.

3.  Change variables in the algebra of operators according to the change of polarization $\widetilde\Pi = g\circ\{\Pi_i\}$. That is, if $g=\sigma^R(\alpha)\,\sigma^F(\alpha)\,g_{Sp}$, define a new basis of logarithmic operators via the affine linear transformation

    $$\begin{pmatrix} \hat X_{i\,\pm}'\\\hat P_{i\,\pm}' \end{pmatrix} = g_{Sp}\begin{pmatrix} \hat X_{i\,\pm}\\\hat P_{i\,\pm} \end{pmatrix} \pm \alpha\big(i\pi+\tfrac\hbar2\big)\,,$$

    as in Section [\[sec:ops\]](#sec:ops){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:ops"}. We can then exponentiate to obtain the new basis of $q$-commuting operators $\hat x_{i\,\pm}'=\exp{\hat X_{i\,\pm}'},\, \hat p_{i\,\pm}'=\exp{\hat P_{i\,\pm}'}$ .

4.  Rewrite the tetrahedron Lagrangians [\[tetiops\]](#tetiops){reference-type="eqref" reference="tetiops"} in terms of the new $(\hat x_\pm',\hat p_\pm')$, obtaining $N$ pairs of operators

    $$\hat \mathcal{L}^{(i)}(\hat x_\pm',\hat p_\pm';q^{\pm 1})\,. \label{tetiops2}$$

    Due to the crucial intertwining property [\[intertwine\]](#intertwine){reference-type="eqref" reference="intertwine"} from Section [\[sec:ops\]](#sec:ops){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:ops"}, these $N$ pairs all annihilate the transformed product index $\mathcal{I}_{\{\Delta_i\},\tilde\Pi}(m',e') = \big(-q^{\frac12}\big)^{\langle\alpha,\gamma'\rangle}\mathcal{I}_{\{\Delta_i\},\{\Pi_i\}}(g_{Sp}^{-1}\gamma')$ of [\[Iprod2\]](#Iprod2){reference-type="eqref" reference="Iprod2"}. Note that the generators $(\hat x_\pm',\hat p_\pm')$ now act on $(m',e')$ as $\hat x_{i\,\pm}'=\exp\big(\tfrac\hbar2m_i'\mp\partial_{e_i'}\big)$ and $\hat p_{i\,\pm}'=\exp\big(\tfrac\hbar2e_i'\pm\partial_{m_i'}\big)$.

5.  Finally, suppose that the addition of a superpotential $\sum_I \mathcal{O}_I$ to $T_M$ breaks the $U(1)$ symmetries with electric charges $e_i'$ for $i=\tfrac12\dim\mathcal{P}_{\partial M}+1,...,N$. Then, working in the left ideal defined by the $N$ pairs [\[tetiops2\]](#tetiops2){reference-type="eqref" reference="tetiops2"}, eliminate the corresponding $\hat p_{i\,\pm}'$, and set $\hat x_{i\,\pm}' = q^{\pm 1}$. (Working in a left ideal means that we are allowed to add and subtract operators [\[tetiops2\]](#tetiops2){reference-type="eqref" reference="tetiops2"}, and to multiply only on the *left* --- since some index should be sitting on the right.) What remains are $\tfrac 12\dim \mathcal{P}_{\partial M}$ pairs

    [^1]

    of operators that only involve the fundamental generators $(\hat x_{i\,\pm}',\hat p_{i\,\pm}')$ for $i=1,...,\tfrac12\dim\mathcal{P}_{\partial M}$, corresponding to the charges $(e_i',m_i')$, $i=1,...,\tfrac12\dim\mathcal{P}_{\partial M}$ for the unbroken $U(1)$ symmetries of $T_M$. Call these remaining pairs of operators

    $$\hat \mathcal{L}_M^{(i)}(\hat x_\pm',\hat p_\pm ';q^{\pm 1})\,,\qquad i=1,...,\tfrac12\dim \mathcal{P}_{\partial M}\,. \label{rempairs}$$

    By construction, they will annihilate the final index $\mathcal{I}_M(m',e')$

The last step here --- elimination in an operator algebra --- may seem a little complicated. However, it follows directly from the final sum [\[indsum\]](#indsum){reference-type="eqref" reference="indsum"} defining the index $\mathcal{I}_M$. For every broken $U(1)$ symmetry, we set some $m_i'=0$. Then it no longer makes sense to shift this charge $m_i'$, so $\hat p_{i\,\pm}'$ must be eliminated from [\[tetiops2\]](#tetiops2){reference-type="eqref" reference="tetiops2"}. Moreover, we multiply the index by $q^{e_i'}$ and sum over electric charge sectors $e_i'$ for the broken $U(1)$. Acting on this sum, $\exp(\partial_{e_i'})$ is equivalent to multiplication by $q^{-1}$. Therefore,

$$\hat x_{i\,\pm}' \;\to\; q^{\pm1} \,,$$

just as dictated above. From now on, we will remove the "primes" from $(\hat x_\pm',\hat p_\pm')$ as well as from charges $(m',e')$ when discussing the final index $\mathcal{I}_M$.

The rules found here for constructing the "quantum Lagrangian operators" [\[Meqs\]](#Meqs){reference-type="eqref" reference="Meqs"} are identical to the construction of quantized Lagrangians discussed in [@Dimofte-QRS]. More precisely, we find here two independent copies of the quantized Lagrangians of [@Dimofte-QRS]: one involving fundamental generators $(\hat x_+,\hat p_+)$ and a quantization parameter $q=e^\hbar$, and another involving generators $(\hat x_-,\hat p_-)$ and a quantization parameter $q^{-1}=e^{-\hbar}$. This correspondence forms the basis of our argument in Section [\[sec:CS\]](#sec:CS){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:CS"} that the index is an $SL(2,{\mathbb C})$ Chern-Simons wavefunction.

To get a feeling for how quantized Lagrangians actually look, we can consider a few examples. First, let's take the bipyramid. By triangulating it into either 2 or 3 tetrahedra and applying the gluing rules above, we find two pairs of operators

$$\hat \mathcal{L}_{\rm bip}^{(1)} = \hat p_{1\,\pm}+\hat x_{1\,\pm}^{-1}\hat p_{2\,\pm}-1 \,,\qquad \hat \mathcal{L}_{\rm bip}^{(2)} = \hat p_{2\,\pm} + \hat x_{2\,\pm}^{-1}\hat p_{1\,\pm}-1\,.$$

that both annihilate the index $\mathcal{I}_{\rm bip}(m_1,m_2,e_1,e_2) = \mathcal{I}_\Delta(m_1-e_2,e_1)\,\mathcal{I}_\Delta(m_2-e_1,e_2)$ from [\[ind2\]](#ind2){reference-type="eqref" reference="ind2"}. A complete, detailed derivation of these Lagrangian operators appears in Appendix [\[app:L\]](#app:L){reference-type="ref" reference="app:L"}.

In the case of a knot complement $M_K$, we saw that the theory $T_K$ has a single $U(1)$ symmetry. In knot theory, the single equation that cuts out the classical Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_K$ is usually called the A-polynomial of $K$ [@cooper-1994], and correspondingly $\hat \mathcal{L}_K$ is the "quantum A-polynomial" [@garoufalidis-2004; @gukov-2003]. Conforming to the knot theory literature, let us denote the exponentiated operators acting on the index $\mathcal{I}_K(m,e)$ as

$$\hat M_\pm = e^{\hat U_\pm} \equiv \hat x_\pm = \exp\big(\tfrac\hbar2m\mp\partial_e\big)\,,\qquad  \hat\ell_\pm=-e^{\hat v_\pm} \equiv -\hat p_\pm =-\exp\big(\tfrac\hbar2e\pm\partial_m\big) \,.$$

Then, for example, it is easy to see that the index for the trefoil $\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{3_1}}(m,e) = \delta_{e,3m}$, is annihilated by

$$\hat \mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{3_1}\,+} = \hat \ell_++q^{\frac32}\hat M_+^3\,,\qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{3_1}\,-} = \hat \ell_-+q^{-\frac32}\hat M_-^3\,,$$

which are both quantizations of the classical A-polynomial $A =\ell+M^3$.

The figure-eight knot is a little less trivial. Following the above gluing rules (detailed in Appendix [\[app:L\]](#app:L){reference-type="ref" reference="app:L"}) leads to an operator

$$\hat \mathcal{L}_{\mathbf 4_1} = \big(q^{\frac12}\hat M-q^{-\frac12}\hat M^{-1}\big)\ell^{-1}-\big(\hat M-\hat M^{-1}\big)\big(\hat M^{-2}-\hat M^{-1}-q-q^{-1}-\hat M+\hat M^2\big)+\big(q^{-\frac12}\hat M-q^{\frac12}\hat M^{-1}\big)\hat\ell \label{A41M}$$

in its '$+$' version (with '$+$' subscripts suppressed). This is the well known quantum A-polynomial of the figure-eight knot [@garoufalidis-2004], in the normalization of [@DGLZ; @Dimofte-QRS]. It can be checked computationally that both [\[A41M\]](#A41M){reference-type="eqref" reference="A41M"} and its '$-$' version, obtained by sending $\hat M\to \hat M_-$, $\hat \ell\to\hat \ell_-$, $q\to q^{-1}$, annihilate the index $\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{4_1}}(m,e)$ in [\[ind41\]](#ind41){reference-type="eqref" reference="ind41"}. We emphasize that the above gluing rules actually *prove* algebraically that this must be the case.

[^1]: We slightly oversimplify the counting for purpose of exposition: in general the result of elimination may be $\geq \tfrac 12\dim \mathcal{P}_{\partial M}$ pairs of operators, even in the classical limit (*e.g.* if the equations are not a complete intersection).
