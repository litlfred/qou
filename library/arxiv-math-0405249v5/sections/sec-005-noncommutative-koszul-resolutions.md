[]{#koszull label="koszull"}

Propositions [\[tatata\]](#tatata){reference-type="ref" reference="tatata"} and [\[tor\]](#tor){reference-type="ref" reference="tor"} allow us to compute $HH_\ast^\sigma(\mathcal{A})$ for Hopf algebras $\mathcal{A}$ and diagonalizable $\sigma$ from a resolution of the trivial $\mathcal{A}$-module $k$. In the commutative case, such a resolution can be constructed in form of a Koszul complex associated to a minimal set of generators of $\mathrm{ker}\, \varepsilon$. We will see that one can proceed in the same way for quantum $SL(2)$. The general scheme of the construction of the resolution is as follows.

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an algebra and $d$ be a positive integer. Let $x_{i,j}$, $1 \leq i, j \leq d$ be elements of $\mathcal{A}$ satisfying $$\label{braid}
    x_{i,j}x_{i-1,k}=x_{i,k}x_{i-1,j}.$$ In the commutative case one can take $x_{i,j}=x_{1,j}$, and in many examples the $x_{i,j}$ will be uniquely determined by the $x_{1,j}$.

For $0 \leq n \leq d$, let $K_n(x_{i,j})$ be the $\mathcal{A}$-bimodule $\mathcal{A}^{(\!\!\!\tiny{\begin{array}{c} d \\ n\end{array}}\!\!\!)}$, which we identify for $n>0$ with the submodule of $\mathcal{A}^d \otimes_\mathcal{A}\cdots \otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^d$ ($n$ factors) spanned over $\mathcal{A}$ by $$e_{i_1} \otimes_\mathcal{A}\cdots \otimes_\mathcal{A}e_{i_n},\quad
    1 \le i_1 < \ldots < i_n \le d,$$ where $e_i$ is a basis of $\mathcal{A}^d$. For $n>d$ we set $K_n(x_{i,j}):=0$.

For an $\mathcal{A}$-bimodule $\mathcal{N}$, set $K_n(x_{i,j},\mathcal{N}):=
K_n(x_{i,j}) \otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}$ and define $\mathcal{A}$-module maps $$k_m : K_n(x_{i,j},\mathcal{N}) \rightarrow
    K_{n-1}(x_{i,j},\mathcal{N}),\quad m=1,\ldots,n$$ (we suppress the index $n$ at the $k_m$) by $$k_m : e_{i_1} \otimes_\mathcal{A}\cdots \otimes_\mathcal{A}e_{i_n} \otimes_\mathcal{A}y \mapsto
    e_{i_1} \otimes_\mathcal{A}\cdots
     \hat e_{i_m}  \cdots \otimes_\mathcal{A}e_{i_n} \otimes_\mathcal{A}
    y \triangleleft x_{n,{i_m}}.$$ Then for $r<s$: $$\begin{aligned}
&&(k_r k_s - k_{s-1} k_r)
    (e_{i_1} \otimes_\mathcal{A}\cdots \otimes_\mathcal{A}e_{i_n} \otimes_\mathcal{A}y) \nonumber\\
&=& e_{i_1} \otimes_\mathcal{A}\cdots  \hat e_{i_r}  \cdots
     \hat e_{i_s}  \cdots \otimes_\mathcal{A}e_{i_n} \otimes_\mathcal{A}
    y \triangleleft (x_{n,i_s}x_{n-1,i_r}-x_{n,i_r}x_{n-1,i_s}).\nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ The last bracket vanishes by ([\[braid\]](#braid){reference-type="ref" reference="braid"}). Thus we get

::: prop
[]{#serrek label="serrek"} The map $k:=\sum_{r=1}^{n} (-1)^r k_r$ makes $K_\ast(x_{i,j},\mathcal{N})$ into a complex which we call the Koszul complex associated to $x_{i,j}$ and $\mathcal{N}$.
:::

The zeroth homology of this complex is obviously the quotient of $\mathcal{N}$ by the submodule generated by all elements of the form $y \triangleleft x_{1,i}$, $y \in \mathcal{N}$, $i=1,\ldots,d$. The classical application of Koszul complexes is to produce resolutions of this quotient, but the Koszul complex is not always acyclic (see [@serre] for the commutative case). In our application we will take $\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{A}$ to be a Hopf algebra with the standard bimodule structure, and the $x_{1,j}$ ($1 \leq j \leq d$) will generate $\mathrm{ker}\,\varepsilon$ as a an (left or right) $\mathcal{A}$-module. The associated Koszul complex will be checked by hand to be acyclic (see Proposition [\[resolution\]](#resolution){reference-type="ref" reference="resolution"} below), so it provides a resolution of $\mathcal{A}/\mathrm{ker}\, \varepsilon$, and $\mathrm{Tor}^\mathcal{A}_\ast(\mathcal{M}',k)$ equals the homologies of the complex $\mathcal{M}' \otimes_\mathcal{A}K_\ast(x_{i,j},\mathcal{A})$. The quasi-isomorphism from this complex to the standard complex $(C_\ast,d)$ calculating the $\mathrm{Tor}$-groups described in the proof of Proposition [\[tor\]](#tor){reference-type="ref" reference="tor"} is then given by $$\label{morphism_of_complexes}
    e_{i_1} \otimes_\mathcal{A}\cdots \otimes_\mathcal{A}e_{i_n} \mapsto
    x_{1,i_1} \wedge \ldots \wedge x_{1,i_n} :=
    \sum_{s \in S_n} (-1)^{|s|}
    x_{n,i_{s(n)}} \otimes \cdots \otimes x_{1,i_{s(1)}}.$$

# Quantum $SL(2)$ {#section_slq2_suq2}

In this section, we introduce the main facts on the standard quantized coordinate ring $\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(SL_q (2))$ that will be used below.

## the hopf algebra $\mathcal{A}(SL_q (2))$ {#section_sl2su2}

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero, and $q \in k$ some nonzero parameter, which we assume is not a root of unity. The coordinate algebra $\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(SL_q (2))$ of the quantum group $SL_q (2)$ over $k$ is the $k$-algebra generated by symbols $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$ with relations $$ab=qba, \quad
ac=qca, \quad
bd=qdb, \quad
cd=qdc, \quad
bc=cb$$ $$\label{ad=}
ad-qbc=1, \quad
da - q^{-1} bc=1$$ There is a unique Hopf algebra structure on $\mathcal{A}$ such that $$\Delta(a)=a \otimes a + b \otimes c,\quad \Delta(b)=a \otimes b+b \otimes d,$$ $$\Delta(c)=c \otimes a+d \otimes c,\quad \Delta(d)=c \otimes b+d \otimes d,$$ $$\varepsilon(a)=\varepsilon(d)=1,\quad \varepsilon(b)=\varepsilon(c)=0,$$ $$S(a)=d,\quad S(b)=-q^{-1}b,\quad S(c)=-qc,\quad S(d)=a.$$ A vector space basis of $\mathcal{A}$ is given by the monomials $$\label{ZNN_filtration}
    e_{i,j,k}:=a^i b^j c^k,\quad i \in \mathbb{Z}, \; j,k \in \mathbb{N},\quad
    a^i:=d^{-i}\quad\mbox{for}\quad i<0,$$ (we use the convention that $x^0 =1$, for $x \in \mathcal{A}$, $x \neq 0$). We have $$e_{i,j,k}e_{l,m,n}=q^{-l(j+k)}e_{i+l,j+m,k+n}+
    \sum_{r>0} \lambda_{i,j,k,l,m,n}(r) e_{i+l,j+m+r,k+n+r}$$ for some constants $\lambda_{i,j,k,l,m,n}(r)$. It follows that $\mathcal{A}$ admits a $\mathbb{Z}$-grading and three separating decreasing $\mathbb{N}$-filtrations $$\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{i \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathcal{A}^a_i,\quad
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^x_0 \supset \mathcal{A}^x_1 \supset \ldots,\quad
x=b,\; c,\; bc,$$ where $\mathcal{A}^a_i=\mathrm{span}\{e_{i,j,k}\}_{j,k}$ and $\mathcal{A}^x_n$ is the span of $e_{i,j,k}$ with $j,k,j+k \ge n$ for $x=b,c,bc$, respectively. For $x \in \mathcal{A}$, let $x_i$ be its component in $\mathcal{A}^a_i$. Set $\mathcal{A}^x_{i,n}:=\mathcal{A}^x_n \cap
\mathcal{A}^a_i$. Then $\mathcal{A}^x_{i,n}\mathcal{A}^x_{j,m}=\mathcal{A}^x_{i+j,n+m}$. Define a Hermitian inner product on $\mathcal{A}$ by requiring that $e_{i,j,k}$ are orthonormal and let $\pi_x$, $\pi_{i,j,k}$, denote the orthogonal projections onto $(\mathcal{A}^x_1)^\perp$, $e_{i,j,k}$. We freely consider $\pi_{i,j,k}$ as a map $\mathcal{A}\rightarrow k$. Note that $\pi_x(y)_i=\pi_x(y_i)$ for all $y \in \mathcal{A}$.

Finally, $\mathcal{A}$ has a $\mathbb{Z}^2$-grading given by $$\label{Z^2_grading}
\deg( e_{i,j,k}) = (i, j-k)$$ This grading extends to $\mathcal{A}^{\otimes (n+1)}$ and is preserved by the Hochschild boundary and the maps $B_n$ ([\[B_n\]](#B_n){reference-type="ref" reference="B_n"}). Hence $HH_\ast^\sigma(\mathcal{A})$ and $HC_\ast^\sigma(\mathcal{A})$ are naturally $\mathbb{Z}^2$-graded.
