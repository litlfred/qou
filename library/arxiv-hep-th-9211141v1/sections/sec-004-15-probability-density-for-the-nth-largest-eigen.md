An elementary probability argument shows that if $F(n;s)$ ($n=0,1,\cdots$) denotes the probability density for the (scaled) position of the $n^{th}$ largest eigenvalue ($n=0$ is the largest), then $$F(0;s) = {d E(0;s)\over ds}$$ and for $n\geq 1$ $$F(n;s)-F(n-1,s)={d E(n;s)\over ds}\, .$$

From the preceding expressions for $E(n;s)$ in terms of $q(s;1)$ and $q_n(s)$ we can derive formulas for $F(n;s)$ which can in turn be numerically evaluated to produce tables of values for these probability densities. We have done this for $n=0$ and $n=1$ and in Fig. 1 we plot $E(0;s)$ and $E(1;s)$ and in Fig. 2 we plot $F(0;s)$ and $F(1;s)$.
