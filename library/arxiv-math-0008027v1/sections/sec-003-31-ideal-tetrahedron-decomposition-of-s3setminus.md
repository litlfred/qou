We will decompose $S^3\setminus({K\cup\text{two points}})$ into tetrahedra so that all the vertices are either on $K$ or on the two points outside $K$.

First we put an octahedron ${\rm E}_i$-${\rm A}_i{\rm B}_i{\rm C}_i{\rm D}_i$-${\rm F}_i$ at the $i$th crossing of the knot diagram so that ${\rm E}_i{\rm F}_i$ is perpendicular to the plane, that ${\rm E}_i$ and ${\rm F}_i$ touch the over-crossing point and the under-crossing point respectively, and that ${\rm A}_i$, ${\rm B}_i$, ${\rm C}_i$, and ${\rm D}_i$ are to the northeast, northwest, southwest, and southeast, respectively. (see Figures [1](#fig:fig8_octa){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:fig8_octa"} and [3](#fig:octa_plus_minus){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:octa_plus_minus"}). We call this the $i$th octahedron.

![Put octahedra at crossings.](fig8_octa.eps){#fig:fig8_octa}

We decompose each octahedron into four tetrahedra as shown in Figure [2](#fig:octa){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:octa"}. (For the figure-eight knot, we now have 16 tetrahedra.) We name each vertex of the tetrahedra as in Figure [2](#fig:octa){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:octa"}. (Here we drop subscriptions.)

![Decompose an octahedron into four tetrahedra.](octa.eps){#fig:octa}

We deform an octahedron attached to a positive crossing as follows. We pull the vertices ${\rm B}$ and ${\rm D}$ upward and identify the edge ${\rm EB}$ with the edge ${\rm ED}$. Similarly we pull the vertices ${\rm A}$ and ${\rm C}$ downward and identify the edge ${\rm FA}$ with the edge ${\rm FC}$. (See Figure [3](#fig:octa_plus_minus){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:octa_plus_minus"}.)

![Pull the pairs of vertices to $\pm\infty$.](octa_plus_minus.eps){#fig:octa_plus_minus}

Now the resulting *twisted octahedron* consists of four *twisted tetrahedra* (Figure [4](#fig:twisted_tetra){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:twisted_tetra"}).

<figure id="fig:twisted_tetra">
<p><embed src="twisted_tetra3.eps" /> <embed src="twisted_tetra2.eps" /> <embed src="twisted_tetra4.eps" /> <embed src="twisted_tetra1.eps" /></p>
<figcaption>Twisted tetrahedra.</figcaption>
</figure>

The surface of a twisted octahedron consists of four *leaves* made from $\triangle{b^+}{b^r}{b^l}\cup\triangle{c^+}{c^r}{c^l}$, $\triangle{c^-}{c^l}{c^r}\cup\triangle{d^-}{d^l}{d^r}$, $\triangle{d^+}{d^r}{d^l}\cup\triangle{a^+}{a^r}{a^l}$, and $\triangle{a^-}{a^l}{a^r}\cup\triangle{b^-}{b^l}{b^r}$, which we will call Leaf $\gamma$, Leaf $\delta$, Leaf $\alpha$, and Leaf $\beta$, respectively (Figure [5](#fig:twisted_octa){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:twisted_octa"}).

![A twisted octahedron and its four leaves.](twisted_octa.eps){#fig:twisted_octa}

Now this twisted octahedron and the near positive crossing look like Figure [6](#fig:twisted_octa_cross){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:twisted_octa_cross"}.

![A twisted octahedron and a crossing.](twisted_octa_cross.eps){#fig:twisted_octa_cross}

For an octahedron attached to a negative crossing we deform it similarly. We pull the vertices ${\rm B}$ and ${\rm D}$ downward, and ${\rm A}$ and ${\rm C}$ upward to identify the edge ${\rm EB}$ with the edge ${\rm ED}$, and the edge ${\rm FA}$ with the edge ${\rm FC}$. In this case Leaf $\alpha$ is made from $\triangle{d^-}{d^l}{d^r}$ and $\triangle{a^-}{a^l}{a^r}$, and Leaves $\beta$, $\gamma$, and $\delta$ are from $\triangle{a^+}{a^r}{a^l}$ and $\triangle{b^+}{b^r}{b^l}$, $\triangle{b^-}{b^l}{b^r}$ and $\triangle{c^-}{c^l}{c^r}$, and $\triangle{c^+}{c^r}{c^l}$ and $\triangle{d^+}{d^r}{d^l}$, respectively.

Now the whole picture for the figure-eight knot is as shown in Figure [7](#fig:fig8_twisted_octa){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:fig8_twisted_octa"}.

![Figure-eight knot with twisted octahedra.](fig8_twisted_octa.eps){#fig:fig8_twisted_octa}

Next we identify two leaves facing each other along an arc by thickening the octahedra and shortening the arc. For the figure-eight knot case, we identify Leaves $\delta_1$ and $\alpha_2$, $\gamma_2$ and $\alpha_3$, $\gamma_3$ and $\beta_4$, $\delta_4$ and $\beta_2$, $\delta_2$ and $\alpha_1$, $\gamma_1$ and $\alpha_4$, $\gamma_4$ and $\beta_3$, and $\delta_3$ and $\beta_1$. Then the eight vertices ${\rm A_1}$, ${\rm C_1}$, ${\rm A_2}$, ${\rm C_2}$, ${\rm B_3}$, ${\rm D_3}$, ${\rm B_4}$, and ${\rm D_4}$ are identified. We call the resulting vertex $+\infty$. Similarly the eight vertices ${\rm B_1}$, ${\rm D_1}$, ${\rm B_2}$, ${\rm D_2}$, ${\rm A_3}$, ${\rm C_3}$, ${\rm A_4}$, and ${\rm C_4}$ are identified with the new vertex $-\infty$. Moreover the other eight vertices $E_1, F_1,\dots,E_4,F_4$ are also identified, which we call ${\rm G}$.

Now we have an ideal tetrahedron decomposition of $S^3\setminus({K\cup\pm\infty})$ with three ideal vertices. The boundary torus of the regular neighborhood of ${\rm G}$ and the boundary spheres of the regular neighborhoods of $\pm\infty$ look like Figures [8](#fig:nbd_K){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:nbd_K"}, [9](#fig:nbd_plus){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:nbd_plus"}, and [10](#fig:nbd_minus){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:nbd_minus"} respectively if $K$ is the figure-eight knot. Note that we always look at boundaries from the outside.

![Boundary of the regular neighborhood of ${\rm G}$. (The wavy lines, the dotted lines, and the sidelines are identified respectively.)](snake.eps){#fig:nbd_K}

Boundary of the regular neighborhood of $-\infty$ (right).](nbd_minus.eps){#fig:nbd_minus}

## Ideal tetrahedron decomposition of $S^3\setminus{K}$

We will deform the ideal tetrahedron decomposition of $S^3\setminus({K\cup\pm\infty})$ given above to obtain an ideal tetrahedron decomposition of $S^3\setminus{K}$.

To do this we collapse Leaf $\beta_1$ (= Leaf $\delta_3$) into the point $G=E_1=F_1=\dots=E_4=F_4$ and extend this collapsing over the other tetrahedra linearly. Note that this leaf corresponds to the broken point of the knot diagram to make it a $(1,1)$-tangle in § [\[sec:algebra\]](#sec:algebra){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:algebra"}.

I will show how octahedra will collapse.

In the first octahedron ${\rm E_1}$-${\rm A_1B_1C_1D_1}$-${\rm F_1}$, there are two faces $\triangle{a^+_1}{a^r_1}{a^l_1}$ and $\triangle{b^+_1}{b^r_1}{b^l_1}$ which make Leaf $\beta_1$. Therefore the tetrahedron ${\rm F_1E_1A_1B_1}$ and ${\rm F_1E_1B_1C_1}$ (they are the cones over $\triangle{a^+_1}{a^r_1}{a^l_1}$ and $\triangle{b^+_1}{b^r_1}{b^l_1}$ with ${\rm F_1}$) are collapsed to edges and so three edges ${\rm F_1A_1}$, ${\rm F_1B_1}$ and ${\rm F_1C_1}$ are identified with the edge ${\rm F_1E_1}$. The tetrahedron ${\rm F_1E_1C_1D_1}$ and ${\rm F_1E_1D_1A_1}$ (they are the joins ${\rm E_1C_1}*{\rm D_1F_1}$ and ${\rm E_1A_1}*{\rm D_1F_1}$) are collapsed to triangles and so two triangles ${\rm F_1D_1C_1}$ and ${\rm F_1A_1D_1}$ are identified with the triangle ${\rm E_1F_1D_1}$. So the first octahedron is collapsed to the triangle ${\rm F_1E_1D_1}$. See Figure [11](#fig:collapsed_octa1){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:collapsed_octa1"}.

![The first octahedron is collapsed to the triangle ${\rm F_1E_1D_1}$. (The shaded triangles and the wavy lines are collapsed.)](collapsed_octa1.eps){#fig:collapsed_octa1}

Similarly the third octahedron is collapsed to the triangle ${\rm F_3E_3B_3}$ since it contains the faces $\triangle{c^-_3}{c^l_3}{c^r_3}$ and $\triangle{d^-_3}{d^l_3}{d^r_3}$ which make Leaf $\delta_3$. Here two triangles ${\rm E_3B_3C_3}$ and ${\rm E_3A_3B_3}$ are identified with the triangle ${\rm F_3E_3B_3}$.

On the other hand the edge ${\rm E_4C_4}$ is identified with the edge ${\rm C_3F_3}={\rm A_3F_3}$, which is contained in Leaf $\delta_3$. Moreover the edge ${\rm B_4A_4}$ is identified with the edge ${\rm C_1B_1}$, which is contained in Leaf $\beta_1$. Therefore the fourth octahedron is collapsed to the tetrahedron ${\rm F_4E_4D_4A_4}$ as in Figure [12](#fig:collapsed_octa4){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:collapsed_octa4"}. Here the tetrahedron ${\rm F_4E_4B_4C_4}$ is collapsed to the triangle ${\rm F_4E_4B_4}$, which is identified with the triangle ${\rm F_4E_4B_4}$. Then the tetrahedron ${\rm F_4E_4A_4B_4}$ is collapsed to the triangle ${\rm F_4E_4A_4}$. The tetrahedron ${\rm F_4E_4C_4D_4}$ is collapsed to the triangle ${\rm F_4E_4D_4}$.

![The fourth octahedron is collapsed to the tetrahedron ${\rm F_4E_4D_4A_4}$. (The wavy lines are collapsed.)](collapsed_octa4.eps){#fig:collapsed_octa4}

Similarly the second octahedron is collapsed to the tetrahedron ${\rm F_2E_2B_2C_2}$ since two edges ${\rm E_2A_2}$ and ${\rm C_2D_2}$ are collapsed to points respectively.

After all only two tetrahedron ${\rm F_2E_2B_2C_2}$ and ${\rm F_4E_4D_4A_4}$ survive.

Now we want to know the torus boundary of the regular neighborhood of this new vertex. To do that we first look at the regular neighborhood of Leaf $\beta_1$ (= Leaf $\delta_3$). Figures [13](#fig:nbd_octa1){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:nbd_octa1"} and [14](#fig:nbd_octa4){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:nbd_octa4"} indicate how it appears in the first and the fourth octahedra respectively and how it is collapsed if we collapse the leaf to a point.

![The regular neighborhood of Leaf $\beta_1$ (= Leaf $\delta_3$) in the fourth octahedron.](nbd_octa4.eps){#fig:nbd_octa4}
