SIMULATION
The LBW process’s polarized scattering amplitude, with
arbitrary initial photon polarization and ﬁnal positron (elec-
tron) spins, can generally be described through the photon and
lepton density matrixes. Stokes parameters and mean spin
vectors can be utilized to describe the arbitrary polarization
states of the photon and e± in the cross section. To establish
a connection between theoretical predictions and experimen-
tal procedures, a numerical simulation method was developed
using MC sampling, which permits the consideration of beam
eﬀects stemming from the energy spread and initial polariza-
tion distributions of photon beams. The no-time-count method
was employed to implement the numerical simulation, con-
verting the macro beam-beam collision into the micro single
scattering [31]. Our MC simulation strategy for binary colli-
sion is described in detail in [2, 30].
