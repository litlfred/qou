*What can we say about the representations of the symmetric groups $\mathfrak{S}(n)$ in the limit $n\to\infty$?* This very general question is the subject of investigations of *the asymptotic representation theory of the symmetric groups*. Even though for almost any question of the representation theory of the symmetric groups the answer is known, usually this answer is given by a combinatorial algorithm (for example, Murnaghan-Nakayama rule or Littlewood-Richardson rule) involving manipulations with boxes of a Young diagram. As $n$, the number of boxes, tends to infinity, such combinatorial algorithms become very cumbersome and it is not easy to extract from them some reasonable asymptotic answer. For this reason one has to look for new, alternative approaches, which would less depend on the details of boxes of a given Young diagram, but rather on its 'global' features.
