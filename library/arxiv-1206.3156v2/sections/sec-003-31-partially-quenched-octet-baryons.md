While we see that this is a relatively straightforward generalisation of eq. (4),
we note that the term proportional to B0 remains unchanged. In addition the
C0 term also depends entirely on sea terms, while the C1 and C2 terms are a
mixture of sea and valence terms. Thus if we wish to determine these coeﬃcients
we must vary the sea quark masses; to determine the other coeﬃcients it is
suﬃcient to vary the valence quark masses alone, while keeping the sea quark
masses constant. So this gives the possibility of extending the (computationally
expensive) sea quark mass simulations with (computationally cheaper) valence
quark mass simulations to determine most of the coeﬃcients. If we work on a
single sea background, then the C0 term can be absorbed into the M2
0 term, while
the C1 and C2 terms can be absorbed into the A1 and A2 terms. If we vary the
sea quark masses this allows a determination of these coeﬃcients. However due
to the constraint m = const., or equivalently eq. (6), δmq cannot vary much and
we know from [13] that in this range the LO dominates, so these coeﬃcients are
diﬃcult to determine and contribute just noise. So practically we shall ignore
these terms in ﬁts. (Alternatively, the constraint m = const. could be relaxed,
but then we have additional expansion coeﬃcients, which we wish to avoid.) Thus
in this article we regard the NNLO terms as ‘control’ on the LO and NLO terms.
