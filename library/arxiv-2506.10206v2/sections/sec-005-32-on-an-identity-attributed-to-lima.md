The $u = 
 \sqrt{2}$ case of Theorem [\[maintheorem\]](#maintheorem){reference-type="ref" reference="maintheorem"}, we obtain, from the $A$-value involved in Theorem [\[maintheorem\]](#maintheorem){reference-type="ref" reference="maintheorem"} together with the duplication formula in [\[duplicationformula\]](#duplicationformula){reference-type="eqref" reference="duplicationformula"}, that $$\begin{gathered}
 \operatorname{Li}_2\left(-i\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\right)\right) - \operatorname{Li}_2\left(-i\left(-1 + \sqrt{2}\right)\right) = \\ 
 \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{Li}_2\left(-3 - 2 \sqrt{2}\right) + \frac{\pi^2}{6} + \frac{1}{2} \ln^2\left(i\left(\sqrt{2} - 1\right)\right) 
\end{gathered}$$ and that $$\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1 - \sqrt{2}}{2} 
 \right) = -\operatorname{Li}_2\left(3 - 2 \sqrt{2}\right) - \frac{1}{2} \ln^2\left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$$ For the right-hand side of the identity in [\[mainABCD\]](#mainABCD){reference-type="eqref" reference="mainABCD"}, the associated integral admits an evaluation that can be derived using a factorization property associated with roots lying on the unit disk, yielding $$\int_0^1 \frac{(1 - 3x^2) \ln(x)}{(x^2 - 2)(x^4 + 1)} \, dx = 
 \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4} \left( -2 \, \operatorname{Li}_2\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right) + \frac{7\pi^2}{24} - \frac{(\ln(2))^2}{4} \right)$$ We thus obtain the formula $$\label{attributedLima}
 \operatorname{Li}_2\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right) - \operatorname{Li}_2\left( 1 - \sqrt{2} \right) - 
 \frac{\pi^2}{6} + \frac{\ln^2(2)}{8} - \frac{1}{2} \ln(2) \ln\left( \sqrt{2} - 1 \right) = 0$$ that has been attributed to Lima [@Lima2012] and subsequently explored by Stewart [@Stewart2022] and generalized by Lima [@Lima2024] and that can be derived using a classical relation for the Legendre's $\chi$-function together with the Landen transform in [\[Landenformula\]](#Landenformula){reference-type="eqref" reference="Landenformula"}.
