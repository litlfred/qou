\label{sec:geometry}

To begin with, we must understand the homology theory that governs the thimbles associated to the function $V$ and the state integrals $\calI_{m,\ell}$. All of the topology is concentrated near $z\in\BR$ in the coordinates used in Equation~\eqref{eq:V}. There are logarithmic branch cuts at $z\in\BZ$ thus the first homology of $\Sigma$ is not finitely generated. However, for a given $\vartheta\in (-\pi/2,\pi/2)\cup(\pi/2,3\pi/2)$, we are only interested in a finitely generated subgroup. These groups can be described and depend on $\vartheta$ but allows for a decomposition of the thimbles associated to $V$ into state integral contours.
