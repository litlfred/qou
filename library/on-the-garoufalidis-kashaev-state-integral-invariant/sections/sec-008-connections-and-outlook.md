While the focus of this thesis is on a specific construction, the state-integral
approach touches on several areas of broader mathematical interest:
• Quantum topology: The construction provides different quantum invariants
than those usually derived from modular categories and skein-theoretic methods
(SL2(C) as opposed to compact gauge groups) [18].
• Teichm¨ uller theory:The angle-deformed quantum dilogarithm Ψ a,c corre-
sponds to the charged tetrahedra operator defined in [1], section 4, which is
related to functions arising in quantum Teichm¨ uller theory (Faddeev quantum
dilogarithm [5]).
• Arithmetic topology: Since the construction applies to both Archimedean
and non-Archimedean local fields, it suggests arithmetic parallels to topological
quantization frameworks.
Some potential directions for future investigation include:
• Explorations in positive characteristic: It seems interesting to study the
theory for the case of local fields in positive characteristic, where the authors
of [7] put forward ideas related to counting representations into PSL 2(Fp) and
number theory around Fp for finite fields Fp.

• Classification of self-dual LCA groups: Understanding which self-dual 
groups admit a Gaussian exponential could clarify structural aspects of har-
monic analysis on such groups. Characterizing self Pontryagin dual groups 
remains a Sisyphean open problem [10].
• Categorical interpretation: The symmetries of Ψa,c suggest possible cate-
gorical interpretations, perhaps involving ⋆-autonomous or quantale-enriched 
categories [8], [20].
• Quantum computation: Given its discrete and compositional nature, the 
state-integral could be studied as a toy model for certain quantum compu-
tational architectures, particularly those leveraging topological or continuous-
variable encodings.
