Pontryagin Characters and Gauss Characters
A Pontryagin character is a continuous homomorphism χ : L −→T, T := {z ∈C |
|z| = 1}, so χ(x + y) = χ(x)χ(y). The set of all such characters forms the Pontryagin
dual bL, an LCA group in its own right.
Gauss characters (quadratic exponentials) on G
A Gauss character on the Gaussian group G is a map ⟨·⟩: G −→T with Evenness
⟨−g⟩= ⟨g⟩; polarisation ⟨g + h⟩= ⟨g⟩⟨h⟩⟨g; h⟩, ⟨g; h⟩:=
⟨g+h⟩
⟨g⟩⟨h⟩, where ⟨g; h⟩is a
symmetric bicharacter (a Pontryagin character in each slot).
Hence ⟨·⟩is in general quadratic, not linear.
Fix h ∈G. The map g 7−→⟨g; h⟩is a Pontryagin character of G. Thus the
polarisation induces a group homomorphism Φ : G −→bG, h 7−→(g 7→⟨g; h⟩).
If the bicharacter ⟨g; h⟩is non-degenerate (no non-zero element pairs trivially with
everything), then Φ is an isomorphism. In that situation G is self-dual via its Gauss
character.
Example G = R ⟨x⟩:= eπix2 =⇒⟨x; y⟩= e2πixy.
For each y ∈R, g 7→e2πiyg is a Pontryagin character; the map y 7→(g 7→e2πiyg)
identifies R with bR.
