Let M be a compact, oriented 3-manifold equipped with an ordered ideal tri-
angulation X. The analytic target of the Archimedean state-integral depends on
the topology and boundary structure of M, and reflects the deformation parameters
introduced via the angle-dependent quantum dilogarithm Ψ a,c.

5.2.1 Manifolds with Torus Boundary (∂M ∼=T 2)
For manifolds with toroidal boundary components:
• The quantum state-integral acquires dependence on longitudinal–meridional
holonomy data via
H1(∂M ;C)∼=C2, whereC = R×F×.
Here,C is the group parameterizing the angle structure—specifically, the addi-
tive and multiplicative angle holonomies associated to each peripheral direction
of the boundary torus. In this framework, holonomies are not generic complex
numbers but reflect the geometric data assigned by the state-integral construc-
tion.
• After enforcing the internal balancing (gluing) equations, the state-integral
yields a holonomy-labeled quantum invariant
FF,M : H1(∂M ;C)−→ C.
This function is well-defined for each (λ,µ) ∈ H1(∂M ;C), corresponding to
choices of longitudinal and meridional angle holonomy data on the boundary.
• For each such pair (λ,µ), the quantum state-integral is defined as a sum over
all compatible assignments of internal angle structures that glue to produce the
prescribed boundary holonomies.
