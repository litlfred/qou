By cellularity of $\SpheAlg(t) $ we have $ \dim \SpheAlg(t) = \sum_{ \lambda \in \Lambda^k } (\dim e_k \Delta_k(\lambda) )^2 $, which via Theorem \ref{lemma2} and Theorem \ref{stateandproveA} becomes the following identity involving $ bp_k $ \begin{equation}\label{415} bp_k = \sum_{ \lambda \in \Par_l \subseteq \Lambda^k }\biggl ( \sum_{i=l}^k \sum_{\substack{ \nu \in \Par_i \\ \Psi(\nu) \in \Par_l }} K_{\lambda, \Psi(\nu)} p_{{k-i}} \biggr)^2. \end{equation} It may be surprising that the identity \eqref{415} can in fact be proved with combinatorial tools, as we shall now briefly explain.
