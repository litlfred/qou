A homomorphism f : Λ → Λ′ is called an isogeny if it is an (f.p.p.f.)
epimorphism with ﬁnite locally free kernel.
Let p be a prime number. Λ is a p-divisible group (or Barsotti-Tate
group) if Λ = lim−→n
Ker[pn] and for all n, the multiplication [pn] by pn on Λ is
an isogeny.
IfS is connected, the rank of Ker[ pn] is then of the form phn, where h is an
integer called the height of Λ.
From the fact that [p] is an isogeny, it follows that for p-divisible groups,
HomS(Λ , Λ′) is a torsion-free Zp-module.
A quasi-isogeny of p-divisible groups Λ , Λ′ is a global section ρ of the
Zariski sheaf Hom
S(Λ , Λ′)⊗Z Q such that there exists locally an integer n
for which pnρ is an isogeny. By abuse, one writes ρ : Λ → Λ′ as for homo-
morphisms. We denote by qisog S(Λ , Λ′) the Qp-space of quasi-isogenies.
The objects of the category of p-divisible groups on S up to isogeny
are the p-divisible groups over S; morphisms between Λ and Λ ′ are global
sections of Hom S(Λ , Λ′)⊗Z Q. This is a Qp-linear category.
