5.4. Conclusion and vista.
We have seen many instances of the following scenario: some f amiliar
notion or phenomenon from the complex realm shows itself in t wo diﬀer-
ent aspects; each of these aspects has a natural p-adic counterpart; these
counterparts are not complementary aspects of the same p-adic entity, but
belong to totally diﬀerent theories.
In II, we shall see that the theory of p-adic period mappings is a priv-
ileged ﬁeld where this semantic splitting process integrat es into an harmo-
nious picture, where the complementarity between the p-adic counterparts
is restored at a deeper level.
Thep-adic period mappings which we shall deal with relate some de for-
mation spaces ofp-divisible groups to certain grassmannians. It will turn ou t
that they can be described by quotients of analytic solution s of the Gauss-
Manin connection, just as in the complex case. In particular , at the modular
level, the Fontaine-Messing periods (which links up directly the ´ etale and
crystalline representations of p-divisible groups) go oﬀstage or remain at the
