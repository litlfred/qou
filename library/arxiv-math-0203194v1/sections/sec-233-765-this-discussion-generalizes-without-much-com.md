7.7. Conclusion.
Roughly speaking, the period mapping P attaches to each member of an
algebraic family of algebraic complex varieties (with addi tional structure) a
point in some ﬂag space, which encodes the Hodge ﬁltration in the cohomol-
ogy of this variety. This mapping can be expressed in terms of quotients of
solutions of the fuchsian diﬀerential equation (Picard-Fu chs/Gauss-Manin)
which controls the variation of the cohomology.
We have described the theory of period mappings for p-divisible groups
in somewhat similar terms.
Sometimes, it is possible to go through the looking-glass of sheer analo-
gies. This occurs when we restrict our attention to p-divisible groups at-
tached to polarized abelian varieties with prescribed endo morphisms and
ﬁxed “Shimura type”. In this situation, we have three algebr aic objects
deﬁned over a number ﬁeld (the reﬂex ﬁeld):
(a) the Shimura variety Sh parametrizing certain “decorated” abelian
varieties,
(b) the Gauss-Manin connection ∇GM describing the variation of these
abelian varieties over Sh, and
(c) the ﬂag variety D∨.
These objects are transcendentally related, both in the com plex and
p-adic sense.
The period mapping may be viewed as multivalued and deﬁned on p-
adic open domains in Sh; it is given as in the complex case by quotients of
solutions of the Gauss-Manin connection (as a p-adic connection).
In most situations, these open domains are small and P is single-valued.
