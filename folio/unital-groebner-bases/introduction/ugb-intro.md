There have been several generalizations of Gröbner--Shirshov basis theory, coming in one
of two flavors: noncommutative theory and theories for working over special
ground rings, e.g. Euclidean domains, PID's or UFD's. Over a field, one of the
main uses of a Gröbner--Shirshov basis is to find a basis for the quotient of an algebra $A$
by a (left or two-sided) ideal $I$.

If one regards the motivating examples closely, one sees that the ground field
$k$ never enters into the picture. Specifically, one does not need to invert any
constants, and this leads to the notion of a **unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis**. The key
observation is that for many natural quotient algebras — polynomial rings
modulo monomial ideals, universal enveloping algebras — the Gröbner--Shirshov basis
argument works over an arbitrary commutative ground ring $R$, provided the
leading coefficients are units.
