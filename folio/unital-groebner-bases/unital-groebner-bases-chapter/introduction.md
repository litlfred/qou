There have been several generalizations of Gröbner--Shirshov basis theory,
coming in one of two flavors: noncommutative theory and theories for
working over special ground rings, e.g.\ Euclidean domains, PID's or
UFD's. Over a field, one of the main uses of a Gröbner--Shirshov basis is to
find a basis for the quotient of an algebra $A$ by a (left or
two-sided) ideal $I$.

A consideration of the standard examples shows that the ground field
$k$ never enters into the picture. Specifically, one does not need to
invert any constants, and this leads to the notion of a *unital
Gröbner--Shirshov basis*. We view the inability of classical Gröbner--Shirshov basis
techniques to apply to these mild generalizations as an unsatisfactory
state of affairs.
