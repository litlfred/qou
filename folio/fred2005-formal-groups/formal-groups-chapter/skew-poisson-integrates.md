Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p > 0$. A formal group $G$ over $k$
is **geometric** if there are commutative formal subgroups $H_i \subseteq G$,
$i \in I$, with $G \cong \prod_i H_i$ as formal varieties; its underlying
commutative group is $H = \prod_i H_i$ (\cite{leitner2006}, Definition 3.1).

**Theorem** (\cite{leitner2006}, Theorem 3.7). Let $\{H_i, H\}$ be a
commutative geometric formal group over $k$, and let $\pi$ be a strongly
filtered, [strongly multiplicative](#def:strong-multiplicativity)
[Poisson structure](#def:skewed-poisson-structure) on the splay $T$ of the
$\mathrm{Dist}(H_i)$. Let $J \subseteq T$ be the ideal
$$
  J = \bigl(fg - gf - \pi(f, g) \;\big|\; f, g \in T\bigr).
$$
Then $U = T / J$ is the distribution algebra of a
[formal group](#def:non-commutative-formal-group) $G$, and $\{H_i, G\}$ is a
geometric formal group. A Poisson morphism of such splays induces a
morphism of the resulting geometric formal groups.

Conversely, a geometric formal group $\{H_i, G\}$ gives a strongly
filtered, strongly multiplicative Poisson structure on the splay of the
$\mathrm{Dist}(H_i)$ (\cite{leitner2006}, Lemma 3.5), independent of choices
up to equivalence (Proposition 3.6). The commutator bracket $\pi_c$ returns
$U \cong T$ unchanged.

What is formalised: the construction $U = T / J$ and the identity
$[f, g]_U = \pi(f, g)$ in $U$. What is cited: that $U$ is
$\mathrm{Dist}(G)$ of a formal group.

Reference: \cite{leitner2006}, §3.
