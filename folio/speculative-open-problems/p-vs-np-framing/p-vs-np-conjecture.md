*Let $\varepsilon > 0$ be the positive infinitesimal in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No}$ \cite{conway1976} with $q = 1 + \varepsilon$, and let $\mathrm{BQP}_q$ denote a posited class of decision problems solvable using polynomial skein resources over a Reeb period $\Omega_q \asymp \varepsilon^{-1}$ \cite{freedman2003}. This speculative formulation posits $\mathbf{P} \neq \mathbf{NP}$ as the failure of $\mathrm{BQP}_q$ computations to compress under the standard-part map $\mathrm{st}_{\varepsilon \to 0}$ into $\mathbf{P}$ ([obstruction-residue counterpart](#conj:qou-p-neq-np)).*

**Heuristic motivation and why it is not a proof.** Positing a simulation cost $2^n \delta_n^2 \Omega_q \asymp 2^n \delta_n^2 \varepsilon^{-1}$ against a dissipation budget $2^n \Delta_q \asymp 2^n \varepsilon^3$ invokes $\delta_n^2 \varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon)$. Neither the inclusion $\mathbf{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{BQP}_q$ nor the lower bound $2^n \delta_n^2 \Omega_q$ on classical deterministic simulation is derived from Turing-machine semantics, and multiplying both sides of $\delta_n^2 \varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ by $2^n$ cancels $2^n$ immediately, leaving a purely algebraic inequality in $\varepsilon$ with no dependence on input size $n$ or circuit depth.
