**What this chapter says about P versus NP, and what it does not.** Nothing in the Quantum Observable Universe monograph \cite{leitner2026} or in this chapter proves $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$, and nothing here supplies evidence for either answer. Throughout, $\mathrm{P}$ and $\mathrm{NP}$ denote the classical complexity classes for deterministic and nondeterministic Turing machines \cite{cook1971}. No Turing-machine model of those classes is constructed here: wherever an abstract predicate mentions $\mathrm{P}$ or $\mathrm{NP}$, they enter as opaque propositions, and the proved content reduces to propositional logic or pure algebra.

**Proved algebraic facts (retained in the main monograph).** The algebraic results that motivate the dictionary below hold independently of complexity theory:

1. Every trace vanishes on commutators, every derivation annihilates central idempotents and preserves each Wedderburn block, and inner automorphisms fix central idempotents while the Goldman--Iwahori involution $\theta(\sigma_i) = -\sigma_i^{-1}$ is outer for $n \ge 2$ and $q + q^{-1} \neq 0$.
2. Evaluating the trace of a product of $c$ matrices of size $d \times d$ requires at most $d^3 c$ ring multiplications, so any family of braids on a bounded number of strands has $O(c)$ trace evaluation cost and an empty skein-tree horizon; any family with super-polynomial skein-trace cost requires unbounded braid index.
3. Exact evaluation of the Jones polynomial $V_L(t)$ is $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard outside six roots of unity of orders $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ \cite{jaeger1990} (corresponding under $t = q^{\pm 2}$ to orders $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8\}$, which is not $\{n : n \mid 6\}$).
4. Because $2^n$ dominates every polynomial and $2^n \le 3^{3n}$, any restricted procedure forced to inspect all $3^{3n}$ leaves of a skein resolution tree runs in super-polynomial time.

**Why none of these reduces or settles P versus NP.**

- *Counting versus decision.* $\#\mathrm{P}$-hardness of exact Jones evaluation \cite{jaeger1990} does not separate decision classes: counting perfect matchings in bipartite graphs is $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard while deciding whether one exists lies in $\mathrm{P}$. Any uniform counting-to-decision transfer would also face the relativization \cite{bakergillsolovay1975}, natural-proofs \cite{razborovrudich1997}, and algebrization \cite{aaronsonwigderson2009} barriers.
- *Fixed-rank algebra rigidity.* At a split semisimple parameter $q$ \cite{geckpfeiffer2000}, $H_n(q)$ is separable, so $HH^i(H_n(q), M) = 0$ for $i \ge 1$ and outer derivations vanish; any nonzero polynomial obstruction vanishes at only finitely many parameter values. Uniform asymptotic complexity concerns growth as $n \to \infty$ across all algorithms, which no invariant of a fixed-rank algebra $H_n(q)$ can encode.
- *Unfixed QOU observation cost model.* If one QOU observation of a $c$-crossing braid returned its exact degree-$0$ Markov trace at linear intent cost $O(c)$, QOU polynomial time would compute $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard invariants and contain $\mathrm{NP}$, making QOU-internal $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$ false; if instead observation returns Born-rule samples, the model behaves like quantum polynomial time $\mathrm{BQP}$, where separation from $\mathrm{NP}$ remains open.
