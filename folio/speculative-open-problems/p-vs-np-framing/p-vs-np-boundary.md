*Let $L = \hat{\beta}$ be the closure of a braid $\beta$ encoding a decision instance \cite{cook1971,freedman2003}, and let $\mathcal{H}_c(L, q) = \overline{\mathcal{R}^\circ(L, q)} \setminus \mathcal{R}^\circ(L, q)$ denote the frontier of its polynomial observable skein-tree subcategory. The speculative boundary conjecture posits the dictionary correspondence*

$$
  \mathrm{P} = \mathrm{NP} \Longleftrightarrow \mathcal{H}_c(L, q) = \varnothing \text{ for every NP link family } L,
$$

*and at a non-torsion real parameter $q_0 \in \mathbb{G}_m^{\mathrm{nt}}(\mathbb{R})$, posits the existence of an $\mathrm{NP}$-complete family $\{L_N = \hat{\beta}_N\}_{N \ge 1}$ of unbounded strand width with $\mathcal{H}_c(L_N, q_0) \neq \varnothing$ for all $N \ge N_0$.*

**Scope.** The horizon $\mathcal{H}_c(L, q)$ is defined with respect to skein-tree evaluation of a single link invariant. Non-emptiness of $\mathcal{H}_c(L_N, q_0)$ expresses super-polynomial cost for that skein-tree evaluation procedure; equating it to $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$ over all uniform Turing machines is an unproved dictionary postulate, and the formal backing proves only the logical dichotomy between emptiness and non-emptiness of an abstract predicate.
