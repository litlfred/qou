This companion paper isolates the speculative formulations of classical open problems that arise alongside the Quantum Observable Universe (QOU) monograph \cite{leitner2026}. None of the constructions recorded here proves, resolves, or reduces a Millennium Prize problem or the Collatz conjecture. Every algebraic theorem on which the analogies draw remains in the main monograph under a neutral mathematical name; this paper holds the problem-specific dictionaries, conjectural translations, and heuristic surreal-number comparisons so that proved algebra and speculative framing are never conflated.

Five open-problem families and one cross-problem comparison are treated:

1. **Yang--Mills existence and mass gap.** Elementary positivity $\tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})(q - 1)^2 > 0$ for $q > 1$ is a rational inequality on the crossing-energy functional, and the categorical mass-gap structure takes its lower bound as a postulated field rather than deriving a four-dimensional Wightman quantum field theory on $\mathbb{R}^4$.
2. **The Riemann hypothesis.** Weil's bound confines the Frobenius eigenvalues of a smooth projective curve over $\mathbb{F}_p$ to $|\alpha_i| = p^{1/2}$, and for Bring's curve those eigenvalues build $L(E, s)^4$ in $\zeta_{\mathcal{B}}(s) = \zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)/L(E, s)^4$ for the elliptic curve $50\mathrm{a}1$ rather than $\zeta(s)$. Neither local Frobenius unitarity nor the surreal inequality $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ bears on the zeros of $\zeta(s)$.
3. **The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.** Vanishing of the categorical invariant $r_q = \mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, E_q)$ follows from the categorical observability condition $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0$ on a representation-theoretic Hom group, not on the arithmetic Mordell--Weil group $E(\mathbb{Q})$. Rank zero for the rational elliptic curve $50\mathrm{a}1$ is a classical theorem of Kolyvagin, not a consequence of QOU axioms.
4. **P versus NP.** Exact evaluation of the Jones polynomial is $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard at generic parameters \cite{jaeger1990}, and matrix-word trace evaluation on a fixed number of strands takes $O(c)$ multiplications. Neither $\#\mathrm{P}$-hardness of counting nor Hochschild rigidity of a fixed-rank Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$ supplies a uniform counting-to-decision separation, and the computational power of a QOU depends on an unfixed choice of observation cost model.
5. **The Collatz problem.** Specialising the affine $[2]_q$-division map $\mathcal{C}_q$ on $\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]$ at $q = 1$ recovers the classical Syracuse map, so classical convergence implies $q$-convergence on domain-stable lifts rather than the converse. The contact-average threshold $\langle v_{[2]} \rangle \geq 2$ fails on the fundamental cycle $\{q^{-1}, 1 + q^{-1}\}$, where $\langle v_{[2]} \rangle = 1$.
6. **Cross-problem surreal and Kummer tables.** The surreal squeeze $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ is a generic monomial inequality in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon)$ that uses no analytic property of the problem to which it is applied; the Navier--Stokes row compares two fixed rates rather than squeezing a drift $\delta$ to zero; one-dimensionality of $HH^2 \cong k$ does not align evaluation maps landing in distinct cohomology groups; and sharing the abstract record of a length-filtered observation system couples two properties of a single dynamical instance rather than linking separate problems.

Navier--Stokes regularity is not moved here: the fluid-dynamics chapter of \cite{leitner2026} retains its $q$-deformed Navier--Stokes PDE model directly, scoped as an algebraic-regularisation model whose classical $q \to 1$ blow-up transfer remains open. Hodge and Poincaré conjectures are not treated in either paper.
