A recurring speculative proposal in earlier drafts treated the classical-limit obstructions for the Riemann hypothesis, Navier--Stokes regularity, Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, and [P versus NP](#conj:qou-p-neq-np) as evaluations of one generator $\chi(A) \in HH^2 \cong k$. Even under the hypothesis $\dim_k HH^2 = 1$, that shared source transfers no verdict between the problems:

1. **Distinct target groups.** One-dimensionality of the domain $HH^2 \cong k$ implies only that every class in $HH^2$ is a scalar multiple of a single generator, so the image of each evaluation map lies in a one-dimensional subspace of *its own* codomain. Because the proposed evaluation maps land in distinct groups ($H^2(\mathcal{S}, \mu_n)$, local cohomology $H^3_{\mathrm{loc}}$, and formal skein-tree residues) with no canonical isomorphism between them, one evaluation map can vanish identically while another is injective.
2. **Simultaneous opposite verdicts in the Kummer tower.** On Bring's surface ($\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(\Sigma_4) = -6$), the Kummer reduction $-6 \bmod n \in H^2(\Sigma_4, \mu_n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$ vanishes at $n \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\}$ and is nonzero at every $n \nmid 6$, while a generic real parameter $q_0 > 1$ sits outside all roots of unity. One class therefore yields vanishing and non-vanishing reductions simultaneously, refuting any claim that sharing $\chi(A)$ forces uniform answers across problems.
