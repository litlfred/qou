The four drift-squeeze surreal heuristics—[Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer](#rem:bsd-surreal-bound), [the Riemann hypothesis](#rem:rh-surreal-bound), [P versus NP](#rem:p-vs-np-surreal-bound), and [Collatz](#rem:collatz-surreal-bound)—apply the same ordered-field inequality $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No}$ \cite{conway1976} while indexing their formal descriptions by iterated-integral depth ($0$ for parity-step counting, $1$ for logarithmic zero density, $2$ for elliptic periods, and higher braid depth for multi-strand traces). That depth assignment organises the heuristic vocabulary alongside the Gröbner--Shirshov period-depth conjecture of the main monograph; because each individual row rests on unproved cost-scaling postulates, the depth indexing is taxonomic rather than deductive.
