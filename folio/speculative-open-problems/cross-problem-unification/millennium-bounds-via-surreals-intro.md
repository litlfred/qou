Earlier exposition grouped six classical open problems—the Riemann hypothesis, Navier--Stokes regularity, the Collatz problem, $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$, Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, and Yang--Mills existence and mass gap—under three cross-problem comparison tables: a surreal $\omega$-order table built on $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ \cite{conway1976}, a Hochschild--Kummer obstruction comparison built on $HH^2 \cong k$, and a length-filtration comparison built on an abstract observation-system record.

None of those three constructions links the truth values of the classical problems or reduces one problem to another:

1. **Why $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ does not unify or settle the rows.** For any standard real $\delta > 0$ and positive infinitesimal $\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No}$, $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ is an elementary monomial inequality in an ordered field. It uses no analytic, arithmetic, or complexity-theoretic property of the problem to which $\delta$ is attached, and in every drift-squeeze row the scaling $E(\delta) \asymp \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1}$ is postulated rather than derived. Moreover, the Navier--Stokes row is not even an instance of that drift-vanishing schema: its cost column carries no $\delta$ at all, comparing a fixed enstrophy scaling $\varepsilon^{-4/3}$ against $\varepsilon^3$ to argue that an excess occurs rather than that a drift vanishes.
2. **Why $HH^2 \cong k$ does not align verdicts across problems.** One-dimensionality of a source group $HH^2 \cong k$ does not make evaluation maps into distinct target groups proportional to one another. Even within the Kummer tower of Bring's surface ($\chi_{\mathrm{eu}} = -6$), the reduction $-6 \bmod n$ vanishes at $n \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\}$ and is nonzero for $n \nmid 6$, producing opposite verdicts simultaneously from one class.
3. **Why sharing a length-filtration record does not couple separate problems.** Packaging a state transition map, a length function $\ell$, and a trace observation into a common abstract record couples boundedness and periodic-attractor descent *within one dynamical instance* (a halting trajectory on a periodic attractor visits finitely many states and is therefore bounded). Instantiating that record on distinct systems—Laurent polynomials under $\mathcal{C}_q$ versus brane vortex words—shares a type signature, not a dynamical state, and transfers no theorem between Collatz, $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$, and Navier--Stokes.
