**Status: heuristic order comparison, not a proof.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$ be the positive infinitesimal in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No}$ \cite{conway1976} with $q = 1 + \varepsilon$, Reeb period $\Omega_q \asymp \varepsilon^{-1}$, and crossing-energy gap $\Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3$. For a hypothetical zero $\rho$ of $\zeta(s)$ with standard-real off-line drift $\delta = \mathrm{Re}(\rho) - 1/2 > 0$, positing a relocation cost $E(\delta) \asymp \delta^2 \Omega_q \asymp \delta^2 \varepsilon^{-1}$ against a dissipation budget $\Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3$ gives

$$
  \delta^2 \cdot \varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3
$$

in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon)$, equivalently $\ordw(E(\delta)) = 1 > -3 = \ordw(\Delta_q)$, leaving $\delta = 0$ as the only standard real with $E(\delta) \le \Delta_q$.

**Why this is not a proof.** Neither modelling assumption is derived: nothing in the QOU framework attaches an energy functional $E(\delta) \asymp \delta^2 \Omega_q$ to a zero of $\zeta(s)$ or restricts zeros to configurations below $\Delta_q$, and the local Frobenius exponents of Bring's curve belong to $L(E, s)$ for $50\mathrm{a}1$ rather than $\zeta(s)$. Decisively, the inequality $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ holds for every standard real $\delta > 0$ without invoking a single property of $\zeta(s)$—neither its Euler product nor its Dirichlet coefficients. The identical lines would exclude off-line zeros for the Davenport--Heilbronn function, which satisfies a functional equation and possesses zeros off the critical line. An inequality that cannot distinguish the [classical Riemann hypothesis](#conj:classical-riemann-hypothesis) from a known counterexample on a sibling Dirichlet series carries no evidence for it.
