Nothing in the Quantum Observable Universe monograph \cite{leitner2026} or in this chapter proves the classical Riemann hypothesis, and no construction here reduces it to a categorical or geometric theorem. Separating the proved finite-field curve algebra from the speculative global assertions locates each gap explicitly:

1. **Finite-field curve zetas versus the Riemann zeta function.** For a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}$, Weil's theorem \cite{weil1948} constrains the Frobenius eigenvalues $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{2g}$ on $H^1$ to $|\alpha_i| = p^{r/2}$, equivalently $\mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = 1/2$ under $\alpha_i = p^{r\rho_i}$. For Bring's genus-four curve $\mathcal{B}$, whose Jacobian is isogenous to $E^4$ for the elliptic curve $E : y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - x - 2$ (Cremona $50\mathrm{a}1$), assembling the local curve zeta functions across good primes gives, up to finitely many Euler factors,

$$
  \zeta_{\mathcal{B}}(s) = \frac{\zeta(s)\,\zeta(s - 1)}{L(E, s)^4}.
$$

The Frobenius eigenvalues of $\mathcal{B}$ build $L(E, s)$, whereas $\zeta(s)$ enters only through $H^0$, which carries no Frobenius eigenvalue other than $1$. Local critical-line alignment at each prime therefore concerns the $L$-series $L(E, s)$—whose own global Riemann hypothesis in $1/2 < \mathrm{Re}(s) < 3/2$ remains open—and supplies no constraint on the non-trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$.

2. **Finite-field cardinality versus the aether parameter.** In the local curve zeta function over $\mathbb{F}_p$, the parameter is a prime cardinality, not a continuous real deformation parameter $q > 1$; there is no continuous limit $p \to 1$.

3. **Unconstructed spectral operators and generic surreal monomials.** No Reeb operator $\xi_q$ whose spectrum equals the non-trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ is constructed. Similarly, the surreal inequality $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ holds for every standard real $\delta > 0$ without using any property of $\zeta(s)$, so it would apply verbatim to functions such as the Davenport--Heilbronn Dirichlet series that satisfy a functional equation and Euler-type symmetries fails while possessing zeros off the critical line.
