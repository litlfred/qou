*Let $E$ be the elliptic curve $y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - x - 2$ (Cremona $50\mathrm{a}1$), whose fourth power $E^4$ is isogenous to the Jacobian of Bring's curve. Suppose that at every prime $p \nmid 10$ of good reduction the Frobenius trace $a_p = p + 1 - |E(\mathbb{F}_p)|$ satisfies $a_p^2 \le 4p$. Then every zero $s$ of the Hasse--Weil $L$-function $L(E, s)$ in the critical strip $\tfrac{1}{2} < \mathrm{Re}(s) < \tfrac{3}{2}$ satisfies $\mathrm{Re}(s) = 1$.*

**Why the statement targets $L(E, s)$ rather than $\zeta(s)$.** At each good prime $p$, the local zeta numerator of Bring's curve is $(1 - a_p T + pT^2)^4$, and multiplying the local curve zeta functions over primes gives $\zeta(s)\zeta(s - 1)/L(E, s)^4$ up to bad Euler factors. The Frobenius traces $a_p$ enter only through $L(E, s) = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n n^{-s}$. An earlier version of this conjecture concluded the [classical Riemann hypothesis](#conj:classical-riemann-hypothesis) for $\zeta(s)$ from local exponent alignment; because $\zeta(s)$ is the $H^0$ factor and shares no Frobenius data with the antecedent, that earlier version reduced on any aligned input to asserting the classical Riemann hypothesis outright and is retained only as a documented fallback.

**The implication carries the entire open distance.** The antecedent $a_p^2 \le 4p$ holds unconditionally at every prime by Hasse's theorem \cite{weil1948}, whereas the global Riemann hypothesis for $L(E, s)$ is open. Writing the statement as an implication displays the local input alongside the global conclusion; it does not bring the global conclusion closer to proof.
