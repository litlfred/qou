*Every non-trivial zero $s$ of the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ satisfies $\mathrm{Re}(s) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.*

The statement is the classical Millennium Problem, which remains open. Neither the main monograph nor this paper proves any step toward it or assumes a proof of it. Weil's bound gives $\mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = 1/2$ for the local Frobenius exponents of Bring's curve at each prime of good reduction separately, but those exponents assemble into $L(E, s)$ for the elliptic curve $50\mathrm{a}1$ rather than into $\zeta(s)$.
