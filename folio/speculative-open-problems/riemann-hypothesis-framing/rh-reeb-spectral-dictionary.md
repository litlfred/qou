Two heuristic pictures motivate the conjectural statements above, and neither is a mathematical reduction of the [classical Riemann hypothesis](#conj:classical-riemann-hypothesis):

1. **Reeb holonomy unitarity as a picture of Weil's bound.** Over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$, the local curve zeta function has reciprocal roots of modulus $p^{1/2}$ by the Castelnuovo--Severi positivity inequality on the correspondence divisor \cite{weil1948,rosen2002}. Interpreting those Frobenius eigenvalues as oscillation modes of a Reeb flow pictures $\mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = 1/2$ as unitary balance without exponential growth or decay. That picture neither constructs a Hilbert--Pólya Reeb operator for $\zeta(s)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ nor transfers local Frobenius unitarity across primes ([adelic persistence](#conj:adelic-critical-line-persistence)).
2. **The $q$-explicit formula analogy.** Replacing $\log p$ by a $q$-deformed Chebyshev weight $\psi_q(X)$ and summing oscillatory waves $X^\rho / [\rho]_q$ imitates Weil's explicit formula for prime-counting fluctuations ([coupled stability conjecture](#conj:macroscopic-stability-equivalence)). When written over a finite eigenvalue list, the $-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln_q(1 - X^{-2})$ tail diverges as $X \to 1^+$ against bounded finite sums, refuting the finite-list form; an infinite-sum formulation requires analytic continuation and conditional-convergence control not supplied by the algebraic QOU axioms.
