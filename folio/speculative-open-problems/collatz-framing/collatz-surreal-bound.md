**Status: formal analogy only; orbit-termination conclusion withdrawn.** Earlier versions of this remark ended by concluding that all classical Collatz orbits terminate at $1$ \cite{lagarias2010}. That conclusion is withdrawn ([chapter introduction](#sec:collatz-intro)).

Setting $q = 1 + \varepsilon$ in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No}$ \cite{conway1976} and positing a per-step contact-volume drift cost $V(\delta) \asymp \delta^2 \varepsilon^{-1}$ against $\Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3$ gives $\delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3$ ($\ordw = 1 > -3$) for every standard real $\delta > 0$. Neither scaling is derived from [the partial Laurent map $\mathcal{C}_q$](#conj:q-collatz), no invariant of a Laurent or integer trajectory is shown to satisfy $V(\delta) \le \Delta_q$, and passing from $\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]$ to $\mathbb{N}$ via a standard-part map assumes the existence of a domain-stable lift that sparse integer checks already exclude for general $n \in \mathbb{N}$. The surreal inequality compares two $\varepsilon$-monomials and rules out no trajectory.
