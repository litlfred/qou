For combinatorial $q$-integers $[m]_q = 1 + q + \cdots + q^{m-1}$, elementary polynomial algebra gives:

- $[3]_q - q[2]_q = 1$, so $\gcd([2]_q, [3]_q) = 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}[q]$;
- $[2]_q^2 - [3]_q = q > 0$ for every real $q > 0$;
- $[2]_q^3 - [3]_q = q^3 + 2q^2 + 2q$ fails to stay positive across higher $(p, 2^k)$ comparisons once degree mismatches flip the leading term (for example, $[3]_q$ versus $[8]_q$ reverses sign for large $q$).

These inequalities are statements about the polynomials $[m]_q$ at real $q > 0$, not theorems about convergence of [the $q$-Collatz map](#conj:q-collatz) \cite{collatz1937,lagarias2010}. As noted in [the withdrawn contact-threshold block](#conj:collatz-contact-boundary), the contact-average criterion with which they were once paired fails on the fundamental cycle, and the parity sequence of any domain-stable orbit is fixed at $q = 1$.
