*Every domain-stable nonzero element $P \in \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]$ under the partial map $\mathcal{C}_q$ eventually reaches one of the periodic orbits $\{0\}$, $\{-q^{-2}\}$, or $\{q^{-1}, 1 + q^{-1}\}$ \cite{collatz1937,lagarias2010}.*

**Direction of implication with classical Collatz.** By the Syracuse-lift theorem of the main monograph, evaluating a domain-stable orbit at $q = 1$ produces the exact classical Syracuse trajectory of $n_0 = P(1)$, and eventual periodicity of that integer trajectory forces $P$ onto one of the three Laurent cycles above. Accordingly, classical Collatz convergence implies this conjecture on all domain-stable lifts with $P(1) > 0$, whereas proving this conjecture would yield classical convergence only for the sparse subset of integers $n_0$ that admit a domain-stable Laurent lift ([chapter introduction](#sec:collatz-intro)).

**Heuristic contraction and Kummer caveats.** Evaluating $[2]_q^2 - [3]_q = q$ at a real $q > 1$ motivates a heuristic contraction picture when halvings outnumber triplings, but because the parity sequence of a domain-stable orbit equals the classical parity sequence of $P(1)$ independently of $q$, the $q$-deformation does not alter the combinatorial parity problem. Similarly, vanishing of the level-$2$ Kummer class $-6 \bmod 2 = 0$ on Bring's surface is an arithmetic divisibility property of $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}} = -6$ that settles no orbit \cite{aaronsonwigderson2009,grochow2017,razborovrudich1997}.
