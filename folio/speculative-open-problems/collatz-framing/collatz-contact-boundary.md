**Status: false as stated; retained as a documented negative record.** Earlier drafts proposed that a domain-stable orbit under [the $q$-Collatz map](#conj:q-collatz) converges if and only if its [orbit contact average](#def:collatz-contact-boundary) satisfies $\langle v_{[2]} \rangle(P) \ge 2$.

That biconditional fails on the attractor itself: the fundamental cycle $\{q^{-1}, 1 + q^{-1}\}$ converges trivially while having $\langle v_{[2]} \rangle = 1 < 2$. The threshold $2$ double-counted the division by $[2]_q$ already built into the odd branch $([3]_q P + 1)/[2]_q$; subtracting that built-in division lowers the classical heuristic threshold to $1$, and every domain-stable orbit with infinitely many odd steps satisfies $\limsup \langle v_{[2]} \rangle \le 1$ by polynomial degree accounting ([chapter introduction](#sec:collatz-intro)). Meanwhile, the formal module backing this label proves only that $2 \mid \chi_{\mathrm{eu}}$ annihilates the level-$2$ Kummer class together with the real inequalities $[2]_q < [3]_q < [2]_q^2$ for $q > 0$, neither of which constrains orbit convergence.
