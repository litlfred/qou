In the categorical definition $\mathcal{E}_q(\mathbf{1}) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, E_q)$, the integer $r_q$ counts copies of the monoidal unit $\mathbf{1}$ inside the $G$-representation $\tau(E_q)$:

| Setting | Symmetry group | $\tau(E_q)$ as $G$-module | Categorical rank $r_q$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quantum universe (without observability) | unconstrained | may contain $\mathbf{1}$ | $\geq 0$ |
| QOU (with $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0$) | semisimple ($\mathfrak{g}^G = 0$) | non-trivial on colour fibres | $= 0$ |
| Descartes model ($\chi_{\mathrm{eu}} = -6$) | $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ | $V_{1/2} \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ | $= 0$ |

On a colour fibre where $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts through its irreducible two-dimensional representation $V_{1/2}$, $V_{1/2}^{\mathrm{SU}(2)} = 0$, and on the adjoint object $\mathrm{Ad}(A)$ the observability axiom stipulates $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0$ directly.

**Distinction from $E(\mathbb{Q})$.** This vanishing is a representation-theoretic identity on invariants of a Lie-group or quantum-group module. It says nothing about rational solutions $(x, y) \in \mathbb{Q}^2$ of a Weierstrass equation. In [the $q$-BSD conjecture](#conj:q-birch-swinnerton-dyer), identifying $\mathcal{E}_q(\mathbf{1})$ as a categorical analogue of $E(\mathbb{Q})$ is a naming convention, not an isomorphism of abelian groups.
