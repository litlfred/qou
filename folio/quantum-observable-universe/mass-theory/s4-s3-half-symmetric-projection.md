*In the half-symmetric part of $H_4(q)$ at $q = 1$ (one irreducible from each conjugate pair $\{(4),(1^4)\}$ and $\{(3,1),(2,1,1)\}$, plus half of the self-conjugate $(2,2)$), the weighted dimension is $5$ and the weighted multiplicity of the trivial $S_3$-representation under $S_4 \downarrow S_3$ is $2$. The $S_3$-trivial share of the half-symmetric part is therefore $2/5$.*

This is a statement about branching multiplicities only. It does not identify a cell of a hyperbolic decomposition. The complement $S^3 \setminus \mathcal{B}_3$ of the Borromean rings is the union of **two** regular ideal octahedra \cite{adams1985hyperbolic}, so $\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}_3) = 2V_{\mathrm{oct}} = 8G$, where $V_{\mathrm{oct}} = 4G \approx 3.6639$ and $G$ is Catalan's constant. There is no eight-cell octahedral decomposition for the ratio $2/5$ to select from.
