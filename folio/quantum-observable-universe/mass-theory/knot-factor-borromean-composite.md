*Consider a Brunnian $k$-component composite, where every $(k{-}1)$-component sublink is trivial. Suppose its central-idempotent expansion **decouples**: the composite knot factor is the product of the components' factors, with no cross-term,*

$$
  a_{\mathrm{Bor}_k} \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^{k} a_i .
$$

*Then if the components share a common factor $a_i = a$ for all $i$, the composite knot factor is $a^k$. In particular, with each component at the figure-eight pairing $a_{4_1} = 1 - h^2$,*

$$
  a_{\mathrm{Bor}_k} \;=\; (1 - h^2)^k .
$$

Decoupling is the Brunnian content; the $k$-th power is what the product becomes under uniformity, and the two are stated separately because only the first is a hypothesis about the link.

**The per-component value is a hypothesis here, not an import.** [Knot factor for Markov-amphicheiral knots: sum of positive squares](#cor:knot-factor-amphicheiral) derives, under the block normalisation it states, $a_K = 1 - w_{\mathrm{alt}}(1 + h^2)$ rather than the displayed $1 - h^2$; that block records the difference as a normalisation question about the non-alternating blocks, not as a rearrangement. The two agree exactly when $w_{\mathrm{alt}}(1 + h^2) = h^2$, and at $w_{\mathrm{alt}} = 0$ every component is $1$, so the composite is $1$ for every $h$ and every $k$ and the corollary constrains $h$ not at all.

**Archimedean specialisation.** What follows sits on the archimedean side of the substrate wall. It invokes real- or complex-analytic constructs. Any generic categorical identities it draws on remain valid over an abstract coefficient ring. Only their archimedean realisation is specialised here.
