**Proposition (brane-tower growth; GAP-A-3).**
*The branched brane-tower weight $w_{\mathrm{std}}(k) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Planch}}\!\left[f^{\lambda/(2,1)}\right]$ vanishes for $k < 3$, and satisfies*

$$
  \tfrac{1}{30}\,\bigl(\sqrt{2}\bigr)^{k} \;\leq\; w_{\mathrm{std}}(k) \qquad (k \geq 3).
$$

**Origin of the $k = 3$ threshold.** Below $k = 3$ the weight is identically zero rather than merely small: no Young diagram of fewer than three cells contains $(2,1)$, and the skew count $f^{\lambda/\mu}$ is defined to vanish off containment. So $k \geq 3$ is the whole range on which any positive lower bound is possible, and a bound asserted for *every* $k$ is false rather than open.

**The geometric form is a weaker shadow of what is proved.** The underlying result is the square bound $k!/(81 \cdot 2^{k}) \leq w_{\mathrm{std}}(k)^{2}$, valid for $k \geq 3$, which is genuinely super-exponential since $k!/2^{k}$ outgrows every geometric sequence. The constants $r = \sqrt{2}$ and $c = 1/30$ above are one convenient pair extracted from it, chosen so that the threshold falls at $k = 3$; the measured growth is far stronger, with successive ratios rising $1.38 \to 2.88$ over $k \leq 10$.

## Combinatorial structure

Both the displayed geometric lower bound from $k_0 = 3$ and the vanishing below $k_0 = 3$ follow from the skew branching rule and the up-operator identity of Young's lattice — finite combinatorics on Young diagrams, with no appeal to the Robinson–Schensted correspondence or to the hook-length formula as a general theorem.

The tower geometric lower-bound condition takes the threshold $k_{0}$ as a parameter and is satisfied at $k_{0} = 3$. That the threshold is necessary is itself a theorem: every threshold $k_{0} < 3$ fails for all $r$ and $c$.
