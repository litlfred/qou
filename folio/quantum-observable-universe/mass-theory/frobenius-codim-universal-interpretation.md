**Physical content.**
*Any vortex on the self-dual Frobenius algebra inherits a tower residual. The residual equals the trace of the quantum-co-dimension element scaled by the electron mass $m_e$:*

$$
  \Delta m_{\text{vortex}}
  \;=\;
  m_e \cdot \tau\bigl(C_{\dim_q}^{(A)}\bigr).
$$

*Whether a proton tower residual equals this quantity is the comparison recorded in [Proton tower residual compared with $m_e\sum_{\lambda\vdash3}1/\dim_q\lambda = m_e(2 + 1/[2]_q)$](#conj:partition-sum-proton-residual); no derivation of the residual is claimed. The quantity is not the Markov trace of the Frobenius Casimir of $H_3(q)$ ([Frobenius Casimir of $H_3(q)$](#prop:h3-frobenius-casimir-markov-trace)).*

**What the theorem does and does not supply.**
The identity is a consequence of semisimplicity, not an independent constraint. The element $C_{\dim_q}$ is constructed so that its trace is $\sum_\lambda 1/\dim_q(\lambda)$; once the central idempotents $p_\lambda$ exist with $\tau(p_\lambda)$ and $\dim_q(\lambda)$ invertible, linearity of $\tau$ over that basis gives the identity with nothing further to check. The word *universal* in the title records that the argument uses no property of $A$ beyond being a semisimple Frobenius algebra with a trace — it does not record a discovery about $A$.

The content therefore sits in the hypothesis rather than the conclusion. What is being asserted about a quantum universe is that its substrate *has channels at all*: the [Wedderburn semisimplicity axiom](#def:quantum-universe) supplies the finite block set, the non-vanishing Frobenius weights, and the invertible quantum dimensions. Given those, the co-Casimir trace follows. Read the other way round — as though the numerical identity constrained the algebra — it would be circular.
