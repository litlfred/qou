**Remark (prior art: the square of a single-slope $q$-series needs two levels of summation).**
Di Vizio, Ramis, Sauloy and Zhang work through the series $\hat y = \sum_{n \ge 0} q^{n(n-1)/2} x^n$, whose equation has a single non-trivial slope, and its square $\hat Y = \hat y^{\,2}$ \cite[\S 4.4, p. 43]{divizio-ramis-sauloy-zhang}. The $q$-Borel transform of $\hat Y$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ with simple poles in $q^{\mathbb{N}}$ and has $q$-exponential growth of order exactly two at infinity, so $\hat Y$ is not $q$-Borel–Laplace summable at a single level as $\hat y$ is. The equation of $\hat Y$,
$$
\Delta \hat Y = 1 + x, \qquad \Delta = q^2 x^3 \sigma_q^2 - x(1+x)\sigma_q + 1 = (x\sigma_q - 1)(x^2\sigma_q - 1),
$$
has two non-trivial slopes, and $\hat Y$ is $(1, \tfrac12)$-summable by a composite procedure: a $q$-Borel transform, an acceleration, then a $q$-Laplace transform of level $\tfrac12$ \cite[Remark 4.12, eq. (4.12.1), Theorem 4.13, p. 44]{divizio-ramis-sauloy-zhang}.

What this gives here. The module $\mathcal{K}$ of the [conjecture](#conj:qdifference-kernel-prolongation-symbol) is pure isoclinic of slope $\tfrac12$ at $x = \infty$. Products and squares of its solutions are governed by $\mathcal{K}^{\otimes 2}$, which the [bar-parity conjecture](#conj:c1-ggm-bar-parity-faces) records as anisoclinic with slopes $\{+1, 0, -1\}$. The worked example shows, for one explicit series, the same phenomenon: squaring a single-slope series produces an equation with more than one slope, and its formal solutions then need a multi-level summation. A summation argument for the squares $f_\pm^{2}$ therefore cannot be inherited from one for $f_\pm$; it has to be made at the levels of $\mathcal{K}^{\otimes 2}$.

What it does not supply. The example concerns one series with integral slopes and is not a theorem about $\mathcal{K}$. It does not settle whether the formal solution of $\mathcal{K}$ at infinity, whose slope $\tfrac12$ is not integral, is summable at a single level after the ramification $q \mapsto q^{1/2}$, or only by a multi-level procedure. The test that Remark 4.12 applies would discriminate the two readings: compute the $q$-Borel transform of that formal solution and find the order of its $q$-exponential growth at infinity. Nor does the example show that the levels of $\hat Y$ match those of $\mathcal{K}^{\otimes 2}$.
