**Statement.**
*Let $\gamma = c_0\,\mathbf{1} + \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \phi_i\, \sigma_i^{\epsilon_i}$, with $\epsilon_i \in \{\pm 1\}$, be a degree-one element of $H_n(q)$. Every braid word in the support of $\gamma$ has a non-hyperbolic closure: $\overline{\mathbf{1}}$ is the $n$-component unlink and $\overline{\sigma_i^{\pm 1}}$ is the $(n-1)$-component unlink. Hence the hyperbolic volume vanishes word by word:*

$$
  \mathrm{Vol}\bigl(\overline{w}\bigr) \;=\; 0
  \qquad \text{for every word } w \in \{\mathbf{1}, \sigma_1^{\pm 1}, \dots, \sigma_{n-1}^{\pm 1}\}.
$$

*The volume is a function on braid words, not a linear map on $H_n(q)$. No linear map on $H_n(q)$ agrees with the hyperbolic volume on all braid words, so the statement does not extend to a volume of the element $\gamma$ itself.*

*The same holds for a [photon](#def:photon) $\gamma(\varphi) = \mathbf{1} + \varphi_0\sigma_0 + \varphi_1\sigma_1 + \varphi_{01}\,\sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1} \in H_3(q)$. Its support words are $\mathbf{1}, \sigma_0, \sigma_1, \sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1}$. They close to the three-component unlink, the two-component unlink (twice) and the unknot. The vertex word $\sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1}$ uses each generator once, so its closure is the unknot. Every support word of a photon therefore has volume $0$. The degree-one element above is not a photon, because it has no vertex term. On one strand pair with all $\epsilon_i = +1$ and $c_0 = 1$ it is the photon's specialisation $\varphi_{01} = 0$.*
