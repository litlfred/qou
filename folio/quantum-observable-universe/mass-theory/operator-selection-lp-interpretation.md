The general LP for knot operator selection in $H_n(q)$ applies for arbitrary strand number $n$; the confinement LP is the special case $n = 3$, $d = 6$. The Borromean link $\mathcal{B}_3$ is **feasible** for the confinement LP — its normal form satisfies every Frobenius positivity constraint strictly, $\min_i (Gx)_i \approx 0.342$ at the proton-anchored $q_0$ — but it is **not** a minimiser: its objective is $\mathrm{tr}_M(\mathcal{B}_3) \approx 0.9986$ against an optimal value of $0$. The program does not select it, and no minimiser is distinguished (see [linear-programming operator selection](#prop:operator-selection-lp), *Degeneracy of this program*).

The objective $\sum_{i} t_i x_i = \mathrm{tr}_M(\Phi)$ is the categorical action. Minimising it does **not** select an operator: because $b_1 = \mathbf{1}$, the Frobenius positivity constraint at $i = 1$ is $\mathrm{tr}_M(\Phi) \geq 0$, so the objective is bounded below by its own constraint and the minimum is identically $0$. Least action here means *zero* action, which is attained on a whole face. The Frobenius positivity constraints $Gx \geq 0$ encode the demand–supply balance: $\Phi$ must pair non-negatively with every basis element under the Frobenius inner product. No channel carries unbounded demand.

**LP over motivic shadows rather than real numbers.**
The data feeding the LP—Markov traces $t_i = \mathrm{tr}_M(b_i)$ and Frobenius Gram entries $G_{ij} = \mathrm{tr}_M(b_i \cdot b_j)$—are formal elements of $\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/2}, (q-q^{-1})^{-1}]$, hence elements of the Habiro completion $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}[q]}$. The selection of the operator $\Phi^* = \sum_i x_i^* b_i$ is a combinatorial problem over the motivic shadows of the motivic aether (the algebraic data of the Hecke algebra independently of any place-realization). Strong duality is a purely algebraic consequence of the LP. Only after the optimizer is selected does the archimedean realization $\rho_\infty$ project the dual prices and objective value into physical binding energies in $\mathbb{R}$. The non-archimedean realizations $\rho_p$ recover the LP's cyclotomic shadows at each finite place $p$.

The dual variable $y_0$ would be the shadow price of the net constraint — the marginal cost, in units of categorical action, of increasing $n_{\mathrm{target}}$ by one unit. In this program it is identically zero: strong duality gives $n_{\mathrm{target}} \cdot y_0^* = 0$ with $n_{\mathrm{target}} > 0$. The demand-pricing reading below therefore describes a quantity that does not vary, and $n_{\mathrm{target}}$ acts as a pure scale. The dual variables $y_i \geq 0$ are the shadow prices of the Frobenius positivity constraints (channel demand prices). A positive $y_i^*$ at optimality indicates that the $i$-th Frobenius constraint is tight, meaning the $i$-th channel is saturated.
