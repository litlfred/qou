The Borromean braid of a nucleon with quarks $(q_1, q_2, q_3)$ contributes a pair Skein mass at each pair of strands. Each pair meets at one positive crossing $\sigma$ and one negative crossing $\sigma^{-1}$, and the pair Skein mass is the Markov trace of the weighted crossing element at the canonical Markov parameter $z = q^3/(q^2+1)$:

$$
  \mathcal{S}_{ab}(q)
  =
  \mathrm{tr}_M\!\left(\mu_a\,\sigma + \mu_b\,\sigma^{-1}\right)
  =
  \frac{q^{3}\mu_a + q^{-1}\mu_b}
       {q^{2} + 1}.
$$

The total nucleon mass is:

$$
  m_N
  =
  \mathcal{S}_{q_1 q_2}(q)
  + \mathcal{S}_{q_1 q_3}(q)
  + \mathcal{S}_{q_2 q_3}(q).
$$

At $q = 1$ the pair Skein mass is the mean $(\mu_a + \mu_b)/2$. Exchanging the two species gives $\mathcal{S}_{ab} - \mathcal{S}_{ba} = (q - q^{-1})(\mu_a - \mu_b)$, the Skein coefficient.
