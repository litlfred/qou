The Borromean braid of a nucleon with quarks $(q_1, q_2, q_3)$ contributes a pair Skein mass at each color-exchange crossing:

$$
  \mathcal{S}_{ab}(q)
  =
  \frac{q\mu_a +  q^{-1}\mu_b}
       {q^{1/2} + q^{-1/2}}.
$$

The total nucleon mass is:

$$
  m_N
  =
  \mathcal{S}_{q_1 q_2}(q)
  + \mathcal{S}_{q_1 q_3}(q)
  + \mathcal{S}_{q_2 q_3}(q).
$$

The denominator $q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2}$ is the reciprocal of the half-argument weight $1/(q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2}) = \sqrt q/(q+1)$ rather than the canonical Markov parameter $z = q^3/(q^2+1)$. Substituting $z = q^3/(q^2+1)$ directly is not a valid move, because the crossing-pair trace must be re-derived under that parameter.
