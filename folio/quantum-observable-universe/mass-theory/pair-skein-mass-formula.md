Work in the canonical frame: the Hecke generators satisfy $\sigma_i^2 = (q - q^{-1})\,\sigma_i + 1$, and the Markov parameter is the convention-D value $z = q^3/(q^2+1)$. The Borromean braid of a nucleon with quarks $(q_1, q_2, q_3)$ contributes a pair Skein mass at each color-exchange crossing pair:

$$
  \mathcal{S}_{ab}(q)
  =
  (q - z)\,\bigl(q^{2}\mu_a + q^{-2}\mu_b\bigr)
  =
  \frac{q^{2}\mu_a + q^{-2}\mu_b}
       {q + q^{-1}}.
$$

The total nucleon mass is:

$$
  m_N
  =
  \mathcal{S}_{q_1 q_2}(q)
  + \mathcal{S}_{q_1 q_3}(q)
  + \mathcal{S}_{q_2 q_3}(q).
$$

The factor $q - z = 1/[2]_q$ is the Markov trace of the Temperley–Lieb generator $e_i = q - \sigma_i$, and the weights $q^{\pm 2}$ are the skein coefficients that the Markov trace at $z$ forces. The earlier convention-C form $(q\mu_a + q^{-1}\mu_b)/(q^{1/2} + q^{-1/2})$ is the same formula written in the square-root frame, where the Hecke parameter is $q^{1/2}$. Substituting $q \mapsto q^{1/2}$ above recovers it. That a crossing pair contributes the trace of $e_i$ times the skein-weighted dressed masses is a reading of the formula, not a consequence of the Borromean braid.
