**Conjecture (Step 2 as monodromy).** Let $P(X, z) = X^3 + (z+1)X^2 + (z^2+z+1)X + (z^3+z^2+z+1)$ be the colour cubic, whose root $X(z)$ and its jets are the Seiberg--Witten periods, and let $\Delta \subset \mathbb{C}$ be its discriminant locus in $z$. The substrate connection on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \Delta$ has braid-group monodromy $\varrho \colon B_n \to H_n(q)$ into the Hecke tower carrying the Markov trace, such that for every generator $g_i = \varrho(\sigma_i)$:

1. the Hecke quadratic $g_i^2 = (q - q^{-1})\, g_i + 1$ holds, together with the braid relation $g_i g_{i+1} g_i = g_{i+1} g_i g_{i+1}$ and far commutation;
2. the tower is defined over the Laurent ring $\mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$, and the classical specialisation $\varepsilon \colon H_n(q) \to \mathbb{Q}S_n$ sends $q \mapsto 1$; then each $g_i$ becomes an involution, $\varepsilon(g_i)^2 = 1$ --- the Picard--Lefschetz transposition of the two roots of $P$ that collide at a branch point.

The two eigenvalues $q$ and $-q^{-1}$ of the monodromy quadratic $F(\sigma) = \sigma^2 - (q - q^{-1})\sigma - 1$ are then a consequence: $(g_i - q)(g_i + q^{-1}) = 0$. The polynomial $F$ is an eigenvalue relation of the monodromy of a flat connection, not a curvature: a curved connection has path-dependent holonomy and gives no braid representation. For the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov connection on two points, the quadratic and the involutive shadow are theorems, derived from the Casimir eigenvalues $\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac32$.

## What is derived and what is conjectured

The discriminant is the reciprocal irreducible sextic $16z^6 + 24z^5 + 35z^4 + 50z^3 + 35z^2 + 24z + 16$, with six simple complex branch points and no branching at infinity. Continuing the three roots of $P$ around each branch point gives a transposition every time; three distinct transpositions occur, so the monodromy group is $S_3$, and the ordered product of the six local monodromies is the identity. On the two colliding roots a transposition is the swap, an involution with eigenvalues $\pm 1$ and minimal polynomial $\sigma^2 - 1 = (\sigma - q)(\sigma + q^{-1})|_{q=1}$. So item 2, and the $q = 1$ specialisation of item 1, are theorems about the geometry the framework already carries. Since $\varepsilon(q) = 1$, the term $\varepsilon\bigl((q - q^{-1})\, g_i\bigr)$ vanishes, and pushing item 1 through $\varepsilon$ gives the involution: the classical shadow forgets exactly the deformation parameter. The parameter $q$ must vary for this to make sense. Over a field in which $q - q^{-1}$ is invertible, a map sending $q$ to $1$ kills the whole algebra.

What remains conjectural is the deformation $1 \mapsto q$ itself: no connection with $q$-deformed monodromy has been constructed, and the monodromy quadratic the chain names (historically called a curvature form) is stipulated for the substrate connection rather than computed. The literature model is Kohno--Drinfeld: the monodromy of a Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov connection factors through $H_n(q)$. At real level that monodromy has unit-modulus eigenvalues, which the real substrate pin $q_0 \approx 1.11$ is not; a Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov realisation therefore requires the non-unitary level $k + 2 = 2\pi i/\ln q_0 \approx 60.218\, i$ in the convention $q = e^{2\pi i/(k+2)}$. Whether the substrate connection is such a connection is the open construction. That hypothesis is now stated precisely (the KZ pullback-generator hypothesis): each substrate generator is conjugate in the tower to a monodromy generator of the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov connection on $\mathrm{Conf}_3(\mathbb{C})$ pulled back along the root map $z \mapsto \{X_1(z), X_2(z), X_3(z)\}$ at the level fixed by the substrate parameter ($k + 2 = i\pi/\ln q_0$ in Kohno's convention $q = e^{i\pi/(k+2)}$, $q_0$ an input), and from that hypothesis the Hecke quadratic is derived rather than assumed. What is established on the way, and what is not: the six branch points are fixed, so the base is $\mathbb{C} \setminus \Delta$ with free fundamental group and the braid group enters only through the root map; every lasso lifts to a positive half-twist, the six composing to the full twist $\Delta^2$ (probe, 13/13); the convention-D Markov parameter $z_D = q^3/(q^2+1)$ is exactly the $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ fundamental quantum trace, and $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ never reproduces it; flatness of a Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov connection is independent of the level, so the level enters only through the monodromy's target. Constructing the connection itself, and hence a realisation of the hypothesis, remains open.

A second route to the same deformation --- the family's own periods, rather than an imported Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov connection --- is now narrowed from both sides, and the narrowing is negative. The framework's one candidate connection on this family is the abelianised Cartan form $A^0 = X\,dz$, which is the Seiberg--Witten differential itself; its push-down $\sum_j X_j\,dz = -(z+1)\,dz$ is exact by Vieta, and around every branch point both the fixed sheet's circle integral and the exchanged pair's sum vanish to $10^{-30}$ at 50 digits, so every lasso has holonomy $1$. That connection supplies no twist. Untwisted periods fare no better in principle: Gauss--Manin monodromy on such a family is quasi-unipotent, and an eigenvalue of finite order --- or, more generally, a quasi-unipotent one --- is never a real number greater than one, so neither $q_0 \approx 1.11$ nor $-q_0^{-1}$ can arise that way. What survives is a *twisted* period: a local system with exponent $\mu$, whose pseudo-reflection monodromy has eigenvalues $\{1, e^{2\pi i \mu}\}$. Matching the Hecke ratio forces $e^{2\pi i \mu} = -q^2$, and that forces $\operatorname{Im} \mu = -\ln q / \pi$, which is non-zero for every $q > 1$; so no unitary local system, no rational twist and no root-of-unity twist can carry the deformation. The exponent is where $q_0$ enters, and it enters as an input: $\mu(q_0)$ is a label for the substrate parameter, not a derivation of it. Whether such a twisted family exists on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \Delta$ is the open construction on this route, exactly as a realisation of the KZ pullback-generator hypothesis is on the other.
