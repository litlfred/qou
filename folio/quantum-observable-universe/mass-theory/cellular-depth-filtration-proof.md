**The $a$-function value.** That the two-sided cell of shape $\lambda$ has $a$-value $\sum_i\binom{\lambda_i}2$ (with $(n)$ the index representation) is the type-A case of Lusztig's $a$-function, with each two-sided cell carrying exactly one irreducible [Kazhdan–Lusztig 1979]. It was checked exactly for $n\le5$ by computing the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis, the cells and $a$ from the structure constants (probe recorded on issue #8094). Writing $\binom{\lambda_i}{2}=0+1+\dots+(\lambda_i-1)$, $a(\lambda)$ is the sum over cells $(i,j)$ of $\lambda$ of the column index $j$, and $n(\lambda)$ is the sum of the row index $i$. Transposition swaps $i$ and $j$, so $a(\lambda')=n(\lambda)$.
