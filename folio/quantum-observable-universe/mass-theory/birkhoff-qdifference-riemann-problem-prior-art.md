Birkhoff studies systems $Y(qx) = Q(x)\, Y(x)$ with $|q| > 1$ and $Q$ a matrix of polynomials of degree at most $\mu$ \cite[\S 18, eq. (54), p. 559]{birkhoff1913}. He restricts to the case in which there are matrix solutions $Y_0$ at $x = 0$ and $Y_\infty$ at $x = \infty$ of a prescribed form, built from series analytic at the respective point whose leading coefficients have non-zero determinant; only that case is considered \cite[\S 18, p. 561]{birkhoff1913}. The connection matrix $P$, defined by $Y_0(x) = Y_\infty(x)\, P(x)$, is analytic away from $0$ and $\infty$ and satisfies $P(qx) = P(x)$ \cite[\S 18, p. 561]{birkhoff1913}. Counting the constants in $Y_0$, $Y_\infty$ and $P$ that survive the freedom to rescale columns, he finds $n^2\mu + 1$ characteristic constants, the same number as the invariants of the system, and proves that a system can be constructed with any assigned set of them, up to integer shifts of some exponents \cite[\S\S 20--21, pp. 565--566]{birkhoff1913}.

This is the original source for the connection matrix between $0$ and $\infty$ that the [kernel conjecture](#conj:qdifference-kernel-prolongation-symbol) refers to, and its convention $|q| > 1$ is the one the corpus uses for the aether parameter.

It does not cover the corpus's carrier. The module $\mathcal{K}$ is pure isoclinic of slope $\tfrac12$ at infinity, so its formal solutions there need the ramified variable $x^{1/2}$ and are not of the form Birkhoff assumes. His existence and counting results therefore do not apply to $\mathcal{K}$ as stated, and citing them does not reopen a route that depends on that hypothesis.
