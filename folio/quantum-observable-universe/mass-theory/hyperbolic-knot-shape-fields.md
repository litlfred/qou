## The shape field of a triangulation

For a cusped hyperbolic $3$-manifold with an ideal triangulation, the **shape field** is the number field generated by the tetrahedron shape parameters. By a theorem of Neumann and Reid it coincides with the invariant trace field, so it is an invariant of the manifold rather than of the chosen triangulation.

Because the shapes are algebraic and Thurston's gluing equations — one consistency equation per edge class and two completeness equations per cusp, a solution of which with every $\operatorname{Im} z_i > 0$ is a complete finite-volume hyperbolic structure \cite{martelli2016} (§14.1, Prop.~14.1.7) — have **integer** exponents, the whole verification can be carried out exactly, with no floating point: express each shape in $\mathbb{Q}(\theta)$ for a single generator $\theta$, form $\prod_j z_j^{a_j}(z_j')^{b_j}(z_j'')^{c_j} - 1$, clear denominators, and reduce modulo the minimal polynomial of $\theta$. The residual is zero or it is not.

**Proposition.**
*For each knot below, the shape parameters lie in the stated field and satisfy every gluing equation exactly over it.*

| knot | tetrahedra | minimal polynomial | discriminant |
|---|---|---|---|
| $4_1$ | $2$ | $x^2 - x + 1$ | $-3$ |
| $5_2$ | $3$ | $x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x - 1$ | $-23$ |
| $6_1$ | $4$ | $x^4 - 3x^3 + 6x^2 - 5x + 2$ | $257$ |
| $7_2$ | $4$ | $x^5 - 2x^4 + x^3 + 3x^2 - 5x + 3$ | $4409$ |

The figure-eight case is the familiar one: $x^2 - x + 1$ is the minimal polynomial of a primitive sixth root of unity, so the shape field is the Eisenstein field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ and both tetrahedra are regular.

## The degree is not bounded by the tetrahedron count

$7_2$ is triangulated by four tetrahedra and has a **quintic** shape field. Nothing forces the degree below the number of simplices, and a formalisation sized to the tetrahedron count will be wrong for it. The examples above run from degree $2$ to degree $5$ on triangulations of two to four tetrahedra.

## Stating the identities for an arbitrary root

The verification lives in $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(P)$, so each gluing identity holds at **every** root of $P$ rather than only the geometric one. That is the form in which the identities are proved: for a root $t$ of the minimal polynomial, with each shape written as an explicit polynomial in $t$,

$$
  \prod_j z_j(t)^{a_j} \cdot \prod_j \bigl(z_j(t) - 1\bigr)^{c_j}
  \;=\;
  \prod_j \bigl(1 - z_j(t)\bigr)^{b_j} \cdot \prod_j z_j(t)^{c_j} ,
$$

the gluing equation with denominators cleared. Stating it this way avoids having to exhibit a root in the upper half plane, which for a quartic or quintic is a substantial obstacle and is not needed for the algebra.

Selecting the geometric root is then a separate, numerical step. It is necessary: a shape field typically carries several Galois-conjugate solutions, and only one of them has all shapes in the upper half plane and reproduces the hyperbolic volume as $\sum_j D(z_j)$.

## Scope

Satisfying the gluing equations does **not** establish that a triangulation is a triangulation *of* a given knot complement. That is Thurston's hyperbolisation theorem together with Mostow rigidity, and it requires a theory of hyperbolic $3$-manifolds which is not developed here. Nor is any statement made about the volume conjecture.

The mathematics recorded here is classical — Thurston, Neumann-Zagier, Neumann-Reid, and the arithmetic-invariant computations of `snap`. What is contributed is the exact, mechanically checked form.
