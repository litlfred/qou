The parameter $k_{\max}$ recorded in witness data represents the Coxeter-length filtration. This strictly differs from the structural GB-depth $k_\star(T_w)$. The witness $k_{\max}$ overestimates depth due to strand-padding artifacts.

With GB-depth fixed as Lusztig's $a$-function, $k_\star(T_w)=\binom n2$ for every $w$ ([cell-depth trace truncation](#prop:cell-depth-truncation), item 4), so the structural truncation removes nothing on normal-form inputs; under the max-hook depth $k_\star(T_w)=n$, which is an identity rather than a bound. The uniform $z$-Habiro bound was meant to regularise a level-wise $\Phi_q$ identity that is false (item 5), so it is not needed here. Any finite cut-off used in compute, such as the $k=2$ truncation, is an empirical choice that this statement does not support.
