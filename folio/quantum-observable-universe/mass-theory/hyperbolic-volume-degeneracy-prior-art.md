**Remark (prior art: how many manifolds share a volume).**
By Jørgensen and Thurston, only finitely many orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds have a given volume $v$; write $N(v)$ for that number. Hodgson and Masai record that it is often larger than one \cite[\S 1, pp. 1--2]{hodgson2012}. At the lowest cusped volume $v_\omega = 2.029883\ldots$ there are exactly two cusped manifolds, the figure-eight knot complement and its sister $m003$ (Cao–Meyerhoff). For link complements in $S^3$ the count grows at least exponentially: for each $n \ge 3$ there are at least $2^n/(2n)$ hyperbolic link complements of volume $4nV_8$, where $V_8$ is the volume of the regular ideal octahedron \cite[Thm 8.5, p. 22]{hodgson2012}.

For the [volume of the Reeb-bundle complement](#prop:reeb-bundle-complement-equals-volume) this sets a scope: $\mathrm{Vol}(K)$ is a well-defined invariant of each knot or link, but it is not an identifying label. Two different link complements can have the same volume, so a quantity that depends on $K$ only through $\mathrm{Vol}(K)$ cannot tell them apart. In particular $\mathrm{Vol}(4_1)$ is also the volume of the sister manifold $m003$. The paper says nothing about the other factors of the mass law, so it neither supports nor rules out any QOU mass assignment.
