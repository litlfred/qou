*Let $M_N = (m_{\mathfrak{p}} + m_{\mathfrak{n}})/2$ and $\mu_{\text{avg}} = (\mu_{\mathfrak{u}} + \mu_{\mathfrak{d}})/2$. The nucleon mass split is given by the substrate flavor-split formula:*

$$
  \frac{m_{\mathfrak{n}} - m_{\mathfrak{p}}}{M_N}
  \;\approx\;
  \frac{(q - 1)^2 \, (1 - q^{-4})}{3},
  \qquad
  \frac{m_{\mathfrak{n}} - m_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\mu_{\text{avg}}}
  \;\approx\;
  \hbar_q^2 \cdot h_q \cdot [2]_q .
$$

Here $\hbar_q = 1 - q^{-1}$, $h_q = q - q^{-1}$ and $[2]_q = q + q^{-1}$. By [the closed and factored forms of the substrate flavor split](#prop:flavor-split-substrate), the first right-hand side is the second divided by $3$. The two displayed claims are therefore equivalent exactly when $M_N = 3\mu_{\text{avg}}$, which is the additive-strand relation of Step 6 of that proposition's proof; the conjecture asserts the first claim, and the second follows from it under that relation.

**Status.** The derivation sketch in the proof of that proposition (Steps 1, 2 and 6) obtains this formula from the pair Skein formula and an ansatz for the per-quark split $(\mu_{\mathfrak{d}} - \mu_{\mathfrak{u}})/\mu_{\text{avg}}$. The ansatz was chosen partly because it agrees with experiment, and no derivation of it is on record. For nucleon masses that are additive over strands with a mass ratio that is a nonzero power of $q$, it is excluded: every such split vanishes to first order at $q = 1$, while the ansatz vanishes to third order. The pair Skein formula itself holds only in the convention-C frame.
