In the particle dictionary, a knot type is *fundamental* if it is the unknot or a torus knot: the unknot (neutrino), the trefoil $3_1$ (electron), the cinquefoil $5_1$ (tau) and the torus $(p, 2)$ family. Every fundamental knot has $\mathrm{Vol}(K) = 0$, but not conversely: satellite knots, including composites such as $3_1 \# 3_1$, also have volume zero. The Hopf link $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}$ is read as the binding operation, and a hadron $H \in \mathrm{Sym}^n(K) \otimes \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}$ as a configuration of fundamental knots joined by Hopf links.

The [skein-module generation proposition](#prop:skein-generation) resolves every such $H$ to $P_H(a, q)\,[U]$. In particular the fundamental knots and the Hopf link generate the skein module, but so does the unknot alone, and the resolution records linking only through the polynomial $P_H$.

The mass reading takes the form $\mathfrak{m}(H) = Q(q)\, \mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}) + \sum_i \mathrm{Vol}(K_i)/\hbar_q^2$ of [Skein linking-mass lemma](#lem:skein-mass-from-linking), so that a hadron built from fundamental constituents takes its whole mass from linking. That step needs a specialisation in which the Hopf link is not identified with the unknot. The relation $q L_+ - q^{-1} L_- = (q - q^{-1}) L_0$ corresponds to the case $a = q$, where $P_L = 1$ for every link and the Hopf link equals the unknot, so it does not supply that step. At $a = 1$ the Hopf link resolves to $(q - q^{-1})\,[U]$ with the orientation for which its crossings are positive, and the split unlink to $0$, so linking is visible there. Which specialisation the mass reading uses is not fixed by the proposition.
