For a braid on three strings $z_1(t), z_2(t), z_3(t)$ in $\mathbb{C}$, Evans and Berger use Arnol'd's relation in the cohomology of the braid group to show that the one-form
$$
\psi_{ijk} = \lambda_{ij}\,d\lambda_{jk} + \lambda_{jk}\,d\lambda_{ki} + \lambda_{ki}\,d\lambda_{ij}
$$
is closed, so that $\Psi_{ijk} = \mathrm{Re}\int \psi_{ijk}$ is a homotopy invariant of the braid, the third-order winding number \cite[eqs. (21)--(22), p. 6]{evans-berger1992}. They stress that it is well defined whether or not the net second-order winding numbers vanish; when they do vanish it agrees with the third-order linking number of the closed link \cite[pp. 6--7]{evans-berger1992}.

This bounds what part (1) of the [activation gate](#prop:alpha-cluster-activation-gate) says. The gate is about the Milnor residue of the closed link, which lives in $\mathbb{Z}/\Delta\mathbb{Z}$ and carries nothing once a pairwise linking is a unit. $\Psi_{ijk}$ is an invariant of the braid with its ends held fixed, not of the closed link, so the gate does not rule it out, and it is defined on braid words, which is what the corpus manipulates. It is not shown here to be a binding channel: whether $\Psi_{ijk}$ is well defined on the corpus's braid words under its closure and basepoint conventions, and whether it has the right scale for a binding term, are both unchecked.
