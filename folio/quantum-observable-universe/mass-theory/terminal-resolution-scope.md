Part (ii) of [nucleon masses under the pair Skein formula](#prop:terminal-resolution) means that reproducing the measured proton and neutron masses with this formula is a change of variables, not a prediction: the dressed quark masses can always be chosen to fit. The formula carries predictive content only if the dressed masses are fixed independently. The only such input on record is the ratio in [the substrate flavor split](#prop:flavor-split-substrate). That proposition's own proof calls the per-quark split a phenomenological ansatz chosen by its agreement with experiment, so it does not yet supply an independent input. Absolute masses in MeV are not predictions in any case: the electron mass fixes the MeV scale by definition.

The proposition is stated in the canonical convention-D frame, $z = q^3/(q^2+1)$. Moving from the earlier convention-C frame did not change either conclusion. The convention-D map is the convention-C map with $q^{1/2}$ replaced by $q$, so the bijection survives and the splitting coefficient becomes $[4]_q/[2]_q^2$. The two choices inside the pair Skein formula (the sign of the writhe factor, and normalising by the trace of $e_i$ rather than by $z$) would each rescale every $\mathcal{S}_{ab}$ by one common factor, so neither could make the map predictive.
