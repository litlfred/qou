For $G = GL_n$ and a unipotent element $x$ with Springer fibre $\mathcal{B}_x$ of dimension $d_x$, Springer's theorem identifies the top cohomology $H^{2d_x}(\mathcal{B}_x)$ with an irreducible representation $V_\lambda$ of $S_n$, where $\lambda$ is the Jordan type of $x$, under the normalisation in which $V_{(n)}$ is the trivial and $V_{(1^n)}$ the sign representation \cite[Thm 2.1 and eq. (2.1), \S 2, pp. 3--4]{shoji2019}. The same survey uses the statistic $n(\lambda) = \sum_i (i-1)\lambda_i$ \cite[eq. (6.3), \S 6]{shoji2019}, which is the grade $n(\lambda)$ of the [unification proposition](#prop:delta-lambda-dt-index-unification).

What this gives here. The two factors of $\Delta_\lambda = (-1)^{n(\lambda)} \dim V_\lambda$ behave differently under the choice of convention:

- the magnitude is convention-free, since $\dim V_\lambda = \dim V_{\lambda'}$;
- the sign depends on whether $\lambda$ or its conjugate $\lambda'$ labels $V_\lambda$, because $n(\lambda) \neq n(\lambda')$ in general.

Under Shoji's normalisation the sign is the parity of the Springer-fibre dimension, $(-1)^{n(\lambda)} = (-1)^{d_x}$ for $x$ of Jordan type $\lambda$. This is checked at the two ends of the dominance order. For $\lambda = (n)$, $x$ is regular, $\mathcal{B}_x$ is a point, $n(\lambda) = 0$, and the representation is trivial. For $\lambda = (1^n)$, $x = 1$, $\mathcal{B}_x$ is the whole flag variety of dimension $n(n-1)/2 = n(1^n)$, and the representation is the sign. The general equality $d_x = n(\lambda)$ is standard, but the survey cited does not state it, so it is not attributed to that source here.

What it does not supply. The proof of the [unification proposition](#prop:delta-lambda-dt-index-unification) does not use this citation; the Springer reading is an interpretation of its sign, not an input to its proof. It also does not bear on the many-body binding magnitude, which that proposition leaves open.
