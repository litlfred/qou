## Lifted-crossing locality hypothesis

**Conjecture.**
*The joint mass contribution of lifted crossings factorizes into independent local evaluations:*

$$
  \mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{GB}}(\beta\lvert_{S \text{ lifted}})
  \;=\;
  \mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{GB}}(\mathcal{K}) \cdot
  \prod_{c \in S} \eta(c).
$$

*The local factor $\eta(c)$ depends strictly on the individual crossing $c$, its sign, and the substrate parameter $q$, completely independent of the broader configuration.*

Locality requires that parallel transport of the shape parameter around multiple crossings accumulates zero phase. The Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov connection is flat at every level, because the infinitesimal braid relations that characterise flatness are homogeneous in the coefficients; what singles out the physical substrate $q_0$ is not flatness but semisimplicity of the monodromy target $H_n(q_0)$, which holds because $q_0$ is not a root of unity. Because $q_0$ is real and greater than one, a Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov monodromy eigenvalue $e^{i\theta}$ at real level can never equal it; the level is necessarily non-unitary, $k + 2 = 2\pi i/\ln q_0 \approx 60.218\, i$ in the convention $q = e^{2\pi i/(k+2)}$. The property fails at roots of unity or at the golden-ratio degeneration threshold, identifying $q_0$ as the distinguished regime supporting exact exponential mass scaling.

**Archimedean specialisation.** What follows sits on the archimedean side of the substrate wall. It applies $q$-deformation identities over an abstract coefficient ring and specializes them to real-valued observables at $q_0$.
