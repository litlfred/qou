Arc II turns from the algebraic trace on $H_n(q)$ to its three-dimensional geometric realization. On the genus-$4$ Bring surface, the Lagrange resolvents diagonalise the cyclic $C_5$ action globally, but local particle interactions occur at blown-up brane points. Blowing up a brane point $p$ replaces it with an exceptional divisor $\mathcal{S}_p \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$ carrying a Hopf circle bundle $\mathcal{R}_p \cong S^3 \to \mathcal{S}_p$ and a contact Reeb vector field.

On the colour coordinates of $\mathcal{R}_p$, the $q$-deformed Reeb flow $\dot\psi = q$ transports strands along closed trajectories. Threading this flow with the six elements of the symmetric group $S_3$ generates the six canonical three-strand knot operators: the trivial braid, the two adjacent transpositions $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$, the Garside half-twist $\Delta_3 = \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$, and the two three-cycles. At the boundary discs $\phi \in \{0, \pi\}$, the three colour tubes fuse into a triply ramified cover whose monodromy reproduces the $S_3$ Galois action on the Lagrange resolvents.

Excising tubular neighbourhoods of the closed Reeb trajectories from $\mathcal{R}_p \cong S^3$ yields a link complement whose JSJ decomposition separates the spectrum into two geometric classes: pure rotational three-cycles trace torus links whose complements are Seifert-fibered with vanishing hyperbolic volume, whereas alternating Garside and Borromean trajectories cross incompressible Heegaard tori and produce hyperbolic complements with strictly positive volume.
