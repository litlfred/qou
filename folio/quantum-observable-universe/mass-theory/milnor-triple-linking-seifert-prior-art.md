**Remark (prior art: a geometric form of the triple-linking residue).**
Let $\delta$ be the greatest common divisor of the pairwise linking numbers of components $L_i, L_j, L_k$. Mellor and Melvin prove that for any choice of Seifert surfaces $F = F_i \cup F_j \cup F_k$,
$$
\bar\mu_{ijk} \equiv m_{ijk}(F) - t_{ijk}(F) \pmod{\delta},
$$
where $t_{ijk}(F)$ is the signed count of triple points of $F$ and $m_{ijk}(F)$ is read off from how the double curves meet the boundaries \cite[Theorem, p. 561]{mellor-melvin2003}. When the boundary of $F$ misses the double-point set, which is possible only if all pairwise linking numbers vanish, this reduces to Cochran's formula $\bar\mu_{ijk} = -t_{ijk}(F)$, a pure signed triple-point count \cite[p. 562]{mellor-melvin2003}.

This gives the open case of the [activation gate](#prop:alpha-cluster-activation-gate), the Brunnian pattern with all pairwise linkings zero, a surface-level object to count, in addition to the braid-word definition. It applies only to an actual link with Seifert surfaces. The corpus's cluster arrangements are given as contact graphs and braid words, not yet realised as links with chosen Seifert surfaces, so the formula cannot be applied to them until that realisation is made.
