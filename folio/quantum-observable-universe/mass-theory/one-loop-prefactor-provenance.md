**Proposition (what is literature, what is measured, what is derived).** Write the sub-leading constant of the volume-conjecture asymptotic as a power law in the Dimofte–Garoufalidis 1-loop invariant,

$$
  c_\sigma \;=\; k \cdot |\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}|^{\,p}.
$$

Then the two factors have different provenance.

- **The form is literature.** Dimofte–Gukov–Lenells–Zagier expand the perturbative $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ Chern–Simons partition function as $Z^{(\rho)}(M;\hbar) = \exp\big(S_0/\hbar - \tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{(\rho)}\log\hbar + \sum_{n\ge 0} S_{n+1}\hbar^n\big)$ and identify the one-loop coefficient with the Ray–Singer torsion of $M$ twisted by the flat bundle $E_\rho$: $S_1^{(\rho)} = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(T(M;E_\rho)/2\big)$. The statement is for a **general** $3$-manifold with a flat $G_\mathbb{C}$ bundle — no restriction to knots, to two-bridge knots, or to cusp number. So the dependence $c_\sigma \propto (\text{torsion})^{-1/2}$ is not an assumption of this corpus; it is a cited result. What it is *not* is a theorem: it is a one-loop saddle-point/Feynman-diagram derivation.

- **The exponent is normalisation-invariant, hence measurable.** Rescaling $c_\sigma \mapsto A\,c_\sigma$ by any nonzero constant leaves the two-point exponent $p_{ij} = (\log c_i - \log c_j)/(\log \tau_i - \log \tau_j)$ unchanged. Every convention offset is such an $A$, so no change of convention can move $p$.

- **And $p = -1/2$ was already measured, by checks that were read as measuring something else.** For any *pair* of manifolds, $c_1\sqrt{\tau_1} = c_2\sqrt{\tau_2} \iff p_{12} = -1/2$. The corpus validated the law by checking that $k_c := c_\sigma|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}|^{1/2}$ agrees across manifolds and recorded the exponent as *assumed*; those are the same statement. The $17$-digit agreement $k_1 = k_2$ between $4_1$ and the Whitehead link **is** the measurement $p = -1/2$ to $17$ digits.

- **The constant cannot be lifted from DGLZ — but it need not be.** DGLZ carry at least three distinct additive offsets between conventions — the $/2$ inside their logarithm, a $+\log 2$ relating $S_1$ to the colored-Jones coefficient $s_1$, and a $\log\pi$ shift against Gukov–Murakami — and fix their integration constants "by comparison to the asymptotics of the colored Jones polynomial". They calibrate too. So $k = 2^{-1/2}$ cannot be read off their formula. It does not have to be: it is now derived directly from the colored Jones, at both legs, with no convention to reconcile at all.

- **And $k = 2^{-1/2}$ is forced by the saddle condition, before any manifold is named.** Laplace's method on a state-sum whose logarithm expands as $-(2N/\pi)\Lambda(\theta) + \log(2N\sin\theta)$ produces three quantities: the saddle condition $\Lambda'(\theta) = -\log(2\sin\theta) = 0$, i.e. $2\sin\theta^\ast = 1$; the Stirling factor $2\sin\theta^\ast$; and the Hessian $|\Lambda''(\theta^\ast)| = |\cot\theta^\ast|$. These give $c_\sigma = 2\sin\theta^\ast\,|\cot\theta^\ast|^{-1/2}$ and $|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}| = |\cot\theta^\ast|/2$ — so in $k := c_\sigma|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}|^{1/2}$ **the Hessian cancels before it is ever evaluated**, leaving $k^2 = (2\sin\theta^\ast)^2/2 = 1/2$. No trace field, no cusp number, no choice of manifold enters. The $4_1$ calibration was never carrying information.

**The $N$-power is theorem-backed at two and three cusps.** The extraction $c_\sigma := \lim_N |J'_N|\,N^{-3/2}e^{-\mathrm{Vol}\cdot N/2\pi}$ uses the same power $3/2$ at every cusp number. Van der Veen proves, for the Whitehead-chain family $W_{a,b,c,d}$, that $J_N \sim \exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2\pi}[(\mathrm{Vol}+i\,\mathrm{CS})N + D\log N + E]\big)$ with $D = 3\pi$ whenever $b = 1$ — a value independent of $c$ and $d$, hence of the number of components. The Whitehead link is $W_{0,1,1,0}$ and the Borromean rings are $W_{0,1,1,1}$, both with $b = 1$, so both carry $N^{3/2}$. The cusp-independence of the exponent is therefore proved for exactly the links this corpus uses, and not merely fitted. The mechanism is Lemma 3's constant: $\tilde S_N \sim e^{4N\Lambda(\pi/4)/\pi}(2N)^{-1/2}$ contributes $N^{-(c+d)/2}$ against Lemma 4's $N^{(c+d+3)/2}$, and the cusp-dependence cancels exactly.

**On the link side the constant is derived, not calibrated.** Van der Veen's $E$ *is* the object measured here as $c_\sigma$, and his proof gives it explicitly as a $(c+d+1)$-dimensional Gaussian integral for the whole family; he evaluates it only far enough to show it is nonzero, and his closing paragraph defers the rest — citing the conjecture of Gukov–Murakami that $E$ "is determined by the Ray-Singer torsion of the complement twisted by the holonomy representation" — to a subsequent publication he never wrote. Evaluating that integral, after correcting a sign in his Lemma 2a and one in his Step-3 Gaussian coefficient (neither affecting his theorem), gives in closed form for every $b = 1$ chain

$$
  c_\sigma\big(W_{a,1,c,d}\big) \;=\; 2^{\,1/2 - n}\,\big(n^2 + m^2\big)^{-1/4},
  \qquad n := c+d,\quad m := 4a - c + d .
$$

Combined with the law, this forces $|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}| = 2^{\,2n-2}\sqrt{n^2+m^2}$. That is a prediction, not a fit: it reproduces the corpus's independently computed $|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}|$ at the Whitehead link and the Borromean rings exactly, and it was **stated in advance** and then confirmed to $18$ digits at three further chains the corpus had never touched — $L8n5$ at three cusps, $L10n100$ and $L10n103$ at four. So $k = 2^{-1/2}$ survives a test at a cusp number it was never fitted to, against machinery that knows nothing about the colored Jones polynomial.

Squaring leaves no radical: $\tau^2 = 16^{\,n-1}(n^2+m^2)$, so **every $b=1$ chain has integer $\tau^2$**, and $n^2+m^2$ is the norm of the Gaussian integer $n-mi$. The $a$-dependence is the sharpest part of this, because $a$ enters van der Veen's Lemma 1 *only through the phase* $\chi^{4a-c+d}$ — the modulus of the summand does not know about it, so the entire $a$-dependence of $|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}|$ is manufactured by interference in the $n$-sum. Since $\mathrm{Vol}$ is independent of $a$, the twisted chains are one complement reframed, and framing by $\mu-a\lambda$ on one cusp gives $\tau^2 = 2, 10, 26, 50, 82$ at two cusps and $64, 320, 320, 1088, 1088$ at three — $25$ framings, no mismatch, with the three-cusp values coming out equal on every cusp and symmetric under $a\mapsto-a$, neither of which was put in.

**The knot side closes by the same method, and needs no link at all.** A $b=1$ Whitehead chain has $c+d+1 \ge 2$ components, so nothing above reaches $4_1$. But what made the link computation close was not van der Veen: it was that his critical point sits at $(\pi/2, \pi/4)$, a *rational* point, because those complements are glued from regular ideal **octahedra**. The figure-eight is two regular ideal **tetrahedra**, and Kashaev's invariant $\langle 4_1\rangle_N = \sum_k |(q;q)_k|^2 = \sum_k e^{-2s_k}$ is a one-variable sum in the same $s_k$. Its saddle is at $\theta^\ast = 5\pi/6$, the Stirling factor is $2\sin\theta^\ast = 1$, the Hessian is $|\cot\theta^\ast| = \sqrt3$, and

$$
  \langle 4_1\rangle_N \;\sim\; 3^{-1/4}\,N^{3/2}\,e^{\mathrm{Vol}\cdot N/2\pi},
$$

so $c_\sigma(4_1) = 3^{-1/4}$ and the power $3/2$ are both derived. In one variable the saddle condition *is* $2\sin\theta^\ast = 1$, so the Stirling factor is pinned to $1$ and $c_\sigma = |\cot\theta^\ast|^{-1/2} = 3^{-1/4}$ with no freedom: $3^{-1/4}$ is a property of *one summation variable*, not of $4_1$. The $4_1$ calibration on which the whole $c_\sigma$ arc rested is thereby retired.

**Where closed forms exist, and why there are exactly two.** The saddle sits at a root-of-unity shape precisely for the regular ideal polyhedra — $z = e^{i\pi/3}$ (tetrahedron, trace field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, $|\tau| = \sqrt3/2$) and $z = i$ (octahedron, trace field $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, $|\tau| = 2^{2n-2}|A|$ with $A = n - mi$ a **Gaussian integer** and $|A| = \sqrt{n^2+m^2}$ the root of its norm). Those are the two imaginary quadratic fields with extra units, the two Bianchi cases. For a general knot the trace field is a general number field and $\det(\mathrm{Hess})$ — which is what $\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}$ *is* — is an algebraic number in it with no reason to admit a radical expression. The obstruction to extending the method is arithmetic, not analytic.

**What remains open, stated exactly.** The law itself: what is established is that a derived Jones-side constant and an independently computed $1$-loop invariant satisfy $c_\sigma \propto |\tau|^{-1/2}$ on six manifolds at one, two, three and four cusps, which is a far stronger test than the corpus previously had and is still not a proof. The analytic inputs — the Euler–Maclaurin expansion of $s_k$ and Laplace's method — are established separately, while the algebraic statements above take the saddle conditions as hypotheses and prove the downstream rigidity. Specifically, the Euler–Maclaurin expansion gives $\log g_k = -\tfrac{2N}{\pi}\Lambda(\theta) + \log(2N\sin\theta) + \tfrac{\pi}{6N}\cot\theta + O(1/N^2)$ with an explicit remainder and constant term derived from $\Lambda(b) = b - b\log 2b + O(b^3)$ rather than fitted, while the Jacobi-theta identity gives Laplace's method for the Gaussian sum to $1 + O(e^{-\pi^2/a})$. What is still assumed is the connecting step extracting the saddle condition, the Stirling factor and the Hessian from the expansion. That is a smaller and much better-defined gap than the two it replaced, and it is the one to state when citing this proposition.

The distinction the proposition draws also fixes the shape of the residual risk. Any missing cusp-dependent step $g$ in the dictionary between the two normalisations satisfies $k_{\mathrm{meas}}(c) = k_{\mathrm{true}}\,g(c)$, so agreement of the measured constants at two cusp numbers forces $g$ to agree there — which excludes every candidate with $g(1)\neq g(2)$, the retracted per-cusp factor $2^{(c-1)/2}$ among them. The tension lives in the exponent bookkeeping, not in the constant, at the cusp numbers tested.
