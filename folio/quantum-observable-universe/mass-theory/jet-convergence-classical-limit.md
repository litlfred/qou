**Conjecture (exact at $k = 3$, convergence at $k > 3$).**
*At $k = 3$, the jet-filtered knot invariant evaluates exactly using the $q$-deformed Wedderburn weights $W_\lambda = d_q(\lambda)^2 / [3]_q!$. As the jet level $k \to \infty$, the invariant converges to the linear $q$-dimension scaling $f_\mu^\infty = d_q(\mu)/\sum d_q(\nu)$.*

The classical branching multiplicities remain $q$-independent, but the $q$-hook corrections do not factorize over the branching. The Kerov $q$-hook walk and the Féray-Méliot $q$-Plancherel Central Limit Theorem ensure $O(1/\sqrt{k})$ convergence. The $q = 1$ antecedent is Kerov's central limit theorem for Plancherel-random Young diagrams, in which the rescaled diagram fluctuates around the limit shape at order $1/\sqrt{n}$ \cite[\S7]{ivanov-olshanski2003}. That order is the scale of the fluctuations, not a proved rate of convergence in distribution. For the normalised character ratio $\tfrac{n-1}{\sqrt 2}\,\chi^{\lambda}(12)/\dim\lambda$ under Plancherel measure, the best proved Berry--Esseen bound is $40.1\,n^{-1/4}$, with a bound of order $n^{-1/2}$ conjectured \cite[Thm.~1.2]{fulman2003}. The $O(1/\sqrt{k})$ convergence stated above is therefore stronger than the proved $q=1$ distributional rate for this statistic, and is read here as a scale, not as a proved rate. The $q$-deformation critically enhances the standard channel at low $k$, explaining why the nuclear topology dominates binding exclusively at physical energy scales.
