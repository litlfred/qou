Convention D does not annihilate the three-strand antisymmetriser by accident.
The row cap propagates up the whole tower, and what it leaves behind is a named
algebra.

Because the fundamental representation of $SU(2)$ is two-dimensional, no
$q$-antisymmetriser exists on three of its strands, and the Wedderburn weight of any
partition with three or more rows vanishes identically --- not numerically at $q_0$,
but as a rational function of $q$. The surviving blocks of $H_n(q)$ are therefore
exactly the two-row shapes, which is the stratum
[the two-row stratum](#def:two-row-stratum) isolates for the ${}^4\mathrm{He}$
fingerprint. Summing the squared Specht dimensions over those shapes gives, level by
level, the Catalan numbers:

| $n$ | surviving shapes | $\sum \dim^{2}$ | $\dim H_n(q)$ | $C_n$ |
|:---:|------------------|----------------:|--------------:|------:|
| 2 | $(2),\,(1,1)$ | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 3 | $(3),\,(2,1)$ | 5 | 6 | 5 |
| 4 | $(4),\,(3,1),\,(2,2)$ | 14 | 24 | 14 |
| 5 | $(5),\,(4,1),\,(3,2)$ | 42 | 120 | 42 |
| 6 | $(6),\,(5,1),\,(4,2),\,(3,3)$ | 132 | 720 | 132 |
| 7 | $(7),\,(6,1),\,(5,2),\,(4,3)$ | 429 | 5040 | 429 |

The quotient of $H_n(q)$ by the radical of its Markov form thus has the dimension of
the Temperley--Lieb algebra $TL_n(q)$, and the blocks match shape by shape. At
$n = 4$ the bookkeeping closes exactly: $24 - 14 = 10 = 3^{2} + 1^{2}$, the discarded
$(2,1,1)$ and $(1^{4})$ blocks, so the quotient is the direct sum of the three
surviving matrix algebras and not a proper quotient of it.

## Preservation of observable channels under the Temperley–Lieb quotient

The metric obstruction below asks for a quotient by the radical, and taken on its own
that reads as an ad hoc repair. It is not. The radical is the Temperley--Lieb ideal,
generated by the three-strand $q$-antisymmetriser, and the quotient is a
well-studied algebra with a diagrammatic calculus and a standard bilinear form.
"Quotient by the radical" and "work in $TL_n(q)$" are one instruction.

Stated the other way: the degeneracy is not a defect convention D introduces, it is
what convention D means. Under convention C the framework computes in the full Hecke
algebra; under convention D it has been computing in Temperley--Lieb throughout.

## Proved algebraic identities versus numerical checks

The dimension count above is an exact algebraic theorem at every $n$, first
identified by numerical measurement. The
weights were evaluated for every partition of $n = 2,\dots,7$ at $50$ decimal places,
with worst residual $1.34 \times 10^{-51}$ against the closed form
$y_{(n-k,k)} = [n-2k+1]_q/[2]_q^{\,n}$. Strengthening this numerical check, the normalisation holds as an **exact rational identity in $q$**,

$$
  \sum_{k} f^{(n-k,k)}\,[n-2k+1]_q \;=\; [2]_q^{\,n},
$$

which simplifies algebraically to $0$ in $\mathbb{Q}(q)$ for $n = 2,\dots,10$ — so the closed
form is a normalised weight family for *every* $q$ at once, which no
finite-precision evaluation can establish. The Specht dimensions are cross-checked
by two independent routes (ballot and hook-length), and the table above is
reproduced rather than cited.

**Algebraic identities at general $n$.** Four statements,
none of which is the isomorphism itself:

- $\sum_\lambda \dim_\lambda\, y_\lambda = 1$ --- the normalisation
  $\mathrm{tr}(1) = 1$;
- $\sum_k m_k(n)\,[n{+}1{-}2k]_q = [2]_q^{\,n}$ --- the same identity with the
  denominator cleared, i.e. the $q$-dimension count of $V^{\otimes n}$;
- $\sum \dim^2 = C_n$ over the two-**row** shapes and,
  separately, over the two-**column** shapes;
- $\dim \lambda = \dim \lambda'$ **for this family**.

**External literature input: the Temperley–Lieb algebra isomorphism.** A
count of blocks and a count of dimensions are not an isomorphism of algebras $H_n(q)/\mathrm{rad} \cong TL_n(q)$. The
identification remains what this section calls it: something the counts support,
deliberately not a proposition. What *is* established directly is the trace-theoretic half:
the trace radical is a two-sided ideal, which is
what licenses passing to the quotient as an algebra at all, and convention D is
the standard normalised Temperley--Lieb trace at loop parameter $\delta = [2]_q$.

## The two-row versus two-column convention collision

In the **conjugate** Wenzl–Schur convention at
$X = Q^{-N}$, the surviving blocks are those with $\lambda_1 \le N$, i.e. at most
$N$ **columns**. This section, and convention D, state the survivors as at most
two **rows**. Both are correct in their own convention, and passing between them requires
partition transposition $\lambda \mapsto \lambda'$.

The trap is that the two conventions label **different sets of partitions** while
carrying **equal dimension sums**. At
$n = 3$ the row-set is $\{(3),(2,1)\}$ and the column-set is
$\{(2,1),(1^3)\}$ — disjoint but for $(2,1)$, both summing to $5$; at $n = 6$ the
two sets share *nothing at all* and both still total $C_6 = 132$. So the
Catalan counts agree under either reading, and a check that looks only at dimensions cannot tell the two apart, as verified explicitly at $n = 3, 4, 5$.

Both halves of that distinction hold at every $n$:
transposition is an explicit bijection between the two families,
so they are equinumerous; the families are nevertheless
different sets (witnessed at $n = 3$ by the single
row $(3)$); and their squared-dimension totals agree because $\dim \lambda = \dim \lambda'$.

The practical consequence: transposition is the only correct transport between
the two conventions, and it is not the identity. Matching totals is not evidence
that a theorem carries across.
