Every knotted component of the [canonical closure](#prop:canonical-closure-torus-chain)
is a $(2, 2g+1)$-torus knot with $g \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, and a torus knot is
fibered: its complement is an open book whose pages are Seifert surfaces of
genus $g$ sharing the knot as binding, with monodromy of order $2(2g+1)$ and a
unimodular Seifert form, so $H_1(\text{page})$ is self-dual -- a frame with a
dual frame. Since the [cohomological embedding criterion](#prop:cohomological-embedding)
that fixes the genus of [Bring's surface](#def:brings-surface) is
$\operatorname{rank} H_1(\mathrm{Seifert}(T_{2,s})) = (s-1)/2 \le g$ at the
forced $g = 4$, the pages of $T(2,3)$, $T(2,5)$ and $T(2,7)$ all embed on
Bring's surface, and $T(2,9)$ is the last of the family that does
(proved).

The complement of $T(2, 2g+1)$ is the unit tangent bundle of the
$(2, 2g+1, \infty)$ triangle orbifold, $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\Delta(2, 2g+1, \infty)$;
the link of the Bring cone is, canonically, the unit tangent bundle of Bring's
curve, $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_4$ (Bézout gives the canonical
embedding degree $6 = 2g - 2$). Both therefore carry the global left-invariant
frame $\{X, Y, V\}$ -- geodesic, horocyclic, fibre rotation -- and two Reeb
flows: $V$, whose orbits are the Seifert circles transverse to the pages, and
$X$, whose closed orbits are closed geodesics of the base. This is the
concrete form of the coordinate frame the osculating-circle pencil of a plane
curve suggests and cannot itself supply, since that pencil lives on a curve
and the closure is an isotopy class.

Two limits. The frame is the split real form $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R})$,
whereas the Reeb triad modelled on the Bring cone link elsewhere in this
manuscript is the compact form $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; a genus-$4$ unit tangent
bundle has $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_2}$ geometry and admits no 3-Sasakian
structure, so which real form is intended there is a question for that lemma.
And none of this reaches the twist: the framing datum the binding gap needs is
the torsion integral, which neither the pages nor the circle pencil carry by
themselves.
