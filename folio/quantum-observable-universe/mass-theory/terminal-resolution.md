*Work over a field of characteristic not $3$, with $q \neq 0$, $q^2 + 1 \neq 0$ and $q^2 + q^{-2} \neq 0$, and let $z = q^3/(q^2+1)$ be the convention-D Markov parameter. Under the [pair Skein mass formula](#lem:pair-skein-mass-formula), write $\mathcal{S}_{ab} = (q^2\mu_a + q^{-2}\mu_b)/[2]_q$ with $[2]_q = q + q^{-1}$, and take the nucleon masses to be*

$$
  m_p = \mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{u}} + 2\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{d}},
  \qquad
  m_n = 2\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{d}} + \mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{d}},
$$

*where $\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}$ and $\mu_{\mathfrak{d}}$ are the dressed quark masses. Then:*

*(i) The splitting is exactly*

$$
  m_n - m_p = \frac{q^2 + q^{-2}}{[2]_q}\,(\mu_{\mathfrak{d}} - \mu_{\mathfrak{u}}) = \frac{[4]_q}{[2]_q^{2}}\,(\mu_{\mathfrak{d}} - \mu_{\mathfrak{u}}).
$$

*(ii) The map $(\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}, \mu_{\mathfrak{d}}) \mapsto (m_p, m_n)$ is linear with determinant $3\,(q^2 + q^{-2})^2/[2]_q^2$, and it is a bijection: each pair $(m_p, m_n)$ comes from exactly one pair $(\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}, \mu_{\mathfrak{d}})$.*
