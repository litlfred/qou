**Remark (prior art: the adjoint torsion as a derivative, with a signed duality).**
Let $M$ be a hyperbolic $3$-manifold whose boundary is $b$ tori, $\rho$ an irreducible $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})$-representation on the geometric component, and $\varphi\colon \pi_1(M) \to \mathbb{Z}^n$ a surjection. Dubois and Yamaguchi define the twisted Alexander invariant $\Delta^{\varphi \otimes \mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho}_M$ as the torsion of an acyclic complex, show that it is a Laurent polynomial up to a monomial factor, and prove that its limit at $t_1, \ldots, t_n \to 1$, divided by one factor $t^{a^{(\ell)}} - 1$ per boundary torus, is $(-1)^b$ times the non-abelian (adjoint) Reidemeister torsion $\mathbb{T}^M_\lambda(\rho)$ \cite[\S 1, p. 2]{dubois-yamaguchi2012}. They also prove a signed duality: for a link exterior,
$$
\Delta^{\varphi \otimes \mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho}_{E_L}(t^{-1}) = (-1)^b\, \Delta^{\varphi \otimes \mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho}_{E_L}(t)
$$
up to a factor $t^k$, so that for a knot exterior the polynomial is odd under $t \mapsto t^{-1}$ \cite[Theorem 14 and eqs. (32)--(33), \S 8, p. 23]{dubois-yamaguchi2012}. For the Whitehead link exterior at a lift of the holonomy representation and its conjugate they obtain $\mathbb{T}^{E_L}_\lambda = \tau_0 \cdot 8(1 \mp \sqrt{-1})$, with $\tau_0 = \pm 1$ a sign \cite[\S 6.3, eq. (29), p. 19]{dubois-yamaguchi2012}.

This gives the [1-loop prefactor proposition](#prop:one-loop-prefactor-provenance) a second, independent way to compute the torsion it uses, through an acyclic complex rather than a triangulation. Because the torsion is a topological invariant, any formula for it that changes value when the same manifold is retriangulated is a defect of that formula, not evidence that the quantity is ill-defined. The duality is a checkable constraint on any torsion polynomial the corpus produces.

The normalisations have not been reconciled. At the Whitehead link the proposition's closed form gives $|\tau_{\mathrm{DG}}| = \sqrt{2}$, while the value above has modulus $8\sqrt{2}$, for the curve fixed by their substitution $t_1 = t_2 = t$. Which choice of curve and which convention factor account for the ratio $8$ has not been worked out here, so the two numbers should not be used interchangeably.
