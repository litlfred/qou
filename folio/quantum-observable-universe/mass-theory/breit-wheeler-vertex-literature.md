Identifying $\sigma_0\sigma_1$ as the Hopf-link intermediate connects the QOU framework to established models of knotted matter and topological photonics.

**Braid-preon mapping.** The Bilson-Thompson topological preon model maps fermions to framed braids in $B_3$, with chirality determined by the crossing signs. A related topological model takes fermions to be hyperbolic knot complements and bosons to be torus bundles, with knot complements presented as $3$-fold branched covers of the $3$-disk along $3$-braids, and obtains the spectrum of electric charges from the topology \cite{arxiv-1910.09966v1}. The $e^-/e^+$ pair in the QOU vertex adopts this construction exactly. The QOU explicitly identifies $\sigma_0\sigma_1$ as the topological seed that breaks the parity-symmetric photon vacuum into the chiral trefoil channel.

**Hecke asymmetry.** The Hecke deformation $q \neq q^{-1}$ asymmetrises crossings, providing the standard mechanism for parity-violating anyon statistics. In the QOU Breit-Wheeler vertex, the Hecke roots are the circular polarisation eigenvalues. The algebraic asymmetry drives both anyonic parity violation and the left/right helicity splitting.

**Photonic hopfions.** Structured light experiments realize three-dimensional polarisation solitons mapping to the Hopf fibration, where topological charge equals helicity. The QOU presentation of $L_+$ and $L_-$ as positive and negative Hopf links is the categorical analogue. In both settings, the link type is the topological object whose oriented presentations are the helicity states. Beyond the Hopf link, there are null solutions of the free Maxwell equations whose field lines realise every torus knot and torus link, with the knot type preserved under time evolution \cite{arxiv-1302.0342v1}.
