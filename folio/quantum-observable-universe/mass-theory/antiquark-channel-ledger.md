Step 2 of the [derived atom](#def:derived-atom) disposes of the antiquarks in six words — *"either annihilate or form antinucleons"* — and the [nucleon-from-photons summary](#prop:four-term-pair-nucleon) carries them through its conservation table as "3 quarks + 3 antiquarks" before keeping only the quarks. A third channel is available and is not named: three $q\bar q$ pairs can bind as **three mesons**. This remark closes that gap, and the closure is not "the channel was overlooked" but "the channel carries zero binding on the framework's own formula".

**The competition is between two channels, not three.** Three pairs give three quarks and three antiquarks. Conserving baryon number, lepton number and charge, the exhaustive partitions are:

| Channel | Products | Constituent knot | $\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathrm{hyp}}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | nucleon $+$ antinucleon | Borromean rings $L6a4$, twice | $8G \approx 7.328$ each |
| **B** | three mesons | $L_+$, three times | $0$ each |
| **C** | annihilation back to $\gamma\gamma$ | — | — |

Channel C is the corpus's "annihilate"; channel A is its "form antinucleons". Channel B is the one step 2 omits.

**Why A is selected, on the framework's own terms.** The [categorical mass invariant](#conj:atom-knot-mass-derivation) gives the physical binding energy as $B(\mathfrak{H}) = m_\mathfrak{e}\cdot M(\mathfrak{H})|_{q = q_0}$, and the hyperbolic volume of the constituent knot is what carries the geometric normalisation — torus knots contribute zero, hyperbolic knots contribute $\mathrm{Vol}(K)/\hbar_q^2$. The Borromean rings of channel A are hyperbolic; the Hopf link of channel B is a solid-torus complement with $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$. So channel A has strictly positive binding and channel B has none. A minimum-action selection between them is not close.

**This is the same mechanism that makes $m_\pi$ a calibration.** Channel B's vanishing and [the pion's missing mass scale](#rem:pion-mass-hyperbolic-threshold) are one fact seen twice: the meson sits below the hyperbolic threshold of the $(\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})^n$ ladder, so the volume route reaches neither its mass nor its binding. The omission in step 2 is therefore justified — but for a reason the text does not give, and the reason is worth having, because it also predicts where the argument would fail. Were the meson's binding topology hyperbolic, channel B would compete and the derived atom would not follow.

**What this does not say.** Mesons are bound; the framework does not claim otherwise. The [categorical meson](#def:categorical-meson) records the Hopf-link binding raising the net normal-form amplitude by $\approx 0.097$ over the bare $T_{01}\cdot T_{10}$ pair, and replacing that pair's neutrino component. So there is binding in the Hecke normal form and none in the volume normalisation, and the two are not in contradiction — they measure different things. What follows is only that $B(\mathfrak{H})$, as defined, does not reach channel B, which is exactly why $m_\pi$ enters as CAL-3 rather than as a derivation.

**A caution about the net.** The three channels cannot be ranked by the normal-form net amplitude, even though all three are elements of the same $H_3(q)$ at the same $q_0$. Binding *raises* the net: $\mathrm{net}(\mathfrak{M}_+) \approx +1.515$ against the unbound pair's $+1.418$, while $\mathrm{net}(\mathfrak{p}) \approx +0.999$. If minimum action meant minimum net, the bare pair would beat the bound meson and no meson would form. The minimised functional is the confinement SDP's optimal dual objective — the $\nu_{\mathrm{SDP}}$ of the mass invariant — and the net is not a proxy for it.
