When modeling the macroscopic atomic mass of heavy isotopes up to Oganesson ($A=294$), evaluating the absolute topological Hecke trace of the $A$-nucleon knot becomes computationally intractable. The Jaco–Shalen–Johannson (JSJ) decomposition glues the atomic knot complement along essential (inter-nucleon) tori, and hyperbolic volume is additive under torus gluing. The *leading* mass term is therefore volume-additive — linear in the nucleon counts:

$$
\mathfrak{m}_{\text{qCLT,lead}}(Z, N) = Z\,\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{p}} + N\,\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{n}}.
$$

Accordingly the absolute mass prediction partitions into two computational channels:

1. **Symbolic trace channel ($m_{\mathrm{symbolic}}$)**: the exact closed-form evaluation of the Hecke matrix traces, computationally constrained to light isotopes ($A \le 8$).
2. **JSJ-additive channel ($m_{\mathrm{qCLT,approx}}$)**: the JSJ-additive estimator $Z\,\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{p}} + N\,\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{n}} - B(Z,N)$, where $B$ is the binding energy (the $\Delta_\lambda$ correction).

The additive **structure** is exact: the inter-nucleon tori make the volume — hence the leading mass term — linear in $(Z,N)$. The *absolute* prediction depends on two residuals: the binding fraction $B/\mathfrak{m}$ (which plateaus near $0.9\%$ under nuclear saturation, so it does **not** vanish for heavy $A$) and the nucleon-mass gap. The nucleon-mass gap is $+2.89\%$ in the *proved* pipeline (the production linking weight); the derived, no-fit spectral-phase correction $\theta/(8\hbar_q^2)\approx 0.9711$ reduces it $\sim 35\times$ to $-819$ ppm, but is **exploratory and not part of the production route** (its $\theta/(8\hbar_q^2)$ step remains an unproved hypothesis). Validated on the three light compound nuclei where $B$ is available (D, T, ${}^3\mathrm{He}$), the binding-corrected channel reproduces the CODATA atomic mass to within the nucleon-mass gap, *uniformly* across isotopes — production residual $\approx 2.89\%$ (cross-isotope spread $0.014\%$), spectral residual $\approx -800$ ppm. The uniformity is the signature that the additive structure is sound; the asymptotic claim is about the linear **structure**, not absolute exactness. See the computational witness `m-qclt-approx.witness.json`.

The binding $B$ admits two evaluations in this channel: the framework's per-isotope $\Delta_\lambda$ prediction, and a self-contained **factorized q-CLT** binding $B = \alpha \sum_i \ln\lvert V_i\rvert$, with the derived scale $\alpha = \mathfrak{m}_e/\!\left(q_0(q_0-1)\right)$ (fixed by $q_0$ and the electron mass alone — not $Q_\beta$, not a fit) and each $V_i$ the Markov-trace ratio of a two-nucleon sub-braid for one bond type ($p$–$p$, $p$–$n$, $n$–$n$). Because the $n$–$n$ bond is more suppressing than the $p$–$p$ bond, this form resolves the ${}^3\mathrm{H}/{}^3\mathrm{He}$ isospin splitting — which the isospin-blind leading term cannot — to within $1.1\times$ of the AME binding energy with no fit. The bond-count scaling (chain versus all-pairs) is a modeling choice rather than a derived quantity, and $A \ge 4$ undershoots, so this remains a structural estimator: the absolute mass still carries the nucleon-mass gap above.
