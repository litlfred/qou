Habiro computes the Kauffman-bracket invariant of the Borromean rings with each component coloured by an element of the skein module of the solid torus. In the natural basis $\{e_n\}$, where $e_n$ corresponds to the $(n+1)$-dimensional irreducible representation of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, the value $\langle e_l, e_m, e_n\rangle$ is a finite sum over $0 \le i \le \min(l,m,n)$ of powers of $(a - a^{-1})$ times ratios of quantum factorials, symmetric in $l, m, n$ \cite[\S 3, p. 37]{habiro2000}. In his modified basis $\{R'_n\}$ the same invariant is diagonal: it is $(a - a^{-1})^n\,[2n+1]!/[n]!$ when $l = m = n$ and $0$ otherwise \cite[Prop. 7.1, p. 41]{habiro2000}.

The [closed form of the Borromean Markov trace](#prop:borromean-markov-trace-closed-form) is the uncoloured case, so this is the published all-colours version of the same invariant, and Prop. 7.1 is a precise locator for the corpus's citations of this key. The paper works in the Kauffman variable $a$ and its own normalisation; it does not compute the Markov trace in $H_3(q)$, so matching the two at the fundamental colour needs the change of variables and framing to be checked first.
