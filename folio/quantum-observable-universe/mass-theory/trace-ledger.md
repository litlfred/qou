Four different operations in this paper are called a trace, and the Euler characteristic of the base curve is none of them. This remark lists them once, says how they are related, and marks which relations are proved.

**1. The rigid trace and the quantum dimension.** In a rigid monoidal category every endomorphism $f \colon X \to X$ has a trace $\mathrm{tr}(f) \in \operatorname{End}(\mathbf{1})$, obtained by closing $f$ with the evaluation and coevaluation of $X$. The trace of the identity is the quantum dimension $\dim_q(X)$. For the fundamental object of $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ it is $[2]_q = q + q^{-1}$; for the irreducible of highest weight $m - 1$ it is $[m]_q$. On the Hecke tower this trace is what one obtains by closing a braid on one strand.

**2. The Markov trace.** On the Iwahori–Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$ a Markov trace is a family of linear functionals $\mathrm{tr}_M \colon H_n(q) \to R$ that is normalised, cyclic and compatible with adding a strand at a fixed parameter $z$. It is a linear combination of the irreducible characters, $\mathrm{tr}_M = \sum_{\lambda \vdash n} y_\lambda\, \chi_\lambda$, and the [Markov-trace selection proposition](#prop:markov-trace-frobenius-selection) says which weights the framework uses: the row cap $y_\lambda = 0$ for partitions with three or more rows and $y_{(n-k,k)} = [n - 2k + 1]_q / [2]_q^{\,n}$, which is convention D, $z = q^{3}/(q^{2} + 1)$. Its value on a closed braid is the Jones polynomial, and its value on the identity of $H_1$ is $1$ — after the normalisation by $[2]_q^{\,n}$ that the weights carry, the quantum dimension of $V^{\otimes n}$ has been divided out.

**3. The Hecke characters.** The $\chi_\lambda$ themselves are the traces of the irreducible representations of $H_n(q)$, one for each partition $\lambda$ of $n$; they are the Wedderburn blocks of the algebra. Any trace on $H_n(q)$ is a linear combination of them, so the content of a trace is its weight vector $(y_\lambda)$.

**4. The categorical trace.** For a $k$-linear category $\mathbf{C}$ the categorical trace, or zeroth Hochschild homology $HH_0(\mathbf{C})$, is the quotient of $\bigoplus_X \operatorname{End}(X)$ by the span of the commutators $fg - gf$ \cite{bghl-2017}. The rigid trace of item 1 is the composite $\operatorname{End}(X) \to HH_0(\mathbf{C}) \to \operatorname{End}(\mathbf{1})$, and the class of $\mathrm{id}_X$ in $HH_0(\mathbf{C})$ is the categorical Chern character of $X$. For a semisimple category with finitely many simple objects, $HH_0(\mathbf{C})$ is $k^{\{\text{simples}\}}$, so the Chern character of $X$ is its vector of multiplicities and the rigid trace evaluates it against the vector of quantum dimensions. On a derived category of coherent sheaves the same construction gives Hochschild homology of the variety and the Mukai pairing \cite{caldararu-willerton-2010}; on the Hecke category it gives the horizontal trace, which specialises to Markov-type traces on $H_n(q)$ \cite{trinh-2021}.

**Proved relations among the four trace functionals.** Closing the fibre action of $H_n(q)$ on $V^{\otimes n}$ by the categorical trace produces a Markov trace; that this Markov trace is convention D — that the row cap and faithfulness on the two-row blocks force $z = q^{3}/(q^{2} + 1)$ — is established algebraically at $n = 3$ and verified numerically for every partition of $n \leq 7$. Whether the trace the mass channel actually uses *is* the categorical trace of the fibre action is the open antecedent of that proposition, and the general-$n$ statement is proved only as fixed-$n$ instances of the recursion $[m + 2]_q + [m]_q = [2]_q [m + 1]_q$. The deformation-quantisation index theorem \cite{nest-tsygan-1995} is the model for a trace evaluated through a Chern character and a characteristic class; the paper does not yet construct the corresponding map for the aether.

**Absence of the base Euler characteristic from the fibre traces.** None of the four traces produces $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = 2 - 2g$. The number $-6$ enters the paper through the base curve — its genus, its tangent bundle's Euler class, the alternating sum of its Hochschild homology — and not through the closure of any braid ([three Euler characteristics](#rem:three-euler-characteristics)).
