Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe with
substrate parameter $q$ and Skein module
$\mathrm{Sk}_q = \mathrm{Sk}_q(\mathbf{C})$.
The Skein module $\mathrm{Sk}_q$ is generated as an
algebra by:

1. The fundamental knot types $\{K_i\}_{i \in \mathcal{F}}$, defined as the
   unknot and the torus knots, and
2. The Hopf link $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}$ (the binding operation).

Every hadron admits a terminal Skein resolution into a finite
polynomial in these generators with coefficients in
$\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]$.

**Scope.** Two limits apply. First, "fundamental" means torus, not
"volume zero": composite knots such as $3_1 \# 3_1$ and satellite knots also
have $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$ but are not torus knots. Second, when $\mathrm{Sk}_q$ is
the skein module of $S^3$ with the relation used in the proof, it is free of
rank one, and every link equals the unknot there (see the proof). Generation
then holds trivially, and the resolution coefficients do not single out the
Hopf link.
