The transfer-matrix evaluation was first exercised on light nuclei, where the nucleon count is small. Heavy atoms test whether that evaluation holds as the count grows across the periodic table. The worked examples below form an increasing-mass ladder — ${}^{14}\mathrm{C} \to {}^{14}\mathrm{N}$, ${}^{60}\mathrm{Co} \to {}^{60}\mathrm{Ni}$, and ${}^{210}\mathrm{Bi} \to {}^{210}\mathrm{Po}$ — and carry the transfer-matrix evaluation through each in turn.
