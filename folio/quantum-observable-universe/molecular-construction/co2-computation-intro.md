With heteronuclear bonding in place, adding a third atom yields the linear triatomic test case. Carbon dioxide $CO_2$ as a molecular braid couples a central carbon to two oxygens, each via a shared electron pair on the molecular Hecke strands. The LP dual selects linear $O{=}C{=}O$ geometry. The optimum places the two $C{=}O$ trefoils on opposite sides of the central strand. This configuration gives the VSEPR molecular geometry from $\Phi_1$ minimisation. The linear triatomic case isolates molecular geometry as a consequence of $\Phi_1$ minimisation, independent of bond order.
