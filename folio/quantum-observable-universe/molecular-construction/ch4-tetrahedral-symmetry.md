*Methane's tetrahedral geometry ($109.5^\circ$ bond angles) is a direct consequence of $\Phi_1$ minimisation. In the 26-strand braid, the optimal ordering distributes the four H atoms equidistant from each other in strand space. This corresponds to the vertices of a regular tetrahedron in physical space.*

*The computation establishes the mapping:*

| Braid quantity | Physical quantity |
|:-------------|:----------------|
| Strand separation between H atoms | H--C--H bond angle |
| $\Phi_1$ penalty per H--H pair | Steric repulsion energy |
| $S_4$ orbit of optimal ordering | Tetrahedral symmetry group $T_d$ |
| Breaking $S_4$ (asymmetric ordering) | Jahn--Teller distortion |

*For comparison, the same construction for $NH_3$ (nitrogen + 3 hydrogens) gives $C_{3v}$ symmetry with a lone pair occupying the 4th tetrahedral site. This produces the observed pyramidal geometry with bond angles $\approx 107.8^\circ$. The lone pair acts as a "phantom hydrogen" in the strand ordering, occupying space but contributing no bonding crossings.*

*Across small covalent hydrides, equilibrium bond geometry follows directly from the linear-programming dual of the molecular braid without external spatial coordinates.*
