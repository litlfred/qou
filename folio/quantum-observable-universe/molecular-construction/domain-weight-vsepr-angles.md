## Molecular consequence

Every electron domain is a braid word weighted $q^{\ell}$ on the symmetric block. A single bond $\sigma$ ($\ell{=}1$) weighs $q$, a double bond $\sigma^2$ ($\ell{=}2$) weighs $q^2$, and a lone pair $e^- = \sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_0$ ($\ell{=}3$) weighs $q^3$. There is one factor of $q$ per bond order, and the lone-to-bond ratio is $q^3/q = q^2$. The angle then needs only the $AX_nE_m$ domain counts and the hybridisation base $\theta_0$. Whether the central atom's self-dual $s$-channel lies in the bonding active set — controlled by the atom's size (its $s$–$p$ promotion gap) — determines $\theta_0$ without fitting any angle.

The second-row atoms (C, N, O) have a small $s$–$p$ gap. They hybridise, so the $s$-channel is active and the domains take the sp/sp$^2$/sp$^3$ base by steric number ($180/120/109.47^\circ$). The $q^3$ lone or $q^2$ double-bond weight then shifts this base. This reproduces the angles for $\mathrm{CH_4}, \mathrm{NH_3}, \mathrm{H_2O}, \mathrm{C_2H_4}, \mathrm{CO_2}$ within $0.6^\circ$ and specialises the [$\mathrm{H_2O}$ bond-angle shift proposition](#prop:h2o-bond-angle-shift).

Third-row sulfur has a large $s$–$p$ gap and does not hybridise. The $s$-channel drops from the active set, so the two bonds use the orthogonal pure-$p$ orbitals with base $\theta_0 = 90^\circ$. The residual $+2.1^\circ$ ($\mathrm{H_2S} \approx 92^\circ$) is a lone-pair braid asymmetry that $\mathrm{H_2O}$ lacks. With the $s$-channel inert, the two lone pairs are inequivalent — the self-dual $3s^2$ (the $e^-$ palindrome, defect $0$, $\ell{=}0$) and the chiral $3p_z$ (non-palindromic, defect $2\ell{=}2$). The chiral pair's directional bias opens the orbital-locked $90^\circ$. $\mathrm{H_2O}$'s two lone pairs are equal self-dual $e^-$ (defects $0,0$), so it has no such tilt. The sp$^3 \leftrightarrow$ pure-$p$ flip follows from the shell braid via the self-dual-$s$ versus chiral-$p$ split together with the $ns$ penetration of the $(n{-}1)$ inner shells, $\Delta(n)\propto(n{-}1) = \text{central-atom size}$. This produces a discrete flip at $n{=}2{\to}3$ with no fit. The group-16 sequence $\mathrm{O}\,104.5^\circ \to \mathrm{S/Se/Te}\sim 90^\circ$ confirms the saturation.
