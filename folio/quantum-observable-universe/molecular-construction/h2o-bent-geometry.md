*Interpretation of the [H$_2$O molecular braid](#def:h2o-molecular-braid).*

Oxygen contributes two bonding sites and two lone pairs. In the assembly, the lone pairs occupy two of the four tetrahedral directions around oxygen as non-bonding *phantom sites*. This is the $NH_3 \to H_2O$ extension of the methane $T_d$ optimisation. Lone-pair repulsion relaxes the $H$–$O$–$H$ angle from the ideal $109.5^\circ$ toward the observed $\approx 104.5^\circ$.

The four directions (two donor $H$ and two acceptor lone pairs) provide the per-molecule input to the ice lattice. Each water molecule hydrogen-bonds to four neighbours, two as donor and two as acceptor. The resulting tetrahedral coordination tiles into hexagonal ice $I_h$ (space group $P6_3/mmc$). The open tetrahedral network explains why ice is less dense than liquid water. The ice rule requires every bond to hold exactly one hydrogen. Which two of the four bonds carry this molecule's hydrogens is then the categorical origin of the Pauling residual entropy of ice, $\approx R\ln(3/2)$. The detailed tiling and its symmetry are developed in the phase-symmetry model.
