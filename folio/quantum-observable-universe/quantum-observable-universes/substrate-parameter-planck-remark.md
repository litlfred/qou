The substrate parameter $q$ governs the non-commutativity of the algebraic substrate. At $q = 1$, the algebra becomes commutative and the quantum structure degenerates to its classical shadow. The pair $(q, \hbar_q)$ parametrizes the deviation of $A$ from commutativity: in the classical limit $q \to 1$, $\hbar_q \to 0$ recovers the commutative coordinate ring and the smooth manifold geometry.

The invertibility condition $q \ne 0$ ensures that $A$ is a genuine Hopf algebra with well-defined antipode. Because $\hbar_q \ne 0$ whenever $q \ne 1$, the non-commutative regime always carries a non-trivial quantum scale.
