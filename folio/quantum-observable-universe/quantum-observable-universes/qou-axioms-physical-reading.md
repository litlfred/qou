A quantum universe supplies mechanics. These five conditions are what it takes for that mechanics to be watched, and each answers a physical question.

**Rigidity.** A dual is the same worldline read backwards. Evaluation annihilates a particle against its dual, coevaluation creates the pair out of the vacuum, and the snake identities say that creating a pair and then annihilating one member leaves the survivor untouched. Rigidity is therefore the statement that every particle has an antiparticle, and that creation and annihilation are available as processes.

Rigidity is also what closes a knot. A braid is an open thing with loose ends; only a category in which a strand may bend back on itself admits a closure, and only closed strands carry knot invariants. A braided universe has interactions. A rigid one has particles that can be counted.

**Non-degenerate integration.** The Frobenius form is a categorical Haar measure, and non-degeneracy is the requirement that it be faithful. An element pairing to zero against everything would sit in the algebra yet be invisible to every integral, beyond the reach of any experiment. Non-degeneracy also makes the categorical volume a well-defined topological constant.

**Observability.** A symmetry acting on nothing measurable is a redundancy of the description rather than a symmetry of the universe. The vanishing of $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A))$ eliminates gauge transformations that act as the identity on every observable, so each internal degree of freedom is reachable by measurement.

**Measurability.** Vanishing Chern classes are an anomaly-freedom condition. The vanishing of $c_1$ makes the adjoint bundle orientable, removing the primary obstruction to flatness; the vanishing of $c_2$ stops the volume form acquiring a phase under transport around a non-contractible loop. An observer may therefore carry a reference frame around the universe and return with it intact. Together these satisfy the hypotheses of the non-abelian Stokes theorem, which is what makes the integral well defined.

**Categorical volume constraint.** Fixing a volume form on the fundamental object forces every symmetry to preserve it, cutting the gauge group from $\mathrm{GL}(2)$ to $\mathrm{SL}(2)$. Physically it fixes a unit: the universe has a definite quantum of phase-space volume rather than a freely chosen scale. This is the condition that makes the reconstructed gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and not something larger.
