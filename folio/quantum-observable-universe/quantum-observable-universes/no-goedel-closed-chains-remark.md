**What is proved.** Let $r$ be any well-founded relation on a set $X$. Then no $x \in X$ satisfies $x \mathrel{r^{+}} x$, where $r^{+}$ is the transitive closure of $r$. The reason is that $r^{+}$ is again well-founded, and a well-founded relation is irreflexive. In particular, when $X \neq \varnothing$, the statement "$x \mathrel{r^{+}} y$ for all $x, y \in X$" is false. Applied to the braiding-induced ordering $\prec_c$ of ([braiding-induced ordering](#prop:braiding-induced-ordering)), this gives: no monomial of $H_n(q)$ returns to itself through a finite chain of strict $\prec_c$-steps. The argument uses only the well-foundedness of $\prec_c$ and nothing else about the braiding.

**Reading.** Gödel's rotating solution of the Einstein equations is homogeneous, and every event lies on a closed timelike curve. Equivalently, the chronological future $I^{+}(p)$ is the whole spacetime for every event $p$. If the chronological precedence of a QOU is read as the transitive closure of $\prec_c$, as in the arrow-of-time reading of ([braiding-induced ordering](#prop:braiding-induced-ordering)), then the statement above excludes this. It excludes more than Gödel's solution: under that reading, no event lies on any closed causal chain. Van Stockum and Kerr-interior closed timelike curves are excluded in the same way.

**What this does not say.** It is not a statement about general relativity. Gödel's metric remains an exact solution of the Einstein equations. The exclusion holds only under the identification of chronological precedence with $\prec_c$. That identification is interpretive and is not a theorem of this paper. The non-symmetry hypothesis $c_{V,V}^{2} \neq \mathrm{id}$ plays no role in the argument. So a symmetric braiding does not, by itself, reopen closed chains. Closed chains require giving up the identification.
