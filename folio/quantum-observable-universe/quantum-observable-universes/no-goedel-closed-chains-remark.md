Take time order to be the order of crossing applications, so one tick is one application of a crossing, as in the proof of ([braiding-induced ordering](#prop:braiding-induced-ordering)). This is a modelling choice. What follows from it is ([crossing-application chains](#lem:crossing-application-chains)).

Gödel's rotating solution \cite{goedel1949} is homogeneous, and a closed timelike curve passes through every event. Since Gödel's spacetime is connected, this is equivalent to saying that the chronological future $I^{+}(p)$ is all of spacetime for every $p$.

Under the tick definition there are three cases. On words, for any braiding, no state lies on a closed chain. On a state monoid with a writhe-type degree, a homomorphism to $\mathbb{Z}$ taking every crossing to $1$, no state lies on a closed chain either; the braid group $B_n$ is an example. When a crossing has finite order, every state lies on a closed chain; for a symmetric braiding the chain has length two. The statement concerns the tick model only; Gödel's metric remains an exact solution of the Einstein equations.
