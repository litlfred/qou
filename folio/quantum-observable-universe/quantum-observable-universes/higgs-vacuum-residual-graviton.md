**Interpretive remark (physics reading, not a mathematical claim).** The [mass-endomorphism conjecture](#conj:mass-endomorphism) says that the graviton is "the residual of $\Phi_{\rm mass}$ after subtracting the Higgs contribution". [The vacuum-residual proposition](#prop:higgs-vacuum-residual-idempotent) gives one precise reading of that sentence at the level of idempotents. Read $e = t^{-1}\varrho_\Phi\varrho_\Phi^{*}$ as the Higgs contribution on $X = A^{(H)}$, and $r = \mathrm{id} - e$ as the residual. On the two-vacuum object $X \otimes X$, the residual of $e \otimes e$ splits along the symmetry $\beta_{X,X}$. Its purely residual, symmetric summand $P_{+} \circ (r \otimes r)$ is the **graviton candidate (candidate 3)**: the symmetric part of (vacuum residual) $\otimes$ (its dual), using $X \cong X^{*}$.

This is a choice of identification. It is not derived. By the "defined residual $\neq$ derived decomposition" rule, $r := \mathrm{id} - e$ is a definition. The proposition proves only that $r$, $R = \mathrm{id} - e \otimes e$ and $P_{\pm} \circ R$ are complementary idempotents. It does not prove that any of them is the graviton, and it does not prove that $\Phi_{\rm mass}$ splits as "$e$-part plus $r$-part". That would need $\Phi_{\rm mass}$ to commute with $e$, which is open.

**What remains before "spin 2" could be claimed.**

1. *A rotation action on $X$.* The labels $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ in [Table 1](#tbl:g-action-on-substrate-table1-data) are $(j, y)$ for $G = \mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{U}(1)_Y$: weak isospin and hypercharge. They are not $(j_L, j_R)$ for a Lorentz or rotation group. So a spin label on $P_{+}\circ(r \otimes r)$ first needs an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ that acts on $X$, fixes $\varrho_\Phi$, and is identified with spatial rotation. The custodial diagonal of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ acting on the real 4-dimensional doublet is the natural choice. The corpus does not supply it.
2. *The residual is spin 1 under that action.* For the real form $X \cong \mathbb{R}^4$ with stabiliser $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ of the vacuum line, $\operatorname{im} r \cong \mathbb{R}^3$ is the vector representation. This is standard representation theory but is not formalised here.
3. *Removing the trace.* $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\text{spin }1) = \text{spin }2 \oplus \text{spin }0$. Isolating spin 2 needs a further idempotent on $P_{+}\circ(r\otimes r)$ that subtracts the trace part through the evaluation / coevaluation of $\operatorname{im} r$ (a rigid / pivotal datum). The present Lean development does not use one.
4. *Quantum dimensions.* In any pivotal category $e$ factors through $\mathbf{1}$, so $\dim_q(\operatorname{im} e) = \dim_q \mathbf{1} = 1$. The text of (G-4) assigns $[2]_q$ to "its trace dimension". That figure fits $\dim_q A^{(H)}$ in (G-2), not the rank-one image of $e_H$. The mismatch is recorded here and left unchanged, for the owner to decide.

No empirical quantity enters the proposition. The value $t = v^2/m_e^2$ in (G-3) plays no role: only $t \neq 0$ is used.
