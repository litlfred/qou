Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe, and let $V$ denote the fundamental object of its fibre, of dimension $N$. Suppose

1. **(volume)** the volume form is non-degenerate in the strong sense $\Lambda^2 V \cong \mathbb{1}$, equivalently $\dim \Lambda^2 V = 1$; and
2. **(realisation)** the gauge group $G = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$ acts on $\tau(V) \cong \mathbb{C}^N$ by matrices, each preserving the volume form ($\det g = 1$) and the Frobenius dagger ($g\,g^{\dagger} = 1$).

Then

$$
  N = 2
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  G \subseteq \mathrm{SU}(N),
$$

so $G \subseteq \mathrm{SU}(2)$ — or its split real form $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, depending on the signature of the Frobenius pairing.

**The two conclusions are independent, and only the first is about the rank.** That $g$ unitary with $\det g = 1$ lies in the special unitary group holds at *every* $N$; it carries no rank content whatever. What fixes $N = 2$ is hypothesis (1) alone, via $\dim \Lambda^2 V = \binom{N}{2} = 1 \iff N = 2$.

**Non-vacuity.** Hypothesis (1) is doing work rather than following from (2). At $N = 3$ it fails — $\dim \Lambda^2 V = 3$ — while the subgroup half applies at $N = 3$ unchanged. So the volume constraint is exactly what distinguishes rank two.

**Unproved realisation hypothesis.** Hypothesis (2) is a realisation assumption: that $\mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$ *is* a set of matrices satisfying those two constraints. Nothing here derives that identification, and the statement is a theorem about such a set, not about the tensor-automorphism group as constructed. The weaker reading of the volume constraint, $\Lambda^2 V \neq 0$, gives only $N \geq 2$ and admits $N = 3$.
