Let $p \colon \mathbf{1} \to A$ be a categorical point. Consider two independent observations at $p$ with probe directions $w_1, w_2 \in h\,W_1[[h]]$ acting on a fermion $\alpha$. To first order their discrepancy is

$$
  [w_1, w_2] \cdot (\alpha_V)_p.
$$

This vanishes if and only if the two directions commute or the particle is a boson ($(\alpha_V)_p = 0$). The Hecke algebra is non-commutative, so $[w_1, w_2] \neq 0$ for generic directions, for example $w_1 = h\sigma_0$ and $w_2 = h\sigma_1$: independent observations of a fermion at the same point generically disagree.

At a point $p$, the commutator of two observations equals the $q$-curvature $F_q$ of the $q$-connection evaluated on the loop spanned by the two observation directions: $[w_1, w_2] = F_q$. The observers agree if and only if the $q$-curvature vanishes. Swapping the observers negates the curvature.
