Let $W_n$ be the free monoid on the letters $\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_{n-1}$, and fix a total order on these letters. The degree-lexicographic order $\prec_c$ on $W_n$ (compare word length first, then compare lexicographically at equal length) is well-founded, and it is compatible with concatenation: $w \prec_c w'$ implies $u\,w\,v \prec_c u\,w'\,v$ for all $u, v \in W_n$.

The braiding $c_{V,V}$ enters only by fixing which letters are read as positive crossings: $\sigma_i$ applies $c_{V,V}$ and $\sigma_i^{-1}$ applies $c_{V,V}^{-1}$. Well-foundedness holds for any braiding, and the order on the letters is a choice, so $\prec_c$ is not canonical. The non-symmetry condition $c_{V,V}^2 \neq \mathrm{id}$ is not used on words. It matters on states, where a crossing of finite order forces closed chains. Restricting $\prec_c$ to reduced positive monomials, or to the $n!$ standard basis of the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$, gives a well-founded order on those subsets. Reading $\prec_c$ as the categorical arrow of time is an interpretation, discussed after the proof.
