The deformation parameter $q$ enters the framework categorically rather than by hand. It is the eigenvalue of the braiding $c_{V,V}$ on the symmetric summand of $V \otimes V$, named by the Hecke relation; the Tannaka--Krein reconstruction (reconstruction) then recovers $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ as $\mathrm{End}^{\otimes}(\tau)$ with that same $q$. The parameter is read off structure the category already carries.

**Unsettled numerical value of the eigenvalue.** The braiding is *assumed data* — a braided monoidal category comes equipped with a braiding, and different braidings give different eigenvalues. Reading $q$ off the braiding therefore fixes the parameter's *role* rather than its magnitude. The aether value $q_0 \approx 1.10998$ is not obtained this way. It is the CAL-2 calibration, pinned by matching $m_\mu/m_e = \mathrm{Vol}(4_1)/\hbar_q^2$ against the measured lepton ratio (epistemic status of the calibrations); in the words of that block, $q_0$ is *defined* to enforce the match.

The two statements live at different levels and neither implies the other. At the fibre level $q$ is a formal parameter whose categorical meaning is the braiding eigenvalue. At the archimedean level $q_0$ is a number fixed by one of the three external calibration inputs (three-calibration discipline). No first-principles derivation of the numerical modulus $q_0$ from the categorical axioms alone exists or is asserted within the framework.

Using the Hecke relation and the determinant constraint to relate braiding eigenvalues is standard (Kassel). What the extraction buys is an identification: the parameter governing the macroscopic fluid PDE is the same object as the microscopic braiding eigenvalue, so the two descriptions share one parameter instead of two. That is a structural statement about which quantity appears where, and it is silent on what that quantity's value is.
