Given a QOU $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$, the aether parameter $q \in \mathrm{End}(\mathbf{1})$ is uniquely determined *as a parameter*: the braiding spectrum and the positive-definite dagger select exactly one scalar from the Hecke eigenvalue pair $\{q, -q^{-1}\}$ (the positive one).  This is parameter uniqueness — the *extraction procedure* is canonical, with no ambiguity or choice.  It does *not* mean $q$ takes a single value.  The aether parameter is a field $q \colon M \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ that varies across the manifold and with energy scale; high-energy processes see a different $q$ than low-energy ones.  The observed value $q_0$ at a given spacetime location and energy scale is empirical.
