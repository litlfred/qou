By [the traceless symmetric square proposition](#prop:sym2-adjoint-traceless-spin-two), the adjoint $V_1$ of the gauge group $G = \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau) \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)$ of [the emergence theorem](#thm:su2-emergence) satisfies $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V_1) \ominus V_0 \cong V_2$, the [spin](#prop:spin-labels)-$2$ object of dimension $5$. A symmetric traceless tensor of spin $2$ is the shape of a graviton, so the scope of this result needs stating.

**Gauge spin and spacetime spin.** The proposition is representation theory of the gauge group $\mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$. "Spin $2$" there means that $V_2$ is the five-dimensional simple representation of that group. A graviton is a massless particle of helicity $\pm 2$, that is, a representation of the Poincaré group whose field is a symmetric rank-$2$ tensor of the Lorentz group $\mathrm{SO}(3,1)$. The two statements concern different groups. No proved statement of the corpus identifies $\mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$ with, or maps it onto, a spacetime rotation or Lorentz group, and none is used here. The closest text is the spatial-isotropy condition in the preamble to Bring's surface, which relates the spatial rotation algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ to Reeb degrees of the adjoint involution. That condition is a motivating requirement on the choice of surface, not a theorem, and it concerns spatial rotations only, not the Lorentz group.

A reading of $V_2$ as a graviton would therefore need two further results:

1. a homomorphism from $\mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$ to the spacetime Lorentz group under which the gauge adjoint $V_1$ transforms as a spacetime vector;
2. a reason why the symmetric traceless tensor built from $V_1$, rather than from a spacetime vector representation, carries the metric fluctuation.

Without them the same group theory holds for every $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge theory and says nothing specific about gravity. In the electroweak $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$, for example, $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ of the $W$ triplet contains a weak-isospin-$2$ quintet, which is not a graviton.

**What this remark does not claim.** It makes no claim that any object of $\mathbf{C}$ is a graviton, no claim about a coupling, a mass or a field equation, and no empirical prediction.
