Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe with aether parameter $q$ and Planck time $t_P(q) = q/(q-1) = 1/\hbar_q$. The **quantum temperature** of a region $U \subset M$ is defined as
$$
  T_q(U) = \hbar_q = \frac{q - 1}{q}.
$$
The inverse temperature is $\beta_q = 1/T_q = t_P(q)$.

The Kashaev volume--density reading introduces the aether density $\rho_q = 1/\hbar_q^{2}$. In these terms, the temperature and its inverse square are
$$
  T_q = \rho_q^{-1/2}, \qquad \beta_q^{2} = \rho_q.
$$
Temperature and aether density form conjugate square roots. A state at high drag density (small $\hbar_q$) rests at low temperature, while the inverse-temperature squared matches the density itself. This relationship absorbs several dimensional-analysis identities.

The quantum temperature remains the thermodynamic conjugate of the quantum entropy $S_q(U)$:
$$
  \frac{1}{T_q} = \frac{\partial S_q}{\partial \mathfrak{E}_q}\bigg|_V = \frac{1}{\hbar_q}.
$$
