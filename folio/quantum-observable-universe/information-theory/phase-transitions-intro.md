The quantum ideal gas law yields a partition function whose dominant saddle point sits inside one Hecke channel of $H_n(q)$. Phase transitions represent the loci where this dominance shifts. Critical temperatures follow from the $S^3$ boundary action, which restricts the categorical action to the Hopf base $S^2$ of the fibration $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3_q \to S^2$. The quantities $[2]_q$ and $[n_K]_q$ control the transition boundaries $T_q^{(g \to l)}$ and $T_q^{(l \to s)}$.

Gas, liquid, and solid states correspond to the symmetric, mixed, and antisymmetric Hecke channels, locating the triple point. Sublimation (the direct solid--gas transition) is recorded without a derivation: the Hecke channels are complementary summands rather than an extension of one by another, so $\mathrm{Ext}^1_{H_n(q)}$ supplies no obstruction to the liquid phase. An observational test of the $Z$-parity sublimation rule establishes a metallic-bonding criterion that separates sublimation from continuous condensation, and the boundary action provides a canonical example of degree-$0$ opacity.
