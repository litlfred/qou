**What this section says about P versus NP, and what it does not.** This section does not prove $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$, and nothing in it is evidence for either answer. It does three things. It translates the question into the language of skein resolution trees and horizons. It proves a number of exact algebraic facts that the translation rests on. And it locates where each QOU formulation meets the open problem. Throughout, $\mathrm{P}$ and $\mathrm{NP}$ are the standard classes for deterministic and nondeterministic Turing machines \cite{cook1971}. Mathlib does not define them, so no formal statement in this section is about them: wherever the formalisation names $\mathrm{P}$ or $\mathrm{NP}$, they are opaque propositions, and the formal content is propositional logic about those propositions.

**The translation.** A computation of $N$ gates is encoded as a braid $\beta$ with $c = 3N$ crossings ([Skein Turing machine](#rem:skein-turing-machine)). Its output is the Markov trace of $\beta$, a Jones-type invariant of the closure $L = \hat{\beta}$. Expanding every crossing by the skein relation gives a resolution tree with $3^c$ leaves. The [computational horizon](#def:computational-horizon) is the part of that tree whose degree-$0$ value cannot be reached within a polynomial budget. [The boundary conjecture](#conj:p-vs-np-boundary) restates $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$ as non-emptiness of that horizon, and [the QOU conjecture](#conj:qou-p-neq-np) restates it as non-vanishing of an obstruction residue. Both are offered as a dictionary. Neither is a reduction, because no statement here shows that either side implies the other.

**Status, sorted.**

1. *Proved, with statements that can be read against Mathlib and QOU definitions.* Each of these is a theorem of algebra or arithmetic. None is a theorem about $\mathrm{P}$ or $\mathrm{NP}$.
   - Every trace vanishes on commutators. Hence every inner derivation $a \mapsto xa - ax$ is invisible to every trace, and a derivation with a nonzero class in $HH^1 = \mathrm{Der}/\mathrm{Inn}$ is not inner.
   - The Goldman–Iwahori map $\theta(\sigma_i) = -\sigma_i^{-1}$ is an involutive automorphism of $H_n(R, q)$ for every commutative ring $R$ and unit $q$. For $n \geq 2$ and $q + q^{-1} \neq 0$ it is not inner, and it preserves the convention-D Markov trace only if $q^4 = -1$.
   - On a graph with edge variables in a group, a gauge change conjugates the holonomy of every closed walk, so every class function of that holonomy is unchanged. A flux change by an integer cochain $\omega$ and a central element $z$ multiplies the holonomy by $z^{\oint \omega}$, and a flux by a coboundary is a gauge change.
   - $H_n(q)$ is semisimple at $n = 2, 3, 4$. For $Q > 1$ and $X \leq 0$ every Schur element is nonzero at every rank. Every real $q > 1$ satisfies $q^k \neq 1$ for every $k \geq 2$.
   - $2^n$ eventually exceeds every polynomial, and $2^n \leq 3^{3n}$, so any step count $s(n)$ with $B \cdot s(n) \geq 3^{3n}$ is super-polynomial. This is the whole content of the physical-intractability statement in [the QOU conjecture](#conj:qou-p-neq-np): a device that must read all $3^{3n}$ leaves is slow. It does not show that a device must read them.
   - A word of $c$ matrices of size $d$ has its trace computed with $d \cdot c \cdot d^2$ multiplications, which is linear in $c$ for fixed $d$ ([bounded width](#prop:bounded-width-trace-cost)). So every family of braids on a bounded number of strands has an empty computational horizon, and any family that could witness the boundary conjecture needs unboundedly many strands. By the same count, unless $\mathrm{FP} = \#\mathrm{P}$, the hard families of \cite{jaeger1990} have unbounded braid index.
   - Every derivation kills every central idempotent, so it maps each Wedderburn block into itself. Automorphisms permute central idempotents, and inner automorphisms fix them ([block rigidity](#prop:block-rigidity)).
   - The six tractable points of \cite{jaeger1990} have orders $1, 2, 4, 4, 3, 3$, and none has order $6$ ([tractable orders](#prop:jvw-tractable-orders)).

2. *Provable now, by ordinary mathematics, and not yet formalised.*
   - *No Hochschild room at a semisimple point.* Where $H_n(K, q)$ is split semisimple it is separable, so $HH^i(H_n, M) = 0$ for every $i \geq 1$ and every bimodule $M$. Every derivation is then inner, and every automorphism is inner up to a permutation of Wedderburn blocks of equal size (Skolem–Noether). The outer automorphism group at such a point is finite, and $\theta$ acts on it as the transpose $\lambda \mapsto \lambda'$ of partitions.
   - *Semisimplicity at every rank,* by formalising the criterion of \cite{geckpfeiffer2000} on top of the Schur elements already computed.

3. *Open, tractable with new machinery, and not P versus NP.*
   - *Cellular automata modulo the inner ones.* For quantum cellular automata, finite-depth local circuits play the role of inner automorphisms, and the index of Gross, Nesme, Vogts and Werner is an invariant of the outer class: a circuit has index $1$ and a shift does not. Where Temperley–Lieb is a unitary fusion category, at suitable roots of unity, the index of the strand shift is defined, and computing it for the [$q$-Game of Life](#prop:q-game-of-life) is a tractable problem. At a non-torsion $q$ the fusion hypothesis fails, and even the definition is open.
   - *A correct non-CA theorem for intents.* The proved flux-versus-gauge statement does not use locality, since a one-edge flux already changes a holonomy. The intended theorem needs the class of cellular automata restricted to curvature-preserving local gates.
   - *Lower bounds in restricted models,* such as the skein-expansion algorithm itself, or read-once and bandwidth-limited models with a stated access rule. Once the model is fixed these are genuine theorems. They do not reach general Turing machines.

4. *Out of reach.* $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$ itself, and with it every step that turns hardness of counting into hardness of deciding. Exact evaluation of the Jones polynomial is $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard \cite{jaeger1990}, but $\#\mathrm{P}$-hardness does not separate decision classes: counting perfect matchings is $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard, while deciding whether one exists is in $\mathrm{P}$. An argument that worked uniformly at every non-torsion $q$ would also have to pass the relativization \cite{bakergillsolovay1975}, natural-proofs \cite{razborovrudich1997} and algebrization \cite{aaronsonwigderson2009} barriers. These steps are carried as named hypotheses with no instance, and are never discharged.

**Where earlier statements say more than they prove.** Read against their intent, five statements in this section go beyond their formal content.

- *"The inequality localizes strictly at the non-torsion aether."* Not supported. Exact evaluation is $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard at every $q$ outside a finite set, and that set excludes most roots of unity, so hardness does not single out non-torsion points. At a non-torsion point $H_n(q)$ is semisimple, so by item 2 it has no outer derivations, and a residue described by outer derivations can only live at roots of unity.
- *"The Kummer reduction vanishes exactly at the tractable set, $n \mid 6$."* The tractable values of $V_L(t)$ in \cite{jaeger1990} have orders $1, 2, 3, 4$, and for $t = q^{\pm 2}$ the orders of $q$ are $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8\}$ ([tractable orders](#prop:jvw-tractable-orders)). In neither convention is the set $\{n : n \mid 6\}$, so the claimed exact match does not hold.
- *"The residue is non-vanishing."* Formally this is the fact $-6 \neq 0$ about a defined constant. Its reading is "$-6 \neq 0$", not "$\chi(A) \neq 0$". The value $-6$ comes from a separate [chain](#ex:chi-minus-six-derivation) with its own hypotheses.
- *"$\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$ in the QOU."* Formally this says that a given cost function $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ is not polynomially bounded. No machine model is attached to the cost function, so the statement is a schema whose meaning depends on which cost function is supplied.
- *"$\mathrm{P} = \mathrm{NP}$ if and only if every horizon is empty."* $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$ is a statement about uniform algorithms on infinitely many inputs. The horizon is defined for one link at a time, with one fixed exponent $d$ and one evaluation procedure, the Markov trace on the skein tree. A non-empty horizon shows that this one procedure is slow on that family. It says nothing about other algorithms.

**Inner and outer automorphisms: why a fixed algebra cannot carry complexity.** An observer reads traces, and a trace is blind to every inner automorphism and every inner derivation. At a semisimple point the algebra has no other continuous freedom: outer derivations vanish, and outer automorphisms form a finite group of block permutations. So at a fixed rank the algebra the observer reads is rigid, and every algebraic invariant of it, including any residue, is decided by the parameter alone. The corpus proves the matching statement for residues: a nonzero polynomial obstruction vanishes at only finitely many parameter values, so its non-vanishing is the generic case. Complexity is not a quantity of this kind. It describes how cost grows along the tower $n \to \infty$, for algorithms that are uniform in $n$, and no invariant of a single $H_n(q)$ can encode it.

**Intent and cellular automata: what an observation returns decides the question.** The [$q$-Game of Life](#prop:q-game-of-life) and the [Reeb flow](#prop:reeb-turing-completeness-classical) make the QOU computationally universal, so whatever counts as polynomial time in the QOU contains ordinary $\mathrm{P}$, provided the simulation overhead is polynomial. The upper end depends on what one [observation](#def:observation) returns, and the corpus has not fixed this. [Intent](#rem:observation-intent-energy) charges a cost for each inserted crossing, so reading the trace of a $c$-crossing braid costs on the order of $c$ intents. If an observation returned the exact degree-$0$ value at that price, the QOU would evaluate the Jones polynomial exactly in polynomial time, and so solve every $\#\mathrm{P}$ problem and every $\mathrm{NP}$ problem. In that model "$\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$ in the QOU" is false. If instead an observation returns a sample, as under the Born rule, the QOU behaves like a quantum computer, and the question becomes one about quantum polynomial time and $\mathrm{NP}$, which is at least as open as the classical one. In both cases the QOU does not supply a separation. It supplies a choice of cost model, and that choice has to be stated before any separation can be.

The blocks that follow are to be read this way: the definitions are exact, the correspondence with horizons is an analogy, and each conjecture has the status given above. The open problems are collected in [the closing remark](#rem:p-vs-np-open-problems).
