Three hard problems reformulated inside a quantum observable universe (the $q$-Collatz halting question, the $P$ versus $NP$ boundary, and $q$-Navier–Stokes regularity) are the **same question asked of one length filtration** on the skein aether $\mathcal{S}(q) \cong \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]$. Each computation is a braid word $\beta \in H_n(q)$; its observed output is the Markov trace $\mathrm{tr}_M(\beta)$ (the Jones–Skein invariant an observer reads); and its complexity is graded by the crossing-number filtration $\ell$.

Write $\ell$ for that filtration and $\mathrm{obs}$ for the Markov-trace observation. The three problems differ only in what they ask of $\ell$ along an orbit:

| Reformulation | Question on the filtration $\ell$ |
| --- | --- |
| $q$-Collatz | does $\ell$ **descend to the normal form** (the orbit halts)? |
| $P$ versus $NP$ | is the observation reachable within a **polynomial** $\ell$-budget, rather than the naive $3^{c}$ resolution leaves? |
| $q$-Navier–Stokes | does $\ell$ stay **bounded** (no finite-time blow-up of the vortex-knot complexity)? |

The observation is the common, tractable shadow: because the Skein relations cut a single fixed ideal whose quotient is $\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]$, the Markov trace is computable, so "the observed output reaches the normal form's value" follows from halting for free. The difficulty is not algebraic; it does not live in an ideal. By the Reeb-flow Turing-completeness of the aether dynamics, the halting set is undecidable, hence not cut out by any ideal; the content sits entirely in the filtration $\ell$.

For $q$-Navier–Stokes the dictionary is concrete: a vortex tube of the brane fluid is a braid word evolving in time, its closure is a knotted vortex, its conserved helicity is the Markov-trace observation, and its crossing number is $\ell$. A non-increasing filtration (a monotone Lyapunov complexity, the categorical form of bounded enstrophy) rules out blow-up with bound the initial complexity, the abstract shadow of the $q$-Beale–Kato–Majda criterion.

**The legs are coupled rather than merely parallel.** Two consequences follow directly from the shared filtration. First, a counterexample on *either* the descent leg or the boundedness leg destroys the **shared** ingredient (a controlled descent on $\ell$) from which the two positive conclusions are jointly derived, so the walls do not fail independently. Second, within any **single** system whose normal form is periodic, the boundedness question and the descent question are coupled outright: an orbit whose filtration is unbounded cannot reach the normal form, because a halting orbit lands in the attractor's cycle and thereafter visits only finitely many states, hence has bounded complexity with an explicit bound depending on that orbit. The implication runs one way only (a bounded orbit may cycle forever without ever reaching the normal form), which is why descent is the harder of the two questions.

**This couples two questions asked of one instance; it does not identify a Navier--Stokes singularity with a $q$-Collatz divergence.** The two legs instantiate distinct dynamical systems: the $q$-Collatz system, whose complexity measure is an unspecified parameter, and the vortex-reduction system, on which unbounded complexity is unsatisfiable because that system is proved regular. Sharing the skein-observation abstraction means sharing a mathematical structure rather than a dynamical state.

The hypothesis the coupling needs is that the normal form be **periodic**, and the $q$-Collatz attractor satisfies it: the $q$-Collatz specification carries $\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}(\alpha)) = \alpha$ for the attractor $\alpha$, the $2$-cycle $\{q^{-1}, 1 + q^{-1}\}$ that is the $q$-lift of the classical $\{1, 2\}$ ([$q$-Collatz](#conj:q-collatz)). Periodicity at period $2$ holds immediately from $\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}(\alpha)) = \alpha$. The hypothesis is periodicity rather than the more obvious fixed-point condition $\mathcal{C}(\alpha) = \alpha$ because that condition is not merely stronger, it is **false here**: the same specification carries $\mathcal{C}(\alpha) \neq \alpha$. A transfer stated for fixed points would be vacuous on precisely the system it was written for.

**A descent measure is not a cheaper target than the conjecture.** Since descent implies halting, it is tempting to read "find a descent measure for $\ell$" as a weaker goal than "prove $q$-Collatz". It is not: on a forward-invariant cone the two are **equivalent**, because if every element halts then the hitting time $\mu(P) = \min\{k : \mathcal{C}_q^k(P) = \alpha\}$ is itself a strict $\mathbb{N}$-valued descent measure. So any search whose success criterion is the mere *existence* of a descent measure is searching for a restatement of the conjecture.

This sharpens rather than abandons the descent route. Its point is that an *explicit, independently motivated* $\mu$ (a valuation, a degree, a contact volume) can discharge the conjecture; a candidate is worth pursuing precisely for the properties the hitting time lacks, namely computability, a closed form, and monotonicity under the algebra of $[2]_q$ and $[3]_q$. The obvious candidate $v_{[2]}$ is already refuted, scope-(a): the $q$-odd element $P = -q^{-1} - 2$ has $v_{[2]}(P) = 0$ while its odd branch has $v_{[2]} = 2$, excluding that one candidate while leaving the class untouched.

The three legs share the skein aether and diverge only in the filtration question; none is settled here. Each remains an open conjecture in its own right: the $q$-Collatz descent measure, the faithfulness leg of the $P$ versus $NP$ boundary, and $q$-Navier–Stokes regularity are the respective open cores. What the shared structure supplies is a common language in which the tractable observation and the hard filtration question are cleanly separated.
