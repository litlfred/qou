The information-theoretic time ([information-theoretic time](#def:information-theoretic-time)) rests on a microscopic dynamical theory: the Julia-set iteration $f_q$. The thermodynamic limit of this iteration yields the continuous Reeb flow.

**Julia set dynamics as the microscopic theory.**
The iteration $f_q(z) = z^2 + c(q)$ on the exceptional divisor provides the microscopic dynamics of time. Each iterate $f_q^{\circ n}(z_0)$ constitutes one microstate of the temporal evolution. The continuous Reeb flow $\phi_t^q$ emerges as the macroscopic limit, obtained by averaging over $t_P(q)$ microscopic iterates per unit.

The Julia set $J(f_q)$ is the boundary separating escaping and bounded orbits. Its Hausdorff dimension $d_H(q)$ measures the complexity of the time evolution at parameter $q$. A higher $d_H$ indicates that the microscopic dynamics explores more phase space per operation, so macroscopic time carries more information per unit.
