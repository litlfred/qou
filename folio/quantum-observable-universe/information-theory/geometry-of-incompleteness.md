*Given a QOU whose aether is Turing-complete, the jet horizon $\mathcal{H} = V(\mathfrak{h})$ is non-empty: there exists a degree-0 datum whose truth is inaccessible. Equivalently, the degree-0 sections vanish on a non-trivial region, $\Gamma(\mathcal{H}, A_0) = 0$.*

A **degree-0 datum** is an observable answer read off the aether — a decision result, a particle identity, or the truth value of a self-referential sentence. It is **inaccessible** — behind the horizon — when it fails either route to access:

- **formally**, it is unprovable (Gödel incompleteness, forced in any aether expressive enough to encode arithmetic — every $q \neq 1$ aether, whose $q$-Toffoli gates are universal); or
- **physically**, extracting it is super-polynomial. For the boundary family a bandwidth-limited device must read the full $3^{3n}$-leaf surviving skein resolution tree, so by the Bekenstein bandwidth bound its step count grows faster than every polynomial (the physical-intractability theorem).

These are **two witnesses of one phenomenon** — the vanishing of degree-0 sections. Gödel supplies a formally-inaccessible degree-0 datum; the resolution-tree information bound supplies a physically-intractable one. Either establishes a non-empty horizon; the QOU exhibits both, so the horizon is doubly confirmed. In the framework's reading, this common cause is exactly what a black-hole event horizon *is*: the region across which degree-0 truth cannot pass while degree-$\geq 1$ data (witnesses, Hawking radiation) leaks. The event horizon is the geometry of incompleteness.

**Scope.** This is an existence statement about the aether's section structure: a Turing-complete QOU hosts an inaccessible degree-0 region via Gödel's incompleteness theorem and via the bandwidth bound on naive $3^{3n}$-leaf skein-tree inspection. Only the identification of this joint degree-0 vanishing with a physical horizon is an interpretive reading of the model.
