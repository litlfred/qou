The Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$ carries irreducible representations corresponding to Young diagrams. At a generic temperature $T_q$, a single representation dominates the partition function. At a critical temperature $T_q^{(c)}$, two representations contribute equally, introducing a non-analyticity into the partition function.

**The three phases.**

1. **Gas (symmetric channel, eigenvalue $q$).** At high $T_q$, each knot confines to a single $S^3$ fibre. The equation of state mirrors the quantum ideal gas law.
2. **Liquid (mixed channel).** At intermediate $T_q$, knots initiate Hopf-linking across fibres through the Hopf base $S^2$. This inter-fibre linking establishes structural correlations.
3. **Solid (antisymmetric channel, eigenvalue $-q^{-1}$).** At low $T_q$, inter-fibre links dominate. Knots form a rigid braid lattice, occupying the strict sign representation.

**Sublimation mechanism.**
The Hecke algebra supplies no mechanism. The three channels are complementary summands rather than an extension of one by another: at an aether parameter that is not a root of unity $H_n(q)$ is semisimple, so every short exact sequence of $H_n(q)$-modules splits and no extension class distinguishes an unstable liquid phase from a stable one ([the phase-transition boundary](#prop:phase-transition-boundary)). The correlation with odd crossing number recorded in [the composite crossing sublimation criteria](#rem:composite-crossing-sublimation-criteria) stands on its own arithmetic and carries no derivation from here.

**Triple point.**
All three phases coexist only when three Hecke representations contribute identically. This defines a rigid codimension-2 condition in the $(T_q, P_q)$ plane. It obeys the Gibbs phase rule.

**Classical theory comparison.**
Classical theory generates phase transitions artificially by appending *ad hoc* inter-particle potentials (van der Waals or Lennard-Jones) to the ideal gas. The QOU generates physical interactions through inter-fibre braid linking across the Hopf base of $S^3_q$. The $S^3$ topology supplies the binding potential via its boundary action. The Hecke representation theory dictates the exact discrete phase structure.
