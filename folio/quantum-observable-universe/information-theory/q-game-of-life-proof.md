*Proof.* 
The operator $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{q\text{-}GoL}}$ is built from $q$-Toffoli generators. By [Toffoli transfer matrix](#prop:toffoli-transfer-matrix), these act as elements of $H_3(q) \otimes \cdots \otimes H_3(q)$ on the tensor product of Reeb bundles $\bigotimes_i \mathcal{S}_{p_i}$. Each generator is *invertible* in $H_n(q)$, and it is invertibility — not unitarity — that this argument establishes. The eigenvalues of $\sigma_i$ are $q$ and $-q^{-1}$, of modulus $\neq 1$ whenever $q \neq 1$, so $\sigma_i$ is not unitary with respect to any inner product away from the classical limit; and the Markov trace form is not available as one, being degenerate under the production trace convention. Reversibility therefore rests on invertibility alone: the quadratic relation $\sigma_i^2 = (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i + 1$ gives the explicit inverse
$$
  \sigma_i^{-1} = \sigma_i - (q - q^{-1})\cdot 1 ,
$$
so every $\sigma_i$ is a unit and the composite is invertible. The generators are *not* involutions at generic $q$: $\sigma_i^{-1} = \sigma_i$ holds precisely when $q = q^{-1}$, i.e. in the classical limit $q \to 1$, which is where the $q$-Toffoli reduces to the ordinary Toffoli gate.

Any reversible computation is simulated by Toffoli gates \cite{bennett1973, toffoli1980, fredkin1982}. That Toffoli together with Hadamard is universal for *quantum* computation is the theorem of Shi and of Aharonov \cite{shi2002, aharonov2003}, stated for the standard Toffoli gate; its extension to $\mathrm{Toff}_q$ at $q \neq 1$ is not established here.

The bandwidth bound follows by counting. Each $\mathrm{Toff}_q$ in the $3$-strand Hecke algebra requires three elementary crossings, taking three Planck steps. One generation applies one Toffoli gate per neighbour triple. This yields the stated bound.

The well-definedness condition requires $P_{7,7}(q) > 0$ and $P_{7,6}(q) \neq 0$. In the canonical convention $z_D = q^3/(q^2+1)$ the diagonal is $P_{7,7}^{D}(q) = q^{3}(q^{4}+1)/(q^{2}+1)^{2}$; numerator and denominator are strictly positive for every $q > 0$, so the first clause holds throughout and places no constraint. The q-AND is $P_{7,6}^{D}(q) = -(q-1)(q+1)(q^{6}+q^{4}+2q^{2}+1)/\bigl(2q(q^{2}+1)^{2}\bigr)$; for $q > 0$ the factor $(q+1)$ is positive and the sextic has no positive real root, so the zero set is exactly $\{q = 1\}$ and the second clause holds on $q \neq 1$. The condition therefore holds on $q \neq 1$, which contains the aether parameter. $\square$

Two notes on what this does and does not use. The requirement is **non-vanishing** rather than positivity: the argument needs the q-AND not to degenerate, which is what invertibility of the tick requires and what the universality argument of [Toffoli q-AND](#rem:toffoli-q-and) turns on. Strict positivity would restrict to $0 < q < 1$ and exclude the aether parameter; it is a stronger condition than anything used here.

And the earlier argument read the region off three operator zeros — $q_\star = 1/\varphi^{2} \approx 0.3820$ for $P_{7,7}$, a phase reversal at $q_\Phi \approx 0.4656$, and $q = 1$ — concluding $q > 1$. All three are convention-$C$ quantities: under $z_D$ the first two do not exist and only $q = 1$ survives, now as the single zero of $P_{7,6}^{D}$. Substituting $z_D$ into a convention-$C$ closed form is not a valid migration and does not reproduce these forms; they are taken from [Toffoli LP-stability](#prop:toffoli-lp-stability) and [Toffoli q-AND](#rem:toffoli-q-and), where they are derived in $D$.

Throughout, the target-flip amplitude is written $P_{7,6}$ rather than $\Phi$: $\Phi$ named a published closed form the Toffoli construction does not produce under any indexing or convention, so it is retired as an object rather than re-derived in convention $D$ ([Toffoli transfer matrix](#prop:toffoli-transfer-matrix)). The criterion, the zero set and every number above are unchanged by the renaming.
