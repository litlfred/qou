Let $A$ be an associative algebra over a commutative ring $R$, and let $z \in A$ be a central idempotent: $z^2 = z$ and $za = az$ for every $a \in A$.

1. Every derivation $D$ of $A$ satisfies $D(z) = 0$, and hence $D(za) = z\,D(a)$ for every $a$. So $D$ maps the block $zA$ into itself.
2. Every algebra automorphism of $A$ sends central idempotents to central idempotents.
3. Every inner automorphism $x \mapsto u x u^{-1}$ fixes every central element. Hence an automorphism that moves a central element is not inner.

For a split semisimple Hecke algebra $H_n(K, q)$, the central primitive idempotents are the units of the Wedderburn blocks. So no derivation mixes blocks, and an automorphism can be visibly outer at the level of the centre only by permuting blocks. The Goldman–Iwahori involution $\theta(\sigma_i) = -\sigma_i^{-1}$ is of this kind: it exchanges the two one-dimensional characters $\sigma_i \mapsto q$ and $\sigma_i \mapsto -q^{-1}$.
