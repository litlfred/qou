The convention of [def:boltzmann-constant](#def:boltzmann-constant) is chosen as follows. The quantum ideal gas law (see [quantum ideal gas law](#prop:quantum-ideal-gas-law)) gives $P_q V_q = N T_q = N \hbar_q$ at leading order. The classical form is $PV = N k_B T$. 

In this framework, the temperature $T_q = \hbar_q$ ([quantum temperature](#def:quantum-temperature)) naturally carries units of energy. It is the Reeb tick rate, not a statistical parameter. Comparing the two forms, the choice consistent with $T_q = \hbar_q$ is:
$$
  N \, k_B \, T_q \;=\; N \, \hbar_q
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  k_B \;=\; 1.
$$

The constant can be set to unity because the temperature is defined in energy units. No separate conversion factor is required for a "degrees of freedom" temperature scale.

In SI units, physical temperature $T_{\mathrm{phys}}$ is measured in kelvin, and $k_B = 1.381 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K provides the energy conversion. At the comparison with measurement, the reading is $k_B T_{\mathrm{phys}} = \hbar_q$. The thermal energy per degree of freedom precisely equals the quantum Planck constant.
