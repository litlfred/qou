By induction on the word, applying $M_1, \dots, M_c$ to a vector $v$ one at a time returns $(M_1 \cdots M_c)v$ and uses $c$ matrix–vector products of $d^2$ multiplications each. Applied to the standard basis vector $e_i$, the $i$-th entry of the result is the diagonal entry $(M_1 \cdots M_c)_{ii}$, so summing over the $d$ basis vectors gives the trace with $d \cdot c \cdot d^2$ multiplications. Since $d \cdot c \cdot d^2 = d^3 \cdot c^1$, the polynomial $d^3 c$ is never exceeded. $\square$
