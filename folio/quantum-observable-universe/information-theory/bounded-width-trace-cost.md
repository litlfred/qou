Let $K$ be a commutative semiring and let $M_1, \dots, M_c$ be $d \times d$ matrices over $K$. Applying the word to each standard basis vector, right to left, and summing the diagonal entries computes $\mathrm{tr}(M_1 \cdots M_c)$ with $d \cdot c \cdot d^2$ multiplications in $K$. For fixed $d$ this count is at most $d^3 c$, so it is not super-polynomial in $c$.

Consequently a braid word on a fixed number $n$ of strands, acting through a representation of fixed dimension (the regular representation of $H_n$ has dimension $n!$), has a Markov trace computable with $O(c)$ ring operations, with no use of the $3^c$ leaves of the skein resolution tree. Every family of braids with bounded strand number therefore has an empty [computational horizon](#def:computational-horizon), and any family with a non-empty computational horizon must have unboundedly many strands. The count is in ring operations; the growth of coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]$, which is polynomial in $c$, is not counted here.
