$1$ has order $1$ and $-1$ has order $2$. Since $i^2 = -1$ and $i^4 = 1$, $i$ is a primitive $4$th root of unity, and $-i = i^3$ is one too because $\gcd(3, 4) = 1$. The number $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ is a primitive cube root of unity, and so is $\omega^{-1}$. A primitive root of unity has a unique order, so none of the six points is a primitive $6$th root. Finally $4 \nmid 6$. $\square$
