The following problems are open. They are listed with what is known and what each would and would not settle. Only the first is $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$; the rest are ordinary mathematics that the section's statements depend on.

1. **The counting-to-decision transfer.** Exact evaluation of the Jones polynomial is $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard \cite{jaeger1990}, and $\mathrm{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{P}^{\#\mathrm{P}}$ gives $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP} \Rightarrow \mathrm{FP} \neq \#\mathrm{P}$. The converse direction is what every QOU formulation would need, and it is $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$ itself. A uniform argument over all non-torsion $q$ must pass the relativization \cite{bakergillsolovay1975}, natural-proofs \cite{razborovrudich1997} and algebrization \cite{aaronsonwigderson2009} barriers. Out of reach.

2. **What an observation returns.** The cost model of the QOU is not yet fixed by [the definition of observation](#def:observation). If an observation of a $c$-crossing braid returns the exact degree-$0$ value at a cost linear in $c$, as [intent](#rem:observation-intent-energy) suggests, then QOU polynomial time contains $\#\mathrm{P}$ and hence $\mathrm{NP}$, and "$\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}$ in the QOU" is false. If it returns a sample, the question is one about quantum polynomial time. Choosing between these is a definition, not a theorem, and it has to be made before any QOU-scoped separation can be stated.

3. **Polynomial overhead of universality.** The [$q$-Game of Life](#prop:q-game-of-life) is universal for reversible computation, and the [Reeb flow](#prop:reeb-turing-completeness-classical) simulates Turing machines. For "QOU polynomial time contains $\mathrm{P}$" one needs the simulation of a Turing machine to have polynomial overhead in the QOU cost model of item 2. Not yet proved.

4. **Outer derivations at roots of unity.** By [block rigidity](#prop:block-rigidity), no derivation mixes the blocks of a split semisimple $H_n(K, q)$, and by separability $HH^{i} = 0$ for $i \geq 1$ there. At a root of unity $H_n(\zeta)$ can be non-semisimple and $HH^1$ can be nonzero. Computing $\dim HH^1(H_n(\zeta))$ for small $n$ and $\zeta$ is finite linear algebra and tractable. It would locate where an outer residue can live. It cannot carry complexity, because it is an invariant of one algebra of fixed rank.

5. **Separability in Lean.** Formalise that a split semisimple algebra over a field has $HH^i(A, M) = 0$ for $i \geq 1$, and the Skolem–Noether description of its automorphisms as inner automorphisms composed with permutations of equal-size blocks. Formalise semisimplicity of $H_n(q)$ at every rank via \cite{geckpfeiffer2000} on top of the computed Schur elements. Standard and tractable.

6. **Trace-preserving outer automorphisms.** Classify the automorphisms of $H_n(K, q)$ that preserve the Markov trace. By item 5 they permute blocks of equal size and equal trace weight. The Goldman–Iwahori involution preserves no Markov trace with $q^4 \neq -1$. Tractable.

7. **Cellular automata modulo inner ones.** For quantum cellular automata, finite-depth local circuits play the role of inner automorphisms, and the index of Gross, Nesme, Vogts and Werner is an invariant of the outer class. Where Temperley–Lieb is a unitary fusion category, at suitable roots of unity, the index of the strand shift of the $q$-Game of Life is defined and can be computed. At a non-torsion $q$ the fusion hypothesis fails and the index is not even defined. Tractable at roots of unity; open as a definition elsewhere.

8. **A correct non-CA theorem for intents.** The gauge-versus-flux distinction for edge variables is proved and does not use locality, since a one-edge flux already changes a holonomy. The intended statement, that a flux-threading intent is not a cellular automaton, needs the automata restricted to curvature-preserving local gates. Tractable once that class is defined.

9. **Lower bounds in restricted models.** The skein-expansion algorithm reads $3^c$ leaves, and [bounded width](#prop:bounded-width-trace-cost) shows other algorithms need not. Lower bounds against a fixed model, such as read-once evaluation or a bandwidth-limited device with a stated access rule, are genuine theorems once the model is fixed. They do not reach general Turing machines.
