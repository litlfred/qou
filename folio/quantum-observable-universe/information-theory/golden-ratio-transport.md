The golden ratio $\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ appears twice in the Descartes elliptic curve $\mathcal{E}_q$ ([golden ratio degeneration](#rem:golden-ratio-degeneration)). It appears as a **metric property** (the conformal modulus of the elliptic fiber at $q = q_0$) and as an **algebraic property** (the root of the degeneration polynomial $s^2 + s - 1 = 0$ at $q_{\mathrm{gold}} = 1/\varphi^2$). Parallel transport along the $q$-connection ([categorical $q$-connection](#def:categorical-q-connection)) reveals these are the same $\varphi$, viewed from opposite ends of a geodesic in aether parameter space.

**Setup.**
The elliptic fiber $\mathcal{E}_q$ at aether parameter $q$ is a complex torus $\mathbb{C}/\Lambda(q)$. The period lattice $\Lambda(q)$ is generated by $1$ and $\tau(q) \in \mathbb{H}$. The modular parameter $\tau(q)$ is fixed by the $j$-invariant $j(q) = j(\tau)$. <!-- literal-source: elliptic integral real period of Weierstrass curve E_{q_0} --> At $q = q_0$, $j = 129.9$ and the real period is $\omega \approx 7.5565$ ([computation data](#prop:descartes-elliptic-curve)).

Consider a parallelogram $\Pi(q)$ in the period lattice with diagonal-to-side ratio $\varphi$. This fundamental domain encodes the conformal structure of the chiral fiber.

**Transport equations.**
The $q$-connection $\nabla_q = q^{-1}\nabla + (1 - q^{-1})(\iota_\xi\nabla) \varTheta_A$ depends continuously on $q$. As $q$ decreases from $q_0$ to $q_{\mathrm{gold}} \approx 0.382$, the connection weight shifts:

| $q$ | $q^{-1}$ (spatial weight) | $1 - q^{-1}$ (Reeb weight) |
|-----|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| $q_0$ <!-- literal-source: closed-form evaluations q_0^{-1} = 0.9009 and 1 - q_0^{-1} = 0.0991 --> | $0.9009$ | $+0.0991$ |
| $1.000$ | $1.000$ | $0$ (classical) |
| $1/\varphi^2 \approx 0.382$ | $\varphi^2 \approx 2.618$ | $-\varphi \approx -1.618$ |

Below $q = 1$, the Reeb term flips sign. It actively unwinds internal spin rather than accumulating it.

**Degeneration.**
As $q \to q_{\mathrm{gold}}$, the $j$-invariant diverges, forcing $\mathrm{Im}(\tau) \to \infty$. The period lattice stretches without bound along one generator. The parallelogram degenerates:

$$
  \Pi(q) \xrightarrow{q \to q_{\mathrm{gold}}}
   \text{infinite strip}
  \xrightarrow{ \text{pinch}}
   \text{node}.
$$

The golden-ratio diagonal is destroyed as the torus collapses into a nodal rational curve.

**The identity.**
The golden ratio transitions from a length ratio in $\Pi(q_0)$ to the root of $(s^2 + s - 1)$, which annihilates the Weierstrass coefficient $r(q)$. The polynomial $s^2 + s - 1$ is the minimal polynomial of $1/\varphi$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. It appears in $r(q)$ because Bring's surface is defined by $\sum x_i = \sum x_i^2 = \sum x_i^3 = 0$ in $\mathbb{CP}^4$. Its $S_5$ symmetry acts through fifth roots of unity $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/5}$ satisfying $\varphi = 2\cos(\pi/5)$.

The shape information of the parallelogram and the algebraic degeneration condition represent the same number. Parallel transport from $q_0$ to $q_{\mathrm{gold}}$ transforms the geometric length into the algebraic instability threshold.

**Reciprocal asymmetry.**
The inversion $q \to 1/q$ maps $z = \sqrt{q} - 1/\sqrt{q}$ to $-z$. At $q_{\mathrm{gold}}$, $z = -1$ and the chiral cubic factors as $X(X^2+1) = 0$. At $q_{\mathrm{gold}}^{-1} = \varphi^2$, $z = +1$ and the cubic $X^3 + 2X^2 + 3X + 4 = 0$ is irreducible. The fiber remains smooth with $\Delta = -200$ and $j = 160$. Bring's surface breaks the $q \leftrightarrow 1/q$ symmetry. Although $U_q(\mathfrak{g}) \cong U_{q^{-1}}(\mathfrak{g})$, the elliptic curves $\mathcal{E}_q \not\cong E_{1/q}$.

**Information transition.**
The parallelogram $\Pi(q)$ is a fundamental domain containing exactly one period of chiral fiber information. Transporting it to $q_{\mathrm{gold}}$ forces the information capacity of the fiber to diverge along one axis and collapse along the other. At the node, the distributed loop information concentrates at a singularity. The golden ratio marks the phase transition where chiral fiber information collapses from a distributed torus to a localized node.
