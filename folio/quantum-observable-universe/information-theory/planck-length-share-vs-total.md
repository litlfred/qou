The symbol $\ell_P$ appears twice in this chapter with two different referents, and
read carelessly the two look like a disagreement by a factor of $t_P \approx 10.1$.
They are not in disagreement. One is a share and the other is the total.

**The share.** At a point $p$ of the brane,
[the Planck information unit](#sec:planck-info-unit) sets the minimal quantisable size
of an information unit and scales it as $\ell_P^{\mathrm{step}} \sim \hbar_q$. What is
being named is the fraction of one Reeb circuit traversed by a single application of
the connection: the per-step share. The relation is a proportionality, not an
equality, and $\hbar_q$ enters it as a weight: it is the Reeb coefficient of the
categorical Dirac operator, which is exactly what
[the Reeb coefficient identity](#prop:reeb-coefficient-is-hbar-q) establishes.

**The total.** After one Planck step, [the discrete Planck step](#rem:discrete-planck-step)
records the displacement as

$$
  \ell_P(q) \;=\; \hbar_q \cdot t_P \;=\; \bigl(1-q^{-1}\bigr)\cdot\frac{q}{q-1} \;=\; 1 .
$$

What is being named here is the normalised total: one full Reeb circuit, an angular
displacement carrying no length scale, set to unity so that the transcendental period
$2\pi$ never enters and the construction survives over a finite field.

## Consistency of per-channel share and total scale

The Planck time is defined as the reciprocal of the Reeb share, $t_P = 1/\hbar_q$, so
the product of share and reciprocal is the circuit by construction. Writing
$\hbar_q \approx 0.0991$ and $\ell_P \equiv 1$ side by side asserts that a step covers
about a tenth of a circuit and that the circuit is one unit: two compatible
statements about the same flow rather than two values for one quantity.

The defect the pairing exposes is notational. A single glyph is doing duty for a
per-step fraction and for a normalised whole, and the notation register admits no
symbol reuse of that kind. The two uses are separated by writing
$\ell_P^{\mathrm{step}}$ for the share and reserving $\ell_P(q)$ for the total.

A third symbol for the same share circulates in the fluid-dynamics chapter, where
$\ell_q = (q-1)/q$ is written for what is here $\ell_P^{\mathrm{step}}$. Three names
for one quantity is one more collision of the same kind; unifying them is a separate
edit and is not made here.
