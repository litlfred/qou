The symbol $\ell_P$ appears twice in this chapter with two different referents, and
read carelessly the two look like a disagreement by a factor of $t_P \approx 10.1$.
They are not in disagreement. One is a share and the other is the total.

**The share.** At a point $p$ of the brane,
[the Planck information unit](#sec:planck-info-unit) sets the minimal quantisable grain
of an information unit and scales it with $\hbar_q = 1 - q^{-1}$. What is
being named is the fraction of one Reeb circuit traversed by a single application of
the connection: the per-step share. The scaling carries $\hbar_q$ as a weight: it is
the Reeb coefficient of the categorical Dirac operator, which is what
[the Reeb coefficient identity](#prop:reeb-coefficient-is-hbar-q) establishes.

**The total.** After one Planck step, the displacement is

$$
  \ell_P(q) \;=\; \hbar_q \cdot t_P \;=\; \bigl(1-q^{-1}\bigr)\cdot\frac{q}{q-1} \;=\; 1 .
$$

What is being named here is the normalised total: one full Reeb circuit, an angular
displacement carrying no length scale, set to unity so that the transcendental period
$2\pi$ never enters and the construction survives over a finite field.

## Why the two are consistent

The Planck time is defined as the reciprocal of the Reeb share, $t_P = 1/\hbar_q$, so
the product of share and reciprocal is the circuit by construction. Writing
$\hbar_q$ for the share and $\ell_P(q) = 1$ for the circuit states that each step covers
the fraction $\hbar_q$ of a single unit-normalised Reeb period.

The notation register keeps one symbol per mathematical object by writing
$\hbar_q = 1 - q^{-1} = (q-1)/q$ directly for the per-step share across both information
theory and brane fluid dynamics (where kinematic viscosity is $\nu_q = \hbar_q / 2$),
while reserving $\ell_P(q) = \hbar_q \cdot t_P = 1$ for the normalised full circuit.
