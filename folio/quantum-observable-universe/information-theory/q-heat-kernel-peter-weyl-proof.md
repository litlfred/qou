> **Scope note (2026-11-15).** Only part of this is derived. The factor $\hbar_q^2 = (1-q^{-1})^2$ is derived: it is the coefficient of $\Delta_\xi$ in the expansion of rem:q-laplacian-expansion. The eigenvalue of $\Delta_\xi$ on a Wedderburn block is not derived. The step "the Casimir value equals $\sum [c][c+1]$ (Jucys--Murphy, $q$-deformed by Ram)" has no reference and no computation, and nothing shows that the Reeb-spin Laplacian $\Delta_\xi$ acts through a Jucys--Murphy central element at all. The companion Lean proves identities about the formula, not that it is an eigenvalue. Note also that with symmetric integers, $\sum_k [c_k][c_k+1]$ is a Laurent polynomial in the Jucys--Murphy eigenvalues $q^{2c_k}$, so it is the eigenvalue of a central element of $H_n(q)$; with Jackson integers it is not, because it needs $q^{c_k}$. That central element is now explicit: $C_n = q\sum_k L_k + q^{-1}\sum_k L_k^{-1} - (n+1)(q+q^{-1})$ acts on every Jucys--Murphy weight vector of shape $\lambda$ by $(q-q^{-1})^2\sum_{\square\in\lambda}[c][c+1]$ (`QOU.SchurWeyl.contentCasimir_smul_of_weight`, sorry-free, conditional on the seminormal form). It cannot be identified with $\Delta_\xi$ as defined in rem:q-laplacian-expansion: $\Delta_\xi$ contains no $q$, so its eigenvalues form one fixed countable set, while $\sum[c][c+1]$ varies continuously with $q$ (e.g. $(q^2+1)/q$ for $\lambda=(2,2)$). The formula therefore cannot hold for all $q$ in any interval; see `docs/audits/2026-11-15-q-laplacian-eigenvalue-derivation.md`. The binding code (`hecke_core_legacy.q_laplacian_eigenvalue`) uses the bare sum with symmetric integers and no $\hbar_q^2$, so it matches neither this formula nor any derivation. Measured at the proton $q_0$, the deuteron term of `_unified_iterative_correction_exact(1, 1)` is $-17.69$ MeV with the code's $\mu_\lambda$, $-1302.39$ MeV with $\hbar_q^2$ and symmetric integers, and $-1358.06$ MeV with this formula as written. All three have the wrong sign against AME $+2.2246$ MeV.

*Proof.* Schur's lemma on the irreducible Wedderburn block $M_{d_\lambda(q)}$ forces $\Delta_\xi$ to act by a scalar. We compute this scalar by evaluating the $q$-Casimir on a highest-weight vector. For $H_n(q)$, the Casimir value equals the sum of $[c(\square)]_q[c(\square) + 1]_q$ over cells $\square \in \lambda$ (a classical Jucys--Murphy result, $q$-deformed by Ram). Multiplying by $\hbar_q^2 = (1 - q^{-1})^2$ yields $\mu_\lambda(q)$.

The content-Casimir formula decomposes via the Jackson-integer shift identity (`jacksonInt_mul_succ` in `q-heat-kernel-peter-weyl.lean`):
$$[c]_q \cdot [c+1]_q = q \cdot [c]_q^2 + [c]_q,$$
yielding the equivalent form $\mu_\lambda(q) = \hbar_q^2 \bigl(q \sum_{{\square \in \lambda}} [c(\square)]_q^2 + \sum_{{\square \in \lambda}} [c(\square)]_q\bigr)$. The $q \cdot [c]_q^2$ term connects to the bar-symmetrised Jucys--Murphy sum $\Theta_n = \sum_k (L_k + L_k^{-1})$ (defined in `QJucysMurphy.lean`) whose block eigenvalue is $\sum_{c \in \lambda} (q^{2c} + q^{-2c})$, via the per-cell identity $q^{2c} + q^{-2c} = (q^c - q^{-c})^2 + 2$. The linear term $\sum [c]_q$ is the $q$-deformed first content moment, determined by $\lambda$ alone. $\square$
