The energy $\mathfrak{E} = \mathfrak{m} \cdot c_q^2$ ([energy](#def:mass-energy)) and the crossing energy $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}}$ ([crossing energy](#def:crossing-energy)) are different quantities. No identity $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}} = \mathfrak{E}$ holds for all braids.

**Counterexample.** The mass of a braid class is the framing-corrected trace $(1+q^2)^n q^{-n-2w(\beta)} \int_A \rho(\beta)$ ([mass identity](#def:mass-identity)). Take the trivial braid on $n$ strands and a normalised Markov trace, $\int_A \rho(1) = 1$. Then $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}} = 0$, because there are no crossings, while $\mathfrak{m} = (1+q^2)^n q^{-n} \neq 0$, so $\mathfrak{E} \neq 0$.

**Distinction between crossing energy and trace mass.** The crossing energy adds a weight $q$ or $-q^{-1}$ per crossing. The mass comes from a trace, which is not additive over crossings. The quantum trace \cite{reshetikhin1991} is not a sum of per-crossing weights. On two strands of the fundamental representation of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, with $K = \mathrm{diag}(q, q^{-1})$ and the braiding $\check{R}$ normalised to eigenvalue $q$ on the symmetric square, $\mathrm{Tr}((K \otimes K)\check{R}) = q(q^2+1)$ and $\mathrm{Tr}((K \otimes K)\check{R}^2) = (q^2+1)(q^4+1)/q^2$.

Whether some energy is both additive over crossings and determined by the framed trace is open. One candidate is the logarithm of the framed trace, whose framing factor contributes the crossing-additive term $-2w(\beta)\log q$.
