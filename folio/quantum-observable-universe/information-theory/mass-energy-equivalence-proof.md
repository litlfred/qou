The rest energy $\mathfrak{E} = \mathfrak{m} \cdot c_q^2$ ([mass--energy equivalence](#prop:mass-energy)) and the crossing energy $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}}$ ([crossing energy](#def:crossing-energy)) are distinct quantities. No identity $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}} = \mathfrak{E}$ holds across all braids.

**Counterexample.** The mass of a braid class is the framing-corrected trace $(1+q^2)^n q^{-n-2w(\beta)} \int_A \rho(\beta)$ ([mass identity](#def:mass-identity)). Take the trivial braid on $n$ strands and a normalised Markov trace, $\int_A \rho(1) = 1$. Then $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{cross}} = 0$ because there are no crossings, whereas $\mathfrak{m} = (1+q^2)^n q^{-n} \neq 0$, so the Legendre rest energy $\mathfrak{E} = \mathfrak{m}\,c_q^2$ is nonzero.

**Why the two differ.** The crossing energy sums $q$ or $-q^{-1}$ additively over individual crossings prior to tracing. By contrast, $\mathfrak{E}$ arises from the Legendre transform ([categorical Hamiltonian](#def:categorical-hamiltonian)) applied to a traced holonomy observable, and the quantum trace \cite{reshetikhin1991} is not additive over crossings. On two strands of the fundamental representation of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, with $K = \mathrm{diag}(q, q^{-1})$ and the braiding $\check{R}$ normalised to eigenvalue $q$ on the symmetric square, $\mathrm{Tr}((K \otimes K)\check{R}) = q(q^2+1)$ and $\mathrm{Tr}((K \otimes K)\check{R}^2) = (q^2+1)(q^4+1)/q^2$.

Whether some auxiliary functional is simultaneously additive over crossings and determined by the framed trace remains open; one candidate is the logarithm of the framed trace, whose framing factor contributes the crossing-additive term $-2w(\beta)\log q$.
