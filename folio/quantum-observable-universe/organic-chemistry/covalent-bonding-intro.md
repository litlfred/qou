Where metallic bonding shares residual sub-links across an entire lattice, molecular chemistry localises the sharing between two atoms. A **covalent bond** of order $k$ makes this precise as a shared sub-Hopf link of $k$ trefoils simultaneously linked to one open site in each partner. Single, double, and triple bonds correspond to $k = 1, 2, 3$. Each increment in bond order adds one shared trefoil, so higher bond order registers as additional rigidity. The explicit crossing-energy formula is stated in the covalent-bond definition.

The section closes by partitioning carbon's electron cloud into a closed $1s^2$ pair and a residual sub-link of size four. This four-site residual link is exactly half-filling of the second shell's capacity. This places carbon at the maximally covalent midpoint of the metallic-character spectrum.
