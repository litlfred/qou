This section instantiates the chain–ring machinery at $N = 6$. The **linear $\mathrm{C}_6$ Hopf chain** assembles six carbons with a $6 \times 6$ tridiagonal linking matrix. Interior carbons consume two of their four sites for the backbone. Endpoint carbons carry three dangling links each. These soft modes make linear $\mathrm{C}_6$ flexible.

**Benzene** is the closed counterpart: six carbons threaded by $T(2,6)$, whose two components are the two delocalised $\pi$ electrons. Its reduced Alexander polynomial $q^2 + 1 + q^{-2}$ replaces six pairwise Hopf weights with a single threading invariant. This explains bond-order equalisation and the $\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_6$ formula. 

Comparing the two structures, the threaded ring binds one more atom at reduced Alexander weight $\approx 3$ versus the chain's $5$. The chain persists because the transition is discontinuous. The geometric reconfiguration sets an activation barrier. The estimate $\Delta E \approx 2 \cdot |q + q^{-1}|_{\mathrm{mol}} \approx 193\text{--}386\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$ is **consistent with** the experimental $200\text{--}400\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$ window — an order-of-magnitude check, not a sharp parameter-free prediction: both the estimate and the window span a factor of two, and the molecular energy scale $|\cdot|_{\mathrm{mol}}$ is an input here, not derived from the substrate calibration. The $1/2$ threading factor resolves the pairwise picture's overshoot.
