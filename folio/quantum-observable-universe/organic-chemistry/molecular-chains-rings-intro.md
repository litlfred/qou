Extended molecular structures arise by composing covalent bonds along a backbone. A **[molecular Hopf chain](#def:molecular-hopf-chain)** of length $N$ composes $N - 1$ covalent bonds along a backbone, giving a tridiagonal linking matrix with free endpoint sites. Passing to closed rings replaces the naïve picture — $N$ pairwise Hopf links — with a single non-local invariant: the **[threading link](#def:threading-link)** $T(2,N)$. Its braid word winds twice through every atom, and its reduced Alexander polynomial at $t = 1$, the linking number $N/2$, is half the pairwise linking number $N$. A **[molecular Hopf ring](#def:molecular-hopf-ring)** binds atoms globally by the threading link rather than locally by Hopf links.

The remaining blocks read off the dynamical consequences. Open chains behave as topological springs. Ring closure is a topological phase transition. The transition state is the barrier where pairwise bonds dissolve before the threading link forms. The same mechanism explains ring super-stability.
