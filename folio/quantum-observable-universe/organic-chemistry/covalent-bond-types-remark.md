| Bond order $k$ | Chemical name | Shared trefoils | Linking sites consumed |
|:-:|---|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | Single bond | 1 | 1 per atom |
| 2 | Double bond | 2 | 2 per atom |
| 3 | Triple bond | 3 | 3 per atom |

In the crossing energy $E_{\mathrm{bond}}(k)$, the first term accounts for $k$ individual Hopf links, and the second for inter-link interactions within the shared sub-link. Higher bond order increases both binding strength and rigidity: a triple bond is stronger and stiffer than a single bond because the three shared trefoils form a closed sub-link that resists Reidemeister moves.
