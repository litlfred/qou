The Iwahori–Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$ admits a quantum Langlands dual $H_n(q^{-1})$ obtained by inverting the deformation parameter, in the sense of the deformed-$W$-algebra duality $W_{q,t}(\mathfrak{g}) = W_{t,q}({}^L\mathfrak{g})$ \cite{frenkel-reshetikhin1998}. Under Cartan-folding, this duality satisfies:

1. It is distinct from the bar involution $\bar q = q^{-1}$. Langlands duality acts on the Hecke algebra structure at fixed generators, whereas the bar involution acts on the generator action at fixed root data.
2. It coincides with the reverse-limit $q \to \infty$ for non-classical specialisations.
3. For non-simply-laced types, it yields a genuinely distinct dual algebra.

For Type $A_{n-1}$, the Langlands dual is self-dual at the algebra level. The parameter inversion $q \to q^{-1}$ produces a non-trivial $\mathbb{Z}/2$ duality. The $H_n(q)$ algebra possesses a natural Langlands-dual perspective at $q^{-1}$ that connects to the reverse-limit at $q \to \infty$ and to Type-B folding for non-simply-laced extensions. It supplies a categorical origin for the symmetric/antisymmetric mass-tower decomposition, equating matter with Langlands-dual antimatter.

**What is proved for Type $A_{n-1}$, for every $n$.** Present $H_n(q)$ over a commutative ring in which $q$ is a unit by generators $T_1, \dots, T_{n-1}$ with $(T_i - q)(T_i + 1) = 0$, the braid relations and far commutation. Two algebra isomorphisms $H_n(q^{-1}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H_n(q)$, both linear over the ground ring, exist for every $n$:

$$
  T_i \mapsto -q^{-1} T_i
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  T_i \mapsto T_i^{-1}.
$$

Their composite is the automorphism $\theta(T_i) = (q - 1) - T_i$ of $H_n(q)$, which is an involution. It exchanges the index character $T_i \mapsto q$ with the sign character $T_i \mapsto -1$, so for $n \ge 2$ and $q \neq -1$ it is not the identity: this is the non-trivial $\mathbb{Z}/2$ of the statement above. The bar involution sends $q$ to $q^{-1}$ on scalars, and no map linear over the ground ring can do that unless $q = q^{-1}$, which separates it from the duality (item 1). In the balanced normalisation $\sigma_i^2 = (q - q^{-1})\sigma_i + 1$ the same duality is $\sigma_i \mapsto -\sigma_i$. Items 2 and 3 remain open.
