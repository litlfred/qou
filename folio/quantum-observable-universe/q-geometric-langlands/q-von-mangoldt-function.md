Measuring the arithmetic weight of fundamental defects in a quantum observable universe deforms the classical von Mangoldt function.

The :defterm[$q$-von Mangoldt function]{#q-von-mangoldt-function} is defined over the maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m}$ of the coordinate ring $\mathcal{O}_q(\mathcal{B})$. Let $N(\mathfrak{m})$ denote the norm of $\mathfrak{m}$, given by the cardinality of its residue field, and set:

$$
\Lambda_q(\mathfrak{m}) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\hbar_q} - 1}{\hbar_q} & \text{if } \mathfrak{m} \text{ is a fundamental defect}, \\[6pt]
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

A maximal ideal constitutes a fundamental defect if it lies over a prime ideal of the center of $A$. The fraction $\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\hbar_q} - 1}{\hbar_q}$ deforms the classical logarithm. As $q \to 1$, $\hbar_q \to 0$, and L'Hôpital's rule recovers $\Lambda_q(\mathfrak{m}) \to \log N(\mathfrak{m}) = \Lambda(\mathfrak{m})$, restoring the classical von Mangoldt function.
