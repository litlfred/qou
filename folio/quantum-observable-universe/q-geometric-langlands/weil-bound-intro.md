Throughout this section $q = |\mathbb{F}_q|$ denotes the cardinality of a finite field—a prime power, not the real deformation parameter of the archimedean chapters—so no continuous limit $q \to 1$ is taken here.

For a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, Weil (1948) proved that the Frobenius eigenvalues $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{2g}$ on $H^1$ satisfy $|\alpha_i| = q^{1/2}$. Taking Weil's two structural inputs as named hypotheses—the functional-equation pairing $\alpha \mapsto q/\alpha$ and the Castelnuovo--Severi inequality $|\alpha|^2 \le q$—the Hasse--Weil theorem below derives equality $|\alpha_i| = q^{1/2}$, and its spectral-half-line corollary rewrites that equality as $\mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = 1/2$ under $\alpha_i = q^{\rho_i}$ (with the functional equation alone forcing $\sum_{i=1}^{2g} \mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = g$).

For Bring's genus-four curve $\mathcal{B}$, the Jacobian is isogenous to $E^4$ for the elliptic curve $E : y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - x - 2$ (Cremona $50\mathrm{a}1$). At every prime $p \nmid 10$ of good reduction, the local curve zeta function has numerator $(1 - a_p T + pT^2)^4$ with $a_p = p + 1 - |E(\mathbb{F}_p)|$, reducing $|\alpha_i| = p^{1/2}$ to the integer Hasse inequality $a_p^2 \le 4p$, verified directly for $7 \le p \le 41$ below. Multiplying the local curve zeta functions over good primes produces $\zeta(s)\zeta(s - 1)/L(E, s)^4$, in which the Frobenius eigenvalues build $L(E, s)$ while $\zeta(s)$ enters only as the $H^0$ factor with no Frobenius eigenvalues beyond $1$; accordingly, these finite-field curve bounds carry no implication for the classical Riemann zeta function.
