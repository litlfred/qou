By Tannaka--Krein reconstruction, the fibre functor $\tau$ on $\mathbf{Rep}(\check{G}_q)$ recovers the quantum group $\check{G}_q$ as $\mathrm{Aut}^\otimes(\tau)$. A $\check{G}_q$-local system $\mathcal{L}_q$ is equivalent to a tensor functor $\mathbf{Rep}(\check{G}_q) \to \mathrm{Vect}_X$. This functor is uniquely determined by the fibre data $\{V_\lambda^{(q)}(\mathcal{L}_q)_x\}$ over all coweights $\lambda$ and points $x$.

The Hecke eigensheaf property provides exactly this data. For each $(\lambda, x)$, the eigenvalue isomorphism $\mathbf{T}_{x,\lambda}^{(q)}(\mathcal{M}) \simeq \mathcal{M} \otimes V_\lambda^{(q)}(\mathcal{L}_q)_x$ extracts the fibre $V_\lambda^{(q)}(\mathcal{L}_q)_x$ as a multiplicity space.

The tensor compatibility of the Hecke isomorphisms ensures that the assignment $V_\lambda^{(q)} \mapsto V_\lambda^{(q)}(\mathcal{L}_q)_x$ is a tensor functor. It thus defines a point of $\mathrm{LocSys}_{\check{G}_q}(X)$. The rigidity of $\tau$ ([fibre-functor rigidity](#rem:fibre-functor-rigidity)) guarantees that any two tensor functors with isomorphic fibre data are uniquely isomorphic. $\square$
