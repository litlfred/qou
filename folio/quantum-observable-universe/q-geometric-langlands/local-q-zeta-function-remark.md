The geometric Riemann hypothesis constrains the absolute values of the
Frobenius eigenvalues of Bring's curve; the [local $q$-zeta function](#def:local-q-zeta-function)
packages those eigenvalues as a single generating function. Because $g = 4$ for Bring's curve, the polynomial $P(t)$ has degree $2g = 8$. The roots $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_8$ are the fundamental eigenvalues of the Frobenius action on the first étale cohomology. The denominator factors $(1-t)$ and $(1-qt)$ correspond to $H^0$ and $H^2$ respectively.
