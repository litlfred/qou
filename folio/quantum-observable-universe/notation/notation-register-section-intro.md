**Reading convention: every object here is mathematical by default.** The symbols in these tables name *constructions inside the model*. Where a construction is named after a physical entity (electron, proton, neutron, mass, binding energy, decay width), the name is expository, in the same way a topologist names a knot "the trefoil". Writing $\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}$ asserts a quantity defined by the framework rather than a claim about the physical proton.

The empirical content of this paper is located precisely at the points where a model quantity is set against a measured one (AME2020, CODATA, PDG). Those comparisons are the physical claims of the work, and they are marked as such where they occur. Everything upstream of a comparison is mathematics, and should be read and checked as mathematics.

Two consequences follow plainly. First, an incomplete derivation upstream is a gap in the mathematics rather than a defect in the empirical claims that are made. Second, and more demanding: because the physics enters *only* at the comparison points, those points carry the entire empirical burden. A systematic uncertainty affecting a compared quantity is therefore all the more consequential under this reading: it acts on the one place where the paper makes contact with measurement.
