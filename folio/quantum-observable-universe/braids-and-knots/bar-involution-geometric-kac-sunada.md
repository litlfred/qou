The bar-involution projection corresponds to orientation-averaging geometrically. It yields averaged crossings for knots, unsigned winding for color tubes, symmetrized Wilson loops on the exceptional divisor, and the contact volume form $\alpha \wedge d\alpha$.

The Kac-Sunada theorem establishes that distinct manifolds can be isospectral. Similarly, distinct braids can share the same Hecke trace $\mathrm{tr}_M(\beta)$ at fixed $q$. However, requiring the trace to match at both $q$ and $q^{-1}$ imposes a much tighter constraint. The physical theory must hear the bar-symmetrized spectrum, forcing the use of the bar-symmetric projection.

**Two different objects called "Fricke" (literature).** The name is attached in this corpus to two unrelated things. The *Fricke trace relation* invoked in the mass-theory blocks is a relation among traces of matrices, used to fix a knot's chirality factor. The *Fricke involution* is the map $\tau \mapsto -1/(N\tau)$ on the upper half-plane, which normalises $\Gamma_0(N)$ and extends to the Atkin–Lehner involutions; it is the object proposed as a candidate partner of the bar-involution in the $\tau$-dictionary audits, where every natural candidate tested so far fails the gate set there. In string compactifications the same map, acting on the axio-dilaton as $S \mapsto -1/(NS)$, is called Fricke S-duality, and a model is self-dual under it exactly when its electric–magnetic charge lattice is $N$-modular, that is, isometric to its dual lattice rescaled by $N$ \cite[\S1.1]{arxiv-1504.07260v3}. That lattice characterisation is a statement about the involution, not about the trace relation, and nothing in this block identifies the bar-involution with either.
