Photons entering the eye are unknotted strands. Reflection from environmental matter braids these photon strands with the environment's state, producing a braid word in $H_n(R, q)$. Observation applies the functor $\tau$ to this state, yielding a classical scalar.

The dimension of the accessible phase space is fixed by the strand count $n$. In low light, $n$ is small, providing insufficient topological complexity to encode the scene. Vision fails in the dark due to a structural truncation of the observation channel rather than a failure of the retina.
