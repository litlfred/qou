The three sheets $r, g, b$ of the colour tube over Bring's surface represent the three colour states of a strand. Jimbo's braiding needs more than a set of three states: it needs them in an order, because its $(q - q^{-1})$ term sits only on ordered pairs. The proposition shows this order is real structure at generic $q$. Exchanging two colours does not send the singlet $\varepsilon_q$ to a multiple of itself unless $q^2 = 1$.

Going around a branch point of the colour tube exchanges two sheets. So the identification of the sheets with an ordered basis of $K^3$ holds on a patch of the surface, not globally. Changing the order conjugates the braiding by the relabelling and carries $\varepsilon_q$ to the singlet of the conjugated braiding, so every trace is unchanged. The two uses of the word colour therefore agree, and the ordering is a local choice that no observable sees.
