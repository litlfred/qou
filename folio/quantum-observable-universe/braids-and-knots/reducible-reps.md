When a representation $\rho\colon\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})\setminus K)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is reducible, all generators fix the same point on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$. They are simultaneously conjugate to upper-triangular matrices, acting as rotations around a common axis. Categorically, this corresponds to the abelianisation of the knot group.

The PBW filtration on $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ equips the associated graded algebra $\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{U}_q$ with a commutative product. A Groebner--Shirshov basis for the $q$-deformed relations exhibits $\mathcal{U}_q$ as a flat deformation of $\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{U}_q$. The leading monomials of the $q$-Serre relations provide non-overlapping obstructions whose resolution yields a normal-form basis coinciding with the Lusztig divided-power basis. A positive-characteristic analogue is the integration theorem: over a field of characteristic $p$, the quotient of a free product of one-dimensional distribution algebras by the relations $fg - gf - \pi(f,g)$ has a PBW basis by a Gröbner basis argument, and strong multiplicativity of $\pi$ is the condition for the comultiplication to descend to that quotient. That theorem concerns formal groups over the field itself, not lifts.

When the representation $\rho$ is reducible, all monomials in the Groebner--Shirshov basis reduce to the abelian Cartan subalgebra. Passing to irreducible $\rho$ activates the off-diagonal generators $E_\pm$, whose normal-form monomials generate the full non-commutative algebra.
