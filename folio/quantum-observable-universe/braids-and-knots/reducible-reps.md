When a representation $\rho\colon\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})\setminus K)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is reducible, all generators fix the same point on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$. They are simultaneously conjugate to upper-triangular matrices, acting as rotations around a common axis. Categorically, this corresponds to the abelianisation of the knot group.

The PBW filtration on $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ equips the associated graded algebra $\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{U}_q$ with a commutative product. Over a field with $q \neq 0$ and $q^2 \neq 1$, the overlaps of the $q$-Serre rewriting rules all resolve, so the normal forms $F^{c}E^{a}K^{n}$ ($c, a \in \mathbb{N}$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$) are a basis of $\mathcal{U}_q$, with the same index set for every such $q$. When $q$ is not a root of unity they agree with the Lusztig divided-power monomials $F^{(c)}E^{(a)}K^{n}$ up to the nonzero scalars $[a]_q!\,[c]_q!$; at a root of unity they do not. That $\mathcal{U}_q$ is a flat deformation of $\mathrm{gr}\,\mathcal{U}_q$ over the ring of the parameter does not follow from this, since the basis theorem holds for each value of $q$ separately. A positive-characteristic analogue is the integration theorem: over a field of characteristic $p$, the quotient of a free product of one-dimensional distribution algebras by the relations $fg - gf - \pi(f,g)$ has a PBW basis by a Gröbner basis argument, and strong multiplicativity of $\pi$ is the condition for the comultiplication to descend to that quotient. That theorem concerns formal groups over the field itself, not lifts.

When the representation $\rho$ is reducible, all monomials in the Groebner--Shirshov basis reduce to the abelian Cartan subalgebra. Passing to irreducible $\rho$ activates the off-diagonal generators $E_\pm$, whose normal-form monomials generate the full non-commutative algebra.
