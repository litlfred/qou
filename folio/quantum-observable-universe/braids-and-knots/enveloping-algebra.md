The universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the associative algebra generated by $e_1,e_2,e_3$ subject to the commutation relations. It provides the scaffolding for every finite-dimensional representation of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and is the classical limit ($q\to 1$) of the quantum group $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$.

By the PBW theorem, the monomials $\{e_1^{a}e_2^{b}e_3^{c} : a,b,c \geq 0\}$ form a $\mathbb{C}$-basis of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$. In the $q$-deformed setting, present $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ by generators $E, F, K^{\pm 1}$ over a field $k$ with $q \neq 0$ and $q^2 \neq 1$, as $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. The Groebner--Shirshov resolution of the $q$-Serre relations shows that the ordered monomials $\{F^{c}E^{a}K^{n} : c, a \in \mathbb{N},\ n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ form a $k$-basis. When $q$ is not a root of unity, $[a]_q!\,[c]_q! \neq 0$, so the rescaled monomials $F^{(c)}E^{(a)}K^{n}$ in the Lusztig divided powers $E^{(a)} = E^{a}/[a]_q!$, $F^{(c)} = F^{c}/[c]_q!$ are again a basis. At a root of unity the divided powers are not defined in this algebra, and the basis is the one in ordinary powers.

This basis provides the explicit coordinates for the fibre algebra $\mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2)$ at each brane-tower level, and its index set $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{Z}$ is the same at every level. Flatness of the deformation $q \mapsto q_n$ over the ring of the parameter, which the transition maps in the direct limit $\mathcal{A}_\infty = \varinjlim\Gamma(J^1 \mathcal{S}_n, \mathrm{SU}_{q_n}(2))$ use, is a stronger statement: the basis theorem is proved for each value of $q$ separately, not over the parameter ring.
