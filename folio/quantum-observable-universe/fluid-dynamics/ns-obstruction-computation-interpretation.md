**What the computation establishes, and what is retracted.** Part (a) identifies the global surface Euler class $e(\xi) = -6 \in H^2(\Sigma_4, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ of the Reeb bundle on Bring's surface — the non-zero global obstruction $\mathrm{NS}(A) = e(\xi)$ of [NS-regular QOU](#def:ns-regular-qou) — together with the excision isomorphism $H^2_{\{p\}}(\Sigma_4, \mathbb{Z}) \cong H^2(D^2, S^1; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by which $e(\xi)$ localises as the Poincaré--Hopf index sum $\sum_p \mathrm{ind}_p(s) = -6$ of a chosen section $s$. By contrast, the complementary-subgroup decomposition $\kappa_n|_\Sigma = \mathrm{lk}\cdot e(\xi)|_\Sigma + \delta_n|_\Sigma$ on a one-dimensional collision curve $\Sigma$ (parts (c)--(d)) does not hold: $H^2(\Sigma, \cdot) = 0$ forces the curve restriction to vanish identically, $H^2(\Sigma_4, \mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_4}) = 0$ forces the sheaf-cohomological jet corrections $\delta_n$ to vanish, and the Biot--Savart jet rate $|j^n v| \sim (T^* - t)^{-(n+1)/2}$ in part (b) is a pointwise scaling ansatz rather than a cohomology class.
