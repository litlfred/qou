The following results constrain a nonzero [discretely self-similar Leray profile](#def:leray-profile) $V$ solving the Leray system. Throughout, $P = 2 \log \lambda$ is the period and $\lambda > 1$ the scaling factor.

**Steady profiles.** A steady profile $V \in L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)$ solving the Leray system is zero \cite{necas-ruzicka-sverak1996}. The same holds for $V \in L^q$, $3 < q \leq \infty$, and for solutions satisfying local energy estimates \cite{tsai1998}.

**Profiles in $L^3$.** The $L^3$ norm is invariant under the Leray rescaling, so along the field $v$ it equals $\|V(\cdot, s)\|_{L^3}$ at $s = -\log(T^* - t)$, a periodic function of $s$. If it is continuous in $s$ it is bounded, so it does not tend to $+\infty$ as $t \to T^{*-}$ (Lean `dss_L3_norm_not_tendsto_atTop`). Since $\|v(t)\|_{L^3} \to \infty$ at any blow-up time \cite{seregin2012}, no discretely self-similar blow-up has a profile continuous in $s$ with values in $L^3$; this is proved directly in \cite{chae2013adss}. A bounded profile in $L^2$ lies in $L^3$, by $\|V\|_{L^3}^3 \leq \|V\|_{L^\infty} \|V\|_{L^2}^2$, so finite-energy bounded profiles are excluded too.

**The surviving decay class.** A profile with $|V(y, s)| = O(|y|^{-a})$ and $a > 1$ is in $L^3$ at infinity. If instead $|V(y, s)| \gtrsim |y|^{-a}$ for large $|y|$, uniformly in $s$, with $a < 1$, then $|v(x, t)| \gtrsim |x - x_0|^{-a} (T^* - t)^{(a-1)/2}$, which is unbounded as $t \to T^{*-}$ at every fixed $x \neq x_0$. What remains is $|V(y, s)| \sim |y|^{-1}$, the scale-invariant decay, with $V(\cdot, s) \notin L^3$.

**The scaling factor.** Discretely self-similar blow-up with $\lambda$ close to $1$ is excluded under a scale-critical bound, and such a blow-up has a single singular point \cite{chae-wolf2016dss}. For $\lambda$ well above $1$ the question is open \cite{bradshaw-tsai2018survey}. The period is not fixed by the QOU: the root-of-unity ladder $q_\kappa = e^{i\pi/\kappa}$ satisfies $q_\kappa^{2\kappa} = 1$ (Lean `qKappa_pow_two_kappa`), and [the substrate parameter](#def:substrate-parameter) is not a root of unity.

**Angular content.** On the spin-$j$ summand of an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-representation the central element $-1$ acts by $(-1)^{2j}$. On velocity fields on $\mathbb{R}^3$ the rotation action factors through $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, so $-1$ acts trivially and only integer $j$ occur (Lean `IntegerSpin.even_of_neg_one_pow_eq_one`). The profile decomposes into vector spherical harmonics of integer degree.

**Scope of finite models.** The [Descartes theorem](#thm:ns-singularity-descartes) is a statement about the global Euler class of the surface bundle, not a blow-up theorem for the Navier--Stokes equation on $\mathbb{R}^3$. A finite truncation of the Leray system is a Galerkin model. Finite-dimensional cascade models can blow up without the blow-up transferring to the unforced Navier--Stokes equation (Tao, *J. Amer. Math. Soc.* **29** (2016), 601--674), so a solution of a truncated system is evidence about the truncation only.
