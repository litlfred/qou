The [proposition](#prop:reeb-beltrami-regular-flow) exhibits one explicit family of solutions, the multiples of a single field, on one Riemannian $3$-manifold. It says nothing about solutions with other initial data. Global regularity on $M$ remains as open as on the three-torus $T^3 = \mathrm{UT}(T^2)$ of the [torus example](#ex:torus-ns-regular). The proposition does settle a question of topology. The bundle $M \to \Sigma_4$ has Euler number $-6$, and this topology carries a smooth global solution. Hence no invariant of the bundle can force every solution on $M$ to blow up, and the explicit family is the witness.

The field $V$ generates the free $\mathrm{U}(1)$ action on the fibres, which is how the [Reeb bundle](#def:reeb-bundle) specifies its Reeb field. A finer identification is inherited and is not re-derived here: that $V$, with this normalisation, is the Reeb field of the Boothby--Wang contact form that $M$ carries as the link of Bring's cone.

The metric parameter $s > 0$ is a free choice. Up to an overall scale, every left-invariant metric invariant under the fibre rotation has the form $g_s$, and $s = 1$ is the Sasaki metric of the hyperbolic surface. The corpus does not fix $s$. No value of $s$ is compared with data, so the parameter is unconstrained and no fit is involved. The contact form $\theta^V$ dual to $V$ and its Reeb field are independent of $s$.

The solution the field gives depends on the viscous operator. The three operators agree on flat space, where $\mathrm{Ric} = 0$, and differ on $M$. Under the deformation operator the field $V$ is an infinitesimal isometry, so $\mathrm{Def}\,V = 0$ and the dissipation $2\nu\int_M |\mathrm{Def}\,u|^2$ vanishes. This is the curved analogue of solid-body rotation. The kinetic energy $\tfrac{1}{2}\int_M |aV|^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}a^2 s^2\,\mathrm{vol}(M)$ and the helicity $\int_M \langle aV, \mathrm{curl}(aV)\rangle = -a^2 s^3\,\mathrm{vol}(M)$ are conserved.
