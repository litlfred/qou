The [conservation of categorical helicity](#prop:helicity-conservation) physically manifests topological charge conservation within the fluid. Under smooth *inviscid* (Euler) evolution, vortex lines are transported by the flow, so the evolution cannot alter the total helicity of a vortex configuration: helicity is preserved by every volume-preserving diffeomorphism, and it equals the average asymptotic linking number of pairs of trajectories \cite[Thm.~2.2, Thm.~2.5]{khesin2003}. It merely redistributes helicity among writhe, twist, and linking, as demonstrated by [trefoil chirality flips](#rem:trefoil-vortex-chirality-flip). The statement does not extend to viscous flow. For Navier--Stokes with viscosity $\nu > 0$ the helicity $H = \int v \cdot \omega\,dV$ obeys $dH/dt = -2\nu \int \omega \cdot \mathrm{curl}\,\omega\,dV$, which has no sign and does not vanish in general, and vortex lines of any knot or link type can reconnect while the solution stays smooth \cite{enciso2017}.

The exact quantisation of $\Gamma_i^{(q)} = [j_i]_q[j_i+1]_q$ mandates that each term in the helicity sum remains individually quantised. Consequently, helicity is restricted to a discrete lattice structured by the $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ representation labels $\{j_i\}$.

Two sources bound helicity by energy, and in both the bound is geometric, not topological. For a smooth field $V$ on a compact domain $\Omega$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$, $S^3$ or $H^3$, the Biot--Savart operator gives $|H(V)| \le N(R)\,\|V\|^2$, where $N(R)$ depends only on the radius $R$ of a ball with the volume of $\Omega$ \cite[\S13, Thm.~7]{deturck-gluck2008}. That note states it gives only a hint of the proofs in $S^3$ and $H^3$, so its constants are to be checked against the journal version before any use. When the pairwise linking numbers vanish, as for a Borromean configuration, the pairwise helicities $H_{12} = \mathrm{lk}(L_1,L_2)\,\mathrm{Flux}(B_1)\,\mathrm{Flux}(B_2)$ are zero \cite[\S6, eq.~(6.1)]{komendarczyk2008}, and the invariant that remains is the third-order helicity. On Borromean flux tubes it equals $\bar\mu_{123}(L)\,\mathrm{Flux}(B_1)\,\mathrm{Flux}(B_2)\,\mathrm{Flux}(B_3)$ \cite[\S6, eq.~(6.6)]{komendarczyk2008}, so it is cubic in the fluxes, not quadratic, and it is totally antisymmetric in the three components because $\bar\mu_{123}$ is \cite[Rem.~2.3]{komendarczyk2008}. Its bound by the $L^2$-energy, $|H_{123}(B;T)| \le C_T\,E_2(B)^{3/2}$, has a constant set by the geometry of the invariant domain, and the source records that the estimate is meaningless once the handlebodies come close to one another \cite[\S7, Thm.~7.1 and eqs.~(7.6)--(7.7)]{komendarczyk2008}. The Freedman--He lower bound quoted there, eq.~(7.8), sums over components, each component taken against the whole link, and carries the pure constant $(\pi/16)^{1/4}$ with no volume factor. These are statements about smooth fields; they bound the classical helicity and say nothing about the quantised labels $\Gamma_i^{(q)}$.
