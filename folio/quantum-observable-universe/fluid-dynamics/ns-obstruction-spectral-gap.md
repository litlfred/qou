Rooted in the categorical origin of the [crossing energy](#def:crossing-energy), taking $q \to 1^+$ sends the two eigenvalues $(q, -q^{-1})$ of the Hecke generator $\sigma$ on $V \otimes V$ to $(1, -1)$, while the channel contraction ratio $q^{-2} \to 1$ and the skein asymmetry $q - q^{-1} \to 0$ vanish to first order in $q - 1$.

- Within the $q$-deformed brane model, $q > 1$ separates the symmetric channel $S^2 V$ from the antisymmetric channel $\Lambda^2 V$ by a strict per-crossing contraction $q^{-2} < 1$, whereas at $q = 1$ both channels have unit modulus and that contraction disappears.
- On the global spectral surface $\mathcal{S}$, the [NS obstruction class](#def:ns-obstruction) $\mathrm{NS}(A) = e(\xi) \in H^2(\mathcal{S}, \mathbb{Z})$ records the topological twist $\chi(A)$ of the Reeb bundle independently of $q$, while the Hecke gap $q - q^{-1}$ tracks the algebraic damping rate that vanishes at $q = 1$.

This Hecke gap closure explains why the per-crossing contraction mechanism of the $q > 1$ model degenerates as $q \to 1^+$; it is an algebraic feature of the $q$-deformed channel algebra rather than a standalone criterion deciding smoothness or blowup of the classical three-dimensional PDE.
