With the vortex spectrum and helicity invariant in place, this section examines collective behaviour: how discrete modes assemble into turbulent tangles and how perturbations propagate across the brane fluid as acoustic excitations.

The $q$-deformed energy cascade and modified Kolmogorov spectrum open the section by deriving the inertial-range scaling of the energy spectrum $E(k)$ as a function of $q$ and the Planck--Reynolds number, yielding a testable high-wavenumber deviation from classical Kolmogorov scaling for Bose--Einstein condensates and superfluid experiments. Adiabatic transport across filament configurations determines the Berry phase on the vortex moduli space, while the $q$-Laplacian eigenvalue problem governs Kelvin-wave dispersion and recovers the classical self-induction law as $q \to 1$. Finally, longitudinal perturbations propagate on an effective Gordon acoustic metric whose transonic trapping loci form kinematic scalar horizons. The next section applies this spectral apparatus to control the nonlinear $q$-Navier--Stokes flow for $q > 1$.
