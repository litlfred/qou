Consider the $q \to 1^+$ degeneration of solutions to the $q$-NS model, proposing an anti-parallel vortex-pair scenario in which the limiting flow loses smoothness at a finite time $T^*$.

**The initial data.**
Configure an anti-parallel vortex pair: two counter-rotating vortex tubes approaching each other by mutual induction. In the $q$-deformed setting, the circulation is quantised at $\Gamma_q = [j]_q[j+1]_q$ with $j = 1/2$, and separation $d$ is measured in units of the aether grain $\hbar_q = 1 - q^{-1} = (q-1)/q$.

**Phase 1: conjectured $q$-deformed evolution ($q > 1$).**
Conditional on $q$-NS well-posedness for $q > 1$, each deformed trajectory remains globally smooth, with filament interaction governed by the Hecke channel algebra and short-range core overlap opposed by quantum pressure $Q_q$ at scale $d_{\min} \sim \hbar_q > 0$.

**Phase 2: the $q \to 1^+$ degeneration.**
As $q \to 1^+$, the grain scale $\hbar_q \to 0$ and quantum pressure $Q_q \to 0$ vanish alongside the unrescaled viscosity $\nu_q = (q-1)/(2q) \to 0$ (landing in the inviscid Euler limit unless rescaled to the physical unit layer). The conjecture posits that without quantum pressure to arrest mutual induction, the limiting cores collide at a finite time $T^*$.

**Phase 3: conjectured singular-set structure.**
At the posited collision time $T^*$, the conjecture proposes that vorticity concentrates along a one-dimensional collision segment $\Sigma$:

1. **Hausdorff dimension.** $d_H(\Sigma) = 1$, modelling mutual-induction approach along a one-dimensional intersection curve.
2. **Sobolev stratum.** $n^* = 2$: a limiting velocity field with bounded $L^2$ norm and unbounded vorticity would fail to extend in $H^s$ for $s \geq 2$.
3. **Cohomological scope.** Because $\dim \Sigma = 1$, direct sheaf cohomology $H^2(\Sigma, \mathcal{B})$ vanishes identically, so a non-zero restriction class in $H^2(\Sigma, \mathcal{B})$ does not exist; any topological witness of jet-prolongation discontinuity across $\Sigma$ must instead be formulated in relative cohomology $H^2_\Sigma(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{B})$ or local cohomology.
4. **Local-cohomology fibre.** At a collision point $p \in \Sigma$ where each filament carries the fundamental representation $V_{1/2}$, the local-cohomology fibre is modelled by $H^1_{\mathfrak{m}_p}(A) \cong V_{1/2} \oplus V_{1/2}$. Whether this algebraic fibre corresponds to a genuine finite-time singularity of a classical three-dimensional viscous flow remains open.
