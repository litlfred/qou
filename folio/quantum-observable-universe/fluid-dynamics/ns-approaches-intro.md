The classical Navier--Stokes regularity question sits inside a one-parameter family of $q$-deformed equations that are expected, but not yet proved, to be well-posed for $q > 1$. The degeneration at $q = 1$ forms a research programme. This section treats the classical limit as a phase transition and conjectures a critical exponent for the enstrophy divergence. The universality conjecture predicts that this exponent depends only on the Euler characteristic. It formulates a $q$-deformed Aubin--Lions compactness conjecture; because $\nu_q \to 0$, its limit object is an Euler solution. Conditional on these conjectures, the representation-theoretic truncation at roots of unity then locates the remaining gap in the growth rate of the vorticity bound. It tracks the geometry of vortex collision via the Levi degeneracy and explicit Townsend strain dynamics. Finally, it provides a self-similar Leray blowup profile constrained by $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ representation theory.
