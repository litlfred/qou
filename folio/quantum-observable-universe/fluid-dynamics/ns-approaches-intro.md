The classical Navier--Stokes regularity question sits inside a one-parameter family of $q$-deformed equations that are expected, but not yet proved, to be well-posed for $q > 1$. The degeneration at $q = 1$ forms a research programme. This section treats the classical limit as a phase transition and conjectures a critical exponent for the enstrophy divergence. The universality conjecture predicts that this exponent depends only on the Euler characteristic. It formulates a $q$-deformed Aubin--Lions compactness conjecture; because $\nu_q \to 0$, its limit object is an Euler solution. Conditional on these conjectures, the representation-theoretic truncation at roots of unity then locates the remaining gap in the growth rate of the core aspect ratio. Subsequent remarks track the geometry of vortex-line twist via the Levi scalar and compute core-shape evolution under Townsend strain dynamics. Finally, the section records the definition and known $L^3$ exclusions for discretely self-similar Leray profiles alongside their integer-spin spherical-harmonic truncations.
