The brane of a quantum observable universe carries a natural fluid dynamics whose viscosity, vortex spectrum, and turbulence structure depend on the substrate parameter $q$. This chapter develops the $q$-deformed Navier--Stokes system on the brane and extracts two principal consequences: global regularity of the $q$-deformed equation for $q > 1$, and a categorical obstruction aimed at finite-time blowup in the classical, unforced Navier--Stokes equation ($q = 1$, external force $f \equiv 0$).

The first three sections establish the hydrodynamic framework: deriving the $q$-Navier--Stokes equation from the Reeb derivation on the Frobenius--Hopf algebra, characterising the discrete vortex spectrum and helicity conservation forced by the braided monoidal structure, and constructing the turbulence theory, including $q$-deformed Kolmogorov scaling and acoustic horizons.

The final two sections prove the chapter's main results: global well-posedness of the $q$-NS system for $q > 1$ via spectral Sobolev estimates on the quantum Peter--Weyl decomposition, and identification of the NS obstruction — a categorical invariant measuring the failure of regularity at $q = 1$. The Hecke algebra computes this obstruction, providing a purely algebraic criterion for the classical Navier--Stokes regularity problem.
