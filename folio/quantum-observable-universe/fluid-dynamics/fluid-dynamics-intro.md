The brane of a quantum observable universe carries a natural fluid dynamics whose viscosity, vortex spectrum and turbulence structure depend on the aether parameter $q$. This chapter sets up the $q$-deformed Navier--Stokes system ($q$-NS) on the brane and studies what it can and cannot say about the classical, unforced Navier--Stokes equation at $q = 1$. The chapter does not solve the classical regularity problem, and nothing in it should be read as a claim to. The paragraphs below sort its contents by what is actually established in the Lean formalisation; only results with a faithful human statement (in Mathlib or in this corpus) are counted as proved.

**What is proved.** The proved content is algebraic. The two-vortex channel algebra satisfies the Hecke relation. On $\mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SU}_2)$, the spin-raising triad coupling has finite reach, and its closed form follows from the Haar state and the little $q$-Legendre polynomials, whose norms are computed exactly and shown to be positive. The normalised squared coupling $R(q,s)$ is a rational function defined at $q = 1$, with
$$
R(1,s) \ge \frac{(s+1)^2}{16}, \qquad R(q,s)\,q^{4s} \le \frac{q^{8}}{(q^2-1)^3(q^4-1)^2(q^6-1)} \quad (q^2 > 1),
$$
so the coupling is uniform in $s$ for $q > 1$ and unbounded in $s$ at $q = 1$. This non-uniformity is the first genuine algebraic evidence for the degeneration at $q = 1$; it is evidence about one coupling, not a blowup theorem. Also proved: the shell-model tail collapse, the exact Beltrami and ABC solutions together with the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ Reeb computation at the left-invariant level, the pointwise Levi-scalar identity with the Beltrami counterexample to branch (A) of the Levi-degeneracy conjecture, and the arithmetic fact that $n \mid -6$ exactly for $n \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\}$.

**What is conditional.** Global well-posedness of $q$-NS for $q > 1$ (Section **Global regularity**) is stated conditionally. Existence, uniqueness and smoothness are hypotheses of the formal statement; only the energy inequality is derived from them. No concrete $q$-NS evolution on $\mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SU}_2)$ is yet defined in the formalisation, so the theorem records the expected shape of the result rather than proving it. The spectral Sobolev estimate, the quantum Peter--Weyl theorem, the nonlinear uniqueness estimate and the $q$-Beale--Kato--Majda criterion are in the same state.

**What is provable now.** Several of these gaps can be closed with machinery already in the corpus: orthogonality of the $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ matrix coefficients from the Haar state, the discrete spectrum $\mu_k = [k]_q^2$ of the $q$-Laplacian with its two-sided bounds, the Kelvin ladder $z D_q z^k = [k]_q z^k$, and the $q$-independence of observability computed on genuine Hom spaces.

**What is tractable open.** Global well-posedness for a concretely defined $q$-NS with $q > 1$ is plausible, because the dissipation symbol $[k]_q^2$ grows exponentially. Part A of the regularity-breakdown conjecture, that the enstrophy supremum $\Omega(q)$ diverges as $q \to 1^+$, is the main open target of the chapter. Since the viscosity $\nu_q$ tends to zero, the formal limit of $q$-NS is the Euler equation rather than Navier--Stokes; the same correction applies to the $q$-Aubin--Lions conjecture, whose limit object is an Euler solution. The critical exponent of $\Omega(q)$ and the Kummer cohomology $H^2(\mathcal{S}, \mu_n)$ are open in the same sense.

**What is hard open.** Existence of a classical blowup solution (Part B of the regularity-breakdown conjecture), the anti-parallel vortex counterexample, the vorticity lower bound, the Levi-degeneracy criterion, sufficiency of the obstruction-tower criterion, and the identification of the standard part of the surreal $q$-flow with a Leray--Hopf solution are each at least as hard as the classical problem. They are recorded as conjectures. The invariant $\mathrm{NS}(A) = e(\xi) = -6$ of Section **The NS obstruction** is a surface Euler class; its restriction to any one-dimensional collision locus vanishes, so it is not by itself a blowup criterion.

The first three sections build the hydrodynamic framework: the $q$-NS equation from the Reeb derivation on the Frobenius--Hopf algebra, the vortex spectrum and helicity, and the turbulence theory. The fourth section states the conditional regularity theorem for $q > 1$, the fifth and sixth define the NS obstruction and its tower, and the last collects the open approaches to the degeneration at $q = 1$.
