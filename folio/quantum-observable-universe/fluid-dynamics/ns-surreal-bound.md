Within the $q$-deformed model, the asymptotic competition between cascade growth and viscous damping as $q \to 1^+$ admits a formal rate comparison in the surreal extension.

Let $\varepsilon \in \mathrm{Der}(A)$ be a positive infinitesimal parameterising $q = 1 + \varepsilon$. In the $q > 1$ regime, the gap scale $\Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3$ acts as an effective dissipation scale alongside the conjectured Kolmogorov-closure enstrophy ceiling of [the critical-exponent conjecture](#conj:critical-exponent).

**The surreal rate comparison.** Near $\varepsilon = 0$, two formal rates are compared:
- Dissipation gap $\Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3$ (damping scale of the unrescaled $q$-model)
- Conjectured enstrophy ceiling $\mathcal{Z}_q \asymp \varepsilon^{-4/3}$ (under the Kolmogorov-cascade closure)

By surreal rate calculus, $\mathcal{Z}_q \cdot \varepsilon^{4/3} \asymp 1$, while $\Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3$ is a positive infinitesimal. The inequality

$$
  \mathcal{Z}_q \;>\; \Delta_q
$$

holds in the surreal ordering: the posited cascade ceiling sits at higher $\omega$-order than the vanishing dissipation scale.

**Scope.** As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, the posited enstrophy ceiling outruns the unrescaled gap scale in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon)$. Because the unrescaled viscosity $\nu_q = \varepsilon/(2(1+\varepsilon))$ tends to zero and the standard-part bridge from surreal $q$-flows to classical Leray--Hopf solutions is an open conjecture, this comparison records formal $\varepsilon$-scaling inside the $q$-deformed model rather than proving finite-time blowup of the fixed-viscosity classical Navier--Stokes equation.
