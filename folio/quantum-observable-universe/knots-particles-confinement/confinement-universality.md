*Let $\gamma_k = \mathbf{1} + \varphi_0\sigma_0 + \varphi_1\sigma_1 + \varphi_{01}\sigma_0\sigma_1$ be a 4-term photon. Let $\mathfrak{N}(\varphi_0, \varphi_1, \varphi_{01})$ denote the nucleon NF obtained by the derivation chain $\gamma\gamma \to q\bar{q} \to \mathcal{B}_3 \cdot \mathrm{crossings} \to \mathfrak{N}$.*

*Then for all $\varphi_0, \varphi_1, \varphi_{01} \in \mathbb{R}$:*

$$
  \mathfrak{N}(\varphi_0, \varphi_1, \varphi_{01})
  =
  \mathfrak{N}(0, 0, 0)
  =
  \mathrm{NF}(\mathcal{B}_3 \cdot \mathrm{crossings}).
$$

*The nucleon NF depends only on $q$ and the quark flavor assignment.*

This is the categorical expression of asymptotic freedom: the strong interaction washes out the electromagnetic initial conditions. At short distances, only the quark flavor matters. The photon polarization is a long-distance electromagnetic property that decouples under confinement.

**Corollary.** Under the same hypothesis, a quantity of the photon that can be read off the nucleon NF is constant in the photon polarisation. In particular a chirality unit inherited from the photon polarisation is invisible at nucleon level. This is a one-line consequence of the statement.

**Scope.** The polarisation coefficients may be taken in any coefficient ring; the statement uses no property of $\mathbb{R}$. At $\varphi_0 = \varphi_1 = \varphi_{01} = 0$ (for both photons) the $\gamma\gamma$ product is the vacuum $\mathbf{1}$ and contains no crossing, so the reference value $\mathfrak{N}(0,0,0)$ is not produced by the derivation chain at that point. Because the $\sigma_0$ channel depends on the photon coefficients, the quark flavours that the chain produces depend on them, and the statement is meaningful only on the set of photon states that produce the nucleon's flavours.
