*Fix a quark flavour assignment $f = (f_1, f_2, f_3)$ with $f_i \in \{\mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{d}\}$, and let*

$$
  \mathfrak{N}_f
  =
  \mathrm{NF}\bigl(\mathcal{B}_3 \cdot C(f_1, f_2; \sigma_0) \cdot C(f_1, f_3; \sigma_0) \cdot C(f_2, f_3; \sigma_1)\bigr).
$$

*Let $\gamma_k = \mathbf{1} + \varphi_0\sigma_0 + \varphi_1\sigma_1 + \varphi_{01}\sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1}$ be a 4-term photon, and let $\mathfrak{N}(\varphi_0, \varphi_1, \varphi_{01})$ be the nucleon NF of the derivation chain $\gamma\gamma \to q\bar{q} \to \mathcal{B}_3 \cdot \mathrm{crossings}$ with the flavours $f$ supplied as input to the second stage. Then for all photon coefficients in any coefficient ring,*

$$
  \mathfrak{N}(\varphi_0, \varphi_1, \varphi_{01}) = \mathfrak{N}_f .
$$

*The nucleon NF depends only on $q$ and the flavour assignment $f$.*

The statement is true by construction. The confinement word contains no photon coefficient, so $\varphi$ cannot enter the second stage. It is not a derived decoupling of the electromagnetic initial conditions, and it is not evidence for asymptotic freedom.

**Corollary.** A quantity of the photon that can be read off the nucleon NF is constant in the photon polarisation. In particular a chirality unit inherited from the photon polarisation is invisible at nucleon level. This is a one-line consequence of the statement.

**Scope.** The statement takes the flavours as an input. Whether the pair-production stage produces a given flavour assignment from given photon coefficients is a separate question, and it is open. At $\varphi_0 = \varphi_1 = \varphi_{01} = 0$ for both photons the $\gamma\gamma$ product is the vacuum $\mathbf{1}$ and contains no crossing.
