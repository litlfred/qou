*The lepton and meson mass towers are both indexed by torus knots $T(2, n)$ for odd $n = 3, 5, 7, \ldots$. We establish the following correspondence:*

| Generation | $n$ | Lepton ($H_2$) | Quark flavor ($H_3$) | Meson at $2n$ crossings ($q\bar{q}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 3 | $\mathfrak{e}$ (electron) | $u, d$ | $\pi$ (pion) |
| 2nd | 5 | $\mu$ (muon) | $\mathfrak{c}, s$ (charm, strange) | $K$ (kaon) |
| 3rd | 7 | $\tau$ (tau) | $\mathfrak{t}, \mathfrak{b}$ (top, bottom) | $\eta, \phi$ |

*The lepton at generation $k$ is the closure of $\sigma_1^{2k+1}$ in $H_2(q)$. The meson in the same row is the Hopf-bound quark pair whose **total** crossing number is $2n = 2(2k+1)$ in $H_3(q)$ — the meson column is indexed by that total, not by the row's quark generation.*

The two indices run in the same step of $2$, so the column reads as aligned while being offset: only the first row is a pure same-generation pair. By the constituent content of the proof, $\pi = \bar{u}d$ is $3 + 3$, $K = \bar{u}s$ is $3 + 5$ and therefore mixes the first and second generations, and $\phi = s\bar{s}$ is $5 + 5$ and is a pure *second*-generation pair occupying the third row. There is no pure third-generation entry: the third-generation quarkonia are $\Upsilon = \mathfrak{b}\bar{\mathfrak{b}}$, which would sit at $7 + 7 = 14$ crossings and is not computed here, and $\mathfrak{t}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}$, which is not a bound state.
