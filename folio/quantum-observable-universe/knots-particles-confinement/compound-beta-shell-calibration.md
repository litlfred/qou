*Let $E_0^{(2)} = \mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ be the free-neutron β-decay Q-value, the CAL-2 proxy, kept as a symbol. A β-decay $(Z, N) \to (Z+1, N-1)$ preserves $A$, so the shell it can cross is a proton or neutron shell: let it cross a magic number, either $Z < Z_{\mathrm{magic}} \le Z+1$ or $N-1 < N_{\mathrm{magic}} \le N$, with magic numbers in $\{2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126\}$. Assume the parent contains $\sigma_{\mathrm{shell}}$ and the daughter contains a different shell operator or a cross-shell transition. Then the Q-value of the magic-crossing decay is:*

$$
  \mathfrak{Q}_\beta(\text{magic-crossing})
  \;=\;
  E_0^{(2)} \cdot
  \Bigl(
    \bigl|\mathrm{tr}_M^{\mathrm{parent}}\bigr|
    \;-\;
    \bigl|\mathrm{tr}_M^{\mathrm{daughter}}\bigr|
    \;-\;
    \bigl|\mathrm{tr}_M^{\mathrm{e^- + \bar\nu}}\bigr|
  \Bigr).
$$

*Equivalently, the shell-binding target $y_{\mathrm{target}}(A_{\mathrm{magic}})$ correlates to the cumulative β-decay chain reaching the magic anchor via the categorical trace. To formalize the full binding scale, one combines multiple β-decay Q-values along the isobar chain through the magic anchor.*

**Scope.** The single magic-crossing Q-value identity above rests on the categorical trace. The compound-chain extension — combining Q-values along the isobar chain to isolate a shell-binding target — is exploratory: it subtracts model-predicted non-magic Q-values (a circular step) and carries an unresolved unit mismatch. It is therefore not a fit-free calibration and is not wired into any production shell-binding target.
