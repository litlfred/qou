**Crossing orientation.**
Within $\mathcal{B}_3$, each component passes over and under the other two at specific crossings. The positive orientation means the up quark's crossings contribute $+q$ to the Skein polynomial at each crossing point.

**What the orientation fixes, and what it does not.**
The [Coulomb charge](#def:coulomb-charge) $Q = \tfrac{1}{2}(n_+ - n_-)$ is odd under reversal of every crossing. Orientation therefore fixes the *sign* of the up quark's charge, and fixes its magnitude relative to the [down quark](#def:down-quark) — the two are exact mirrors, so their charges are equal and opposite. It does not fix the value $+\tfrac{2}{3}$, for two independent reasons: a mirror pair has ratio $-1$, whereas $(+\tfrac{2}{3}, -\tfrac{1}{3})$ has ratio $-2$; and $Q$ takes values in $\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}$, so thirds lie outside its range for every braid word. The Standard Model values are adopted here, not derived from the crossing count. The parameter-free ribbon route to them is recorded as tested and refuted in [the flavor charge assignments](#rem:flavor-charge-assignments).

**Isolation.**
An isolated up quark does not exist. Removing it from $\mathcal{B}_3$ unlinks the remaining two unknots, which destroys the baryon.

**Environment, not knot type.** The [up quark](#def:up-quark) is not a distinct knot type. It is the unknot distinguished by its environment. It is threaded through a color tube and Borromean-linked with two other unknots.
