**Conjecture (Brunnian property of $\mathcal{B}_3$).** *Let $\mathcal{B}_3 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be the standard Borromean link, presented as the closed braid $\overline{(\sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1})^3}$ in $B_3$. For each $i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, let $\mathcal{B}_3 \setminus K_i$ denote the two-component sub-link obtained by removing the $i$-th component. Then $\mathcal{B}_3 \setminus K_i$ is topologically equivalent to the trivial two-component unlink $U_2$.*

This formalizes the confinement mechanism. Removing any single component dissolves the binding between the remaining two. This is the defining property of Brunnian links \cite{kawauchi1996}. The three-component binding is invisible pairwise: all three pairwise linking numbers of $\mathcal{B}_3$ vanish, while Milnor's triple linking number is $\mu_{123} = \pm 1$, since one delta move turns the three-component unlink into $\mathcal{B}_3$ and raises $\mu$ by $1$. For such links $2\mu$ equals Pontryagin's $\nu$-invariant of the characteristic map $T^3 \to S^2$, an honest integer when the pairwise linking numbers vanish \cite[Thm.~A, \S4]{dgkmsv2009}. Naive strand-deletion at the braid word level does not immediately yield the empty braid. Deleting any one strand from $(\sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1})^3 \in B_3$ removes the four crossings that strand participates in and leaves two crossings of opposite sign, $\sigma^{+}\sigma^{-}$, with exponent sum $0$. The closure therefore has two components, and the opposite-sign pair cancels under a single R2 move, giving $U_2$ directly. The three deletions are related by the cyclic symmetry of the word, so the same count holds for each.
