Step 4 of the [derived atom](#def:derived-atom) says the integer LP dual "selects the minimum gluon", and the [proton](#rem:categorical-proton-properties) block says it "enumerates all feasible gluon combinations and selects the configuration minimizing $|\mathrm{tr}_M|$". Neither says what the enumeration ranges over. This remark says it. The answer dissolves the question it was written to settle: nothing is excluded, so no exclusion argument is owed.

**The feasible set.** The [universal ILP](#def:universal-ilp-algorithm) fixes it. The stabiliser $K$ ranges over non-trivial braid words up to a crossing cap; the constraint is Frobenius positivity of the resulting state, $G\cdot\mathrm{NF}(x\cdot K)\ge 0$; the objective is minimum crossing number with $|\mathrm{tr}_M(x\cdot K)|$ as tiebreak. The producer behind the nucleon numbers enumerates words over $\{\sigma_0,\sigma_1,L_+,L_-\}$ of length at most three — $4 + 4^2 + 4^3 = 84$ candidates, which is the count [the unwrap-depth remark](#rem:ilp-solution-count-unwrap) reports for the proton at baryon level. So the range is not "combinations of $L_\pm$". It is every braid word inside the cap, and the four-letter alphabet composes to all six [normal-form basis words](#fundamental-knots-operator-basis), $e^-$ included.

**The electron word is inside it.** The natural worry is why the stabiliser may not be $e^- = \sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_0$: it is a basis word exactly as $L_\pm$ are, and like them it has $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$, so nothing topological separates them. It is not excluded. As a three-letter braid word it sits in the enumeration — reached as $\sigma_0\cdot L_-$, or as $L_+\cdot\sigma_0$ — and it is admissible: applied to either bare nucleon it leaves the state Frobenius-positive. It loses, and it loses under either objective the corpus states. On $|\mathrm{tr}_M|$ it is beaten by an order of magnitude or more for both nucleons. On crossing count it costs three, and both nucleons have admissible stabilisers costing one or two. That is a defeat in the minimisation, not a prohibition, and it is the only answer the construction supports.

**Nor is double counting.** The Hopf gluon does redistribute normal-form weight from $\sigma_1$ into the $e^-$ channel, so $e^-$ is the output of stabilisation. That is a consequence of the selection, not a constraint on the search: an $e^-$ *input* would be a different, worse candidate, not a quantity counted twice.

**Hyperbolic volume decides nothing here.** Where a competing channel carries a hyperbolic constituent, volume settles the selection outright — the Borromean rings carry $8G$ and a torus link carries none. That is what happens one step earlier, at the antiquark disposal. That route is unavailable at step 4. All five non-identity [basis words](#fundamental-knots-operator-basis) are torus-type with $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$, so volume separates none of them, and the whole selection falls to $\mathrm{tr}_M$ and crossing count.

**What is genuinely unsettled is the crossing count, not the feasible set.**

~~The proton takes two gluon crossings and the neutron four, and that difference is what makes the neutron heavier and so feeds $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$.~~ The neutron's gluon count is two, not four. The [neutron block](#rem:categorical-neutron-properties) records eleven crossings in total — six from $\mathcal{B}_3$, three from quarks, two from gluons — since its stated $\mathrm{net}(\mathfrak{n}) = +0.7976$ is the eleven-crossing value on the $k$-ladder, the thirteen-crossing value being $1.2359$. The [proton block](#rem:categorical-proton-properties) applies a single Hopf link $L_- = \sigma_1\sigma_0$, also two. The two nucleons therefore carry the same gluon-crossing count, so the struck sentence's premise does not hold, and whatever carries $\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{n}} - \mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is not this difference.

The disagreement that survives is narrower. The [universal ILP](#def:universal-ilp-algorithm)'s own witness records the gluon as a single $\sigma_1$ at one crossing; its instantiation table records $\sigma_0\sigma_0$ at two; the proton block records $L_- = \sigma_1\sigma_0$ at two; the neutron block records two. Four statements of one quantity, two answers. The spread is not a rounding difference: under the minimum-crossing objective the universal ILP states, a one-crossing stabiliser strictly beats a two-crossing one, so the two readings are not both selections of the same rule.

The feasible set is well defined and $e^-$ is fairly beaten inside it; which stabiliser the stated objective actually selects is a separate open question, on which the mass split depends.
