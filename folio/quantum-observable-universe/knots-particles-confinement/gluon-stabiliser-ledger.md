Step 4 of the [derived atom](#def:derived-atom) says the integer LP dual "selects the minimum gluon", and the [proton](#rem:categorical-proton-properties) block says it "enumerates all feasible gluon combinations and selects the configuration minimizing $|\mathrm{tr}_M|$". Neither says what the enumeration ranges over. This remark says it. The answer dissolves the question it was written to settle: nothing is excluded, so no exclusion argument is owed.

**The feasible set.** The [confinement-layer operator selection](#rem:confinement-layer-operator-selection) rule frames the candidate search. The stabiliser $K$ ranges over non-trivial braid words up to a crossing cap; the constraint is positivity of the resulting state on the Temperley--Lieb quotient; the objective is minimum crossing number with $|\mathrm{tr}_M(x\cdot K)|$ as tiebreak. The producer behind the nucleon numbers enumerates words over $\{\sigma_0,\sigma_1,L_+,L_-\}$ of length at most three — $4 + 4^2 + 4^3 = 84$ candidates, which is the count [the unwrap-depth remark](#rem:ilp-solution-count-unwrap) reports for the proton at baryon level. So the range is not "combinations of $L_\pm$". It is every braid word inside the cap, and the four-letter alphabet composes to all six [normal-form basis words](#fundamental-knots-operator-basis), $e^-$ included.

**The electron word is inside it.** The natural worry is why the stabiliser may not be $e^- = \sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_0$: it is a basis word exactly as $L_\pm$ are, and like them it has $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$, so nothing topological separates them. It is not excluded. As a three-letter braid word it sits in the enumeration — reached as $\sigma_0\cdot L_-$, or as $L_+\cdot\sigma_0$ — and it is admissible: applied to either bare nucleon it leaves the state Frobenius-positive. It loses, and it loses under either objective the corpus states. On $|\mathrm{tr}_M|$ it is beaten by an order of magnitude or more for both nucleons. On crossing count it costs three, and both nucleons have admissible stabilisers costing one or two. That is a defeat in the minimisation, not a prohibition, and it is the only answer the construction supports.

**Nor is double counting.** The gluon does redistribute normal-form weight from $\sigma_1$ into the $e^-$ channel, so $e^-$ is the output of stabilisation. That is a consequence of the selection, not a constraint on the search: an $e^-$ *input* would be a different, worse candidate, not a quantity counted twice.

**Hyperbolic volume decides nothing here.** Where a competing channel carries a hyperbolic constituent, volume settles the selection outright — the Borromean rings carry $8G$ and a torus link carries none. That is what happens one step earlier, at the antiquark disposal. That route is unavailable at step 4. All five non-identity [basis words](#fundamental-knots-operator-basis) are torus-type with $\mathrm{Vol} = 0$, so volume separates none of them, and the whole selection falls to $\mathrm{tr}_M$ and crossing count.

**Crossing-count open question.** Both nucleons carry two gluon crossings rather than a two-versus-four asymmetry. For the neutron braid $\mathcal{B}_3(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d})$, the eleven-crossing decomposition — six crossings from $\mathcal{B}_3$, three from the quark triad, and two from the gluon stabiliser — yields $\mathrm{net}(\mathfrak{n}) = +0.7976$ on the $k$-ladder (whereas thirteen crossings give $1.2359$). The [proton construction](#rem:categorical-proton-properties) likewise applies a single $L_- = \sigma_1\sigma_0$ of two crossings. Because both nucleons share the same gluon-crossing count, the mass splitting $\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{n}} - \mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}$ (and hence $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$) does not arise from a gluon-crossing difference.

A narrower discrepancy remains between the one-crossing and two-crossing stabiliser readings: under the minimum-crossing objective of [confinement-layer operator selection](#rem:confinement-layer-operator-selection), a single-crossing stabiliser ($\sigma_1$, crossing count $1$) strictly dominates a two-crossing stabiliser ($\sigma_0\sigma_0$ or $L_- = \sigma_1\sigma_0$, crossing count $2$), so the two counts cannot both arise from the same objective without an additional tie-breaking or parity constraint.

The feasible set itself is well defined and $e^-$ is strictly suboptimal within it; determining which stabiliser the canonical objective selects remains open.
