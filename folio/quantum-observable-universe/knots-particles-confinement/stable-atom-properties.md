**Independence of neighborhood.**
*Stability is a **fixed-$q$** property. It does not require the ILP to be feasible on a neighborhood of $q$. An atom may be stable at $q_0$ and unstable at $q_0 + \epsilon$ without contradiction. Stability tracks ILP-feasibility pointwise.*

**Connection to LP-feasibility.**
*The continuous LP relaxation ($x \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^N$, $\mathrm{G}(q) x \geq 0$) is always feasible, since the trivial identity normal form $T_e = e_{(k)}$ has $y_{(k)} = 0$. The ILP version is strictly stronger.*

**Confined operators only.**
*One solves the ILP within the confinement filtration ([confinement level](#def:confinement-level)) at $q$. One only considers primitives $e_\lambda$ in $J^{\ell(K, q)}(q)$, since operators above the atom's confinement level are unavailable without further phase transitions.*
