The computations rest on a purpose-built stack. The core is a Rust library for Hecke algebras (Gröbner–Shirshov reduction to normal form, seminormal representations, Markov traces), with bindings to Python and WebAssembly. Semidefinite and linear programs are solved with [a fork of the Clarabel interior-point solver](https://github.com/litlfred/Clarabel.rs) extended to higher-precision arithmetic, so that the dual variables, which carry structural information here, are certified rather than read from double-precision output. The Gröbner–Shirshov reductions are organised around the confinement structure of the bound states, which restricts the work to the blocks that structure leaves active; these are engineering choices, and no claim of novelty is made for them relative to the computer-algebra literature.

Expensive results are cached in the repository. Numerical witnesses are committed alongside the scripts that produce them, and prebuilt Lean libraries are kept on dedicated branches of the repository, so that the formalisation can be checked without rebuilding its dependencies from source.
