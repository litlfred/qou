The computations rest on a purpose-built stack. The core is a Rust library for Hecke algebras (Gröbner–Shirshov reduction to normal form, seminormal representations, Markov traces), with bindings to Python and WebAssembly. Semidefinite and linear programs are solved with [a fork of the Clarabel interior-point solver](https://github.com/litlfred/Clarabel.rs) extended to higher-precision arithmetic, so that the dual variables, which carry structural information here, are certified rather than read from double-precision output. Markov traces of long braid words are computed by peeling: the word is multiplied into the Hecke algebra one crossing at a time, reduced to Gröbner–Shirshov normal form after each step so the number of terms stays bounded, and the top generator is then removed with the Markov property, recursing down the tower $H_n(q) \supset H_{n-1}(q) \supset \cdots \supset H_1(q)$, so no matrix representation of the algebra is ever built. The confinement structure of the bound states restricts the remaining work to the blocks it leaves active. The Markov property and normal-form reduction are standard, and these are engineering choices: no claim of novelty is made for them relative to the computer-algebra literature.

Expensive results are cached in the repository. Numerical witnesses are committed alongside the scripts that produce them, and prebuilt Lean libraries are kept on dedicated branches of the repository, so that the formalisation can be checked without rebuilding its dependencies from source.
