**Conjecture (computational-time identification of the Schwinger variable).**

Let $s$ denote the Schwinger proper-time variable of the $\zeta$-regularized one-loop photon determinant on the Bring Jacobian, whose loop expansion weighs proper time by the scale-invariant measure $\varsigma\,\frac{ds}{s}$ with unit $\varsigma$. Let $N(n)$ denote the binding normalizer forced by the normalization bridge — the unique strip $N(n) = (n-1)!$ making the binding generating function the carrier of the proved depth-2 confluent kernel (the normalization leg of [The q-difference kernel R is the jet-prolongation symbol, carried by the binding q-lift module](#conj:qdifference-kernel-prolongation-symbol)).

The framework premises are named:

- **P1 (scale-freedom):** the substrate is scale-free; the locally realized modulus $q_0$ is local data, not a structural scale of the substrate.
- **P2 (computational time):** proper time is computational time — $s$ counts the substrate's computation steps.

**Statement.** *The binding sector's Borel variable and the loop's Schwinger proper time $s$ are the same substrate object.* Under P2 both are the substrate's computational time, and the identification takes the quantitative form: the normalizer is the $n$-th moment of the loop's proper-time measure,

$$
N(n) \;=\; \int_0^\infty s^{\,n}\, e^{-s}\,\varsigma\,\frac{ds}{s} \;=\; \varsigma\,(n-1)! .
$$

**Conditional consequences.** Both are conditional on this conjecture (together with P1/P2 where noted); neither is a derivation of it.

1. **The unit is forced: $\varsigma = 1$.** The proved identity $n \cdot N(n) = n!$ rescales under a unit $\varsigma$ to $\varsigma \cdot n! = n!$, so $\varsigma = 1$ exactly; the rival value $\varsigma = \tfrac12$ gives $n!/2 \neq n!$ (an exact distinguisher, proved unconditionally). Conditionally, this fixes the unit — one nat per proper-time e-fold, with $k_B = 1$ so that entropy is counted in nats.
2. **The $\tfrac12$ is located per-channel bar-even.** With total unit $\varsigma = 1$, the working per-channel coefficient $\tfrac12\,a_0 = 1/(8\pi)$ — where $a_0 = 1/(4\pi)$ is the derived two-dimensional heat-kernel unit entering the $\alpha_{EM}$ insertion ([$\alpha_{EM}$ as the root of the self-consistent fixed-point cubic](#prop:em-coupling-fixed-point)) — arises from the doubled-pair channel split. For the doubled bar pair $(q, q^{-1}) \leftrightarrow (t, 1/t)$ and a bar-even integrand $f$, i.e. $f(t) = f(1/t)$ under the bar involution $t \mapsto 1/t$, the exact fold identity $\int_0^\infty f(t)\,\tfrac{dt}{t} = 2\int_1^\infty f(t)\,\tfrac{dt}{t}$ places the $\tfrac12$ per channel on the bar-even side, while a bar-odd integrand receives $0$. This tips the $\tfrac12$-adjudication toward the $\rho$-split reading (total unit one, the $\tfrac12$ in the channel split) and against the energy-Jacobian reading ($\varsigma = \tfrac12$ on the proper-time side), which would break the moment identity.

The identification is a dictionary premise between two sectors — the $\alpha_{EM}$ loop measure and the binding $q$-Borel transform. It is stated here as a precise, falsifiable structural claim: a derivation would fix the unit; a refutation would rule the identification out. The two sectors would share one unit, and the binding side's is forced by a proved identity; the checkable content is the moment identity above, verified exactly at orders $n = 1$ through $6$.

**Fibre-level refinement (bar-parity).** The statement above is archimedean ($q = 1$), where the proper time $s$ is real and hence bar-invariant. At the fibre level ($q \neq 1$) the identification is consistent only in the *balanced* $q$-Borel convention: the one-sided normalizer $[n-1]_q!$ is bar-covariant with exact weight $q^{-(n-1)(n-2)/2}$, so it cannot equal a bar-invariant proper-time object, while the balanced normalizer $q^{-(n-1)(n-2)/4}\,[n-1]_q!$ is exactly bar-invariant with vanishing first-order $q$-correction. The conjecture is therefore to be read in the balanced convention; the one-sided reading is refuted at $q \neq 1$ (exact weight mismatch, machine-verified at both substrate pins).

**Structure of the open content.** The binding generating function enters this paper only formally — the proved normalizer strip, the proved depth-2 recurrence, and formal $q$-Borel coefficient maps; no binding number is produced by an integral resummation. The conjecture's representability content is therefore a claim about a *canonical choice* of integral representation for a formally performed operation, and under that reformulation its two levels separate into distinct problems.

- **Archimedean ($q = 1$): closed.** The proper-time kernel is derived, not chosen: $e^{-s}\,ds$ is the unique positive measure whose $\frac{ds}{s}$-moments are $(n-1)!$ (Carleman determinacy), and the explicitly constructed family of balanced fibre kernels converges weakly to it — tightness is proved from the second-moment confluence, so every Prokhorov subsequential limit is the Schwinger measure (conditional only on the classical Prokhorov-selection and moment-passage theorems, which are taken as hypotheses).
- **Fibre ($q \neq 1$): the choice has exact coordinates.** The balanced moment functional determines a canonical Jacobi operator $J(q)$ with no convention entering (Favard); the balanced moment problem is indeterminate at $q \neq 1$, $J(q)$ has deficiency indices $(1,1)$, and its self-adjoint extensions form a circle whose spectral measures are exactly the representing kernels. Three prior descriptions of the ambiguity are one object in three frames: the translate continuum $u \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}/\tilde q^{\,\mathbb{Z}}$ of theta-grid kernels ($\tilde q = \sqrt{q}$), the nonzero Poisson dual-mode sector of a single log-normal Gaussian — the atomic kernels are its lattice samplings, the absolutely continuous kernel its zero mode — and the self-adjoint-extension circle itself.
- **The closing question, named.** What remains open is the distinguished extension. The moment functional is Stieltjes, so the Friedrichs and Krein extensions are canonical candidates requiring no further input. The bar-involution route as first posed is closed in its pushforward reading: the reflection $s \mapsto \tilde q^{-1}/s$ does not act on the extension circle by pushforward at all — the image of an extension measure has bounded support, so its moments grow too slowly to represent the balanced sequence — hence it cannot exchange the extremal extensions in that reading; an action on the Nevanlinna parameter not induced by pushforward remains open. Two structural inputs replace it. First, the family is identified in the classical literature: the balanced kernel family factors exactly as a Stieltjes–Wigert log-normal core — Stieltjes' 1894 indeterminate example at base $\sqrt{q}$ — convolved with a determinate compact Jackson factor, and the one-sided convention is exactly Moak's $q$-Laguerre family under a pure scaling. Second, as measured: the Friedrichs and Krein atom supports occupy complementary $\tilde q$-sublattices whose common translate class extrapolates to the unit class $u \equiv 1$; the bar-fixed classes are proved to be exactly $\{1, \tilde q^{1/2}\}$, so the measured alignment lands on one of the two symmetric points. The sharpened open content: derive the extension choice (Friedrichs versus Krein) from substrate structure, or derive the $u \equiv 1$ alignment that is at present only measured.

The second route reduces to a single analytic statement. For the pure Stieltjes–Wigert core the extremal extensions are explicit: its Jacobi data have the closed form $\alpha_k = \tilde q^{\,k-1}(\tilde q^{\,k}(1+\tilde q) - 1)$, $\beta_{k+1} = \tilde q^{\,3k}(\tilde q^{\,k+1} - 1)$ — proved at general order (the iterated Jacobi vector in closed form, hence $(M^n)_{00} = \tilde q^{\,n(n-1)/2}$ exactly) — and the Nevanlinna function is the Ramanujan entire function $A_{\tilde q^{-1}}$ up to a constant, so the Krein extension is supported on its zeros and the Friedrichs extension on those zeros shifted by exactly one $\tilde q$-step; the two occupy complementary sublattices of the integer $\tilde q$-lattice class.

Transporting that class to the balanced family requires no perturbation argument. Comparing the two families through their moment ratios rather than their Jacobi data makes the transport exact: the factorization gives $m_{k+1}/m_k = \tilde q^{\,k} E (1 - p^{\,k+1})$ against the core's exact $\tilde q^{\,k}$, so the class shift is exactly $\log_{\tilde q} E$ with $E = q/(q-1)$, and the whole discrepancy is the single closed-form term $\log_{\tilde q}(1 - p^{\,k+1})$, which vanishes geometrically. Both ratio identities and the vanishing are proved. Hence the balanced tropical class is $\varphi_1 = \log_{\tilde q} E \bmod 1$ — the unit class — by derivation.

What remains is one analytic statement: that the extremal supports are asymptotic to those ratios. It is the same input the pure Stieltjes–Wigert core already requires (the asymptotics of the zeros of $A_{\tilde q^{-1}}$), so the transport introduces no further analytic debt. Numerically the conclusion holds to $8 \times 10^{-10}$ at $q = 2.0736$ and remains consistent, though not certified, on rungs approaching the substrate; at the pins the core certifies but the balanced truncation does not, so no class is claimed there.
