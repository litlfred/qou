The nuclear stability problem is a Diophantine problem on an algebraic curve. Stable hadrons are precisely the integer solutions to an equation derived from the Skein binding Lagrangian.

**The stability cubic.**

Substituting $t = A^{1/3}$ into the beta-stability line $p^*(A) = A/(2 + cA^{2/3})$ of [beta stability](#prop:beta-stability) and clearing denominators yields the stability cubic:

$$
  t^3 =  p(2 + ct^2),
  \qquad
  c = \frac{a_C}{2a_A}.
$$

This plane curve $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathbb{A}^2$ parametrizes the locus of beta-stable nuclei in the $(p, t)$-plane. The physical nuclei are the integer points of $\mathcal{S}$: pairs $(p, A) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ with $t = A^{1/3}$ and $A = t^3 \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Comparison with the color fibration.**

The color elliptic curve of Bring's surface is the cubic:

$$
  X^3 + (z + 1)X^2 + (z^2 + z + 1)X
  + (z^3 + z^2 + z + 1) = 0
$$

in the color variable $X$ at spacetime ratio $z$. The stability cubic and the color cubic are both one-variable cubics parametrized by another variable, arising from the same $\chi = -6$ surface. The elliptic stability conjecture posits that when the pairing energy lifts the stability cubic to genus $1$, the resulting elliptic curve is isogenous to the color fiber.

**The $n/p$ ratio.**

For each proton number $p$, the stable neutron number $n^*(p)$ satisfies:

$$
  \frac{n^*(p)}{p}
  = 
  \frac{A^*(p)}{p} - 1
  = 
  1 + c \cdot {A^*}^{2/3}
$$

where $A^*(p)$ solves $p = A/(2 + cA^{2/3})$. The ratio $n/p$ is a slowly growing function of $p$. Its integer-part transitions occur at specific elements.

**Stable hadrons as integer points on an algebraic curve.**

Every stable nucleus corresponds to a lattice point $(p, A) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ on the stability cubic $\mathcal{S}$. The nuclear chart is not a phenomenological fit; it is the set of integer solutions to an algebraic equation determined by the single parameter $q$.

When the pairing correction lifts $\mathcal{S}$ from genus $0$ to genus $1$, the stability pairs become rational points of an elliptic curve $\mathcal{E}_p$ over $\mathbb{Q}(q)$. Mordell's theorem constrains them to a finite set. This finite constraint potentially explains why only finitely many stability pairs exist.

**Connection to the aether parameter.**

The curvature parameter $c = \lambda_C / (2a_{\mathrm{asym}})$, with $\lambda_C = (q - q^{-1})[6]_q\,\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{e}}$, is controlled entirely by $q$. At $q = 1$, the stability cubic degenerates to $t^3 = 2p$, a rational parametrization where all nuclei are symmetric ($n = p$). As $q$ increases from $1$, the $ct^2$ term bends the curve. The integer solutions disperse. The structure of the nuclear chart—neutron excess, stability pairs, and even-odd staggering—emerges from a single deformation parameter.
