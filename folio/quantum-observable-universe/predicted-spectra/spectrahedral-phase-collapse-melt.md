## The Cosmological Melt

The rigid algebraic bounds of the Hecke representations govern the stability of matter. This section investigates the topological hierarchy determining when physical structures dissolve as a function of the substrate parameter $q$. This hierarchical decay constitutes the cosmological melt.

Covalent molecules define the lowest threshold of stability. The $H_2$ molecular bond has $k=7$ crossings across the atomic boundary. This forces a trace evaluation through the Wedderburn channels. The mixed-symmetry standard channel collapses completely at $q \approx 1.045$. Concurrently, the bond loses energetic stability against unbonded atomic components at $q \approx 1.17$. These thresholds mark the algebraic dissolution of molecular coherence.

Nucleons rely strictly on the $(2,1)$ partition of the $H_3(q)$ algebra. The interpreted proposition writes the Gram matrix in the square-root variable $u = \sqrt{q}$ and places its degeneracy at $u = 1/\psi \approx 0.68233$, where $\psi$ is the Supergolden Ratio — that is, at $q = 1/\psi^2 \approx 0.4656$. The $(2,1)$ Wedderburn weight is positive above this value and negative below it, so the positive-definite Gram requirement of the observable spectrahedron fails on the **low**-$q$ side. Hadronic matter cannot embed as a stable, unitary state below this threshold.

The two hadronic thresholds are ordered. Below the $(2,1)$ degeneracy lies the golden-ratio degeneration of the Descartes elliptic fibre at $q_{\mathrm{gold}} = 1/\varphi^2 \approx 0.382$, where $\varphi$ is the golden ratio. Both lie below the aeon boundary $q \to 1^+$, and both lie below the substrate $q_0 \approx 1.10998$, which the interpreted proposition places on the non-singular side of every zero of the Gram determinant. The molecular thresholds of the preceding paragraph lie above the substrate; molecular and hadronic dissolution are therefore approached along opposite directions in $q$, not as one monotone ladder.

Gauge bosons and leptons evade this structural collapse. The electron manifests as a topologically unknotted abelian braid. It projects purely into the 1-dimensional Wedderburn channels. These traces scale strictly as $q^n$. Their Gram matrices remain unconditionally positive-definite. These structures survive indefinitely across the entire $q > 0$ regime. At substrate parameters below the supergolden threshold the algebra retains only these one-dimensional channels, leaving a continuum of leptons and gauge connections.
