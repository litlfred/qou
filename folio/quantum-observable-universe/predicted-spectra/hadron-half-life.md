A nucleus displaced from the valley of stability ([integer stability](#prop:integer-stability)) undergoes beta decay. The substrate parameter $q$ ([substrate parameter](#def:substrate-parameter)) governs the half-life via the $q$-deformed transition amplitude.

**Proposition.**
Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be the Descartes universe with substrate parameter $q$, and let $\mathcal{N}(Z, N)$ ([stable nucleus](#def:stable-nucleus)) be a nucleus with proton displacement $\delta p = p - p_{\mathrm{stab}}(A)$ from the valley of stability. The beta-decay half-life is

$$
  t_{1/2}(p, A)
  = 
  \frac{\ln 2}{\Gamma_\beta(p, A)},
  \qquad
  \Gamma_\beta(p, A)
  = 
  \frac{|\mathcal{W}(q)|^2 \cdot \mu_0}{2\pi^3}
   \cdot 
  [2]_q
   \cdot 
  |\mathcal{M}_q|^2
   \cdot 
  f(Q),
$$

where $\mathcal{W}(q) = \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Markov}}((\sigma_1\sigma_2)^5)$ in $H_3(q)$ is the weak vertex form factor of the mediating torus knot $T_{3,5}$ ([beta decay as crossing change](#def:beta-decay-skein)); $[2]_q = q + q^{-1}$ is the $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ fundamental dimension; $|\mathcal{M}_q|^2$ is the parent/daughter Markov-trace ratio; and $f(Q) \propto Q^5$ is the Sargent phase-space integral in the $Q$-value ([transition energy](#def:Q-value-transition)). The decay rate depends only on $q$ and the dimensional scale $\mu_0$.
