## Mechanism

The Maxwell sector of the categorical Lagrangian restricts to the UV-completing Bring surface $M_4$. The surface carries the force-carrier pair $\{T_{2,9}, T_{3,5}\}$ ([$(T_{2,9}, T_{3,5})$ is the unique pair of genus-$4$ torus knots](#prop:force-carrier-bring-uniqueness)). The zero-momentum two-point function is governed by the kernel of the $1$-form Laplacian $\Delta_1$. By Hodge theory, $\dim\ker\Delta_1 = b_1(M_g) = 2g$. The heat-kernel zero-mode of a Laplacian contributes one constant mode $1/\mathrm{Vol}$ per harmonic generator. This yields an infrared shift of $b_1/\mathrm{Area}$. Gauss–Bonnet fixes the area at curvature $-1$ to $4\pi(g-1)$. The resulting infrared shift is $2g/(4\pi(g-1))$. It evaluates to $2/(3\pi)$ at $g=4$.

**Dimensional reduction.** A naive attempt places the zero-mode on the $3$-manifold $M_g\times S^1$. Here $b_1 = 2g+1$ and $\mathrm{Vol} = 2\pi\mathrm{Area}$. This gives $(2g+1)/(8\pi^2(g-1)) = 9/(24\pi^2) \approx 0.038$. Crossing with $S^1$ adds a spurious harmonic generator and a factor of $2\pi$ to the volume. The photon zero-mode lives strictly on the $2$-surface.

**Jet-order placement.** The Archimedean realisation decomposes into jet grades: $\tau = \sum_{n\ge 0} a_D^{(n)}$ ([The Seiberg–Witten differential is the jet decomposition of the Archimedean realisation](#prop:jet-sw-tau-decomposition)). The $n=0$ term is the electron period. The photon zero-mode forms the $n=1$ infrared term. This is the first prolongation beyond the tree coupling $q[9]_q[10]_q$. The $n=2$ self-energy takes the form $\alpha\,[2]_q/(8\pi)\,(1-h^2\alpha)$. Its denominator depends on the same Hodge datum:

$$
  8\pi \;=\; \pi\cdot\dim H^1(M_{\text{Bring}}) \;=\; \pi\,b_1.
$$

The Betti number $b_1 = 8$ controls both infrared corrections. The zero-mode carries it in the numerator ($b_1/\mathrm{Area}$). The self-energy carries it in the denominator ($\pi\,b_1$). The factors isolate the topological $b_1$ and the archimedean $\pi$. 

**Habiro structure.** The $n=2$ form groups as $\alpha\,H(q)/(\pi\,b_1)$, where $H(q) = [2]_q(1-h^2\alpha)$ is the $\alpha_{EM}$ Habiro element ([The $\alpha_{EM}$ Habiro element $[2]_q(1-h^2\alpha)$ is a bar-involution fixed point, with $H(1)=2$, $H(-1)=-2$](#prop:alpha-em-habiro-bar-involution)). Identity 2 acts as the Habiro deformation $(1-h^2\alpha)$. The $n=2$ self-energy decomposes into three sectors: substrate $H$, archimedean $\pi$, and topological $b_1$. This factorization connects the Hodge realization ($b_1/\mathrm{Area}$) to the dilogarithm realization via root-of-unity evaluations in the Habiro ring. It indicates a bridge to the Bloch group.
