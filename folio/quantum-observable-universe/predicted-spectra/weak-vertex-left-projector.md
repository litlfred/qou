Let $\sigma$ be a generator of $H_n(q)$, so that $\sigma^2 = h\,\sigma + 1$ with $h = q - q^{-1}$, and assume $q \neq 0$ and $[2]_q = q + q^{-1} \neq 0$. The generator has two eigenlines: the line on which it acts by $q$, with projector $P_+ = (\sigma + q^{-1})/[2]_q$, and the line on which it acts by $-q^{-1}$, with projector $P_- = (q - \sigma)/[2]_q$.

The **left line** $P_L$ is the eigenline on which $\sigma$ acts with an eigenvalue of modulus greater than $1$, and the right line $P_R = 1 - P_L$ is the other one. For $|q| > 1$ this gives $P_L = P_+$. The **weak vertex** $W$ is the element that acts as $\sigma^{-1}$ on the left line and as $\sigma$ on the right line:

$$
W \;=\; \sigma^{-1} P_L + \sigma\, P_R .
$$

**Proposition.** The difference between the weak vertex and the free crossing is a multiple of the left projector:

$$
W - \sigma \;=\; -(q - q^{-1})\, P_L .
$$

This holds for $|q| > 1$ and for $|q| \leq 1$. Three consequences follow.

1. The difference $W - \sigma$ annihilates the right line, and it acts on the left line by the scalar $-(q - q^{-1})$. It vanishes exactly when $q - q^{-1} = 0$.
2. An element $a + b\,\sigma$ annihilates the right line if and only if it is a scalar multiple of $P_L$. Within the algebra generated by one crossing, "the right line does not couple" and "proportional to $P_L$" are the same statement.
3. Let $t$ be the eigenvalue of $\sigma$ of modulus greater than $1$, whichever of $q$ and $-q^{-1}$ that is, and weight the positive crossing by it. Then the weighted crossing difference is

$$
t\,\sigma - t^{-1}\sigma^{-1} \;=\; (t - t^{-1})(t + t^{-1})\, P_L .
$$

So the choice of which crossing carries the weight, left open in [the force-carrier identification](#rem:force-knot-identification), is the same choice as the left line.

**Scope at the level of a knot.** The statement is about one crossing. It does not extend to the trace of the weak knot $T_{3,5}$, the closure of $(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^5$ in $H_3(q)$. Under the convention-D Markov trace $\tau_D$, the part of that trace on the right line of $\sigma_1$ is

$$
\tau_D\bigl((\sigma_1\sigma_2)^5\, P_R\bigr) \;=\; -\frac{q^2}{(q^2+1)^3} \;\neq\; 0 .
$$

At the proton parameter $q_0$ this is $-9.33\,\%$ of $\tau_D\bigl((\sigma_1\sigma_2)^5\bigr)$. All of it comes from the two-dimensional block of $H_3(q)$, because convention D gives the sign block weight zero. For the torus words $(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^N$ the right-line part repeats with period $3$ in $N$ and is never zero.

This is a property of every trace of this shape, not only of convention D. Write a trace on $H_3(q)$ as $\tau = y_{(3)}\chi_{(3)} + y_{(2,1)}\chi_{(2,1)} + y_{(1^3)}\chi_{(1^3)}$. Assume $|q| > 1$, so that $P_R = P_-$, and $[3]_q \neq 0$. Then $\tau(x P_R) = 0$ for every $x$ if and only if $y_{(2,1)} = y_{(1^3)} = 0$. With $\tau(1) = 1$, such a trace is the augmentation $\sigma_i \mapsto q$. It already suffices that $\tau$ vanish on $P_R$ and on $\sigma_2 P_R$. Convention D has $y_{(2,1)} \neq 0$, so no word-level one-sidedness is available under it.

**Where the weak vertex comes from.** The definition of $W$ rests on two inputs that are chosen, not derived.

1. *The one-sided requirement.* $W$ is required to act as $\sigma^{-1}$ on one eigenline of $\sigma$ and as $\sigma$ on the other. This is an ansatz.
2. *The side.* The eigenline on which $W$ acts as $\sigma^{-1}$ is the left line, by the definition of $P_L$ above.

Given the two inputs, $W$ is unique: an element $x$ with $x P_+ = a P_+$ and $x P_- = b P_-$ equals $a P_+ + b P_-$. With $J = P_+ - P_-$ and $|q| > 1$, the two one-sided elements are

$$
\sigma^{-1} P_+ + \sigma P_- \;=\; q^{-1} J, \qquad \sigma P_+ + \sigma^{-1} P_- \;=\; q\, J .
$$

So $W = q^{-1}J$, and the element with the opposite side, $qJ$, differs from it only by the scalar $q^2$. The Proposition above follows from the inputs and is, in this sense, close to a restatement of them.

**What this does not say.** It does not identify $W$ with the physical weak coupling.
