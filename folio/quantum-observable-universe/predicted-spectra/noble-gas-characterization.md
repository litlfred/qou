An atom $\mathcal{A}(p,n)$ constitutes a noble gas if and only if its electron cloud $p \cdot e^-$ decomposes into a disjoint union of closed sub-Hopf links. Every shell has no free linking site.

**Statement.**
Let $\mathcal{A}(p,n)$ be a stable atom with electron cloud $\mathcal{E} = p \cdot e^-$. The atom $\mathcal{A}$ is chemically inert (noble) if and only if a partition

$$
  \mathcal{E} = S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup \cdots \sqcup S_k
$$

exists satisfying:

1. **Closure**: Each $S_i = 2n_i^2 \cdot e^-$ forms a closed sub-Hopf link at level $n_i$. The electron chain within $S_i$ admits no additional linking without increasing total linking number.
2. **Exhaustion**: Every electron belongs to an $S_i$. No unlinked electrons remain.
3. **Monotone nesting**: The sequence $n_1 < n_2 < \cdots < n_k$ holds. The linking of $S_{i+1}$ threads through $S_i$ without disrupting its closure.
