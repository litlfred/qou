**Remark (Topological back-reaction).** The signed-crossing excess $\binom{p}{2} - \binom{n}{2}$ acts as a topological self-deformation of the aether field. Nuclear linking density $\ell_{\mathrm{net}}/A^2$ drives this. This provides a geometric mechanism for nuclear self-correction.

## Effect on beta decay: the differential shift

In beta decay, $A$ remains fixed while $(p, n)$ shifts by $(+1, -1)$. The daughter nucleus exhibits a different self-deformation from the parent. The change in effective $q$ across the decay is

$$
  \Delta q_{\mathrm{eff}}
  = 
  \delta q(p+1, n-1) - \delta q(p, n)
  = 
  \frac{A - 1}{A^2} \cdot \hbar_q^2.
$$

This shift is strictly positive. The daughter acquires one more proton and thus more positive crossings, yielding a higher effective $q$; a higher $q$ increases the crossing asymmetry, giving a larger $Q$-value ([transition energy](#def:Q-value-transition)) than predicted by the background alone. The Sargent rule $f(Q) \propto Q^5$ applies, so the enlarged $Q$-value increases the decay rate and shortens the half-life.

The shift $\Delta q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is exactly the $q$-anomaly $\delta_q$ of the transition energy: the daughter is evaluated at the shifted $q + \Delta q_{\mathrm{eff}}$, not the background $q$. This embodies $q$-deformed energy balance, overriding classical conservation.

## Effect on the decay rate

The differential shift $\Delta q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ modifies the effective aether parameter at the converting nucleon. The decay rate depends on $q$ with sensitivity $\approx 48$ ([half-life regimes](#rem:hadron-half-life-regimes)). A shift of $\Delta q / q_0 \sim 0.1$--$0.4\%$ induces a measurable change in the decay rate.

## Universality

The self-deformation applies whenever $q$ acts on a specific hadron configuration. This extends beyond half-lives: the beta-stability line ([beta-stability line](#prop:beta-stability)) and the topological mass formula receive identical structural corrections when $q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ replaces $q_0$.
