The leading-order half-life formula ([hadron half-life](#prop:hadron-half-life)) evaluates the Skein polynomial on the nucleus alone. It treats the emitted electron as a free particle. However, the electron $e^-$ ([electron](#def:electron-knot)) traverses the Coulomb field of $Z$ protons as it exits the nucleus. Each electron-proton crossing carries a Skein weight. Evaluate the Skein polynomial on the full outgoing link $e^- \cup \mathcal{N}(Z, N)$. This generates the Coulomb enhancement.

Evaluate precisely the same Skein polynomial on the complete outgoing link rather than truncating at the weak vertex.

**Magnitude.**
Average the integrated Coulomb enhancement over the electron energy spectrum. This yields a large, $Z$-dependent factor.

These enhancements change decay rates by factors of 1.4 to 5. Shell closures, pairing, and deformation modify the Gamow--Teller strength $\sigma_{\mathrm{GT}}$ at the individual-nucleus level.

**Transition classes.**
The nuclear matrix element $|\mathcal{M}_q|^2$ varies by six orders of magnitude across the verification set. Measured $\log ft$ values (NNDC/ENSDF) classify the transitions.

See [Table: Beta-decay classification](#tbl:skein-coulomb-enhancement-table2-data).

The QOU framework dictates the $q$-dependent modulation of each transition class via $[2]_q$, $\tanh(\ln q)$, and $\sigma_{\mathrm{GT}}$. It does not predict the absolute matrix element from the leading-order Hecke computation alone. Predicting $|\mathcal{M}_q|^2$ for individual nuclei requires resolving the discrete shell structure. The precise quantum numbers of the parent and daughter nuclear states must be specified.

**Predictions of the QOU.**
Within a single transition class of fixed $\log ft$, the QOU correctly predicts:

1. **The $q$-dependent scale**: The factor $(q + q^{-1})(q - q^{-1})^5$ sets the overall decay-rate scale entirely from $q$.
2. **The $A$-dependence**: This gives $t_{1/2} \propto A^{5/3}$ at fixed $|\delta p|$ and fixed transition class.
3. **The $q$-sensitivity**: The prediction is $\partial \ln \Gamma / \partial \ln q \approx 48$, without free parameters.
4. **The Coulomb enhancement structure**: The term $F_q(Z, E)$ emerges directly from the Skein crossing weights. This unifies the electromagnetic and weak interactions through the single deformation parameter $q$.
5. **The self-deformation**: A differential $\Delta q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ correction induced by the hadron linking structure is observed.
