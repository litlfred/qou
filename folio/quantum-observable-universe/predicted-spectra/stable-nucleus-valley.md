**Restricting the hadron definition.**
The abstract hadron ([hadron](#def:hadron)) permits arbitrary multi-component composites. The stability condition restricts the physically realised nuclei to the **valley of stability**. Only configurations $(Z, N)$ satisfying the beta-stability equation of the [stable nucleus](#def:stable-nucleus) ([beta-stability](#prop:beta-stability)) are long-lived. Nuclei outside this valley undergo beta decay ($\mathfrak{n} \to \mathfrak{p} + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$ or $\mathfrak{p} \to \mathfrak{n} + e^+ + \nu_e$) until they reach the stable configuration.

This is not an additional axiom. It is a strict *consequence* of the Skein binding Lagrangian. The parameter $q$ determines both the strong binding (via $(q + q^{-1})/2$) and the Coulomb repulsion (via $(q - q^{-1})/2$). Their balance selects the unique stable $(Z, N)$ for each mass number $A$.

**Closed form of the critical point.** The summary reading of the [stable nucleus](#def:stable-nucleus) is a multi-nucleon Hopf link that extremizes the Skein binding Lagrangian. By [beta-stability](#prop:beta-stability), its criticality condition is equivalent to

$$
  Z = \frac{A}{2 + a_C  A^{2/3} / (2a_A)},
$$

where $a_C = (q - q^{-1}) \cdot \lambda_C$ and $a_A$ is the Pauli coefficient. The letter $Z$ is the standard nuclear-physics notation for the proton count, which frees $p$ for use as a brane point.
