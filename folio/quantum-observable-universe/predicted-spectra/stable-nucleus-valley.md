**Restricting the hadron definition.**
The abstract hadron ([hadron](#def:hadron)) permits arbitrary multi-component composites. The stability condition restricts the physically realised nuclei to the **valley of stability**. Only configurations $(Z, N)$ satisfying the beta-stability equation of the [stable nucleus](#def:stable-nucleus) ([beta-stability line](#prop:beta-stability)) are long-lived. Nuclei outside this valley undergo beta decay ($\mathfrak{n} \to \mathfrak{p} + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$ or $\mathfrak{p} \to \mathfrak{n} + e^+ + \nu_e$) until they reach the stable configuration.

This is not an additional axiom. It is a strict *consequence* of the Skein binding Lagrangian. The parameter $q$ determines both the strong binding (via $(q + q^{-1})/2$) and the Coulomb repulsion (via $(q - q^{-1})/2$). Their balance selects the unique stable $(Z, N)$ for each mass number $A$.
