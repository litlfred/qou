For a field $F$, the Bloch group $B(F)$ admits an explicit quotient presentation

$$
  B(F) \;:=\; \mathbb{Z}\bigl[\mathcal{E}(F)\bigr]
            \;\big/\; \mathcal{P}(F),
$$

where $\mathcal{E}(F) = F \setminus \{0, 1\}$ is the set of pre-Bloch elements and $\mathcal{P}(F)$ is the subgroup generated by the five-term pentagon relations on the free abelian group $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{E}(F)]$. The quotient inherits an abelian group structure together with a canonical projection $\pi \colon \mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{E}(F)] \to B(F)$ satisfying the universal property of quotient abelian groups.

This quotient yields a concrete abelian group rather than an abstract axiomatic postulate, with the real specialization $B(\mathbb{R})$ non-trivially inhabited as a carrier.

An unconstrained five-tuple parametrization $\mathcal{E}(F)^5$ of pentagon generators would make the relation subgroup $\mathcal{P}(F)$ too large: degenerate shapes with repeated entries collapse the alternating sum to a single generator $[e_1]$, trivializing the quotient. Non-triviality requires restricting the five-tuples to valid Rogers shapes satisfying

$$
  e_3 \;\equiv\; e_2 / e_1, \quad
  e_4 \;\equiv\; (1 - e_2^{-1})/(1 - e_1^{-1}), \quad
  e_5 \;\equiv\; (1 - e_2)/(1 - e_1).
$$

These relations arise from a Rogers pair $(x, y) \in F^\times \times F^\times$ with $x \neq y$, $x \neq 0$, $x \neq 1$, $y \neq 0$, and $y \neq 1$. Enforcing them requires the domain-inequality conditions on $F$, the Rogers shape map $(x, y) \mapsto (e_1, \dots, e_5)$, and the restriction of the generator set $\mathcal{G}(F)$ to valid Rogers five-tuples. The quotient presentation accommodates this restriction directly through the constrained generator set.

Combining this quotient construction with the archimedean functor uniqueness framework isolates the analytic inputs needed for Bloch–Suslin uniqueness: the five-term pentagon identity for $\mathrm{Cl}_2$ on the unit circle, injectivity of the rationalized Bloch–Wigner regulator $D \otimes \mathbb{Q}$, and the Suslin homomorphism $K_3^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathbb{C}) \to \mathbb{R}$.
