## Established identifications and the residual bridge

The numerical identity $m_p = 2\,m_\mu F_{\mathrm{Bor}}(q_0) - 12\,m_e$ holds to $0.24\,\mathrm{ppm}$ at $50$ decimal places, and each factor has an established categorical *identification*:

- $F_{\mathrm{Bor}}(q) = |\mathrm{tr}_M(\beta_{\mathrm{Bor}}, q)| \cdot [3]_q!/q^3$ is the Borromean Markov-trace observable (categorical, [the Borromean observable](#def:f-bor-borromean-observable));
- the transpose-pair identity $y_{(3)}(q)\cdot y_{(1,1,1)}(q) = 1/[3]_q!^2$ holds identically as a rational function of $q$;
- the integer $12 = 2\cdot 6 = \mathrm{mult}\cdot\mathrm{bind}$ is a categorical count ($|S_3|\cdot\dim V_{(2,1)}$), and not the inverse of an Ocneanu leading-pole coefficient $-\tfrac{1}{12}$: the Markov trace of $\beta_{\mathrm{Bor}}$ has a $q^{-4}$ pole of coefficient $1$, not the $q^{-10}\cdot(-\tfrac{1}{12})$ of the measured no-interface braid. That identification is refuted for $\beta_{\mathrm{Bor}}$ and is not part of the derivation.

What remains **conjectural** is the *bridge* from these identifications to the assembly law above — specifically why the trace-dominant irrep's dimension $\dim V_{(2,1)}$ enters as a **linear** multiplier and $|S_3|$ as the electron-binding count. The Wedderburn block expansion of the Markov trace (the $(2,1)$ block carries $\approx 49\%$ of the proton's $T_1$ contribution) is the natural framework, while the categorical derivation of the assembly law is open. Under these two structural hypotheses and the factored assembly law, the [proton-mass aether identity](#prop:proton-mass-substrate-identity) yields the flat expression directly.
