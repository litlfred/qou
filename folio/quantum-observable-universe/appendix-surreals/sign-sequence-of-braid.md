Let $V$ be the fundamental representation of $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$, and let $\beta = \sigma_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1} \cdots \sigma_{i_k}^{\epsilon_k}$ be a braid word in $B_n$. Fix a standard tableau $t$ of shape $\lambda \vdash n$ with $\ell(\lambda) \leq 2$. The **sign-sequence surreal** $\mathrm{surr}(\beta, t) \in \mathbf{No}$ of $\beta$ at $t$ is constructed as follows.

The Hecke representation $\rho_n : B_n \to H_n(q)$ sends each $\sigma_i^{\pm 1}$ to $\sigma_i^{\pm 1}$. The Hoefsmit seminormal vector $v_t \in S_\lambda$ at $t$ is an eigenvector of every Young--Jucys--Murphy element. The braid $\beta$ acts on $v_t$ by successively decomposing $\rho(\sigma_{i_j}^{\epsilon_j})\, v_{t_j}$ into symmetric and antisymmetric components, recording $s_j = +$ for the symmetric component and $s_j = -$ for the antisymmetric. The resulting sign sequence $s = (s_1, \ldots, s_k)$ defines the finite-birthday surreal $\mathrm{surr}(\beta, t) := \mathrm{Gonshor}(s) \in \mathbf{No}$, where $\mathrm{Gonshor}: \{+, -\}^* \to \mathbf{No}$ is the canonical sign-sequence-to-surreal map.

For each fixed standard tableau $t$, the assignment $\mathrm{surr}(-, t) : B_n \to \mathbf{No}$ is a multiplicative monoid map: $\mathrm{surr}(\beta_1 \beta_2, t)$ is computed by first applying $\beta_2$'s sign sequence at $t$ to obtain $t'$, then concatenating with $\beta_1$'s sign sequence at $t'$. Changing the tableau permutes the sign sequence by a Young--Jucys--Murphy permutation. Quotienting by this action yields a tableau-independent functor $\widetilde{\mathrm{surr}} : B_n \longrightarrow \mathbf{No} / \sim$.
