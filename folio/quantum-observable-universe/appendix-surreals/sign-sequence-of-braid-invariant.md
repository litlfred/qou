A surreal number $x \in \mathbf{No}$ admits a canonical sign-sequence presentation: a function $s_x : \alpha \to \{+, -\}$ for some ordinal $\alpha = b(x)$. The sequence encodes Conway's $\{L \mid R\}$ construction step-by-step: at stage $\beta < \alpha$, $s_x(\beta) = +$ means "$x$ is the rightmost option", and $s_x(\beta) = -$ means "leftmost option". Hecke generators have two eigenvalues, $q$ (symmetric channel) and $-q^{-1}$ (antisymmetric channel) ([Hoefsmit seminormal form](#prop:hoefsmit-seminormal-form)). This binary choice at each level is exactly the sign-sequence data, providing a canonical functor from braid words to surreals.

The sign sequence has length equal to the crossing number $c(\beta)$, so $b(\mathrm{surr}(\beta, t)) \leq c(\beta)$. Equality holds when the braid is reduced. This cleanly refines the crossing-number invariant by an ordinal-valued quantity.

The sign sequence carries strictly more information than the crossing number: it records which eigenchannel is chosen at each crossing. Distinct braids with the same crossing number generally give distinct surreals. Surrealisation is a stronger braid invariant valued in $\mathbf{No} / \sim$. It is the precise sense in which "Hoefsmit seminormal form is the surreal $\{L \mid R\}$ construction".
