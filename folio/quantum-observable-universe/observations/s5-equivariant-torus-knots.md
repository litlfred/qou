*Let $\Sigma$ be a Riemann surface with automorphism group $\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma) = S_5$ ([Bring's surface](#def:brings-surface)). A torus knot $T_{2, s}$ with $s \geq 3$ coprime to $2$ admits an equivariant embedding into $\Sigma$ if and only if*

$$
  s  \in  \{3,\, 5\}.
$$

*An equivariant embedding preserves the intrinsic cyclic symmetry $\mathbb{Z}_s$ of the knot.*

*Two tests.* The fibration of $T_{2,s}$ has monodromy of order $2s$. For odd $s$ the monodromy group is $\mathbb{Z}_{2s} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_s$, the two rotations of the torus knot taken together. The criterion above is the **$\mathbb{Z}_s$ test**: it asks only that the $\mathbb{Z}_s$ rotation be realised in $S_5$. The stronger **monodromy test** asks that the whole $\mathbb{Z}_{2s}$ be realised, that is, that $S_5$ contain an element of order $2s$. This holds if and only if

$$
  s = 3.
$$

*Under the monodromy test only $T_{2,3}$ passes; $T_{2,5}$ fails because $S_5$ has no element of order $10$. The $\mathbb{Z}_s$ test is the weaker of the two, and a statement that a knot is $S_5$-equivariant should say which test it uses.*
