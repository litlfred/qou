Because binding energy $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{bind}} = \operatorname{Re}\mathfrak{V}$ and decay endpoint $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta = \operatorname{Im}\mathfrak{V}$ are the real and imaginary parts of a single complex volume $\mathfrak{V}$, local maxima of nuclear binding at closed magic shells coincide with sharp discontinuities in $\beta$-decay $Q$-values and half-lives.

This section examines the complex-volume signature of magic shells: the anomalous longevity of ${}^{14}\mathrm{C}$ relative to the maximal-braid ${}^{14}\mathrm{N}$ ground state, the Markov trace-ratio observable across verification nuclei, and the diagrammatic Littlewood–Richardson fusion rules that enforce channel exclusion in doubly magic ${}^{40}\mathrm{Ca}$.
