In the torus-knot quark classification, the strange quark $\mathfrak{s} = T_{2,5}^\sigma$ carries five crossings whereas the down quark $\mathfrak{d} = T_{2,3}^\sigma$ carries three. Although the additional crossings increase the local strong pairing energy by a factor of $4/3$ per baryon and render equal-flavour $(\mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{d}, \mathfrak{s})$ matter electrically neutral ($Q = 0$), the cinquefoil Jones polynomial at $q_0 \approx 1.1100$ carries a skein-energy cost roughly $93$ times larger than the crossing-energy gain, ensuring that ordinary non-strange nuclear matter is the true ground state at zero external pressure.

Inside the cores of massive neutron stars, degenerate Fermi pressure overcomes this skein-energy threshold and drives a three-stage topological phase transition:

1. **Stage 1 — Borromean dissolution ($\rho \gtrsim 2\rho_0$):** Under extreme baryon chemical potential, the three-strand Borromean confinement links $\mathcal{B}_3$ (whose hyperbolic complement volume $\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}_3) = 8G \approx 7.32772$ carries $99\%$ of the nucleon rest mass) begin to delocalize.
2. **Stage 2 — Hopf diquark condensation ($\rho \gtrsim 5\rho_0$):** Liberated quark strands re-pair into two-component Hopf links $\mathcal{L}_H$ with linking number $\pm 1$, whose antisymmetric colour–flavour orientation generates the color-flavor-locked (CFL) superconducting gap $\Delta_{\mathrm{CFL}}$ \cite{alford1999,witten1984}. Because the Hopf link complement is Seifert-fibered with $\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{L}_H) = 0$, the transition from Borromean baryons ($749$ skein units) to Hopf diquarks ($3$ skein units)—a factor of $250$ in binding scale—drops the effective carrier mass while stiffening the equation of state toward the conformal sound-speed limit $c_s^2 = 1/3$.
3. **Stage 3 — The golden-ratio deconfinement wall ($q_{\mathrm{local}} \to q_{\mathrm{gold}} = 1/\varphi^2$):** At the golden-ratio threshold, the elliptic fibre of Bring's surface degenerates and Milnor's triple linking invariant $\bar\mu(123)$ jumps discontinuously from $1$ (Borromean) to $0$ (Hopf), producing sharp observable signatures in neutron-star mass–radius curves, binary merger gravitational-wave post-merger frequencies, and double-beta decay pathways.
