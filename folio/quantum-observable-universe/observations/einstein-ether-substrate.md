Einstein, in his 1920 Leiden address \cite{einstein1920}, characterised the relativistic ether by arguing that physics may retain an ether provided one gives up "ascribing a definite state of motion to it", abstracting away "the last mechanical characteristic which Lorentz had still left it", a standpoint "justified by the results of the general theory of relativity."

What it carries instead is the braided monoidal algebra: the Hecke deformation parameter governing braid-group representations.

Where Lorentz's medium supplied a preferred rest frame (and was therefore excluded by special relativity), the Frobenius–Hopf aether supplies only a preferred algebraic deformation class. The aether parameter $q_0$ is fixed by the cohomological structure of $H^1(\mathrm{Bring}) = 2W$, the first cohomology of Bring's surface, rather than by a mechanical medium at rest. It is a categorical rather than a mechanical invariant.

This reading aligns directly with Einstein's conclusion that physics retains an aether once every mechanical state of motion is removed: the Frobenius–Hopf aether is a relational algebraic invariant fixed by the global structure of the observable category rather than a local mechanical datum.
