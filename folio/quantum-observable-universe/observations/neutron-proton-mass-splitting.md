*Assume $(\mathrm{H}_\alpha)$: a change of the quark-ordering traced volume $\Phi$ is converted to energy by the nuclear binding scale $\alpha = m_e \times 8G$, as $\alpha\,\lvert\Delta\Phi\rvert$. Under $(\mathrm{H}_\alpha)$, the model neutron--proton mass difference is*

$$
  \Delta m
  := \alpha \cdot
    \bigl\lvert
      \Phi(\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{d}) -
      \Phi(\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{d})
    \bigr\rvert
  \approx 1.054 \;\mathrm{MeV}
$$

*where $\alpha = m_e \times 8G$ is the binding scale and $\Phi$
is the quantum dilogarithm volume.*

The volumes are the traced-volume closed forms evaluated at the canonical
Markov parameter $z_D = q^3/(q^2+1) = 0.6126880371$ at $q_0$, pairing each
strand with every later strand. This gives
$\Phi(\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{d}) \approx -0.614886$,
$\Phi(\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{d}) \approx -0.896395$ and
$\lvert\Delta\Phi\rvert \approx 0.281509$, so with $\alpha = 3.7445$ the model
value is $\Delta m \approx 1.0541$ MeV.

**Comparison with measurement.** The CODATA splitting is
$m_{\mathfrak{n}} - m_{\mathfrak{p}} \approx 1.29333$ MeV, so the model value
is about $18.5\%$ below it. This comparison is the empirical claim of the
block. For historical comparison only: the earlier value used the retired
convention-C parameter $z = 4993/10000$ (the rounded $\sqrt{q}/(q+1)$) and gave
$1.302$ MeV, about $0.64\%$ above the measured value. That figure is not the
model's value, and a closer residual is not evidence for a convention.

**A second model value.** The corpus has a second, independent route to the
splitting. The T³ weight split of the $z_D$ trace on the $\gamma\gamma$
nucleon gives $Q_\beta = \lvert t_{\mathfrak n} - t_{\mathfrak p}\rvert \cdot E_0
\approx 0.3254$ MeV, where $E_0 = m_e/(q(q-1))$ (tower-summed) is the corpus's
derived hadronic scale. The decay kinematics
$m_{\mathfrak n} - m_{\mathfrak p} = Q_\beta + m_e$ then give
$\Delta m \approx 0.8364$ MeV, about $35.3\%$ below the measured value. That
route uses $E_0$ in place of $(\mathrm{H}_\alpha)$. It fixes the size, not the
sign: on its own it does not make the neutron the heavier nucleon. The two
values come from different constructions, and neither is selected by its
residual.

That is a claim about this construction, and not an exclusion: nothing here
shows a quark-ordering volume cannot carry the splitting.

**Two-term charge-insertion probe (record, not a derivation).** The following facts about the 3-strand weight spaces $W_p = (2,1)$ and $W_n = (1,2)$ are proved: the quark-charge operators $Q_iQ_j$ break the intertwiner $P(q)$ between the two nucleon weight spaces; $\mathrm{tr}(e_{(2,1)} V^3) = 2 - 3h^4 - h^6$ on the Borromean word $V^3 = (\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})^3$; and the closed forms of the adjacent-pair traces. These are statements about a charge insertion. Two features of how they were turned into masses are choices, not derivations:

- **The braid content comes only from omitting a pair.** Summed over all three quark pairs, the charge insertion differs between $W_n$ and $W_p$ by $-\tfrac13\,\mathrm{tr}(w)$ for every word $w$; that is static charge counting, with no braid dependence. The word-dependent "connected" term keeps the pairs $1$–$2$ and $2$–$3$ and drops $1$–$3$. The Borromean word does not single out the adjacent pairs, since each of its three pairs crosses twice.
- **The scale is chosen.** The figures were formed with $m_e$, with $E_0 = m_e/(q(q-1))$ (a definition), or with $\alpha_{\mathrm{bind}}$, under four weightings (plain, $K_n/K_p$, $K_p^3$, $q$), and compared against the $\mu$–$p$ tension, the $Q_\beta$ residual and lattice benchmarks.

So the keV, ppm and ppb values recorded with these files describe selected combinations, are not predictions, and are not scored. The splitting still requires a quantity the framework does not yet derive.
