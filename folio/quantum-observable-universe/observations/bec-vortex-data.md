Bose--Einstein condensates (BECs) provide the cleanest experimental realisation of quantised vortex dynamics \cite{fetter2009,navon2021}.

**1. Quantised circulation.**
In ${}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ and ${}^{23}\mathrm{Na}$ BECs, vortex circulation is measured to be $\Gamma_n = n \cdot h/m$, with $n$ an integer \cite{madison2000}. No intermediate values have ever been observed. In the QOU framework, this is the $q \to 1$ limit of the $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ Casimir quantisation. The spin label $j$ reduces to the winding number $n$, and $[j]_q[j+1]_q \to j(j+1) = n(n+1)$ gives the discrete circulation levels. The BEC data confirms the selection rule $|\Delta n| = 1$ predicted by the ladder-mode transition mechanism.

**2. Vortex lattice formation.**
Above a critical rotation frequency $\Omega_c$, BECs nucleate regular lattices of singly-quantised vortices \cite{abo-shaeer2001}. The number of vortices follows the Feynman rule $N_v = m \Omega A / (\pi\hbar)$, where $A$ is the condensate area. This is consistent with the $q \to 1$ limit of the $q$-Feynman rule, in which the vortex count is controlled by $\hbar_q$.

**3. Kelvin-mode excitations.**
Bretin et al. \cite{bretin2003} observed quadrupole oscillations of a single vortex in a ${}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ condensate, identified as the lowest Kelvin mode ($k = 1$). The measured frequency $\omega_1^{\text{exp}} = 2\pi \times (6.2 \pm 0.1)$ Hz matches the Kelvin dispersion relation. The $q$-deformed prediction replaces $k^2$ with $[k]_q^2 = [1]_q^2 = 1 + O((q-1)^2)$. This is indistinguishable from the classical value at laboratory $q$.

**4. Vortex clustering and turbulence.**
Gauthier et al. \cite{gauthier2019} observed giant vortex clusters in 2D ${}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ condensates, where same-sign vortices aggregate into macroscopic circulation structures. The cluster circulation is quantised in multiples of the single-vortex quantum. This directly demonstrates the additive structure $j_{\text{cluster}} = j_1 + j_2 + \cdots$. In the $q$-deformed framework, cluster formation corresponds to the tensor product of $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ representations. The total spin $j_{\text{total}}$ follows the Clebsch--Gordan decomposition of $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$.
