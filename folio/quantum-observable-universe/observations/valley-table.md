[Table: Representative stable isotopes](#tbl:valley-table-data) shows representative elements spanning the periodic table. It compares the continuous beta-stability prediction $p^*(A)$ ([beta-stability line](#prop:beta-stability)) with the observed stable proton number, the neutron-to-proton ratio, and the Diophantine error $\varepsilon = |p_{\mathrm{stab}} - p^*|$.

**Observations.**

1. **Light nuclei** ($A \leq 40$). The ratio $n/p \approx 1$. The Coulomb correction $c \cdot A^{2/3}$ is small. The curve coincides with the diagonal $p = A/2$.
2. **Medium nuclei** ($40 < A \leq 120$). The ratio $n/p$ rises from $1.0$ to $1.4$. The Coulomb term bends the curve below the diagonal, requiring extra neutrons.
3. **Heavy nuclei** ($A > 120$). The ratio $n/p \to 1.6$. The $p^2$ growth of the Coulomb penalty dominates. The curve flattens.
4. **Stability pairs** ($\diamond$, $\star$). Helium, calcium, tin, and lead have anomalously low Diophantine error and closed Skein shell structure. The doubly-magic stable nuclei ($^4$He, $^{40}$Ca, $^{208}$Pb) combine $\varepsilon < 0.8$ with shell closure.
5. **Iron peak**. The maximum binding energy per nucleon $B/A \approx 8.79$ MeV occurs at $^{56}$Fe. Here the competition between volume binding ($\propto A$) and Coulomb repulsion ($\propto p^2/A^{1/3}$) is optimally balanced.

The continuous curve $p^*(A)$ and the integer lattice points $(p_{\mathrm{stab}}, n_{\mathrm{stab}})$ are illustrated in the [valley of stability simulator](#sim:valley-stability).
