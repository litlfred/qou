At $q = q_0$, write the beta-stability line of [beta stability](#prop:beta-stability) in the variable $t = A^{1/3}$:

$$
  t^3 = p\,(2 + ct^2),
  \qquad c = \frac{\lambda_C}{2a_{\mathrm{asym}}} \approx 0.0145158337, \quad A = t^3,
$$

with $c$ the asymmetry constant of [neutron prediction comparison](#prop:neutron-prediction-comparison). The [Diophantine valley](#rem:diophantine-valley) writes the same curve as $t^3 = 2p(1 + ct^2)$, which carries twice this $ct^2$ term; the table below uses the form above.

See [Table](#tbl:stability-cubic-plot-table-data).

The predicted neutron number is $N_{\mathrm{pred}} = \operatorname{round}(A^*) - p$, where $A^*$ is the real root of the cubic, and $N_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is the neutron number of the most abundant isotope (NUBASE2020), or of the longest-lived ground state when there is none. Over the fourteen rows shown, $\Delta N = |N_{\mathrm{pred}} - N_{\mathrm{obs}}|$ is at most $5$ (at barium) with mean $31/14 \approx 2.21$; over all elements $p \le 103$ the mean is $\approx 1.8155$ and the maximum is $7$, at tellurium.

The Coulomb term $a_C \cdot p^2/A^{1/3}$ penalizes proton-rich nuclei. The predicted neutron excess $N_{\mathrm{pred}}/p - 1$ is $0\%$ at helium and carbon and grows to $55\%$ at uranium (observed $59\%$).
