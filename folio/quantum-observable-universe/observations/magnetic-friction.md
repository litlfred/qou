Recent experiments by Gu, Luders, and Bechinger demonstrate that a two-dimensional array of rotatable magnetic dipoles sliding over a commensurate magnetic aether exhibits a pronounced non-monotonic dependence of friction on interlayer separation. Friction peaks at an intermediate distance. Here, competing ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic interactions induce dynamical frustration and hysteretic torque cycles during sliding.

This collective rotor phenomenon admits a natural explanation within the QOU framework. The competing magnetic alignments realise a discrete analogue of $q$-torsion frustration. This frustration arises when a lattice's translational symmetry is incommensurate with the characteristic scale of the $q$-torsion leaves ([$q$-torsion leaf](#ex:q-torsion-leaf)). In this reading, the non-monotonic friction profile reflects a resonance--anti-resonance transition. The local value of the aether parameter $q$ ([aether parameter](#def:substrate-parameter)) governs this transition. Maximal dissipation occurs at the separation where the rotor array's magnetic periodicity matches the $q$-harmonic form spectrum ([$q$-harmonic form](#def:q-harmonic-form)).
