Double-slit interferometry provides a direct experimental probe of the $q$-deformed propagation law. A complete quantum description must account simultaneously for the fine interference fringes, the macroscopic diffraction envelope, and the loss of phase coherence under path detection. On the categorical substrate, the diffraction pattern separates into two distinct propagation regimes according to whether the incident state carries hyperbolic volume:

1. **Massive particles (trefoil and hyperbolic closures):** A massive fermion propagates via the full $q$-Laplacian ([Particle ($q$-harmonic form)](#def:q-harmonic-form)), whose cross-coupling terms mix spatial translation with the internal Reeb fibre to produce a $q$-deformed $\mathrm{sinc}^2$ diffraction envelope.
2. **Photons (exceptional-divisor flux modes):** A photon is a volumeless excitation on the exceptional divisor ([Exceptional divisor at $p$](#def:exceptional-divisor)) carrying the two-strand Hecke colour content $\gamma = \mathbf{1} + \varphi_0\sigma_0 + \varphi_1\sigma_1 + \varphi_{01}\sigma_0\sigma_1$ ([Color content as formal linear combination in $H_3(q)$](#rem:knot-basis-color-content)), where $\sigma_0, \sigma_1$ encode transverse polarization and generate a self-similar Julia-set envelope measure $\mu_J$.

In the classical limit $q \to 1$, both envelopes collapse to standard Fraunhofer diffraction, whereas at $q_0 \approx 1.1100$ the predicted fringe-width deviations are compared directly against the $600\;\mathrm{eV}$ electron diffraction dataset of Bach et al. \cite{bach2013}.
