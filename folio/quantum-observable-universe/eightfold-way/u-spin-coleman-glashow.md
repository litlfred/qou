Beyond isospin, the flavour $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{f}}$ contains two further $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
subgroups: **U-spin**, exchanging $d \leftrightarrow s$, and **V-spin**,
exchanging $u \leftrightarrow s$.  U-spin is the useful one, because the electric
charge is invariant under it --- $d$ and $s$ both carry $Q = -\tfrac{1}{3}$ ---
which makes the electromagnetic interaction a U-spin scalar.

The classical consequence is the **Coleman--Glashow relation** among the baryon
octet's electromagnetic splittings,

$$
  \bigl(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{p}} - \mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{n}}\bigr)
  - \bigl(\mathfrak{m}_{\Sigma^+} - \mathfrak{m}_{\Sigma^-}\bigr)
  + \bigl(\mathfrak{m}_{\Xi^0} - \mathfrak{m}_{\Xi^-}\bigr)
  = 0,
$$

which experiment satisfies to $-0.064$ MeV against splittings of $1.3$ to $8.1$
MeV --- about $0.8\%$ of the largest.

**The relation carries no information about the breaking mechanism.**  This is
a theorem: the combination vanishes for
*every* choice of the six first-order parameters, over any commutative ring, so two arbitrarily different breaking patterns
give the same answer.

## Why it vanishes

Two standard premises, and both are needed.  First, isospin is exact in the
strong sector, so the strong mass depends only on the isospin multiplet ---
one parameter each for $(\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{n})$, $(\Sigma^+, \Sigma^-)$
and $(\Xi^0, \Xi^-)$.  Second, the electromagnetic self-energy is a U-spin
scalar, hence constant on a U-spin multiplet.

The octet's U-spin multiplets are exactly the pairs the relation uses, each at
fixed charge, which is why the relation pairs the states it does:

| U-spin multiplet | Quark content | $Q$ | EM shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathfrak{p} \leftrightarrow \Sigma^+$ | $uud \leftrightarrow uus$ | $+1$ | $a$ |
| $\mathfrak{n} \leftrightarrow \Xi^0$ | $udd \leftrightarrow uss$ | $0$ | $b$ |
| $\Sigma^- \leftrightarrow \Xi^-$ | $dds \leftrightarrow dss$ | $-1$ | $c$ |

Six parameters --- three strong, three electromagnetic --- and the alternating
combination cancels every one: $a$ appears in $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\Sigma^+$
with opposite sign, $b$ in $\mathfrak{n}$ and $\Xi^0$, $c$ in $\Sigma^-$ and
$\Xi^-$.  Switching the electromagnetic shifts off makes each splitting vanish
separately, so the relation is empty
without electromagnetism rather than being a statement about the strong sector.

## One difference from the other two null results

Gell-Mann--Okubo and [the decuplet equal-spacing
rule](#rem:decuplet-equal-spacing) each follow from a single premise.
Coleman--Glashow rests on a **conjunction**.
Dropping the U-spin premise --- letting the electromagnetic shift differ
between the two members of a multiplet --- makes the combination equal the
difference of those two shifts rather than zero, which is detectable.

The relation therefore does test that the electromagnetic self-energy is U-spin
invariant and that isospin is exact in the strong sector.  What it cannot do is
distinguish among the mechanisms satisfying both, and the framework has offered
no reason to doubt either premise.  **Reproducing Coleman--Glashow would
therefore be no evidence for this framework, or for any other.**

## What remains testable

The residual, as with the other two.  At $\approx 0.8\%$ it is the *tightest*
of the three --- against $\approx 6\%$ for Gell-Mann--Okubo on the pseudoscalar
octet and $\approx 9.2\%$ for the decuplet spacings --- which makes it the most
demanding second-order target and the least likely to be reached first.  The
framework derives no second-order term and so predicts $0$.

Nothing here is fitted, and nothing is wired to any computation.  V-spin
($u \leftrightarrow s$) yields no independent relation for the octet and is not
treated separately.
