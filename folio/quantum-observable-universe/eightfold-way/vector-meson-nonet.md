The pseudoscalar octet of [the meson-octet
proposition](#prop:meson-octet-decomposition) is one of two nonets with
identical $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{f}}$ content.  The other is the vector nonet $1^-$ ---
$\rho$, $K^*$, $\omega$, $\phi$ --- obtained from the same
$\mathbf{3} \otimes \bar{\mathbf{3}} = \mathbf{8} \oplus \mathbf{1}$ with the
quark spins aligned rather than anti-aligned.  **The two nonets mix their
octet and singlet almost oppositely, and no $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{f}}$-symmetric
statement distinguishes them.**  That makes the mixing the first
eightfold-way quantity whose derivation would carry information.

## The contrast

Reported as quark content rather than as a mixing angle: angle conventions
differ across the literature ($\theta_V \approx 39^\circ$ and
$\approx 51^\circ$ are the same physics read two ways), whereas the
$s\bar{s}$ probability of the heavy state is convention-independent, and
*ideal* mixing is exactly $1$.

| Nonet | Heavy state | $s\bar{s}$ purity | Departure from ideal |
|---|---|---|---|
| pseudoscalar $0^-$ | $\eta'$ | $50.3\%$ | $49.7$ pp |
| vector $1^-$ | $\phi$ | $\mathbf{99.5\%}$ | $\mathbf{0.5}$ pp |

The vector nonet is **ideally mixed** --- the $\phi$ is $s\bar{s}$ to within
half a percentage point --- while the pseudoscalar nonet sits about as far
from ideal as it can; PDG central values enter solely as comparators, and nothing here is fitted.

This is also why Gell-Mann--Okubo appears to *fail* on the vector nonet.
Applied to the physical states, the relation deviates by $\approx 6\%$ for the
pseudoscalar $\eta$ but by $\approx 32\%$ for the physical $\omega$.  The
discrepancy is not a failure of $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{f}}$: it is that the physical
$\omega$ is not the octet member at all, because the nonet is ideally mixed.

## Distinction from first-order sum rules

[The GMO relation](#rem:meson-mass-splitting) is an **identity** of the
first-order octet-breaking ansatz: it holds for every choice of the breaking
parameters, so reproducing it is evidence for nothing.  The decuplet
equal-spacing rule behind the $\Omega^-$ prediction is the same
(established later in this
chapter).

The mixing is the opposite case, and this too is a theorem.  Holding fixed the
octet member $m_8$ that Gell-Mann--Okubo determines, *every* pair of physical
masses is realisable by choosing the singlet entry and the octet--singlet
mixing:

$$
  m_1^2 = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 - m_8^2,
  \qquad
  e^2 = (m_8^2 - \lambda_1)(\lambda_2 - m_8^2),
$$

whereupon the characteristic polynomial is exactly
$(x - \lambda_1)(x - \lambda_2)$.  The ansatz therefore constrains the mixing
**not at all**, and the observed value is genuinely extra data.  Setting
$e = 0$ recovers the unmixed states, so the mixing is carried entirely by that
one parameter --- the parameter on which the two nonets disagree.

## Availability of the nonet in the framework

**Open, and not currently attemptable.**  The nonet needs the singlet, which is
unavailable at $\dim V = 2$: the alternating channel vanishes below $N = 3$, so the $\eta'$ and its vector counterpart do not
exist framework-side.  The vector nonet therefore inherits the $N = 3$ problem
of [the open-problems ledger](#sec:eightfold-open-problems) in full.

What is recorded here is a statement about the **target** rather than about prospects.
Of the eightfold way's three headline quantitative successes, two are now proved
content-free as derivation targets and this one is proved content-bearing, which
makes its derivation the highest-value open item in this chapter.

In QCD the near-ideal vector mixing is attributed to the OZI (quark-line) rule,
and the pseudoscalar departure from it to the axial anomaly.  The framework has
no account of either, and no candidate mechanism is proposed here.
