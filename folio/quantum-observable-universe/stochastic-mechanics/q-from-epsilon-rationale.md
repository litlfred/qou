**Consistency with $q - 1$ expansions.** The Frobenius--Hopf substrate $A_q$ is recovered as the evaluation of the $\varepsilon$-expansion $A_q = A_1 + \varepsilon \, \phi_1 + \varepsilon^2 \, \phi_2 + \cdots$. Setting $q = 1 + \varepsilon$ preserves every algebraic $q$-identity unchanged: expansions in $q - 1$ become expansions in $\varepsilon$, and the classical point $\varepsilon = 0$ coincides with $q = 1$.

**Motivation for taking $\varepsilon$ as primitive.** First, categorical origin. The Reeb infinitesimal $\varepsilon$ is intrinsic to $A$ before any braiding is chosen. Second, exact error bounds. With $\varepsilon$ primitive and living in an ordered field with infinitesimals, the scaling laws become literal arithmetic inequalities.
