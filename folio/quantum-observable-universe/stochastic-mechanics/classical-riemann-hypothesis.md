All non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ satisfy $\mathrm{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}$.

The statement is the classical one, and it is open. The book proves nothing toward it and assumes no proof of it. The Hasse--Weil bound gives $\mathrm{Re}(\rho) = 1/2$ for the Frobenius exponents of Bring's curve at each prime separately, but those exponents build $L(E,s)$ for the elliptic curve 50a1, not $\zeta(s)$ ([geometric stability via the Weil bound](#sec:q-gl-weil-bound-stability)). The hope that a local alignment "survives the adelic assembly" into $\zeta(s)$ is recorded as the [adelic persistence conjecture](#conj:adelic-critical-line-persistence), with the caveats stated there.
