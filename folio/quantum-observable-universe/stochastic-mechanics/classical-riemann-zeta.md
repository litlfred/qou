The **classical Riemann zeta function** $\zeta(s)$ is the convergent Dirichlet series and Euler product

$$
\zeta(s) = \prod_{p \text{ prime}} \frac{1}{1 - p^{-s}}
         = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^s},
  \qquad \mathrm{Re}(s) > 1.
$$

It is the Hasse--Weil zeta function of $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}$: each Euler factor $(1 - p^{-s})^{-1}$ is the zeta function of the point $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{F}_p$, which has trivial $H^1$ and therefore carries no Frobenius eigenvalues. By contrast, multiplying the local curve zeta functions $Z(\mathcal{B}/\mathbb{F}_p, p^{-s})$ of Bring's curve across good primes yields $\zeta(s)\,\zeta(s - 1)/L(E, s)^4$ for the elliptic curve $50\mathrm{a}1$, in which $\zeta(s)$ appears solely as the $H^0$ denominator factor.
