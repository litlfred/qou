The preceding sections established that the algebraic layer is unconditionally stable at the $q$-scale. The Hasse--Weil bound guarantees all Frobenius eigenvalues lie on the critical circle. The Reeb spectrum inherits this alignment. The aether-side prime-counting estimate holds with the optimal square-root error term.

The question now is what happens in the classical limit $q \to 1$. The limit collapses the quantum gap $\hbar_q \to 0$, smooths the Jackson $q$-derivative into the ordinary derivative, and degrades the braided tensor category into a symmetric one. The framework reads the Riemann hypothesis as the statement that the macroscopic continuum inherits the stability of the $q$-scale. That is a reading, not a reduction: the local zeta functions of Bring's curve multiply to $\zeta(s)\,\zeta(s-1)/L(E,s)^4$ rather than to $\zeta(s)$, and the Riemann hypothesis stays open (see [the implications sub-section](#sec:classical-limit-implications)).
