The preceding sections established that the algebraic substrate is unconditionally stable at the $q$-scale. The Hasse--Weil bound guarantees all Frobenius eigenvalues lie on the critical circle. The Reeb spectrum inherits this alignment. The substrate-side prime-counting estimate holds with the optimal square-root error term.

The central question of this section is what happens in the classical limit $q \to 1$. The limit collapses the quantum gap $\hbar_q \to 0$, smooths the Jackson $q$-derivative into the ordinary derivative, and degrades the braided tensor category into a symmetric one. The local $q$-geometries at each prime fiber glue into the classical Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$. The Riemann hypothesis is exactly the statement that the macroscopic continuum inherits the stability of the $q$-scale.

**Skeleton of this section.** The remaining results share the [surreal rate calculus](#prop:surreal-rate-calculus) machinery. Treating the Reeb infinitesimal $\varepsilon$ ([Reeb infinitesimal](#def:reeb-epsilon)) as a strictly positive infinitesimal in $\mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No}$ produces two categorical invariants:

$$
  \Delta_q \;\asymp\; \varepsilon^3
  \qquad\text{(mass-gap dissipation, infinitesimal),}
$$
$$
  \Omega_q \;\asymp\; \varepsilon^{-1}
  \qquad\text{(Reeb period, transfinite).}
$$

The [surreal squeeze](#lem:surreal-squeeze) $\delta^2 \cdot \Omega_q > \Delta_q$ holds strictly for every standard real $\delta > 0$. Each millennium-problem remark applies this squeeze.
