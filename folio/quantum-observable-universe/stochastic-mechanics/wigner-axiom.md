Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a quantum observable universe with archimedean base curve $M_0$, and let $\mathrm{Obs}(\mathbf{C}, q \to 1) = HH^2(\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{g}), \mathrm{Ad})$ be its [classical limit obstruction](#def:classical-limit-obstruction).

**Conjecture (the Wigner axiom).** *The classical-limit obstruction space is one-dimensional, and its generator is realised by the Euler class of the base:*

**(W1)** $\dim_k \mathrm{Obs}(\mathbf{C}, q \to 1) = 1$;

**(W2)** *the archimedean realisation $\mathrm{Obs}(\mathbf{C}, q \to 1) \to H^2(M_0; k)$ carries a generator $w(A)$ onto the Euler class $e(TM_0)$, which is non-zero; consequently $\langle e(TM_0), [M_0] \rangle = \chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = 2 - 2g(M_0)$ is the integer that determines the obstruction.*

**Axiomatic status of (W1).** Two arguments that were offered for (W1) do not go through. The first invoked Poincaré duality for Hochschild cohomology in the untwisted form $HH^{2} \cong (HH^{1})^{\vee}$; for the Hopf algebras in question the duality that holds is Brown and Zhang's twisted form $HH^{i}(H, M) \cong HH_{d-i}(H, {}_{\nu}M)$ \cite{brown-zhang-2008}, cohomology to *homology* with the Nakayama automorphism $\nu$ on the coefficients, and at $q \neq 1$ the untwisted version fails outright: the top Hochschild homology of $\mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SL}_2)$ drops, and the one-dimensional top group reappears only after the twist \cite{hadfield-krahmer-2005}. The second counted the deformation direction $q$ as a class in the obstruction space; it is a class in $HH^{1}$, one degree lower. And at the classical point the statement is false: for $\mathfrak{g}$ semisimple and $M$ finite-dimensional, Whitehead's lemmas give $H^{1}(\mathfrak{g}, M) = H^{2}(\mathfrak{g}, M) = 0$ \cite{weibel1994}, so $\mathrm{Obs} = 0$ at $q = 1$. One-dimensionality at $q \neq 1$ is therefore a hypothesis about the aether rather than a theorem of the axioms.

**Equivariant target of (W2).** For Bring's surface the symmetry group $S_5$ acts on everything in sight, and an equivariant realisation must land in the $S_5$-invariant part of the base's Hochschild theory. That invariant part is $k^2$ in $HH_0$ — spanned by $H^0(M_0, \mathcal{O})$ and $H^1(M_0, K) = H^2(M_0; k)$ — and $k$ in $HH^0$, and it is zero in $HH^2(M_0) = H^1(TM_0)$, the deformation space of the curve. An equivariant image of a one-dimensional obstruction space among the deformations of the base is therefore zero, and the only invariant line that can carry the integer is $H^2(M_0; k)$, where the Euler class lives. The axiom is stated with that target and no other.

**Deductive consequences and falsification criteria.** Every block that reduces a Millennium-type persistence question to the divisibility $n \mid \chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0)$ is conditional on this axiom; the reduction itself ([obstruction reduction](#thm:qou-obstruction-reduction)) is then a theorem. A computation of $HH^{2}(\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2), \mathrm{Ad})$ at generic $q$ with dimension different from one refutes (W1); an equivariant realisation whose image of the obstruction line vanishes in $H^2(M_0; k)$ refutes (W2). Neither computation is carried out in this monograph.

The name follows Wigner's Courant lecture of 11 May 1959 on the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics \cite{wigner1960}: the axiom is exactly the assumption that a single scalar of the aether governs the persistence of its structure into the classical world.
