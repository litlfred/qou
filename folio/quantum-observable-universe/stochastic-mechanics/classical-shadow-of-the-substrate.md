The [classical limit functor](#def:classical-limit-functor) $\mathcal{L} \colon \mathbf{C}_q \to \mathbf{C}_1$ sends the aether at parameter $q$ to its value at $q = 1$. This remark asks what it does to the [classical limit obstruction](#def:classical-limit-obstruction), the Hochschild cohomology $HH^{2}(\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{g}), \mathrm{Ad})$, and the answer is that the shadow is zero.

**At the classical point.** For $q = 1$ the aether algebra is the universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ of the semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ (or its dual, the coordinate ring of the group). Its Hochschild cohomology with coefficients in a bimodule $M$ is the Lie algebra cohomology of $\mathfrak{g}$ with coefficients in $M$ made into a $\mathfrak{g}$-module by the adjoint action, $HH^{n}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}), M) \cong H^{n}(\mathfrak{g}, M^{\mathrm{ad}})$ \cite{cartan1956}. Whitehead's lemmas state that $H^{1}(\mathfrak{g}, M) = H^{2}(\mathfrak{g}, M) = 0$ for $\mathfrak{g}$ semisimple over a field of characteristic zero and $M$ finite-dimensional \cite{weibel1994}; the adjoint bimodule $\mathrm{Ad}(A)$ is a direct sum of finite-dimensional modules, so the vanishing holds for it degree by degree. Hence

$$
  \mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathrm{Obs}(\mathbf{C}, q \to 1)\bigr) \;=\; HH^{2}\bigl(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}), \mathrm{Ad}\bigr) \;=\; 0 :
$$

the classical shadow of the obstruction space is the zero space. The only one-dimensional cohomology that $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ does have is $H^{3}(\mathfrak{su}_2; k) = k$, the Cartan three-form, in degree three and with trivial coefficients; it is an invariant of the group rather than a deformation obstruction.

**In the formal neighbourhood.** Expanding in $\hbar = q - 1$ rather than evaluating at a fixed $q$ places the aether in the setting of formal deformation theory, where the [Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg theorem](#rem:hkr-theorem) has a $q$-side companion: Kontsevich's formality theorem identifies the Hochschild cohomology of a formal deformation of a smooth algebra with the Poisson cohomology of its classical limit \cite{kontsevich-2003}, and for a symplectic classical limit that Poisson cohomology is de Rham cohomology \cite{nest-tsygan-1995,dolgushev-rubtsov-2005}. In that setting the [Hochschild homology](#def:hochschild-homology) and cohomology of the formal aether are computed from the classical shadow, and the shadow of the obstruction is still zero: a formal expansion around $q = 1$ sees no obstruction line.

**At the physical point.** At a fixed $q \neq 1$ neither theorem applies, and the groups have to be computed. What is known is that the untwisted Hochschild theory of $\mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SL}_2)$ has a dimension drop in top degree \cite{feng-tsygan-1991}, and that the one-dimensional top group is recovered only in the theory twisted by the Nakayama automorphism \cite{hadfield-krahmer-2005}. No computation in this monograph evaluates $HH^{2}(\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{g}), \mathrm{Ad})$ at the aether parameter. A one-dimensional obstruction space at $q \neq 1$ would therefore be a phenomenon of the archimedean evaluation, invisible both at the classical point and in its formal neighbourhood; this is the fibre-versus-observable boundary of the introduction, and it is why the one-dimensionality is stated as an axiom of the aether rather than derived from the axioms of a quantum observable universe.
