A derivation of the Standard Model must ultimately account for its free parameters. In the counting used here, the renormalisable Standard Model carries 19 parameters — 9 charged-fermion masses, 4 quark-mixing (CKM) parameters, 3 gauge couplings, 2 Higgs-sector parameters, and the strong-CP angle — and the neutrino-sector extension adds 7 more (3 masses, 3 mixing angles, 1 Dirac CP phase), for a total of 26. If the neutrinos are Majorana, two further phases appear; they are noted in the neutrino section and not counted in the 26.

The following sections walk the 26 parameters in chunks, one section per chunk, and record for each parameter its status in the framework using the tiers of [the appendix introduction](#prose:sm-intro): *input* (one of the three calibration observables, not a prediction), *proxy* (a calibration proxy, exact by construction under the canonical pin), *partial* (a derived or conditional structure exists but the parameter is not closed), *conjectural* (a precise identification exists without derivation), and *open* (nothing here closes the parameter).

The headline count is stated up front so that the chunk tables cannot be misread as progress claims: of the 26 parameters, **1 is an input** ($m_e$, the dimensional unit), **1 is a calibration proxy** ($m_\mu$, pinning the substrate modulus through the $\beta$-sector bar-skew calibration), **1 is partial** ($\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}$, conditional structure with open per-loop measure), **2 are conjectural** ($\sin^2\theta_W$, $m_H$), and **21 are open**. No parameter beyond the calibration set is currently derived to experimental precision.
