If the archimedean truncation of [the truncation conjecture](#conj:sm-gauge-truncation) is the right picture — observable-scale symmetry descending from the fibre's discrete automorphisms rather than a continuous rotation group — then atomic orbital degeneracies inherit a specific discrete fingerprint. Restricting the $(2l+1)$-dimensional $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ representations to the icosahedral group $A_5 \subset S_5$ (the orientation-preserving automorphisms of the Bring fibre) gives, by exact character arithmetic:

| orbital | $2l+1$ | $A_5$ decomposition |
|---|---|---|
| $s$ | $1$ | $\mathbf{1}$ |
| $p$ | $3$ | $\mathbf{3}$ |
| $d$ | $5$ | $\mathbf{5}$ |
| $f$ | $7$ | $\mathbf{3}' \oplus \mathbf{4}$ |
| $g$ | $9$ | $\mathbf{4} \oplus \mathbf{5}$ |
| $h$ | $11$ | $\mathbf{3} \oplus \mathbf{3}' \oplus \mathbf{5}$ |

The $s$, $p$, $d$ degeneracies are *native* — they are exactly the dimensions $1, 3, 5$ of $A_5$ irreducibles and restrict irreducibly. Every degeneracy above five fractures: the group has no irreducible of dimension greater than $5$, so the $f$ orbital's seven states are, under the truncated symmetry, a composite of a $\mathbf{3}'$ and a $\mathbf{4}$ that a continuous rotation group would hold together. The branching arithmetic is standard finite-group character theory and holds identically over $\mathbb{Z}$.

The physical reading — that $f$-shell degeneracy is only approximate, with a fine-structure split along the $\mathbf{3}' \oplus \mathbf{4}$ seam — is conditional on the truncation conjecture and is recorded here as an interpretive consequence rather than a prediction wired to any computation.

**No falsifiability claim is made.** One might argue that a truncated discrete symmetry *cannot* hold seven states exactly degenerate, while exact $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ can, so the two pictures differ "at some resolution" on lanthanide-scale spectroscopy. Two objections. First, free-atom $f$-shell structure is already described to spectroscopic precision by rotationally invariant theory: states are labelled by $J$ with $(2J+1)$ degeneracy exact by isotropy, and the observed lifting is spin--orbit, in a $J$ pattern rather than along a $\mathbf{3}' \oplus \mathbf{4}$ seam. Second, and more fundamental, "at some resolution" names no resolution. The statement is compatible with every null result, which makes it untestable rather than falsifiable.

**Upstream frame-orientation ambiguity.** The branching establishes that the seam exists; it fixes no splitting magnitude, and none follows from character arithmetic alone. The experiment that would constrain such a splitting is itself undetermined, because the framework does not say how the icosahedral frame attaches to space:

- if the $A_5$ frame is **fixed in space**, the atomic Hamiltonian is anisotropic, so the binding constraint is the Hughes--Drever family of clock-comparison tests of rotational invariance, which are far tighter than any lanthanide measurement;
- if each atom carries an **independently oriented** frame, the anisotropy averages out and the constraint is line structure in an ensemble spectrum, which is the lanthanide measurement.

Until that is specified one cannot choose the experiment, let alone compute a bound. Supplying it is the open item here, ahead of any spectroscopic campaign.

This is a scope-(a) objection. The branching arithmetic stands: the six rows above were re-derived independently by $\mathrm{SO}(3) \downarrow A_5$ character arithmetic and reproduce exactly. Nothing here excludes the truncation picture. What is withdrawn is the claim that this block's value lies in falsifiability.
