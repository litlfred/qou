The electron mass is the framework's dimensional unit and enters as an input rather than a prediction. The muon mass is the numerical proxy for the neutron $\beta$-sector bar-skew calibration $P_{\mathrm{skew}}^{\beta}$: the ratio $m_\mu/m_e \approx \mathrm{Vol}(4_1)/\hbar_q^2$ pins the aether parameter $q_0$, so under the canonical (muon) pin the muon mass is exact *by construction* as a calibration tautology rather than an accuracy result. Under the experimental (proton) pin, $m_\mu$ becomes a genuine prediction and misses its hundredfold-experimental-precision target by $970.2\times$ ($211.2$ ppb against a target of $0.2177$ ppb), the $\mu{-}p$ aether-parameter tension recorded in the precision-goals ledger. Either way, $m_\mu$ does not count as a derived Standard-Model parameter.

The tau mass is open, and the obvious route has a recorded negative: [the tau knot-prediction remark](#rem:m-tau-knot-prediction) reports that a direct search over 25 hyperbolic knots found no $K_\tau$ with $m_\tau/m_e \approx \mathrm{Vol}(K_\tau)/\hbar_q^{k_\tau}$ within one percent, giving a scope-limited refutation of the naive volume reading as tested rather than an exclusion of a tau carrier altogether. The framework does treat the tau's *decay structure* through the $T(3,5)$ weak vertex ([the tau leptonic-universality proposition](#prop:tau-leptonic-universality) derives lepton universality of the leptonic branching ratios from the Hecke amplitude's independence of the daughter lepton), though universality of ratios is not a mass. The missing object is the tau's carrier identification, together with whatever correction to the direct volume reading the 25-knot negative is pointing at.
