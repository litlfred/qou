**Proposition.** *Let $\varphi$ be an isometry of Bring's surface with orientation sign $\varepsilon = \det d\varphi$. Its lift to the unit tangent bundle $UT(\Sigma_4)$ has orientation sign $\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon = 1$. Consequently no rule that reads a hypercharge vector off the orientation of $UT(\Sigma_4)$ can send the two orientations of Bring's surface to $Y$ and $-Y$.*

*Proof.* The lift acts by $d\varphi$ on the base and by the induced map on the fibre angle, whose orientation sign is again $\varepsilon$; the product is $\varepsilon^2 = 1$. For the reflection $z\mapsto -\bar z$ this is the Jacobian $\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,-1)$ of `iota_lift_det` in `QOU/BringsSurface/BringUTContactChirality.lean`. So the orientation reversal $c$ leaves the orientation of $UT(\Sigma_4)$ unchanged, while in the reflection-odd copy it sends $Y$ to $-Y$ ([`prop:hypercharge-sign-is-bring-orientation`](hypercharge-sign-is-bring-orientation.md)). A rule giving $Y$ and $-Y$ would then force $Y = -Y$, which is false. $\square$

**Consequence.** The corpus fixes crossing signs on $UT(\Sigma_4)$ from $q_0 > 1$ and the canonical contact orientation $\alpha\wedge d\alpha$. Every orientation-reversing isometry of Bring's surface preserves that structure, so braid chirality is blind to the orientation of Bring's surface and cannot fix the hypercharge sign. A source that sees the orientation of the base directly, such as the complex structure of Bring's curve, is not excluded. The sign stays open.
