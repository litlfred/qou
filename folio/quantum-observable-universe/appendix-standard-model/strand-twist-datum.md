Let $\beta$ be an $n$-strand state of the gauge-action groupoid. A :defterm[per-strand framing datum]{#strand-twist-datum} on $\beta$ is a function

$$
  t \colon \{1, \dots, n\} \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z},
$$

assigning to each strand an integer ribbon twist, together with the total twist $T(t) = \sum_i t_i$. The pair $(\beta, t)$ is a framed state. The datum is an independent label on $\beta$, not the twist the ambient ribbon structure already carries: in a ribbon category the twist $\theta$ is determined by the braiding rather than freely assigned, and on the $n$-th Jones–Wenzl idempotent $e_n$ it acts by the scalar $(-1)^n A^{n(n+2)}$ \cite{habiro2000}, whose exponent is the Casimir $n(n+2)$ of the colour. A ribbon twist therefore admits no free per-strand integer, and $t$ is an addition to the structure, not a reading of it.

Three properties are proved. The residual $S_n$ strand-relabelling action of the gauge groupoid preserves both $T(t)$ and the full multiset $\{t_1, \dots, t_n\}$, so the datum descends to the **horizontal** ($S_n$, fixed-$n$) orbits. It is not yet known to descend to the vertical direction: stabilisation $\beta \in B_n \mapsto \beta\sigma_n^{\pm 1} \in B_{n+1}$ adds a strand, hence a new entry $t_{n+1}$, and generically changes both the multiset and $T(t)$. Since the writhe fails at exactly this move, and the point of this datum is to supply what the writhe cannot, the vertical behaviour is the property most worth establishing — it is untested here. As with $Q$ and $I_3$, the working convention evaluates the datum on the canonical Gröbner–Shirshov word at the tower's minimal level, where only the horizontal action survives. The bar involution (orientation reversal, the conjugate state) negates the datum, $T(\bar t) = -T(t)$, so conjugate states carry opposite twist. Finally, $T$ separates states at fixed writhe: over any fixed braid word — hence any fixed $w$ — there exist framings with distinct total twist. [The hypercharge conjecture](#conj:sm-hypercharge-invariant) requires exactly such a coordinate, since its factorization criterion shows any hypercharge invariant must separate a writhe-degenerate pair and no function of $w$ can. The separation is a property of the free label. It is not a property of the ambient ribbon twist, which is constant on a fixed object by the paragraph above, so a candidate resting on it inherits the freedom of the label rather than the rigidity of the category. Supplying a separating coordinate is a necessary condition on the candidate, not evidence that the separation is physical.

The normalised candidate pairs the datum with the derived isospin: with $Q_{\mathrm{twist}} = T(t)/3$ (the colour-tube charge quantum of the quark picture, the helon-model normalisation \cite{bilson-thompson2005}) and $I_3$ the writhe-derived isospin, the candidate hypercharge is $Y_t = 2\,(Q_{\mathrm{twist}} - I_3)$. This makes the Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation hold by construction *if and only if* the two independent charge routes agree, $Q_{\mathrm{twist}} = Q$ against [the crossing-derived Coulomb charge](#def:coulomb-charge) — a falsifiable consistency between framing and crossing data, not a definition. That consistency, together with the first-generation spectrum and its $\mathbb{Z}_6$ compatibility \cite{baez-huerta2010}, is the battery any candidate must pass before wiring; it is exactly the open content of the hypercharge conjecture, and no numerical claim is made here.
