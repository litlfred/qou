The mass endomorphism $\mathcal{M}$ carries more information than its real trace. Over $\mathbb{C}$, the projection defect $\mathrm{tr}_M\bigl((\Pi_{\text{free}}-\mathbf{1})\circ\mathcal{M}\bigr)$ defines a single complex observable, the complex volume $\mathfrak{V}(K)$. Here, $\Pi_{\text{free}}$ is the factorized projector onto unlinked constituents, and $\mathfrak{V}(K) = E_0\,\mathrm{tr}_M\bigl((\Pi_{\text{free}}-\mathbf{1})\circ\mathcal{M}\bigr)$. This acts as the QOU analogue to the Neumann–Zagier complex volume $\mathbb{V}(K) = \mathrm{Vol}(K) + i\,\mathrm{CS}(K)$.

The framework's two nuclear observables serve as the two real shadows of $\mathfrak{V}$. Binding energy forms the real part ($\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{bind}} = \mathrm{Re}\mathfrak{V}$). This is the bar-even, $\cosh$, strong channel representing stable binding. The $\beta$-decay $Q$-value forms the imaginary part ($\mathfrak{Q}_\beta = \mathrm{Im}\mathfrak{V}$). This is the bar-odd, $\sinh$, weak channel representing unstable decay.

The unified reconstruction $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{bind}} + i\,\mathfrak{Q}_\beta = \mathfrak{V}$ holds unconditionally as an algebraic identity. The physical conjecture asserts that the measured binding and $Q$-value precisely equal these real and imaginary components under a shared normalization.

## Fibre-level caveat

At the archimedean, observable level, $\mathrm{Im}(q_0) = 0$. Binding and $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ are both real measured quantities. The complex structure exists strictly at the fibre level (pre-$\tau$). $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{bind}}$ and $\mathfrak{Q}_\beta$ are real observable shadows of one complex fibre object under different archimedean evaluations (the real-$q$ volume channel versus the root-of-unity Chern–Simons channel). There is no "complex mass."
