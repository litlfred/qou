**Remark (prior art: the polarization choice in the deformed volume function away from $\operatorname{Re} u = 0$).**
Murakami's parameterized volume conjecture relates the limit of $\log J_N\bigl(K; \exp((u + 2\pi\sqrt{-1})/N)\bigr)/N$ to the volume function $V(K;u)$ of the $SL(2;\mathbb{C})$ representation whose meridian has eigenvalue ratio $\exp(u)$, through
$$
V(K;u) = \operatorname{Im} H(K;u) - \pi \operatorname{Re}(u) - \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}(u)\operatorname{Im} v_K(u)
$$
\cite[Conjecture 2.1 and eq. (2.3), \S 2, p. 2]{murakami2006}. For the figure-eight knot this is a theorem of Murakami and Yokota, recorded in the same section \cite[\S 2, pp. 2--3]{murakami2006}. Murakami notes that Gukov's generalization uses a different polarization: the two agree when $\operatorname{Re}(u) = 0$, and differ when $\operatorname{Re}(u) \neq 0$ by the choice of polarization \cite[\S 2, p. 3]{murakami2006}. The two $\operatorname{Re}(u)$ terms in the formula above, which vanish on $\operatorname{Re}(u) = 0$, are where such a difference can appear.

What this gives here. The [conjecture](#conj:binding-qbeta-complex-volume) reads the $S$ channel as the Chern–Simons part of the deformed complex volume of $4_1$ at a substrate deformation $u(q_0)$, and its surviving necessary condition for a sign flip of $S$ is that $u(q_0)$ cross the diagonal $|\operatorname{Re} u| = |\operatorname{Im} u|$ between the anchors. On that path $\operatorname{Re} u \neq 0$, which is exactly the region where the published volume functions depend on the polarization. The leading harmonic form used there, and the refutation of the three candidate families built on it, are therefore statements in one polarization.

What it does not supply. It has not been checked which polarization the corpus's deformed-volume series for $4_1$ realises, nor whether the refutation of the three families survives the change of polarization. Murakami does not give the explicit relation between the two conventions; he defers it to later work \cite[\S 2, p. 3]{murakami2006}. Until that check is made, the sign-flip analysis should be read as conditional on the polarization as well as on the deformed-volume channel hypothesis.
