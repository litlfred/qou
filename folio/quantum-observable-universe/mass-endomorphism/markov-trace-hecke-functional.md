## Statement

*Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $z, h \in R$. Let $H_\infty$ be the Hecke tower over $R$: generators $\sigma_0, \sigma_1, \ldots$ with $\sigma_m^2 = h\,\sigma_m + 1$, the braid relations and far commutation. Write $H_m \subset H_\infty$ for the subalgebra generated by $\sigma_0, \ldots, \sigma_{m-1}$, and $y^{\uparrow k}$ for $y$ with every generator index raised by $k$. There is an $R$-linear map $\mathrm{tr}_M : H_\infty \to R$ that agrees with the word trace of [the recursive reducer](#prop:markov-trace-recursive-reducer) on every positive word. It satisfies:*

1. *$\mathrm{tr}_M(1) = 1$ and $\mathrm{tr}_M(xy) = \mathrm{tr}_M(yx)$;*
2. *$\mathrm{tr}_M(x\,\sigma_m) = z\,\mathrm{tr}_M(x)$ for $x \in H_m$;*
3. *$\mathrm{tr}_M(x\,y^{\uparrow m}) = \mathrm{tr}_M(x)\,\mathrm{tr}_M(y)$ for $x \in H_m$ and any $y$;*
4. *(connected-sum rule) $\mathrm{tr}_M(x\,\sigma_m\,y^{\uparrow (m+1)}) = z\,\mathrm{tr}_M(x)\,\mathrm{tr}_M(y)$ for $x \in H_m$ and any $y$;*
5. *for an interface letter $a + b\,\sigma_m$ with $a, b \in R$,*
$$
  \mathrm{tr}_M\bigl(x\,(a + b\,\sigma_m)\,y^{\uparrow (m+1)}\bigr) = (a + b z)\,\mathrm{tr}_M(x)\,\mathrm{tr}_M(y).
$$

*Let $s, h \in R$ with $s^2 = q$ and $s h = q - 1$. Then $\sigma_i \mapsto s\,\sigma_i$ is an algebra map from $H_n(q)$, presented with $\sigma_i^2 = (q-1)\sigma_i + q$, into $H_\infty$. Composing gives a linear trace on $H_n(q)$ with $\mathrm{tr}_M(\sigma_i) = s z$. When $R$ is a field and $q \neq 0$, for every splitting of $H_n(q)$ into matrix blocks this trace equals $x \mapsto \tau(x\,\kappa)$ for a central element $\kappa$, where $\tau$ is the canonical symmetrizing trace.*

## Proof

The word trace satisfies the Hecke, braid and far-commutation relations inside any two-sided context of words. Extended linearly to the free algebra, it therefore vanishes on the two-sided ideal of the defining relations and descends to $H_\infty$. Cyclicity passes from words to the algebra by linearity. Every element of $H_m$ is a linear combination of words in $\sigma_0, \ldots, \sigma_{m-1}$, so the Markov property and the shared-strand product law of the reducer extend linearly to (2) and (3). Then (4) is (3) applied to $x\,\sigma_m \in H_{m+1}$, followed by (2). For (5), expand the interface letter and apply (3) and (4).

For $H_n(q)$, $(s\sigma)^2 = q(h\sigma + 1) = (q-1)(s\sigma) + q$, because $q h = s(sh) = s(q-1)$. The braid and far-commutation relations are homogeneous, so the rescaling respects them. The knot-factor statement is the general result for any trace on a split semisimple algebra whose pairing trace has nonzero block weights, applied to this trace.

## Remark on scope

Rule (5) shows that a bond made of one crossing contributes the same factor $a + b z$ whichever two clusters it joins. The connected-sum rule is stated on the tower, where the inclusions $H_m \subset H_{m+1}$ and the shift are defined. The finite algebras $H_n(q)$ in the Lean library have no inclusion maps yet.
