**Interpretive remark (physical reading; not part of the mathematical claim).**

In the geometric picture the [Frobenius pairing](#def:frobenius-pairing) plays the role of a metric (compare the Levi form as metric in the GR substrate model), so a fluctuation $\delta\lambda$ of the pairing is the categorical analogue of a metric perturbation $h_{\mu\nu}$. Under that reading, [the proposition](#prop:pairing-gauge-fluctuation-massless) is the algebraic shadow of the familiar statement of linearised gravity that pure-gauge perturbations $h \mapsto h + \mathcal{L}_\xi \bar g$ cost no action: the infinitesimal automorphism $f$ plays the role of the vector field $\xi$, the Leibniz combination $f \otimes \mathrm{id} - \mathrm{id} \otimes f^{\vee}$ that of the Lie derivative of the background, and the vanishing of $S(\delta_f\lambda)$ together with the radical condition is what "no mass term along gauge directions" means for a quadratic action.

This gives one precise candidate content for the sentence in the [mass endomorphism conjecture](#conj:mass-endomorphism) that "the graviton is the residual of $\Phi_{\mathrm{mass}}$ after subtracting the Higgs contribution": *graviton candidate 1* is a pairing fluctuation, and the proposition shows that its gauge part carries no mass term for the contraction action. What is **not** shown, and would be needed before the reading could be called a derivation:

1. that the gauge directions *exhaust* the zero modes of a non-degenerate pairing action (the contraction action is rank one, so it cannot decide this);
2. that the physical (non-gauge) fluctuations transform as a spin-$2$ isotypic piece, i.e. the symmetric traceless part of $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ of a spin-$1$ object;
3. any relation between the contraction action and $\Phi_{\mathrm{mass}}$ itself.

No physical constant, dimension or signature enters the proposition or this reading.
