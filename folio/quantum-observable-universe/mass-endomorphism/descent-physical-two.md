At dimension $2$ the physical content is that of a closed surface, and one feature separates it sharply from the stratum above.

1. **Primordial vortex.** Vortex filaments are curves inside a surface. They may intersect, and intersection number is defined, but they cannot knot: knotting needs codimension $2$, which a curve attains only in a $3$-manifold. The knot-theoretic content of the mass therefore does not exist at this stratum.
2. **Mass is central.** The surface $\Sigma$ of the [mass identity](#def:mass-identity) is the base $M_k$ itself, and it is closed, so $\partial\Sigma = \varnothing$. In a crossed module the $2$-holonomy maps under the feedback to the holonomy of its boundary; with no boundary that image is trivial, so the surface holonomy lies in the kernel of the feedback, which is central. Dimension-$2$ mass is accordingly an abelian invariant of the whole surface. The non-abelian content of a $2$-holonomy is exactly its boundary holonomy, and here there is none.
3. **Lepton generations.** The base admits vertical harmonic forms up to degree $2$ ([Betti numbers on branes](#rem:betti-numbers-brane)), so the generation count is not yet collapsed to the single fundamental state of the $1$-dimensional stratum. The Euler characteristic is unchanged by the :refterm[descent]{#descent} ([Euler characteristic stability](#prop:euler-char-stability)), so $\chi = -6$ is carried down from above rather than produced here; for the genus-$4$ realisation this is $b_0 = 1$, $b_1 = 8$, $b_2 = 1$.
