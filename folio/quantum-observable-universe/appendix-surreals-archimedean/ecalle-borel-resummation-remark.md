A divergent Gevrey-1 asymptotic series acquires a canonical analytic
meaning through the Borel transform and its Laplace–Borel sums
\cite{ecalle1981} ([Écalle–Borel resummation](#def:ecalle-borel-resummation)).

The formal Borel transform is the coefficient-wise division $a_n \mapsto a_n / n!$. When $\tilde f$ is Gevrey-1, its Borel coefficients grow at most
geometrically, so $\mathcal{B}\tilde f$ is a germ analytic on the
disc $|\xi| < A$. The lateral sum $\mathcal{L}^{\theta} \tilde f$ is
analytic and asymptotic to $\tilde f$ (Gevrey-1) on a half-plane
bisected by the conjugate direction.

Along a Stokes ray the integral through the singularity $\omega$
is obstructed, and only the two lateral sums
$\mathcal{L}^{\theta^+}$, $\mathcal{L}^{\theta^-}$ are defined. Their difference is exponentially
small, of order $e^{-\omega z}$ — the Stokes phenomenon. The median resummation is the canonical average of the two lateral sums across a Stokes ray; in
the simplest one-singularity case it is
$\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\mathcal{L}^{\theta^+} + \mathcal{L}^{\theta^-}\bigr)$.
For a series with real
coefficients whose Stokes ray is the positive real axis, the median
sum is the unique *real and single-valued* resummation.
