**Example.**
Helium-4 $\mathcal{A}(2,2)$ contains two protons, two neutrons, and two electrons. It forms the doubly-magic alpha particle.

## Derivation

Twelve $\gamma\gamma$ pair productions generate four nucleons. Each is confined by $\mathcal{B}_3$ and stabilized by LP-derived gluon crossings. Inter-nucleon typed crossings link them: $+\sigma$ for $p$-$p$, mixed for $p$-$n$, and shifted $-h_{\mathrm{A}}$ for $n$-$n$.

## Nucleus NF ($\alpha$ particle)

$$
  \mathrm{NF}(\mathcal{N}(2,2)) = +0.041\gamma - 0.133\sigma_0 + 0.169\sigma_1 - 0.071 L_+ - 0.297 L_- + 1.040 e^-.
$$

$$
  \mathrm{net}(\mathcal{N}(2,2)) \approx +0.749
$$

The chain of inter-nucleon crossings among the four nucleons puts a large coefficient on the electron word $\sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_0$. The $e^-$ term dominates.

## Full atom NF (with $1s^2$ shell)

Attach two electron words $\sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_0$ (each closing to the Hopf link), as in the hydrogen example:

$$
  \mathrm{NF}(\mathcal{A}(2,2)) = +0.236\gamma - 0.061\sigma_0 + 0.176\sigma_1 + 0.384 L_+ - 0.037 L_- + 1.190 e^-.
$$

$$
  \mathrm{net}(\mathcal{A}(2,2)) \approx +1.889
$$

**Stability analysis.**
He-4 retains minor deficit in $\sigma_0 = -0.061$ and $L_- = -0.037$. He-3 exhibits zero deficit because all normal form coefficients are positive. He-4 exhibits two small deficit channels. While both are stable, He-4 attains a lower Markov trace than He-3 ($1.037$ versus $1.230$). This compares He-4 with He-3 only; no other $A = 4$ configuration is computed here.

## Comparison: He-3 vs He-4

The He-3 atom achieves full deficit closure with only three nucleons. He-4 adds a second neutron. This fourth nucleon slightly reopens the $\sigma_0$ and $L_-$ deficit channels while reducing the overall Markov trace.
