## Two-layer Gröbner architecture: microscopic $\mathrm{HeckeGS}$ versus macroscopic $\mathrm{ChamberGS}$

Nuclear assembly separates into two distinct reduction layers:

1. **Layer 1 ($\mathrm{HeckeGS}$ --- noncommutative braid reduction).** Confluent Gröbner--Shirshov reduction in $H_n(q) \twoheadrightarrow TL_n(q)$, following **Leonid A. Bokut** \cite{bokut2001} and **Seok-Jin Kang**, **Kyu-Hwan Lee**, **Kyungyong Lee**, and **Hee Oh** \cite{kangleeleeoh2002}, evaluates the exact rational trace polynomials of elementary colour-singlet motifs as functions of $q$.
2. **Layer 2 ($\mathrm{ChamberGS}$ --- commutative toric reduction over motifs and capacitated shells).** Assembling $(Z, N)$ from integer motif and Mayer--Jensen shell-slack counts $u \in \mathbb{N}^m$ under $A u = b(Z, N)$ is a parametric integer program in the sense of **Jack E. Graver** \cite{graver1975}, **Pasqualina Conti** and **Carlo Traverso** \cite{conti-traverso1991}, and **Bernd Sturmfels** and **Rekha R. Thomas** \cite{sturmfels-thomas1997}, dual to the regular-triangulation and secondary-polytope flips of **Louis J. Billera**, **Israel M. Gel'fand**, and **Bernd Sturmfels** \cite{billera-gelfand-sturmfels1990}. Working directly over $\mathbb{N}^m$ via finite Graver and reduced Gröbner test sets automatically produces integer remainder monomers---accounting for odd--even pairing staggering without fractional half-$\alpha$ continuous relaxation artifacts---and unites regime switches, magic-shell closures ($w_s = 0$), and $\alpha$-emission walls ($x_{\mathrm{par}} \to x_{\mathrm{dau}} x_\alpha$) as Gröbner flips across chamber facets, while relegating interior-point solvers to offline discovery oracles.

## Finite-nucleon kinks without a thermodynamic limit

In canonical statistical mechanics at positive temperature, **Tsung-Dao Lee** and **Chen-Ning Yang** \cite{lee-yang1952} proved that a finite sum of positive analytic Boltzmann weights is smooth, so non-analytic phase boundaries arise only as partition-function zeros pinch the real axis in the thermodynamic limit $N \to \infty$. Ground-state variational minimization over a finite motif set $F$ or a finite-dimensional Wedderburn spectrahedron obeys the opposite geometry---the zero-temperature Maslov dequantization limit developed in tropical geometry by **Oleg Viro** \cite{viro2001}. For any finite nonempty family $F$ over an ordered field, the envelope $E(\theta) = \min_{i \in F}(a_i + b_i \theta)$ is concave and piecewise-linear. At any chamber wall $\theta^*$ where the active minimizer switches from branch $i$ of slope $b_i$ to branch $j$ of strictly smaller slope $b_j < b_i$, the left and right chemical potentials jump discontinuously by $b_i - b_j > 0$ at finite $|F|$, producing exact kinks at finite nucleon numbers ($A = 4, 16, 40, \dots$ and coordination $k = 12$) with no $N \to \infty$ limit.

## Magic-shell closures as normal-cone shadow-price jumps

In the dual program, these finite-$A$ vertices correspond to multi-dimensional normal cones where the dual shadow prices $u_\lambda^-$ and $u_\lambda^+$ on either side of a shell closure cease to agree. **Maria Goeppert Mayer** \cite{mayer1949} and **Otto Haxel**, **J. Hans D. Jensen**, and **Hans E. Suess** \cite{haxel-jensen-suess1949} identified the nuclear magic numbers $\{2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126\}$ empirically through abrupt drops in nucleon separation energies across closed spin--orbit shells. In many-body ground-state theory, **John P. Perdew**, **Robert G. Parr**, **Mel Levy**, and **Jose L. Balduz Jr.** \cite{perdew-parr-levy-balduz1982} established piecewise linearity of $E(N)$ between integer particle numbers together with exact derivative discontinuities (chemical-potential jumps) at closed shells, while **Brecht Verstichel**, **Helen van Aggelen**, **Dimitri Van Neck**, **Paul W. Ayers**, and **Patrick Bultinck** \cite{verstichel-vanaggelen2009} and **Valentin Zauner**, **David Draxler**, **Laurens Vanderstraeten**, **Jutho Haegeman**, and **Frank Verstraete** \cite{zauner-verstraete2016} showed that first-order transitions and shell kinks correspond respectively to flat exposed facets and non-smooth vertices of the reduced-density-matrix spectrahedron.
