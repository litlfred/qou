The selection rule is carried by a conjecture class with two fields: a coupling
assigning a colour system to each nucleon-pair channel, and the constraint that the
coupling equals $\mathrm{select}$. Stated that way the class is **inhabited by
construction**. Taking the coupling to be $\mathrm{select}$ itself and the constraint to
be reflexivity exhibits a witness, so the class constrains nothing as a mathematical
object.

The opposite was previously asserted. A class
whose only field is pinned to a definition already in scope cannot fail to be
satisfiable, and an argument that leaned on its non-vacuity would be leaning on
nothing.

## Physical identification versus algebraic non-vacuity

The class does not assert that *some* coupling satisfies the rule. It asserts that the
coupling induced by the **physical** interface is $\mathrm{select}$. No formal
inhabitation can establish that, and none is claimed: what the witness shows is that
the mathematical content of the class is empty, leaving its physical content intact
and entirely unproved. The four consequences drawn from it therefore stay conditional
--- charge symmetry between $pp$ and $nn$, the forcing of the like-nucleon channels
onto $T_1$, the freeing of the deuteron channel alone, and the grouping of the
symmetric $pn$ state with the like-nucleon pairs --- but the hypothesis they wait on
is a physical identification rather than an open theorem.

Inhabitation is recorded as a statement about non-emptiness and deliberately **not**
as a typeclass instance. Registering an instance would let resolution discharge the
hypothesis automatically, silently converting all four conditional theorems into
unconditional statements about $\mathrm{select}$. Those statements would be true, they
would follow by evaluating a two-line table, and they would be worthless --- while
reading, to anyone scanning the file, as though the physics had been proved. Keeping
the fact as a non-emptiness statement records it without changing what anything
downstream depends on.
