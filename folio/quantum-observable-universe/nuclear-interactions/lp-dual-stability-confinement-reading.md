Two further properties hold for the specific six-channel Gram matrix at the substrate. They are computed, not derived from linear-programming duality, and are recorded here rather than in the proposition:

- The dual shadow prices are bounded by $|y_i^*| \leq \kappa(G) \cdot \|t\|$, where $\kappa(G) \approx 7.0$ is the condition number of $G$. Linear-programming duality alone does not bound shadow prices by the condition number, so this is a property of this matrix.
- The $B_3$ sub-braid $\sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1}$ is LP-unstable: $G\vec{x}$ has a negative $\sigma_0$ component. Partial confinement is therefore not sufficient, and all three units are required.

Individual quarks are LP-stable, but the $B_3$ sub-unit is not. This is the precise algebraic statement of confinement. The gluon field contains an LP-unstable intermediate, but the full 3-fold Borromean product restores stability. The confinement mechanism is not that quarks are unstable, but that the binding operator requires a topologically complete structure to be stable.

The negative eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min} \approx -0.956$ (a historical figure; the canonical-weight value is $\approx -0.7527$, see the [Frobenius Gram matrix](#prop:frobenius-gram-matrix)) is the reason the LP has a global minimum at $\mathrm{tr}_M = 0$. The braid group realizability constraint prevents this.
