Two further properties are claimed for the specific six-channel Gram matrix at the substrate. They are not derived from linear-programming duality, and they are not currently established numerically: the Gram matrix built by the confinement operator-selection computation is singular, so it has no finite condition number, and that computation does not produce shadow prices. They are recorded here rather than in the proposition:

- The dual shadow prices are bounded by $|y_i^*| \leq \kappa(G) \cdot \|t\|$, where $\kappa(G)$ is the condition number of $G$ (a value $\approx 7.0$ was recorded earlier, but the Gram matrix the computation now builds is singular, so this figure is not reproduced). Linear-programming duality alone does not bound shadow prices by the condition number, so the bound would have to come from the matrix itself; for a singular $G$ ($\kappa(G) = \infty$) it says nothing.
- The $B_3$ sub-braid $\sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1}$ is LP-unstable: $G\vec{x}$ has a negative $\sigma_0$ component. Partial confinement is therefore not sufficient, and all three units are required.

Individual quarks are LP-stable and the $B_3$ sub-unit is LP-unstable. In this reading, that is what confinement means algebraically: the gluon field contains an LP-unstable intermediate, and stability returns only for the full 3-fold Borromean product. The binding operator is stable on topologically complete structures and on no proper part of them.

The negative eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min} \approx -0.956$ (a historical figure; the canonical-weight value is $\approx -0.7527$, see the [Frobenius Gram matrix](#prop:frobenius-gram-matrix)) gives the unconstrained LP a global minimum at $\mathrm{tr}_M = 0$. Requiring the solution to be realised by a braid excludes that minimum.
