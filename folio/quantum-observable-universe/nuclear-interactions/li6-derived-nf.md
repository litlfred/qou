**Example.**
Lithium-6 ($Z = 3$, $N = 3$, $A = 6$) is the lightest bound nucleus beyond the doubly-magic helium-4 core; no $A = 5$ nucleus is bound. Its construction demonstrates the operator transition from Regime II ($L_\pm$, whose closure in $B_3$ is the unknot) back to Regime III (bare crossing) after the He-4 magic shell closes.

## Inductive construction

The He-4 core is built by Regime I $\to$ II operators. The fifth step of the construction still uses an $L_\pm$ operator. The intermediate $A = 5$ configuration is a step of the construction, not a bound nucleus. The sixth nucleon transitions to the bare mixed crossing $C_{pn}$ (Regime III).

## Normal form at $q_0$

$$
  \mathrm{NF}(\mathcal{A}(3,3))
  \approx
  +0.087\gamma + 0.130\sigma_0 + 0.455\sigma_1 + 0.205 L_+ + 1.288 L_- + 0.589 e^-
$$

$$
  \mathrm{net} \approx +2.754, \qquad
  |\mathrm{dem}| = 0
  \quad \text{(all coefficients positive).}
$$

## Physical interpretation

1. All coefficients are positive. Li-6 is deficit-free. This is consistent with Li-6 being the lightest stable lithium isotope.
2. The dominant term is $L_-$. This reflects the strong neutron-proton $L_-$ binding inherited from the He-4 core.
3. The operator transition at step 6 marks the passage from the magic-shell regime to the open-shell regime. Beyond this point, nucleons interact via bare crossings. The collective shell effect diminishes.
4. The $e^-$ coefficient is positive. Li-6 therefore carries no deficit in the electron word $\sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_0$, unlike the confined nucleons, whose $e^-$ coefficients are negative.
