Gröbner--Shirshov reduction produces a canonical normal form for every braid word in $H_A(q)$ ([Unique nuclear normal form via Gröbner reduction in $H_n(q)$](#prop:nuclear-groebner-reduction)), yet not every normal form represents a physically bound composite state. Confinement in this paper operates across two distinct algebraic layers whose roles must be kept separate.

- **Stage 1 ($\mathrm{HeckeGS}$ --- microscopic colour confinement as the alphabet filter).** Noncommutative Bokut--Shiao and Kang--Lee--Lee--Oh reduction in $H_n(q) \twoheadrightarrow TL_n(q)$ \cite{bokut2001,kangleeleeoh2002} acts at the strand level ([Borromean topological confinement](#rem:borromean-topological-confinement)): isolated quarks and open colour strands carry negative trace weight or divergent vortex cost and are excluded from the macroscopic generator alphabet, so only closed $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$-singlet motifs $x_p, x_n, x_d, x_t, x_h, x_\alpha, \dots$ enter nuclear assembly with finite trace costs $c_j(q_0)$. Driving the local deformation parameter toward the golden-ratio wall $q_{\mathrm{gold}} = 1/\varphi^2$ alters this Stage-1 alphabet itself, admitting colour--flavour-locked Hopf diquark pairs $x_{qq}$ while dissolving three-strand Borromean baryon columns.
- **Stage 2 ($\mathrm{ChamberGS}$ --- macroscopic nuclear stability via toric binomial orientation).** At fixed vacuum modulus $q_0$, assembling $(Z, N)$ from colour-singlet motifs $u \in \mathbb{N}^m$ under nucleon conservation $A u = (Z, N)$ defines a commutative toric ideal $I_A \subset \mathbb{Q}[x_1, \dots, x_m]$ \cite{sturmfels-thomas1997}. Nuclear binding versus dissociation is governed by the cost orientation of binomials in $I_A$: exothermic fusion rules $x_A x_B \to x_{AB}$ ($c_{AB} < c_A + c_B$) bind clusters inside the valley of stability, whereas outward Gröbner flips $x_{AB} \to x_A x_B$ govern nucleon drip lines ($x_{{}^5\mathrm{He}} \to x_\alpha x_n$), the endothermic ${}^8\mathrm{Be}$ resonance ($x_{{}^8\mathrm{Be}} \to x_\alpha^2$) paired with the degree-$3$ Hoyle fusion binomial $x_\alpha^3 \to x_{{}^{12}\mathrm{C}}$, and Coulomb-driven heavy $\alpha$-emission ($x_{(A, Z)} \to x_{(A-4, Z-2)} x_\alpha$ for $Z \ge 84$).

| Feature | Stage 1: Microscopic $\mathrm{HeckeGS}$ | Stage 2: Macroscopic $\mathrm{ChamberGS}$ |
|:--------|:----------------------------------------|:------------------------------------------|
| Ambient algebra | Noncommutative Hecke quotient $H_n(q) \twoheadrightarrow TL_n(q)$ | Commutative toric ideal $I_A \subset \mathbb{Q}[x_1, \dots, x_m]$ |
| Physical scale | Quark colour confinement | Nuclear binding, drip lines, and $\alpha$-stability |
| Mechanism | Excludes open colour strands; fixes singlet alphabet and trace costs $c_j(q_0)$ | Orients conserved reaction binomials $x^{g^+} \to x^{g^-}$ by $c \cdot g > 0$ |
| Phase boundary | Modulus shift $q_{\mathrm{local}} \to 1/\varphi^2$ admits $x_{qq}$ diquarks | Chamber-wall flips $c \cdot g = 0$ trigger drip dissociation or $\alpha$-decay |

In the continuous conic relaxation of Stage 2, operator selection reduces to a finite-dimensional linear program over the normal-form basis. Strong duality assigns a nonnegative shadow price to each irreducible Wedderburn block, and Frobenius reciprocity expresses every downstream binding observable as a Gram-inverse dual pairing against the active support. Lifting scalar positivity to matrix positivity yields a semidefinite program whose feasibility is equivalent to a Hecke spectral-gap condition, while integer-lattice constraints recover the exact discrete normal forms of $\mathrm{ChamberGS}$.
