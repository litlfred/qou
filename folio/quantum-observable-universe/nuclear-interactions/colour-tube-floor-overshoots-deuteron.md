The colour-tube channel weight is a natural candidate for the interface tilt $d$: it
already assigns a rational weight to each pair of colour sheets, it is normalised, and
it is upstream of the same $D_6$ structure the interface sits in. It cannot supply the
tilt, and the reason is a category error rather than a numerical miss.

## The category error

The colour-tube weight is a function
$W_{c_1c_2}(F_1, F_2)$ of two colour sheets and two **nucleon colour contents**, where
a content is a definite assignment of a quark flavour to each of the three sheets ---
$(u,u,d)$ for the proton, $(u,d,d)$ for the neutron. The chirality sign it is built
from compares $F_1$ at sheet $c_1$ against $F_2$ at sheet $c_2$, so the whole
construction presupposes that each nucleon of the pair *has* a definite flavour
content.

The deuteron does not. Its flavour state is the antisymmetric line
$(pn - np)/\sqrt2$, on which $F_1$ and $F_2$ are not definite: the state is a
superposition of the two content assignments, and the weight is not a linear
functional on the flavour space but a function of the labels. Evaluating
$W_{c_1c_2}$ on the deuteron therefore requires a choice of branch that the
construction does not supply, and any such choice breaks the exchange antisymmetry
that defines the channel. The obstruction is structural: it is a property of what the
weight is allowed to depend on, exactly as in
[the content-keyed refutation](#prop:content-keyed-interface-refuted), and no
adjustment of the weights repairs it.

## The numerical corroboration, and what backs it

Independently of the above, the family the rule generates has been scanned for the
$pn$ binding it would predict. **The figures in this paragraph are provisional: they
are recorded from an exploratory scan and are not yet reproduced by a committed
producer, so no witness backs them.** <!-- literal-source: provisional exploratory hundredth-grid scan over six colour alignments -->
Scanning the mixing parameter on a hundredth
grid across the six colour alignments at thirty digits gives a family *floor* of
$B_{pn} = 2.29580\ \mathrm{MeV}$, against the measured
$B_{\mathrm{AME}}({}^2\mathrm{H}) = 2.224566\ \mathrm{MeV}$ --- an overshoot of
$0.0712\ \mathrm{MeV}$, or $3.2\,\%$, by the *most favourable* member of the family.
<!-- literal-source: provisional exploratory S_3 alignment average and required mixing parameter -->
The $S_3$ average that confinement demands lands higher still, at
$2.46755\ \mathrm{MeV}$, some $11\,\%$ above target. Reaching the measured value
requires a mixing parameter of $0.35768$, which is to say it requires knowing the
answer first.

The two arguments are of different kinds and should not be conflated. The category
error excludes the construction outright; the scan reports that this family, on its
own and on the grid scanned, does not reach the target. The scan is corroboration, and
it is the weaker of the two.

## Convergence with the band separation

Three independent routes now arrive at the same wall. The interface band lies strictly
between the two isospin weights, so no admissible element realises either; the
colour-tube family's floor sits above the deuteron; and the content-keyed proposal is
refuted by the two $pn$ channels. Each closes a different way of trying to derive the
tilt from structure already in hand, and none of them is closed by a better parameter.
What they leave standing is the arena question: whether widening the algebra, rather
than re-weighting inside it, is where the tilt comes from.
