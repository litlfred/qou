## Beta decay emerges from the confined deficit

The $\mathcal{B}_3$-confined proton NF has residual deficit:

$$
  \mathrm{NF}(\mathfrak{p})
  =
  \underbrace{-0.100}_{[\mathbf{1}] < 0}\,\mathbf{1} + 0.036\sigma_0 + 0.998\sigma_1 + 0.099 L_+ + 0.009 L_- + \underbrace{-0.043}_{e^- < 0}e^-.
$$

- $[\mathbf{1}] < 0$, where $[\mathbf{1}]$ is the coefficient of the identity word $\mathbf{1}$: vacuum energy deficit—the source of the nucleon's mass.
- $e^- < 0$: **electron-word deficit**—the coefficient of the basis word $\sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_0$ (the electron, whose closure is the Hopf link) is negative, so the nucleon possesses a topological deficit corresponding to an electron. This is the categorical beta decay channel.

For the neutron, the deficit is larger: $[\mathbf{1}] = -0.202$, $L_+ = -0.010$, $e^- = -0.044$. The additional $L_+$ deficit and larger $[\mathbf{1}]$ deficit provide the energy that drives $\mathfrak{n} \to \mathfrak{p} + e^- + \bar\nu$.

## Confinement universality

The confined nucleon NF depends only on $q_0$ and the quark types. It is independent of the photon polarization $(\varphi_0, \varphi_1, \varphi_{01})$ that created the quarks.
