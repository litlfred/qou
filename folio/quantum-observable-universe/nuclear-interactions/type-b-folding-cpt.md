**Conjecture (Type-B folding closure).**
*The involution $\iota: s_i \leftrightarrow s_{n-i}$ of the Coxeter diagram folds the Weyl group $S_n$ into a Coxeter group of type $\mathrm{B}_{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}$ (of type $\mathrm{A}_1$ at $n = 3$), and folds the canonical atomic-braid Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$ into the Hecke algebra of that group with the weight function below. The folded algebra carries the bar involution and canonical basis of this weight function, and its GS normal forms are bar-involution invariant by construction. Stable matter = fixed points of the folding; antimatter = the $\iota$-orbit complement. Under this folding, the composite CPT operator decomposes naturally as a tensor of:*

- *C from color-sheet flavor conjugation*
- *P from Type-B Coxeter folding*
- *T from Markov-trace cyclicity*

Type-B folding provides an alternative categorical realisation where the role of "P (braid mirror)" is replaced by "the Coxeter-fold Z/2", and the bar-invariance emerges from the folded Hecke algebra rather than from explicit $\sigma_{i} \leftrightarrow \sigma_{i}^{-1}$ braid manipulation. The Type-B folding view is more compact but requires more category theory.

**Rank and generator weights.** The statement is made at the level of Coxeter groups and Hecke algebras for every $n$. For odd $n = 2m+1$ the corresponding fold of the $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ Cartan datum would put the adjacent roots $\alpha_m$, $\alpha_{m+1}$ in one orbit, so the conjecture makes no claim about Cartan data. The rank is the number of $\iota$-orbits on the $n-1$ simple reflections, which is $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$. At the level of Coxeter groups this is Lusztig's quasisplit case: with $s_i$ the simple reflection of $\alpha_i$, the $\iota$-fixed subgroup of $S_n$ is a Coxeter group generated by the longest elements of the $\iota$-orbits, and the folded Hecke algebra carries the weight function given by their ambient lengths \cite[Sec.~16.2]{lusztig2003}. The two parities differ only at the middle of the diagram. For even $n$ the middle root $\alpha_{n/2}$ is fixed, so its generator $s_{n/2}$ has weight $1$; the other generators $s_i s_{n-i}$ have weight $2$. For odd $n = 2m+1$ the middle roots $\alpha_m$ and $\alpha_{m+1}$ are adjacent and form one orbit, so its generator is $s_m s_{m+1} s_m$ of weight $3$; at $n = 3$ this is the whole folded group, of type $\mathrm{A}_1$. For $n \ge 4$ the weights are unequal, so the folded algebra is a Hecke algebra with unequal parameters. With the ambient parameter equal to $q$, as for $H_n(q)$, these are $q^2$ on the generators $s_i s_{n-i}$ and $q$ or $q^3$ on the middle generator.

On the type-B side the Markov traces are classified: there is exactly one for each parameter $z$ and each sequence $(y_k)_{k\ge 1}$ of values on the elements $t_0't_1'\cdots t_{k-1}'$, and each restricts to Ocneanu's trace on the type-A subalgebra \cite[Def.~4.1, Thm.~4.3]{geck-lambropoulou-2004}. Which of these traces the T factor uses is not fixed by the conjecture.

**Scope at fixed $q$.** Three involutions send a Hecke letter $\sigma_i$ to a letter: $\sigma_i \mapsto \sigma_i^{-1}$, $\sigma_i \mapsto -\sigma_i^{-1}$ and $\sigma_i \mapsto -\sigma_i$. They form a Klein four-group, the composite of any two being the third. Let $P_+$ and $P_-$ be the idempotents of $\sigma_i$ for its eigenvalues $q$ and $-q^{-1}$, and let $e^- = \sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_0$ with mirror $e^+ = \sigma_0^{-1}\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_0^{-1}$, as in the [Breit–Wheeler vertex](#rem:breit-wheeler-vertex). The braid mirror $\sigma_i \mapsto \sigma_i^{-1}$ keeps $P_+$ and $P_-$ and sends $e^-$ to $e^+$, which is the profile of $C$ (of $CT$ if $q \mapsto q^{-1}$ is read as antilinear). The map $\sigma_i \mapsto -\sigma_i^{-1}$ exchanges $P_+$ and $P_-$ and sends $e^-$ to $-e^+$, which is the profile of $CP$. Only $\sigma_i \mapsto -\sigma_i$ has the profile of $P$: it exchanges $P_+$ and $P_-$ and sends $e^-$ to $-e^-$.

Neither $\sigma_i \mapsto \sigma_i^{-1}$ nor $\sigma_i \mapsto -\sigma_i$ is realised by an algebra map of $H_n(q)$ that is linear over the scalars, unless $q^2 = 1$. Both exist only as maps covering $q \mapsto q^{-1}$ on the scalars, and that map has no counterpart at the real substrate value $q_0$. Hence no letter-level map has the profile of $P$ at fixed real $q$.
