The [spatial realisation functor](#def:spatial-realization-functor) states that its target $M = \mathrm{Spec}(A)$ is an ordinary smooth manifold. That is an assertion of the definition, not a consequence of the axioms, and it is worth separating the two: which properties of $M$ the constructions of this paper use, and which are decoration.

**Required algebraic properties.** Four properties, each a property of the algebra $A$ in $\mathbf{C}$ rather than of a manifold.

1. *The points.* $M$ is the set of [categorical points](#def:categorical-point) $p \colon \mathbf{1} \to A$, and the local germ $A/\mathfrak{m}_{p}^{2} \cong \mathbf{1} \oplus \mathfrak{m}_{p}/\mathfrak{m}_{p}^{2}$ at $p$ is Zariski's description of a tangent space; it makes sense for any commutative algebra and gives a vector space of constant dimension exactly when $A$ is regular.
2. *The one-forms.* The module $\Omega^{1}(A)$ of Kähler differentials exists by hypothesis, and the constructions use that $\tau(\Omega^{1}(A))$ is the module of sections of a bundle — that is, that $\Omega^{1}(A)$ is a dualisable object of $\mathbf{C}$ of constant rank. For a finitely generated algebra over a field, $\Omega^{1}(A)$ finitely generated projective of rank $\dim A$ is equivalent to smoothness \cite{hartshorne-1977}; it is the same hypothesis under which the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg theorem identifies Hochschild homology with differential forms \cite{hkr-1962}.
3. *The contact form* $\varTheta_{A}$, which needs only $\Omega^{1}(A)$, the Frobenius form and the universal derivation.
4. *The [Reeb derivation](#def:categorical-reeb)*, which needs a torus action and the contraction $\iota_{\xi}$ on $\Omega^{1}(A)$.

Nothing uses a topology on $M$, a partition of unity, or the real numbers.

**Admissible replacements for the smooth-manifold hypothesis.** Any base on which $\Omega^{1}(A)$ is dualisable of constant rank: a smooth affine variety over a field, which is the case of the finite-field models of a later section of this chapter; a formal scheme, when $A$ is completed at a point; a smooth rigid-analytic space, for the $p$-adic models; a supermanifold, when $A$ is $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded; or a quotient stack $[M/G]$ when $A$ carries the action of a finite group such as the $S_{5}$ of Bring's curve, in which case the categorical points of the invariant subalgebra are the orbits. The proofs of this chapter go through verbatim in each case, because they never leave the category $\mathbf{C}$. The word "manifold" fixes the real-analytic case for the reader and is the case in which the physical interpretation is made; it is not what the theorems require.

**The singular base.** The one case the constructions exclude is a base whose Kähler module fails to be projective. The affine cone over Bring's curve is such a base: away from the vertex it is smooth, but at the vertex the fibre of $\mathfrak{m}_{p}/\mathfrak{m}_{p}^{2}$ jumps to the dimension of the ambient space, $\Omega^{1}$ is not locally free, and the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg identification fails. The link of that vertex — the boundary of a small neighbourhood of it — is a three-manifold fibred by circles over the curve with Euler number $2 - 2g = -6$ \cite{orlik-wagreich1971}, whose fundamental group is infinite; a quaternionic model of the cone describes the smooth fibre near a point of the curve rather than this link. The phrase "ordinary smooth manifold" therefore has a single function in the definition: it excludes the vertex. Stated as the categorical condition, that exclusion is a checkable hypothesis, and the generality of the previous paragraph follows without further work.
