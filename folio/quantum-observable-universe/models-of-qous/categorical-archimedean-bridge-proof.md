The claim is that the image of the categorical differential $d_q$ under the fibre functor $\tau$ equals the archimedean $q$-exterior derivative. 

The functor $\tau$ dictates the following geometric correspondences:
- The Frobenius form $\int_A$ maps to the contact integral $\int_M \alpha \wedge (d\varTheta)^n$.
- The maximal torus generator $\xi$ maps to the Reeb vector field, satisfying $\iota_\xi\varTheta = 1$ and $\iota_\xi d\varTheta = 0$.
- The categorical weight decomposition $\pi_H, \pi_V$ maps to the geometric splitting $\Omega^k(M) = \Omega^k_H(M) \oplus \varTheta \wedge \Omega^{k-1}_H(M)$ determined by the Reeb direction.

Let $\alpha \in \Omega^k(M)$ be a differential form. The vertical projection $\pi_V(d\alpha)$ isolates the component of $d\alpha$ with non-zero weight under the $T$-action. Geometrically, this $T$-action is the Reeb flow $\phi_t \colon M \to M$ generated by the Lie derivative $\mathcal{L}_\xi$. The non-zero-weight component lies entirely along the Reeb direction:

$$
  \pi_V(d\alpha) = \varTheta \wedge \iota_\xi(d\alpha)
  = \varTheta \wedge (\mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha - d(\iota_\xi\alpha)).
$$

For horizontal forms ($\iota_\xi\alpha = 0$), this simplifies to $\varTheta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha$. For arbitrary forms, write $\alpha = \alpha_H + \varTheta \wedge \beta$. Because $\mathcal{L}_\xi\varTheta = 0$, the identity $\pi_V(d\alpha) = \varTheta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi\alpha$ holds in all cases. Hence

$$
  \tau(d_q) = q^{-1} d + (1 - q^{-1})\varTheta \wedge \mathcal{L}_\xi,
$$

which matches the archimedean definition of $d_q$ ([archimedean $q$-exterior derivative](#ex:q-ext-deriv)).
