Archimedean models realise the substrate parameter $q$ as a smoothly varying real or complex number, so they see the substrate only through its differential-geometric shadow. This picture does not record the arithmetic of the $q$-commutation data itself—its integral form and its behaviour in positive characteristic. Recovering that structure requires completions carrying a non-archimedean valuation, in which $q$ is $p$-adic rather than continuous.

The concrete phenomenon these models expose is a lifting problem. The question is whether the substrate can be reconstructed from the algebra generated by its infinitesimal $q$-commutation relations. The Heisenberg uncertainty relation—read as the $q$-commutator $[\hat{X},\hat{P}]_q = i\hbar_q$—is the germ to be lifted. This construction is traced from the enveloping algebras onward, through the Lusztig integral form and its $q$-deformed PBW basis to the $p$-adic and finite-field realisations.
