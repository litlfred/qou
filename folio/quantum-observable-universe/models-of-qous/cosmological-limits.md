**Conjectural interpretation.** The parameter range of the substrate field $q$ models a cosmological trajectory for a quantum observable universe. The limits of $q$ represent a "big bang" and a "heat death".

Consider $M = S^3$ equipped with the standard contact form $\varTheta$ and the standard instanton on the Hopf bundle. As the instanton curvature concentrates near a point $p_0 \in S^3$, the substrate field $q$ partitions into two regimes:

- **Near $p_0$ ($q(p) \to \infty$, $\hbar_q \to 1$):**
  Uncertainty becomes maximal ($[\hat{X},\hat{P}]_q \to J$). Observables become maximally non-commutative. The quantum observable universe becomes fully quantum-mechanical, retaining no classical shadow. This is the "big bang" regime.

- **Away from $p_0$ ($q(p) \to 1$, $\hbar_q \to 0$):**
  The commutator $[\hat{X}, \hat{P}]_q$ vanishes. The Hopf algebra becomes cocommutative ($\sigma \circ \Delta = \Delta$). Observations commute, $q$-deformed operators reduce to their classical limits, and the transport equation reduces to classical parallel transport ($\nabla\psi = 0$). This is the "heat death" regime: a fully classical, frozen universe.

At both limits, the quantum observable universe structure degenerates:

- As $q \to \infty$, the Frobenius pairing $\langle f, g \rangle = \int_A(\mu(f \otimes g))$ loses non-degeneracy. Axiom (1) of the definition of a quantum observable universe fails. The universe ceases to be observable.

- As $q \to 1$, the $q$-Hodge star $\star_q$ reduces to the classical Hodge star $\star_1$. The quantum gauge group $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ collapses to the classical Lie group $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The deformation vanishes, and the substrate parameter carries no information.

The physically meaningful regime is conjectured to satisfy $1 < q < \infty$ strictly, where quantum and classical features coexist. Rigorously analyzing these limits requires controlling the singular behavior of the Frobenius form as $q \to \infty$. This remains an open problem.
