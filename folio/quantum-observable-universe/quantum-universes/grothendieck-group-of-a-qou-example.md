For $\mathbf{C} = \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$, with simple objects $V_n$ of dimension $n + 1$, the Clebsch–Gordan rule $V_1 \otimes V_n = V_{n-1} \oplus V_{n+1}$ makes $K_0(\mathbf{C}) = \mathbb{Z}[x]$ with $x = [V_1]$: each $[V_n]$ is the polynomial in $x$ determined by $x\,[V_n] = [V_{n-1}] + [V_{n+1}]$, and the rank of $[V_n]$ is $1$ while $\dim_q [V_n] = [n+1]_q$. The quantum group $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}_q(2))$ at generic $q$ has the same simple objects, the same Clebsch–Gordan rule, and hence the same Grothendieck ring; what changes with $q$ is not the group $K_0$ but the homomorphism $\dim_q$ out of it. That is the sense in which the quantum dimension, and not the Grothendieck class, is the $q$-dependent object \cite{bghl-2017}.
