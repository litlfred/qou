Continuing [rigid monoidal Vect](#ex:rigid-monoidal-vect), this example verifies the [quantum universe](#def:quantum-universe) axioms for $G = \mathrm{SU}(2)$.

**(i) Category.** $\mathbf{C} = \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ is the category of finite-dimensional complex representations. This is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear rigid :refterm[monoidal category]{#monoidal-category}. The $G$-action on tensor products is diagonal; on duals it is the contragredient.

**(ii) Frobenius--Hopf object.** $A = \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ is the coordinate ring. Its Hopf structure encodes the group operations (see [Table 1](#tbl:quantum-universe-su2-table1-data)). The [Frobenius pairing](#def:frobenius-pairing) is positive definite.

**(iii) Fibre functor.** $\tau \colon \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2)) \to \mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}$ is the forgetful functor. It is exact, faithful, and monoidal. By Tannaka--Krein duality, $G \cong \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$.

**(iv) Differential calculus.** The left-invariant calculus on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ provides $\Omega^1(A) = \mathrm{span}\{\omega_1, \omega_2, \omega_3\}$. The universal derivation $d \colon A \to \Omega^1(A)$ acts by $df = \sum_i (\partial_i f) \omega_i$.

**(v) Braiding.** In $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ the braiding is the flip $c_{V,W}(v \otimes w) = w \otimes v$, which is symmetric: $c_{W,V} \circ c_{V,W} = \mathrm{id}$. The first axiom asks for the well-ordering $\prec_c$ that a braiding induces on the Hecke basis, and a symmetric braiding induces none — the ordering is supplied only when $c_{V,V}^{2} \neq \mathrm{id}$, as the statement on braiding-induced orderings in the next chapter makes precise. So the example at $q = 1$ verifies axioms 2–4 and is the *symmetric point* of the family that verifies all four: $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}_q(2))$ for $q \neq 1$, whose braiding on $V \otimes V$ is the $R$-matrix $\check R(q)$ with eigenvalues $q$ and $-q^{-1}$ on the symmetric and antisymmetric parts, so that $\check R$ satisfies the Hecke relation $(\check R - q)(\check R + q^{-1}) = 0$ and is not symmetric \cite{kassel1995}. Items (i)–(iv) go through for the family with the same simple objects and the same Clebsch–Gordan rule, the Haar state in place of the Haar measure, and the three-dimensional left-covariant calculus in place of the left-invariant forms; what changes at $q \neq 1$ is exactly the braiding, and with it the ordering that axiom 1 requires. In the Hecke normalisation $\check R(q)$ is the flip plus a diagonal correction that vanishes at $q = 1$, which is the sense in which the symmetric example is the limit of the braided ones.
