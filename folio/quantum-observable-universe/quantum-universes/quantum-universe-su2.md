Continuing [rigid monoidal Vect](#ex:rigid-monoidal-vect), this example verifies the [quantum universe](#def:quantum-universe) axioms for $G = \mathrm{SU}(2)$.

**(i) Category.** $\mathbf{C} = \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ is the category of finite-dimensional complex representations. This is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear rigid :refterm[monoidal category]{#monoidal-category}. The $G$-action on tensor products is diagonal; on duals it is the contragredient.

**(ii) Frobenius--Hopf object.** $A = \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ is the coordinate ring. Its Hopf structure encodes the group operations (summarised in the table immediately following this example). The [Frobenius pairing](#def:frobenius-pairing) is positive definite.

**(iii) Fibre functor.** $\tau \colon \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2)) \to \mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}$ is the forgetful functor. It is exact, faithful, and monoidal. By Tannaka--Krein duality, $G \cong \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$.

**(iv) Differential calculus.** The left-invariant calculus on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ provides $\Omega^1(A) = \mathrm{span}\{\omega_1, \omega_2, \omega_3\}$. The universal derivation $d \colon A \to \Omega^1(A)$ acts by $df = \sum_i (\partial_i f) \omega_i$.

**(v) Braiding.** In $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ the braiding is the flip $c_{V,W}(v \otimes w) = w \otimes v$, which is symmetric: $c_{W,V} \circ c_{V,W} = \mathrm{id}$. The first axiom asks for the well-founded ordering $\prec_c$ that a braiding induces on a basis of $A$, and a symmetric braiding induces none — a strict ordering arises only when $c_{V,V}^{2} \neq \mathrm{id}$, as the analysis of braiding-induced orderings in the next chapter makes precise. The classical group $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ therefore verifies axioms 2 and 3 and forms the *symmetric boundary point* of the non-symmetric braided representation categories \cite{kassel1995} whose braiding $c_{V,V}$ carries distinct eigenvalues on the symmetric and antisymmetric summands of $V \otimes V$. Items (i)–(iv) hold across the deformed family with the same simple objects, the same Clebsch--Gordan rule, the invariant Haar functional in place of the classical Haar measure, and the three-dimensional left-covariant calculus in place of the left-invariant forms; what changes away from the symmetric point is the braiding, and with it the directional ordering required by axiom 1.
