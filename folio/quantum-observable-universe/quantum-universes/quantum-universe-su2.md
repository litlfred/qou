Continuing [rigid monoidal Vect](#ex:rigid-monoidal-vect), the [quantum universe](#def:quantum-universe) axioms are checked here for $G = \mathrm{SU}(2)$.

**(i) Category.** $\mathbf{C} = \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ is the category of finite-dimensional complex representations. This is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear rigid :refterm[monoidal category]{#monoidal-category}. The $G$-action on tensor products is diagonal; on duals it is the contragredient.

**(ii) Frobenius--Hopf object.** $A = \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ is the coordinate ring. Its Hopf structure encodes the group operations (see [Table 1](#tbl:quantum-universe-su2-table1-data)). The [Frobenius pairing](#def:frobenius-pairing) is positive definite.

**(iii) Fibre functor.** $\tau \colon \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2)) \to \mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}$ is the forgetful functor. It is exact, faithful, and monoidal. By Tannaka--Krein duality, $G \cong \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\tau)$.

**(iv) Differential calculus.** The left-invariant calculus on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ provides $\Omega^1(A) = \mathrm{span}\{\omega_1, \omega_2, \omega_3\}$. The universal derivation $d \colon A \to \Omega^1(A)$ acts by $df = \sum_i (\partial_i f) \omega_i$.

**(v) Braiding.** In $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ the braiding is the flip $c_{V,W}(v \otimes w) = w \otimes v$, which is symmetric: $c_{W,V} \circ c_{V,W} = \mathrm{id}$. The first axiom asks for the ordering $\prec_c$ that a braiding induces on the basis indexed by braid words. That ordering is well-founded for any braiding, the flip included, as the statement on braiding-induced orderings in the next chapter makes precise; non-symmetry $c_{V,V}^{2} \neq \mathrm{id}$ is not used on words. So the example verifies all four axioms. What the flip lacks is non-symmetry on states: each crossing has order two, so exchange on states closes into chains. The example is the *symmetric point* of a one-parameter family whose braiding is not symmetric: the representation categories of the quantum deformation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, constructed in the next chapter. On $V \otimes V$ the braiding of a member of that family acts on the symmetric and antisymmetric parts by two scalars with product $-1$ whose ratio is not $-1$, so it satisfies a quadratic relation with two distinct roots and is not symmetric \cite{kassel1995}. Items (i)–(iv) go through for the family with the same simple objects and the same Clebsch–Gordan rule, the Haar state in place of the Haar measure, and the three-dimensional left-covariant calculus in place of the left-invariant forms; what changes away from the symmetric point is exactly the braiding, which for a generic member of the family has infinite order on states. In a suitable normalisation the deformed braiding is the flip plus a diagonal correction that vanishes at the symmetric point, which is the sense in which the symmetric example is the limit of the braided ones.
