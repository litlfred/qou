Are the four axioms of a quantum universe independent?

**Temporal braiding.** Over a characteristic-zero field, Deligne's theorem shows every symmetric :refterm[monoidal category]{#monoidal-category} admits a fibre functor. Only a braiding is required, and only over a commutative ring, so no fibre functor is supplied. The braiding axiom is independent of the fibre functor.

**Frobenius--Hopf.** A general Hopf algebra need not carry a non-degenerate Frobenius form. The Frobenius condition $A \cong A^{\vee}$ is strictly stronger than the Hopf axioms.

**Symmetric Frobenius.** A Frobenius algebra need not be symmetric: the left and right traces induced by a non-symmetric form differ by the Nakayama automorphism, which is trivial exactly when the form is symmetric. The axiom is therefore a genuine restriction on the Frobenius--Hopf datum, not a consequence of it.

**Wedderburn semisimplicity.** A Frobenius object need not be semisimple: a truncated polynomial algebra $k[x]/(x^n)$ carries a non-degenerate Frobenius form and is local, hence has a single block and a nilpotent radical. Semisimplicity is therefore independent of the preceding three axioms. It is the assumption that does the work later: given it, the co-Casimir trace identity, proved in the mass-theory chapter, follows by linearity over the central idempotents rather than being assumed.

The differential calculus $\Omega^1(A)$ is a separate matter. For smooth algebraic groups Kähler differentials exist automatically, but for quantum groups away from the classical point a differential calculus need not exist or be unique, so its existence is an assumption rather than a theorem.

In full generality, all four axioms are independent.
