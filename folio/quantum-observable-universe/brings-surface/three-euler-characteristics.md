Three quantities in this paper have at one time or another been written $\chi$, and only two of them are Euler characteristics. They are kept apart by name, and this remark says what each one is, what it depends on, and what it equals for Bring's surface.

**The topological Euler characteristic** $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0)$ of the base curve is the alternating sum of its Betti numbers: for a closed orientable surface of genus $g$, $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = 2 - 2g$. For Bring's surface $g(M_0) = 4$, so $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = -6$ ([Euler characteristic of Bring's surface](#lem:brings-euler-characteristic)). Every downstream use of the number $-6$ — the count of torus-knot generators, the Hurwitz bound, the genus-ideal filtration, the Kummer divisibility $n \mid 6$ — consumes the genus, and this is the quantity it consumes. It has a Hochschild reading: by the [Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg theorem](#rem:hkr-theorem) the Hochschild homology of a smooth curve is $HH_n(M_0) = \bigoplus_i H^i(M_0, \Omega^{i+n})$, so $\dim HH_0 = 2$, $\dim HH_1 = \dim HH_{-1} = g$, and the alternating sum $\sum_n (-1)^n \dim HH_n(M_0) = 2 - 2g$ is $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0)$ again.

**The sheaf Euler characteristic** $\chi(M_0, V) = h^0(M_0, V) - h^1(M_0, V)$ of a vector bundle $V$ on the base always carries two arguments. By Riemann–Roch ([Chern character and Riemann–Roch](#rem:chern-character-and-riemann-roch)) it equals $\deg V + \operatorname{rk} V \cdot (1 - g)$, so it depends on the bundle, and it coincides with $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0)$ exactly when $\deg V = (2 - \operatorname{rk} V)(1 - g)$ — for example for a degree-zero bundle of rank two, which is the case of the state bundle. It is the Euler characteristic of a *cohomology*, not of a space.

**The quantum dimension** $\dim_q(X) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{id}_X) \in \operatorname{End}(\mathbf{1})$ of an object $X$ of a rigid monoidal category is a trace, not an Euler characteristic. For the fundamental object $V$ of $\mathrm{SU}_q(2)$ it is $[2]_q = q + q^{-1}$, positive and $q$-dependent. For the Frobenius–Hopf object $A$ the Peter–Weyl sum $\sum_{m \geq 1} [m]_q^2$ diverges at every real $q > 1$, and what one writes for $\dim_q(A)$ depends on a regularisation; neither of the two schemes measured, $\zeta$-regularisation (value $0$) and Hadamard finite part (value $-1/(q-q^{-1})^2$), gives $-6$ at the substrate parameter. Other schemes are untested, and a scheme chosen so as to give $-6$ would be a fit. Writing $\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{id}_A) = -6$ identifies a trace with a topological Euler characteristic; the two are different objects, and the equation is a collision of names rather than a theorem.

| symbol | what it is | depends on | for Bring's surface |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0)$ | topological Euler characteristic of the base curve | the genus only | $2 - 2 \cdot 4 = -6$ |
| $\chi(M_0, V)$ | $h^0 - h^1$ of a bundle $V$ on the base | the bundle | $\deg V + \operatorname{rk} V \cdot (-3)$; $-6$ for the rank-two degree-zero state bundle |
| $\dim_q(X) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{id}_X)$ | rigid trace of an object of $\mathbf{C}$ | the object and $q$ | $[2]_q \approx 2.011$ for $V$; regularisation-dependent for $A$ |

The rule that follows from the table is the one the notation register now enforces: a bare $\chi(A)$ does not occur; an Euler characteristic carries either the roman subscript $\mathrm{eu}$ or two arguments; a trace is written $\mathrm{tr}$ or $\dim_q$.
