The classical hyperbolic geometry of Bring's curve (genus $g=4$) admits a canonical $q$-deformation governed by the quantum hyperbolic plane $\mathrm{SU}_q(1,1)$.

While the Euclidean sector ($E_q(2)$) of the theory is governed by the Hahn–Exton $q$-Bessel function (the third kind, $J_\nu^{(3)}(x;q)$) as seen in the ladder coefficients, the $q$-hyperbolic geometry on the Bring's surface fibre is intrinsically described by **Jackson's $q$-Bessel functions of the first and second kinds** ($J_\nu^{(1)}(x;q)$ and $J_\nu^{(2)}(x;q)$). These functions appear as matrix elements of the representations of $\mathrm{SU}_q(1,1)$ and encode the "radial" components of the deformed hyperbolic structures.

The first-order contiguity recurrences satisfied by these coefficients ([First-order contiguity recurrence for the Jackson first- and second-kind $q$-Bessel coefficients (fibre-level)](../mass-theory/jackson-q-bessel-contiguity-recurrence.md)) supply the natural orthogonal basis for the $q$-hyperbolic deformation of Bring's curve. Their $q$-weights are adapted to the $\mathrm{SU}_q(1,1)$ Casimir invariants, unlike the third kind's $q^{k(k-1)/2}$ weight.
