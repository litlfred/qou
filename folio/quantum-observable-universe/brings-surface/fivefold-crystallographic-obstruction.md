By the classical crystallographic restriction (Nicolas Bourbaki \cite[Ch.~VI, §1.3]{bourbaki1968}; James E. Humphreys \cite[§2.8]{humphreys1990}), a lattice $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ ($n = 2, 3$) admits a rotational symmetry of order $k$ only when $2\cos(2\pi/k) \in \mathbb{Z}$, restricting $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$. Periodic lattices therefore cannot possess $5$-fold rotational symmetry, whose experimental realisation requires non-periodic quasicrystalline order as discovered by D. Shechtman, I. Blech, D. Gratias, and J. W. Cahn \cite{shechtman1984}.

The $5$-fold symmetry of Bring's surface uniquely breaks periodicity. It is carried by $T_{3,5}$, the weak force mediator, which is the only force violating parity and utilizing massive carriers ($W^{\pm}$, $Z^0$). The $5$-fold symmetry confines the weak force to local, aperiodic interactions, accounting for its weakness.
