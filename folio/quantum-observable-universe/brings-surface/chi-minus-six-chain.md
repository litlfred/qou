The following chain fixes $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = -6$, the topological Euler characteristic of the base curve ([the three Euler characteristics](#rem:three-euler-characteristics)). Steps 1--7 run forward from the QOU triple without presupposing Bring's surface. Step 5 determines the topological invariants $b = 6$, $g = 4$, and $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = -6$ of the moduli stratum $\mathcal{M}_4$ from the minimal hyperbolic uniform-chirality $\mathbb{Z}_3$-color Kummer cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ and cohomological Seifert-rank saturation in $H^{1,0}(\Sigma_g)$. Step 7 then selects Bring's surface $\Sigma_4 \in \mathcal{M}_4$ as the unique maximally symmetric genus-4 curve, and Step 8 applies the [motivic perpetual obstruction](#thm:motivic-perpetual-obstruction) theorem (proved via Route A: prime-ideal collapse, $C = a_0(-1) + 2 \cdot a_1(-1)$).

**Step 1: Start with the QOU triple.** The triple $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ has gauge group $G = \mathrm{SU}(2)$. The chain tracks the fundamental object $V$ — the two-dimensional simple object of the volume axiom — which is distinct from the Frobenius–Hopf object $A$; $\tau$ is the fibre functor.

**Step 2: The fibre functor produces a rank-2 bundle.** The fibre functor $\tau$ maps $V$ to a rank-2 vector bundle, still written $V$, over a compact Riemann surface $M_0$. Since $G = \mathrm{SU}(2)$, the determinant $\det(V)$ is trivial. Thus $\deg(V) = 0$.

**Step 3: Apply Riemann--Roch.** For a rank-$r$ bundle of degree $d$ over a genus-$g$ surface:

$$
  \chi(M_0, V) = d + r(1 - g) = 0 + 2(1 - g) = 2 - 2g
$$

**Step 4: Obstructions and hyperbolic volume require $g \geq 2$.** The lifting obstructions require $\kappa_n \neq 0$ at every level of the brane tower. The obstruction classes lie in $H^2(M_0, B)$, whose non-triviality requires $H^2(M_0, \mathbb{C}^*) \neq 0$ ($g \geq 1$), while negative topological Euler characteristic $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = 2 - 2g < 0$ (hyperbolic base) excludes the abelian torus $g = 1$ and requires $g \geq 2$.

**Step 5: Uniform-chirality $\mathbb{Z}_3$-color ramification and cohomological Seifert-rank saturation fix $b = 6$ and $|\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0)| = 6$.** Two explicit mechanisms determine the topological invariants $b = 6$ and $g = 4$ of the genus-4 moduli stratum $\mathcal{M}_4$ without reading Weierstrass points off Bring's surface:
1. *Minimal hyperbolic uniform-chirality $\mathbb{Z}_3$-color cyclic cover:* A general cyclic degree-$3$ (3-color) Kummer cover $y^3 = \prod_{j=1}^b (z - z_j)^{\epsilon_j}$ of $\mathbb{P}^1$ with exponents $\epsilon_j \in \{1, 2\}$ is unramified at $\infty$ if and only if $3 \mid \sum_{j=1}^b \epsilon_j$, with Riemann--Hurwitz relation $2g - 2 = 3(0 - 2) + 2b = -6 + 2b$ (total ramification $R = 2b$). While mixed chirality ($\epsilon_j \in \{1, 2\}$) would permit $b = 4, g = 2$ ($\epsilon = (1, 1, 2, 2)$) or $b = 5, g = 3$ (the Picard curve $\epsilon = (1, 1, 1, 1, 2)$), requiring uniform local chirality ($\epsilon_j = 1$ at every finite branch point, i.e., the cusp-free squarefree model $y^3 = \prod_{j=1}^b (z - z_j)$ forbidding anti-chiral $\epsilon_j = 2$ branch points) forces $3 \mid b$. At $b = 3$, $g = 1$ and $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}} = 0$ (excluded by Step 4). The unique minimal uniform-chirality branch-point count with $3 \mid b$ and $g \geq 2$ is $b = 6$, which fixes the topological surface invariants $2g - 2 = 6$, $g = 4$, and $|\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0)| = 6$.
2. *Cohomological Seifert-rank saturation under the discrete gauge-carrier cutoff:* A coprime torus knot $T_{r,s}$ embeds in $H^{1,0}(\Sigma_g)$ ($\dim H^{1,0}(\Sigma_g) = g$) if and only if its Seifert rank satisfies $g_S(r, s) = (r - 1)(s - 1)/2 \leq g$. Given the discrete gauge-carrier cutoff requiring the prime-power carriers $T_{2,3}$ ($g_S = 1$), $T_{2,9}$ ($g_S = 4$), and $T_{3,5}$ ($g_S = 4$) to embed while excluding the next prime carrier $T_{2,11}$ ($g_S = 5$), the bound $4 \leq g < 5$ forces $g = 4$, $\dim H^*(\Sigma_g) = 2 + 2g = 10$, and $|\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0)| = 6$.

**Step 6: The sign is negative.** The bundle has more obstructions than sections. Specifically, $\chi(M_0, V) = -6$ means $h^0(V) - h^1(V) = -6$, so $h^1(V) \geq 6$ (obstructions dominate).

**Step 7: Therefore $g = 4$ and maximal symmetry selects Bring's surface $\Sigma_4 \in \mathcal{M}_4$.** From $\chi(M_0, V) = 2 - 2g = -6$, the base curve lies in the genus-$4$ moduli stratum $\mathcal{M}_4$. (The sheaf Euler characteristic $\chi(M_0, V)$ coincides with the topological $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = 2 - 2g$ and the Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg alternating Betti sum $\dim HH_0 - \dim HH_1 - \dim HH_{-1} = 2 - 2g$ only because $\mathrm{rk}\, V = 2$ and $\deg V = 0$; see [the three Euler characteristics](#rem:three-euler-characteristics).) Within $\mathcal{M}_4$, the unique maximally symmetric genus-4 curve is Bring's surface $\Sigma_4 = \{[z_0 : z_1 : z_2 : z_3 : z_4] \in \mathbb{P}^4 \mid \sum_{i=0}^4 z_i^k = 0\text{ for }k = 1, 2, 3\}$, with automorphism group $S_5$ of order $120$. Note that $\Sigma_4$ is not itself a cyclic $\mathbb{Z}_3$-Galois cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ (every order-$3$ subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_3 \subset S_5$ acts fixed-point-freely on $\Sigma_4$, yielding an unramified degree-$3$ cover of a genus-$2$ curve): rather, on $\Sigma_4$ the six-fold ramification invariant $|\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(\Sigma_4)| = 6$ is realized directly by the $6$ fixed points of each transposition involution $\tau \in S_5$ (together with the $6$ simple branch points of the non-Galois $3$-sheeted colour-cubic projection to $\mathbb{P}^1$). Specifically, for the transposition $\tau = (0\,1)$, the isolated $(-1)$-eigenpoint $[1 : -1 : 0 : 0 : 0] \in \mathbb{P}^4$ has $\sum z_i^2 = 2 \neq 0$ and does not lie on $\Sigma_4$, while the boundary locus $z_0 = z_1 = 0$ on the $(+1)$-fixed hyperplane $z_0 = z_1$ forces $z_2 = z_3 = z_4 = 0$ by Newton's identities. Normalizing to $z_0 = z_1 = 1$, the Bring equations $2 + \sum_{i=2}^4 z_i^k = 0$ ($k = 1, 2, 3$) reduce via Newton's relations to the elementary symmetric values $e_1(z_2, z_3, z_4) = -2$, $e_2(z_2, z_3, z_4) = 3$, and $e_3(z_2, z_3, z_4) = -4$, so $(z_2, z_3, z_4)$ are the three roots of the characteristic transposition cubic
$$
  P_\tau(t) = t^3 + 2t^2 + 3t + 4 = 0,
  \qquad
  \Delta(P_\tau) = -200 = -2^3 \cdot 5^2 \neq 0.
$$
Because $\Delta(P_\tau) \neq 0$, the three roots are pairwise distinct and their $3! = 6$ permutations yield exactly $6$ fixed points $[1 : 1 : r_{\sigma(0)} : r_{\sigma(1)} : r_{\sigma(2)}] \in \Sigma_4^{\tau}$. Fixed-point sets of distinct transpositions $\tau \neq \tau'$ in $S_5$ are pairwise disjoint (overlapping transpositions $z_0 = z_1 = z_2 = 1$ force the contradictory elimination identity $60 = 0$, while disjoint transpositions $z_0 = z_1 = 1, z_2 = z_3$ contradict $\Delta(P_\tau) = -200 \neq 0$), so the $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ transpositions in $S_5$ account for all $10 \times 6 = 60 = 4^3 - 4$ simple Weierstrass points of $\Sigma_4$ \cite{braden-disney-hogg-2022}. Under the degree-$2$ involution quotient $\Sigma_4 \to E_\tau = \Sigma_4 / \langle\tau\rangle$ onto the elliptic quotient curve $E_\tau$ of genus $g(E_\tau) = 1$ ($\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(E_\tau) = 0$) branched at these $b = 6$ fixed points, Riemann--Hurwitz yields $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(\Sigma_4) = 2\,\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(E_\tau) - 6 = -6$. Among compact Riemann surfaces of genus $g > 2$ whose Weierstrass points are simple, the automorphism group acts transitively on the Weierstrass points if and only if $g = 4$ and the surface is Bring's curve, or $g = 3$ and the surface lies in one explicit family \cite[Thm.~2]{reyescarocca2022}.

**Step 8 (motivic-Galois reading).** *Steps 4 and 5 lift to a single algebraic statement at the level of the [motivic aether](#def:motivic-substrate). The condition $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = -6$ controls the value of the aether's motivic $L$-function at the boundary of analytic continuation. The non-vanishing of the obstruction classes $\kappa_n$ at every level becomes the non-vanishing of the Frobenius character at every finite place $p \nmid 6$. This is the content of the [motivic perpetual obstruction](#thm:motivic-perpetual-obstruction) theorem applied with $\chi_{\mathrm{eu}}(M_0) = -6$ ([Kummer tower](#rem:kummer-tower) §motivic-Galois). The "$g \geq 1$" condition (Step 4) is the cyclotomic-place criterion $H^1_{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p/\mathbb{Q}_p), \mu_n(1)) \neq 0$, realised at every $p$. The Riemann–Roch derivation is the archimedean shadow ($v = \infty$ realization) of this stronger adelic statement.*
