Each axiom of a quantum observable universe ([quantum observable universe](#def:quantum-observable-universe)) is realised on Bring's surface as follows.

**(i) Non-degeneracy.**
The Frobenius form $\int_A = h_q$ is the Haar state on $A = \mathcal{O}_q(\mathrm{SU}(2))$. Faithfulness of $h_q$ for compact quantum groups supplies the isomorphism $A \xrightarrow{ \sim } A^{\vee}$. The antipode $S$ and $h_q$ define the dagger $f^{\dagger} = h_q \circ \mu(S(f) \otimes -)$. This dagger is positive-definite ([positive definite dagger](#prop:positive-definite-dagger)) because the Haar state satisfies $h_q(a^* a) > 0$ for $a \neq 0$.

**(ii) Observability.**
The adjoint representation decomposes as $\mathrm{Ad}(A) \cong V_1 \oplus V_2 \oplus \cdots$ with no spin-$0$ summand. Thus $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0$ by the quantum Schur lemma. Geometrically, Bring's surface has no global holomorphic vector field (it has genus $g = 4 > 1$).

**(iii) Categorical volume constraint.**
The fundamental object $V$ of $\mathbf{C} = \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{SU}_q(2))$ has categorical dimension $2$. In the archimedean realisation, $V$ is the rank-$2$ defining representation carried by the state bundle fibre $\mathcal{S}_p \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ ([Bring's QOU 4-tuple](#rem:brings-qou-4tuple), item 3). The non-degenerate epimorphism $\epsilon \colon \Lambda^2 V \to \mathbf{1}$ is instantiated by the Levi form $L_\varTheta = -id\varTheta|_{H \times H}$ ([archimedean realisations](#rem:archimedean-realisations)). Because $L_\varTheta$ is alternating and non-degenerate on the rank-$4$ real distribution $H = \ker\varTheta$, it furnishes the symplectic pairing that makes $V$ self-dual. This forces $\det(g_V) = 1$, selecting $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ over $\mathrm{GL}(2)$. In the spectral coordinates ([Lagrange resolvents](#rem:lagrange-resolvents)), this constraint manifests as the Hermitian quadric $w_1 w_4 + w_2 w_3 = 0$, which is the $\mathrm{SL}(2)$ Plücker relation for $\Lambda^2 V$. The top-degree extension to the full color fibre is the spectral volume constraint. See Seiberg–Witten for the explicit symplectic form and its relation to $\epsilon$.
