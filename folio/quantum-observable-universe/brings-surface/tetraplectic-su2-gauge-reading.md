The bracket $[M_i, M_j] = 2M_k$ is a theorem about the quaternions. Reading it
as a *physical* symmetry is a separate step, and this remark keeps the two
apart.

A prior scope is settled first. The compact $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ triad lives on
the quaternionic model $\mathbb{H} = \mathbb{C}^2$, not on the link of the
Bring cone: that link is $UT(\Sigma_4)$, whose Reeb triad is the split
$\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R})$
([the Reeb twist lemma](#lem:bring-cone-reeb-quaternionic-twist)). So the
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ read here is the structure group of a bundle over the
localized states, which is the one place it survives in this layer — it is not
a triad of Reeb fields on the link, and nothing below depends on its being
one.

The reading is: the Reeb flow along the fibres of the Bring cone carries an
internal $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ structure on the localized states, so that the
[quaternionic spectral resolvents](#def:quaternionic-spectral-resolvents),
which already take values in $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$, are sections of a
bundle whose structure group is the one the triad generates. On the formal
side this is the unit-quaternion picture: $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is
$\{q \in \mathbb{H} : |q|^2 = 1\}$, acting on a quaternionic state $\psi$ by
$\psi \mapsto g\psi$ on the left and $\psi \mapsto \psi g$ on the right, and
the two actions commute. A quaternion's four real degrees of freedom are two
complex ones, so $\psi$ is an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ doublet, and the $\mathrm{U}(1)$
generated by a single imaginary unit sits inside that $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ — the
electroweak alignment.

Two limits are worth stating plainly.

First, the identification of the Reeb $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ with a gauge symmetry
of the physical theory is interpretive. The bracket forces the Lie algebra; it
does not by itself say which field the algebra acts on.

Second, the triad is an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and nothing larger. Its three Reeb
fields are the three dimensions of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, the adjoint — not a
three-dimensional fundamental representation. This layer is therefore not a
source of colour, and reading the count "three" as evidence for
$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ would be an equivocation between the adjoint and the
fundamental.

The first limit has a testable consequence, which is what keeps it from being
a disclaimer. Morita's quaternionic formulation of the
Weinberg–Salam theory \cite{morita1982} builds fermions and Higgs mesons over
$\mathbb{H}$ and finds that what it reproduces is the **left–right symmetric** gauge group,
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R \times \mathrm{U}(1)$, rather than the Standard Model one — precisely
because both the left and the right unit-quaternion actions are available and
neither is distinguished by the algebra. The same is true of the Reeb triad
here: the construction has no way to prefer $g\psi$ over $\psi g$.

So if the Reeb $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the electroweak $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, the
framework inherits a left–right symmetric gauge sector and owes an account of
how the right-handed factor is broken — Morita correlates that breakdown with
the existence of a Fock space for gauge singlets but not for quaternionic
states. That consequence is checkable against the framework, so the
identification is falsifiable and not only interpretive.
