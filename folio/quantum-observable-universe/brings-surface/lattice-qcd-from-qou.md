The non-abelian Stokes representation of the archimedean functor writes each
observable's holonomy as a surface-ordered integral of the $q$-deformed
curvature $F_q$ over a spanning Seifert surface $\Sigma$,
$$
  \mathrm{Hol}(\partial\Sigma) \;=\; \overset{\rightarrow}{\mathcal{P}}\!\!\int_{\Sigma} F_q .
$$
Discretizing $\Sigma$ — tessellating it into cells that each carry
quantum-group data, with the surface-ordered product in the role of the
$2$-holonomy — realizes a $q$-deformed lattice gauge theory on the surface.

Two points about the displayed formula. When $\partial\Sigma$ bounds a disk it is the operator form of the non-Abelian Stokes theorem \cite[\S2.1.3]{broda2002}. A Seifert surface of a nontrivial knot has genus $g \geq 1$ and is not a disk; there the theorem in its original form does not apply, and the holonomy is instead written as a product of surface-ordered factors over topologically trivial pieces of $\Sigma$ \cite[\S2.3.1]{broda2002}. The single surface-ordered integral above is exact only in the disk case.

Two features separate it from a naive lattice regularization. First, the
$q$-deformed surface state-sum is triangulation-invariant: refining the
tessellation leaves the holonomy unchanged, so no continuum limit in the cell
size is required and no per-cell coupling is free to tune. Second, the
deformation carries an intrinsic geometric scale hierarchy $q^{-n}$, so a
nested tessellation supplies its own regulator; the cell size is a
computational scaffold rather than a physical scale.

Ordinary lattice quantum chromodynamics is recovered as the $q\to 1$
(classical) shadow of this construction: the quantum-group cell data degenerate
to ordinary gauge holonomies and the surface-ordered product becomes the
plaquette product of the Wilson action.

This correspondence is a structural observation, not a theorem. Agreement
between a $q$-deformed surface state-sum and a lattice-gauge computation is an
empirical cross-check — of the same evidential tier as agreement with an
experimental datum, and strictly weaker than a formal derivation. A proof of
the state-sum / lattice-gauge correspondence remains open.
