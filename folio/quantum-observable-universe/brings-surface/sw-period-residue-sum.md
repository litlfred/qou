Let $\mathcal{S}_c \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$ be the exceptional divisor at a branch point $z_c$ ([Bring's surface smoothness](#rem:brings-smoothness)). Let $\lambda_{\mathrm{SW}}^{(n)}$ be the $n$-th prolonged Seiberg--Witten differential of the [MI--SW abelianisation](#prop:mi-sw-abelianisation), a meromorphic $1$-form with poles only at the branch points. Let $\gamma$ be a cycle enclosing a subset $E$ of those poles. Then

$$
\oint_{\gamma}\lambda_{\mathrm{SW}}^{(n)}
\;=\;
2\pi i \sum_{p\,\in\,E}\mathrm{Res}_p\,\lambda_{\mathrm{SW}}^{(n)}.
$$

**The enclosed set $E$ is part of the claim, not a bookkeeping detail.** The sum cannot range over *all* $p \in \mathcal{S}_c$ by appeal to the residue theorem on the compact Riemann surface: on a **compact** surface the residue theorem says the residues of a meromorphic $1$-form **sum to zero**, so summing over every pole would force $a_D^{(n)} = 0$ at every jet level — see `QOU.Brings.period_eq_zero_of_total_residue_vanishes`. Relatedly, $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$ has $H_1 = 0$ and so has no generating cycle for a period to be taken over; only a *bounding* cycle has a residue-sum value at all. Identifying $E$ was recorded here as the open geometric content the keystone `conj:sw-prolongation-period-eq-mean-qdim` still needs.

**$E$ is not identifiable, because under the classical-jet rule there is nothing to enclose.** Every residue vanishes *individually*, so every subset gives period zero and the choice of $E$ carries no information. The reason is local and needs neither a global primitive nor the compact surface: writing $z = z_c + t^2$ at a simple branch point, $d/dz = (1/2t)\,d/dt$ acts on Laurent coefficients as $f \mapsto \bigl(e \mapsto (e+2)f(e+2)\bigr)$ — the operator `ddz` that `QOU.Brings.RamificationParityMonodromy` already defines for this exact setting — and at the residue slot $e = -2$ its multiplier is $(-2)+2 = 0$. The $t^{-2}$ coefficient of a $z$-derivative is always zero, because the only input that could reach it is the *constant* term and differentiating a constant kills it (`QOU.Brings.ColourCubicBranchLocus.ddz_residue_slot_eq_zero`).

**The branch locus is also bar-symmetric, which excludes the natural contour.** $\Delta$'s coefficient list $[16,24,35,50,35,24,16]$ is palindromic, so $z^6\Delta(1/z) = \Delta(z)$ and the locus is invariant under the Hecke bar-involution $z \mapsto 1/z$. Reducing by $w = z + 1/z$ gives $16w^3 + 24w^2 - 13w + 2$, whose discriminant $-80000 < 0$ leaves one real root, located in $(-2,-1)$ by $C(-2) = -4 < 0 < 23 = C(-1)$; and a real $w$ with $|w| < 2$ gives $|z| = 1$ exactly. So the six branch points split $2+4$: two bar-**fixed** points lying *on* the unit circle, and one bar-orbit of four with reciprocal moduli. **The unit circle is therefore not an admissible contour** — it passes through two branch points, and it is the candidate the $\oint_{\mathcal{S}_c}$ notation above suggests.

So the open problem is *upstream* of $E$: which prolongation. The $q$-difference prolongation is untouched by this, as it is by [`prop:sw-classical-jet-exactness`](#prop:sw-classical-jet-exactness).

Subject to that, once $E$ is fixed the prolonged Seiberg--Witten period $a_D^{(n)} = \oint_{\gamma}\lambda_{\mathrm{SW}}^{(n)}$ functions as an **algebraic residue sum**: it is evaluated by collecting the local residues at the poles in $E$, with no contour integration beyond identifying which poles $\gamma$ encloses.

**Residue support and body order.** The branch-point poles support the residues. The $n$-th prolongation lifts the connection to the $n$-jet bundle $J^n E$, so the weight data of rank $n$ grades the residue family at level $n$; the residue sum therefore carries the **body-order grading**, and the period at vertical degree $n$ yields the $n$-body contribution. Each residue constitutes a per-block quantum-dimensional weight on the Wedderburn decomposition restricted to that level.

**What the grading cannot be.** It is *not* a one-to-one indexing of the level-$n$ residues by the blocks $\lambda \vdash n$, and the vertical degree $n$ does not increase the number of poles. The pole locus is the branch locus of the colour cubic, and that is **exactly $6$ points independent of $n$** — its discriminant $\mathrm{disc}_X = -16z^6 - 24z^5 - 35z^4 - 50z^3 - 35z^2 - 24z - 16$ is squarefree, with three conjugate pairs at $|z| \approx 0.943,\, 1.000,\, 1.061$. A bijection onto the blocks would need $p(n) \leq 6$, which fails from $n = 5$ ($p(5) = 7$, $p(6) = 11$, $p(n) \to \infty$). What grows with $n$ is the pole **order**, $X^{(n)} \sim (z - z_k)^{1/2 - n}$, not the number of poles. This bounds the *indexing*, not the equality $a_D^{(n)} = \mathrm{lw}(n-1)$: six residues may of course sum to what eleven quantum dimensions sum to.
