The Reeb decomposition ([Reeb decomposition](#ex:reeb-decomposition)) applied to a Descartes universe yields two classes of fermionic excitations ([fermion](#def:fermion-boson-decomposition)). The degree of the vertical component $\alpha_V$ distinguishes them. Forms with $\deg\alpha_V = 1$ correspond to electrons, while forms with $\deg\alpha_V = 2$ correspond to muons. More generally, each additional unit of vertical degree identifies a successive lepton generation. The constraint $\chi(\mathcal{S}) = -6$ bounds the number of such generations, since the Hodge decomposition of $\mathcal{S}$ admits only finitely many independent vertical harmonic forms. This generation-dependent structure produces testable predictions, including a $q$-correction to the proton charge radius that differs between muonic and electronic hydrogen ([the proton charge-radius section](#ssec:proton-radius)).
