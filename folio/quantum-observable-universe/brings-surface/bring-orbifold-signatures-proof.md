On the map, $S_5$ acts freely on darts, so every cell stabiliser is cyclic. At a vertex it is a conjugate of $\langle S\rangle \cong C_4$; at an edge it is generated by a conjugate of the transposition $A$, which reverses the edge; at a face it is a conjugate of $\langle AS\rangle \cong C_5$. Let $n_d$ count the cells whose $H$-stabiliser has order exactly $d$. These cells fall into $d\,n_d/|H|$ orbits, each a cone point of order $d$. By Burnside, $n_d$ is a sum of fixed-cell counts over elements of $H$. A $4$-cycle fixes $2$ vertices and each $C_4$ has two generators, so $n_4 = k_{(4)}$. A double transposition fixes $2$ vertices and each $C_4$ contains one, so $n_2^V = 2k_{(22)} - k_{(4)}$. A transposition fixes $6$ edges, so $n_2^E = 6k_{(2)}$. A $5$-cycle fixes $4$ faces and each $C_5$ has four generators, so $n_5 = k_{(5)}$. This gives (i). The genera agree with [the harmonic types](#prop:bring-harmonic-types), and the Euler characteristic check is integer arithmetic. For (ii), $(g,c)$ fixes a point only when $g$ is a transposition or a double transposition. The quotient complexes of the barycentric $(2,4,5)$ triangulation, on which $S_5\times C_2$ acts simply transitively on the $240$ triangles, give the table: orbits of cells, stabilisers, mirror edges, and a $2$-colouring for orientability. Lean checks this table against (iii), against the mirror criterion, and against the classification of compact surfaces. $\square$
