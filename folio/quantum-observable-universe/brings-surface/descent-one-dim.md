By the :refterm[descent]{#descent} theorem ([jet prolongation](#prop:jet-prolongation)), a two-complex-dimensional Descartes universe $(\mathbf{C}, \varTheta, G, \mathcal{S})$ with $M = \mathbb{C}^2$ descends to a one-complex-dimensional variety $M_0$. The stability of the Euler characteristic under :refterm[descent]{#descent} and lifting ([Euler characteristic stability](#prop:euler-char-stability)) ensures that the descended universe retains $\chi(\mathcal{S}) = -6$. The Descartes universe may therefore be studied using one-complex-dimensional varieties. The :refterm[descent]{#descent} may continue to dimension zero ([:refterm[descent]{#descent} reduction to zero](#rem:descent-reduction-zero)), where fibre data at a single point captures the entire quantum observable universe.
