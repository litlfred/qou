The trivial (identity) operator $\mathbf{1}$ implies a photon pair collision without inserted crossings. Every resulting structure derives strictly from the photons' inherent color content. The 16 cross-terms from the full expansion reduce to normal form via the Hecke and Yang--Baxter relations.

Here "trivial" means the **trivial Hecke representation** — the identity element $\mathbf{1} = T_e \in H_n(R, q)$, carrying no crossings and no closed strand. It is *not* the unknot $0_1 = T_{1,0}$, which is a closed strand and denotes the neutrino ([torus-knot generators](#prop:torus-knot-generators)). See the photon-vs-unknot rule in the [notation collisions](../notation/notation-collisions.md).

Each photon is $\gamma(\varphi) = \mathbf{1} + \varphi_0\sigma_0 + \varphi_1\sigma_1 + \varphi_{01}V$, whose fourth term is the chiral vertex $V = \sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1} = L_+ - h\sigma_0$, with $h = q - q^{-1}$.

**Vacuum channel ($\gamma$):** Co-polarized photons amplify the vacuum channel. The vertex weight $\varphi_{01}$ feeds the vacuum only at order $h$, through the remainders of terms (8), (14) and (16).
**Linear polarization channels ($\sigma_0, \sigma_1$):** Receive symmetric additive and Hecke contributions, plus rotation terms where a vertex meets a linear polarization. In term (15) the vertical crossing cancels the vertex's inverse crossing and $\sigma_0$ is emitted exactly.
**Positive Hopf link ($L_+$):** Fed by cross-polarization (term 7) and by the vertex itself, whose normal form contains $L_+$.
**Negative Hopf link ($L_-$):** Fed by reversed cross-polarization (term 10), by the vertex self-product (term 16), and at order $h$ by term (12).
**Electron channel ($e^-$):** Opens only when at least one photon carries a vertex weight. Its coefficient is $\varphi_{01}\psi_0 + \varphi_1\psi_{01}$, from terms (12) and (14).

If all polarizations vanish, $\gamma_1 \cdot \gamma_2 = \mathbf{1}$. $L_+$ and $L_-$ vanish when photons are co-polarized on a single axis. Electrons cannot emerge from photons without a vertex weight. The vertex acts as a polarization rotator: $V\sigma_1 = \sigma_0$ and $\sigma_0 V = \sigma_1^{-1} + hV$.
