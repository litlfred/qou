## Approach: from base $M$ to fibre $\mathcal{S}_p$

In the QOU archimedean tuple $(\mathbf{C}, \varTheta, G, \mathcal{S})$, the base manifold $M$ carries the smooth metric in which two free vortices propagate. As the vortex cores approach a brane point $p \in M$, the smooth metric becomes degenerate. The base coordinate ratio $z = \sqrt{q} - 1/\sqrt{q}$ drives the exceptional divisor $\mathcal{S}_p$ ([state bundle fibre](#def:exceptional-divisor)) into the foreground. "Below Planck scale" in QOU is a **categorical** statement, not a metric one. The relevant geometry switches from the base $M$ to the fibre $\mathcal{S}_p$, where the morphisms are knot operators.

The encounter unfolds in three moments:

1. **Approach.** Two vortex tubes propagate on $M$ and approach a common brane point $p$. The Reeb flow on $M$ tracks the motion.
2. **Crossing onto the fibre.** At the threshold $\partial F / \partial \mathbf{c} = 0$ on the colour-tube cubic, the base coordinate $z$ hits a discriminant zero. The smooth base structure pinches. The operative morphisms transition to those of $\mathcal{S}_p$ — the $\Phi_q$-piece $K_S$.
3. **Merging of fundamental groups.** The two vortex cores become parallel strands in a single braid. Their independent fundamental groups $\pi_1(\nu_1)$ and $\pi_1(\nu_2)$ amalgamate into the fundamental group $\pi_1(U \setminus K)$ of the merged knot complement. Here $U$ is a tubular neighbourhood of $p$, and $K$ is the link formed by the two strands inside it.

After moment (3), the encounter constitutes a knot-theoretic problem. The merged complement admits a hyperbolic structure. The SDP/LP machinery of [Chapter 11](#chap:nuclear-interactions) selects the lowest-energy link representative (SDP as thimble flow). Mostow rigidity (geometric confinement) makes the resulting topology—and hence the mass—discrete.
