Let $\mathcal{X}_5$ denote the smooth projective completion of the affine curve

$$
  \mathcal{X}_5 \colon y^2 =  x^5 - 1
  \qquad
  \text{in } \mathbb{A}^2(\mathbb{C}).
$$

The curve $\mathcal{X}_5$ forms a double cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ via the projection $(x, y) \mapsto x$. Writing $\zeta_5 = e^{2\pi i/5}$ for a primitive fifth root of unity, the ramification locus of this double cover consists of the six branch points

$$
  \{x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4, \infty\}  =
   \{1, \zeta_5, \zeta_5^2, \zeta_5^3, \zeta_5^4, \infty\}.
$$

Five distribute symmetrically on the unit circle; the last lies at infinity. By Riemann–Hurwitz, the genus of $\mathcal{X}_5$ is

$$
  g(\mathcal{X}_5)  =  \frac{6 - 2}{2}  =  2.
$$

Thus $\mathcal{X}_5$ is a **hyperelliptic** Riemann surface of genus $2$ in the Bolza family \cite{bolza1887}. Its hyperelliptic involution is $\iota \colon (x, y) \mapsto (x, -y)$.

**Relation to Bring's surface.** The coordinate $x$ on $\mathcal{X}_5$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_5$-equivariant observable. It identifies with the first Lagrange resolvent $w_1$ of [discrete Fourier transform diagonalises $C_5$ on the Bring's surface](#prop:lagrange-resolvent-transform), restricted to the cyclic subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_5 \subset S_5$ of [Bring's surface](#def:brings-surface). Under this identification, the five branch points $\zeta_5^k$ are the cyclic images of a single $\mathbb{Z}_5$-orbit.
