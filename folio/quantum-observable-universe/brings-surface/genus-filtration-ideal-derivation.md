The [genus-filtration ideal](#prop:genus-filtration-ideal) is established in four steps.

## Part 0: the genus is determined by the algebra

Let $(\mathbf{C}, A, \tau)$ be a QOU with $G = \mathrm{SU}_q(2)$. The braiding on $V_1^{\otimes n}$ yields the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$. The relevant strand counts $n = 2$ and $n = 3$ provide $5$ irreducible representations:
- $H_2(q)$ has $2$ irreps: $(2)$ trivial, $(1,1)$ sign.
- $H_3(q)$ has $3$ irreps: $(3)$, $(1,1,1)$, $(2,1)$.

At genus $g$, the embeddable torus knots $T_{r,s}$ with $(r-1)(s-1) \leq 2g$ and $\gcd(r,s) = 1$ are those whose Jones polynomials evaluate on $\Sigma_g$. Completeness requires these evaluations to span all $5$ irrep characters: the evaluation matrix $M_{ij} = \chi_{\lambda_j}(\sigma_i)$ must have rank $5$. 

Strand counts $m \geq 4$ contribute no new valid knots under the constraints. Thus, $H_2(q) \oplus H_3(q)$ forms the complete Hecke algebra of the QOU.

- At $g = 3$: only $4$ knots are available. The rank is $\leq 4 < 5$, so the set is incomplete. This violates observability.
- At $g = 4$: $T_{2,9}$ and $T_{3,5}$ bring the total to $6$ knots spanning $5$ irreps. The rank is $5$, satisfying completeness.
- At $g \geq 5$: further knots embed but add no new irreps. This redundancy predicts unobserved particles and violates minimality.

Therefore, $g = 4$ is the unique minimal genus where Hecke completeness holds.

## Part I: Chebyshev degree = Seifert genus

The Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_2(q)$ is generated by $T$ with $T^2 = sT + 1$ where $s = q - q^{-1}$. The generator $T$ has eigenvalues $q$ and $-q^{-1}$. The $p$-th power reduces as:
$$
  T^p =  a_p \cdot T +  a_{p-1} \cdot \mathbf{1}
$$
The coefficients $a_n$ satisfy the Chebyshev recurrence $a_{n+1} = s \cdot a_n + a_{n-1}$ with $a_0 = 0$, $a_1 = 1$. Here, $a_p = U_{p-1}(s/2)$. For odd $p$, letting $u = s^2$:
$$
  a_p(u) = \sum_{j=0}^{(p-1)/2} c_j u^j
$$
with leading coefficient $c_{(p-1)/2} = 1$. The polynomial degree $\deg_u a_p$ precisely matches the Seifert genus $g(T_{2,p})$:
$$
  \deg_u a_p =  \frac{p-1}{2} =  g(T_{2,p}).
$$
Each monomial $u^k$ in $a_p$ represents an independent $1$-cycle of the Seifert surface. When $\deg_u a_p > g$, the polynomial encodes more topology than the surface can hold. This Chebyshev overflow indicates the knot does not fit.

The saturation at $a_9$ (degree $4$) and the overflow at $a_{11}$ (degree $5$) reflect the Hecke completeness condition.

## Part II: the genus-filtration ideal

Define the genus-$g$ filtration ideal $\mathfrak{I}_g \subset \mathbb{Z}[u]$ by:
$$
  \mathfrak{I}_g
  = \bigl\{ f(u) \in \mathbb{Z}[u] : \deg f > g \bigr\}
  = (u^{g+1}).
$$
A torus knot $T_{2,p}$ admits a localized knot structure on $\Sigma_g$ if and only if $a_p \notin \mathfrak{I}_g$. For $g = 4$, $\mathfrak{I}_4 = (u^5)$. Every $T_{2,p}$ with $p \geq 11$ has $a_p \in (u^5)$ because $\deg_u a_p \geq 5$.

## Part III: obstruction = leading coefficient

The obstruction class $\kappa(T_{2,p}) \in H^2(\Sigma_g, \mathbb{Z})$ is the image of the leading monomial of $a_p$ under projection $\pi_g$:
$$
  \kappa(T_{2,p})
  = \begin{cases}
    0 & \text{if } (p-1)/2 \leq g, \\[6pt]
    c_{(p-1)/2} \cdot [u^{(p-1)/2}] \neq 0 & \text{if } (p-1)/2 > g.
  \end{cases}
$$
Since $c_{(p-1)/2} = 1$ for all odd $p$, the obstruction is nonzero for every prime $p \geq 2g + 3 = 11$.
