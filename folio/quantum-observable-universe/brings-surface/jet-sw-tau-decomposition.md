Let $\tau$ be the Archimedean realisation functor (step 9 of the canonical chain). Applied to the surface-ordered multiplicative integral of the mass-identity connection--curvature pair over the exceptional divisor $\mathcal{S}_c$, it decomposes as the sum over vertical (jet) degrees of the prolonged Seiberg--Witten periods:

$$
\tau\!\left(\mathrm{MI}\!\!\int_{\mathcal{S}_c}\bigl(\nabla_A q,\,L\wedge F^+\bigr)\right)
\;=\;
\sum_{n\geq 0} a_D^{(n)}
\;=\;
\sum_{n\geq 0}\oint_{\mathcal{S}_c}\lambda_{\mathrm{SW}}^{(n)}.
$$

The $n=0$ term is the electron period $a_D^{(e)}$. Each higher term is the $n$-th prolonged period of [Abelianisation of the MI integral on exceptional divisors](#prop:mi-sw-abelianisation), which [The Seiberg–Witten period is an algebraic residue sum](#prop:sw-period-residue-sum) identifies as an algebraic residue sum. Consequently, the Seiberg--Witten differential **is the jet-graded decomposition of the Archimedean realisation**. The functor $\tau$ observes the entire prolongation tower simultaneously, and the vertical (jet/body) order $n$ indexes its graded pieces. This provides the structural counterpart, on the realisation side, of the "observe last" principle. The jet tower travels intact through the filtration, and $\tau$ collapses it to the graded sum only at observation.
