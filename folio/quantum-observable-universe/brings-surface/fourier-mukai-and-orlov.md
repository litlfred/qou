The equivalences this paper uses are Tannakian — a quantum observable universe is reconstructed from its fibre functor as $\mathbf{C} \simeq \mathrm{Rep}(H)$ — and Tannakian equivalences are monoidal, not derived. This remark records what the derived language would add, where it could close a gap, and why it does not do so at present.

**The transform.** For smooth projective varieties $X$ and $Y$ and an object $K$ of the bounded derived category $D^{b}(X \times Y)$, the Fourier–Mukai transform with kernel $K$ is the functor $\Phi_{K} \colon D^{b}(X) \to D^{b}(Y)$, $E \mapsto Rp_{Y*}(p_{X}^{*}E \otimes^{L} K)$: pull back, tensor with the kernel, push forward. Orlov's theorem states that every fully faithful exact functor $D^{b}(X) \to D^{b}(Y)$ is of this form, with $K$ unique up to isomorphism \cite{orlov1997}. On the base curve itself the transform buys nothing: by Bondal and Orlov a smooth projective curve of genus at least two is determined by its derived category, and its autoequivalences are generated by automorphisms, twists by line bundles and shifts, $\mathrm{Aut}(D^{b}(M_{0})) = \mathrm{Aut}(M_{0}) \ltimes \mathrm{Pic}(M_{0}) \times \mathbb{Z}$ \cite{bondal-orlov2001}. There is no hidden derived partner of Bring's curve.

**Where it would help.** What the paper does not have is a trace map from the substrate to the base — a map under which the [Chern character](#def:chern-character) of a substrate object becomes a class on $M_{0}$, so that the [Hochschild homology](#def:hochschild-homology) of the substrate and the Hochschild homology of the curve, which the [Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg theorem](#rem:hkr-theorem) identifies with $\bigoplus_{i} H^{i}(M_{0}, \Omega^{i+n})$, are compared by a canonical map rather than by an assumed one. An integral transform supplies exactly this. A functor $\Phi_{K} \colon \mathcal{D}(A) \to D^{b}(M_{0})$ induces a map $HH_{\bullet}(\Phi_{K})$ on Hochschild homology, canonically, and that map is the correspondence defined by the Chern character of the kernel, $\mathrm{ch}(K) \in HH_{0}(X \times Y)$, by the theorem of Căldăraru and Willerton \cite{caldararu-willerton-2010}. That is a construction, and it forces numbers: the Hochschild homology of the curve has dimensions $2, 4, 4$ in degrees $0, \pm 1$ and its Hochschild cohomology $1, 4, 9$ in degrees $0, 1, 2$, with $S_{5}$-invariant parts $2, 0, 0$ and $1, 0, 0$ ([the three Euler characteristics](#rem:three-euler-characteristics)); a substrate mapped to the curve by a fully faithful transform would have to carry the same, and the [Euler characteristic of Bring's surface](#lem:brings-euler-characteristic) would be the alternating sum of a map, not of a coincidence.

**Why it does not apply yet.** Orlov's theorem needs a functor between bounded derived categories of smooth projective varieties. The paper's archimedean realisation is the [spatial realisation functor](#def:spatial-realization-functor), which lands in modules over the ground ring — the fibre functor takes values in $R$-$\mathbf{Mod}$, not in a derived category of the base — and no object of the substrate is given as a complex of sheaves on $M_{0}$. Stating the realisation at the derived level, with a kernel on $\mathrm{Spec}(A) \times M_{0}$, is the missing step; it is a definition to be made, not a theorem to be proved, and until it is made the comparison of Hochschild theories in this chapter is a comparison of dimensions, not of maps.
