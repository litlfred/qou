*Open question. Is Bring's surface $\mathcal{B}$, with its hyperbolic metric, Laplace-isospectral to a hyperbolic surface that is not isometric to it? The same question can be asked of the quantum observable universe built on $\mathcal{B}$, with the spectrum of the [categorical Dirac operator](#def:categorical-dirac-operator) in place of the Laplacian. No answer is conjectured here.*

Kac asked whether the spectrum of a domain determines its shape \cite{kac1966}, and Sunada gave a general construction of isospectral non-isometric manifolds \cite{sunada1985}: a finite group $G$ acting on a manifold $X$, with two subgroups $H_1, H_2 \le G$ that meet every conjugacy class of $G$ in the same number of elements but are not conjugate, gives isospectral quotients $X/H_1$ and $X/H_2$. Three facts bear on the question for $\mathcal{B}$.

1. *Sunada pairs inside the automorphism group.* Neither $\mathrm{Aut}^+(\mathcal{B}) \cong S_5$ nor the full group $S_5 \times C_2$ contains such a pair of subgroups. A Sunada partner of $\mathcal{B}$ would therefore need a group acting on a proper cover $X$ of $\mathcal{B}$, with $\mathcal{B} = X/H_1$.
2. *The trace parameter is determined.* Under the Markov trace of convention D, the one-photon response determines $q$ ([one-photon response](#prop:photon-response-determines-q)).
3. *States are determined up to the radical of the trace form.* The zero-photon response $\mathrm{tr}_M(x)$ factors through the cocenter $H_n(q)/[H_n(q), H_n(q)]$. The $k$-photon responses $\mathrm{tr}_M(\gamma(\varphi^1)\cdots\gamma(\varphi^k)\,x)$ determine much more. Products of photons span $H_n(q)$ ([stopping depth](#prop:photon-filtration-depth-bounds)), so these responses determine $x$ modulo the radical $\{u : \mathrm{tr}_M(yu) = 0 \text{ for all } y\}$ of the trace form. Under convention D on $H_3(q)$, that radical is the line spanned by the sign idempotent: a state is heard except for its alternating component.

The question would be settled by a group $G$ acting on a cover of $\mathcal{B}$ with a pair $H_1, H_2$ as above and $X/H_1 \cong \mathcal{B}$, $X/H_2 \not\cong \mathcal{B}$, or by a proof that the spectrum of $\mathcal{B}$ determines it. The eigenvalues of surfaces with large symmetry groups are studied in \cite{cook2021}.
