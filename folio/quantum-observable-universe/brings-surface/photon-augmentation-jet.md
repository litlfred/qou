*Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $q \in R$ a unit, $h = q - q^{-1}$ and $[2]_q = q + q^{-1}$. If $\sigma$ in an $R$-algebra satisfies $\sigma^2 = h\,\sigma + 1$, then*

$$
  (\sigma - q)^2 = -[2]_q\,(\sigma - q).
$$

*Let $\varepsilon : H_n(q) \to R$ be the augmentation $\varepsilon(\sigma_i) = q$ and $I = \ker\varepsilon$. If $R$ is a field and $[2]_q \neq 0$, then $I = I^2$, so $I/I^2 = 0$. The same holds for the sign augmentation $\varepsilon(\sigma_i) = -q^{-1}$. For every [photon](#def:photon),*

$$
  \varepsilon\bigl(\gamma(\varphi_0, \varphi_1, \varphi_{01})\bigr) = 1 + q\,(\varphi_0 + \varphi_1) + \varphi_{01},
$$

*so $\gamma(\varphi) - 1 \in I$ only on the hyperplane $q(\varphi_0 + \varphi_1) + \varphi_{01} = 0$.*

**Proof.** Expand $(\sigma - q)^2 = \sigma^2 - 2q\sigma + q^2$ and substitute $\sigma^2 = h\sigma + 1$; the result is $-(q + q^{-1})\sigma + q^2 + 1 = -[2]_q(\sigma - q)$. The ideal $I$ is generated by the elements $\sigma_i - q$. When $[2]_q$ is invertible, each generator equals $-[2]_q^{-1}(\sigma_i - q)^2 \in I^2$, and $I^2$ is a two-sided ideal, so $I \subseteq I^2$. For the photon, $\varepsilon(\sigma_0\sigma_1^{-1}) = q \cdot q^{-1} = 1$. $\square$
