*(1).* The :refterm[descent]{#descent} map $\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}$ is a surjective $*$-homomorphism of fibre algebras ([:refterm[descent]{#descent}](#lem:descent)). Its kernel is zero generically because the ascent $\ell_n$ embeds $A$ as a direct summand of $J^1(A)$, with $r_n$ acting as the left-inverse. The kernel is non-trivial only where this splitting degenerates—a closed algebraic condition on the [Frobenius pairing](#def:frobenius-pairing) $\varepsilon \circ m$. Since $A$ is a Frobenius--Hopf object in a rigid :refterm[monoidal category]{#monoidal-category}, standard vector-bundle degeneracy bounds give this locus a real codimension of at least 2.

*(2).* At a blow-up centre $p \in \partial \mathcal{S}_n$, $\ker(r_n|_p)$ is the fibre of $\Omega^1(A)$ over $p$ modulo the image of $\eta\colon \mathbf{1} \to A$. Because $\Omega^1(A) \hookrightarrow A \otimes A$ and $A$ has fibre $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, this quotient is isomorphic to the projectivisation of the $2$-dimensional representation: $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C}) \cong S^2$. This yields the exceptional divisor resolving the singularity at $p$.

*(3).* The :refterm[descent]{#descent} involution $\hat{\tau}_n = \ell_n \circ \mathfrak{d}_{n+1}$ ([:refterm[descent]{#descent} involution](#def:descent-involution)) on $J^1 \mathcal{S}_{n+1}$ restricts to the boundary locus $\partial \mathcal{S}_n$. Because $\partial \mathcal{S}_n$ is defined by $\ker(\mathfrak{d}_{n+1}|_p) \neq 0$ and $\hat{\tau}_n$ projects onto the image of $\ell_n$, the restriction $\hat{\tau}_n|_{\partial \mathcal{S}_n}$ acts as an involution. Its fixed points are the sections entirely within the descended subalgebra—matching the monodromy fixed-point condition of the [quantum characteristic classes](#def:quantum-characteristic-classes).

*(4).* At $n = 1$, the base is $M$ and $\mathcal{S}_1$ fibres over $M$ with fibre $\mathrm{SU}(2)/\mathrm{U}(1) \cong S^2$. The boundary $\partial \mathcal{S}_1$ marks the locus where jet restriction degenerates. By (2), each fibre of $\partial \mathcal{S}_1$ is $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$, separating base (spacetime) and fibre (chiral) coordinates. The Reeb vector field $\xi$ is vertical, so the reversal $\xi \mapsto -\xi$ exchanges the two sides of $\partial \mathcal{S}_1$. The positive Reeb eigenspace carries left-handed fermions, and the negative carries right-handed.
