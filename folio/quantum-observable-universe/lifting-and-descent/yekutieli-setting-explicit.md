The [lifting proposition](#prop:yekutieli-lifting) invokes Yekutieli's theory of central extensions of gerbes and twisted deformation quantisation \cite{yekutieli2010}. That theory has standing hypotheses, and the axioms of a quantum observable universe supply some of them and not others. This remark lists them side by side, so that each use of the theory in this chapter can be checked against the hypothesis it needs.

| Hypothesis in the source | What it is needed for | What the axioms supply | Reading adopted |
|---|---|---|---|
| A formal, pro-nilpotent parameter | convergence of the Maurer–Cartan series and of the gauge action in a pro-nilpotent differential graded Lie algebra | a real number $q - 1$ at which everything is evaluated | work formally in $\hbar = q - 1$ on the fibre; the evaluation at the aether value is the archimedean functor's task and a separate convergence claim |
| Which theorem is used | the [lifting condition](#def:yekutieli-lifting-condition) and the [quantum characteristic classes](#def:quantum-characteristic-classes) cite only the Čech-theoretic classification of central extensions of gerbes, which needs no nilpotence | the Čech side is spelled out; the Maurer–Cartan side appears in two rows of the dictionary | keep the two apart: only "vacuum state = unique Maurer–Cartan element" and "aether field = local deformation parameter" belong to the formal side |
| Smoothness of the base | the identification of Hochschild classes with geometric ones, and the very definition of a gerbe on a smooth separated scheme | asserted in the [spatial realisation functor](#def:spatial-realization-functor) ("an ordinary smooth manifold") | the categorical condition is that $\Omega^{1}(A)$ be a dualisable object of constant rank — Kähler differentials finitely generated projective, which for a finitely generated algebra is formal smoothness \cite{hartshorne-1977}; it is the hypothesis the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg theorem needs as well \cite{hkr-1962} |
| Characteristic zero, $6$ invertible | the differential graded Lie algebra half (formality, the $L_\infty$ machinery) \cite{kontsevich-2003} | the finite-field and $p$-adic models of an earlier chapter | the Lie-algebra half is developed over $\mathbb{Q}$-algebras; the finite-field models receive the Čech half only |
| The Čech–de Rham double complex and its Lie algebra | the cosimplicial differential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer–Cartan equation is the descent condition | the Čech side in the descent chapter; the Lie side in two table cells | one statement of the cosimplicial Lie algebra and its Maurer–Cartan equation, whose solution set is the descent condition already present |

**Impact on the obstruction criterion.** For the proposition as stated, nothing: the lift from level $n$ to level $n + 1$ exists if and only if $\kappa_n \in H^{2}(M, \mathcal{B})$ vanishes, and that is a Čech statement about a central extension of gerbes, which the corpus has in full. What the ledger affects is the register in which the corollaries are read. A claim that the vacuum is *the* Maurer–Cartan element, or that the aether field is a local deformation parameter, is a claim in the formal category and holds for the formal aether $A[[\hbar]]$; its evaluation at the aether value is a further assertion. And a claim that an obstruction class in the [Hochschild homology](#def:hochschild-homology) or cohomology of $A$ is a geometric class on the base is an assertion about a smooth base, which the axioms do not state and the spatial realisation functor asserts by hand. Making those two hypotheses explicit is cheaper than discovering, three chapters later, that a theorem was invoked outside its scope.
