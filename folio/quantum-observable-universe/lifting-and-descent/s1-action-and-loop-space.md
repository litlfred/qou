[Hochschild homology](#def:hochschild-homology) carries a circle action, and the paper's Reeb generator is a circle action of a different kind. This remark states the first, recalls where the second comes from, and says what would be needed to identify them.

**The circle on the loop space.** Let $X$ be a space with trivial fundamental group and $LX = \mathrm{Map}(S^{1}, X)$ its free loop space, on which the circle acts by rotating loops. Jones's theorem identifies the Hochschild homology of the singular cochain algebra of $X$ with the cohomology of $LX$, $HH_{\bullet}(C^{\bullet}(X)) \cong H^{\bullet}(LX)$, and identifies the circle-equivariant cohomology of $LX$ with the negative cyclic homology of the same algebra \cite{jones1987-cyclic}. For the algebra of functions on a smooth manifold the [Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg theorem](#rem:hkr-theorem) gives instead $HH_{\bullet}(C^{\infty}(X)) \cong \Omega^{\bullet}(X)$, the differential forms; the two statements are the two sides of the same picture, one for the topology of $X$ and one for its smooth structure \cite{loday-1998}.

**The operator that is the circle.** On the Hochschild complex of any algebra $A$ there is Connes's operator $B \colon HH_{n}(A) \to HH_{n+1}(A)$, of square zero, which is the algebraic form of the circle action; under the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg isomorphism it becomes the de Rham differential, $B = d$, so the Hochschild complex with $B$ is a mixed complex whose homotopy-fixed points for the circle are the cyclic homology $HC_{\bullet}(A)$ \cite{connes1994}. The Cartan homotopy formula $L_{v} = d\iota_{v} + \iota_{v}d$ for a vector field $v$, and the contraction $\iota_{v}$ itself, also lift to the Hochschild complex; this is the noncommutative Cartan calculus.

**The Reeb generator.** The [categorical Reeb derivation](#def:categorical-reeb) $\xi$ of a quantum observable universe is the generator of the maximal torus action, normalised by $\iota_{\xi}\varTheta_{A} = \mathrm{id}$ and $\iota_{\xi}d\varTheta_{A} = 0$ against the contact form. It comes with the same three operators — contraction $\iota_{\xi}$, Lie derivative $L_{\xi}$, and $d$ — satisfying the Cartan formula, so it is a circle action on the differential calculus of $A$ in exactly the sense in which $B$ is one on Hochschild homology. The two circles are related in the commutative case: for $A = C^{\infty}(X)$ the flow of a vector field on $X$ induces a map of $LX$ to itself commuting with loop rotation, and the corresponding operators on $\Omega^{\bullet}(X) = HH_{\bullet}(A)$ are $L_{\xi}$ and $B = d$.

**What would establish it, and what is not claimed.** The statement that the Reeb generator's action on $HH_{\bullet}(A)$ *is* Connes's circle, for the aether algebra $A$ at $q \neq 1$, is not established in this paper. It would require the Hochschild homology of $A = \mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$ with its $B$-operator, computed rather than assumed; the untwisted theory is known to lose a dimension at $q \neq 1$ and to recover it only after a twist by the Nakayama automorphism \cite{hadfield-krahmer-2005}, so the identification, if it holds, holds in the twisted theory. Until that computation is made, the correspondence $\xi \leftrightarrow B$ is an analogy that fixes the normalisation of the Reeb generator by reference to a standard object; it is not a theorem of the corpus, and nothing downstream is conditioned on it.
