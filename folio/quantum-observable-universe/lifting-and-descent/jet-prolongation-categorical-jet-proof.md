**Proof.** *Step 1: Categorical jet object.* The first jet bundle $J^1 \mathcal{S}$ is represented categorically by the object $A \otimes \Omega^1(A)$, where $\Omega^1(A) = \ker(m\colon A\otimes A \to A)$ is the kernel of the multiplication (the bimodule of Kähler differentials, i.e., the universal first-order differential calculus over $A$). Because $A$ is Frobenius, the non-degenerate pairing $\varepsilon \circ m \colon A \otimes A \to \mathbf{1}$ exhibits $\Omega^1(A)$ as a direct summand of $A \otimes A$; hence $J^1(A) := A \otimes \Omega^1(A)$ is again an object of the ambient category.

*Step 2: Inherited Frobenius–Hopf structure.* The Frobenius relation $(\mathrm{Id} \otimes m) \circ (\Delta \otimes \mathrm{Id}) = \Delta \circ m = (m \otimes \mathrm{Id}) \circ (\mathrm{Id} \otimes \Delta)$ ensures the comultiplication $\Delta\colon A \to A \otimes A$ restricts to a well-defined comultiplication $\Delta'\colon J^1(A) \to J^1(A) \otimes J^1(A)$, and the counit $\varepsilon\colon A \to \mathbf{1}$ induces a counit $\varepsilon'$ on $J^1(A)$. The Hopf antipode $S$ extends to $J^1(A)$ by the functoriality of the Kähler differential construction. Thus $J^1(A)$ is a Frobenius–Hopf object.

No deformation enters this step: it uses only the Frobenius relation of $A$, which is part of the definition of a Frobenius–Hopf object. The strong multiplicativity condition of the positive-characteristic integration theorem \cite{leitner2006} is a different condition, on a Poisson bracket and the comultiplication of a free product, and is not used here. $\square$
