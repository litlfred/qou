Let $A$ be a bialgebra over a commutative ring $R$, and give $A \otimes A$ its tensor-product coalgebra structure. If the comultiplication of $A \otimes A$ maps $\Omega^1(A) = \ker m$ into $\Omega^1(A) \otimes \Omega^1(A)$, then $\Omega^1(A) = 0$. Indeed, the counit of $A \otimes A$ is $\varepsilon \circ m$, so it vanishes on $\Omega^1(A)$, and the counit law $(\mathrm{Id} \otimes \varepsilon)\,\Delta(x) = x \otimes 1$ then gives $x = 0$ for every $x \in \Omega^1(A)$.

So a Frobenius–Hopf structure on $A$ is not inherited by the jet object through restriction of $\Delta$, unless $m$ is injective. The smallest example is the group algebra $A = k[\mathbb{Z}/2]$ with generator $g$: the element $x = g \otimes g - 1 \otimes 1$ lies in $\Omega^1(A)$ because $g^2 = 1$, while applying $m \otimes \mathrm{Id}$ to $\Delta(x)$ gives $1 \otimes (g \otimes g - 1 \otimes 1) \neq 0$, and $m \otimes \mathrm{Id}$ vanishes on $\Omega^1(A) \otimes \Omega^1(A)$.
