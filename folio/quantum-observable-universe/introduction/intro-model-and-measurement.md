Everything described so far is mathematics. The words *electron*,
*proton*, *mass* and *binding energy* name constructions inside the
model, and a statement about them is a statement about those
constructions. The physical content of the paper is located in one
place: where a quantity computed in the model is set beside a measured
one — the CODATA fundamental constants, the Particle Data Group
tables, the AME2020 atomic-mass evaluation. Those comparisons are the
empirical claims, and the [Physical Observations](#chap:observations)
chapter collects them.

Three measured numbers are admitted, and only three. They are the
calibration inputs, and they match one for one the three real degrees
of freedom of the first cohomology of the genus-four state space
([calibration–cohomology alignment](#prop:calibration-cohomology-alignment);
[three calibration inputs](#prop:three-calibration-inputs)):

- the electron mass $m_e$ fixes the unit of mass, the *scale*;
- the neutron $\beta$-decay channel fixes the aether parameter
  $q_0$, the *modulus*; because that modulus is overdetermined, its
  numerical value is read off the most precisely measured mass ratio
  available;
- the pion channel fixes the one remaining *orthogonal* class, the
  meson scale.

Nothing else is fitted. In particular no object — a knot, a partition,
a truncation order — is chosen because it reproduces a measured value;
such a choice would be a fourth calibration in disguise
([calibration discipline](#def:three-calibration-discipline);
[epistemic status of the calibrations](#rem:epistemic-status-of-calibrations)).
Because $m_e$ sets the unit by fiat, an absolute mass in MeV carries no
information beyond the dimensionless ratio behind it, so the
comparisons are made on ratios, binding energies, decay $Q$-values and
mass splittings.

The scope of these comparisons is defined by the division of labour
between categorical spectral theory and local continuum quantum field
theory. The framework computes discrete ultraviolet spectral invariants
— dimensionless rest-mass ratios, threshold couplings, and bound-state
normal forms — which continuum effective field theory treats as
external renormalization parameters, whereas infrared $3+1$-dimensional
momentum-space scattering cross sections $\sigma(s,t)$, continuous
renormalization-group running, and spacelike detector correlation
functions belong to continuum perturbation theory.

Each comparison is graded against both the experimental uncertainty
band and the theoretical truncation budget of its expansion order,
rather than against a uniform fraction of the experimental error bar
that would misgrade tree-level topological formulas against
eleven-digit Penning-trap uncertainties. At finite Kashiwara truncation
depth $N$, the per-knot brane-tower remainder carries an explicit bound
of order $O(\hbar_q^{2(N+1)})$, while leading-order topological mass and
binding formulas omit uncomputed higher-loop radiative corrections of
order $O(\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}/\pi)$ and higher-body nuclear cumulants.
The observations chapter reports every residual against these
theoretical truncation budgets alongside the experimental uncertainty
band, separating the quantities that already close at sub-parts-per-billion
precision from those where higher-loop radiative or multi-body corrections
are not yet derived. A derived quantity that misses its truncation budget is
reported as a miss rather than tuned away, because only an untuned
model can be corrected by what its errors teach.
