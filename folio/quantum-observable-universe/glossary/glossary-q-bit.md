A **:defterm[$q$-bit]{#q-bit}** is the natural unit of information in a QOU with substrate parameter $q$. It is the information generated by one application of the $q$-connection $\nabla_q$ across a fibre, which corresponds to one crossing resolution in the Iwahori--Hecke algebra $H_n(q)$ or one iteration of the quadratic map $f_q(z) = z^2 + c(q)$ on the exceptional divisor.

**Definition.** One $q$-bit $= \eta_q$ in classical natural units, where

$$
  \eta_q =  \frac{2q - 1}{q^3}
$$

is the operational entropy.

**The two Hecke eigenvalues.** The name "$q$-bit" reflects the Iwahori--Hecke algebra's two eigenvalues $q$ (symmetric channel) and $-q^{-1}$ (antisymmetric channel). At $q = 1$ the eigenvalues merge to $\pm 1$ and one $q$-bit equals one classical nat.

**"It from $q$-bit."** Wheeler's "it from bit" asserts that physics emerges from information. The QOU refines this: observable physics emerges from $q$-deformed information. The observability axiom forces every observable quantity to be a rational function of $q$. Each elementary process produces exactly one $q$-bit. The substrate parameter $q$ controls non-commutativity.
