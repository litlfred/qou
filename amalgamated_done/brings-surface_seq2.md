<!-- FILE_START: bring-algebraic-substructure-intro.md -->
This section develops the algebraic substructure of Bring's surface. The construction proceeds from the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ generators.
<!-- FILE_END: bring-algebraic-substructure-intro.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: su2-generators.md -->
Every finite subgroup of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ lifts via the double cover $\mathrm{SU}(2)\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{SO}(3)$ to a binary subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. For $S_3\cong D_3$, the lift is the binary dihedral group $\mathrm{Dic}_3\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)$ of order $12$. It is generated by

```tex
\begin{align}
  \alpha &= \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}e_3\bigr)
  = \begin{pmatrix}e^{i\pi/3}&0\\[2pt]0&e^{-i\pi/3}\end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:su2-3cycle}\\[4pt]
  \beta &= \exp(\pi e_1)
  = \begin{pmatrix}0&i\\[2pt]i&0\end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:su2-2cycle}
\end{align}
```

with $\alpha^3=\beta^2=-I$, dihedral relation $\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}=\alpha^{-1}$, and commutator $[\beta,\alpha]=\alpha^{-2}$.
<!-- FILE_END: su2-generators.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: hyperelliptic-quintic.md -->
Let $\mathcal{X}_5$ denote the smooth projective completion of the affine curve

$$
  \mathcal{X}_5 \colon y^2 =  x^5 - 1
  \qquad
  \text{in } \mathbb{A}^2(\mathbb{C}).
$$

We view $\mathcal{X}_5$ as a double cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ via the projection $(x, y) \mapsto x$. Writing $\zeta_5 = e^{2\pi i/5}$ for a primitive fifth root of unity, the ramification locus of this double cover consists of the six branch points

$$
  \{x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4, \infty\}  =
   \{1, \zeta_5, \zeta_5^2, \zeta_5^3, \zeta_5^4, \infty\}.
$$

Five distribute symmetrically on the unit circle; the last lies at infinity. By Riemann–Hurwitz, the genus of $\mathcal{X}_5$ is

$$
  g(\mathcal{X}_5)  =  \frac{6 - 2}{2}  =  2.
$$

Thus $\mathcal{X}_5$ is a hyperelliptic Riemann surface of genus $2$ in the Bolza family \cite{bolza1887}. Its hyperelliptic involution is $\iota \colon (x, y) \mapsto (x, -y)$.

**Relation to Bring's surface.** The coordinate $x$ on $\mathcal{X}_5$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_5$-equivariant observable. We identify it with the first Lagrange resolvent $w_1$ of [prop:lagrange-resolvent-transform](#prop:lagrange-resolvent-transform), restricted to the cyclic subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_5 \subset S_5$ of [Bring's surface](#def:brings-surface). Under this identification, the five branch points $\zeta_5^k$ are the cyclic images of a single $\mathbb{Z}_5$-orbit.
<!-- FILE_END: hyperelliptic-quintic.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: x5-z5-automorphism.md -->
**Proposition.**
Let $\mathcal{X}_5 \colon y^2 = x^5 - 1$ be the hyperelliptic quintic of Definition [def:hyperelliptic-quintic](#def:hyperelliptic-quintic). The map

$$
  \phi_5 \colon \mathcal{X}_5 \longrightarrow \mathcal{X}_5,
  \qquad
  \phi_5(x, y)  =  (\zeta_5 x,\, y),
  \qquad
  \zeta_5  =  e^{2\pi i/5},
$$

is an automorphism of $\mathcal{X}_5$ of order $5$. It generates a cyclic group $\langle \phi_5 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_5$ inside $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{X}_5)$.
<!-- FILE_END: x5-z5-automorphism.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: x5-z5-automorphism-interpretation.md -->
**Remark.**  Under the identification of $x$ with the first Lagrange resolvent $w_1$ (Remark [rem:lagrange-resolvents](#rem:lagrange-resolvents)), the automorphism $\phi_5$ is the diagonalised action of the cyclic generator $\sigma$ on $w_1$ via $\phi_5 \leftrightarrow \sigma$ with $\sigma(w_1) = \zeta_5^{-1} w_1$. The orbit $\{x, \zeta_5 x, \zeta_5^{2} x, \zeta_5^{3} x, \zeta_5^{4} x\}$ forms a single $\mathbb{Z}_5$-orbit of size five, matching the five finite branch points of the double cover.
<!-- FILE_END: x5-z5-automorphism-interpretation.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: x5-bring-weight-identification.md -->
**Proposition.**
Let $\sigma$ denote the cyclic generator of $\mathbb{Z}_5$. We write its induced action on regular functions on the left. On Bring's surface, the first Lagrange resolvent $w_1$ is an eigenvector of $\sigma$ with eigenvalue $\zeta_5^{-1}$ ([prop:lagrange-resolvent-transform](#prop:lagrange-resolvent-transform)). On $\mathcal{X}_5$, the coordinate $x$ is an eigenvector of $\sigma$ with the same eigenvalue ([prop:x5-z5-automorphism](#prop:x5-z5-automorphism)). Consequently, there is a canonical $\mathbb{Z}_5$-equivariant identification

$$
  \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}_5)_{\zeta_5^{-1}}
   \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}
   \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Br}})_{\zeta_5^{-1}},
  \qquad
  x  \longmapsto  w_1,
$$

of the weight-$(-1)$ isotypical components of the coordinate rings. This identification extends to a $\mathbb{Z}_5$-equivariant map of graded rings on the entire $\mathbb{Z}_5$-isotypical decomposition by sending $x^{k} \mapsto w_1^{\,k}$ for $0 \leq k \leq 4$ (with $x^5$ and $w_1^{\,5}$ both invariant).
<!-- FILE_END: x5-bring-weight-identification.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: x5-bring-weight-geometric-content.md -->
**Remark.**  This identification is categorical, not a morphism of curves. By Riemann–Hurwitz, there is no non-constant curve morphism $\mathcal{X}_5 \to \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Br}}$ ($g = 2 < 4$). Furthermore, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Br}}$ has no genus-$2$ Galois quotient because $S_5$ has no subgroups of index $2$ or $3$ with the requisite ramification. The proposition instead asserts a compatibility of $\mathbb{Z}_5$-graded observables under the Lagrange ordering. The same categorical weight-$(-1)$ coordinate parametrises the single $\mathrm{U}(1)$ phase tube of [rem:u1-phase-tube](#rem:u1-phase-tube) and the first Fourier mode of Bring's geometry. See [rem:x5-bring-prym-jacobian](#rem:x5-bring-prym-jacobian) for the parallel structure at the level of Jacobians.
