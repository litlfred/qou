<!-- FILE_START: molecular-assembly-projector-intro.md -->
A molecule is a composite braid: $k$ sub-units assembled into a single word in $H_N(q)$. Before specialising to any one molecule, we record the general algebraic structure that the assembly forces.

The $k$ sub-units determine a strand-partition $\lambda = (n_1,\dots,n_k)$ with $\sum_i n_i = N$. The assembly factors through the parabolic subalgebra $H_\lambda(q) \hookrightarrow H_N(q)$. Every such factorisation carries canonical assembly projectors onto the Wedderburn blocks of $H_N(q)$. Projection onto the symmetric block is delocalisation.

Three things must be kept distinct. Existence of the projector is pure algebra. Optimality is the physics, selected by the binding LP/SDP. Energetics is the binding energy, corresponding to the trace-weight gap between the selected block and the next configuration.
<!-- FILE_END: molecular-assembly-projector-intro.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: molecular-strand-partition.md -->
**Definition.**
*Let a molecule be assembled from $k$ sub-units carrying strand counts $n_1,\dots,n_k$. The **molecular strand-partition** is the composition*

$$
  \lambda = (n_1,\dots,n_k), \qquad |\lambda| = \sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i = N,
$$

*which records how the $N$ strands of the composite braid in $H_N(q)$ are distributed among the sub-units. We write $\ell(\lambda) = k$ for the number of sub-units.*

The strand-partition is fixed by what is joined to what. For three acetylene units, the carbon strand-partition is $\lambda = (2,2,2)$, with $N = 6$ and $k = 3$.
<!-- FILE_END: molecular-strand-partition.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: parabolic-hecke-subalgebra.md -->
**Definition.**
*Given a strand-partition $\lambda = (n_1,\dots,n_k)$ with $\sum_i n_i = N$, the **parabolic Hecke subalgebra** is the tensor product*

$$
  H_\lambda(q) \;=\; H_{n_1}(q) \otimes \cdots \otimes H_{n_k}(q) \;\hookrightarrow\; H_N(q),
$$

*generated by the Hecke generators $\sigma_j$ that act within a single sub-unit block. The cross-block generators are omitted; they are precisely the crossings created when the sub-units bond.*

Equivalently $H_\lambda(q)$ is the $q$-deformation of the Young subgroup $S_\lambda \subset S_N$. Its intra-unit generator count is $N - k$. The bonding degrees of freedom number $k-1$. For benzene's $\lambda = (2,2,2)$, there are 3 intra-unit generators and 2 bonding generators linking the three units into a ring, with the third join closing the ring.
<!-- FILE_END: parabolic-hecke-subalgebra.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: molecular-assembly-projector.md -->
**Definition.**
*Let $\lambda$ be a strand-partition of $N$ and $\mu \vdash N$ a Wedderburn block of $H_N(q)$. The **assembly projector** $e_{\lambda,\mu} \in H_N(q)$ is the canonical idempotent projecting the composite's module onto the irreducible block $\mu$.*

It is the $q$-Young symmetriser for the pair $(\lambda,\mu)$. The distinguished case $\mu = (N)$—projection onto the one-row symmetric block—is delocalisation.

For benzene, $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ projects the six $\pi$-electrons onto the symmetric block. This represents the categorical content of the aromatic ring, realised geometrically as its $T(2,6)$ threading link.
<!-- FILE_END: molecular-assembly-projector.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: assembly-projector-exists.md -->
**Proposition.**
*For every strand-partition $\lambda$ of $N$ there exists a symmetric-block assembly projector $e_{\lambda,(N)} \in H_N(q)$, and it is idempotent for generic $q$.*

This is the $q$-deformation of the classical Young symmetriser.
<!-- FILE_END: assembly-projector-exists.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: assembly-projector-scope.md -->
Existence holds for every $\lambda$ and says nothing about energy or stability. A molecule is stable only when the binding LP/SDP selects this symmetric block as its optimum. The binding energy is the trace-weight gap to the next admissible configuration. The high stability of an aromatic ring follows from the irreducibility of $e_{\lambda,(N)}$ over all $N$ strands, not from the projector's bare existence.
<!-- FILE_END: assembly-projector-scope.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: benzene-symmetric-projector.md -->
*Benzene is the special case $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ of the assembly projector: three acetylene sub-units, each contributing two carbons, give six carbon strands. The projector $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ projects the six $\pi$-electrons onto the symmetric Wedderburn block $\mu = (6)$.*

Projection onto $\mu = (6)$ is the categorical content of aromaticity. The shared $\pi$-content is spread symmetrically over all six carbons. The partition $\lambda = (2,2,2)$ is forced by the assembly of three two-carbon units. The symmetric block is selected by the binding LP only when the geometry supports delocalisation.
<!-- FILE_END: benzene-symmetric-projector.md -->

<!-- FILE_START: aromaticity-as-symmetric-projection.md -->
Stability requires three steps:

1. **Existence**. For any $\lambda$ the symmetric-block projector $e_{\lambda,(N)}$ exists.
2. **Optimality**. Among admissible configurations, the binding LP/SDP selects the one minimising the trace objective under Frobenius positivity. Stability is this selection.
3. **Energetics**. The binding energy is the trace-weight gap between the selected block and the next configuration. For the six-carbon ring, the threaded $T(2,6)$ configuration carries Alexander weight $\approx 3$ while the open chain carries weight $5$. The gap $5 - 3 = 2$, scaled by the molecular energy unit, is the ring-closure resonance energy.

The ring's projector $e_{(2,2,2),(6)}$ is irreducible over all six strands. One cannot open the ring a single bond at a time. Breaking it collapses the entire symmetric block back to the localised chain, paying the whole weight-gap cooperatively. This irreducibility of the global projector is the categorical content of aromatic stability.
<!-- FILE_END: aromaticity-as-symmetric-projection.md -->
