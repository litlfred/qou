1. **Algebra structure:** The morphisms $\mu \colon A \otimes A \to A$ and $\eta \colon \mathbf{1} \to A$ make $A$ an associative unital algebra object in $\mathbf{C}$.
2. **Coalgebra structure:** The morphisms $\Delta \colon A \to A \otimes A$ and $\varepsilon \colon A \to \mathbf{1}$ make $A$ a coassociative counital coalgebra object, with antipode $S \colon A \to A$ satisfying the Hopf compatibility $\varepsilon \circ \mu = u \circ (\varepsilon \otimes \varepsilon)$, where $u$ is the canonical unit isomorphism.
3. **Frobenius relation:** The Frobenius form $\lambda$ satisfies $\mu \circ \Delta = (\Delta \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \alpha \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes \mu)$, where $\alpha$ is the associator in $\mathbf{C}$.
4. **Non-degeneracy:** The Frobenius pairing $\langle a, b \rangle_F = \lambda(\mu(a \otimes b))$ is non-degenerate. The induced map $A \to A^\vee$ is an isomorphism.
5. **Self-duality:** The algebra $A$ is self-dual as a substrate. The non-degenerate Frobenius pairing identifies $A$ with its dual $A^\vee$ canonically, coupling the observer trace $\Phi = \lambda \circ \mu$ and the observed state space intrinsically.

Let $A$ be the Frobenius--Hopf substrate algebra and let $D(A)$ denote its derived category of bimodules. A **rigid dualizing complex** for $A$ is a complex $\omega_A^\bullet$ of $A$-bimodules satisfying:

1. **Finite injective dimension:** The complex $\omega_A^\bullet$ has finite injective dimension as a left $A$-module and as a right $A$-module.
2. **Rigidifying isomorphism:** There exists a canonical isomorphism $\omega_A^\bullet \simeq R\mathrm{Hom}_{A^e}(A, A^e)$ in $D(A^e)$, where $A^e = A \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the enveloping algebra. This rigidifying structure pins down $\omega_A^\bullet$ up to unique isomorphism.
3. **Van den Bergh duality:** For any finitely generated $A$-bimodule $M$, the rigid dualizing complex induces a duality $\mathrm{Ext}^i_A(M, \omega_A^\bullet) \cong H^{d-i}(M)^\vee$, where $d$ is the homological dimension of $A$.

The rigidity condition ensures that the dualizing complex is intrinsic to $A$, independent of base ring or presentation. This forms the categorical substitute for the canonical sheaf in the non-commutative setting.
