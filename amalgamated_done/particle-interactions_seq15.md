<!-- FILE_START: intro-confinement-level.md -->
Categorical confinement is a stratification, not a binary property. Each composite braid word carries a confinement level $\ell \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$. This integer records how many Hecke-reduction steps separate the word from a trivial closure. The limit of this stratification is confinement level infinity. This defines the cemetery group: the categorical subgroup of braid words that become quotient-trivial under the substrate flow.

This section defines the confinement level via the Hecke-reduction filtration. We prove this level is preserved under Markov moves. We identify the cemetery group as the kernel of the substrate-flow quotient. We compute the confinement level for standard particle braid words and tabulate the cemetery group generators. The result is the level function used to organize confined-particle shells.

<!-- FILE_START: confinement-level.md -->
**Definition.**
*Fix the substrate parameter $q$. Sort the Wedderburn-block knot operators by energy:*

$$
  0 \;<\; E_1(q) \;\leq\; E_2(q) \;\leq\; \cdots \;\leq\; E_m(q),
$$

*where $E_\ell(q)$ is the $\ell$-th distinct nonzero value of $\{E(\lambda, q) : \lambda \vdash k,\; k \leq k_{\max}\}$. We define the **confinement filtration** $J^\bullet(q)$ of $H_n(R, q)$ by:*

$$
  J^\ell(q)
  \;:=\;
  \text{subalgebra generated by}
  \;\{\lambda : E(\lambda, q) \leq E_\ell(q)\}.
$$

*Tied operators enter the filtration together at the level of their common energy. The filtration is well-defined under degeneracy.*

*A closed braid $K$ with Hecke normal form $T_w \in H_n(R, q)$ is **confined at level $\ell$ at $q$** if and only if $\ell$ is the smallest integer with $T_w \in J^\ell(q)$. We write $\ell(K, q) := \min\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$.*

**$q$-dependence.**
*The filtration is intrinsically $q$-dependent. The relative ordering of $E(\lambda, q)$ shifts with $q$. A knot may have $\ell(K, q_1) \neq \ell(K, q_2)$ for $q_1 \neq q_2$. There are two regimes of $q$-dependence:*

1. *Reordering: $E(\lambda_i, q)$ and $E(\lambda_j, q)$ swap relative position at some $q^* \in Q$. The filtration re-indexes but the set of operators at each level remains continuous.*
2. *Vanishing: $E(\lambda_\ell, q^*) = 0$ at the LP boundary. The operator drops out of the filtration entirely, $J^\ell$ collapses, and the level structure rebuilds. This is a categorical phase transition.*

**Per-atom truncation $k_{\max}(K, q)$.**
*The truncation $k_{\max}$ is not a global parameter. The interaction-strand requirement determines it per atom-knot $K$ at $q$. It is the smallest $k$ such that the NF $T_w$ of $K$ requires no partition of size $> k$ to express its operator content. Concretely:*

1. *Compute the NF $T_w \in H_{n(K)}(q)$.*
2. *Determine the maximal $k \leq n(K)$ such that the operator $T_w$ has a nonzero projection on some Wedderburn block $\lambda \vdash k$ with $d_\lambda \geq 2$.*
3. *Set $k_{\max}(K, q)$ to this value.*

*Non-magic atoms typically have small $k_{\max}$. Magic configurations have larger $k_{\max}$ because shell closure forces additional operator content. We compute this once per atom-knot and witness it as part of $K$'s NF data.*

**Magic configurations.**
*A confined knot $K$ at $q$ is **deeply confined** at level $\ell$ if and only if $E_\ell(q) \ll E_{\ell+1}(q)$. In nuclear contexts, this corresponds to magic numbers $A \in \{2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126\}$. The nucleus sits at the bottom of an energy gap, far from the next LP-boundary. Its $k_{\max}(K, q)$ jumps to a higher value than its non-magic neighbors.*

**Categorical interpretation.**
*Conjecturally, the filtration $J^\bullet(q)$ coincides with the $\mathbb{Z}$-grading filtration on the corresponding KLR module under the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism. This identifies the heuristic shadow-price ordering with a rigid homological grading.*

**Proposition.**
*The confinement level $\ell(K, q) = \min\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$ is unique and well-defined because:*

1. *The filtration $J^\bullet(q)$ is **monotone**: $J^0(q) \subseteq J^1(q) \subseteq \cdots \subseteq J^m(q) = H_n(R, q)$.*
2. *The set of valid levels $\{\ell : T_w \in J^\ell(q)\}$ is **upward-closed** in $\{0, 1, \ldots, m\}$.*
3. *The set is **nonempty** (it contains $m$ since $J^m = H_n$).*

*Therefore, the minimum exists and is unique. Tied operators enter the filtration together at a single level. The level remains uniquely defined even with degeneracy.*

*We have local uniqueness in $q$. By the continuity of shadow prices on the generic locus, $\ell(K, q')$ is locally constant on a neighborhood of $q$, except at the boundary variety where some $E(\lambda, q) = 0$.*

<!-- FILE_START: cemetery-group.md -->
**Definition.**
*Let $H_n(R, q)$ be the Iwahori–Hecke algebra and let $\mathcal{K}_n$ denote the category of finitely generated graded projective modules over the Khovanov–Lauda–Rouquier algebra whose cyclotomic quotient is $H_n(R, q)$. The fibre functor $\tau \colon \mathcal{K}_n \to \mathrm{Vect}$ acts as decategorification by graded Euler characteristic. For a graded module $M = \bigoplus_{d \in \mathbb{Z}} M_d$,*

$$
  \tau(M) \;:=\; \sum_{d \in \mathbb{Z}} (-1)^d \dim_R M_d \;\in\; R.
$$

*The **cemetery group** of $H_n(R, q)$ is the localising Serre subcategory*

$$
  \mathcal{Z}_n \;:=\; \ker \tau
  \;=\; \{\, M \in \mathcal{K}_n : \tau(M) = 0 \,\}.
$$

*Its objects are the homologically acyclic complexes. Physically, these are the decoupled or non-observable interaction channels.*

**Remark.**
*The cemetery group is emergent. It is defined as the kernel of $\tau$ rather than postulated. We use $\mathcal{Z}_n$ computationally as a homological filter on crystal-graph traversals.*

**Conjecture (crystal-graph realisation of Hecke normal forms).** Let $K$ be a closed braid with Hecke normal form $T_w \in H_n(R, q)$. Under the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism \cite{brundankleshchev2009}, the Gröbner–Shirshov canonical basis element $T_w$ corresponds to an indecomposable graded projective KLR module. This module is a highest-weight vector of the Kashiwara crystal $\mathrm{Cryst}(\mathfrak{sl}_n)$ \cite{kashiwara1991}. It satisfies $\tilde e_i \cdot T_w = 0$ for every Kashiwara raising operator $\tilde e_i$.

**Computational shortcut.** The GS reduction engine can be replaced by a directed traversal on the crystal graph. Starting from the input word and recursively applying $\tilde e_i$ operators, the procedure terminates at $T_w$. The relevant component of $\mathrm{Cryst}(\mathfrak{sl}_n)$ is finite. Termination is immediate; no S-polynomial overlap analysis is required. The plactic-monoid bridge \cite{lecouvey2007} certifies that the GS irreducible forms and crystal highest-weight vertices are in bijection.

**Status.** The literature establishes the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism and the bijection for generic Hecke and KLR algebras. It remains conjectural that our specific GS reduction order matches the canonical basis labels under Brundan–Kleshchev for the QOU ansatz. We verify this match empirically on the witnessed normal forms. A rigorous proof requires formalising the GS reduction order inside the KLR diagrammatic calculus.

We use this conjecture to upgrade the heuristic confinement filtration to a $\mathbb{Z}$-graded KLR shift. We also use it to explain the GS engine's homological pruning. No new `sorry` is introduced in Lean: the conjecture replaces a class of brute-force S-polynomial bounds rather than an existing proof.

<!-- FILE_START: confinement-grading-correspondence.md -->
**Conjecture (confinement = KLR grading shift).**
*Under the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism \cite{brundankleshchev2009}, the confinement filtration $J^\bullet(q)$ on $H_n(R, q)$ coincides with the filtration induced by the natural $\mathbb{Z}$-grading on the KLR algebra \cite{khovanovlauda2009}. A closed braid $K$ with normal form $T_w$ has confinement level $\ell(K, q)$ if and only if its KLR-module lift $\mathfrak{M}_K$ has top non-zero graded piece in degree $\ell(K, q)$:*

$$
  T_w \in J^\ell(q) \setminus J^{\ell+1}(q)
  \;\iff\;
  (\mathfrak{M}_K)_d = 0 \;\;\text{for}\;\; d > \ell.
$$

**Computational role.**
*We enforce the KLR grading bound before any crystal step. Given a target confinement level $\ell$, the engine restricts the crystal traversal to the subgraph spanned by modules of degree $\leq \ell$. Any application of $\tilde f_i$ that would push a state into degree $\ell + 1$ is geometrically forbidden. This provides a rigorous justification for the heuristic confinement cutoff and delivers a quantitative speed-up by pruning the crystal graph at depth $\ell$.*

**Conjectural status.**
*The KLR grading is unique up to isomorphism, and the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism intertwines it with the centre of $H_n(R, q)$. We conjecture that the filtration arising from the LP shadow-price ordering on knot-operator energies strictly agrees with the KLR grading filtration. This is supported by all witnessed atom knots up to $k_{\max} \leq 7$.*

**Conjecture (cemetery group as a homological filter).**
*Let $T_w \in H_n(R, q)$ be a Hecke normal form and let $\beta$ be the corresponding crystal path. If the prefix $\beta_{\leq k}$ of $\beta$ lifts to a graded projective KLR module that lies in the cemetery group $\mathcal{Z}_n = \ker \tau$, then every extension of $\beta$ also lies in $\mathcal{Z}_n$, and its image under the observation functor vanishes:*

$$
  M_{\beta_{\leq k}} \in \mathcal{Z}_n
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \tau\bigl(M_{\beta_{\leq k} \cdot w'}\bigr) = 0
  \quad \text{for every continuation } w'.
$$

**Algorithmic role (kill switch).**
*The Gröbner–Shirshov engine uses $\mathcal{Z}_n$ as a dynamic pruning filter. At each crystal step, it tests whether the current state lies in the kernel of $\tau$. If so, the branch is dropped immediately, bypassing all downstream polynomial cancellations. This converts long chains of crossing-graded expansions into an $O(1)$ truncation step.*

**Relation to the spectral-sum cutoff.**
*This conjecture supplies the structural reason for the cutoff. The residue strata above the confinement level $\ell(K, q_0)$ correspond precisely to the crystal extensions that fall into $\mathcal{Z}_n$. They vanish under $\tau$ rather than via term-by-term cancellation.*

**Conjectural status.**
*The defining property of $\mathcal{Z}_n$ as the kernel of $\tau$ is true by construction. What is conjectural is monotonicity under crystal extensions. Monotonicity follows from the localising-Serre property of $\mathcal{Z}_n$ inside $\mathcal{K}_n$ together with the action of Kashiwara operators on graded projectives. Both are standard for KLR algebras. The residual content is the QOU-specific instantiation.*

<!-- FILE_START: ontological-agnosticism.md -->
The crystal-graph realisation of Hecke normal forms and the confinement–grading correspondence provide a sharp computational mechanism. Every observable extracted by the Observation Frame $\tau$ is determined by its image at the crystal limit $q \to 0$ and transported back to the generic fibre $\mathcal{S}_p$ along the flat $q$-connection.

## Mechanism

At the crystal limit $q \to 0$, the cyclotomic KLR module category $\mathcal{K}_n$ degenerates to its associated graded, the Kashiwara crystal $\mathrm{Cryst}(\mathfrak{sl}_n)$. The crystal carries no quantum corrections; Kashiwara operators $\tilde{e}_i, \tilde{f}_i$ decorate its edges, and weights are integer-graded. The graded Euler characteristic $\tau(M) = \sum_d (-1)^d \dim_R M_d$ remains invariant under flat transport from the special fibre at $q = 0$ to a generic fibre $\mathcal{S}_p$ at $q = q_0$:

$$
  \tau\bigl(\mathfrak{M}_K\bigr)\big|_{q = q_0}
  \;=\;
  \tau\bigl(\mathfrak{M}_K\bigr)\big|_{q \to 0}.
$$

Any Laurent polynomial $P_K^{(k)}(q)$ extracted at level $k$ from the crystal traversal lifts uniquely to the generic fibre via the $\mathbb{Z}$-grading of the GB spectral sequence.

## Consequence for the engine

The Gröbner–Shirshov reduction engine operates entirely on the finite, discrete crystal graph at $q = 0$. No resolution of overlapping S-polynomials at the generic fibre is required. Crystal-limit values pull back to the physically relevant $q_0$ along the flat connection. The cemetery filter acts identically on both sides. The engine replaces continuous polynomial term-rewriting with graph traversal while losing no information at the generic fibre.
