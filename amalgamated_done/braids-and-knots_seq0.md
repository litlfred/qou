To evaluate observables, we replace continuum path integrals with finite sums over discrete fibres. Because both the base space $B$ and its fibres are finite, the continuum measure $\mathcal{D}\phi$ becomes a combinatorial sum. This discrete formulation avoids divergent integrals and their associated renormalisation.

Particle interactions arise from the braid group acting on fibre states. Instead of Feynman vertices, interactions correspond to braid operators exchanging strands in the fibre bundle. These exchange operations depend on a parameter $q$ reflecting the quantum group structure of the fibres. Specifically, the $R$-matrix of $\mathcal{U}_q(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ provides a $q$-deformed braid operator that determines the phase acquired during particle exchange.

Different knot types define particle identities. The unknot, which has trivial braiding, represents the neutrino. The trefoil knot $3_1$ represents the electron. Heavier charged leptons correspond to higher torus knots $T_{2,2k+1}$ constructed via the brane tower. Quarks correspond to a Borromean braid configuration of three linked unknots residing on the exceptional divisor. These knots are supported by Bring's surface.
