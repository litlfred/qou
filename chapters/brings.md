# The Descartes Universe  {#chap:descartes}

## The Descartes Condition

**Definition** (Descartes condition).
A $\mathbb{C}$ quantum observable universe $(M, G, E)$ satisfies the
*Descartes condition* if the Euler characteristic of the bulk bundle is
$$\chi(E) = -6.$$
A $\mathbb{C}$ quantum observable universe satisfying the Descartes condition
is called a *Descartes universe*.

> *"Je pense, donc je suis."*
> — René Descartes

## Bring's Curve

**Remark** (Bring's curve).
Bring's curve is the unique Riemann surface of genus $4$ with the maximal
automorphism group $S_5$ (order 120) for its genus. It is defined by the
intersection of three symmetric hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{P}^4$:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{5} z_i = 0, \qquad
  \sum_{i=1}^{5} z_i^2 = 0, \qquad
  \sum_{i=1}^{5} z_i^3 = 0.$$

The symmetric group $S_5$ acts by permuting the five homogeneous coordinates,
realising three copies of $S_3 \subset S_5$ corresponding to the three
colour charges (red, green, blue).

## Exceptional Divisor and Chiral Sector Coordinates

Let $p \in M$ be a brane point with outward normal $N(p) = p/|p|$,
positively-oriented tangent frame $(T_1, T_2)$, and $R = R_{\mathbb{CP}^1}$.
Set $O(p) = p + R\,N(p)$.

**Definition** (Exceptional divisor).
The *exceptional divisor* at $p$ is the $\mathbb{CP}^1$ fibre
$$E_p = \pi^{-1}(p) = \bigl\{
  O + R\bigl(\sin\phi\,(\cos\psi\,T_1 + \sin\psi\,T_2) - \cos\phi\,N\bigr)
  : \phi \in [0,\pi],\, \psi \in [0,2\pi)
\bigr\}.$$

**Definition** (Chiral sector coordinates $\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b}$).
Fix substrate parameter $q$, $\phi \in [0,\pi]$, $\psi \in [0,2\pi/3]$:
$$\mathfrak{r}(\phi,\psi) = O + R\bigl(\sin\phi\,(\cos(\psi+q\phi)\,T_1
  + \sin(\psi+q\phi)\,T_2) - \cos\phi\,N\bigr),$$
$$\mathfrak{g}(\phi,\psi) = \mathfrak{r}(\phi,\psi + \tfrac{2\pi}{3}), \qquad
  \mathfrak{b}(\phi,\psi) = \mathfrak{r}(\phi,\psi + \tfrac{4\pi}{3}).$$

The chiral twist $q\phi$ rotates each sector by $q$ full turns pole-to-pole.
The three sectors tile exactly: $E_p = \mathfrak{r}(p) \cup \mathfrak{g}(p) \cup \mathfrak{b}(p)$,
sharing only boundary meridians.

## Descartes Theorem

**Theorem** (Descartes theorem).
Let $\mathcal{C}_{-6}$ denote the category of $\mathbb{C}$ quantum observable
universes satisfying the Descartes condition $\chi(E) = -6$, with morphisms
given by QOU maps preserving the Euler characteristic. Then Bring's curve
$\Sigma_4$ is a terminal object in $\mathcal{C}_{-6}$: for every Descartes
universe $(M,G,E) \in \mathcal{C}_{-6}$, there exists a unique morphism
$(M,G,E) \to (\Sigma_4, G, E_{\Sigma_4})$.
