Motivation

There have been several generalizations of Gröbner--Shirshov basis theory, coming in one of two flavors: noncommutative theory and theories for working over special ground rings, e.g. Euclidean domains, PID's or UFD's. Over a field, one of the main uses of a Gröbner--Shirshov basis is to find a basis for the quotient of an algebra A by a (left or two-sided) ideal I.

If one regards the motivating examples closely, one sees that the ground field k never enters into the picture. Specifically, one does not need to invert any constants, and this leads to the notion of a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis. The key observation is that for many natural quotient algebras — polynomial rings modulo monomial ideals, universal enveloping algebras — the Gröbner--Shirshov basis argument works over an arbitrary commutative ground ring R, provided the leading coefficients are units.

✎ edit📣 feedback Example Polynomial quotient. Let A be the polynomial algebra k[x_1, \ldots, x_n] and consider the ideal \mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \ldots, x_n). Taking a graded lexicographic ordering on A with x_i < x_j if i < j, the ideal \mathfrak{m}^2 has a Gröbner--Shirshov basis given by \{x_i x_j\}. Thus the quotient A/\mathfrak{m}^2 is an (n+1)-dimensional k-vector space with a basis:

A/\mathfrak{m}^2 = k\{1, x_1, \ldots, x_n\}

The fact that k was a field is not important — one would have a similar statement with the integers \mathbb{Z}, though not through classical Gröbner--Shirshov basis techniques.

✎ edit📣 feedback Example Universal enveloping algebra. If \mathfrak{g} is a Lie algebra with Lie bracket [\,,\,]_{\mathfrak{g}} over a field k, one forms the universal enveloping algebra U\mathfrak{g} as the quotient of the tensor algebra T\mathfrak{g} by the two-sided ideal:

J = (xy - yx - [x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}} \mid x, y \in \mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow T\mathfrak{g})

Choosing a total ordering on the index set I for a k-basis \mathfrak{g} = k\langle x_i \mid i \in I \rangle, a Gröbner--Shirshov basis argument [bergman1978] yields the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem: U\mathfrak{g} has a basis of non-decreasing words.

If we replace k by an arbitrary commutative ring R, one cannot make use of classical Gröbner--Shirshov basis techniques — one must prove this by ad-hoc methods. We view this inability as an unsatisfactory state of affairs that the notion of a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis resolves.

Main result

✎ edit📣 feedback Theorem Freeness of quotient by unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis. Let R be a commutative algebra with unity. Let A be an R-algebra which is free as an R-module and without quasi-zeros. Let (B, <) be an admissible system on A and let I be a two-sided ideal of A with a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis G. Define \widetilde{O}(G) to be the free R-module spanned by the monomials which do not occur as leading monomials of members of G. Then, as free R-modules,

A/I \;\cong\; \widetilde{O}(G) \qquad\text{and}\qquad A \;=\; I \oplus \widetilde{O}(G).

✎ edit📣 feedback Example Motivating examples. Let k be a (commutative) field with unity.

  1. Let A be the polynomial algebra k[x_1, \ldots, x_n] and consider the ideal \mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \ldots, x_n). Taking a graded lexicographic ordering on A with x_i < x_j if i < j, \mathfrak{m}^2 has a Gröbner--Shirshov basis given by \{x_i x_j\}. Thus the quotient A/\mathfrak{m}^2 is an (n+1)-dimensional k-vector space with basis:

      A/\mathfrak{m}^2 = k\{1, x_1, \ldots, x_n\}.

  2. If \mathfrak{g} is a Lie algebra with bracket [\,{,}\,]_{\mathfrak{g}} over k, then one forms the universal enveloping algebra U\mathfrak{g} as the quotient of the tensor (free) algebra T\mathfrak{g} by the two-sided ideal

      J = \bigl(xy - yx - [x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}
    \;\big|\; x, y \in \mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow T\mathfrak{g}\bigr).

    Choosing a total ordering < on an index set I for a k-basis \mathfrak{g} = k\langle x_i \mid i \in I\rangle, a Gröbner--Shirshov basis argument yields the Poincaré--Birkhoff--Witt (PBW) theorem: U\mathfrak{g} has a basis of non-decreasing words.

In both cases, the fact that k is a field is not essential — similar statements hold over \mathbb{Z}, though not via classical Gröbner--Shirshov basis techniques.

Monomials, orderings, and admissible systems

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Monomials and monomial bases. Throughout, R is a commutative ring with unity and A is an R-algebra without quasi-zeros (i.e.\ elements a \in A such that bac = 0 for all b, c \in A not both 1).

Choose a set of algebra generators A = R\langle x_i \mid i \in \Lambda\rangle for some index set \Lambda.

Let \alpha = \alpha_1 \alpha_2 \cdots \alpha_n be a finite-length word in \Lambda. A monomial in A is the ordered product

  x_\alpha := x_{\alpha_1} \cdot x_{\alpha_2} \cdots x_{\alpha_n}.

Suppose A is free as an R-module. We say A has a monomial basis B subordinate to \Lambda if B is a subset of all finite-length words in \Lambda such that

  A = R\{x_\beta \mid \beta \in B\}.

We identify \beta with x_\beta to simplify notation. The admissible subsets B are the monomial bases of A.

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Leading monomial, coefficient, and term. Let B be a monomial basis well-ordered by <. For f \in A, write f uniquely as

  f = c_1 b_1 + c_2 b_2 + \cdots + c_n b_n,
  \qquad c_i \in R^* := R \setminus \{0\},\;
  b_i \in B,\;
  b_1 > b_2 > \cdots > b_n.

Then define:

  • the leading monomial \mathrm{LM}(f) := b_1,
  • the leading coefficient \mathrm{LC}(f) := c_1,
  • the leading term \mathrm{LT}(f) := \mathrm{LC}(f)\,\mathrm{LM}(f).

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Leading monomial, coefficient, and term. Assume that B is well-ordered by <. Let f \in A, then we may write f uniquely as the finite sum:

f = c_1 b_1 + c_2 b_2 + \cdots + c_n b_n \quad \text{for } c_i \in R^* := R \setminus \{0\},\; b_i \in B

such that b_1 > b_2 > \cdots > b_n. Then:

  • The leading monomial of f: \LM(f) := b_1
  • The leading coefficient of f: \LC(f) := c_1
  • The leading term of f: \LT(f) := \LC(f) \cdot \LM(f)

We say that \beta \in B divides f (written \beta \mid f) if there are u, v \in B and \lambda \in R^* such that \lambda \LT(u \cdot \beta \cdot v) = \LT(f).

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Monomial ordering and admissible system. A monomial ordering for a monomial basis B of an algebra A is a well-ordering < on B such that for b, b', r, s \in B:

  1. If b < b' then r \cdot b \cdot s < r \cdot b' \cdot s whenever r \cdot b \cdot s \neq 0 and \mathrm{LM}(r \cdot b' \cdot s) \neq 0.
  2. If b' = \mathrm{LM}(r \cdot b \cdot s) \neq 0 with r or s \neq 1, then b < \mathrm{LM}(b').

If B admits a monomial ordering <, then the pair (B, <) is an admissible system.

Divisibility. If (B, <) is an admissible system and 0 \neq f \in A, we say \beta \in B divides f (written \beta \mid f) if there exist u, v \in B and \lambda \in R^* such that

  \lambda\,\mathrm{LT}(u \cdot \beta \cdot v) = \mathrm{LT}(f).

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Remainder modules O(I) and \tilde{O}(I). Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module and without quasi-zeros, (B, <) an admissible system on A, and I \subseteq A a subset.

Define the R-submodule O(I) of A — the remainder module — as the R-submodule spanned by the set

  o(I) := \bigl\{\lambda\beta \;\big|\;
    \lambda \in R^*,\; \beta \in B,\;
    \forall\, h \in I\colon \mathrm{LT}(h) \neq \lambda\beta\bigr\}.

Define also the R-module \tilde{O}(I) as the R-submodule spanned by

  \tilde{o}(I) := B \setminus \{\mathrm{LM}(h) \mid h \in I\}.

\tilde{O}(I) is a free R-module.

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Remainder modules O(I) and \widetilde{O}(I). Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module and without quasi-zeros, (B, <) an admissible system on A, and I \subseteq A a subset. Define the R-submodule O(I) of A — the remainder module — to be the R-submodule spanned by the set:

o(I) := \{\lambda\beta \mid \lambda \in R^*,\; \beta \in B,\; \forall h \in I \text{ we have } \LT(h) \neq \lambda\beta\}

We also define the R-module \widetilde{O}(I) as the R-submodule spanned by the set:

\widetilde{o}(I) := B \setminus \{\LM(h) \mid h \in I\}

\widetilde{O}(I) is a free R-module.

Division algorithm and Gröbner--Shirshov bases

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Division algorithm. Input: R a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module and without quasi-zeros, (B, <) an admissible system on A, a subset I \subseteq A, and f \in A.

Output: \tilde{f} \in I and r \in O(I) (the remainder of f on division by I) so that f = r + \tilde{f}.

  1. Set i := 0, f_0 := f.
  2. While f_i \neq 0:
    • Set i := i + 1.
    • If there is no h \in I with \mathrm{LT}(h) \mid f: set r_i := \mathrm{LT}(f) and f_i := f_{i-1} - r_i.
    • Else: choose h_i \in I with 0 \neq \mathrm{LT}(h_i) \mid f_{i-1}; choose \lambda_i \in R^*, u_i, v_i \in B so that \lambda_i \mathrm{LT}(u_i \cdot h_i \cdot v_i) = \mathrm{LT}(f_{i-1}); set f_i := f_{i-1} - \lambda_i u_i \cdot h_i \cdot v_i and r_i := 0.
  3. Return r := \sum_i r_i and \tilde{f} := \sum_i \lambda_i u_i \cdot h_i \cdot v_i = f - r.

Because we do not specify how to choose h_i (nor u_i, v_i), the output is not in general unique.

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Gröbner--Shirshov basis. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module and without quasi-zeros, (B, <) an admissible system, and I a two-sided ideal in A.

A set of generators G = \{g_\gamma \mid \gamma \in \Gamma\} for I is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis with respect to (B, <) if for every h \in I we have a representation

  h = \sum_{k \in K} \lambda_k\, u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k

for K an index set, \gamma_k \in \Gamma, \lambda_k \in R^*, u_k, v_k \in B, such that \mathrm{LM}(u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k) \leq \mathrm{LM}(h) whenever u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k \neq 0.

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Unital subset. Let R, A, and (B, <) be as in the Gröbner--Shirshov basis definition, and let G = \{g_\gamma \mid \gamma \in \Gamma\} be a subset of A.

A subset G \subseteq A is unital if:

  1. For all \gamma \in \Gamma: \mathrm{LC}(g_\gamma) \in R^\times (the units of R).
  2. For all \gamma \in \Gamma and \alpha, \beta \in B: \mathrm{LC}(\alpha \cdot g_\gamma \cdot \beta) \in R^\times whenever \alpha \cdot g_\gamma \cdot \beta \neq 0.

If the ground ring is a field, every Gröbner--Shirshov basis is automatically unital. If B is closed under multiplication, condition (b) follows from (a).

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Gröbner--Shirshov basis and unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module and without quasi-zeros, and let (B, <) be an admissible system on A.

Let I be a two-sided ideal in A with a set of generators G = \{g_\gamma \mid \gamma \in \Gamma\}.

We say that G is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis with respect to (B, <) if for every h \in I we have a representation:

h = \sum_{k \in K} \lambda_k u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k

for K an index set, \gamma_k \in \Gamma, \lambda_k \in R^* and u_k, v_k \in B such that \LM(u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k) \leq \LM(f) whenever u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k \neq 0.

We call a subset G \subseteq A unital if:

  1. For all \gamma \in \Gamma: \LC(g_\gamma) \in R^\times (the units of R).
  2. For all \gamma \in \Gamma and for all \alpha, \beta \in B: \LC(\alpha \cdot g_\gamma \cdot \beta) \in R^\times whenever \alpha \cdot g_\gamma \cdot \beta \neq 0.

Here \LC(a) is the coefficient, in the expansion of a in the R-basis B, of the <-greatest basis element occurring in a.

An S-polynomial about f, f' \in A is constructible if there exist u, u', v, v' \in B and \lambda, \lambda' \in R^* such that \lambda \LT(u \cdot \LT(f) \cdot v) = \lambda' \LT(u' \cdot \LT(f') \cdot v'), in which case:

S(f, f') := \lambda u \cdot f \cdot v - \lambda' u' \cdot f' \cdot v'

The extra conditions of a Gröbner--Shirshov basis being unital are not that strong: if the ground ring is a field, then a Gröbner--Shirshov basis is automatically a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis.

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition S-polynomial. Let R, A, and (B, <) be as in the Gröbner--Shirshov basis definition.

For f, f' \in A, an S-polynomial is constructible about f and f' if there exist u, u', v, v' \in B, \lambda, \lambda' \in R^* such that

  \lambda\,\mathrm{LT}(u \cdot \mathrm{LT}(f) \cdot v)
  = \lambda'\,\mathrm{LT}(u' \cdot \mathrm{LT}(f') \cdot v').

In this case,

  S(f, f') := \lambda\, u \cdot f \cdot v
    - \lambda'\, u' \cdot f' \cdot v'.

Main results

✎ edit📣 feedback Lemma Generator choice in the division algorithm. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module, (B, <) an admissible system, and let I be a two-sided ideal of A generated by a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis G = \{g_\gamma \mid \gamma \in \Gamma\}.

Then in the division algorithm, we may choose h_i \in I so that h_i = g_{\gamma_i} for some \gamma_i \in \Gamma.

✎ edit📣 feedback Proof Set h := h_i, f := f_i. Since G is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis, write

  h = \sum_{k \in K} \lambda_k\, u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k

with \lambda_k \in R^*, u_k, v_k \in B, \gamma_k \in \Gamma. Set \alpha := \mathrm{LM}(h).

Choose a representation with \alpha minimal, and define T := \{k \in K \mid \mathrm{LM}(u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k) = \alpha\}. Further choose so that |T| is minimal. If |T| = 1, we are done.

Otherwise, let k_1 \neq k_2 \in T and set c_{k_i} := \mathrm{LC}(u_{k_i} \cdot g_{\gamma_{k_i}} \cdot v_{k_i}). By unitality, c_{k_i} \in R^\times, so we may form the S-polynomial

  S := \lambda_{k_2}\,\frac{c_{k_2}}{c_{k_1}}\,
    u_{k_1} \cdot g_{\gamma_{k_1}} \cdot v_{k_1}
    - \lambda_{k_2}\, u_{k_2} \cdot g_{\gamma_{k_2}} \cdot v_{k_2}.

Substituting back, either all terms with leading monomial \alpha cancel (contradicting minimality of \alpha) or we reduce |T| by at least one (contradicting minimality of |T|). Thus |T| = 1.

✎ edit📣 feedback Lemma Generator choice in division. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module, (B, <) an admissible system on A, and let I be a two-sided ideal of A generated by a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis G = \{g_\gamma \mid \gamma \in \Gamma\}. Then in the division algorithm, we may choose h_i \in I so that h_i = g_{\gamma_i} for some \gamma_i \in \Gamma.

Proof. Set h := h_i, f := f_i. Since G is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis, we may write:

h = \sum_{k \in K} \lambda_k u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k

with \lambda_k \in R^*, u_k, v_k \in B and \gamma_k \in \Gamma. Denote \alpha := \LM(h). Choose a representation so that \alpha is minimal and let T := \{k \in K \mid \LM(u_k \cdot g_{\gamma_k} \cdot v_k) = \alpha\} with |T| minimal. If |T| = 1 we are done. Otherwise, for k_1 \neq k_2 \in T, the unitality condition gives c_{k_i} := \LC(u_{k_i} \cdot g_{\gamma_{k_i}} \cdot v_{k_i}) \in R^\times, allowing us to form the S-polynomial and reduce |T| by one — contradicting minimality. Thus |T| = 1. \square

✎ edit📣 feedback Theorem Free module decomposition via unital Gröbner--Shirshov bases. Let R be a commutative ring with unity. Let A be an R-algebra which is free as an R-module and without quasi-zeros. Let (B, <) be an admissible system on A and let I be a two-sided ideal of A with a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis G. Define \tilde{O}(G) to be the free R-module spanned by the monomials which do not occur as leading monomials of members of G. Then, as free R-modules,

A/I \;\cong\; \tilde{O}(G) \qquad\text{and}\qquad A \;=\; I \oplus \tilde{O}(G).

✎ edit📣 feedback Proof Let f \in A. The division algorithm writes f = r + \tilde{f} with r \in O(I) and \tilde{f} \in I. By Lemma (generator choice), we can take r \in \tilde{O}(G). As f is arbitrary, A = I + \tilde{O}(G).

It remains to show the sum is direct, which follows from uniqueness of r. Suppose the division algorithm produces two representations:

  f = \tilde{f} + r = \tilde{f}' + r'.

Then \tilde{f} - \tilde{f}' \in I, so r - r' \in I. If r - r' \neq 0, then by Lemma (generator choice) there exists g_\gamma \in G with \mathrm{LT}(g_\gamma) \mid (r - r'). But by construction of r and r', no such g_\gamma exists — contradiction. Thus r = r' and the sum is direct.

✎ edit📣 feedback Proof Proof of the main theorem. Proof of Theorem 2. Let f be an element of A. The division algorithm allows us to write f = r + \tilde{f} with r \in O(I) and \tilde{f} \in I. By Lemma 6 we can take r \in \widetilde{O}(G). As f is arbitrary, we have:

A = I + \widetilde{O}(G)

It remains to show that this sum is direct, which follows from uniqueness of r. Suppose the algorithm produces two representations:

f = \tilde{f} + r = \tilde{f}' + r'

Then \tilde{f} - \tilde{f}' \in I so that r - r' \in I. If r - r' \neq 0, then Lemma 6 shows there is some g_\gamma \in G with \LT(g_\gamma) \mid (r - r'). But by construction of r and r', no such g_\gamma exists — a contradiction. \square

✎ edit📣 feedback Proposition S-polynomial decomposition of cancelling sums. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module, (B, <) an admissible system. Let \alpha \in B and suppose f_1, \ldots, f_n \in A satisfy \mathrm{LM}(f_i) = \alpha and \mathrm{LC}(f_i) \in R^\times. If

  f := \sum_i c_i f_i, \qquad c_i \in R^*,

satisfies \mathrm{LM}(f) < \alpha, then we may write

  f = \sum_{i \neq j} d_{i,j}\, S_{i,j}

where the S_{i,j} are the S-polynomials about f_i and f_j:

  S_{i,j} := \frac{1}{a_i} f_i - \frac{1}{a_j} f_j,
  \qquad a_i := \mathrm{LC}(f_i),\; a_j := \mathrm{LC}(f_j) \in R^\times.

✎ edit📣 feedback Proof Because all terms involving \alpha cancel, \sum_i c_i = 0. Then:

  f = c_1 a_1 \cdot \frac{1}{a_1} f_1 + \cdots + c_n a_n \cdot \frac{1}{a_n} f_n.

Telescoping:

  f = c_1 a_1\, S_{1,2}
    + (c_1 a_1 + c_2 a_2)\, S_{2,3}
    + \cdots
    + (c_1 a_1 + \cdots + c_{n-1} a_{n-1})\, S_{n-1,n}
    + \underbrace{(c_1 a_1 + \cdots + c_n a_n)}_{= 0} \cdot \frac{1}{a_n} f_n,

giving the desired result.

✎ edit📣 feedback Theorem Buchberger's theorem over commutative rings. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module, (B, <) an admissible system. Let I \leq A be an ideal generated by a unital set G := \{g_\gamma \mid \gamma \in \Gamma\}.

Then G is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis for I if and only if all S-polynomials for G have zero remainder under the division algorithm.

✎ edit📣 feedback Proof We show that if all S-polynomials reduce to zero and f \in I, then f has a Gröbner--Shirshov basis representation. Since G generates I, choose a representation

  f = \sum_i h_i \cdot g_{\gamma_i} \cdot h_i', \qquad
  h_i, h_i' \in A,\; \gamma_i \in \Gamma.

Expanding in the monomial basis B, write f = \sum_{i,k,k'} c_k c_{k'}' \beta_k \cdot g_i \cdot \beta_{k'}'.

If for some representation all terms satisfy \mathrm{LM}(\beta_k \cdot g_i \cdot \beta_{k'}') \leq \mathrm{LM}(f), we are done. Otherwise, define \alpha := \max\{\mathrm{LM}(h_i \cdot g_i \cdot h_i')\} with \alpha > \mathrm{LM}(f); choose \alpha minimal over all representations.

Set T := \{i \mid \mathrm{LM}(h_i \cdot g_i \cdot h_i') = \alpha\} and g := \sum_{i \in T} \mathrm{LT}(h_i) \cdot g_i \cdot \mathrm{LT}(h_i'). By unitality, the leading coefficients are units, so Proposition (S-polynomial decomposition) applies:

  g = \sum_{i \neq j \in T} d_{i,j}\, S_{i,j}.

The S_{i,j} are also S-polynomials about g_i, g_j; by assumption they reduce to zero, i.e.
S_{i,j} = \sum_l \lambda_l^{i,j}\, u_l^{i,j} \cdot g_{l}^{i,j} \cdot v_l^{i,j} with \mathrm{LM}(S_{i,j}) < \alpha. Substituting back gives a new representation of f with strictly smaller maximal term — contradiction.

✎ edit📣 feedback Corollary Poincaré--Birkhoff--Witt theorem over commutative rings. Let \mathfrak{g} be a Lie algebra over R, a commutative ring with unity, which is free as an R-module, with Lie bracket [\,{,}\,]_{\mathfrak{g}}. Then the universal enveloping algebra

  U\mathfrak{g} = T\mathfrak{g} / J, \qquad
  J = \bigl(xy - yx - [x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}
    \;\big|\; x, y \in \mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow T\mathfrak{g}\bigr)

is isomorphic as an R-module to S\mathfrak{g}, the symmetric algebra on \mathfrak{g}.

In particular, if R is a \mathbb{Q}-algebra, we obtain a Gröbner--Shirshov basis proof of the equivalence of the category of (finite-dimensional) Lie algebras over R and (finite-dimensional) smooth formal groups over R.

✎ edit📣 feedback Proof Choose a well-ordered basis \{x_i \mid i \in I\} for \mathfrak{g}. Then T\mathfrak{g} has a multiplicative monomial basis consisting of the words of finite length in the x_i's with the graded lexicographic ordering. The symmetric algebra S\mathfrak{g} has a basis of words written in non-decreasing order.

The ideal J is generated by

  G := \{g_{i,j} := x_i x_j - x_j x_i - [x_i, x_j]_{\mathfrak{g}}
    \mid x_i > x_j\}.

The argument that G is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis is exactly as in the classical setting, using Buchberger's theorem. As the leading terms \mathrm{LT}(g_{i,j}) = x_i x_j are monic and the basis is multiplicative, G is a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis. The corollary then follows from the main decomposition theorem.

Buchberger's Theorem and the PBW Corollary

S-polynomial cancellation

✎ edit📣 feedback Proposition S-polynomial decomposition. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module, (B, <) an admissible system on A. Let \alpha \in B and suppose that f_1, \ldots, f_n \in A satisfy \LM(f_i) = \alpha and \LC(f_i) \in R^\times. Then if:

f := \sum_i c_i f_i \quad (c_i \in R^*)

satisfies \LM(f) < \alpha, we may write:

f = \sum_{i \neq j} d_{i,j} S_{i,j}

where the S_{i,j} are the S-polynomials about f_i and f_j given by:

S_{i,j} := \frac{1}{a_i} f_i - \frac{1}{a_j} f_j, \qquad a_i := \LC(f_i),\; a_j := \LC(f_j) \in R^\times

Proof. Because we have cancellation of the terms involving \alpha, we have \sum_i c_i = 0. Then a telescoping argument yields:

f = c_1 a_1 S_{1,2} + (c_1 a_1 + c_2 a_2) S_{2,3} + \cdots + (c_1 a_1 + \cdots + c_{n-1} a_{n-1}) S_{n-1,n}

which gives the desired result. \square

Buchberger's theorem over R

✎ edit📣 feedback Theorem Buchberger's theorem over a commutative ring. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra which is free as an R-module, (B, <) an admissible system on A. Let I \leq A be an ideal generated by a unital set G := \{g_\gamma \mid \gamma \in \Gamma\}. Then G is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis for I if and only if all S-polynomials for G have zero remainder under the division algorithm.

Proof. We show that if all S-polynomials reduce to zero and f \in I, then f has a Gröbner--Shirshov basis representation. Since G generates I, choose:

f = \sum_i h_i \cdot g_{\gamma_i} \cdot h'_i

Expanding in the basis B, if for some representation we have \LM(\beta_k \cdot g_i \cdot \beta'_{k'}) \leq \LM(f) for all terms, we are done. Otherwise, let \alpha := \max\{\LM(h_i \cdot g_i \cdot h'_i)\} > \LM(f) be minimal over all representations. Applying Proposition 7 to the terms with leading monomial \alpha, we write their sum in terms of S-polynomials. By hypothesis, these reduce to zero, giving a representation with strictly smaller maximal term — a contradiction. \square

Jet-stratified reduction

✎ edit📣 feedback Definition Jet-stratified Gröbner--Shirshov reduction. Let R be a commutative ring with unity, A an R-algebra with admissible system (B, <), and \{A_n\}_{n \geq 0} a jet tower with A_{n+1} = A_n \otimes \Omega^1(A_n). At each level, the jet prolongation introduces k new generators (the Kähler directions). Let \Sigma_n be the defining relations of A_n and suppose \Sigma_n is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis for the ideal it generates.

The jet-stratified reduction algorithm exploits two structural features of the jet tower to reduce the cost of verifying and applying the GS basis at level n+1:

  1. Descended/transient splitting. The descent involution \hat{\tau}_n classifies each relation in \Sigma_{n+1} as descended (inherited from level n) or transient (new at level n+1). Descended S-polynomials are already resolved.

  2. Degree bucketing. The deg-lex order compares by word length first. A monomial of degree d can only be reduced by a rule whose leading monomial has degree \leq d.

Algorithm 1: Jet-Stratified GS Verification

Input: Rewriting system \Sigma_{n+1} at level n+1, partitioned as \Sigma_{n+1} = \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{desc}} \cup \Sigma_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}}; proof that \Sigma_n is a GS basis.

Output: Decision whether \Sigma_{n+1} is a GS basis.

  1. Inherit. Accept all \binom{|\Sigma_n|}{2} descended \times descended S-polynomial pairs as resolved (by the level-n GS property).

  2. For each pair (g_i, g_j) with at least one rule transient:

    a. Compute the overlap compositions S(g_i, g_j).

    b. Reduce S(g_i, g_j) to normal form using the division algorithm applied to \Sigma_{n+1} (full system).

    c. If the remainder is nonzero, return "not a GS basis".

  3. Return "\Sigma_{n+1} is a GS basis".

Pair count. Let m = |\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{desc}}| and k = |\Sigma_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}}|. Step 2 processes m \cdot k + \binom{k}{2} pairs (excluding self-compositions). The savings over naive Buchberger are \binom{m}{2} skipped pairs.

Algorithm 2: Degree-Bucketed Division

Input: Element f \in A_{n+1}, GS basis \Sigma_{n+1} partitioned into degree buckets B_0, B_1, B_2, \ldots where B_d contains rules whose leading monomial has degree d.

Output: Normal form \mathrm{NF}(f).

  1. Set r := 0, h := f.

  2. While h \neq 0:

    a. Let d := \deg(\mathrm{LM}(h)).

    b. Scan only buckets B_0, B_1, \ldots, B_d for a rule g with \mathrm{LM}(g) \mid \mathrm{LM}(h).

    c. If no such g exists: set r := r + \mathrm{LT}(h), h := h - \mathrm{LT}(h).

    d. Else: choose g \in B_{\leq d} with \mathrm{LM}(g) \mid \mathrm{LM}(h); choose \lambda \in R^*, u, v \in B with \lambda\,\mathrm{LT}(u \cdot g \cdot v) = \mathrm{LT}(h); set h := h - \lambda\, u \cdot g \cdot v.

  3. Return r.

Per-step cost. At degree d, the scan in step 2b examines |B_{\leq d}| rules instead of |\Sigma_{n+1}|. For the Hecke algebra, |B_2| = O(n) (Hecke + far-commutativity) and |B_3| = O(n) (Yang--Baxter), so most reductions scan O(n) rules instead of O(n^2).

Algorithm 3: Combined Jet-Stratified + Degree-Bucketed Reduction

Input: Element f \in A_{n+1}; jet-stratified system (\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{desc}}, \Sigma_{n+1}^{\mathrm{trans}}); degree buckets B_0, \ldots, B_D; normal-form cache \mathsf{C} from level n.

Output: Normal form \mathrm{NF}(f).

  1. Decompose. Write f = f^{\mathrm{desc}} + f^{\mathrm{trans}} where f^{\mathrm{desc}} contains only descended monomials and f^{\mathrm{trans}} contains at least one transient generator per monomial.

  2. Cache lookup. For each descended monomial m in f^{\mathrm{desc}}:

    a. If m \in \mathsf{C}: replace m by \mathsf{C}[m].

    b. Else: reduce m using degree-bucketed division (Algorithm 2) restricted to \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{desc}}; store result in \mathsf{C}.

  3. Reduce transient part. Apply degree-bucketed division (Algorithm 2) to f^{\mathrm{trans}} using the full system \Sigma_{n+1}.

  4. Combine. Set h := \mathsf{C}[f^{\mathrm{desc}}] + \mathrm{NF}(f^{\mathrm{trans}}).

  5. Final pass. Apply degree-bucketed division to h using \Sigma_{n+1} (cross-terms between descended and transient parts may create new reducible monomials).

  6. Return \mathrm{NF}(h).

Correctness. By the composition lemma (Buchberger's theorem), the normal form is unique. Each sub-step computes a partial normal form; the final pass resolves cross-terms. The cache is valid because the descended subsystem's normal forms are stable under extension by transient rules (the leading monomials of transient rules involve new generators that cannot appear in descended monomials).

The PBW theorem over a commutative ring

✎ edit📣 feedback Corollary Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem over a commutative ring. Let \mathfrak{g} be a Lie algebra over R, a commutative ring with unity, which is free as an R-module, with Lie bracket [\,,\,]_{\mathfrak{g}}. Then:

U\mathfrak{g} = T\mathfrak{g}/J \quad \text{where} \quad J = (xy - yx - [x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}} \mid x, y \in \mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow T\mathfrak{g})

is isomorphic as an R-module to S\mathfrak{g}, the symmetric algebra on \mathfrak{g}.

Proof. Choosing a well-ordered basis \{x_i \mid i \in I\} for \mathfrak{g}, the tensor algebra T\mathfrak{g} has a multiplicative monomial basis of finite-length words with the graded lexicographic ordering, and S\mathfrak{g} has a basis of non-decreasing words. The ideal J is generated by:

G := \{g_{i,j} := x_i x_j - x_j x_i - [x_i, x_j]_{\mathfrak{g}} \mid x_i > x_j\}

The argument that G is a Gröbner--Shirshov basis follows from Theorem 8. As the leading terms \LT(g_{i,j}) = x_i x_j are monic and the basis is multiplicative, G is a unital Gröbner--Shirshov basis. The corollary follows from Theorem 2. \square
