Introduction

Scope and epistemic boundary

✎ edit📣 feedback This companion paper isolates the speculative formulations of classical open problems that arise alongside the Quantum Observable Universe (QOU) monograph [leitner2026]. None of the constructions recorded here proves, resolves, or reduces a Millennium Prize problem or the Collatz conjecture. Every algebraic theorem on which the analogies draw remains in the main monograph under a neutral mathematical name; this paper holds the problem-specific dictionaries, conjectural translations, and heuristic surreal-number comparisons so that proved algebra and speculative framing are never conflated.

Five open-problem families and one cross-problem comparison are treated:

  1. Yang--Mills existence and mass gap. Elementary positivity \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})(q - 1)^2 > 0 for q > 1 is a rational inequality on the crossing-energy functional, and the categorical mass-gap structure takes its lower bound as a postulated field rather than deriving a four-dimensional Wightman quantum field theory on \mathbb{R}^4.
  2. The Riemann hypothesis. Weil's bound confines the Frobenius eigenvalues of a smooth projective curve over \mathbb{F}_p to |\alpha_i| = p^{1/2}, and for Bring's curve those eigenvalues build L(E, s)^4 in \zeta_{\mathcal{B}}(s) = \zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)/L(E, s)^4 for the elliptic curve 50\mathrm{a}1 rather than \zeta(s). Neither local Frobenius unitarity nor the surreal inequality \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3 bears on the zeros of \zeta(s).
  3. The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Vanishing of the categorical invariant r_q = \mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, E_q) follows from the categorical observability condition \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0 on a representation-theoretic Hom group, not on the arithmetic Mordell--Weil group E(\mathbb{Q}). Rank zero for the rational elliptic curve 50\mathrm{a}1 is a classical theorem of Kolyvagin, not a consequence of QOU axioms.
  4. P versus NP. Exact evaluation of the Jones polynomial is \#\mathrm{P}-hard at generic parameters [jaeger1990], and matrix-word trace evaluation on a fixed number of strands takes O(c) multiplications. Neither \#\mathrm{P}-hardness of counting nor Hochschild rigidity of a fixed-rank Iwahori--Hecke algebra H_n(q) supplies a uniform counting-to-decision separation, and the computational power of a QOU depends on an unfixed choice of observation cost model.
  5. The Collatz problem. Specialising the affine [2]_q-division map \mathcal{C}_q on \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] at q = 1 recovers the classical Syracuse map, so classical convergence implies q-convergence on domain-stable lifts rather than the converse. The contact-average threshold \langle v_{[2]} \rangle \geq 2 fails on the fundamental cycle \{q^{-1}, 1 + q^{-1}\}, where \langle v_{[2]} \rangle = 1.
  6. Cross-problem surreal and Kummer tables. The surreal squeeze \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3 is a generic monomial inequality in \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) that uses no analytic property of the problem to which it is applied; the Navier--Stokes row compares two fixed rates rather than squeezing a drift \delta to zero; one-dimensionality of HH^2 \cong k does not align evaluation maps landing in distinct cohomology groups; and sharing the abstract record of a length-filtered observation system couples two properties of a single dynamical instance rather than linking separate problems.

Navier--Stokes regularity is not moved here: the fluid-dynamics chapter of [leitner2026] retains its q-deformed Navier--Stokes PDE model directly, scoped as an algebraic-regularisation model whose classical q \to 1 blow-up transfer remains open. Hodge and Poincaré conjectures are not treated in either paper.

Aether parameter conventions and cross-paper references

✎ edit📣 feedback Throughout this paper, (\mathbf{C}, A, \tau) denotes a quantum observable universe over a commutative ring R with aether parameter q \in R^\times, Iwahori--Hecke quotient H_n(q), Jones--Markov trace \mathrm{tr}_M, and categorical Reeb derivation \xi_q, as defined in [leitner2026]. In surreal-order comparisons, \varepsilon denotes a positive infinitesimal in the ordered field \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) inside Conway's surreal numbers \mathbf{No} [conway1976], with q = 1 + \varepsilon, Reeb period scaling \Omega_q \asymp \varepsilon^{-1}, crossing-energy gap scaling \Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3, and \omega-adic leading-exponent valuation \ordw.

In finite-field curve sections, the prime-power cardinality |\mathbb{F}_p| or |\mathbb{F}_{p^r}| is distinguished explicitly from the deformation parameter q: a finite-field cardinality is a fixed integer prime power admitting no continuous limit q \to 1. Yang--Mills Mass Gap Framing

Crossing-energy positivity versus Wightman field theory

✎ edit📣 feedback The classical Yang--Mills Millennium Problem [jaffe2006] asks for the mathematical construction of a four-dimensional quantum Yang--Mills theory on \mathbb{R}^4 for every compact simple gauge group G, satisfying the Wightman or Osterwalder--Schrader axioms and possessing a strictly positive mass gap \Delta > 0 in the spectrum of the Hamiltonian.

Nothing in the Quantum Observable Universe monograph [leitner2026] or in this chapter proves or reduces that problem. Separating what is proved in the main monograph from what is postulated here clarifies the boundary:

  1. Proved rational and differential-geometric ingredients. For real q > 1, the crossing-energy gap functional \Delta_q = \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})(q - 1)^2 is strictly positive, vanishes on the 2-torsion subgroup \mu_2 = \{1, -1\}, and increases monotonically on (1, \infty). On an archimedean CR realisation, the q-weighted curvature--Levi functional weights the horizontal self-dual curvature by the local Levi-form coupling, and Bogomolny self-duality holds on categorical instanton sectors.
  2. Where the gap is postulated rather than derived. The categorical mass-gap carrier bundles a transfer-matrix spectrum (\lambda_n)_{n \ge 0} together with the inequality \Delta_q \le \lambda_1 as one of its own structure fields. Positivity of \lambda_1 follows from \Delta_q > 0 only once such a structure is supplied; no theorem derives \Delta_q \le \lambda_1 from a four-dimensional continuum gauge action. Calling the mass gap true by categorical construction is therefore a relabelling of an elementary rational inequality, not a solution of the Yang--Mills problem.
  3. Where continuum reconstruction remains open. Passing from a q-deformed Frobenius--Hopf algebra at q > 1 to a renormalised Wightman field theory on \mathbb{R}^4 as q \to 1 requires both Osterwalder--Schrader reflection positivity of the antipode-twisted pairing and a non-trivial continuum limit in which the physical gap does not collapse to zero. Both steps remain open conjectures.

✎ edit📣 feedback Conjecture Categorical Yang--Mills existence and mass-gap dictionary conjecture. Let (\mathbf{C}, A, \tau) be a quantum observable universe over a commutative ring R with real specialization q > 1. Let \nabla be a categorical connection on the Frobenius--Hopf object A with curvature F_\nabla = \nabla^2, let \mathfrak{S}_q[\nabla] be the q-weighted curvature--Levi functional, and let \Omega_0 \colon R \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{C}}(A) be the G_q-invariant vacuum functional on the direct-limit algebra \mathcal{A}_\infty = \varinjlim \mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{C}}(A_n). The speculative categorical analogue of the Yang--Mills problem posits:

  1. Existence. There exists a connection \nabla critical for \mathfrak{S}_q[\nabla] whose vacuum functional \Omega_0 extends to a positive-definite sesquilinear form on a Hilbert completion \mathcal{H}_q.
  2. Spectral gap. Every non-vacuum excitation \varrho \colon \mathbf{1} \to A in the spectrum of the categorical transfer matrix \mathcal{T}_q \colon \mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{C}}(A_n) \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{C}}(A_n) satisfies
  \mathfrak{m}(\varrho) \geq \Delta_q > 0,

where \Delta_q = \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})(q - 1)^2 is the crossing-energy gap functional, and the two-point correlator decays at rate at least \Delta_q.

Proposed dictionary. The correspondence between the classical Wightman formulation on \mathbb{R}^4 [jaffe2006] and the categorical QOU objects is listed in Table 1.

What is proved versus postulated. The inequality

  \Delta_q = \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})(q - 1)^2 > 0 \qquad (q > 1)

is an elementary rational fact: \Delta_q vanishes on \mu_2 = \{1, -1\} and is strictly positive on (1, \infty). What is not proved is that a transfer matrix attached to four-dimensional \mathrm{SU}(N) gauge fields has first excited eigenvalue bounded below by \Delta_q, or that the q \to 1 continuum limit produces a Wightman quantum field theory on \mathbb{R}^4 with a surviving physical gap. In the formal carrier, the bound \Delta_q \le \lambda_1 is an assumed structure field rather than a theorem, and exponential decay of two-point correlators is not derived from spectral theory.

✎ edit📣 feedback Remark Surreal-order relabelling of the crossing-energy gap, and why it does not prove a Yang--Mills mass gap. Status: surreal-order relabelling, not a proof. Evaluating the rational expression \Delta_q = \tfrac{1}{2}(q - q^{-1})(q - 1)^2 at q = 1 + \varepsilon for a positive infinitesimal \varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No} [conway1976] gives

  \Delta_q = \varepsilon^3 - \tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^4 + \cdots \asymp \varepsilon^3 > 0

in the ordered field \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon), equivalently \ordw(\Delta_q) = -3 > -\infty = \ordw(0).

Earlier exposition described this inequality as making the Yang--Mills mass gap true by categorical construction. It does not, for three independent reasons:

  1. Rational positivity is not a spectral theorem. Positivity of \varepsilon^3 in \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) restates the algebraic fact that (q - q^{-1})(q - 1)^2 > 0 for q > 1. No operator on a continuum gauge-field Hilbert space is shown to have spectrum bounded below by \Delta_q; in the categorical dictionary conjecture, that bound is an input field of the postulated transfer-matrix structure.
  2. Mismatch with the surreal squeeze threshold. The monomial squeeze \delta^2 \varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3 is equivalent to |\delta| > \varepsilon^2. It quantifies over standard-real \delta > 0, fails at \delta = 0, and sets a threshold \varepsilon^2 that is larger than \Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3 by an infinite factor \varepsilon^{-1}, so an excitation at scale \Delta_q does not clear the squeeze.
  3. Vanishing standard part. Under the standard-part map \mathrm{st} \colon \mathbf{No}_{\mathrm{fin}} \to \mathbb{R}, \mathrm{st}(\Delta_q) = \mathrm{st}(\varepsilon^3) = 0. Extracting a nonzero real mass gap \Delta_{\mathrm{YM}} > 0 on \mathbb{R}^4 requires a non-trivial renormalisation scaling together with Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction [jaffe2006], neither of which follows from surreal arithmetic in \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon).

Riemann Hypothesis Framing

Local curve zeta functions versus global L-functions and \zeta(s)

✎ edit📣 feedback Nothing in the Quantum Observable Universe monograph [leitner2026] or in this chapter proves the classical Riemann hypothesis, and no construction here reduces it to a categorical or geometric theorem. Separating the proved finite-field curve algebra from the speculative global assertions locates each gap explicitly:

  1. Finite-field curve zetas versus the Riemann zeta function. For a smooth projective curve of genus g over a finite field \mathbb{F}_{p^r}, Weil's theorem [weil1948] constrains the Frobenius eigenvalues \alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{2g} on H^1 to |\alpha_i| = p^{r/2}, equivalently \mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = 1/2 under \alpha_i = p^{r\rho_i}. For Bring's genus-four curve \mathcal{B}, whose Jacobian is isogenous to E^4 for the elliptic curve E : y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - x - 2 (Cremona 50\mathrm{a}1), assembling the local curve zeta functions across good primes gives, up to finitely many Euler factors,
  \zeta_{\mathcal{B}}(s) = \frac{\zeta(s)\,\zeta(s - 1)}{L(E, s)^4}.

The Frobenius eigenvalues of \mathcal{B} build L(E, s), whereas \zeta(s) enters only through H^0, which carries no Frobenius eigenvalue other than 1. Local critical-line alignment at each prime therefore concerns the L-series L(E, s)—whose own global Riemann hypothesis in 1/2 < \mathrm{Re}(s) < 3/2 remains open—and supplies no constraint on the non-trivial zeros of \zeta(s).

  1. Finite-field cardinality versus the aether parameter. In the local curve zeta function over \mathbb{F}_p, the parameter is a prime cardinality, not a continuous real deformation parameter q > 1; there is no continuous limit p \to 1.

  2. Unconstructed spectral operators and generic surreal monomials. No Reeb operator \xi_q whose spectrum equals the non-trivial zeros of \zeta(s) is constructed. Similarly, the surreal inequality \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3 holds for every standard real \delta > 0 without using any property of \zeta(s), so it would apply verbatim to functions such as the Davenport--Heilbronn Dirichlet series that satisfy a functional equation and Euler-type symmetries fails while possessing zeros off the critical line.

✎ edit📣 feedback Conjecture Classical Riemann hypothesis. Every non-trivial zero s of the Riemann zeta function \zeta(s) satisfies \mathrm{Re}(s) = \tfrac{1}{2}.

The statement is the classical Millennium Problem, which remains open. Neither the main monograph nor this paper proves any step toward it or assumes a proof of it. Weil's bound gives \mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = 1/2 for the local Frobenius exponents of Bring's curve at each prime of good reduction separately, but those exponents assemble into L(E, s) for the elliptic curve 50\mathrm{a}1 rather than into \zeta(s).

✎ edit📣 feedback Conjecture Adelic persistence of critical-line alignment for L(E, s). Let E be the elliptic curve y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - x - 2 (Cremona 50\mathrm{a}1), whose fourth power E^4 is isogenous to the Jacobian of Bring's curve. Suppose that at every prime p \nmid 10 of good reduction the Frobenius trace a_p = p + 1 - |E(\mathbb{F}_p)| satisfies a_p^2 \le 4p. Then every zero s of the Hasse--Weil L-function L(E, s) in the critical strip \tfrac{1}{2} < \mathrm{Re}(s) < \tfrac{3}{2} satisfies \mathrm{Re}(s) = 1.

Why the statement targets L(E, s) rather than \zeta(s). At each good prime p, the local zeta numerator of Bring's curve is (1 - a_p T + pT^2)^4, and multiplying the local curve zeta functions over primes gives \zeta(s)\zeta(s - 1)/L(E, s)^4 up to bad Euler factors. The Frobenius traces a_p enter only through L(E, s) = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n n^{-s}. An earlier version of this conjecture concluded the classical Riemann hypothesis for \zeta(s) from local exponent alignment; because \zeta(s) is the H^0 factor and shares no Frobenius data with the antecedent, that earlier version reduced on any aligned input to asserting the classical Riemann hypothesis outright and is retained only as a documented fallback.

The implication carries the entire open distance. The antecedent a_p^2 \le 4p holds unconditionally at every prime by Hasse's theorem [weil1948], whereas the global Riemann hypothesis for L(E, s) is open. Writing the statement as an implication displays the local input alongside the global conclusion; it does not bring the global conclusion closer to proof.

✎ edit📣 feedback Conjecture Conjectural equivalence between Chebyshev error bounds, critical-line zeros, and Reeb spectral alignment. Fix one coupled spectral datum D = (\zeta_D, \psi_D, \rho_D) consisting of a candidate zeta function \zeta_D, its Chebyshev counting function \psi_D, and an indexed family of spectral exponents \rho_D(n, i). The conjecture posits the pairwise equivalence of:

  1. Square-root counting error. \psi_D(X) = X + O(X^{1/2 + \delta}) for every \delta > 0.
  2. Critical-line zeros. Every zero of \zeta_D in the critical strip 0 < \mathrm{Re}(s) < 1 satisfies \mathrm{Re}(s) = 1/2.
  3. Uniform spectral alignment. Every exponent \rho_D(n, i) satisfies \mathrm{Re}(\rho_D(n, i)) = 1/2.

Epistemic separation. For the classical pair (\zeta, \psi), the equivalence (1) \Leftrightarrow (2) is a theorem of analytic number theory [vonkoch1901]. By contrast, the equivalence (2) \Leftrightarrow (3) is speculative: no Reeb operator \xi_q whose eigenvalue family \rho_D is coupled to the zeros of the Riemann zeta function has been constructed.

Bundling requirement. The three predicates must be quantified over a single coupled datum D: quantifying over \psi, \zeta, and \rho independently would force the critical-line predicate on \zeta to be constant across all complex functions, contradicting vacuous satisfaction on \zeta \equiv 1 and failure at s = 1/4 on \zeta \equiv 0.

✎ edit📣 feedback Remark Speculative Reeb-flow and explicit-formula analogies for the Riemann hypothesis. Two heuristic pictures motivate the conjectural statements above, and neither is a mathematical reduction of the classical Riemann hypothesis:

  1. Reeb holonomy unitarity as a picture of Weil's bound. Over a finite field \mathbb{F}_p, the local curve zeta function has reciprocal roots of modulus p^{1/2} by the Castelnuovo--Severi positivity inequality on the correspondence divisor [weil1948, rosen2002]. Interpreting those Frobenius eigenvalues as oscillation modes of a Reeb flow pictures \mathrm{Re}(\rho_i) = 1/2 as unitary balance without exponential growth or decay. That picture neither constructs a Hilbert--Pólya Reeb operator for \zeta(s) over \mathbb{Q} nor transfers local Frobenius unitarity across primes (adelic persistence).
  2. The q-explicit formula analogy. Replacing \log p by a q-deformed Chebyshev weight \psi_q(X) and summing oscillatory waves X^\rho / [\rho]_q imitates Weil's explicit formula for prime-counting fluctuations (coupled stability conjecture). When written over a finite eigenvalue list, the -\tfrac{1}{2}\ln_q(1 - X^{-2}) tail diverges as X \to 1^+ against bounded finite sums, refuting the finite-list form; an infinite-sum formulation requires analytic continuation and conditional-convergence control not supplied by the algebraic QOU axioms.

✎ edit📣 feedback Remark A surreal heuristic for the Riemann hypothesis, and why it is not a proof. Status: heuristic order comparison, not a proof. Let \varepsilon > 0 be the positive infinitesimal in \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No} [conway1976] with q = 1 + \varepsilon, Reeb period \Omega_q \asymp \varepsilon^{-1}, and crossing-energy gap \Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3. For a hypothetical zero \rho of \zeta(s) with standard-real off-line drift \delta = \mathrm{Re}(\rho) - 1/2 > 0, positing a relocation cost E(\delta) \asymp \delta^2 \Omega_q \asymp \delta^2 \varepsilon^{-1} against a dissipation budget \Delta_q \asymp \varepsilon^3 gives

  \delta^2 \cdot \varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3

in \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon), equivalently \ordw(E(\delta)) = 1 > -3 = \ordw(\Delta_q), leaving \delta = 0 as the only standard real with E(\delta) \le \Delta_q.

Why this is not a proof. Neither modelling assumption is derived: nothing in the QOU framework attaches an energy functional E(\delta) \asymp \delta^2 \Omega_q to a zero of \zeta(s) or restricts zeros to configurations below \Delta_q, and the local Frobenius exponents of Bring's curve belong to L(E, s) for 50\mathrm{a}1 rather than \zeta(s). Decisively, the inequality \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3 holds for every standard real \delta > 0 without invoking a single property of \zeta(s)—neither its Euler product nor its Dirichlet coefficients. The identical lines would exclude off-line zeros for the Davenport--Heilbronn function, which satisfies a functional equation and possesses zeros off the critical line. An inequality that cannot distinguish the classical Riemann hypothesis from a known counterexample on a sibling Dirichlet series carries no evidence for it.

Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Framing

Categorical Hom-groups versus arithmetic Mordell--Weil rank

✎ edit📣 feedback The classical Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture [tate1974] relates the arithmetic Mordell--Weil rank \mathrm{rank}\,E(\mathbb{Q}) of an elliptic curve E/\mathbb{Q} to the analytic order of vanishing \mathrm{ord}_{s=1} L(E, s) of its Hasse--Weil L-function, and expresses the leading Taylor coefficient in terms of the Néron--Tate regulator, real period, Tamagawa numbers, torsion order, and Tate--Shafarevich group \mathrm{Sha}(E).

Nothing in the Quantum Observable Universe monograph [leitner2026] or in this chapter proves or reduces any case of the classical conjecture. Three distinct facts separate the proved categorical algebra from the speculative arithmetic analogy:

  1. Categorical Hom-group vanishing is not arithmetic rank zero. In a quantum observable universe, the categorical invariant r_q = \mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, E_q) measures invariant morphisms into an object E_q of the representation category \mathbf{C}. Its vanishing on the adjoint or fundamental colour sector follows directly from the categorical observability axiom \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, \mathrm{Ad}(A)) = 0 (equivalently V_{1/2}^{\mathrm{SU}(2)} = 0), which is a statement of representation theory, not a theorem about the rational point group E(\mathbb{Q}).
  2. Weak and full q-BSD are unproved postulates. The equality \mathrm{ord}_{s=1} L(\mathcal{E}_q, s) = r_q and the rank-zero leading-value formula are packaged as fields of an uninstantiated conjecture class; no theorem proves them from the QOU axioms.
  3. Rank zero for Cremona 50\mathrm{a}1 is classical. That the rational elliptic curve E : y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - x - 2 (Cremona 50\mathrm{a}1) appearing in the Jacobian factorisation J(\mathcal{B}) \sim E^4 of Bring's curve has \mathrm{rank}\,E(\mathbb{Q}) = 0 and L(E, 1) \neq 0 is a classical theorem of Gross--Zagier and Kolyvagin for modular elliptic curves of analytic rank zero, not a prediction or derivation of QOU.

✎ edit📣 feedback Conjecture Speculative q-deformed Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Let (\mathbf{C}, \varTheta, G, \mathcal{S}) be a quantum observable universe with aether parameter q and Descartes elliptic curve \mathcal{E}_q. Let \mathcal{E}_q(\mathbf{1}) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, E_q) be the invariant Hom-group of categorical rank r_q = \mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{E}_q(\mathbf{1}), let L(\mathcal{E}_q, s) be the formal Euler product over places of Bring's coordinate ring, and let \mathrm{Sha}_q(\mathcal{E}_q) be the monoidal localisation kernel. By analogy with classical BSD [tate1974], the speculative q-BSD conjecture posits:

  1. Weak q-BSD. The analytic order of vanishing of L(\mathcal{E}_q, s) at s = 1 equals the categorical rank:
\mathrm{ord}_{s=1} L(\mathcal{E}_q, s) = r_q.
  1. Full q-BSD. The leading coefficient satisfies
\frac{L^{(r_q)}(\mathcal{E}_q, 1)}{[r_q]_q!} = \frac{\Omega_q \cdot \mathrm{Reg}_q(\mathcal{E}_q) \cdot |\mathrm{Sha}_q(\mathcal{E}_q)| \cdot \displaystyle\prod_{\mathfrak{m}} c_{\mathfrak{m}}(q)}{|\mathcal{E}_q(\mathbf{1})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2_q},

collapsing at r_q = 0 (with trivial torsion and empty regulator \mathrm{Reg}_q = 1) to L(\mathcal{E}_q, 1) = \Omega_q \cdot |\mathrm{Sha}_q(\mathcal{E}_q)| \cdot \prod_{\mathfrak{m}} c_{\mathfrak{m}}(q).

Status. Both clauses are unproved hypotheses carried in an uninstantiated conjecture class. Neither clause is connected by a proved functor to the arithmetic Mordell--Weil group E(\mathbb{Q}) or the classical Tate--Shafarevich group \mathrm{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q}).

In the categorical definition \mathcal{E}_q(\mathbf{1}) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{1}, E_q), the integer r_q counts copies of the monoidal unit \mathbf{1} inside the G-representation \tau(E_q):

Distinction from E(\mathbb{Q}). This vanishing is a representation-theoretic identity on invariants of a Lie-group or quantum-group module. It says nothing about rational solutions (x, y) \in \mathbb{Q}^2 of a Weierstrass equation. In the q-BSD conjecture, identifying \mathcal{E}_q(\mathbf{1}) as a categorical analogue of E(\mathbb{Q}) is a naming convention, not an isomorphism of abelian groups.

P versus NP Framing

Skein resolution horizons, counting hardness, and observation cost models

✎ edit📣 feedback What this chapter says about P versus NP, and what it does not. Nothing in the Quantum Observable Universe monograph [leitner2026] or in this chapter proves \mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP}, and nothing here supplies evidence for either answer. Throughout, \mathrm{P} and \mathrm{NP} denote the classical complexity classes for deterministic and nondeterministic Turing machines [cook1971]. No Turing-machine model of those classes is constructed here: wherever an abstract predicate mentions \mathrm{P} or \mathrm{NP}, they enter as opaque propositions, and the proved content reduces to propositional logic or pure algebra.

Proved algebraic facts (retained in the main monograph). The algebraic results that motivate the dictionary below hold independently of complexity theory:

  1. Every trace vanishes on commutators, every derivation annihilates central idempotents and preserves each Wedderburn block, and inner automorphisms fix central idempotents while the Goldman--Iwahori involution \theta(\sigma_i) = -\sigma_i^{-1} is outer for n \ge 2 and q + q^{-1} \neq 0.
  2. Evaluating the trace of a product of c matrices of size d \times d requires at most d^3 c ring multiplications, so any family of braids on a bounded number of strands has O(c) trace evaluation cost and an empty skein-tree horizon; any family with super-polynomial skein-trace cost requires unbounded braid index.
  3. Exact evaluation of the Jones polynomial V_L(t) is \#\mathrm{P}-hard outside six roots of unity of orders \{1, 2, 3, 4\} [jaeger1990] (corresponding under t = q^{\pm 2} to orders \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8\}, which is not \{n : n \mid 6\}).
  4. Because 2^n dominates every polynomial and 2^n \le 3^{3n}, any restricted procedure forced to inspect all 3^{3n} leaves of a skein resolution tree runs in super-polynomial time.

Why none of these reduces or settles P versus NP.

  • Counting versus decision. \#\mathrm{P}-hardness of exact Jones evaluation [jaeger1990] does not separate decision classes: counting perfect matchings in bipartite graphs is \#\mathrm{P}-hard while deciding whether one exists lies in \mathrm{P}. Any uniform counting-to-decision transfer would also face the relativization [bakergillsolovay1975], natural-proofs [razborovrudich1997], and algebrization [aaronsonwigderson2009] barriers.
  • Fixed-rank algebra rigidity. At a split semisimple parameter q [geckpfeiffer2000], H_n(q) is separable, so HH^i(H_n(q), M) = 0 for i \ge 1 and outer derivations vanish; any nonzero polynomial obstruction vanishes at only finitely many parameter values. Uniform asymptotic complexity concerns growth as n \to \infty across all algorithms, which no invariant of a fixed-rank algebra H_n(q) can encode.
  • Unfixed QOU observation cost model. If one QOU observation of a c-crossing braid returned its exact degree-0 Markov trace at linear intent cost O(c), QOU polynomial time would compute \#\mathrm{P}-hard invariants and contain \mathrm{NP}, making QOU-internal \mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{NP} false; if instead observation returns Born-rule samples, the model behaves like quantum polynomial time \mathrm{BQP}, where separation from \mathrm{NP} remains open.

Collatz Problem Framing

Domain-stable Laurent lifts, withdrawn contact thresholds, and open problems

✎ edit📣 feedback What this chapter proves about Collatz, and what it does not. Nothing in the Quantum Observable Universe monograph [leitner2026] or in this chapter proves the classical Collatz conjecture [collatz1937, lagarias2010], and nothing here constitutes a step toward proving it. The underlying algebraic system is a partial affine [2]_q-division map \mathcal{C}_q on the Laurent ring \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]: for [2]_q = q + 1 and [3]_q = q^2 + q + 1,

\mathcal{C}_q(P) =
\begin{cases}
  P / [2]_q & \text{if } P(-1) = 0,\\[4pt]
  \bigl([3]_q P + 1\bigr) / [2]_q & \text{if } P(-1) = -1,
\end{cases}

undefined when P(-1) \notin \{0, -1\}. An element whose entire forward orbit stays in the domain is called domain-stable.

Proved Laurent-ring algebra (retained in the main monograph).

  1. Specialisation at q = 1 recovers the Syracuse map. Evaluating P \mapsto P(1) sends an even step to n \mapsto n/2 and an odd step to n \mapsto (3n + 1)/2, matching the classical parity of P(1) at every step.
  2. Complete cycle classification in \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]. The only periodic orbits of \mathcal{C}_q in \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] are \{0\}, the odd fixed point \{-q^{-2}\}, and the fundamental 2-cycle \{q^{-1}, 1 + q^{-1}\} (lifting \{0\}, \{-1\}, and \{1, 2\}).
  3. Classical convergence implies q-convergence on lifts. If the Syracuse orbit of P(1) is eventually periodic, any domain-stable lift P reaches one of the three cycles above; consequently, classical no-divergence implies q-Collatz convergence, not the reverse, and non-trivial negative integer cycles (if any existed, or classical 3n - 1 counterparts such as \{-5, -7, -10\}) admit no domain-stable lift.
  4. Bounded exponent window implies convergence. Any domain-stable orbit whose support stays within a fixed degree window q^{-N_0}\mathbb{Z}[q]_{\le m} has finitely many \mathbb{F}_2[q] reductions, forcing an eventually periodic parity word and hence convergence to one of the three cycles; any counterexample must therefore have unbounded exponent support and lift an unbounded Syracuse orbit.

Withdrawn over-claims and open boundaries (recorded below).

  • Contact-average threshold \langle v_{[2]} \rangle \ge 2 fails. Because \mathcal{C}_q already divides by [2]_q inside its odd step, the fundamental cycle \{q^{-1}, 1 + q^{-1}\} has \langle v_{[2]} \rangle = 1 < 2, and a degree count forces \limsup \langle v_{[2]} \rangle \le 1 on every domain-stable orbit with infinitely many odd steps.
  • Odd-step output need not be q-even. Differentiating [2]_q R = [3]_q P + 1 at q = -1 gives R(-1) = 1 + P'(-1), as witnessed by the odd fixed point -q^{-2} \mapsto -q^{-2}.
  • Heuristic contractions and surreal monomials. Real-valued Lyapunov expressions \log_q |P(q)| are undefined on general Laurent polynomials, leave the classical parity sequence unchanged, and in the surreal register assert unproved contact-volume scalings whose conclusion is withdrawn.

  • [2]_q^3 - [3]_q = q^3 + 2q^2 + 2q fails to stay positive across higher (p, 2^k) comparisons once degree mismatches flip the leading term (for example, [3]_q versus [8]_q reverses sign for large q).

These inequalities are statements about the polynomials [m]_q at real q > 0, not theorems about convergence of the q-Collatz map [collatz1937, lagarias2010]. As noted in the withdrawn contact-threshold block, the contact-average criterion with which they were once paired fails on the fundamental cycle, and the parity sequence of any domain-stable orbit is fixed at q = 1.

✎ edit📣 feedback Remark Open problems for the Laurent map \mathcal{C}_q. Four open questions surround the q-Collatz conjecture; none is claimed here:

  1. Power-series characterisation of divergent lifts. By the Syracuse-lift and bounded-window propositions of the main monograph, q-Collatz fails if and only if some domain-stable Laurent polynomial lifts an unbounded Syracuse trajectory [lagarias2010] with unbounded exponent support. Writing t = 1 + q, any domain-stable element with odd steps at positions i_1 < i_2 < \cdots expands in \mathbb{Z}[[t]] (where [3]_q = 1 - t + t^2 is a unit) as
P = -\sum_{j \geq 1} t^{\,i_j}\,[3]_q^{-j}.

Determining which infinite sequences (i_j) sum to a finite Laurent polynomial in q is open, and whether this question is more tractable than classical Collatz is unknown.

  1. Characterising liftable integers. For integers n = P(1) admitting a domain-stable lift, q-Collatz implies classical convergence, yet liftable integers are sparse: an odd step into [2]_q^k q^{-1} (lifting 2^k) exists if and only if [3]_q \mid ([2]_q^{k+1} - q), which evaluation at a primitive cube root of unity shows holds precisely for k \equiv 1 \pmod{6} (so 85 = (2^8 - 1)/3 lifts, whereas 5 = (2^4 - 1)/3 and 3 do not, and nonzero constants are not domain-stable).

  2. Dynamics of the mod-2 shadow. Reducing \mathcal{C}_q modulo 2 produces a total map \bar{\mathcal{C}} on \mathbb{F}_2[q^{\pm 1}] sending f \mapsto f/(1 + q) when f(1) = 0 and f \mapsto ((1 + q + q^2)f + 1)/(1 + q) when f(1) = 1. A domain-stable \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] orbit converges if and only if its mod-2 shadow is eventually periodic. Unlike the \mathbb{F}_2[x] analogue with multiplier 1 + x, whose orbits all converge [hicksmullenyucaszavislak2008], numerical experiments indicate unbounded-degree orbits for \bar{\mathcal{C}} on \mathbb{F}_2[q^{\pm 1}], so characteristic-0 lift constraints are essential.

  3. Constructing a Hecke-algebra lift. Formulating the third face of proposed braid faces as a mathematical statement requires defining a partial or total lift of \mathcal{C}_q to H_n(q) compatible with the Markov trace.

Cross-Problem Speculative Tables and Analogies

Surreal monomial tables, Kummer evaluations, and length-filtered systems

✎ edit📣 feedback Earlier exposition grouped six classical open problems—the Riemann hypothesis, Navier--Stokes regularity, the Collatz problem, \mathrm{P} versus \mathrm{NP}, Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, and Yang--Mills existence and mass gap—under three cross-problem comparison tables: a surreal \omega-order table built on \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3 [conway1976], a Hochschild--Kummer obstruction comparison built on HH^2 \cong k, and a length-filtration comparison built on an abstract observation-system record.

None of those three constructions links the truth values of the classical problems or reduces one problem to another:

  1. Why \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3 does not unify or settle the rows. For any standard real \delta > 0 and positive infinitesimal \varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}(\varepsilon) \subset \mathbf{No}, \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} > \varepsilon^3 is an elementary monomial inequality in an ordered field. It uses no analytic, arithmetic, or complexity-theoretic property of the problem to which \delta is attached, and in every drift-squeeze row the scaling E(\delta) \asymp \delta^2\varepsilon^{-1} is postulated rather than derived. Moreover, the Navier--Stokes row is not even an instance of that drift-vanishing schema: its cost column carries no \delta at all, comparing a fixed enstrophy scaling \varepsilon^{-4/3} against \varepsilon^3 to argue that an excess occurs rather than that a drift vanishes.
  2. Why HH^2 \cong k does not align verdicts across problems. One-dimensionality of a source group HH^2 \cong k does not make evaluation maps into distinct target groups proportional to one another. Even within the Kummer tower of Bring's surface (\chi_{\mathrm{eu}} = -6), the reduction -6 \bmod n vanishes at n \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\} and is nonzero for n \nmid 6, producing opposite verdicts simultaneously from one class.
  3. Why sharing a length-filtration record does not couple separate problems. Packaging a state transition map, a length function \ell, and a trace observation into a common abstract record couples boundedness and periodic-attractor descent within one dynamical instance (a halting trajectory on a periodic attractor visits finitely many states and is therefore bounded). Instantiating that record on distinct systems—Laurent polynomials under \mathcal{C}_q versus brane vortex words—shares a type signature, not a dynamical state, and transfers no theorem between Collatz, \mathrm{P} versus \mathrm{NP}, and Navier--Stokes.

✎ edit📣 feedback Remark Why a one-dimensional source HH^2 \cong k does not link verdicts across open problems. A recurring speculative proposal in earlier drafts treated the classical-limit obstructions for the Riemann hypothesis, Navier--Stokes regularity, Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, and P versus NP as evaluations of one generator \chi(A) \in HH^2 \cong k. Even under the hypothesis \dim_k HH^2 = 1, that shared source transfers no verdict between the problems:

  1. Distinct target groups. One-dimensionality of the domain HH^2 \cong k implies only that every class in HH^2 is a scalar multiple of a single generator, so the image of each evaluation map lies in a one-dimensional subspace of its own codomain. Because the proposed evaluation maps land in distinct groups (H^2(\mathcal{S}, \mu_n), local cohomology H^3_{\mathrm{loc}}, and formal skein-tree residues) with no canonical isomorphism between them, one evaluation map can vanish identically while another is injective.

One class therefore yields vanishing and non-vanishing reductions simultaneously, refuting any claim that sharing \chi(A) forces uniform answers across problems.

✎ edit📣 feedback Remark Length-filtration trichotomy on abstract observation systems, and why sharing a type does not couple problems. Three speculative reformulations—q-Collatz convergence, the skein P-versus-NP boundary (obstruction form), and q-Navier--Stokes vortex complexity—can be phrased as predicates on an abstract length-filtered observation system (X, T, \ell, \mathrm{obs}):

Reformulation Predicate on the length filtration \ell along T-orbits
q-Collatz Does every domain-stable orbit reach a finite periodic attractor?
P versus NP boundary Is \mathrm{obs} computable within a polynomial \ell-budget?
q-Navier--Stokes Does \ell remain bounded along forward trajectories?

What is proved within a single instance versus what is not coupled across problems. Within one dynamical system (X, T, \ell, \mathrm{obs}) whose target normal form is periodic (such as the period-2 cycle \{q^{-1}, 1 + q^{-1}\} of \mathcal{C}_q), any trajectory that reaches the periodic cycle visits only finitely many states thereafter and consequently has bounded \ell; conversely, an unbounded trajectory cannot halt on the cycle, and on any forward-invariant halting cone the hitting time \mu(x) = \min\{k : T^k(x) \in \text{cycle}\} is tautologically a strict \mathbb{N}-valued descent measure.

Crucially, this implication couples two predicates asked of the same system instance. Instantiating (X, T, \ell, \mathrm{obs}) once on Laurent polynomials under \mathcal{C}_q and separately on vortex braid words in a fluid model shares a record signature, not a state space or transition map. Neither boundedness of fluid enstrophy nor polynomial-time trace evaluation implies or is implied by q-Collatz convergence.
