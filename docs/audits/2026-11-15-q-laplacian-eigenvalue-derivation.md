# Deriving the q-Laplacian eigenvalue Σ[c][c+1] — pre-registration and result

Owner request, 2026-11-15: "try derive [the eigenvalue] Σ[c][c+1]".
Context: PR #8043 recorded that in `q-heat-kernel-peter-weyl` the factor ħ_q² is derived
(rem:q-laplacian-expansion) but the eigenvalue of Δ_ξ on a Wedderburn block is only asserted.

## Pre-registration (written before any Lean or computation)

The claim "Δ_ξ acts on V_λ by Σ_{□∈λ} [c(□)][c(□)+1]" splits into two steps. Each is judged
separately.

**Step 1 (algebra).** There is a central element C_n of H_n(q) that acts on every
Jucys–Murphy weight vector of shape λ (L_k acting by q^{2c_k}) by Σ_k [c_k][c_k+1].

- Success: a sorry-free Lean proof, over a generic commutative ring, of (a) centrality of C_n
  and (b) its action on a JM weight vector, from the existing `qJM` / `qJMInv` API.
- Honest limit fixed in advance: that every V_λ *has* a JM weight basis with eigenvalues
  q^{2c} is the seminormal form (Ram 1997 Thm 4.1). The corpus proves it only for the trivial,
  sign and (2,1) representations. Step 1 inherits that hypothesis; it does not remove it.
- Prediction: the symmetric-integer form passes; the Jackson-integer form as printed in the
  statement cannot be the eigenvalue of any element of the Laurent algebra in the L_k^{±1},
  because it needs q^{c}, not q^{2c}.

**Step 2 (geometry).** Δ_ξ, defined in rem:q-laplacian-expansion as
(Θ∧L_ξ)(L_ξ* ι_ξ) + (L_ξ* ι_ξ)(Θ∧L_ξ), acts on V_λ through C_n.

- Success: a corpus artifact that defines an H_n(q)-action on the space Δ_ξ acts on, shows Δ_ξ
  commutes with it, and identifies Δ_ξ with C_n (or any element with the same eigenvalues).
- Failure modes named in advance: no such action in the corpus (then the step stays open), or a
  consistency check that fails.
- Pre-registered check: compute Δ_ξ on 0-forms from its own definition and compare with
  Σ c(c+1) at q = 1.

**What would change compute.** Only if both steps close does the "A anyway" code change
(μ_λ = ħ_q² Σ[c][c+1], symmetric integers) become derived. If only Step 1 closes, the code stays
as it is and the gap moves from "the eigenvalue" to "Δ_ξ = C_n".

## Result

### Step 1 (algebra): closes, conditional only on the seminormal form

With symmetric integers, `a = q^c`, `b = q^{-c}`:

    (q − q⁻¹)² [c][c+1] = (a − b)(q a − q⁻¹ b) = q a² + q⁻¹ b² − (q + q⁻¹).

The right-hand side is a Laurent polynomial in `a² = q^{2c}`, the eigenvalue of the Murphy element
`L_k` in the QOU skein normalisation. So

    C_n = q Σ_k L_k + q⁻¹ Σ_k L_k⁻¹ − (n+1)(q + q⁻¹)

is central (from the centrality of `Σ L_k` and `Σ L_k⁻¹`, already sorry-free in
`QJucysMurphy.lean`) and acts on every JM weight vector of shape λ by
`(q − q⁻¹)² Σ_{□∈λ} [c][c+1]`. Lean: `QOU/SchurWeyl/ContentCasimir.lean`
(sorry-free; `lean -DautoImplicit=false -s 65536` exit 0 against its 7-module import cone rebuilt from current source; axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound only). The eigenvalue of `L_k⁻¹` is derived from that of `L_k`, not assumed.
`C_n` is bar-invariant (`q ↦ q⁻¹`, `L_k ↦ L_k⁻¹`), as `[c][c+1]` is.

What remains assumed, as pre-registered: that every `V_λ` has a JM weight basis with eigenvalues
`q^{2c}` (Ram 1997 Thm 4.1). The corpus proves it for the trivial, sign and (2,1) representations
only.

The Jackson-integer form printed in the statement is not covered: `[c]_J = (1 − q^c)/(1 − q)` needs
`q^c`, which no element generated by the `L_k^{±1}` produces as an eigenvalue. The symmetric form
is the only one of the two that is a central-element eigenvalue.

The eigenvalue does not separate partitions. At `n = 4`, `(3,1)` and `(1⁴)` both give
`(q²+1)³/q³`; at `n = 3`, `(2,1)` and `(1³)` both give `(q²+1)/q`. Values of `Σ[c][c+1]`
(symmetric integers) and of its `q = 1` limit `Σ c(c+1)`:

| λ | Σ c(c+1) | Σ [c][c+1] |
|---|---|---|
| (3) | 8 | (q²+1)³/q³ |
| (2,1) | 2 | (q²+1)/q |
| (1³) | 2 | (q²+1)/q |
| (4) | 20 | (q²+1)(q⁸+2q⁶+4q⁴+2q²+1)/q⁵ |
| (3,1) | 8 | (q²+1)³/q³ |
| (2,2) | 2 | (q²+1)/q |
| (2,1,1) | 4 | 2(q²+1)/q |
| (1⁴) | 8 | (q²+1)³/q³ |

### Step 2 (geometry): does not close, and has an obstruction

No corpus artifact puts an `H_n(q)`-action on the space `Δ_ξ` acts on. Searched: `folio/`,
`docs/audits/`, `computations/` for `Δ_ξ` together with Hecke / Jucys / `H_n`. The only hits are
the claim itself, its code twin (`hecke_core_legacy.q_laplacian_eigenvalue`, which asserts it in a
comment), and `2026-10-10-milnor-voice-audit-ch1-ch2.md`, which notes that `Δ_ξ` is not defined
in the chapter and that on functions on SU(2) it is `−ξ²`.

Obstruction, from the definition in rem:q-laplacian-expansion:

1. `Δ_ξ = A A* + A* A` with `A = Θ∧L_ξ`, `A* = L_ξ* ι_ξ`. No `q` appears in it; `q` enters the
   three-term expansion only through the coefficients `q⁻²`, `q⁻¹(1−q⁻¹)`, `(1−q⁻¹)²`.
2. So `Δ_ξ` is one fixed symmetric operator, and its set of eigenvalues is a fixed countable set
   (eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues of a symmetric operator on a separable space are
   orthogonal).
3. The claimed eigenvalue on a nonzero `V_λ` varies continuously and non-constantly with `q`, e.g.
   `(q²+1)/q` for `λ = (2,2)`.

So the formula cannot hold for all `q` in any interval, whatever `H_n(q)`-action is chosen.
Scope: this is a structural exclusion of the formula *as a function of q*, resting on the
`q`-independence of `Δ_ξ` as defined and on countability of point spectrum. It does not exclude
the value at a single `q₀`, and it does not apply if `Δ_ξ` is redefined to depend on `q`.

On 0-forms, `Δ_ξ f = L_ξ* L_ξ f = −ξ² f` (Reeb fields preserve the contact volume), the Laplacian
along the Reeb orbits. Its eigenvalues are squares of Reeb weights up to one scale, and at `q = 1`
the formula's values at `n = 4` are `{20, 8, 2, 4}`, whose ratios to 2 (10 and 2) are not
squares. This second check needs a periodic Reeb flow, so it is weaker than the first.

### What this means for compute

Step 2 does not close, so the "A anyway" change is still not derived, and the code stays as it is.
The gap is now narrower and better located: not "what is the Casimir eigenvalue" (Step 1 answers
that, given the seminormal form) but "what operator is `μ_λ` the eigenvalue of". The obstruction
says it cannot be the `q`-independent `Δ_ξ` of rem:q-laplacian-expansion. A `q`-dependent operator
built from the Hecke action, such as `C_n` itself, would need its own definition in the paper.
