# The binding Δ_ξ operator: a Reeb-holonomy Laplacian (mass-pipeline gap 2)

Date: 2026-11-16. Base main 483b56794. Issue #8091. Follows `2026-11-15-q-laplacian-eigenvalue-derivation.md`.

## Result in one paragraph

The eigenvalue μ_λ = Σ_{□∈λ} [c][c+1] (symmetric q-integers; times the prefactor) is the block
eigenvalue of an explicit operator built only from braiding holonomies:

    Δ_ξ^(q) := (q − q⁻¹)⁻² Σ_{k=0}^{n−1} ( q·Hₖ + q⁻¹·Hₖ⁻¹ − (q + q⁻¹) ),   Hₖ := (Δ²_{k−1})⁻¹ Δ²_k,

where Δ²_k = (σ₀⋯σ_{k−1})^{k+1} is the full twist of the first k+1 strands. Hₖ is exactly the
Murphy element Lₖ (proved in Lean, below), so Δ_ξ^(q) is the content Casimir C_n of
`ContentCasimir.lean`, already proved central. With q = e^h, each summand is
2cosh(h(2Xₖ+1)) − 2cosh h, a symmetric second difference with step h in Xₖ + ½, where Xₖ is the
classical Jucys–Murphy element. As h → 0 the operator tends to Σ Xₖ(Xₖ+1). So it is a
finite-step Laplacian, and its spectrum moves with q because the step is h. The obstruction recorded
on 2026-11-15 (a q-independent operator cannot have this spectrum) does not apply to it.

## Does μ_λ carry ħ_q²? No: derived from the definitions of d_q and δ_q

Owner request (2026-11-16): derive, do not fit, whether μ_λ carries the prefactor ħ_q² = (1 − q⁻¹)²
that `prop:q-heat-kernel-peter-weyl` puts on it.

Definitions used, all from the corpus:
`d_q = q⁻¹ d + ħ_q Θ∧L_ξ` and `δ_q = q⁻¹ δ + ħ_q L_ξ*∘ι_ξ` (`q-codifferential-formula`), with
the Webster inner product, in which Θ(ξ) = 1, |Θ| = 1 and Θ is orthogonal to the contact
distribution H. A spin (Hecke-block) mode is, by the statement's own description, a function f
with no horizontal derivative: df = (ξf)·Θ, i.e. d_H f = 0.

1. `d_q f = q⁻¹(ξf)Θ + ħ_q (ξf)Θ = (q⁻¹ + ħ_q)(ξf)Θ = (ξf)Θ = df`, because q⁻¹ + ħ_q = 1.
2. For a 1-form gΘ: ⟨df', gΘ⟩ = ⟨ξf', g⟩ for every f', so δ(gΘ) = L_ξ* g by adjointness alone
   (no sign convention enters). And ι_ξ(gΘ) = g. So
   `δ_q(gΘ) = q⁻¹ L_ξ* g + ħ_q L_ξ* g = L_ξ* g = δ(gΘ)`.
3. Hence `Δ_q f = δ_q d_q f = L_ξ* L_ξ f`, with coefficient (q⁻¹ + ħ_q)² = 1.

Cross-check against the three-term expansion of `rem:q-laplacian-expansion` on the same f:
the classical core gives q⁻² L_ξ*L_ξ f, the cross terms give
q⁻¹ħ_q[δ(Θ∧L_ξ f) + (L_ξ*ι_ξ)df] = 2 q⁻¹ħ_q L_ξ*L_ξ f, and the spin core gives ħ_q² L_ξ*L_ξ f.
They sum to (q⁻¹ + ħ_q)² L_ξ*L_ξ f = L_ξ*L_ξ f. So the expansion is right, and the statement's two
side claims are what fail on spin modes: "the classical core acts exclusively on spatial modes"
and "Coriolis cross-terms vanish by Schur's lemma". Both terms act on Reeb-varying modes, and they
restore the weight from ħ_q² to 1.

This is the 0-form version of the Bridge A2 Lean fact `dirac_reeb` / `reweight_sum_eq_one_of_reeb`
(`LagrangianKZIdentification.lean`): the reweighting leaves the Reeb component unchanged, exactly
because q⁻¹ + ħ_q = 1.

**Conclusion.** On a Hecke block, Δ_q acts through its Reeb part with weight 1, so
μ_λ = Σ_{□∈λ} [c][c+1] (symmetric integers, normalised as above) with **no ħ_q²**. The code's
`q_laplacian_eigenvalue` already computes this, so compute does not change. The paper's ħ_q² and
its Jackson integers are both corrected in this PR.

**What this does not settle.** The paper's own scope note measures the deuteron term of
`_unified_iterative_correction_exact(1, 1)` at the proton q₀ as −17.69 MeV with this μ_λ against
AME +2.2246 MeV: the wrong sign. The prefactor was not the cause. The sign problem sits downstream,
in how μ_λ enters the binding accumulator, and is untouched here.

## The ½ shift, and one honest caveat

Classically the corpus's Δ_ξ on 0-forms is −ξ², eigenvalue m² on a Reeb mode of weight m.
The limit here is Σ c(c+1) = Σ ((c+½)² − ¼), i.e. −ξ² at a weight shifted by ½, minus ¼.
That shift is the q in q·Lₖ (q·Lₖ = e^{h(2Xₖ+1)}). It is natural (metaplectic / ρ-shift), but
the corpus does not yet say where it comes from on the geometric side. If the paper wants −ξ²
exactly, the per-cell term would be [c]² ↔ (Lₖ + Lₖ⁻¹ − 2), the `barSymJMSum` already in
`QJucysMurphy.lean`, and μ_λ would be Σ[c]² instead of Σ[c][c+1]. These are different spectra;
which one is "the binding μ_λ" is a modelling choice, not something this algebra decides.
