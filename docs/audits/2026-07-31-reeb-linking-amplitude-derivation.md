## The claim

The Reeb pn-bond linking amplitude `g`, defined as the per-unit-arc
Gauss–CWF linking contribution of the bond strand, equals

    ℓ(z) = z / √(1+z²) = sin(arctan z),   at the Markov bond factor z = 1/[2]_v.

Previously this was a **conjecture with no instance** (the corpus flagged
the physical identification as open). Here it is **derived** from three
ingredients — not fitted.

## The derivation

**Step 1 — channel direction `(1, z)` (Markov property, a theorem).**
In the two-channel Temperley–Lieb decomposition the bond strand's tangent
is the direction `(1, z)`: the direct/identity channel has amplitude `1`,
the exchange channel (the TL generator `eᵢ`, the cup-cap) carries the Markov
conditional-expectation weight `z = 1/[2]_v = δ⁻¹` (loop value inverse).
This is the Markov property; it fixes the *direction* in channel space.

**Step 2 — unit tangent (CWF unit-tangent structure, a theorem).**
The Gauss–Călugăreanu–White–Fuller linking integrand reads a direction
through *unit* tangents per unit arc length. Normalizing,

    t̂ = (1, z)/√(1+z²) = (cos θ, sin θ),   θ = arctan z.

This fixes the *object*: the unit-normalized channel direction. It kills the
raw-weight `tan θ = z` reading — CWF operates on unit tangents.

**Step 3 — transverse selection (derived here).**
The Gauss linking integrand is generated by the *transverse* component of
the tangent. Its antisymmetric cross-product structure `ṙ₁ × ṙ₂` vanishes on
the parallel (direct/identity) component — parallel strands do not link —
and the crossing that carries all linking content is the exchange channel.
This is **structural in the Kauffman resolution**

    σ = A·1 + A⁻¹·eᵢ ,

where the identity term `1` is the *no-crossing* smoothing and `eᵢ`
(cup-cap) is the crossing. So the linking amplitude is the *exchange*
(second) component of the unit tangent:

    g = t̂_exchange = sin θ = z/√(1+z²) = ℓ(z).

This kills the direct-component `cos θ` reading. Together with Step 2 the
value is **forced**: unit-normalization rules out `tan θ`, transverse
selection rules out `cos θ`, leaving `sin θ = z/√(1+z²)` uniquely.

## What is and is not closed

**Closed — the amplitude identification.** `ℓ(z) = z/√(1+z²)` is derived
from Markov (Step 1) + CWF unit-tangent (Step 2) + Kauffman transverse
selection (Step 3), with no fit. The one residual structural bridge is the
identification of the **TL exchange channel** with the **CWF transverse
direction**, grounded in the Kauffman resolution `σ = A·1 + A⁻¹·eᵢ` — the
crossing lives in `eᵢ`. A referee could press on that bridge; it is
structural, not numerical. The conjecture's *numerical* content (a fitted
amplitude) is gone: nothing here is tuned to data.

the *unit*. The deuteron law `B(²H) = 5g` remains a **near-match** with its
