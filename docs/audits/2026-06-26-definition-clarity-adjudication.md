## Calibration findings (feed back into the scanner thresholds)

1. **`clarity-defn-single` mis-fires at 100% on majors (59/59 false-positive).** Every flagged
   block is a *single* definiendum followed by property / interpretation / regime notes. The
   heuristic counts **bold section-headers** (`T.singleHeaders`), but a stack of bold sub-
   sections is a *tightness* signal (`defn-tight`), **not** a multiple-definienda signal. Fix:
   drop `boldHeaders` from the `defn-single` trigger; rely on `defines[].length >= 2` and
   distinct defining-verb lead-ins only.
2. **`clarity-defn-misplaced` is clean (7/7 false-positive).** No real misplaced definitions in
   the corpus. The two `defines[]`+`:defterm[…]` 'majors' were a *derived property* name
   (`quantum-flatness`, which the prop *derives*, not assumes) and an externally-cited term
   (`cai` = 't Hooft's Cellular Automaton Interpretation). The minor (defining-verb) cases were
   internal proof/statement notation. The criterion is **valid but the corpus is already clean** —
   keep it as a regression guard, expect ~0 hits.
3. **`defn-tight` / `stmt-tight` are well-calibrated on direction but over-rate severity.** ~46%
   of majors are genuine; ~48% are real-but-minor; ~7% false-positive. Consider raising the
   major-severity word thresholds (`2*T.defnWords` / `2*T.stmtWords`) so the major tier tracks
   the confirmed set more tightly.
