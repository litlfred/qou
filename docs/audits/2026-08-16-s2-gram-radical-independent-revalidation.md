## 2. What was measured, and how

[`computations/probes/gram_radical_cellular_revalidation.py`](../../computations/probes/gram_radical_cellular_revalidation.py)
is written from scratch and **does not import `pyhecke.gram`**, so it is an
independent check rather than a restatement. It rebuilds `H_n(q)` in the
`T_w` basis, the Ocneanu/Markov trace by the coset recursion, and the Gram
form `G_{w,v} = tr_M(T_w T_v)`.

Cross-check that the two agree on the object: my trace reproduces
`gram.py`'s `TR_M` entry for entry, including the `h·z² + z` at `e⁻` that
the 2026-05-19 fix installed. So the module and this probe are checking
each other, and disagree with nothing.

**No float64 anywhere.** Exact `Fraction` over ℚ, sympy over ℚ(q), and a
prime field used *only* for a bound it always satisfies.

```
n=3   dim   6   rank   5   corank  1   = 3! − Catalan(3)
n=4   dim  24   rank  14   corank 10   = 4! − Catalan(4)
n=5   dim 120   rank  42   corank 78   = 5! − Catalan(5)
```

**The corank was predicted before it was measured.** The hypothesis —
that the radical is the Temperley–Lieb ideal `ker(H_n(q) ↠ TL_n(q))`, so
corank `= n! − Catalan(n)` — was written down from the three relayed
numbers 1/10/78 and then tested at n = 3, 4, 5. It holds at all three.
That is a much stronger test than confirming a measurement.

**n = 5 is pinned by two one-sided bounds that meet**, not by one method:

- **rank ≤ 42.** The two-sided ideal generated by the three-strand
  q-antisymmetriser has dimension exactly 78, and `G` annihilates every
  one of its basis vectors — verified in exact rational arithmetic.
- **rank ≥ 42.** Rank over GF(2⁶¹−1) is 42, and rank over a prime field
  can only *understate* the rank over ℚ.

Both meet at `Catalan(5) = 42`. GF(p) is used for the inequality it always
satisfies, never as an approximation.

## 3. The framing correction: generic, not special-parameter

This is the one place the proposed framing does not survive, and it is the
most useful output of the exercise.

The suggested picture was Graham–Lehrer / Jantzen: a **cell module becomes
reducible at a special parameter**, its Gram determinant vanishes there,
and one quotients by the radical to get the simple head. The literature
supports that picture in general — Geck proves Hecke algebras of finite
type are cellular, so the theory does apply to our algebra, and
Künzer–Mathas open with exactly the quotient-by-radical construction.

**But it is not what is happening here.** Measured:

- `det G ≡ 0` **identically over ℚ(q)** at n = 3 — as a rational function,
  not at a point.
- corank is **unchanged at all 12 rational q tested**, spanning `[1/3, 5]`.
- the null vector is `(-1/q)^{ℓ(w)}` **identically in q**.

At generic q the Hecke algebra is semisimple and every Specht module is
irreducible with nondegenerate form. **No cell module is degenerating.**
What is degenerate is the **trace**: `z_D = q³/(q²+1)` is precisely the
specialization at which the Ocneanu trace ceases to be faithful on `H_n`
and factors through `TL_n`. Its radical is the TL ideal — a *two-sided
ideal, present at every q* — not a radical that switches on at `q₀`.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

1. **The proton-vs-muon pin is irrelevant to this.** A special-parameter
   story would make the degeneracy depend on which `q₀` you evaluate at.
   It does not. No re-pin can move it, and none ever could.
2. **"Which cell module degenerates, with what elementary divisors" has no
   answer here**, because none does. The Künzer–Mathas elementary-divisor
   route would be the right tool for a Specht-module Gram at a root of
   unity; it is the wrong tool for this object. Reaching for it would have
   produced a confident, wrong analysis.

The *mechanic* the framing recommended is nonetheless exactly right:
quotient by the radical, and the quotient is the meaningful object. It is
right for a structural reason (the trace is a TL trace by design) rather
than the offered one (a reducibility point).

**Scope of the refutation:** this is a **(b) structural exclusion** in the
AGENTS.md sense, and the cited invariant is the measurement that
`det G ≡ 0` as an element of ℚ(q) together with
`TemperleyLiebTower.conventionD_kills_antisymmetriser`, which proves
`y₍₁³₎ = 0` for **every** `q ≠ 0` over any field. A property that holds
identically in a parameter is not a property that holds *at* a parameter.

## 3bis. The S1 composition does NOT hold — and the fork goes the other way

S1 (PR #5187) proved, sorry-free, that `z_D(q) = q³/(q²+1) > 1/2` for every
`q > 1`, via `2q³ − q² − 1 = (q−1)(2q²+q+1)`. It was relayed to me that in
"the three-row instance `y = (z, 1/2−z, z)`" this makes `y₍₂,₁₎ = 1/2 − z_D`
**negative**, hence convention D is *not a positive Markov trace* — and that
this would be my TL finding seen from the other side, since three-row content
is what TL discards.

I was asked to state the composition if it holds and to call the fork if it
does not. **It does not hold, and I am calling it.**

**First, the arity check** (AGENTS.md §"Grep the claim SHAPE"). `(2,1)` is a
partition of 3 with **two rows**, not three. The instance has three
*components* — one per partition of 3 — which is not the same thing as
three rows. Under the TL identification the two-row block is precisely one
that **survives**, so a negative `y₍₂,₁₎` would not be "what being quotiented
away looks like"; it would be a live block with a negative weight, which is a
much worse claim.

**Second, the premise is false of our object.** Solving the corpus's own three
defining equations (the ones
`TemperleyLiebTower.conventionD_kills_antisymmetriser` takes as hypotheses)
symbolically over ℚ(q) at `z = z_D`:

| λ | rows | dim | `y_λ` under D | sign for `q > 1` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `(3)` | 1 | 1 | `(q⁴+1)/(q²+1)²` | **positive** |
| `(2,1)` | 2 | 2 | `q²/(q²+1)²` | **positive** |
| `(1³)` | 3 | 1 | `0` | identically zero |

`Σ dim²` over the identically-zero weights is `1²= 1`, which **is** the
measured corank at n = 3. The solve reproduces
`conventionD_kills_antisymmetriser` exactly, so these are the corpus's
equations, not a variant of mine.

S1's vector `(z, 1/2−z, z)` satisfies the normalisation `h1` — which is why
it looks plausible — but **fails both `h2` and `h3`**:

```
h1  residual 0                                            satisfied
h2  residual (-q⁴ - 1)/(2q(q²+1))                         NOT satisfied
h3  residual (-2q⁶ + 2q(q²+1)(q⁴+q²+1) - (q²+1)²)/(2(q²+1)²)   NOT satisfied
```

At `q = 1.11`: S1's `1/2 − z_D ≈ −0.1127`, while the actual
`y₍₂,₁₎ ≈ +0.2473`. These are different quantities, and only the second is a
Wedderburn weight of `H₃(q)`.

**So the conclusion runs the opposite way.** Under convention D the surviving
weights are **both strictly positive** and the single dead weight is **exactly
zero, not negative**. That is exactly what a Temperley–Lieb trace should look
like: positive on the `≤ 2`-row blocks, zero on the rest — a **positive
semidefinite** form of signature `(5, 0, 1)`, which is what `gram.py`'s own
post-retraction banner already records. Far from being an argument against D,
this is independent confirmation that D's trace is the TL trace.

**What survives of S1.** The algebra is fine and I reproduce it: `z_D > 1/2`
for `q > 1`, with that factorisation, and `1/2 − z_D < 0`. What does not
survive is the *identification* of `1/2 − z_D` with `y₍₂,₁₎`. This is
"conditional-proved ≠ premise-established" (AGENTS.md STRICT): a lemma proved
for a parametrised instance proves the implication, never that our object
satisfies the premise. The Lean is presumably sound; the reading of it is not.

**Scope:** this is a **(a) scope-limited** correction of an interpretation, not
a refutation of S1's Lean. I have not read `DecayLadderConventionDPositivity.lean`
— I am refuting the *composition* I was asked to test, on the ground that our
object does not satisfy its antecedent.

Registered separately: `library/arxiv-math-9806163v1/9806163v1.pdf` (Orellana, *Weights of Markov
Traces on Hecke Algebras*) uses "type D" for the **Coxeter type** `D_n`, which
has nothing to do with our trace convention D. Not cited here, and flagged so
the collision does not propagate.

## 4. The formalized claim

The claim to settle was *"the D-frame Markov–Gram form has a nontrivial
radical, and the physically meaningful object is the quotient."*

Both halves were already in the corpus — `gram_det_eq_zero_of_radical_combination`
(corank ≥ 1), `pairing_descends` + `traceRadical_two_sided` (the quotient is
a ring carrying the pairing). What was **missing** is the link between them
and the numbers: `corank_three` / `corank_four` / `corank_five` are `norm_num`
identities about integers (`6 − 5 = 1`). Nothing tied them to the rank of any
actual matrix.

Added to
[`MarkovGramDegenerate.lean`](../../content/quantum-observable-universe/lean/QOU/QBeta/MarkovGramDegenerate.lean),
sorry-free:

| Declaration | Statement |
|---|---|
| `mem_ker_mulVecLin_of_radical` | a radical combination is a kernel vector of `G` |
| `gram_rank_add_le_card` | `rank G + dim W ≤ card ι` for any radical **subspace** `W` |
| `gram_rank_le_card_sub` | equivalently `rank G ≤ card ι − dim W` |

`gram_rank_add_le_card` is the step that converts the finite exact check
("the TL ideal is in the radical", now done at n = 3, 4, 5) into a sharp
statement about the rank. It is rank–nullity and nothing more, which is
the point: **no floating-point story survives a dimension count.**

Stated over a `Field` because rank–nullity is — archimedean-free per §7c,
no ordering and no `ℝ`, so it applies over ℚ(q) where the degeneracy lives.

```
#print axioms → [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]     (no sorryAx)
```

Verified with `lean`-direct against the real import cone (`TemperleyLiebTower`
compiled first), exit 0, no warnings. Not via `lake build` — this worktree is
a cache restore with no `.trace` files.

**What is deliberately NOT claimed in Lean.** That the radical *is* the TL
ideal, and that corank `= n! − Catalan(n)`, are **measured** (exactly, but
measured) — formalizing them needs `H_n(q)` and `TL_n` as Lean objects,
which is a much larger build. The Lean proves the general implication; the
probe supplies the instance. Keeping that boundary visible is the point.

## 5. Do the consumers survive?

**Yes for every Python consumer**, and the reason is that there were never
any arithmetic consumers of `G_INV` to break: a survey found no `@`, no
`.dot`, no indexing anywhere in the corpus. The re-export surface
(`__init__.py`, `hecke_core`, `constants`, `atom`, `molecular`, `sdp`) carries
`G`, `G_PINV`, `RADICAL` and is consistent.

**One place the repair did not reach**, found by this check and fixed on
this PR:

The Rust side renamed the certificate field `inverse → quotient_inverse`
and removed `gram_inverse` outright, but
[`gram.schema.json`](../../tools/hecke-engine/schemas/gram.schema.json)
still **required** `inverse` and never mentioned `radical`. The emitted
certificate did not validate against its own schema.

Three tests should have caught it. All three **skip**:

| Test | Skip reason |
|---|---|
| `test_rust_gram_validates_against_schema` | `hecke-gram` binary not built |
| `test_rust_gram_inverse_is_right_inverse` | same module-level skip |
| `test_native_gram_inverse_is_right_inverse` | `pyhecke_native` not installed |

The latter two are worse than stale: they assert `G @ G_inv == I`, an
identity convention D makes **false**, against names that no longer exist.
They are the last C-era residue in the tree, and a skip is precisely why
the migration could complete without tripping over them.

All three fixed to assert what is true instead (no `inverse` key, no
`gram_inverse` attribute, `G·rad = 0`, `G_PINV·rad = 0`, `G·G_PINV·G = G`).
Schema now matches the Rust struct field-for-field, checked mechanically.
**The Rust was not rebuilt here**, so the tests remain skipped locally; the
agreement is verified by comparing declared field sets, not by running the
binary.

## 6. Implications

1. **The repair is correct and is already in place.** Quotient-by-radical
   is not a workaround; it is the classical Jones/Temperley–Lieb
   construction. `G_PINV` is the honest object.
2. **The singularity is not a defect and never was.** It is what selecting
   the TL/SU(2) trace parameter *means*. Convention D was chosen for
   independent reasons; this is a consequence, not a cost.
3. **Convention C was avoiding the degeneration, not avoiding a bug.** Any
   C-era result that relied on `G` being invertible was relying on the
   trace *not* being the TL trace. Those numbers are not approximations to
   D-era ones — the two differ exactly on the radical.
4. **The corank is not "1".** It is `n! − Catalan(n)`, which grows fast
   (1, 10, 78, 588 at n = 6). Any n ≥ 4 code that treats the degeneracy as
   "one bad direction" is wrong at scale. The n = 3 case being corank 1 is
   a small-n coincidence of `3! − 5 = 1`.
5. **Skipped tests hid a live contract break** for the whole C → D
   migration. That is a process finding independent of the mathematics.

## 7. Resolution options

The mathematics needs no resolution — it is settled and the code already
implements it. What remains is a genuine choice about how far to carry the
consequences. These are independent; any subset is coherent.

| # | Option | Cost | Blast radius | What it forecloses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Accept as-is.** Land this PR: probe, Lean, schema + 3 test fixes. Treat the singularity as settled and documented. | ~0 (done) | None — no production number moves | Nothing |
| **2** | **Make the skipping tests non-skipping in at least one environment.** Build `hecke-engine` once in CI or a dev image so the schema contract is actually exercised. | Moderate: a cargo build + image/CI change, against the 2026-06-30 Actions-billing directive | Test infra only | Nothing; strictly reduces the chance of a repeat |
| **3** | **Generalize `gram.py` beyond n = 3.** It hardcodes the 6-element NF basis. Corank 1 at n = 3 is a coincidence; a caller extending to n ≥ 4 meets corank 10 with no infrastructure for it. | Substantial — an n-generic NF + quotient | `pyhecke` consumers; `gram_matrix_n` already exists Rust-side | Nothing, but do not start it before a caller needs n ≥ 4 |
| **4** | **Rename to say what it is.** `RADICAL` → the TL ideal generator; document `H_3/rad ≅ TL_3` explicitly in the module and content blocks. | Small, docs + one alias | Cosmetic | Nothing |
| **5** | **Re-examine C-era witnesses that used the Gram inverse.** Under C those numbers came from a non-TL trace. Any still-live witness is not a D approximation. | Unknown until scoped — needs a survey first | Potentially wide | Nothing; but scope before committing |

**My recommendation, and it is a judgement not a finding:** take **1 and 4**
now — they are done or nearly so and cost nothing. Take **2** if the
Actions-billing situation ever permits, because it is the only option that
prevents a recurrence rather than repairing one. Hold **3** until a caller
actually needs n ≥ 4; building it speculatively is the kind of work that
rots. **5** deserves a scoping pass before anyone commits to it — I did not
survey it, and I am not asserting there is anything wrong there.
