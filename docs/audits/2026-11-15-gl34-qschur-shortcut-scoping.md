# G3.4: scoping the q-Schur / double-centraliser shortcut

Bean `qou-gl34` (parent `qou-gld3`). Sibling 7a works the direct highest-weight route;
this document scopes the shortcut proposed in its place, before any build starts.

## Verdict

**The shortcut as proposed does not avoid the hard part, and the double-centraliser theorem is
not needed for G3.4 at all.** What the scoping did find is a cheap replacement for 7a's step (i),
complete reducibility, and a short way to turn 7a's step (ii) into the inequality G3.4 needs.
That reduces G3.4 to one statement about highest-weight spaces.

Notation: `V = (K^d)^{⊗n}`, `A = ρ(H_n(q)) ⊆ End V`, `B` = the subalgebra generated by
`raiseOp`, `lowerOp`, `cartan` and `weightProj` (G3.1, G3.2), `X'` = commutant,
`N_μ = hwSpace μ` (G3.3).

## (a) Does Mathlib have a double-centraliser theorem?

No, not at the pinned Mathlib (paper tree, toolchain v4.24.0). Searched by hand:
`Subalgebra.le_centralizer_centralizer` and `centralizer_centralizer_centralizer` exist, which are
the trivial directions. There is no Jacobson density theorem and no `A'' = A` for semisimple `A`.
`RingTheory/SimpleModule/WedderburnArtin.lean` and `Isotypic.lean` give Wedderburn–Artin and
isotypic components, but not the bicommutant.

What could be built: the elementary von Neumann argument for a transpose-closed subalgebra of
`Matrix ι ι L` over an ordered field `L`. For `x ∈ B''` and `v`, the orthogonal projection onto
`B·v` lies in `B'`, so `x v ∈ B·v`; then amplify to `V^{⊕ dim V}`. No Jacobson density is needed.
Estimate: 400–600 lines, 1–2 PRs. **Not recommended for G3.4, because the argument below does not
use it.**

## (b) Is `End_H(V)` generated by `E, F, K`? This is the real content

Yes, but the bicommutant only restates G3.4: `B' = A ⟺ A' = B` for semisimple `A, B`. Proving
`A' ⊆ B` directly is the Dipper–James / Du / Green route. It needs the double-coset basis
`{φ_{λμ}^w}` of the q-Schur algebra `S_q(d,n) = End_H(V)` and the triangularity of products of
divided powers `E^{(a)}, F^{(a)}` against that basis. Estimate: weeks, research-scale. That is no
cheaper than the highest-weight route, so **not recommended**.

## What the scoping found instead

### F1. `B` is closed under transpose, so `V` is a semisimple `B`-module over an ordered field

`siteTerm X w s` puts `X` at site `s`, the identity before it, and the diagonal weight `w` on the
sites after it. A nonzero entry needs `a k = b k` off `s`, so `(siteTerm X w s)ᵀ = siteTerm Xᵀ w s`.
`raiseOp i j = siteOp (single j i 1) κ` and `lowerOp i j = siteOp (single i j 1) κ` use **the same**
tail weight `κ`, so

    lowerOp q qi i j = (raiseOp q qi i j)ᵀ,

and `cartan` and `weightProj` are diagonal. (Checked by inspection of
`QuantumGLAction.lean:98–110, 396–401`; not yet compiled.) So `B` is transpose-closed. Over an
ordered field the dot product is anisotropic, so every `B`-invariant subspace `W` has a
`B`-invariant complement `Wᗮ`. This is complete reducibility, 7a's step (i), with no Casimir and
no `sl₂` theory. It is the same positivity mechanism `JimboGL` already uses for `A`
(`transpose_jimbo_mem_range`, `eq_zero_of_qtr_mul_transpose`).

### F2. `V = B·N` with `N = ⊕_μ N_μ`

Let `W = B·N`. Then `Wᗮ` is `B`-invariant (F1). If `Wᗮ ≠ 0`, it is preserved by `weightProj`, so it
has a nonzero weight vector. Take one of maximal weight in dominance order: by
`weightProj_mul_raiseOp_mul_weightProj`, `E_i` raises weight by `α_i`, so that vector is killed by
every `E_i` and lies in `N ⊆ W`. That contradicts `W ∩ Wᗮ = 0`.

### F3. `dim B' ≤ Σ_μ (dim N_μ)²`

Every `φ ∈ B'` commutes with `E_i` and `weightProj`, so it preserves each `N_μ`. The restriction
`B' → Π_μ End(N_μ)` is injective by F2: if `φ` vanishes on `N`, it vanishes on `B·N = V`.
**This needs no irreducibility or classification of `U_q`-modules.**

### Closing G3.4

`A ⊆ B'` (G3.2) and `dim A = Σ_{ℓ(λ)≤d} (f^λ)²` (G1, `finrank_range_jimbo`). With F3, G3.4 follows
from

    (★)   Σ_μ (dim N_μ)² ≤ Σ_{ℓ(λ)≤d} (f^λ)²,

which holds once `N_μ = 0` for non-dominant `μ` and `dim N_λ ≤ f^λ`. (★) is exactly 7a's
highest-weight content (`HW_λ ≅ S_λ`), and only the inequality is needed. The decomposition
`V = ⊕ W_λ ⊗ S_λ` then follows from F1 plus `A = B'`.

The weight-separation hypothesis is real: `weightProj` must lie in `B`. Either put it in the
generating set (Lusztig's modified form `U̇` contains the idempotents `1_μ`), or prove it is a
polynomial in the `cartan`s when `q` is not a root of unity of order ≤ n.

## (★): the exact interface (agreed with 7a, 2026-11-15)

7a has accepted the split. P3 takes (★) as an explicit hypothesis in its **summed** form, so that
7a's two halves can be combined on their side without P3 needing to know about dominance:

```lean
open QOU.SchurWeyl.QuantumGL QOU.SchurWeyl.JimboGL in
/-- The weights that occur in `(K^d)^{⊗(m+1)}`: contents of index words. -/
def occWeights (d m : ℕ) : Finset (Fin d → ℕ) :=
  Finset.univ.image (fun a : Fin (m + 1) → Fin d => content a)

-- (★), as consumed by P3:
--   {L} [Field L] [LinearOrder L] [IsStrictOrderedRing L] [NeZero d]
--   (q : L) (hq : q ≠ 0) (hq2 : GenericParameter.NotRootOfUnity q) (m : ℕ)
(hstar : ∑ μ ∈ occWeights d m, Module.finrank L (hwSpace q m μ) ^ 2
          ≤ ∑ lam : RowCapIdx d m,
              Fintype.card (StandardYoungTableau (partitionShape lam.1)) ^ 2)
```

The right-hand side is literally that of `finrank_range_jimbo`. 7a supplies it from:

1. `hwSpace_eq_bot_of_not_dominant` (draft PR #7870). Dominance is in 7a's orientation:
   `μ i ≤ μ (i+1)`, non-decreasing, because `E_i` raises `e_i → e_{i+1}`.
2. `finrank (hwSpace q m μ) ≤ f^{λ(μ)}` for dominant `μ`, where `λ(μ)` is the reversal of `μ`.
3. A bijection between the dominant elements of `occWeights d m` and `RowCapIdx d m`.

`occWeights` was chosen over `Fin d → ℕ` so that the sum is finite. Off it, `weightProj μ = 0`, so
`hwSpace μ = ⊥`.
