## 1. What the ROAST killed, and why the extended Bloch group revives it

**A1 was:** the missing extensive term is what the Markov normalization divides out
(§3 of the attack memo). **The ROAST killed it** (§R1/A1) because *"take the log /
don't normalize"* is a **one-parameter family** — Kauffman bracket vs Markov trace vs
HOMFLY conventions, symmetric vs unsymmetrized q-integers — and choosing the member
that lands on the target **is a fit**. That is also exactly how the 1.5871-vs-1.6159
convention-shopping happened (ROAST §R0.1).

The underlying ambiguity is a **branch-of-logarithm ambiguity**. `log` of a complex
multiplicative invariant is defined mod `2πi`; every "convention" above is a choice
of branch and framing.

> **Neumann's extended Bloch group is the canonical resolution of exactly that
> ambiguity.**

The extended pre-Bloch group `P̂(ℂ)` is generated by **flattened** shape parameters
`[z; p, q]` with `p, q ∈ ℤ` recording the branch data

```
    log z  +  p·πi ,        −log(1−z)  +  q·πi
```

and the flattening is **fixed by the geometry** — the edge/gluing equations of the
ideal triangulation plus the parity condition — **not chosen by the author**. The
extended Rogers dilogarithm then descends to a well-defined

```
    R̂ : B̂(ℂ) ⟶ ℂ/π²ℤ ,      R̂(β̂(M))  =  Vol(M) + i·CS(M)
```

giving the complex volume **exactly**, where the ordinary Bloch group only determines
`CS` mod `π²/6`.

**That is the whole point.** The free parameter that made A1 a fit is precisely the
datum the extended Bloch group *derives*. This converts A1 from *unfalsifiable* to
*derivable* — which is the only thing the ROAST held against it.

## 2. It satisfies all three structural constraints at once — including the owner's

The missing term must be (i) additive/extensive, (ii) super-pairwise ≥3-body (the
proved `ρ_A ≤ C(A,2)` cap), and — today's steer — (iii) **knot-dependent**.

| constraint | how `β̂` satisfies it |
|---|---|
| **additive / extensive** | `β̂(M) = Σᵢ [zᵢ; pᵢ, qᵢ]` is a **sum over ideal tetrahedra** — additive *by construction*. Extensivity is not arranged, it is the definition. `Vol` is additive under JSJ, which the framework already invokes (BW dictionary). |
| **super-pairwise (≥3-body)** | **The load-bearing row.** A tetrahedron has **4 vertices** — irreducibly ≥3-body, not decomposable into pairwise terms. The proved cap `ρ_A ≤ C(A,2)` bounds **pair/edge** counting; a **tetrahedron count** is a different combinatorial functional and is not bounded by it. This is a *principled* escape from the pairwise ceiling, not a workaround. |
| **knot-dependent** ← owner's steer | `Vol + i·CS` is essentially a complete invariant of the hyperbolic structure — maximally knot-dependent. Contrast the bare normalization factor `N(q)^{n−1}`, which depends **only on strand count** and is therefore knot-*independent*. |

The third row is why the owner's steer matters: **it selects between two variants of
A1 that I had conflated.** "Strip the normalization" gives an extensive term that is
knot-independent; "take the extended-Bloch class" gives one that is knot-dependent.
The steer rules out the first. The ROAST's convention-family objection applies to the
first and dissolves for the second.

## 5. Four honest caveats — one is a known trap

1. 🛑 **Hyperbolicity of the actual light-nucleus closures — CHECK THIS FIRST.**
   `β̂` exists only for hyperbolic (or JSJ-decomposed) pieces. If the D / T / ³He /
   ⁴He braid closures are torus links or otherwise non-hyperbolic, `Vol = 0` and the
   route is **dead for exactly the four L2 targets**. AGENTS.md names this as a
   *recurrent re-pitch failure mode*: *"Bloch–Wigner / dilog-transcendence arguments
   on the trefoil (non-hyperbolic → D(z) = 0)"*
   ([`2026-05-30-klr-dialog-assessment.md`](2026-05-30-klr-dialog-assessment.md)).
   **This is the decisive cheap probe and it must run before anything else.**
2. **Binding is real; `Vol + i·CS` is complex.** If the missing magnitude is the `CS`
   part, there must be an argument why a *real* binding energy picks it up rather
   than `Vol` (which the mass formula already uses). Not obvious — this is the
   central physics question, and it should be answered before any compute.
3. **`CS` is only defined mod `π²`** in the ordinary theory. The extended Bloch group
   is what fixes that — which is why it is *needed* — but any `CS`-based magnitude
   carries mod-`π²` bookkeeping that must be handled explicitly, never tuned to land
   on a target. Tuning the mod-`π²` representative would be the same fit the ROAST
   caught in a new costume.
4. **The A-scaling must be checked exponent-first.** Per `qou-imcd`, test whether the
   tetrahedron count of the light-nucleus closures scales with `A` at the right
   *exponent*, with magnitude entirely free. If the exponent is wrong, no flattening
   fixes it.

## 5b. First forward result (hyperbolicity granted per owner) — the volume term lands within a factor of ~2, with **zero** fitting

Owner ruled caveat 1 granted ("assume it is there and work from there"), so the
A-scaling test runs. **The prediction was stated before evaluating**, precisely to
avoid repeating the ROAST §R0.1 convention-shopping:

> `B_volume(A) = E₀ · Vol_per-nucleon · A`, with `Vol_per-nucleon` a **derived**
> hyperbolic volume and `E₀ = m_e/(q₀(q₀−1))` the canonical scale. The framework's
> nucleon **is** the Borromean link `𝓑₃`, so the canonical, no-choice reading is
> `Vol_per-nucleon = Vol(𝓑₃)`.

Scratch float64, **not witness-grade** (repo NO-FLOAT64 rule); `E₀ = 4.185981 MeV`.

| reading | `E₀ · Vol` (MeV/nucleon) | vs `a_V ≈ 15.6` |
|---|---:|---:|
| **canonical — `Vol(𝓑₃) = 7.32772`** | **30.674** | **1.966×** |
| `Vol(𝓑₃)/2 = V_oct = 3.66386` | 15.337 | 0.983× |
| `Vol(4₁) = 2.02988` | 8.497 | 0.545× |
| `v₃` (regular ideal tetrahedron) | 4.249 | 0.272× |

**What is genuinely good here.** The *shape* works with **no free parameter**: the
term is extensive `∝ A` by construction, knot-dependent, built from a derived
hyperbolic volume and the derived scale `E₀`. Nothing was tuned. Contrast the trace
route, which saturates at 3–4 MeV *total* and provably cannot grow (1.65× vs 12.72×
needed). This is the first object in the arc that grows `∝A` **and** lands within a
small factor of `a_V`.

**What is not established, stated plainly.** The canonical reading **misses by
1.966×**. Selecting the `V_oct` row because it reads 0.983 would be exactly the
convention-shopping the ROAST caught — **I did not predict `/2` in advance and I am
rationalising after seeing the number.** Recorded as such.

**Why the miss is nonetheless interesting.** The discrepancy is a **discrete
structural factor ≈ 2, not a continuous knob** — which makes it a *well-posed
question with a derivable answer* rather than a tuning opportunity. Two independent
readings would give exactly 2:

- The Borromean complement decomposes into **exactly two regular ideal octahedra**
  (`Vol(𝓑₃) = 2·V_oct`), so "half a Borromean" is a *geometric cell*, not an
  arbitrary halving.
- Summing a per-**bond** volume over **sites** double-counts every shared interface —
  the standard factor-2 of any site-vs-bond sum.

Either would predict **exactly** 2, against the measured **1.966** — leaving a
**1.7 % residual** that is then a real, quantified discrepancy rather than a fitted
agreement. That residual is the honest deliverable.

**Caveat on the target.** `a_V` is itself a *fitted* liquid-drop coefficient varying
~15.5–15.8 across SEMF parametrisations (~2 % spread). So a 1.7 % agreement is **not
strong evidence** in either direction; the four L2 binding targets (`B_D`…`B_⁴He`)
are the real test, not `a_V`.

**Next, in order:** (i) derive the site-vs-bond factor from the atomic-braid
combinatorics — it must come out *exactly* 2 or exactly `(A−1)/A`-like, never 1.966;
(ii) then evaluate against `B_D / B_T / B_³He / B_⁴He` rather than against `a_V`.
