## What was asked

The #6474 audit's "Honest scope": *"The prune is for single-shift operators. A
general q-D-module element is a finite sum `Σ_k c_k σ^k`; its residue slot is
the sum of the slots and could in principle cancel or survive. The composition
law is the tool for that case; the sum case is not claimed here."*

## The finding, and a correction of my own plan

The bean's opening plan (written before the maths) said *"no cancellation can
revive what is termwise zero"*. **That is false, and it is false for a reason
that has nothing to do with cancellation.** The residue slot of a *sum* is the
sum of the slots (`sumOp_residue_slot`, trivially), but the residue slot of the
*n-th iterate* of a sum is a sum over **words** — compositions of the terms —
and a word is a single shift operator whose multiplier is the product of the
multipliers met along its path (`shiftOp_comp`, `sumOp_comp`). A derivative's
multiplier vanishes only at the constant slot `t⁰`. A word whose path **never
lands on `t⁰`** keeps every factor.

With shifts `1` and `2` from the residue slot `t^{−2}`, the path
`−2 → −1 → +1` (shift 1, then shift 2) jumps over `t⁰`. Every other length-2
word lands on it and dies. So:

> **`twoOp_iterate_residue`.** For `D = shiftOp 2 c₂ + shiftOp 1 c₁` with **both**
> terms annihilating constants (`c₁(−1) = c₂(−2) = 0`), and **any** seed,
> `Dⁿ⁺² f (−2) = c₁(−2) · c₂(−1) · (Dⁿ f)(1)`.

The residue at level `n+2` is the `t¹` coefficient of the level-`n` jet — at
`n = 0`, the leading square-root coefficient of the branch, which is nonzero for
a genuine branch point. The weight is `−1` for the classical pair
`d/dt + (1/t)d/dt` (`classicalTwo_iterate_residue`: `(−2+1)(−1+2)`) and for the
Jackson + q-deformed-`d/dz` pair (`jacksonPlusQddz_iterate_residue`:
`[−1]_q [1]_q`, needing only `q − q⁻¹ ≠ 0`). It is not a q-phenomenon.

**So the #6474 clause "a residue needs a non-derivative component" does not
extend from single operators to sums.** Two derivatives of different shifts
suffice. What the residue does need among shifts `≤ 2` is the **odd (shift-1)
component**: a sum of shift-2 terms is a single shift-2 operator and stays dead
(probe (4)). This is exactly the case the #6474 audit declined to claim, and the
answer is the opposite of the one its single-shift result suggests.

## The residual restores the conclusion

The second measured target property is the Door-v `(−1)^n` residual: the jet
tower's monodromy eigenvalue must alternate. For `D = E + O`, `E` a sum of even
shifts (commutes with `t ↦ −t`) and `O` a sum of odd shifts (anti-commutes),
the **parity twist** is `mono (D f) = E (mono f) − O (mono f)` — the even part
keeps the eigenvalue, the odd part flips it, so a mixed sum has no eigenvalue at
all. Demanding one at every level is a strong constraint:

> **`residual_tower_is_odd_part`.** If `mono (Dⁿ X) = (−1)ⁿ ε · Dⁿ X` for all `n`
> (`ε ≠ 0`, char ≠ 2, no zero divisors), then at every level `Dⁿ X = Oⁿ X` and
> `E (Oⁿ X) = 0`: **the even part annihilates every level and the tower is
> generated by the odd part alone.**

Proof shape: at each level the twist gives `mono (E Z + O Z) = ε'·E Z − ε'·O Z`,
the residual demands `−ε'·(E Z + O Z)`, so `2ε'·E Z = 0`; induct.

For the two-term derivative-like operator this closes the loop:

> **`twoOp_residual_kills_residue`.** On a power-series fixed-parity seed, a tower
> carrying the full residual is the pure shift-1 tower, and the single-shift
> prune (`residue_slot_zero_shift_one`) kills its residue at every level.

**Residue and residual are mutually exclusive for derivative-like sums of shifts
`≤ 2`.** The probe shows both sides constructively: on a generic bar-even seed
the residual fails by level 2 *and* the level-4 residue is a nonzero rational
function of `q`; on the family `a₀ + a₁ t` the level-1 residual forces `a₁ = 0`
exactly, and then the level-2 residue `−a₁` vanishes.

So the #6474 conclusion — the sought `D_q` needs an **odd shift and a
non-derivative component** — **survives for sums**, but the mechanism is
different: for a single operator the residue alone forces the non-derivative
term; for a sum the residue admits mixed derivatives and it is the *residual*
that forces them out. The general-sum form is `sumOp_residual_even_part_dead`.

## What this does and does not say

> **Measured the same day (`qou-o2n6`,
> [`2026-09-03-door-v-residual-scope.md`](2026-09-03-door-v-residual-scope.md)):
> the residual gap below is real.** The corpus's Door-v residual is *asymptotic*,
> and the full-alternation hypothesis of `twoOp_residual_kills_residue` is
> **unsatisfiable** by the GGM carrier at any `q < 2`
> (`QOU.Mass.GGMAlternationOnset.ggm_full_alternation_impossible_of_lt_two`).
> The exclusivity survives level by level above the window
> (`twoOp_residual_from_level_kills_residue_beyond`); at the proton `q₀` residue
> and residual **coexist at jet levels 2 … 7 at least**. Read "mutually
> exclusive" in this document as "mutually exclusive above the window".

- `D_q` is **not constructed**; W1 stays a wall. The class is narrowed by a
  second, independent argument.
- Shifts `≥ 3` remain unpruned, as before; a shift-4 term reaches `t^{+2}` from
  `t^{−2}` in one step and is not touched by `c(−4) = 0`.
- The residual theorem assumes the *full* alternation at every level with a
  fixed nonzero `ε`. A residual holding only asymptotically, or only on a
  sub-tower, is not covered — that is the honest gap, and it is where a `D_q`
  carrying both properties would have to live if one exists: an odd
  non-derivative component (`c₁(−1) ≠ 0`), or a residual weaker than the one
  the corpus currently measures.
- No C1 kernel coefficient enters anywhere (W3 respected).
