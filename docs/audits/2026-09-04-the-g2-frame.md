## 2. The mathematics, stated precisely

`G₂` has two symmetric-space quotients in play. **They are not the same object
and the framework needs exactly one of them.**

### 2a. `G₂/SU(3) = S⁶` — the nearly-Kähler six-sphere

`G₂ = Aut(𝕆)` acts on `Im 𝕆 ≅ ℝ⁷` preserving the norm, transitively on the unit
sphere. The stabiliser of a unit imaginary octonion `e` is `SU(3)`, of dimension
8, so the orbit is `S⁶ = G₂/SU(3)` of dimension `14 − 8 = 6`.

- **Isotropy representation:** `T_e S⁶ = e^⊥ ∩ Im 𝕆`, real dimension 6. The fixed
  `e` supplies a complex structure `J = L_e` (`L_e² = −id`), under which the
  isotropy is the **complex 3-dimensional fundamental `𝟑` of `SU(3)`**.
- **Frobenius–Schur type: complex (`FS = 0`).** `𝟑 ≇ 𝟑̄` for `SU(3)`; this is the
  Günaydin–Gürsey branching `Im 𝕆 ⊗ ℂ = 𝟏 ⊕ 𝟑 ⊕ 𝟑̄` \cite{gunaydin-gursey1973}
  the corpus already cites in `prop:color-su3-from-g2`. **It is cited input, not
  formalised** — the ledger row says so explicitly, and this note does not
  upgrade it.

### 2b. `G₂/SO(4)` — the quaternionic Wolf space

`SO(4) = (SU(2)×SU(2))/ℤ₂ ⊂ G₂` is the stabiliser of a quaternionic subalgebra
`ℍ ⊂ 𝕆`. Dimension 6, so the quotient has dimension `14 − 6 = 8`. It is the
quaternionic-Kähler symmetric space (Wolf space) of `G₂`.

- **Isotropy representation:** `V₁ ⊗ Sym³(V₂)`, where `V₁, V₂` are the
  2-dimensional representations of the two `SU(2)` factors — real dimension
  `2 × 4 = 8`.
- **Frobenius–Schur type: real (`FS = +1`).** Each `SU(2)` factor is
  quaternionic; a tensor product of two quaternionic representations is real.
  The quaternionic-Kähler tangent bundle is a **real** bundle.

### 2c. Which one the framework needs, and why the other is dead

It needs **`G₂/SU(3) = S⁶`**, on two independent grounds:

1. **Dimension.** The object must be 3-dimensional (complex). `S⁶`'s isotropy is
   `6 = 2·3` real, i.e. complex 3. The Wolf space's is 8.
2. **Type.** `Sym2GateFSTrichotomy.gate_forces_complex_of_dim_three` (sorry-free)
   requires complex type; `real_triple_fails_gate` and
   `two_dvd_dimOf_of_no_complex` exclude the two self-dual types. `S⁶`'s isotropy
   is complex; the Wolf space's is real, i.e. squarely inside the excluded
   sector.

**Scope on the `G₂/SO(4)` negative — two separate claims, two separate scopes:**

- **Scope (b), licensed by an invariant proved here.**
  `no_orthogonal_quotient_of_dim_six` (this note's Lean): `14 − C(n,2) ≠ 6` for
  **every** `n`, because `C(n,2)` jumps `6 → 10` between `n = 4` and `n = 5`. So
  a 6-dimensional `G₂` quotient is **unreachable from the orthogonal series at
  any `n`** — the tetraplectic leg cannot be deformed into the colour leg by
  changing `n`. Mirrored by `no_unitary_quotient_of_dim_eight`
  (`14 − (n² − 1) ≠ 8` for every `n`, since `n² = 7` has no solution): the Wolf
  space is not `G₂/SU(n)` for any `n` either. **The two quotients are supplied by
  disjoint classical series, and they meet only in `G₂` itself.**
- **Scope (b) conditional on a cited input.** The *type* argument — real
  isotropy, hence excluded by `real_triple_fails_gate` — is licensed by a proved
  Lean invariant, but its premise (that the `G₂/SO(4)` isotropy is
  `V₁ ⊗ Sym³V₂`, real) is classical representation theory, **not formalised in
  this corpus and not held in `library/`** (§4). Cite it as such.

### 2d. The degree-3 volume form on `S⁶`, and whether `dΨ = 0` matters

The `SU(3)`-structure on `S⁶` carries a nowhere-vanishing complex volume form
`Ψ = Ψ⁺ + iΨ⁻`, of **degree 3** and **type `(3,0)`**. Its existence is exactly
the statement that `Λ³` of the isotropy is the *trivial* `SU(3)`-representation —
i.e. unimodularity, `det = 1`. That is `Λ³W ≅ 𝟙` in the corpus's language.

**It is not closed.** The nearly-Kähler structure equations are

    dω = 3 Ψ⁺,        dΨ⁻ = −2 ω ∧ ω

(normalisation conventions differ by constants across the literature; the shape
is what matters). Note the refinement the framing usually omits: **`Ψ⁺` *is*
closed** — being `dω/3` it is exact — and it is **`Ψ⁻` that is not**. So
"`dΨ ≠ 0`" is right for `Ψ = Ψ⁺ + iΨ⁻` but the precise statement is
`dΨ⁺ = 0, dΨ⁻ = −2ω²`.

**A stronger fact the non-closedness discussion tends to hide:** the octonionic
almost complex structure on `S⁶` is **not integrable** — its Nijenhuis tensor is
nowhere zero. `S⁶` is not a complex manifold under `J` (whether it admits *any*
integrable complex structure is a famous open problem, and irrelevant here since
the `SU(3)` structure is the octonionic one). So `S⁶` is not Calabi–Yau, and the
"`(3,0)`-form" is `(3,0)` in the almost-complex sense only.

**Does any of this matter for `Λ³W ≅ 𝟙`? No — and the reason must not be
fudged.** `Λ³W ≅ 𝟙` is an isomorphism of *objects in a monoidal category*: an
algebraic condition on a single object, asserting that a particular 1-dimensional
exterior power is the unit. It is **pointwise**. `dΨ` measures how a pointwise
trivialisation varies *from point to point*; integrability measures whether the
pointwise `J` assembles into a holomorphic atlas. Both are conditions on the
*family*, and the categorical statement quantifies over no family. Formally: the
categorical datum is `Λ³_ℂ (T_e S⁶) ≅ ℂ` as `SU(3)`-modules, which is
`det(SU(3)) = 1` and is true whatever `dΨ` does.

**The honest converse, which is the crux.** Because the categorical statement is
pointwise, **it does not need the manifold at all.** Everything `Λ³W ≅ 𝟙` asks
for is present in the single isotropy representation. So the differential
geometry of `S⁶` — nearly-Kähler equations, non-closedness, non-integrability —
is *not an obstruction the framework must overcome*; it is **scaffolding the
framework does not need**. What the framework needs is the fibre at one point,
and that is linear algebra.

**What joining establishes.** Fragment 2 gave one dimension count at one place.
The theorems above turn it into a *characterisation*: the open-orbit condition

stabiliser dimension hits `14` at `n = 7` and nowhere else. Fragment 3 gave one

1. **From the octonion module to the framework's own category.**

**This is why the verdict is "viable but does not discharge the objection".**

## 7. What would settle it, and the default

**The next move (recommended, my judgement — not a finding).** Take step (iii):
build `W := span(e₂,…,e₇)` with `L_{e₁}` as the complex structure, prove
`su3 e₁` preserves it, and prove `Λ³_ℂ W ≅ 𝟙` — the unimodularity of
`su3 ⊂ 𝔰𝔩(W)`. It uses only Mathlib's `ExteriorPower` and objects the corpus
already has, needs no manifold and no Lie group, and would turn the *cited*
Günaydin–Gürsey input into a *derived* one for the first time. It would not close
gap (iv), and must not be reported as doing so.

**The default, if nothing is done.** Nothing breaks. Eightfold item 3 keeps its
"Mathlib gap, infrastructure not structural" label — which §4(i) shows understates
the gap and §4(ii) shows overstates it in the other direction — the three
fragments stay unjoined in prose, and `W` stays a postulate.
