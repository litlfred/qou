# Deriving the 1-loop invariant for `c ≥ 2` cusps — and withdrawing my own blocker

**Date:** 2026-08-07 · **Bean:** `qou-fl4t` · **Follows:** PR #4691, #4697, #4707, #4708
**Probe:** `computations/probes/one_loop_multicusp_derivation_probe.py`
**Witness:** `computations/one-loop-multicusp-derivation.witness.json`

## Headline

The multi-cusp case is now implemented in `computations/one_loop_torsion.py`
and validated. The obstruction I asserted in PR #4708 **was not real**, and
saying why is most of the derivation.

## What PR #4708 got right, and what it got wrong

Right: the multi-cusp formula is **not in Dimofte–Garoufalidis**. They state
Definition 1.2 for one cusp and say only (§1.9) *"The generalization to multiple
cusps is also quite straightforward, but left out mainly for simplicity of
exposition."* So it has to be derived, not looked up. That stands.

Wrong: the reason I gave for it being hard. #4708 quoted DG —

> *"For a one-cusped manifold, every edge begins and ends at the cusp, which
> implies `Σ_I G_Ii = Σ_I G'_Ii = Σ_I G''_Ii = 2`"*

— and concluded the identity "fails for `c ≥ 2`", so the extension "is not a
matter of adding rows". **Measured directly, the identity holds at every cusp
number:**

```
4_1   c=1 N= 2   G,G',G'' sums = {2},{2},{2}   HOLDS
L5a1  c=2 N= 4   G,G',G'' sums = {2},{2},{2}   HOLDS
L6a4  c=3 N= 8   G,G',G'' sums = {2},{2},{2}   HOLDS
s776  c=3 N= 6   G,G',G'' sums = {2},{2},{2}   HOLDS
L8a1  c=2 N=12   G,G',G'' sums = {2},{2},{2}   HOLDS
```

It is not a statement about cusps at all. Each tetrahedron has six edges in
three opposite pairs, one pair of each shape type, and every one of them is
glued into exactly one edge class of the triangulation — so the column sum
counts the two `z`-type edges of that tetrahedron and gets 2. Cusps never
enter. DG's phrasing is about their own one-cusped setting, not a hypothesis,
and I read a restriction into it that is not there.

**Lesson for the corpus:** I turned a quoted sentence into a structural
obstruction without testing the sentence. It is a one-line check.

## The derivation

Write `ζ = 1/z`, `ζ' = 1/(1−z)`, `ζ'' = 1/(z(z−1))`, so that `ζ + ζ' + ζ'' = 0`.

**Step 1 — the specialised edge rows sum to zero, for every `c`.** The row of
edge `I` specialises to `Σ_j (G_Ij ζ_j + G'_Ij ζ'_j + G''_Ij ζ''_j) e_j`, so
summing over `I` and applying the identity above gives `2(ζ_j + ζ'_j + ζ''_j) =
0` in every column. Measured: `max |column sum| ≈ 1e−71` throughout.

**Step 2 — the rank is exactly `N − c`.** Measured on all 13 manifolds. So the
determinant wants `N − c` edge rows and `c` peripheral rows. That guess was
already in the corpus; step 1 is what turns it into a reason.

**Step 3 — *which* `N − c` rows. This is the part that is not obvious, and it
is the whole residual failure.** An independent *subset* of edge rows spans the
right complex subspace but can generate a proper **sublattice** of the edge
lattice, and the determinant then comes out multiplied by the index. Observed
index: exactly 2. Over 25 randomisations of each of the 13 manifolds, the HNF
basis gives one value everywhere, and the subset gives two on three of them:

```
                    HNF lattice basis      greedy edge subset
L5a1  (c=2)         1 value                2 values, ratio 2.0
L6a4  (c=3)         1 value                2 values, ratio 2.0
L7a1  (c=2)         1 value                2 values, ratio 2.0
every other case    1 value                1 value
```

It is a rare event — a longer run at 40 randomisations caught `L6a4` once in 37
trials and `L7a1` twice in 39 — which is why a short sweep can miss it
entirely. All ten remaining manifolds, including four at `c ≥ 2`, agree under
both choices.

Taking a **Hermite-normal-form basis of the lattice generated by all `N` edge
rows** removes it: unimodular row operations preserve the lattice, not merely
its rational span. The relevant lattice lives in `ℤ^{2N}`, because the
specialised row depends only on the Neumann–Zagier pair
`(a, b) = (G − G'', G' − G'')` — a consequence of `ζ'' = −ζ − ζ'`.

**The flattening was never the problem at `c ≥ 2`.** `|τ|` does not depend on
the choice: the kernel of the edge conditions moves the monomial only by a
phase (measured to ~1e−70 on every manifold, with and without Neumann's
peripheral conditions). The module imposes the full Neumann conditions anyway,
since that is the actual definition.

So the formula is

```
τ = ± ½ · det( B ; P_1 ; … ; P_c ) / ∏_j ζ_j^{f_j} ζ'_j^{f'_j} ζ''_j^{f''_j}
```

with `B` an HNF basis of the edge lattice (rank `N − c`) and `P_i` the chosen
peripheral row at cusp `i`.

## Why this is more than self-consistency

Triangulation invariance is necessary but weak — it compares the construction
only against itself, and cannot see a wrong constant. The real check is the
**change-of-curve law**: replacing the meridian row of cusp `k` *alone* by that
cusp's longitude row must multiply `τ` by the conjugate of the **cusp shape** of
cusp `k`. SnapPy computes cusp shapes from the cusp cross-section geometry,
with no reference to any of this, and the test is per cusp — so it probes
exactly the structure that is new at `c ≥ 2`.

It holds to **~1e−59** on all 13 manifolds, at `c = 1, 2, 3`. (The conjugation
is a fixed orientation convention. On `s776`, `L7a1`, `L8a1` and `L10n1`,
SnapPy's `cusp_info` ordering does not match the peripheral-row ordering in
`gluing_equations`; the values match as a multiset. That is a labelling
convention and does not touch `τ_μ`, which uses all meridians together.)

Independent presentations of the same manifold also agree:
`L5a1 = 5^2_1 = m129 = √2`, `L6a4 = t12067 = 8`, `L6a2 = m203 = √3`.
(`6^3_1` is **not** the Borromean rings — it is the magic manifold, and it
agrees with `s776` and `L6a5` at `√14`.)

## What this does NOT settle — and it is the thing the `c_σ` arc wanted

**The overall constant at `c ≥ 2` is not pinned.** The `½` is DG's, fixed at
`c = 1` by their `4_1` example. Triangulation invariance cannot see a constant
depending only on `c`; neither can the change-of-curve law, which is a ratio.
So a normalization `2^{α(c−1)} · ½` would pass every test in this document.

That is **exactly the shape of the per-cusp factor `2^{(c−1)/2}`** that
`whitehead_c_sigma_torsion.py` needed (bean `qou-pn5j`). So:

* this derivation does **not** revive that factor, and must not be cited as
  pinning it;
* `c_σ = 2^{−3/4}` stays at **IDENTIFICATION**, not DERIVATION;
* PR #4697's finding still stands independently — the ambiguity group is the
  trace-field unit group, which no universal constant can absorb.

Pinning the constant needs an external multi-cusp reference value. Nothing in
`library/` supplies one: Garoufalidis–Yoon (2411.03801) prove 1-loop = adjoint
torsion for **two-bridge knots**, which is one-cusped.

## What is now unblocked

`whitehead_c_sigma_torsion.py` (L5a1, `c=2`) and
`probes/borromean_nz_adjoint_torsion.py` (L6a4, `c=3`) can be repointed at
`one_loop_torsion.one_loop`, which now answers at their cusp numbers. Their
old values were `2^{5/2}` and `2^6`; the invariant ones are `√2` and `8`.
Doing that repair is separate work and does not, by itself, resolve `c_σ` —
see the section above.

## Formalized in Lean

`QOU/OneLoopEdgeLattice.lean` — sorry-free, `[propext, Classical.choice,
Quot.sound]` only. It proves the parts of this argument that are algebra rather
than measurement:

* `zeta_sum_eq_zero` — `ζ + ζ' + ζ'' = 0`.
* `nz_pair_reduction` — the specialised row depends only on `(G − G'', G' − G'')`.
* `edge_rows_sum_zero` — column sums `2` ⟹ the specialised edge rows sum to
  zero. **There is no cusp number in the statement or the proof.** That is step 1
  above, and the formal statement is what makes "the identity is not a
  one-cusped hypothesis" checkable rather than asserted.
* `det_basis_change`, `det_basis_change_unimodular`, `det_sublattice_index` —
  a unimodular change of the row lattice fixes the determinant up to sign, and
  rows generating an index-`k` sublattice scale it by `k`. That is step 3, and
  the observed `k` was 2.
* `edge_rows_hypotheses_satisfiable` — non-vacuity witness.

What is *not* formalized: that the construction computes the adjoint torsion, or
that it is a topological invariant. Those stay numerical (and, at `c = 1`, a
theorem of Garoufalidis–Yoon). The shapes are free complex numbers in the Lean
file; no gluing equation is imposed.
