## Error budget — ranked by magnitude

| # | Source | Current ppb | Path to close |
|---|--------|------------:|---------------|
| **1** | **Per-atom cal/m_e variation** (the binding-energy correction) | ~10⁶ | The framework's per-A cal/m_e captures binding energy structurally, but a *single-K* prediction averages it out. To predict per-atom mass without averaging, need an a priori prediction of the cal/m_e A-dependence. |
| **2** | **Length-≤-2 partition truncation** | ~10⁴ | 2-variable Schur formula vanishes for length>2; full Schur–Weyl over all p(3A) partitions captures contributions we currently drop. |
| **3** | **dim_cap=5000 truncation** | ~10³ | Scale via faster Rust kernel (e.g., Geck–Pfeiffer character) or batch caching across atoms. |
| **4** | **q=1 MN tower vs proper q-Hecke** | ~10² | Hybrid uses q=1 MN for the Φ_q tower; proper q-Hecke per-braid throughout would close this (the open P2.2c-norm research task). |
| **5** | **f64 precision in chi_q_atomic** | ~10⁻⁵ | Promote to mpfr (chi_q_atomic_mpfr in Rust). Not the bottleneck currently. |
| **6** | **m_e CODATA uncertainty** | ~0.4 | Already at floor; fundamental to the H⁰ calibration. |
| **7** | **q_0 CODATA uncertainty** | ~15 | Floor unless q_0 is derived algebraically (would close item 1's blocker; research task P1.1). |

## Theoretical floor — set by the three calibration measurements

Per [`def:three-calibration-discipline`](../../content/quantum-observable-universe/observations/three-calibration-discipline.md),
the framework **categorically allows exactly three** external
calibration inputs aligned to (co)homological degrees:

| Degree | Calibration | Current CODATA / PDG precision | ppb floor |
|-------:|-------------|-------------------------------:|----------:|
| $H^0$ | $m_e = 0.51099895000(15)$ MeV | ~**0.29 ppb** relative | 0.29 |
| $H^1$ | $m_\mu/m_e = 206.7682830(46)$ → fixes $q_0$ | ~**22 ppb** relative | 22 |
| $H^2$ | $Q_\beta$ (e.g. tritium 18.59(0.06) keV) | ~**3000 ppm** relative | 3,000,000 |

**The floor is the worst-precision calibration entering the prediction.** For a mass prediction that uses only $m_e$ and $q_0$ (no β-decay channel): floor is **22 ppb**. For predictions involving $Q_\beta$: floor is **3 × 10⁶ ppb** (3000 ppm) — much worse.

These calibrations cannot be "removed" — they are categorically required by the framework. Below the floor is **physically impossible** without tighter experimental measurements:

- (a) **Tighten $m_\mu/m_e$**: from ~22 ppb to ~1 ppb requires future CODATA. Recent muon g-2 / Penning-trap experiments push this direction; might be feasible at 1–10 ppb in 2030s.
- (b) **Tighten $Q_\beta$**: tritium-decay endpoint to ppb precision is **infeasible** — neutrino-mass experiments (KATRIN etc.) are at meV precision on the endpoint, ~10 ppb on the Q-value relative to atomic mass. Other β-decay anchors may help.
- (c) **Pick a different $H^2$ anchor**: free-neutron lifetime $\tau_n$ to ppb? Currently ~10⁻⁴ relative. Probably also infeasible at 1 ppb.

**The 1 ppb goal is therefore experimentally bounded by the calibration inputs, not by the algebraic framework.** The framework can predict at 1 ppb IF the three calibrations are at 1 ppb — which currently only $m_e$ satisfies.

A realistic short-term goal: **reach the $m_\mu/m_e$ floor (~22 ppb) for mass predictions that don't involve $Q_\beta$.**

## Practical work to reach the 15 ppb floor

### Phase A — Capture binding-energy structure (close ~10⁶ ppb)

The cal/m_e variation across A (0.992 → 0.981) IS the binding-energy correction. To convert this into a *first-principles prediction* (no per-atom anchor):

**A.1 — Full Schur–Weyl over all p(3A) partitions.** Replace `length_le_2_partitions_of(n_strands)` with full enumeration. Most of these have zero or small contribution under the 2-variable Schur, but the full Hecke character can be non-zero. Compute:

$$\mathfrak{m}(W) = \sum_{\lambda \vdash 3A} w_\lambda(q) \cdot \chi^\lambda_q(W)/\dim(\lambda)$$

where $w_\lambda = \dim_q(\lambda)^2 / [3A]_q!$ uses the FULL Schur (not the 2-variable specialization).

**A.2 — Per-braid Φ_q tower with q-deformation.** Current hybrid uses q=1 MN for the tower (Φ_q applied to q=1 traces). Switch to q-Hecke per-sub-braid traces with the right Φ_q normalisation. The P2.2c-norm research task: derive the correct coefficient (z² → ?) when sub-braid traces are q-Hecke, not q=1 MN.

**A.3 — Higher-level tower terms.** The current per-braid tower has level 0 (descended), 1 (per-nucleon), 2 (per-pair). Level 3 (triples) is absorbed via pentagon ascension. Verify pentagon at all levels; possibly add level-4 (four-body) for closer match.

**Estimated reduction**: 10⁶ → 10³ ppb (close 3 orders).

### Phase B — Compute completeness (close ~10³ ppb)

**B.1 — Geck–Pfeiffer character algorithm in Rust.** The pyhecke_native module has `chi_q_via_gp_hoefsmit` — bypasses Specht-module dimension explosion. Wire into per-braid path. Unlocks computation of ALL partitions of 3A (no dim_cap), not just small ones.

**B.2 — Caching across atoms.** Per-nucleon and per-pair sub-braid traces are reused across many atoms. Compute once, cache. Saves time for the ⁴⁰Ca test, which currently re-evaluates these.

**B.3 — Multi-level partition contribution.** Even at length-≤-2 within dim_cap, the LOST partitions (length>2 OR dim>5000) contribute. Compute their contribution via Geck–Pfeiffer to verify they're negligible OR include them.

**Estimated reduction**: 10³ → 10² ppb (close 1 order).

### Phase C — Numerical precision (close ~10² ppb)

**C.1 — Promote chi_q_atomic to mpfr.** Add `chi_q_atomic_mpfr(shape, factors, q, prec)` in Rust (uses the existing seminormal_mpfr machinery). Eliminates f64 round-off.

**C.2 — Use mpmath end-to-end.** All intermediate computations at 50dp. The witness already uses mpmath for the sum; ensure no float64 leak.

**Estimated reduction**: 10² → 22 ppb (close to the $m_\mu/m_e$ floor).

### Phase D / E — calibration-input floor (NOT a research task)

The 22 ppb floor (or 3 × 10⁶ ppb when $Q_\beta$ enters) is **set by the precision of the three calibration measurements**. The framework's three-calibration discipline categorically requires these inputs; they are NOT to be replaced by algebraic derivations.

To go below 22 ppb on mass predictions:
- Need a future CODATA $m_\mu/m_e$ at ppb precision (Penning-trap / muon-g-2 advances may deliver this in 2030s)
- Possibly switch the $H^1$ anchor to a different precise observable (e.g., a different lepton-mass ratio or fine-structure constant), if any reaches ppb sooner.

To go below the $Q_\beta$-anchored 3 × 10⁶ ppb floor: tighten $Q_\beta$ measurement (KATRIN / next-gen β-decay) or pick a different $H^2$ anchor (free-neutron lifetime?).

**The framework cannot predict mass to better than the worst-precision calibration input.** This is a structural feature of the three-calibration discipline, not a flaw.
