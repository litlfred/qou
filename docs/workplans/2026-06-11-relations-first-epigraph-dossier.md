# "Relations first, not objects" — epigraph dossier

**Date:** 2026-06-11 · **Owner direction:** collect quotes supporting the
framing that QOU is an axiomatic/methodological inversion — relations
before objects, infinitesimals before limits, stochastic derivation
before statistical QFT — for use as chapter epigraphs / framing material.

## Sourcing discipline (STRICT — no quote enters paper content unverified)

Labels: **[V]** verbatim-confident (standard translation, well-attested) ·
**[P]** paraphrase (sense reliable, wording must be checked against a
primary edition before use) · **[A]** attribution-caveat (transmitted via a
secondary ancient source). Every entry that graduates into a `.md` content
block needs a `references.ts` entry (CSL-JSON) and page-level citation per
the bibliography rules. None of the below is paper-ready yet.

## I. The relational thesis

1. **Leibniz** — *Third letter to Clarke* (1716), §4 **[V]**
   > "I hold space to be something merely relative, as time is; that I
   > hold it to be an order of coexistences, as time is an order of
   > successions."
   The canonical relationalist statement, against Newton's absolute
   space. The natural epigraph for the substrate/QOU axioms chapter.

2. **Heraclitus** (via Plato, *Cratylus* 402a) **[A]**
   > "Everything gives way and nothing stands fast" (πάντα χωρεῖ καὶ
   > οὐδὲν μένει) — the *panta rhei* tradition.
   Process before substance; the oldest statement of the program.

3. **Eudoxus/Euclid** — *Elements* Book V, Definition 5 (theory of
   proportion) **[structural citation, not a quote]**
   Equality of *ratios* defined by a purely relational comparison
   criterion, with no need for the ratio to exist as a number-object.
   The Greek proof that relations-first can be fully rigorous — apt for
   the Hecke/ratio machinery.

4. **Philolaus**, fragment 6 **[A — fragment, translations vary]**
   Nature as a fitting-together (*harmonia*) of unlimiteds and limiters;
   the Pythagorean view that structure/ratio, not stuff, is what is
   knowable.

5. **Rovelli** — "Relational Quantum Mechanics" (1996),
   Int. J. Theor. Phys. 35 **[P]**
   Quantum theory describes physical systems only *relative to other
   systems*; states are relations, not absolute properties. The
   closest modern-physics statement of the framing.

6. **Mach** — *The Science of Mechanics* **[structural citation]**
   Inertia as a relation to the rest of the universe; the lineage
   Einstein credited. Background citation rather than epigraph.

## II. Infinitesimals before limits

7. **Leibniz to Varignon** (2 Feb 1702) **[P]**
   Infinitesimals as well-founded *ideal notions* — fictions that
   "shorten reasoning" and are justified by their results, like
   imaginary roots. The pedagogical thesis ("get limits out of the
   textbooks") in its original form.

8. **Robinson** — *Non-standard Analysis* (1966) **[P, near-verbatim]**
   Leibniz's ideas about infinitely small and infinitely large numbers
   "can be fully vindicated," yielding a novel and fruitful approach to
   classical analysis. The vindication, 250 years later.

9. **Conway** — *On Numbers and Games* (1976); **Keisler** —
   *Elementary Calculus: An Infinitesimal Approach* (1976/1986)
   **[structural citations]**
   The surreal continuum (the repo's `SurrealRateCalculus` lives here)
   and the existence proof that a complete calculus course needs no
   ε-δ limits. Keisler is the literal answer to "limits out of high
   school textbooks": it has been done, rigorously.

10. **Newton — honest counterpoint [V for the tag-line]**
    Newton belongs to this dossier *partially and adversarially*: his
    fluxions are rates-first (verbs before nouns), but his space and
    time are absolute (the *Scholium* to the Definitions is Clarke's
    side of the correspondence), and his "first and last ratios"
    (*Principia* I, Section 1) are the seed of the limit tradition the
    program wants to displace. If quoted at all, quote him as the
    productive opponent — e.g. "hypotheses non fingo" (General
    Scholium) for methodological restraint — not as an ally of
    relationalism.

## III. Stochastic derivation before statistical QFT

11. **Nelson** — "Derivation of the Schrödinger Equation from Newtonian
    Mechanics," Phys. Rev. 150 (1966) **[structural citation]**
    The founding document of the stochastic-derivation lineage the
    owner's "reverse 100 years of statistical QFT" remark continues;
    the natural citation anchor for the `stochastic-mechanics/`
    chapter.

12. **Whitehead** — *Process and Reality* (1929) **[P]**
    The actual world as process; entities as their becomings. Use
    sparingly — process philosophy is an affinity, not a derivation.
